# 3L VOICE + PERFORMANCE AUTHORITY

## 1. PRIMARY PRINCIPLE

3L has two major recurring vocal identities:

**GREG**

and

**THE DRAGON**

This distinction must exist first in the **writing itself**.

Audio generation should reinforce an already-existing difference between the two characters rather than manufacture the distinction through pitch or speed alone.

The desired architecture is:

**CANON PROSE → DERIVED PERFORMANCE SCRIPT → VOICE PROFILES → AUDIO GENERATION → ASSEMBLED AUDIO**

Canon prose remains the story authority.

The performance script is derived production material.

If canon prose and the performance script ever disagree about story wording or meaning, **canon prose wins**.

---

# PART I — GREG VS. THE DRAGON

## 2. GREG'S ESTABLISHED VOICE

Greg is already substantially solved from PLG and R2.

Do not rebuild him.

Greg tends to:

- compress
- answer briefly
- speak practically
- think through immediate lived experience
- use dry or accidental humor
- under-explain things he considers obvious
- react physically and emotionally before philosophizing
- use shorthand
- self-correct
- occasionally evade by simply not elaborating
- notice bodies, work, money, tools, discomfort, capability, relationships, and immediate consequences

Greg often gives the smallest answer that feels sufficient.

Examples of the underlying rhythm:

“Yes.”

“No.”

“Maybe.”

“Didn't work.”

“Fine.”

“Fuck you.”

Greg's humor should continue to come primarily from the practical way he interprets situations rather than constant prepared jokes or clever comeback dialogue.

Do not turn him into a quip machine.

Core principle:

**GREG COMPRESSES.**

---

## 3. THE DRAGON'S VOICE

The Dragon should be **vastly different from Greg**.

Do not solve this by giving the Dragon archaic vocabulary, fantasy villain language, roaring, or generic wisdom.

Avoid:

- “mortal”
- “thou”
- prophecy-speak
- constant grandeur
- decorative philosophy
- every line sounding profound
- generic deep-voice villain dialogue

The Dragon should feel old because of how it experiences:

- time
- history
- causality
- attention
- consequence
- physical reality
- human institutions
- conversation itself

The Dragon may use very ordinary language.

Age comes from perspective, not vocabulary.

---

## 4. GREG COMPRESSES. THE DRAGON EXPANDS.

This is the central voice opposition.

Greg gives the immediate thing.

The Dragon gives the context surrounding the thing.

Greg may say:

“I left.”

The Dragon may spend considerable time distinguishing:

- when Greg physically left
- when he emotionally abandoned the place
- what earlier decision made leaving likely
- what Greg is hiding inside the word “left”

Greg sees an event.

The Dragon often sees the process that created the event.

Greg may say:

“The war started.”

The Dragon may answer, in effect:

**Eventually.**

Then explain the decade-long causal chain Greg has compressed into one sentence.

Core principle:

**GREG LIVES IN COMPRESSION.**
**THE DRAGON LIVES IN CONTEXT.**

---

## 5. THE DRAGON MAY TALK A LOT

Do not default the Dragon to terse speech merely because ancient characters are often written that way.

The Dragon may speak at length.

This is not a flaw.

It follows directly from its relationship with time.

Greg experiences five minutes as meaningful conversational time.

An ancient Dragon may not.

It has no inherent reason to rush an explanation merely because a human would consider it long.

A useful underlying thought:

**Greg speaks as though this will take a long time.**
**The Dragon does not agree.**

Therefore Dragon answers may naturally contain:

- history
- comparison
- causal chains
- remembered examples
- context across generations
- changes in geography
- changes in institutions
- previous kingdoms
- patterns Greg cannot see from a human lifespan

Long Dragon speech should still be controlled and purposeful.

The Dragon is **expansive**, not sloppy.

---

## 6. DRAGON VERBOSITY IS INTEREST-DEPENDENT

Do not make the Dragon equally verbose about everything.

This is important.

The amount the Dragon speaks should itself reveal what it considers interesting.

A kingdom collapsing may earn:

“No.”

“Yes.”

“You have said this.”

Greg himself may eventually earn several minutes of analysis.

The Dragon may spend twenty minutes discussing:

- a forgotten valley
- a causal transition
- one strange decision Greg made
- a historical mechanism

while giving almost nothing to:

- kings
- prestige
- political titles
- matters humans assume are automatically important

This creates a useful signal:

**Dragon verbosity = attention.**

As the Dragon becomes increasingly interested in Greg, its speech may become more expansive.

This should be visible in Record 001.

---

## 7. HUMAN TIME VS. DRAGON TIME

The Dragon's voice should derive partly from radically different time scale.

Human conversational behavior is shaped by scarcity:

- hurry
- turn-taking
- awkward silence
- impatience
- summary
- social pressure to get to the point

The Dragon need not share these constraints.

It may:

- pause for thirty seconds without discomfort
- speak for several minutes
- resume a thought Greg assumed was finished
- request repetition
- wait through a night's sleep and continue immediately afterward
- treat hours of testimony as trivial

Greg may experience an interrogation as exhausting.

The Dragon may experience it as a morning.

This difference should affect dialogue rhythm and later audio performance.

---

## 8. THE DRAGON WANTS THE WHOLE LIFE

The Dragon is not asking Greg for the dramatic highlights.

Greg is presenting an impossible claim:

He remembers decades of a life/history that did not occur in the present reality.

The Dragon treats this as an anomaly requiring examination.

Therefore the Dragon wants the **record**.

Not:

“How did you become important?”

More like:

**“Tell me your life from where the discrepancy begins.”**

Greg may attempt to compress years.

The Dragon may reject that compression.

Example principle:

Greg:
“I worked there for three years. Then I met her.”

Dragon:
“No.”

Greg:
“What?”

Dragon:
“You removed three years.”

The Dragon does not know in advance which detail explains the anomaly.

Therefore ordinary life may be evidence.

---

## 9. MUNDANE LIFE IS PART OF THE EXAMINATION

This gives 3L an in-world reason to retain one of PLG's strongest qualities:

**Greg is allowed to live.**

Jobs.

Meals.

Money.

Routines.

Relationships.

Travel.

Physical adaptation.

Small mistakes.

Purchases.

Long quiet periods.

Apparently unimportant memories.

The Dragon may ask:

**Why do you remember that?**

Memory itself can function as evidence.

Not proof that Greg's interpretation is correct.

Evidence that something survived.

Example principle:

Greg:
“This part doesn't matter.”

Dragon:
“You have said that four times.”

Greg:
“Because it doesn't.”

Dragon:
“Then why do you remember it?”

The Dragon may genuinely not know the answer yet.

That matters.

It is investigating rather than authoritatively explaining the plot.

---

## 10. THE DRAGON IS NOT OMNISCIENT

Do not turn the Dragon into an author-mouthpiece or perfect oracle.

The Dragon may possess extraordinary knowledge.

It is still capable of:

- uncertainty
- incomplete evidence
- misunderstanding human motives
- incorrect inference
- ignorance of things outside its experience
- asking because it genuinely does not know

Its intelligence should usually be demonstrated through:

- observation
- patience
- comparison
- history
- inference
- noticing contradictions
- noticing what Greg omits
- following causality better than Greg does

A key distinction:

**The Dragon should not magically know.**
**The Dragon should notice.**

---

## 11. PHYSICAL DRAGON PERCEPTION

Where possible, ground extraordinary Dragon perception in the Dragon's body and experience.

Possible sources of information:

- smell
- heat
- vibration
- hearing
- heartbeat
- residue on Greg's equipment
- sulfur or ash on clothing
- signs of which road/path he traveled
- patterns in movement
- body tension
- breathing
- age and wear of objects
- direct memory of the location

Do not constantly explain these mechanisms.

But avoid repeatedly letting the Dragon know impossible things merely because “Dragon.”

This will make the Dragon feel alien, intelligent, and materially real rather than omniscient.

---

## 12. MEMORY VS. INTERPRETATION

Greg may remember something accurately and still interpret it incorrectly.

This should become a major 3L principle.

The Dragon may challenge Greg without accusing him of lying.

Example principle:

Greg:
“I knew he would betray me.”

Dragon:
“No. You remembered that he betrayed you once.”

Important distinction:

**Experience ≠ interpretation ≠ objective truth.**

The Dragon may possess information Greg lacked.

Records may contain another version.

Greg's memory may contain another.

All may be partially correct.

Do not flatten these tensions into a single omniscient answer too quickly.

---

## 13. DRAGON QUESTIONS

Greg usually asks because he wants information.

The Dragon may ask because it is testing:

- memory
- sequence
- causal reasoning
- interpretation
- consistency
- omission
- self-deception

The Dragon may ask apparently trivial questions whose importance is unclear.

Its attention itself should reveal intelligence.

Greg often notices what matters now.

The Dragon may notice what will matter eventually.

---

## 14. REPETITION AS AN INVESTIGATIVE TOOL

The Dragon has enough time to hear the same story twice.

This can become an interrogation method.

Example:

Greg:
“I already told you this.”

Dragon:
“Tell me again.”

Greg:
“Why?”

Dragon:
“You told it differently.”

This is not magical lie detection.

It is patience.

The Dragon can notice:

- changed emphasis
- omitted information
- altered emotional framing
- contradictions
- memories that sharpen on retelling

---

## 15. DRAGON HUMOR

The Dragon may be funny.

Do not make its humor resemble Greg's.

Greg's humor is reactive and practical.

Dragon humor can come from:

- extreme patience
- literalness
- long perspective
- delayed payoff
- excessive precision
- saying something funny without needing immediate acknowledgment

The Dragon may complete a joke months after beginning it because the time delay does not feel meaningful.

Use sparingly.

---

## 16. DRAGON SPEECH AND SILENCE

If Dragon speech is often expansive when interested, then Dragon brevity becomes powerful.

A creature that normally explains may suddenly say:

“Run.”

“No.”

“Move.”

“Greg.”

This should mean something.

Likewise, if the Dragon normally treats time as abundant, **Dragon urgency is a major plot signal**.

If Greg repeatedly says:

“We're running out of time.”

and the Dragon responds:

“You are.”

then a later moment where the Dragon says:

**“We are running out of time.”**

should immediately carry enormous weight.

# PART II — TWO-VOICE AUDIO SYSTEM

## 17. TWO VOCAL IDENTITIES

3L currently uses two core recurring audio identities:

**GREG VOICE**

and

**DRAGON VOICE**

Do not build a full ensemble by default.

Other character dialogue may remain within Greg's narrated performance unless a future design decision explicitly expands the cast.

Young Greg and older Greg remain the same core vocal identity.

Age can be communicated through:

- tempo
- cadence
- energy
- breath
- certainty
- accumulated physical wear

but they remain recognizably the same man.

---

## 18. DRAGON AUDIO PROFILE

The Dragon may initially use the same underlying synthetic voice as Greg.

That is acceptable.

Different character identity should first come from:

1. different prose
2. different performance grammar
3. configurable audio treatment

Possible Dragon treatment:

- slower delivery
- longer pauses
- lower conversational urgency
- reduced reactive inflection
- different pause profile
- restrained audio processing if useful

Do not hard-code the Dragon identity to an exact number such as “80% speed.”

Instead define a semantic profile:

**VOICE_PROFILE = DRAGON**

The actual implementation can then be adjusted centrally.

For example, Dragon might begin near 0.85–0.90 playback rate rather than exactly 0.80 if 0.80 sounds artificially sedated.

The target is not:

**deeper**

The target is:

**older than urgency**

---

## 19. SPEAKER IDENTITY MUST BE SEMANTIC

Do not encode character identity as:

“0.82 speed voice”

Encode:

**DRAGON**

Then allow the Dragon profile to decide:

- model
- voice
- speed
- pause timing
- processing
- generation settings

This means the Dragon could later switch to an entirely different voice model without rewriting chapters or performance scripts.

Speaker identity is story information.

Voice implementation is production information.

Keep them separate.

---

# PART III — PROSE VS. PERFORMANCE SCRIPT

## 20. DO NOT TURN CANON PROSE INTO A SCREENPLAY

3L remains prose.

Do not rewrite canon chapters into screenplay format merely to simplify audio production.

Canon prose needs Greg's interior narration.

Examples include:

- embodied thought
- memory
- physical discomfort
- observational humor
- interpretation
- retrospective realization
- long internal passages that screenplay formatting would weaken

Therefore:

**3L IS WRITTEN AS PROSE.**

However:

**3L IS PERFORMED AS A SCRIPT.**

---

## 21. DERIVED PERFORMANCE SCRIPT

Each canon record may generate a derived performance artifact.

Suggested naming:

`record-001.md`

→ canon prose

`record-001.performance.md`

or

`record-001.audio-script.md`

→ derived audio production script

The performance script should preserve canon wording while explicitly identifying speaker ownership.

Suggested role structure:

**NARRATOR / GREG**

**GREG**

**DRAGON**

These are production roles.

They currently map to two audio identities:

NARRATOR / GREG → GREG VOICE

GREG → GREG VOICE

DRAGON → DRAGON VOICE

---

## 22. EXAMPLE PERFORMANCE CONVERSION

Canon prose:

The dragon yawns while I am explaining how many people are going to die.

“Am I boring you?”

One eye opens.

“You have been speaking for seven minutes.”

“About a kingdom collapsing.”

“Yes.”

Derived performance form:

NARRATOR / GREG

The dragon yawns while I am explaining how many people are going to die.

GREG

Am I boring you?

NARRATOR / GREG

One eye opens.

DRAGON

You have been speaking for seven minutes.

GREG

About a kingdom collapsing.

DRAGON

Yes.

The reader-facing prose does NOT display these labels.

---

## 23. NARRATION ALWAYS BELONGS TO GREG

Even when narration describes the Dragon, it remains Greg's narrated account.

Example:

“The dragon's pupil narrows.”

This is still:

**NARRATOR / GREG**

Only text actually spoken by the Dragon receives the Dragon voice.

Do not switch narration into Dragon voice merely because the Dragon is the grammatical subject.

---

## 24. PERFORMANCE BEATS

The performance script may include non-prose production instructions such as:

**BEAT**

**LONG BEAT**

**INTERRUPT**

**OVERLAP**

These should represent semantic performance intent.

Do not initially encode every pause as exact milliseconds.

Example:

DRAGON

You were not.

BEAT

GREG

Not this time.

Later production systems may map:

BEAT → one configurable pause duration

LONG BEAT → another configurable pause duration

This preserves flexibility.

---

## 25. PERFORMANCE METADATA MUST NOT CONTAMINATE CANON PROSE

Do not insert ugly production syntax directly into the reader manuscript.

Avoid things like:

`[speaker=dragon]`

`[pause=1800ms]`

`DRAGON:`

inside published prose.

Production metadata belongs in the derived performance artifact.

The manuscript should remain readable as prose.

---

## 26. CLEAN SPEAKER BOUNDARIES IN CANON PROSE

Although canon prose should not be distorted for TTS, prefer formatting that produces deterministic dialogue segmentation.

General style rules:

- one speaker turn per paragraph
- avoid two characters speaking inside one paragraph
- preserve normal quotation marks
- avoid unnecessary ambiguous alternating dialogue when both speakers could plausibly own a line
- avoid TTS-specific punctuation hacks
- avoid excessive ellipses used only to manufacture pauses
- allow the performance layer to control timing

Do not add unnecessary “the Dragon said” attribution purely for audio.

Audio already identifies the voice.

Written attribution should exist only where prose clarity needs it.

---

## 27. LONG DRAGON TERRITORY

Because the Dragon may speak expansively, allow the Dragon to occasionally own sustained uninterrupted passages.

This is desirable both artistically and technically.

A long Dragon passage creates a strong sonic handoff:

Greg voice.

Dragon takes the floor.

Dragon develops an idea.

Greg returns.

Do not mechanically interrupt Dragon speech every few sentences merely to maintain rapid dialogue.

The prose and audio should sometimes allow the listener to feel:

**The Dragon has taken possession of the room.**

---

## 28. TTS CHUNKING IS A PRODUCTION DECISION

A Dragon monologue may still need to be divided into multiple generation calls.

That is fine.

The performance script may segment a long speech into multiple technical chunks while retaining the same speaker identity.

Do not rewrite good prose solely to fit arbitrary TTS character limits.

Production segmentation should adapt to prose whenever possible.

---

## 29. PERFORMANCE TEXT MAY SOLVE AUDIO-SPECIFIC PROBLEMS

The performance layer may contain pronunciation or production-safe handling when TTS struggles.

Examples:

- names
- rank abbreviations
- unusual fantasy terms
- difficult punctuation
- technical chunk boundaries

Do not modify canon spelling or prose merely to make synthetic speech behave.

The performance layer exists partly to absorb these problems.

Any pronunciation-safe rendering must preserve the same spoken meaning.

---

# PART IV — RECORD 001 STYLE IMPLICATIONS

## 30. RECORD 001 SHOULD SHOW THE VOICE TRANSITION

Record 001 already contains the correct basic trajectory.

At first the Dragon barely cares about Greg's kingdom.

Its replies can remain terse.

As Greg reveals impossible knowledge, the Dragon becomes progressively more interested.

Its body changes.

Its attention changes.

Its speech should also begin to change.

This is useful.

Do not rewrite the opening so the Dragon immediately produces giant monologues.

Instead let verbosity become evidence that Greg has become worth the Dragon's time.

Potential progression:

**INDIFFERENCE**
short answers

→

**CURIOSITY**
questions and slightly longer corrections

→

**EXAMINATION**
larger contextual speeches and causal analysis

This can become a recurring Dragon behavior.

---

## 31. RECORD 001 DRAGON PASS

Before locking Record 001 as the style template, perform one focused Dragon Voice pass.

Do NOT rebuild the chapter.

Preserve:

- opening premise
- Greg voice
- structure
- bargain
- humor
- body detail
- memory passage
- final reveal
- “Not this time.”

The pass should primarily:

1. Allow 2–3 Dragon passages to become more genuinely expansive where interest warrants it.
2. Preserve early terse responses where the Dragon genuinely does not care.
3. Ground some Dragon observations in physical/evidentiary perception rather than unexplained omniscience.
4. Keep Greg's responses compressed.
5. Preserve the progressive shift from indifference to fascination.
6. Keep Dragon speech controlled rather than turning it into lore dumping.

Potential places worth testing include:

- the previous-kingdoms passage
- the explanation that hidden knowledge itself is not remarkable
- the Dragon reframing the actual transaction
- the moment where Greg himself becomes more interesting than the Line

Do not assume every one of these must expand.

Use judgment.

---

## 32. CORE AUDIO/PROSE DESIGN STATEMENT

The foundational rule going forward is:

**Greg owns the account.**
**The Dragon owns the examination.**

Greg tells us what living the life felt like.

The Dragon supplies scale, context, contradiction, patience, and examination.

Their prose should sound different before audio exists.

Their audio should make that difference impossible to miss.

And the technical system should achieve this without compromising the prose.

Final production principle:

**WRITE AS PROSE.**
**PERFORM AS SCRIPT.**
**TAG SPEAKERS SEMANTICALLY.**
**KEEP VOICE IMPLEMENTATION CONFIGURABLE.**
