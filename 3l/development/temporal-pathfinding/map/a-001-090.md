# 3L Temporal Map Slice A

Status: EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE

Source: approved `3L_300_Record_Temporal_Map.md` first pathfinding pass. Content below is preserved as the original 001-090 search surface. Records 001-010 reference canon. Records 011-090 are disposable hypotheses.

### 001 — THE PETITIONER

At fifty-nine, Greg reaches Ithar with a kingdom-scale Line failure and discovers that the dragon is more interested in Greg’s impossible knowledge than in the people who may die.

### 002 — THE CLAIMANT

Greg proves that his knowledge is experiential rather than scholarly, forcing Ithar to confront the mismatch between the man present and the life he claims to remember.

### 003 — THE BARGAINER

Greg and Ithar agree that Life Two will be told as the spine of the account, with First Life admitted whenever remembered knowledge materially changed what Greg did.

### 004 — THE RETURNED

At nineteen, Greg wakes in his younger body carrying an S-class lifetime of memory and immediately learns that remembered technique is not the same thing as recovered capability.

### 005 — THE ASPIRANT

Greg skips the brutal First-Life route he believes he no longer needs and takes practical Line work instead, convinced he is optimizing the same destination.

### 006 — THE HAND

Station labor makes Greg useful before it makes him powerful, teaching him the wages, hazards, maintenance customs, and ordinary dependencies hidden beneath heroic ranker mythology.

### 007 — THE ADVISER

Greg uses First-Life memory to predict a local failure correctly, giving someone else a better outcome and creating his first meaningful divergence.

### 008 — THE RESCUER

A remembered disaster proves harder to prevent than to remember, and Greg saves someone only after discovering that the original cause was embedded in mundane institutional machinery.

### 009 — THE STAYER

An opportunity to rejoin his remembered route appears, but Greg stays because East Four, its people, and the life accumulating around him have begun to matter.

### 010 — THE STRANGER

Bren Calder arrives eight years after Greg failed to meet him, and Greg finally accepts that Life Two is not a damaged copy of the old life but his life.

## A: THE LIFE THAT STAYS

Roughly late 20s into early 30s. Household, work, marriage, ownership, training, and local belonging become real.

### 011 — THE CONFIDANT

Greg gives Nessa the first coherent piece of the truth and discovers that being believed by one person is more frightening than fooling everyone else.

### 012 — THE TENANT

A housing problem turns Greg and Nessa’s private arrangement into a practical shared household full of rent, repairs, schedules, and unromantic intimacy.

### 013 — THE PARTNER

Greg and Nessa begin making decisions whose costs only make sense if each expects the other to remain in the picture.

### 014 — THE INSPECTOR

East Four’s buried chamber earns Greg temporary inspection authority and exposes how often official safety depends on people signing forms they only half understand.

### 015 — THE DEBTOR

Greg borrows against future station work to solve a present infrastructure problem, tying his household finances to the same system he keeps claiming he can leave.

### 016 — THE CONTRACTOR

A small independent repair contract lets Greg discover that owning the risk is different from merely doing the dangerous part.

### 017 — THE FOREMAN

Greg is forced to direct workers less capable than he is without turning every task into a demonstration of how much better he could do it himself.

### 018 — THE WITNESS

A preventable injury becomes an Authority hearing, and Greg learns that telling the truth and proving responsibility are separate professions.

### 019 — THE NEIGHBOR

A quiet neighborhood dispute over Line access makes Greg responsible for people he would never have noticed in First Life.

### 020 — THE HOST

Greg and Nessa host station workers after a long shift, and the ordinary evening becomes the first time Greg notices he has built a social world rather than merely occupied one.

### 021 — THE MESSENGER

Greg carries a technical warning to a neighboring district and discovers that information loses force when it crosses class, guild, and jurisdictional lines.

### 022 — THE PATIENT

A minor injury sidelines Greg long enough for him to see how much of his identity still depends on being the person who can work through pain.

### 023 — THE SUITOR

An embarrassingly practical disagreement about money, furniture, and future work forces Greg to admit that his relationship with Nessa has become courtship whether he planned it or not.

### 024 — THE LIAR

Greg uses First-Life knowledge to hide a danger from Nessa for what he calls her protection, then learns that correct information does not make the deception less corrosive.

### 025 — THE CONFESSOR

Greg tells Nessa enough about Bren, Halden, and the old life that she can finally understand why some strangers make him grieve before he meets them.

### 026 — THE LOVER

With the impossible secret no longer between them, Greg experiences intimacy as something he cannot optimize from memory because this relationship never existed before.

### 027 — THE PLANNER

Greg and Nessa begin planning in years instead of weeks, forcing him to picture a future he has no First-Life map for.

### 028 — THE BIDDER

A larger Line maintenance contract pits Greg against established firms and introduces the economics of insurance, bonds, political favor, and failure liability.

### 029 — THE EMPLOYER

Winning the contract means Greg must hire people, make payroll, and accept that someone else can get hurt because of a decision he made at a desk.

### 030 — THE MECHANIC

A stubborn station fault resists heroic solutions and yields only to patient mechanical work, strengthening Greg’s belief that the Line is an infrastructure system before it is a mystery.

### 031 — THE MEDIATOR

A dispute between rankers and civilian crews makes Greg useful because he understands both danger and labor, not because he is the strongest person present.

### 032 — THE HUSBAND

Greg and Nessa marry with more practical negotiation than ceremony, and the role lands harder on him than any rank because it creates a future he can fail personally.

### 033 — THE HOUSEHOLDER

Owning a modest home turns drainage, taxes, repairs, neighbors, and storage into permanent parts of Greg’s life rather than background texture.

### 034 — THE CITIZEN

A district vote over Line fees drags Greg into public consequence and shows him how infrastructure becomes politics the moment somebody has to pay for it.

### 035 — THE ADVOCATE

Greg publicly backs a maintenance levy that costs him business but protects poorer districts from deferred failure.

### 036 — THE GUARDIAN

A seasonal pressure emergency makes Greg responsible for holding one station long enough for families to evacuate rather than chasing the more prestigious fight elsewhere.

### 037 — THE MENTOR

Greg takes on a young worker whose mistakes remind him that knowing the destination does not teach anyone how to survive the road.

### 038 — THE COUNCILOR

Temporary service on a district works council teaches Greg how competent people produce bad outcomes when every budget line belongs to a different emergency.

### 039 — THE JUROR

A contractor negligence case forces Greg to judge another person by standards he has broken himself when the stakes were high enough.

### 040 — THE SURVIVOR

A regional surge kills people despite Greg’s preparations, puncturing his growing belief that enough competence can make ordinary life safe.

## A → B SEAM: THE LIFE THAT SPREADS

Greg’s local competence expands into regional reputation, institutions, friendship, and system influence.

### 041 — THE TRAVELER

Greg leaves East Four on a circuit of failing stations and sees for the first time how differently the Line shapes towns only a few days apart.

### 042 — THE CONSULTANT

His reputation turns practical knowledge into paid advice, creating a new class of work where Greg’s judgment can affect places he never personally touches.

### 043 — THE CARTOGRAPHER

Greg helps map old Line branches and realizes that official maps describe administrative ownership better than they describe the buried system itself.

### 044 — THE BROKER

A shortage of specialized parts makes Greg the person connecting workshops, stations, guilds, and desperate towns that would otherwise never cooperate.

### 045 — THE RIVAL

A younger contractor begins beating Greg on price and speed, forcing him to distinguish between genuine innovation and shortcuts that merely have not failed yet.

### 046 — THE NEGOTIATOR

Greg prevents a strike from becoming a regional outage by admitting that labor has leverage precisely because civilization depends on work most people barely notice.

### 047 — THE APPRENTICE

An old Line specialist makes Greg start at the bottom of a technical discipline he dismissed in First Life, and he hates how much he has to learn.

### 048 — THE SPECIALIST

Greg earns real competence in pressure architecture and gains a professional identity that First-Life S-class Greg never needed.

### 049 — THE GUEST

A visit to Bren’s city brings Greg near the life his old friend built without him, and he chooses observation over trying to reclaim intimacy.

### 050 — THE ACQUAINTANCE

Bren and Greg work the same contract again and begin liking each other for reasons that belong entirely to Life Two.

### 051 — THE PURCHASER

Greg buys a failing workshop because its location and people matter more than its books, tying him further to East Four’s economic future.

### 052 — THE OWNER

Running the workshop teaches Greg that broken tools, sick workers, slow customers, and unpaid invoices can threaten a life as effectively as monsters.

### 053 — THE LANDLORD

An attached set of rooms turns Greg into a reluctant landlord whose tenants make property ownership socially messier than he expected.

### 054 — THE GUARANTOR

Greg stakes his name on a worker’s license application and learns that reputation becomes a form of capital other people can spend.

### 055 — THE SPONSOR

He funds training for workers who could never afford the Guild route, quietly changing who gets to become technically legible to the system.

### 056 — THE ENFORCER

A contractor repeatedly endangers crews, and Greg uses rank, contracts, and intimidation to remove him from work without pretending persuasion is always enough.

### 057 — THE DEFENDANT

Greg’s methods land him before an Authority panel, where he discovers that being morally right does not erase procedural wrongdoing.

### 058 — THE SETTLER

Years after intending to stay only temporarily, Greg admits that East Four is the place from which every other destination is now measured.

### 059 — THE RETURNING MAN

A brief trip through a First-Life landmark shows Greg how little emotional ownership survives when the people and choices around the place are different.

### 060 — THE CELEBRANT

An anniversary with Nessa makes Greg confront the strange fact that his happiest memories are increasingly ones First Life cannot validate.

### 061 — THE SURVEYOR

A deep-station survey exposes pre-Authority Line structures whose geometry does not match modern engineering assumptions.

### 062 — THE TRANSLATOR

Greg becomes the practical bridge between scholars describing ancient theory and crews who need to know which valve not to open.

### 063 — THE SKEPTIC

A fashionable theory about Line origin collapses under field evidence, strengthening Greg’s distrust of elegant explanations unsupported by work.

### 064 — THE ARCHIVIST

Greg starts keeping his own failure records because institutional reports routinely erase the small causes that actually mattered.

### 065 — THE CORRESPONDENT

Halden, now influential in central review, begins exchanging technical letters with Greg and turns an accidental survival into a long-distance professional alliance.

### 066 — THE VISITOR

Greg and Nessa spend time in the capital and discover how invisible peripheral maintenance becomes once infrastructure is working.

### 067 — THE SUPPLIANT

Greg asks a ministry for money he cannot secure locally and learns exactly how many political promises can fit inside a safety grant.

### 068 — THE LOBBYIST

He trades favors, testimony, and reputation for maintenance funding, becoming the kind of institutional operator nineteen-year-old Greg would have mocked.

### 069 — THE COMPROMISER

Greg accepts a partial repair package that leaves one district exposed because refusing it would leave three districts worse.

### 070 — THE ACCOUNTANT

A budget season forces Greg to quantify which risks are affordable, revealing that every safety system contains an argument about whose future counts.

### 071 — THE RECRUITER

Greg builds a larger crew and begins selecting for judgment and reliability rather than raw ranker potential.

### 072 — THE TRAINER

He develops a practical training sequence from decades of mistakes, discovering that some First-Life knowledge becomes useful only after he learns how to teach it.

### 073 — THE EXAMINER

Serving on licensing tests puts Greg in the uncomfortable position of denying dangerous people opportunities he once took without permission.

### 074 — THE FAILURE

One of Greg’s best trainees causes a serious incident after following Greg’s advice too literally, exposing the limits of authority-by-experience.

### 075 — THE APOLOGIST

Greg makes restitution without hiding behind intention, and the cost reshapes how his company documents judgment calls.

### 076 — THE STANDARD-BEARER

A field practice Greg invented becomes a regional standard, giving him the first taste of changing the system without being present.

### 077 — THE CONSERVATIVE

When younger engineers propose a faster redesign, Greg becomes the person arguing that old infrastructure deserves caution rather than contempt.

### 078 — THE RADICAL

New evidence then forces Greg to support replacing a centuries-old practice, embarrassing everyone who thought they understood his politics.

### 079 — THE HUSBAND AGAIN

Nessa calls Greg out for giving institutions his best attention and bringing only exhaustion home, forcing a renegotiation of what partnership means after success.

### 080 — THE VACATIONER

A deliberately uneventful trip with Nessa becomes difficult precisely because Greg has forgotten how to exist without a problem to solve.

## B: THE LIFE THAT MATTERS

Roughly mid-to-late 30s. Greg becomes established enough that ordinary life, love, reputation, and responsibility can carry serious future cost.

### 081 — THE FISHERMAN

A day of ordinary incompetence outside his profession gives Greg the rare pleasure of being bad at something with no civilizational consequence.

### 082 — THE FRIEND

Bren and Greg finally choose each other as friends in Life Two without Greg manufacturing the conditions, paying off the restraint of their first encounter.

### 083 — THE WITNESS AGAIN

Bren tells Greg a story from his own altered path, proving that the man Greg remembered was only one possible Bren among many.

### 084 — THE BROTHER

A crisis brings Bren into Greg’s household long enough that old emotional language returns with new justification rather than borrowed memory.

### 085 — THE HOSTAGE

A labor dispute traps Greg between workers he respects and officials who can shut down his contracts, making neutrality impossible.

### 086 — THE UNION MAN

Greg sides with maintenance crews on a safety provision and discovers that institutional enemies remember public embarrassment longer than technical correctness.

### 087 — THE BLACKLISTED

A procurement office quietly freezes Greg out of major work, forcing his network to survive without the contracts that made it respectable.

### 088 — THE SMALL MAN

Reduced to local jobs again, Greg rediscovers the scale of work where consequences have names instead of statistics.

### 089 — THE RECOVERER

His workshop survives the blacklist by serving neglected districts, accidentally creating a business model less dependent on central favor.

### 090 — THE PUBLIC MAN

A difficult rescue pushes Greg’s reputation beyond the districts that know him personally, and public expectation begins outrunning the man who owns it.
