# 3L Temporal Map Slice B

Status: EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE

Source: approved `3L_300_Record_Temporal_Map.md` first pathfinding pass. Content below is preserved as the original 081-170 search surface. Every entry is a disposable hypothesis.

### 081 — THE FISHERMAN

A day of ordinary incompetence outside his profession gives Greg the rare pleasure of being bad at something with no civilizational consequence.

### 082 — THE FRIEND

Bren and Greg finally choose each other as friends in Life Two without Greg manufacturing the conditions, paying off the restraint of their first encounter.

### 083 — THE WITNESS AGAIN

Bren tells Greg a story from his own altered path, proving that the man Greg remembered was only one possible Bren among many.

### 084 — THE BROTHER

A crisis brings Bren into Greg’s household long enough that old emotional language returns with new justification rather than borrowed memory.

### 085 — THE HOSTAGE

A labor dispute traps Greg between workers he respects and officials who can shut down his contracts, making neutrality impossible.

### 086 — THE UNION MAN

Greg sides with maintenance crews on a safety provision and discovers that institutional enemies remember public embarrassment longer than technical correctness.

### 087 — THE BLACKLISTED

A procurement office quietly freezes Greg out of major work, forcing his network to survive without the contracts that made it respectable.

### 088 — THE SMALL MAN

Reduced to local jobs again, Greg rediscovers the scale of work where consequences have names instead of statistics.

### 089 — THE RECOVERER

His workshop survives the blacklist by serving neglected districts, accidentally creating a business model less dependent on central favor.

### 090 — THE PUBLIC MAN

A difficult rescue pushes Greg’s reputation beyond the districts that know him personally, and public expectation begins outrunning the man who owns it.

### 091 — THE REPUTATION

Stories about Greg spread faster than corrections, teaching him that a useful public identity is partly built by strangers.

### 092 — THE IMPOSTOR

Greg privately wonders whether admiration earned in Life Two is fraudulent because he still compares every achievement with the S-class man nobody else remembers.

### 093 — THE BRAND

His company profits from Greg’s reputation despite his discomfort, showing how identity becomes commercial infrastructure once enough people believe in it.

### 094 — THE DONOR

Greg and Nessa use some of the money to fund public maintenance training, binding their private success to a civic project that may outlive them.

### 095 — THE TRUSTEE

Greg joins the board overseeing that program and discovers that charitable institutions develop politics as quickly as profitable ones.

### 096 — THE OLD HAND

Workers young enough to have learned from Greg’s standards begin treating him as established authority, although he still measures himself against S-class memory.

### 097 — THE MEASURED MAN

A formal rank evaluation shows Greg improving more slowly than First Life, turning an old private fear into an official number.

### 098 — THE EXCUSE-MAKER

Greg blames work, marriage, and responsibility for his stalled climb before realizing he repeatedly chose those things over the old route.

### 099 — THE CHOOSER

For the first time, Greg consciously refuses an S-class-adjacent opportunity because taking it would require abandoning the life he actually built.

### 100 — THE CONTENTED

Nothing dramatic happens, and Greg realizes with some alarm that he is happy.

### 101 — THE EXPERT

Greg is called into a multi-region Line review because field experience now matters more than his rank.

### 102 — THE MINORITY

He is the only reviewer arguing that scattered small anomalies form one systemic pattern, and the committee records his concern without acting on it.

### 103 — THE ALARMIST

A leaked summary makes Greg look hysterical, damaging the careful institutional credibility he spent years building.

### 104 — THE HUSBAND AT DINNER

Nessa refuses to let a kingdom-sized worry consume every meal, forcing Greg to keep living while the evidence remains incomplete.

### 105 — THE COLLECTOR

Greg begins quietly collecting failure reports from neglected stations and builds the first unofficial map of correlated Line decay.

### 106 — THE STATISTICIAN

He learns enough data work to discover that repair frequency is rising in ways no single district budget reveals.

### 107 — THE HERETIC

Greg proposes that the Line’s oldest foundations may be degrading rather than merely being badly maintained, offending both engineers and traditionalists.

### 108 — THE PROPHET

One predicted failure occurs almost exactly where Greg expected, making the people who mocked him suddenly dangerous in a different way.

### 109 — THE CELEBRITY

Officials parade Greg as evidence that the system is responsive while quietly narrowing what he is allowed to say publicly.

### 110 — THE MOUTHPIECE

Greg gives one sanctioned speech, hears his warning turned into reassurance, and refuses the next invitation.

### 111 — THE DISSENTER

He publishes his own field findings through professional channels and becomes inconvenient to people who preferred his reputation without his conclusions.

### 112 — THE PARTNER AGAIN

Nessa helps Greg organize the archive and becomes indispensable to the work without becoming his assistant or surrendering her own life.

### 113 — THE CURATOR

Together they turn decades of local records into a durable evidence library that can survive Greg’s temper and institutional turnover.

### 114 — THE TEACHER OF TEACHERS

Greg trains regional instructors so practical maintenance knowledge can spread without requiring his physical presence.

### 115 — THE ELDER

A former trainee returns as a competent leader with methods Greg did not teach, showing him what healthy influence looks like when it stops resembling obedience.

### 116 — THE GUEST OF HONOR

A public ceremony celebrating East Four’s safety record makes Greg uneasy because he knows how much of that record depends on luck.

### 117 — THE FOOL

A station he personally certified suffers a major failure from a mechanism he never considered.

### 118 — THE INVESTIGATOR

Greg refuses to let the incident become a scapegoat hunt and traces the failure through procurement, maintenance intervals, design assumptions, and political delay.

### 119 — THE ACCUSER

The evidence points toward a central continuity office that knowingly shifted risk outward to protect high-value districts.

### 120 — THE ENEMY

By naming the office publicly, Greg turns a technical disagreement into a conflict with people who control permits, budgets, and emergency routing.

## B → C SEAM: THE LOSS MACHINE

The Line’s political economy creates the conditions for loss; Nessa’s death is a provisional high-pressure seam, not canon.

### 121 — THE PREPARER

Expecting retaliation, Greg secures records, money, licenses, and alternate supply routes before the bureaucracy can squeeze East Four.

### 122 — THE PARANOID

Several harmless coincidences look like attacks because Greg now has real enemies, and Nessa becomes the person separating evidence from fear.

### 123 — THE TARGET

A contract cancellation, audit, and licensing challenge arrive together, confirming that at least some of Greg’s paranoia was simply pattern recognition.

### 124 — THE DEFENDER

Greg fights the campaign through hearings and public records instead of violence, forcing his opponents to expose how the continuity system allocates acceptable loss.

### 125 — THE LOSER

He wins the argument and loses the ruling, because the Authority decides the central office acted within emergency discretion.

### 126 — THE WORKER AT HOME

Greg retreats into ordinary station work long enough to remember that the Line still needs maintaining even when justice fails.

### 127 — THE WARNING

Halden sends Greg evidence of an impending regional load transfer that would put East Four and several poorer districts at extraordinary risk.

### 128 — THE EVACUATOR

Greg begins moving people before permission arrives, gambling his reputation and finances on a forecast that officials insist is premature.

### 129 — THE HOLDFAST

Nessa remains at East Four to coordinate records, shelter routing, and worker families while Greg holds the outer stations together.

### 130 — THE WIDOWER

The load transfer becomes a cascading failure, Nessa dies during the evacuation she helped make possible, and Greg’s life divides into before and after.

### 131 — THE MOURNER

Greg survives the public funeral while privately reducing every condolence to the fact that none of them can tell him why she had to die.

### 132 — THE EMPTY MAN

The house, workshop, and station continue functioning without Nessa, teaching Greg that systems can survive losses people cannot.

### 133 — THE BLAMER

Greg chooses names for the disaster before he has enough evidence because anger needs human targets faster than engineering does.

### 134 — THE PLAINTIFF

He begins with law, filing claims against the continuity office and the contractors who executed the transfer.

### 135 — THE CLIENT

Greg discovers how little ownership a grieving person has once lawyers convert a life into admissible categories.

### 136 — THE ARCHIVIST OF THE DEAD

Nessa’s records reveal that she documented decisions Greg never knew she was tracking, giving the investigation a second voice after her death.

### 137 — THE LEAKER

Greg releases internal risk memos when official discovery stalls, sacrificing legal advantage for public pressure.

### 138 — THE TRAITOR

Former allies accuse him of weakening confidence in the Line during a fragile period, and some of them are not entirely wrong.

### 139 — THE BLACKMAILER

Greg uses compromising procurement evidence to force testimony from an official who would never cooperate out of conscience.

### 140 — THE INTERROGATOR

He becomes frighteningly good at asking people which part of a disaster they had permission not to know.

### 141 — THE HUNTER

The investigation shifts from public responsibility to the small group who falsified thresholds so the load transfer would be politically defensible.

### 142 — THE TRESPASSER

Greg breaks into a restricted relay site to obtain raw routing logs before they can be overwritten.

### 143 — THE THIEF

He steals records that prove manipulation but also expose unrelated secrets belonging to people who did not kill Nessa.

### 144 — THE EDITOR

Greg chooses what not to publish, discovering that revenge still requires boundaries if he wants to remain someone Nessa would recognize.

### 145 — THE CONSPIRATOR

Bren, Halden, and a handful of workers begin helping Greg outside official channels for different reasons that do not amount to shared morality.

### 146 — THE CELL LEADER

Greg coordinates compartmentalized tasks and realizes how easily a maintenance network can become an insurgent network when trust replaces paperwork.

### 147 — THE SABOTEUR

He disables a lucrative Line bypass to force emergency scrutiny of the continuity office, knowingly causing economic damage without causing deaths.

### 148 — THE EXTORTIONIST

Greg threatens a senior official with financial ruin and public disgrace unless the underlying routing archive is opened.

### 149 — THE VINDICATED

The archive proves East Four was knowingly treated as sacrificial capacity, giving Greg the truth he wanted and none of the relief he expected.

### 150 — THE AVENGER

Greg stops asking whether a crime occurred and starts deciding what each responsible person should lose.

## C: THE REVENGE LIFE

Roughly 40s. Accountability mutates into vengeance, vengeance uncovers deeper Line instability, and Greg’s body pays a permanent cost.

### 151 — THE ACCOUNTANT OF HARM

He builds a private ledger of responsibility that distinguishes bad judgment, cowardice, profit, concealment, and deliberate sacrifice.

### 152 — THE MERCIFUL

Greg spares a junior engineer whose error was real but whose authority was not, angering allies who wanted a cleaner list of enemies.

### 153 — THE PUNISHER

He destroys a contractor’s business by exposing fraud and redirecting every client, supplier, and insurer that kept it alive.

### 154 — THE RUINER

A bureaucrat who buried warnings loses office, pension, and reputation after Greg engineers a sequence of lawful disclosures.

### 155 — THE KILLER

One architect of the coverup responds with violence, and Greg kills him in a fight that feels smaller and uglier than the years of hatred preceding it.

### 156 — THE JUSTIFIER

Greg tells himself the death was self-defense and hears how quickly a true statement can become an excuse.

### 157 — THE WITNESS FOR THE DEFENSE

Bren forces Greg to describe the killing from the other man’s perspective, preserving friendship by refusing to become an audience.

### 158 — THE OBSESSED

Greg keeps going after the central director even after the public case has already shattered the office.

### 159 — THE ABANDONED

Several allies leave because they wanted accountability, not a private war, and Greg interprets their departure as weakness until much later.

### 160 — THE SOLOIST

Working alone makes Greg faster, harsher, and substantially easier to manipulate.

### 161 — THE BAIT

The director feeds Greg a false trail into an obsolete Line complex where legal jurisdiction is unclear and rescue is difficult.

### 162 — THE INTRUDER

Greg enters anyway because revenge has finally become more important to him than the standards he once taught.

### 163 — THE DESCENDER

The old complex reveals machinery deeper than the modern Line and evidence that human routing changes have been stressing structures nobody knew existed.

### 164 — THE DISCOVERER

Greg finds proof that the East Four decision was monstrous but also only one local expression of a much older systemic instability.

### 165 — THE REFUSER

He refuses to let the larger problem absolve the people who knowingly sacrificed Nessa’s district.

### 166 — THE PURSUER

Greg follows the director through failing tunnels where every step requires the infrastructure expertise his revenge tried to reduce to weaponry.

### 167 — THE EXECUTIONER

He finally has the director helpless and chooses a punishment whose exact form remains a deliberate rehearsal branch rather than settled canon.

### 168 — THE COLLAPSING MAN

The complex begins to fail during the confrontation, converting revenge back into the practical problem of getting living people out.

### 169 — THE RESCUER AGAIN

Greg saves workers aligned with his enemy because the body remembers duty even while the mind is still arguing about vengeance.

### 170 — THE CRUSHED

A structural collapse destroys Greg’s leg in a way that has nothing to do with how Peg-Leg Greg lost it in First Life.
