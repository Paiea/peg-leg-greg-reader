# 3L Temporal Map Slice C

Status: EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE

Source: approved `3L_300_Record_Temporal_Map.md` first pathfinding pass. Content below is preserved as the original 161-250 search surface. Every entry is a disposable hypothesis.

### 161 — THE BAIT

The director feeds Greg a false trail into an obsolete Line complex where legal jurisdiction is unclear and rescue is difficult.

### 162 — THE INTRUDER

Greg enters anyway because revenge has finally become more important to him than the standards he once taught.

### 163 — THE DESCENDER

The old complex reveals machinery deeper than the modern Line and evidence that human routing changes have been stressing structures nobody knew existed.

### 164 — THE DISCOVERER

Greg finds proof that the East Four decision was monstrous but also only one local expression of a much older systemic instability.

### 165 — THE REFUSER

He refuses to let the larger problem absolve the people who knowingly sacrificed Nessa’s district.

### 166 — THE PURSUER

Greg follows the director through failing tunnels where every step requires the infrastructure expertise his revenge tried to reduce to weaponry.

### 167 — THE EXECUTIONER

He finally has the director helpless and chooses a punishment whose exact form remains a deliberate rehearsal branch rather than settled canon.

### 168 — THE COLLAPSING MAN

The complex begins to fail during the confrontation, converting revenge back into the practical problem of getting living people out.

### 169 — THE RESCUER AGAIN

Greg saves workers aligned with his enemy because the body remembers duty even while the mind is still arguing about vengeance.

### 170 — THE CRUSHED

A structural collapse destroys Greg’s leg in a way that has nothing to do with how Peg-Leg Greg lost it in First Life.

### 171 — THE AMPUTEE

Greg wakes after surgery to discover that the legendary name has finally caught up with a completely different injury.

### 172 — THE PATIENT AGAIN

Recovery forces Greg to depend on people he trained, paid, offended, loved, and frightened over decades.

### 173 — THE ANGRY MAN

He directs grief at the missing leg because flesh is easier to hate than the fact that revenge changed nothing about Nessa being gone.

### 174 — THE MAKER

Greg becomes involved in designing his own prosthetic because accepting a body part made by strangers feels intolerable.

### 175 — THE BEGINNER

Walking badly with expert knowledge returns Greg to the humiliating gap between knowing a movement and possessing the body to perform it.

### 176 — THE CRIPPLE

A formal rank reassessment drops Greg below the status he spent decades earning, making diminished capability public rather than private.

### 177 — THE RETIREE

Greg attempts to withdraw from field work and discovers that retirement is a social role with expectations he never bothered to imagine.

### 178 — THE MAN AT HOME

Days with no emergency expose how completely Nessa’s absence and his old work had structured his sense of time.

### 179 — THE BEREAVED HUSBAND

Greg finally allows grief to exist without converting it into investigation, policy, training, or revenge.

### 180 — THE SURVIVOR AGAIN

He chooses to keep living without claiming that survival itself is a victory.

## C → D SEAM: THE REBUILDING LIFE

Greg rebuilds without undoing loss, converting institutions, teaching, disability, friendship, and failed experiments into durable capacity.

### 181 — THE REBUILDER

Greg returns to the damaged workshop with a smaller body, a worse rank, and the first desire to make something that is not aimed at an enemy.

### 182 — THE EMPLOYER AGAIN

He reorganizes the company around people who no longer need him to be its strongest worker.

### 183 — THE DESIGNER

Greg helps redesign field tools for injured and older crews, turning bodily limitation into practical knowledge instead of private shame.

### 184 — THE ACCESSIBLE MAN

Changes made for Greg improve work for people with injuries he once treated as individual problems.

### 185 — THE TEACHER AGAIN

He returns to training and finds that diminished physical ability makes his explanations better because demonstration can no longer replace instruction.

### 186 — THE HISTORIAN

Greg begins recording why standards changed, preserving causal memory rather than only the final rule.

### 187 — THE CORRECTED MAN

A young instructor proves one of Greg’s cherished explanations wrong, and he chooses to update the archive rather than defend authorship.

### 188 — THE BUILDER OF PEOPLE

Greg starts judging success by who can replace him rather than by which problem still requires his presence.

### 189 — THE FRIEND AGAIN

Bren and Greg repair the damage revenge did to their friendship through months of ordinary contact rather than one dramatic reconciliation.

### 190 — THE BROTHER AGAIN

Bren becomes the person allowed to remember Nessa with Greg without turning her into either saint or wound.

### 191 — THE VISITOR TO A GRAVE

Greg speaks to Nessa’s grave about a trivial workshop problem and realizes mourning has finally rejoined ordinary life.

### 192 — THE TRUSTEE AGAIN

He returns to the training foundation and redirects it toward neglected districts whose maintenance failures rarely become national news.

### 193 — THE PATRON

Greg funds small experiments in Line repair that central institutions consider too local to matter.

### 194 — THE FAILURE SPONSOR

Several experiments fail cheaply, teaching Greg to value knowledge produced by unsuccessful work.

### 195 — THE NETWORKER

He connects independent stations into a voluntary failure-reporting network outside formal Authority channels.

### 196 — THE FIELD LISTENER

Greg spends more time collecting what operators notice than telling them what he thinks they should notice.

### 197 — THE PATTERN-FINDER

Reports from distant regions reveal the same deep anomalies Greg saw beneath the revenge complex.

### 198 — THE ALARM BELL

Greg warns Halden that the old foundation itself may be entering a new failure regime.

### 199 — THE BELIEVER

Halden finally accepts the systemic hypothesis, giving Greg an institutional ally powerful enough to test it.

### 200 — THE MINORITY OF TWO

Their joint warning reaches central leadership and is rejected because the predicted failure horizon is politically longer than anyone’s term.

### 201 — THE PILOT

Greg runs a small regional stabilization experiment designed to reduce load on the oldest Line structures.

### 202 — THE IMPROVISER

The pilot works for reasons the theory did not predict, reminding Greg that useful interventions can precede understanding.

### 203 — THE SCIENTIST BY ACCIDENT

Repeated field tests turn Greg’s workshop network into an empirical research program nobody formally authorized.

### 204 — THE REPLICATOR

Other districts reproduce the stabilization effect, making it harder for central authorities to dismiss the evidence as local luck.

### 205 — THE HERETIC AGAIN

Greg argues that the Line may not be a single system at all but layers of human use built over something older.

### 206 — THE EXCAVATOR

A sanctioned dig exposes a prehuman junction whose materials and topology do not match any known Line era.

### 207 — THE TREASURELESS MAN

The ancient site contains no convenient artifact, only evidence that civilization has been depending on machinery it cannot repair.

### 208 — THE HUMBLE EXPERT

Greg’s decades of practical knowledge become most valuable at the moment he can finally say how little humans understand.

### 209 — THE INTERPRETER

He translates ancient-system observations into operational rules that can keep modern stations alive without pretending the theory is complete.

### 210 — THE CONSERVATOR

Greg begins advocating load reduction and managed retreat from some Line-dependent settlements rather than endless repair.

### 211 — THE MONSTER

Communities threatened with abandonment treat Greg as the man choosing which towns deserve a future.

### 212 — THE DEFENDER OF RETREAT

He argues publicly that preserving every settlement may destroy the network supporting all of them.

### 213 — THE HYPOCRITE

A district he personally loves appears on the retreat list, and Greg discovers how different sacrifice sounds when the place is his.

### 214 — THE FAVOR TRADER

He spends decades of reputation trying to save that district and hates himself for becoming exactly the political exception he condemned.

### 215 — THE LOSER AGAIN

The district closes anyway, proving that maturity does not make necessary loss feel fair.

### 216 — THE EVACUATION PLANNER

Greg turns painful retreat into a discipline of moving people, businesses, records, and social networks rather than merely drawing lines on maps.

### 217 — THE RESETTLER

He helps displaced families build new routines elsewhere and sees that civilization survives through ordinary continuity more than monumental infrastructure.

### 218 — THE OLD NEIGHBOR

Former East Four residents recreate familiar customs in a new district, making Greg understand how culture travels when buildings do not.

### 219 — THE KEEPER OF NAMES

Greg records abandoned stations and the people attached to them because technical maps erase loss too efficiently.

### 220 — THE MAN WHO STAYS

Despite pressure to move closer to the capital, Greg remains near the western network where failure is becoming hardest to ignore.

## D: THE COLLAPSING KINGDOM

Roughly 50s. Local knowledge scales into kingdom-level triage while First-Life impossible knowledge becomes operationally relevant again.

### 221 — THE SENTINEL

Greg’s reporting network begins detecting synchronized anomalies across hundreds of miles.

### 222 — THE FORECASTER

He builds a rough model that predicts cascading regional failure within years rather than generations.

### 223 — THE UNWELCOME EXPERT

Central leadership wants Greg’s data but not his conclusion that the kingdom’s settlement pattern may be unsustainable.

### 224 — THE COMMISSIONER

A crisis commission gives Greg official authority late enough that every available choice is already expensive.

### 225 — THE BUREAUCRAT

Greg spends months learning how emergency authority actually moves food, labor, transport, money, and fear.

### 226 — THE RATIONER

Shortages force him to allocate scarce Line parts between districts whose failures will kill different kinds of people at different speeds.

### 227 — THE COWARD

Greg chooses the option that saves more people but cannot bring himself to visit the town it condemns.

### 228 — THE VISITOR OF THE CONDEMNED

He goes anyway after Bren calls him a coward, and the residents force him to hear the human version of his arithmetic.

### 229 — THE ACCOUNTABLE MAN

Greg starts signing major allocation decisions personally so responsibility cannot dissolve into committee minutes.

### 230 — THE HATED MAN

Public anger grows around his name because visible responsibility attracts blame more reliably than invisible systems.

### 231 — THE OLD RANKER

A younger generation of stronger rankers treats Greg as strategically useful but physically obsolete.

### 232 — THE COACH

Greg learns to direct high-level fighters through Line emergencies he can no longer survive personally.

### 233 — THE LIABILITY

A field mission goes wrong partly because the team protects Greg’s damaged mobility, forcing him to confront the operational cost of insisting on presence.

### 234 — THE REMOTE MAN

He begins leading some emergencies from maps and reports, accepting that distance can be responsibility rather than cowardice.

### 235 — THE EYE

Greg becomes known for seeing system interactions others miss, replacing some of the identity once supplied by physical rank.

### 236 — THE STUDENT OF NHAL

First-Life memories of Nhal become operationally relevant as ancient Line signatures begin matching things Greg encountered only at S-class.

### 237 — THE UNRELIABLE WITNESS

Greg’s forty-year-old memories of Nhal disagree in small ways with present evidence, forcing him to treat his own experience as data rather than scripture.

### 238 — THE NAME-BEARER

One remembered name opens access to an archive modern scholars thought meaningless, proving his old life still contains rare leverage.

### 239 — THE FALSE KEY

Another remembered phrase fails completely, preventing the story from turning First Life into a cheat code at the moment stakes are highest.

### 240 — THE SEEKER

Greg begins systematically identifying which First-Life encounters might contain knowledge relevant to the Line’s foundations.

### 241 — THE S-CLASS GHOST

Reconstructing those encounters reminds Greg how different the first man was in capability, priorities, friendships, and arrogance.

### 242 — THE ENVIOUS MAN

For the first time in years, Greg envies his former self’s strength more than he values the life that replaced it.

### 243 — THE ASHAMED MAN

That envy feels like betrayal of Nessa and the decades built after abandoning the old route.

### 244 — THE RECONCILER

Greg accepts that valuing Life Two does not require pretending he never lost anything by failing to become S-class again.

### 245 — THE DOUBLE WITNESS

He starts recording First-Life memories beside Life-Two evidence, preserving disagreement rather than forcing one history to explain the other.

### 246 — THE COMPARER

Patterns visible only across both lives reveal that several ancient failures happened in different places but under similar load conditions.

### 247 — THE THEORIST

Greg proposes that the Line is adapting, not merely decaying, and that human maintenance may be fighting a system trying to change state.

### 248 — THE WRONG MAN

Field evidence disproves the adaptation theory but leaves behind one useful prediction about deep pressure migration.

### 249 — THE REVISER

Greg keeps the failed theory in the archive with its surviving evidence, institutionalizing the lesson that wrong paths can still contain tools.

### 250 — THE OLD MAN

A quiet birthday makes fifty-nine feel close enough to touch, and Greg notices that the second life is now almost as long as the first one he once treated as definitive.
