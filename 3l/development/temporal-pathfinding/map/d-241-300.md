### 242 — THE ENVIOUS MAN

### 243 — THE ASHAMED MAN

### 244 — THE RECONCILER

### 245 — THE DOUBLE WITNESS

### 246 — THE COMPARER

Patterns visible only across both lives reveal that several ancient failures happened in different places but under similar load conditions.

### 247 — THE THEORIST

Greg proposes that the Line is adapting, not merely decaying, and that human maintenance may be fighting a system trying to change state.

### 248 — THE WRONG MAN

Field evidence disproves the adaptation theory but leaves behind one useful prediction about deep pressure migration.

### 249 — THE REVISER

Greg keeps the failed theory in the archive with its surviving evidence, institutionalizing the lesson that wrong paths can still contain tools.

### 250 — THE OLD MAN

A quiet birthday makes fifty-nine feel close enough to touch, and Greg notices that the second life is now almost as long as the first one he once treated as definitive.

### 251 — THE PREDECESSOR

Greg formally transfers several responsibilities to younger leaders before crisis can force an uglier succession.

### 252 — THE FOUNDER

Institutions he created begin operating without asking his permission, giving him the unnerving experience of becoming historical while still alive.

### 253 — THE OBSTRUCTION

Greg tries to intervene in a decision and is correctly overruled by people using principles he taught them.

### 254 — THE RETIRED FOUNDER

He learns to let an organization remain his legacy without remaining his possession.

### 255 — THE CARETAKER

Greg reduces his formal load and spends more time on one western station whose failures have become personally legible over decades.

### 256 — THE LAST RESORT

A sequence of station deaths forces central leadership to adopt emergency measures Greg proposed years earlier.

### 257 — THE MAN WHO WAS RIGHT

Vindication arrives too late to feel satisfying because the evidence is now bodies, abandoned towns, and shrinking maps.

### 258 — THE MOBILIZER

Greg helps convert the entire western maintenance economy into emergency stabilization work.

### 259 — THE QUARTERMASTER

The crisis becomes a problem of parts, food, transport, sleep, fuel, and labor before it becomes a heroic battlefield.

### 260 — THE OLD FOREMAN

Greg’s greatest leverage is now getting thousands of ordinary workers pointed at the right problems without wasting them.

## D → FRAME SEAM: THE PETITION ROAD

Late 50s. The Line crisis becomes civilizational, human options narrow, and Greg deliberately chooses Ithar.

### 261 — THE EVACUATOR AGAIN

Multiple towns must be cleared at once, turning the techniques learned from earlier retreat into kingdom-scale survival infrastructure.

### 262 — THE TRIAGE MAN

Greg accepts that some stations will be abandoned without repair so crews can save nodes that still anchor entire regions.

### 263 — THE GRAVEDIGGER

A failed evacuation leaves Greg helping recover bodies because rank and authority do not exempt him from the physical reality of his decisions.

### 264 — THE UNBROKEN MAN

He returns to work after the recovery without pretending resilience means the dead mattered less.

### 265 — THE CORRESPONDENT AGAIN

Halden sends evidence that the western failures are beginning to propagate toward the central network.

### 266 — THE ALARMIST AGAIN

Greg’s warning is finally believed, and the new problem is that belief does not create enough time.

### 267 — THE KINGDOM’S CONSULTANT

Political leaders ask Greg for a survival plan and receive a list of compromises no ruler wants attached to a reign.

### 268 — THE NEGOTIATOR AGAIN

Greg brokers emergency cooperation among rival regions that have spent decades treating Line capacity as competitive advantage.

### 269 — THE COMMANDER

For one season Greg effectively directs a civilian infrastructure war fought with crews, schedules, escorts, engineers, and evacuation trains.

### 270 — THE EXHAUSTED MAN

The system stabilizes just enough for everyone to realize the measures are slowing collapse rather than stopping it.

### 271 — THE SEEKER OF IMPOSSIBLE THINGS

Greg opens the sealed portion of his First-Life memory archive and begins looking for entities or places outside normal human capability.

### 272 — THE REMEMBERER

Nhal’s old warning about Ithar returns with enough detail to suggest that a dragon may understand the foundations humans only service.

### 273 — THE DOUBTER

Greg spends weeks trying to find any human alternative because asking a dragon for help is a plan only after every sane plan has failed.

### 274 — THE PETITION WRITER

He reduces the kingdom crisis to facts an ancient being might find interesting rather than arguments a human ruler would find moral.

### 275 — THE PRICE-SETTER

Greg assembles the rare information, favors, relic knowledge, and First-Life secrets he might trade for Ithar’s attention.

### 276 — THE HUSBAND TO THE DEAD

At Nessa’s grave, Greg admits that part of him has begun wondering whether another reset could produce a life in which she survives.

### 277 — THE LIAR TO HIMSELF

He insists that saving the kingdom is the whole reason he is considering Ithar while carefully avoiding what he would ask if a third beginning were possible.

### 278 — THE HEIR

Greg leaves legal and institutional instructions for the people who will inherit his work if he does not return.

### 279 — THE FRIEND AT THE DOOR

Bren tries to come with him and is refused because Greg will not turn an old friendship into collateral for a dragon bargain.

### 280 — THE MAN WHO LEAVES

Greg walks away from the life he spent forty years learning to stay inside, carrying only what the mountain requires.

### 281 — THE TRAVELER AGAIN

The route toward Ithar crosses regions transformed by choices Greg made decades earlier, turning the journey into a physical audit of Life Two.

### 282 — THE BENEFICIARY

One town survives because of a standard Greg helped create, forcing him to acknowledge that his diminished life produced consequences S-class glory never would have.

### 283 — THE FAILURE’S WITNESS

Another settlement lies empty despite every reform, preventing the journey from becoming a victory lap.

### 284 — THE OLD CUSTOMER

A former tenant or worker recognizes Greg and offers ordinary hospitality that matters more to him than public reverence.

### 285 — THE LEGEND

Young rankers tell distorted stories about Peg-Leg Greg while he sits nearby and realizes public memory has already begun inventing a cleaner man.

### 286 — THE CORRECTOR

Greg corrects one trivial detail and leaves the larger myth untouched because no explanation could fit forty years into a tavern story.

### 287 — THE CLIMBER

The mountain route demands the last useful edge of Greg’s damaged body and the practical tricks accumulated across both lives.

### 288 — THE TRESPASSER AGAIN

Greg crosses a boundary Nhal once described and confirms that First-Life knowledge can still open paths current humanity does not possess.

### 289 — THE SURVIVOR OF THE MOUNTAIN

He reaches the final approach not because he is still S-class but because fifty-nine-year-old Greg knows how to conserve effort, read danger, and refuse unnecessary fights.

### 290 — THE MAN AT THE MOUTH

Greg stands outside Ithar’s cave with the kingdom behind him, Nessa dead, two lives in memory, and a petition that is not the only thing he wants.

## FULL FRAME: THE THIRD LEG

After hearing Life Two, Ithar turns Greg’s own logic back on him and forces the final distinction between saving, undoing, and beginning again.

### 291 — THE LISTENER

Back in the frame after the full account, Ithar summarizes patterns Greg never intended to confess and asks which life Greg believes was the failure.

### 292 — THE DEFENDER OF LIFE TWO

Greg argues that the second life mattered precisely because it did not reproduce the first, even while admitting how often he still measures it by S-class loss.

### 293 — THE ACCUSED

Ithar points out the hypocrisy of condemning dragons for treating civilizations as replaceable while Greg privately treats his own lives as drafts.

### 294 — THE ANGRY PETITIONER

Greg rejects the comparison, then loses ground as Ithar calmly names the people Greg would risk erasing for another chance.

### 295 — THE HONEST MAN

Greg finally admits that saving the kingdom, undoing Nessa’s death, and wanting another beginning have become tangled motives he cannot separate cleanly.

### 296 — THE THIRD-LIFE MAN

For the first time Greg says aloud that he has imagined waking at nineteen again and doing Life Two differently.

### 297 — THE DRAGON’S PRICE

Ithar explains that any path forward will carry a cost or uncertainty that prevents a third life from becoming a clean optimization problem.

### 298 — THE CHOOSER AGAIN

Greg must decide what he is actually asking Ithar to preserve: the kingdom, his memories, the people shaped by Life Two, himself, or the possibility of beginning again.

### 299 — THE THIRD LEG

Greg recognizes that a third beginning cannot be a correction of the first two without making those lives disposable, reframing what the title means before the final choice.

### 300 — THE BEGINNING

The book closes on Greg choosing a future whose exact mechanism remains subordinate to the central truth that he is no longer trying to recreate a previous life.

## First backward-obligation pass

**D → C:** If Greg ends at Ithar because human civilization cannot repair the Line, revenge must uncover evidence that the infrastructure problem is deeper than corruption.

**C → B:** If revenge grows from Nessa’s loss, the loss must emerge from institutions, routing choices, incentives, records, and people established long before the disaster.

**B → A:** If Nessa, Bren, Halden, the workshop, trainees, and East Four matter later, the early life must give them independent trajectories rather than orbiting Greg as plot furniture.

**A → D:** If Greg’s final strength is networks and judgment rather than S-class force, the earliest chapters should repeatedly reward ordinary competence, recordkeeping, teaching, trust, and staying.

## First forward-consequence pass

**A → B:** Greg staying creates marriage, property, business, trainees, standards, political exposure, and a legitimate reason to care about local systems.

**B → C:** Institutional influence exposes Greg to the continuity machinery that can later produce both preventable loss and a believable target for revenge.

**C → D:** Revenge costs Greg allies and a leg but uncovers the Line’s deeper instability; rebuilding converts the wreckage into a distributed network capable of seeing the coming collapse.

**D → FRAME:** The kingdom crisis then grows out of forty years of accumulated setting evidence, while Ithar becomes the last credible source of understanding rather than a late fantasy solution.
