## Whole-life checks

### Causal continuity

For every major transition ask what prior pressure, choice, resource, relationship, institution, or accident made it plausible.

Reject transitions that exist only because a later beat needs them.

### Chronology and years burned

Track actual calendar time in broad eras.

Do not make forty years feel like adjacent quests.

Preserve:

- quiet years
- multi-year jobs
- aging parents and peers
- accumulated home routines
- changes in neighborhood and city
- long friendships
- periods when Greg is not pursuing maximum power
- months or years compressed because their important change is cumulative rather than scene-sized

Do not infer precise ages where canon has not earned them.

### Money, property, work, and institutions

Track whether Greg can actually afford, own, employ, borrow, insure, license, litigate, travel, or influence what a proposed path says he does.

A new role must emerge from earlier contact and competence.

### Body and disability progression

Track training, age, injuries, adaptation, rank, mobility, work capacity, and recovery.

Present-frame body promises must acquire plausible provenance, but no injury survives merely because the Promise Ledger needs a box checked.

### Rank and skill progression

Greg may become unusually capable without repeating First-Life S-class.

Separate:

- technical knowledge
- field judgment
- institutional leverage
- public reputation
- rank classification
- raw physical capability

Do not let one silently stand in for all others.

### Public identity

The eventual named identity **Peg-Leg Greg**, King's commendation, named contracts, free-drink cities, and low-B present status must feel accumulated rather than announced.

### Independent character lives

For Nessa, Bren, Dera, Halden, family members, trainees, workers, rivals, officials, and later recurring people ask:

- What do they want when Greg is not present?
- What changes in their life while Greg is busy elsewhere?
- What do they refuse him?
- What do they misunderstand?
- What do they become that Greg did not cause?

Kill paths where everyone becomes a function of Greg's arc.

### Love and family accumulation

Ordinary intimacy must be worthwhile on its own.

Do not preserve marriage, children, household shape, or Nessa's eventual fate merely because the first map used them.

If loss eventually comes through love/family, the preceding life must still read as a life rather than setup for pain.

### Loss causality

The broad authority requires catastrophic loss, not a predetermined victim or scene.

Test at least:

- the strongest current loss path
- the strongest materially different alternate path

Prefer the path that grows from setting, relationships, and accumulated choices rather than from maximum shock.

### Revenge completion and aftermath

Greg eventually completes revenge.

Define a real completion boundary.

Then require substantial life afterward.

If revenge only exists to move Greg into the Line plot, replace it.

If the deeper Line problem erases human guilt, repair the path.

### Rebuilding

Rebuilding must create something beyond restored mood.

Look for durable changes in:

- people
- work
- teaching
- tools
- access
- archives
- standards
- networks
- institutions
- property
- logistics
- relationships
- bodily adaptation

### Line development

The kingdom-scale Line crisis must grow out of ordinary infrastructure evidence accumulated over decades.

Track how Greg's understanding changes from:

ordinary maintenance -> buried structure -> regional pattern -> deeper mechanism -> civilization-scale failure.

Do not reveal deep answers earlier merely because D needs them later.

### Life-One provenance

Eventually earn:

- real S-class scale
- Nhal
- Ithar's name and route
- ancient Line notation
- lower channels / ninth station / eastern sink
- Bren's First-Life bond and death when causally useful

Life One remains pressure/evidence rather than an equal parallel outline.

### Ithar private and public motive

Both must survive:

1. Greg reaches Ithar because the Line crisis genuinely threatens the civilization and people he spent decades living among.
2. Greg searched for Ithar before that crisis because another beginning, reset, undoing, or impossible future had already become privately relevant.

Neither motive may erase the other.

### Repeated Record grammar

Flag stretches where the book becomes structurally repetitive, for example:

- Greg gets job -> discovers hidden mechanism -> saves people
- institution is foolish -> Greg proves it wrong
- new title -> one illustrative scene -> next title
- Ithar comments at the end of every Record
- every mundane chapter secretly plants a future payoff

Preserve distinctive mundane texture. Compress repeated delivery of the same texture.

### Protagonist gravity

The world must function without Greg.

Kill or repair paths where every institution, relationship, crisis, discovery, or social movement waits for him.

### Generic escalation

Later does not automatically mean bigger.

A quiet adult consequence can matter more than another regional disaster.

### Deterministic foreshadowing

The engine knows possible later outcomes. Young Greg does not.

Remove authorial winks, suspiciously perfect planting, and early scenes whose only purpose is obvious late payoff.

### Ordinary life reduced to setup

Ask whether a Record would still be worth reading if its planned payoff changed.

If not, it is probably scaffolding rather than life.

### Ending obligations planted too mechanically

D may create requirements. Earlier windows must discover how, whether, and when those requirements are earned.

Do not preserve a convenient earlier event solely because it completes a late checklist.

## Confidence vocabulary

- **STRONG**: supported by multiple temporal perspectives or direct near-frontier causality; useful enough to guide upcoming prose.
- **SOFT**: plausible and useful but should remain easy to replace.
- **FORK**: two or more materially different futures remain alive.
- **REPLACE**: current map slot is not carrying its weight; rediscover rather than repair by force.
