# WINDOW A FIRST PASS

Status: FIRST-PASS EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE
Range: Records 011-090

## STATE IN
- age/time position: Record 010 occurs after East Four year seven and before the end of the established eleven-year tenure; Greg's exact Life-Two age remains deliberately unresolved.
- body/rank/skill: Greg is East Four's established Ranker with unusually deep remembered experience, current capability well below First-Life S-class, and permanent left-shoulder damage from the transfer disaster.
- work/money/property: Greg is a long-tenured station worker with tools, credibility, and a shared apartment with Nessa, but no locked ownership, employer, contractor, or wealth status.
- relationships: Nessa is Greg's central Life-Two relationship and already shares years of work and home with him; Bren is a beloved First-Life stranger in Life Two; Dera trusts Greg; Halden's independently continuing career is already propagating consequences; Greg's parents and younger sister remain alive and part of his calendar.
- geography/social range: Greg is locally established around East Four and nearby districts, with broader western advancement deliberately declined rather than unavailable.
- Line/world position: East Four sits over forgotten older Line infrastructure, North Vey's non-rupture proves history is replacing itself, and Greg has practical evidence that mundane institutions can matter more than remembered heroic explanations.
- First-Life pressure: Greg has stopped calling Life Two a return trip, while First-Life memory remains powerful as experience and comparison but increasingly unreliable as prediction.
- unresolved obligations: ordinary love/family life must genuinely accumulate; the eleven-year East Four tenure must finish; Greg must broaden without restarting an S-class optimization run; later public reputation, body damage, catastrophic loss, revenge, renewed climbing, Ithar search, and Line convergence remain distant obligations rather than nearby tasks.

## RECORD PATH

### 011 - THE CONFIDANT
Nessa presses on what Bren's arrival did to Greg, and Greg gives her the first usable account of his impossible history while still admitting that memory is not proof.

### 012 - THE LEDGER
A routine household money problem forces Greg and Nessa to make their shared life legible in wages, savings, debts, obligations, and who quietly covers what.

### 013 - THE BENEFICIARY
Medical and employment paperwork makes Greg and Nessa confront who is allowed to decide for whom in an emergency, deepening commitment without requiring the map to settle marriage yet.

### 014 - THE INSPECTOR
The buried East Four chamber earns Greg narrowly scoped inspection authority and shows him how much official safety rests on signatures made by people who only partly understand the system beneath them.

### 015 - THE SIGNATORY
Greg's first consequential signature exposes the difference between knowing what should happen and accepting legal responsibility for what other workers will actually do.

### 016 - THE SUBCONTRACTOR
Greg takes a small repair job outside ordinary station payroll and learns that independent work begins with scheduling, liability, payment terms, and risk long before it feels like a business.

### 017 - THE DELEGATOR
A job too large for one pair of hands makes Greg rely on another worker's judgment instead of simply doing the dangerous part himself.

### 018 - THE WITNESS
A preventable injury on another crew sends Greg to an Authority hearing where Dera's records, worker testimony, and contract language matter as much as his technical opinion.

### 019 - THE NEIGHBOR
A neighborhood dispute over Line access pulls Greg into consequences measured in kitchens, rents, shop hours, and elderly residents rather than station output.

### 020 - THE SON
An aging parent's reduced ability to work creates a recurring family obligation that competes with Greg's shifts without becoming a one-record tragedy.

### 021 - THE BROTHER
Greg's younger sister makes an adult decision about work or household that he would not have chosen, forcing him to practice loving family without treating foreknowledge or age as authority over them.

### 022 - THE PATIENT
A shoulder flare and enforced recovery make Greg live briefly as the person other people must accommodate, revealing how much pride still hides inside his practicality.

### 023 - THE SECOND CAREER
Nessa pursues a certification or role that matters to her independently of Greg, and their household begins adapting to two adult careers instead of orbiting his station schedule.

### 024 - THE WRONG MAN
Greg acts on a First-Life memory about someone's future behavior and discovers that divergence has changed the person enough to make his old certainty unfair.

### 025 - THE CONFESSOR
After being wrong for the right historical reason, Greg tells Nessa more about how his memories work and explicitly gives her permission to challenge conclusions that arrive wearing experience like certainty.

### 026 - THE OFF NIGHT
An ordinary evening of food, repairs, neighbors, jokes, and no emergency becomes valuable precisely because nothing needs Greg to be exceptional.

### 027 - THE LEASEHOLDER
Greg and Nessa choose a longer or larger rental commitment instead of treating home as temporary, making staying concrete without turning property ownership into a required milestone.

### 028 - THE BIDDER
Greg competes for a larger maintenance job and loses to an established firm, learning the practical gap between being trusted locally and being financially legible at regional scale.

### 029 - THE CREW LEAD
Dera gives Greg temporary responsibility for a mixed crew, making him answer for pace, fatigue, bad judgment, and other people's safety rather than only his own competence.

### 030 - THE MECHANIC
A stubborn station fault yields to patient maintenance and local knowledge rather than rank, reinforcing Greg's growing conviction that infrastructure failure is usually lived before it becomes mysterious.

### 031 - THE MEDIATOR
A dispute between Rankers and civilian crews makes Greg useful because he understands both danger and labor, not because he can dominate either side.

### 032 - THE SUCCESSOR
Dera accepts a district-level post and leaves East Four in other hands, proving that one of Greg's most durable authorities has a career and future that do not wait for his story.

### 033 - THE NEW BOSS
Greg must work under Dera's replacement, whose competent but different priorities force him to separate earned local influence from entitlement to control the station.

### 034 - THE RATEPAYER
A district fight over Line fees makes Greg see that every maintenance standard eventually becomes a household bill somebody resents.

### 035 - THE ADVOCATE
Greg publicly supports a repair levy that costs him goodwill and future side work because deferred maintenance would push the largest risks onto people with the least room to absorb them.

### 036 - THE EVACUATOR
A seasonal pressure emergency makes Greg hold an unglamorous station long enough for civilians to leave safely while stronger Rankers take the visible work elsewhere.

### 037 - THE TEACHER
Greg agrees to train a young worker and discovers that remembered mastery is useless unless he can turn it into instructions another body can safely perform.

### 038 - THE STUDENT
The trainee notices a local condition Greg missed because old experience made him assume too much, establishing that teaching will only work if authority can run in both directions.

### 039 - THE ACCOUNTABLE
A near miss caused partly by Greg's own instruction forces him to rewrite a procedure, document the error, and compensate the worker's lost time instead of hiding behind good intentions.

### 040 - THE ELEVENTH YEAR
East Four's eleventh year closes Greg's station tenure, and he chooses to remain rooted in the district while shifting from permanent station hand toward independent regional maintenance work.

### 041 - THE TRAVELER
Short regional jobs take Greg through towns that depend on the same Line in visibly different ways, widening his world without making travel itself a quest.

### 042 - THE CONSULTANT
Other crews begin paying for Greg's judgment on difficult failures, but the work remains intermittent enough that reputation feels less like promotion than another unstable income stream.

### 043 - THE CARTOGRAPHER
Field surveys show Greg that official Line maps track jurisdiction and ownership more faithfully than buried physical reality.

### 044 - THE BROKER
A shortage of specialized parts makes Greg valuable as the person who remembers which workshops, crews, and districts can actually help one another under pressure.

### 045 - THE RIVAL
A younger contractor repeatedly beats Greg on price and sometimes on method, forcing him to distinguish reckless shortcuts from improvements he simply did not invent.

### 046 - THE NEGOTIATOR
A labor stoppage threatens multiple stations, and Greg helps broker a settlement by treating maintenance workers' leverage as a consequence of dependence rather than disloyalty.

### 047 - THE APPRENTICE
An older pressure specialist accepts Greg only on junior terms, making him learn a technical discipline First-Life S-class Greg could once afford to ignore.

### 048 - THE SPECIALIST
Greg earns practical pressure-architecture competence that enlarges his usefulness through study and repetition rather than restored combat power.

### 049 - THE PASSERBY
A contract takes Greg through Bren's city, and Greg completes the work without manufacturing a meeting simply because old love makes proximity painful.

### 050 - THE COWORKER
An unrelated regional emergency assigns Bren and Greg to the same job again, where they discover genuine professional ease without either man being told what the relationship used to be.

### 051 - THE FOREWOMAN
Nessa accepts a more responsible role of her own, creating opportunities and pressures that originate in her competence rather than in Greg's expanding reputation.

### 052 - THE COMMUTER
Conflicting work sites and shifts force Greg and Nessa to rebuild household routines around distance, tiredness, meals, and missed time instead of assuming intimacy maintains itself.

### 053 - THE CAREGIVER
A parent's aging body turns errands, appointments, repairs, and money help into a long-running family responsibility that burns calendar time without needing a dramatic diagnosis.

### 054 - THE QUESTION
Greg and Nessa have to make a real decision about what kind of family they are trying to build, while the exact path through children, kin, or another household shape remains deliberately unresolved.

### 055 - THE LESSEE
Greg rents shared yard and shop space with other tradespeople, testing the burdens of fixed overhead and equipment access without jumping directly to sole ownership.

### 056 - THE PARTNER
A small repair partnership forms around complementary skills, giving Greg responsibility he cannot escape by simply working harder than everyone else.

### 057 - THE PAYMASTER
Regular employees make payroll, sick days, training time, insurance, and safe staffing part of Greg's moral world because somebody else's rent now depends on his estimates.

### 058 - THE COLLECTOR
Late-paying clients threaten wages and parts orders, teaching Greg that technically successful work can still damage the people who performed it if the money chain fails.

### 059 - THE LOSER
A badly priced contract eats months of savings, and Nessa's independent income keeps the household stable enough to expose how much Greg still equated usefulness with being the one who carries the risk.

### 060 - THE CELEBRANT
A deliberately ordinary anniversary with Nessa makes Greg recognize that many of his most durable memories now belong to a life First Life can neither predict nor validate.

### 061 - THE SURVEYOR
A deep-station survey exposes older Line geometry that modern maintenance doctrine can describe operationally but cannot fully explain.

### 062 - THE TRANSLATOR
Greg becomes useful between scholars who name ancient structures and crews who need to know what can be touched, loaded, drained, or left alone.

### 063 - THE SKEPTIC
A confident theory about the old Line fails against field evidence, strengthening Greg's habit of preferring mechanisms that survive work over explanations that merely sound complete.

### 064 - THE ARCHIVIST
Greg begins keeping detailed failure records because official summaries repeatedly erase the small decisions, delays, substitutions, and human work that actually changed outcomes.

### 065 - THE CORRESPONDENT
Halden, now advancing through central review on his own path, starts exchanging technical letters with Greg because their separate careers keep encountering the same gaps in institutional memory.

### 066 - THE VISITOR
Greg and Nessa spend time in the capital for different professional reasons and see how peripheral maintenance disappears from public attention whenever it is succeeding.

### 067 - THE SUPPLIANT
Greg asks a ministry for repair money his district cannot raise alone and learns that a safety grant is also a negotiation over visibility, prestige, and whose failure will embarrass whom.

### 068 - THE IMPROVISER
When the grant is denied or cut below usefulness, Greg helps assemble a smaller coalition of districts, shops, and labor to solve only the most dangerous part instead of becoming a permanent political operator.

### 069 - THE COMPROMISER
Greg accepts a partial repair plan that leaves one known exposure because refusing it would strand several worse failures, and the decision remains morally ugly even when it is numerically sound.

### 070 - THE ACCOUNTANT
Budgeting regional risk forces Greg to assign numbers to downtime, injury, displacement, and replacement costs, making clear that every safety system contains an argument about whose future is affordable.

### 071 - THE RECRUITER
Greg begins choosing workers for judgment, reliability, and willingness to stop unsafe work rather than raw Ranker potential.

### 072 - THE TRAINER
He builds a practical training sequence from accumulated mistakes and discovers that some First-Life experience only becomes valuable after Life Two teaches him how to transmit it.

### 073 - THE EXAMINER
Serving occasionally on licensing tests makes Greg deny dangerous applicants opportunities similar to ones he once seized without permission, forcing him to own the hypocrisy instead of pretending it is not there.

### 074 - THE DISSENTER
One of Greg's strongest trainees publicly challenges part of his method and produces evidence that the younger worker's revision is safer.

### 075 - THE REVISER
Greg changes his training materials and documentation around the trainee's correction, allowing institutional learning to carry somebody else's judgment instead of preserving his prestige.

### 076 - THE FOOTNOTE
A field practice from Greg's crews spreads regionally through copied procedures and instructors who barely know where it began, giving him influence without requiring celebrity.

### 077 - THE CONSERVATIVE
Greg argues against a fashionable redesign because old infrastructure contains failure modes the proposal has not yet earned the right to dismiss.

### 078 - THE RADICAL
New field evidence later forces Greg to support replacing a centuries-old practice, embarrassing allies and opponents who mistook caution for ideology.

### 079 - THE HOUSEHOLD
Nessa's own increasing responsibility makes Greg renegotiate chores, travel, family care, and work limits instead of treating home as the place where his professional exhaustion gets absorbed.

### 080 - THE VACATIONER
A deliberately uneventful trip with Nessa shows Greg that rest is now a skill he has neglected rather than wasted time he can simply optimize away.

### 081 - THE FISHERMAN
Greg spends a day being bad at something harmless and enjoys competence having no stake in the outcome.

### 082 - THE INVITED
Bren, now connected to Greg through ordinary regional work, asks Greg onto a contract for reasons that belong entirely to Life Two and reverses the old temptation for Greg to manufacture contact.

### 083 - THE LISTENER
Bren tells Greg enough about his altered adulthood to prove that the friend Greg remembers was only one possible shape of the man sitting beside him.

### 084 - THE FRIEND
Repeated work, meals, and voluntary contact finally make Greg and Bren genuine Life-Two friends without requiring the relationship to reproduce First-Life brotherhood on schedule.

### 085 - THE HOSTAGE
A major maintenance tender traps Greg between crews demanding a safety provision and officials who can freeze his regional work if he supports them publicly.

### 086 - THE UNION MAN
Greg sides with the maintenance crews on the safety issue because his years of payroll, training, and station work have made neutrality materially dishonest.

### 087 - THE BLACKLISTED
Procurement officials quietly shut Greg's partnership out of larger contracts, proving that institutional memory can punish reputation just as effectively as it can amplify it.

### 088 - THE SMALL MAN
Forced back toward local jobs, Greg rediscovers work where every outage, missed wage, and repaired room belongs to somebody he can name.

### 089 - THE RECOVERER
Neglected districts keep the partnership alive and gradually produce a more distributed business network that depends less on central approval than Greg's earlier regional success did.

### 090 - THE KNOWN MAN
A multi-district pressure emergency is contained largely through the crews, procedures, parts network, and judgment Greg helped build, making him regionally known for leverage and reliability rather than restored apex strength.

- body/rank/skill: Greg remains an accomplished working Ranker with the old shoulder injury, broader pressure-architecture and teaching competence, and no scheduled provenance yet for the ruined leg, bad ribs, or gate-damaged fingers.
- work/money/property: Greg has moved from permanent station employment into a small repair partnership with leased shop capacity, regular payroll, uneven contracts, and a distributed client network; sole ownership, wealth, named contracts, and larger institutional scale remain open.
- relationships: Greg and Nessa have a deeply interdependent adult partnership with two careers and shared family obligations while exact legal/family form remains open; Greg and Bren have begun an honest Life-Two friendship; Dera and Halden continue independent careers; Greg's parents and sister remain active parts of the lived calendar.
- geography/social range: East Four remains home base, but Greg now has repeated work relationships across nearby districts, several towns, and limited capital exposure without becoming a full-time national figure.
- Line/world position: Greg has accumulated field evidence that modern administrative maps, maintenance doctrine, and ancient physical infrastructure do not cleanly align, while his records and networks give him better causal visibility than raw memory alone.
- First-Life pressure: First-Life memory has lost much of its authority as prediction and survives more usefully as embodied experience, comparison, caution, and a source of biases Greg now knows can be wrong.
- unresolved obligations: Love/family still needs additional calendar depth; catastrophic loss and completed revenge remain far ahead; Peg-Leg identity and named injuries remain untouched; public accomplishment must continue to accumulate without power escalation; Greg's private Ithar motive, ancient notation provenance, Life-One Nhal threshold, named contracts, commendation, three free-drink cities, later climb, and final Line/reset convergence remain open.
