# WINDOW C FIRST PASS

Status: FIRST-PASS EXPERIMENTAL EVIDENCE
Range: Records 161-250

## PROJECTED STATE IN
- Age/time position: mature Life Two, plausibly middle age after decades of accumulation, but exact age and calendar placement remain unresolved.
- Body/rank/skill: experienced Ranker with permanent East Four shoulder damage and broad field competence, but exact tier and later body injuries remain open.
- Work/money/property: projected to have enough professional reputation, contractual leverage, savings, and organizational capacity to affect regional decisions; the exact path to those assets is not canon.
- Relationships: Greg and Nessa have a durable shared life while retaining independent professional judgment; exact marriage, children, and family shape remain open.
- Geography/social range: East Four remains emotionally central, while Greg plausibly works across several western districts and knows crews, officials, contractors, and local institutions beyond one station.
- Line/world position: forgotten infrastructure beneath modern Line systems is established, but kingdom-scale instability is not yet accepted as the dominant explanation for local failures.
- First-Life pressure: Greg's old S-class experience remains useful as embodied comparison and rare evidence, while divergence has made predictive memory increasingly unreliable.
- Unresolved obligations: catastrophic loss, completed revenge, life after revenge, later climbing, ruined leg, Peg-Leg identity, hand and rib injuries, public accomplishments, Ithar's private relevance, and First-Life provenance remain open.
- Explicitly speculative assumptions: senior professional standing, disposable income, some training/organizational capacity, and a mature regional network are projected entry conditions that must be earned earlier or revised.

## RECORD PATH

### 161 - THE SIGNER
Greg's accumulated field reputation now gives his signature regional weight, so he signs a provisional load-sharing plan while recording objections to the assumptions beneath it.

### 162 - THE DISSENTER
Nessa, operating from her own professional judgment rather than as Greg's echo, argues that the plan treats old western districts as expendable buffers.

### 163 - THE TESTER
Greg agrees to a limited field test because refusing every imperfect intervention would leave the deeper anomalies unmeasured.

### 164 - THE GAP
Repeated sensor gaps around one feeder suggest either neglected instrumentation or deliberate suppression, and Greg refuses to decide which without evidence.

### 165 - THE CHEAP ROUTE
A regional board chooses the least expensive reroute because protecting a politically valuable corridor looks safer on current models.

### 166 - THE LOCAL MAN
Greg realizes East Four will absorb disproportionate risk and uses every legitimate lever he has to narrow the exposure rather than simply abandoning the decision.

### 167 - THE LOSING VOTE
Greg loses the allocation fight despite documented objections, establishing that reputation can influence institutions without controlling them.

### 168 - THE WATCHER
Greg stays in East Four through the reroute period to monitor crews and evacuation thresholds, even though leaving would protect his own household.

### 169 - THE FALSE RECOVERY
An early fault clears without major damage, strengthening the board's confidence while making Greg more suspicious of what the instruments are failing to show.

### 170 - THE WARNING
Greg's local reporting network finds the same deep-pressure signature at several stations, and he sends a dated warning that can later establish who knew what when.

### 171 - THE TRANSFER
An emergency load shift pushes the old substrate past a threshold modern models do not represent, turning a managed reroute into a cascading district failure.

### 172 - THE LOST DISTRICT
East Four becomes uninhabitable as a functioning community, Dera and other longtime residents die, and Greg and Nessa survive into a life that no longer has their old home.

### 173 - THE ACCOUNTANT OF PEOPLE
Greg spends the first days tracking evacuees, bodies, medicines, tools, pets, debts, and missing workers because catastrophe is mostly logistics before it becomes narrative.

### 174 - THE DISPLACED
Nessa chooses work with the displaced crews and families instead of attaching herself to Greg's investigation, preserving her own direction inside their shared grief.

### 175 - THE PAPER TRAIL
Greg finds that the board received versions of his warning stripped of the most alarming field notes, making human manipulation real without yet proving a single mastermind.

### 176 - THE COMPLAINANT
He opens a formal inquiry and discovers that every participant can plausibly claim they only handled one harmless piece of the fatal decision.

### 177 - THE WITNESS
A junior analyst produces evidence that senior officials ordered risk language softened to avoid an economically disruptive shutdown.

### 178 - THE PRICE
The witness loses work and housing after cooperating, teaching Greg that legal accountability can punish truth-tellers faster than decision-makers.

### 179 - THE PARTNER
Nessa supports exposing the decision chain but refuses Greg's growing desire to make specific people suffer beyond what evidence and law can sustain.

### 180 - THE UNSATISFIED
Official sanctions remove several minor officials while leaving the central architects protected, and Greg decides the lawful outcome is not enough for him.

### 181 - THE INVESTOR
Greg spends personal money and professional favors on investigators who can follow contracts, shell companies, and maintenance exemptions beyond the inquiry's jurisdiction.

### 182 - THE OUTSIDER
Bren re-enters Greg's life because an independent contract puts him near the same evidence, and he immediately refuses to become a replacement for the brother Greg remembers.

### 183 - THE BOUNDARY
Greg gives Bren only present-life reasons to participate, forcing their cooperation to stand on shared facts rather than First-Life intimacy.

### 184 - THE LEVER
Greg begins withdrawing his company, trainees, and certification support from firms tied to the cover-up, turning accumulated ordinary-life leverage into an instrument of revenge.

### 185 - THE COLLATERAL
Workers with no role in the catastrophe lose jobs because of Greg's pressure campaign, and Nessa forces him to see that revenge can reproduce the same logic of acceptable sacrifice.

### 186 - THE NARROWING
Greg rewrites his target list around demonstrated knowledge and direct benefit, abandoning the fantasy that an entire institution can be morally flattened into one enemy.

### 187 - THE LEDGER
Financial records show that a senior director preserved a profitable corridor after receiving clear evidence that East Four could fail catastrophically.

### 188 - THE PUBLIC MAN
Greg releases enough evidence to destroy the director's public legitimacy while keeping back material that would expose vulnerable witnesses.

### 189 - THE COUNTERMOVE
The director uses remaining influence to frame Greg as a grieving profiteer whose own contracts benefited from the emergency.

### 190 - THE COST OF A NAME
Some districts stop hiring Greg while others rally around him, proving that public reputation is a usable asset only because it can also be spent.

### 191 - THE HIDDEN COMPLEX
A fleeing records custodian leads the investigation into an obsolete lower Line complex where the reroute decision and the deeper infrastructure problem intersect.

### 192 - THE DESCENDER
Greg enters with a small lawful recovery team, not because the map says revenge requires a dungeon but because the missing records can decide whether the catastrophe was policy, fraud, or both.

### 193 - THE OLDER MACHINE
The complex reveals structures beneath the human Line whose load behavior explains why the board's gamble was more dangerous than any participant understood.

### 194 - THE TWO GUILTS
Greg separates two truths that can coexist: the substrate was unstable beyond human knowledge, and named people still knowingly concealed the risk they did understand.

### 195 - THE SABOTEUR
The senior director arrives through a separate route to destroy records that would convert political disgrace into criminal liability.

### 196 - THE RESCUER
When sabotage traps workers from both sides, Greg abandons the chase long enough to organize their escape because his practical habits survive his hatred.

### 197 - THE LAST ROOM
Greg reaches the director after the workers are clear and learns that saving him is still physically possible.

### 198 - THE REVENGER
Greg deliberately leaves the director in the failing complex after securing the evidence, crossing from accountability into a personal act he cannot rename as procedure.

### 199 - THE COMPLETION
The director dies and the exposed decision chain collapses, giving Greg an unmistakable revenge boundary with no remaining human target whose destruction would restore East Four.

### 200 - THE CONFESSOR
Greg reports his choice accurately enough that allies cannot preserve a cleaner public version of what he did.

### 201 - THE CENSURED
Authorities restrict Greg's field standing and strip several privileges without erasing the public service and technical credibility he accumulated before revenge.

### 202 - THE UNFORGIVEN
Nessa stays in Greg's life on the primary branch but refuses to absolve the choice at the complex, making continued love incompatible with moral simplification.

### 203 - THE QUIET WEEK
With the revenge finished, Greg discovers that days continue to require groceries, repairs, displaced-family paperwork, sleep, and conversation.

### 204 - THE USELESS VICTORY
Nothing about the completed revenge returns East Four, Dera, or the dead, and Greg stops pretending completion and satisfaction are the same thing.

### 205 - THE OTHER BEGINNING
Greg privately begins asking whether another rebeginning could restore what was lost, even though any reset might also erase the Life-Two people and relationships he refuses to call mistakes.

### 206 - THE HIDDEN NAME
Ithar's name shifts in Greg's mind from impossible First-Life trivia to a morally dangerous possible route toward that private question.

### 207 - THE RESTRAINT
He does not leave for Ithar because wanting another beginning is not yet sufficient reason to discard the life still asking things of him.

### 208 - THE SUCCESSORS
People Greg trained keep the work functioning during his censure, proving that his most durable achievement may be capacity that survives his absence.

### 209 - THE REQUEST
A former trainee asks Greg to review a failure nobody can reconcile with standard Line theory, pulling him back toward useful work without restoring his old authority.

### 210 - THE ADVISER
Greg returns as a consultant whose value lies more in judgment, memory, and pattern recognition than in being the strongest person on site.

### 211 - THE LISTENER
Greg builds a practice of collecting operators' observations before offering explanations, partly because revenge taught him what certainty can cost.

### 212 - THE ARCHIVIST
He begins preserving the reasons behind changed standards, failed experiments, and dissenting reports so later workers can reconstruct causality instead of inheriting rules without memory.

### 213 - THE FAILURE SPONSOR
Greg funds small stabilization trials designed to fail cheaply, treating unsuccessful work as evidence rather than embarrassment.

### 214 - THE REPLICATOR
Several districts reproduce one unexpected stabilization effect, making the deep-structure hypothesis harder to dismiss as East Four grief dressed as theory.

### 215 - THE DISSENTER AGAIN
Nessa independently supports some of Greg's research while rejecting others, preventing their partnership from becoming a single institutional voice.

### 216 - THE VOLUNTARY NETWORK
Stations begin sharing anomaly reports through a loose network that works because participants trust one another more than they trust central reporting incentives.

### 217 - THE GATE CRUSH
A routine emergency closure jams during a field inspection and crushes Greg's right hand while he helps free a trapped operator, leaving two fingers permanently unable to close correctly.

### 218 - THE LEFT-HANDED MONTHS
Recovery forces Greg to redesign ordinary tasks around damaged grip rather than treating the injury as a heroic credential.

### 219 - THE TOOLMAKER
Greg and working crews adapt handles, fasteners, and procedures for limited hands, producing improvements useful to people whose injuries never earned stories.

### 220 - THE LOWER CEILING
A formal reassessment confirms that age, old shoulder damage, and the hand injury have reduced Greg's safe field ceiling even before any leg loss occurs.

### 221 - THE REGIONAL PATTERN
Reports from multiple western districts show synchronized pressure migration that no local maintenance failure can explain.

### 222 - THE UNWELCOME MODEL
Greg's rough model predicts that keeping every settlement fully loaded will increase the chance of a regional cascade, making conservation politically more dangerous than repair.

### 223 - THE RETREAT ARGUMENT
He begins advocating temporary load reduction and selective evacuation in places he cares about, testing whether he can accept sacrifice without hiding who pays.

### 224 - THE FIELD PROOF
A smaller district survives a severe anomaly because it follows the unpopular reduction plan, giving Greg evidence without proving the model universally correct.

### 225 - THE EXCEPTION
Greg tries to spare one personally beloved place from the same restrictions and catches himself becoming the political exception he once condemned.

### 226 - THE CORRECTION
He withdraws the favor and publishes his conflict, choosing consistency even though the decision costs him relationships.

### 227 - THE LOWER CHANNEL
An inspection beneath a western transfer hub reveals ancient load paths matching fragments Greg remembers from First-Life high-level work, but not enough to reconstruct the old mechanism.

### 228 - THE SECOND ACCIDENT
A deep-pressure shift collapses a service span during evacuation, trapping Greg where his damaged hand prevents the climb his younger body might once have made.

### 229 - THE CHOICE OF WEIGHT
Greg uses his remaining leverage to keep a failing support open long enough for other workers to cross, accepting a crushing leg injury as the price of a decision made in real time rather than to fulfill a prophecy.

### 230 - THE AMPUTEE
Surgeons remove the ruined leg after salvage fails, leaving the exact shape of future mobility to recovery rather than making the injury itself the identity payoff.

### 231 - THE DEPENDENT
Greg's recovery depends on trainees, Nessa, Bren if that Life-Two bond survives, and ordinary caregivers whose competence he cannot replace with old S-class memory.

### 232 - THE MAKER
He participates in prosthetic design because field mobility, stairs, mud, tools, and long standing matter more to him than cosmetic normalcy.

### 233 - THE BEGINNER
Knowing how to move does not teach the altered body how to balance, forcing Greg through a second embodied apprenticeship with none of nineteen-year-old Greg's illusion that memory equals capability.

### 234 - THE NAMED MAN
After Greg returns to limited public work and crews have already begun using the nickname, the Authority formalizes Peg-Leg Greg as a Life-Two named Ranker identity rather than inventing the title at the moment of injury.

### 235 - THE LOW B
A later reassessment places Greg around low B-class, making his diminished physical tier public while his professional reach continues to expand.

### 236 - THE COACH
Greg learns to direct stronger younger Rankers through Line emergencies he should no longer enter, converting old combat and field instincts into other people's survivable decisions.

### 237 - THE REMOTE MAN
A mission succeeds because Greg stays with maps and communications instead of insisting on presence, teaching him that distance can be responsibility rather than cowardice.

### 238 - THE PUBLIC SERVANT
A multi-district evacuation coordinated through Greg's network saves thousands without requiring him to be physically exceptional, creating plausible grounds for later high public recognition without forcing the King's commendation here.

### 239 - THE CONTRACT MEMORY
Several named contracts now matter less as trophies than as repositories of crews, local trust, and failure history that central planners cannot reproduce quickly.

### 240 - THE HOSPITALITY
Greg notices that hospitality follows old work across multiple cities, preserving the possibility that the drink-free legend grows from specific relationships rather than deliberate reputation-building.

### 241 - THE S-CLASS GHOST
Ancient signatures in current reports force Greg to reopen First-Life memories from jobs only an S-class Ranker could have survived, and he finds the memories vivid in consequence but poor in mechanism.

### 242 - THE UNRELIABLE WITNESS
One remembered warning sign fails to recur where Greg expects it, making his First-Life experience useful evidence rather than a predictive key.

### 243 - THE MARKS
Greg recognizes ancient notation on newly exposed structures but cannot yet explain where he learned enough to read it, pushing that provenance backward into unwritten First-Life work.

### 244 - THE NHAL THREAD
Fragments associated with Nhal now resemble current deep-Line evidence closely enough that Greg starts separating what Nhal actually said from what forty years of memory added.

### 245 - THE PRIVATE SEARCH
Before the western crisis becomes the kingdom's public emergency, Greg quietly begins checking the route toward Ithar because the question of another beginning has survived revenge, grief, and rebuilding.

### 246 - THE DISCLOSURE
On the primary Nessa-survives branch, Greg tells her enough about the private search to reveal that a reset capable of restoring the dead might also erase the life they built together.

### 247 - THE REFUSAL TO DECIDE
Nessa refuses to serve as either permission or prohibition, forcing Greg to keep the moral contradiction instead of outsourcing it to the person he might erase.

### 248 - THE PUBLIC CRISIS
Synchronized western failures accelerate from research problem to kingdom-scale threat, giving Greg a public reason to seek Ithar that does not replace the older private one.

### 249 - THE DOUBLE RECORD
Greg begins setting First-Life failure memories beside Life-Two measurements and preserves disagreements between them because the mismatch is now more informative than either timeline alone.

### 250 - THE CONVERGENCE
Greg ends C with the route toward Ithar becoming materially actionable while his motives remain split between saving a failing world and asking whether any life should be allowed another beginning.

## STATE OUT
- Age/time position: late-life territory, plausibly approaching the frame-present years, while exact age at Record 250 remains deliberately unresolved.
- Body/rank/skill: projected low B-class after aging, old shoulder damage, a gate-crushed right hand, and a separate catastrophic leg injury; skill has shifted from direct physical dominance toward judgment, instruction, coordination, and system reading.
- Work/money/property: Greg's earlier organization and reputation survive him imperfectly through trainees, reporting relationships, contracts, archives, and practical standards rather than dependence on his personal field strength.
- Relationships: primary branch keeps Nessa alive as an independent partner who neither abandons nor absolves Greg; Bren's honest Life-Two relationship remains conditional rather than restored by fiat.
- Geography/social range: Greg is embedded across a western network of stations, displaced communities, crews, and institutions rather than belonging only to East Four, which now persists mainly through people and memory.
- Line/world position: local anomalies have become evidence of deep systemic instability, and the western problem is crossing into kingdom-scale public crisis without erasing human responsibility for earlier catastrophe.
- First-Life pressure: S-class memories, Nhal, ancient notation, and old western failures are becoming operationally relevant again, but memory remains incomplete, fallible, and provenance-bound.
- Unresolved obligations: exact King's commendation, five named contracts, three drink-free cities, badly set ribs, full Nhal encounter, ancient-notation provenance, lower-channel/ninth-station/eastern-sink First-Life sequence, final Ithar route, artifact mechanics, reset cost, beneficiary, and ending choice remain open.

## OBLIGATIONS PUSHED FORWARD
- Convert the western failure from accelerating public crisis into the specific present-frame conditions that make Greg's journey to Ithar necessary now.
- Pay the First-Life provenance for Nhal, ancient notation, lower channels, ninth station, eastern sink, and credible S-class-scale experience without turning memory into prophecy.
- Decide whether the King's commendation, five named contracts, and three-city hospitality are earned before, during, or after this window, with concrete local histories rather than checklist exposition.
- Keep the three badly set ribs unresolved until a separate lived causal event earns them.
- Develop the reset/rebeginning mechanism so Greg's private motive and the public Line mission remain simultaneously real and morally incompatible in useful ways.
- Carry life after revenge forward long enough that Greg's rebuilding, disability, and institutional legacy feel like a life rather than an epilogue to vengeance.
- Determine whether Bren becomes a durable Life-Two friend, a periodic ally, or something less symmetrical than the First-Life brotherhood.

## OBLIGATIONS PUSHED BACKWARD
- Earlier Life Two must earn enough regional reputation, contractual standing, money, and organizational capacity for Greg's signature, investigators, trainees, and network to matter by Record 161.
- Greg and Nessa must accumulate a durable shared life plus independent professional identities before the catastrophe so survival together has weight beyond romance.
- East Four must remain a functioning community with Dera and other ordinary relationships alive long enough that losing the district destroys a real social world rather than a set piece.
- Earlier records must establish credible institutions for load allocation, safety reporting, contracting, appeals, and regional Line governance without requiring one omnipotent bureaucracy.
- Greg must have learned enough restraint around First-Life foreknowledge that his evidence discipline during the catastrophe investigation feels developed rather than newly convenient.
- If Bren enters C through an independent contract, earlier development must leave his route plausibly separate from Greg's while preserving reasons their work could intersect again.
- Any company, training program, archive, or certification role used here must either be earned earlier or replaced with a less specific form of professional leverage.

## PROTECTED UNCERTAINTIES / FORKS
- Primary branch: Nessa survives while East Four, Dera, and multiple longtime people are lost; alternate branch: Nessa can still die in the catastrophe if later rehearsal proves that path richer rather than merely harsher.
- A second non-Nessa catastrophe branch remains viable in which an adult family member, apprentice, or other loved person dies while Nessa survives, but exact family shape must not be invented merely to supply a victim.
- The catastrophe can remain a destroyed-place-and-community loss rather than requiring one intimate death if the manuscript makes East Four sufficiently lived and specific.
- Human culpability may be distributed across several actors rather than concentrated in one senior director; the director here is a pathfinding device, not a required villain.
- Revenge completion may be the deliberate non-rescue proposed here, a legally irreversible destruction of the responsible network, or another completed act, provided Greg knowingly crosses a boundary and then lives after it.
- Nessa may stay, separate temporarily, or leave permanently after Greg's revenge; she cannot exist only to forgive or punish him.
- Bren may cooperate, oppose Greg, or remain peripheral; no version may use private First-Life knowledge to manufacture the old friendship.
- The gate-crushed fingers, leg loss, low-B reassessment, and Peg-Leg naming are causally separated here on purpose, but any of them may move later if another lived event earns them better.
- The leg-loss event is not protected simply because it appears in this pass; it may move into later collapse territory if Window D finds a stronger causal threshold.
- Greg's private Ithar search may be disclosed to Nessa, concealed longer, or discovered indirectly, but it must predate the public Line rationale and remain morally uncomfortable.
- Exact marriage, children, property ownership, company structure, public office, and age remain unresolved throughout this pass.

## MAP CHANGES
- KEEP: the useful core that human wrongdoing and deeper Line instability can both be true, revenge must complete, life must continue afterward, disability can redirect rather than end usefulness, reporting networks can scale local knowledge, and First-Life memory must become evidence rather than a cheat code.
- MOVE: move the catastrophe itself into C instead of opening C deep inside a predetermined revenge chase, move leg loss well after revenge so it grows from rebuilding and renewed field obligation, and move Greg's private Ithar search ahead of the fully public kingdom crisis.
- MERGE: merge repetitive revenge-pursuit beats into one evidence-and-leverage campaign, merge multiple rehabilitation identity beats into fewer lived adaptation thresholds, and merge several abstract research titles into field experiments, operator reports, and archives with practical consequences.
- SPLIT: split human culpability from systemic Line cause, split accountability from revenge, split the gate-damaged hand from the ruined leg, split injury from Authority naming, and split Greg's private reset motive from the later public Line mission.
- REPLACE: replace default Nessa-as-catastrophic-victim with a primary branch where East Four as a community is destroyed while Nessa survives, replace immediate execution with a completed but morally uglier deliberate non-rescue, and replace solitary genius research with a distributed operator network that can outlive Greg.
- KILL: kill the assumption that C must begin with a director's bait sequence, kill the revenge-to-leg-loss conveyor belt, kill any automatic equation of amputation with instant Peg-Leg naming, kill mandatory retirement as the only aftermath shape, and kill the requirement that Nessa's grave be the mechanism that teaches Greg how to grieve.
