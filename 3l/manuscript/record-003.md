## RECORD 003

## THE BARGAINER

“Good?”

“Yes.”

“That was not good.”

“It was to me.”

I stare at the Dragon.

It stares back.

I am beginning to suspect this is going to happen a lot.

“You asked what happened,” I say.

“Yes.”

“I said it was a long story.”

“Yes.”

“And that pleased you.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

The Dragon shifts its head against the stone until one horn scrapes somewhere above me.

“Because short explanations have failed.”

“You've heard about ten minutes of explanation.”

“More.”

“Fine. Fifteen.”

“Twenty-three.”

I look at it.

“You've been timing me?”

“No.”

“Then how do you know?”

“You have a pulse.”

I wait.

The Dragon does not elaborate.

“That's unsettling.”

“It is also approximate.”

“Good. Glad we're preserving standards.”

The Dragon ignores that.

“You arrived with a problem you considered large enough to matter to me. It did not. You then offered hidden knowledge as payment. The knowledge was not valuable in the way you believed. Its presence in you was.”

“That's a very flattering summary of me failing to negotiate.”

“You have not yet failed.”

I straighten.

There it is.

Small. Probably accidental.

Still.

“What would success look like?”

“For you?”

“Yes.”

“The Line does not fail.”

“That is an outcome.”

“That's what I asked for.”

“It is one thing you asked for.”

I feel something tighten under my ribs.

“No.”

The Dragon watches me.

I have learned enough in the last half hour to know silence is not surrender.

“You came with three days of food for an eight-day return,” it says.

“We covered that.”

“You came alone.”

“Yes.”

“You entered my territory knowing I might kill you.”

“Yes.”

“You prepared carefully enough to reach me and poorly enough to leave.”

“Again, covered.”

“You did not ask whether I could repair the Line before entering.”

“I knew you could.”

“You think I can.”

“Close enough.”

“No.”

I breathe through my nose.

The Dragon continues.

“You also knew things about me that were unnecessary to the Line. The northern approach. The arch. The warning that was not a warning. My color.”

“I was trying not to die.”

“Before the Line began failing?”

I don't answer.

The eye remains on me.

“Greg.”

“What?”

“If I repair your Line, what do you do next?”

“Go home.”

“Where?”

I almost answer automatically.

Almost.

“South.”

“That is a direction.”

“You don't need my address.”

“No.”

“Then why ask?”

“I asked what you do next.”

“I told you.”

“You did not.”

I look toward the passage again.

I have done that enough times that I know the shape of the first bend now.

Dark rock. Thin line of reflected gold from the cave behind me. The way out sitting there like it has nothing to do with the conversation.

“I go back,” I say.

“To what?”

“The rest of my life.”

The Dragon is quiet.

I hate the silence before it even ends.

“You have already had two.”

My hand tightens around the strap of my pack.

“Don't.”

“Do not what?”

“Turn every sentence into something else.”

“I am asking what your sentence means.”

“It means I go home.”

“And then?”

“Eat.”

“And then?”

“Sleep.”

“And then?”

“Probably complain about my knee.”

“And then?”

I glare at it.

The Dragon waits.

This could continue for years.

That realization is not comforting.

“Fine,” I say. “I live.”

“Good.”

“There. We solved it.”

“No.”

“Of course not.”

“You have answered the question you believe I should have asked.”

“What question did you ask?”

“What do you do next?”

“I live.”

“You were already doing that before the Line failed.”

That gets under my guard.

Not far.

Enough.

I shift the pack again.

The Dragon's eye follows the movement.

“You've decided I have another reason for being here.”

“I have decided nothing.”

“Sure sounds like it.”

“I have noticed that your business with me does not end with the Line.”

I laugh.

Actually laugh.

The sound runs farther into the cave than I expect.

“That's stupid.”

“Possibly.”

“Millions of people live if you solve it.”

“Yes.”

“That is the business.”

“Then why did you search for me before the Line began failing?”

I stop laughing.

There it is again.

Context.

The Dragon can make a hole in a sentence big enough to fall through and then wait for me to do it myself.

I don't.

Not yet.

“The first life,” it says.

“What about it?”

“You became S-class.”

“Yes.”

“You knew the lower channels.”

“Yes.”

“You knew enough about me to reach this place.”

“Apparently.”

“And you died?”

I stop.

It is the first time either of us has used the word.

“Yes.”

“How?”

“No.”

The Dragon's nostrils flare once.

Not anger.

Maybe disappointment.

Maybe just air.

“You are willing to tell me you died but not how.”

“Correct.”

“Why?”

“Because it isn't relevant.”

“To what?”

“The Line.”

“No.”

I rub my forehead.

“We keep doing this.”

“Yes.”

“You know what I mean.”

“I know what you are attempting.”

“Same thing.”

“No.”

I point at it.

“That. We can stop doing that.”

“We cannot.”

“Why?”

“Because you compress.”

I lower my hand.

“What?”

“You reduce events until they fit the answer you want to give. You died. You returned. You knew. You failed. You became weaker. You came here. Each is a container. You speak as though naming it explains what is inside.”

I don't answer.

The Dragon's voice has changed again.

Not louder.

Longer.

It has stopped asking one question at a time.

“You tell me that you were S-class as though the rank explains the life that produced it. It does not. You tell me you returned to nineteen as though the age explains what returned with you. It does not. You tell me the world was mostly the same and expect me to ignore the word mostly. You tell me you did not become S-class again and then offer ignorance as though forty years simply happened around you.”

“That is not what I said.”

“It is what you removed.”

I stare at it.

The Dragon lets the sentence sit between us.

“What do you want?” I ask.

“The discrepancy.”

“You said that.”

“I want to understand it.”

“And then you'll help me.”

“I did not say that.”

“Then we're back where we started.”

“No. When you arrived, I was not interested in you.”

That is so matter-of-fact I almost laugh.

“Progress.”

“Yes.”

I wait.

Nothing else comes.

“So what, exactly, are you proposing?”

The Dragon takes a long breath.

Heat rolls over the floor again.

Not fire.

Just the consequence of something that large filling its lungs.

“You will tell me what happened.”

“All of it.”

“As much as you can.”

“And then?”

“I will decide what I believe.”

“And then?”

“I will decide whether your problem interests me.”

“My problem already doesn't.”

“The kingdom does not.”

I stop.

The Dragon watches me notice the difference.

I wish it wouldn't do that either.

“What problem do you think I have?”

“I do not know.”

“You're asking for forty years of my life because you don't know what you're looking for.”

“Yes.”

“That is insane.”

“No.”

“Of course.”

“I know the claim. I do not know the mechanism. I know some of your memories correspond to things you should not know. I know some contain errors that resemble lived misunderstanding rather than invention. I know your body carries habits that do not match its present capability. I know you arrived here with a public reason and a private one you have not stated.”

I open my mouth.

The Dragon lifts one claw.

Not threatening.

Somehow that makes me stop anyway.

“I do not know whether those facts share one cause. I do not know whether your first life occurred as you believe it did. I do not know whether your return was movement, repetition, duplication, memory, or something for which your language has chosen the wrong noun. If I choose the explanation before I hear the record, I will spend the record proving myself right.”

That is the longest thing it has said to me.

I notice because my knee starts hurting halfway through and I have time to shift twice before it finishes.

“You could've led with that.”

“I did not know enough to say it earlier.”

That shuts me up.

The Dragon settles again.

“You want the whole thing.”

“Yes.”

“Forty years.”

“Yes.”

“You understand how long forty years is?”

“Yes.”

“For a person.”

“Yes.”

“And you're fine with that.”

“Yes.”

“How long do you think this is going to take?”

“I do not know.”

“Weeks.”

“Possibly.”

“Months.”

“Possibly.”

I wait for the objection.

There isn't one.

“You don't care.”

“About the duration?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

I look around the cave.

The stone.

The dark.

The water moving somewhere I cannot see.

The creature whose idea of a long conversation may include a season.

“I have three days of food.”

“Yes.”

“That seems relevant now.”

“It does.”

“Finally, something.”

“There is water.”

“I hear it.”

“There are animals below the eastern shelf.”

“With the cliff?”

“No. The other eastern shelf.”

“Good. Very clear.”

“I can show you.”

I blink.

That may be the first offer the Dragon has made.

Not save the kingdom.

Not repair the Line.

Food directions.

I'll take it.

“You're serious.”

“Yes.”

“You'd let me stay here for months while I tell you my life.”

“If months are required.”

“And if I say no?”

“You leave.”

“That's it?”

“Yes.”

“No burning me.”

“Not for declining conversation.”

“Comforting qualifier.”

The Dragon ignores that.

I finally take the pack off.

My shoulder thanks me immediately.

I set it beside the almost-seat and look at the almost-seat.

Then at the Dragon.

“Can I?”

“Yes.”

I sit.

It is a terrible chair.

Still better than standing.

For a little while neither of us talks.

The quiet feels different once I have agreed, even though I have not actually agreed.

I am sitting in a Dragon's cave with three days of food, a bad knee, and a kingdom coming apart below us.

This was not in the plan.

“The second life,” I say.

The Dragon's eye opens a little wider.

“What about it?”

“If I tell you anything, that's where I start.”

“Why?”

“Because that's the one that matters.”

“To you.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because it's the one I'm in.”

“That is current, not necessarily relevant.”

“It is relevant.”

“How would I know?”

“You'll have to trust me.”

“No.”

“Had to try.”

The Dragon waits.

I sigh.

“The first life is forty years too.”

“Yes.”

“I am not telling you eighty years.”

“Why not?”

I laugh once.

The Dragon does not.

“You're serious.”

“Yes.”

“This could take half a year.”

“Yes.”

“That was not an invitation.”

“I know.”

I rub the side of my knee.

“First Life is context.”

The Dragon says nothing.

“Second Life is the story.”

Still nothing.

I sigh. “Look. I knew who I was the first time. I knew what jobs I took. Who trained me. Where I went. Which mistakes cost me years. The second time, I had all of that in my head already. That's the difference that matters.”

“You began the second life by comparing it to the first.”

“Yes.”

“Then the first life is not merely context. It is the thing against which you measured every change.”

“Not every change.”

“How many?”

“I don't know.”

“Exactly.”

I lean forward and rest my forearms on my knees. The left one complains about the arrangement.

“I am still not telling you eighty years in order.”

“I did not ask for that.”

“You implied it.”

“You assumed it.”

“That's worse.”

“No.”

“That's the deal.”

“You are offering terms.”

“Yes.”

“To me.”

“Yes.”

“In my home.”

“Yes.”

The Dragon considers this.

I wait.

It turns out dragons can make you regret a negotiating position without moving at all.

“Accepted,” it says.

I sit up.

“Really?”

“No.”

“Fuck you.”

The Dragon's mouth shifts slightly.

Badge joke again, maybe.

I can't tell.

“Life Two may be the path you describe,” it says. “Life One enters whenever Life Two depends upon it.”

“That sounds suspiciously like what I just said.”

“It is not.”

“Of course it isn't.”

“You decide what you remember. You do not decide what is relevant before I hear it.”

“I have to decide something. I cannot tell you every breakfast.”

“No.”

“Good.”

“If a breakfast matters, you will tell me.”

“How am I supposed to know?”

“You may not.”

I stare at it.

“That seems like a flaw.”

“Yes.”

“In your plan.”

“In memory.”

That one I don't have a quick answer for.

The Dragon continues.

“You may compress repetition. You may tell me that you worked somewhere for three years if those years truly contain nothing you can distinguish.”

“Excellent.”

“But if you remember the first morning, the woman who sat across from you, the day your pay changed, the argument that made you leave, and the meal you ate afterward, then you have not remembered three years as one sentence.”

I look at it.

“You're going to be annoying about this.”

“Yes.”

“At least we're clear.”

“You have spent forty years deciding which parts of your life explain you.”

I don't like that sentence.

The Dragon is not finished.

“I have no reason to trust your selection.”

“I was there.”

“Yes.”

“I know what mattered.”

“You knew what mattered to you then. You know what matters to you now. Neither is the same as knowing what produced the discrepancy.”

I lean back as far as the stone allows.

It does not allow much.

“Fine.”

“Is that agreement?”

“It is exhaustion.”

“They can coexist.”

“Apparently.”

The Dragon closes its eye.

I wait.

After a while I realize it is waiting too.

“For what?” I ask.

“You.”

“You need to ask a question.”

“Do I?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because otherwise I'm just talking.”

“You came here to talk.”

“I came here to negotiate.”

“Then negotiate.”

I look at the Dragon.

At the open passage.

At my pack on the floor.

At the stupid stone under me that has somehow become my chair.

“After I tell you,” I say, “you look at the Line.”

“Yes.”

“And if you can stop it?”

“I will tell you whether I will.”

“That is terrible.”

“It is what I offer.”

I think about two point three million people.

About eight days back down the mountain.

About the months it took me to get enough of the old records together to be sure the Dragon was here.

About the thing I have not asked yet.

I do not think about that very long.

“Fine.”

“Agreement?”

“Enough of one.”

“No.”

I close my eyes.

“Agreement.”

“Good.”

I open them again.

The Dragon is watching me.

“All right,” I say. “Where do you want me to start?”

It answers without hesitation.

“Where you became different.”
