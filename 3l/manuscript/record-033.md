## RECORD 033

## THE NEW BOSS

Sera changed the lock on my old cabinet.

That is not why I disliked her.

It helped.

Dera had left me a key for years after I stopped working permanent shifts.

Not officially.

The key worked.

Institutions contain many policies expressed entirely through whether a key still turns.

Mine stopped turning on a Monday.

I stood in East Four's lower gallery with the key halfway in and experienced betrayal from a piece of brass.

Sera came down the stairs carrying a work slate.

“You need something?”

“My key does not work.”

“Yes.”

“You changed the lock.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because Dera left.”

“That is not a reason.”

“It was her lock list.”

“I am district inspection.”

“Yes.”

“I use this cabinet.”

“You used this cabinet.”

There it was.

I hated grammar.

The cabinet held legacy survey gear.

Chalk.

Reference weights.

Old inspection hammers.

Two brass gauges.

A spool of line nobody trusted but nobody threw away.

District equipment stored inside East Four because the station had space and I had once worked there.

Reasonable arrangement under Dera.

Sera had moved the district gear to a labeled cage upstairs.

Signed access.

Checkout sheet.

I hated it immediately.

“You made paperwork.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because three people used the brass gauge last month and nobody knew who had it.”

“I knew.”

“Where was it?”

I thought.

Tova had borrowed one.

Lorn maybe another.

East Two survey.

Or Market Five.

Sera waited.

“Not the point.”

“It is exactly the point.”

She handed me the slate.

District cage.

Item list.

Names.

Dates.

My own name appeared twice.

RETURN NOT RECORDED.

I stared.

“This feels targeted.”

“It is alphabetical.”

Cruel woman.

Dera would have shouted.

Sera documented.

Different management style.

I was going to have to grow new defenses.

The actual conflict came two weeks later.

District survey wanted access to East Four's buried chamber for a comparative temperature run.

Nothing urgent.

We were checking whether old bronze responses matched similar structures at East Two and South Three under the same seasonal load.

Tova scheduled Tuesday morning.

Sera refused.

The notice came back.

ACCESS DENIED. RESCHEDULE AFTER PUMP SERVICE.

I went to East Four.

Not angrily.

I would like credit for that.

Nessa would not give it.

Sera was at the pressure desk.

“Why deny chamber access?”

“Pump service.”

“We are not touching the pump.”

“You need lower west passage.”

“Yes.”

“So does pump crew.”

“We take twenty minutes.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because they have equipment in the passage and I am not stacking two jobs in one access route.”

“We can work around them.”

“They should not have to work around you.”

I stopped.

That sentence offended me professionally.

“We are district.”

Sera looked at me.

“So?”

“So this is district survey.”

“Yes.”

“Approved by Halden.”

“Good for Halden.”

I leaned on the desk.

Sera looked at my hand.

I took it off.

Clerks had trained me.

“Dera would have let us in.”

There it was.

The stupid sentence.

Sera's face changed.

Not anger.

Disappointment.

Worse.

“Dera is at district.”

“I know.”

“Then ask her to lend you a station.”

“That is not what I meant.”

“It is what you said.”

I looked away.

East Four hummed around us.

Healthy.

Different shifts.

Different foreman.

Same brick.

I had confused familiarity with permission.

Again.

I was very consistent.

“Fine,” I said.

“No.”

I looked back.

“What?”

“Do not fine me like I am being difficult.”

“You are being difficult.”

“I am protecting a maintenance window.”

“You could fit both.”

“Yes.”

That stopped me.

“You could.”

“Yes.”

“Then why not?”

“Because the pump crew gets one clean window every six weeks and district survey can come Wednesday.”

There it was.

Priority.

Not impossibility.

Sera was choosing ordinary maintenance over my interesting work.

Dera might have too.

I did not know.

That was the point.

“You think survey matters less.”

“Today? Yes.”

“The chamber could show seasonal pressure transfer.”

“The pump could fail if we keep delaying service for everybody who says their job is important.”

Reasonable.

I hated reasonable people.

They multiplied.

We came Wednesday.

Twenty minutes became forty because one temperature tab had loosened.

No pump crew to work around.

No one rushed us.

Tova read Sera's denied notice.

“She was right.”

“I know.”

“Did you tell her?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because then she will become stronger.”

Tova looked at me.

“That is not how authority works.”

“You have not met Sera.”

“I have.”

Of course.

Everyone knew everyone except when I needed advantage.

Sera's differences accumulated.

She moved East Four's break schedule.

Dera had staggered by crew.

Sera staggered by heat exposure because upper gallery workers were getting fewer full breaks in summer.

She changed tool checkout.

Added written handoff at shift change.

Stopped the habit of leaving temporary repairs marked only with red cord because two color-blind workers had apparently spent years pretending that system worked.

Nessa was offended by the cord decision.

“Red is tradition.”

“Red is invisible to Kett.”

“Fine.”

Sera replaced it with tagged metal clips.

Better system.

Ugly clips.

I missed the cord.

Both could be true.

She also stopped letting me wander into lower galleries without signing in.

That one remained personal.

Months later East Four had a real problem.

Market branch regulator fault.

Not dangerous yet.

Could become dangerous if left.

Sera called district for inspection because the failure pattern touched old support geometry.

She called me.

Not Dera.

Not because she liked me.

Because the job fit.

I arrived.

Signed in.

Took a gauge from the cage.

Wrote my name.

Sera saw.

Said nothing.

Cruel restraint.

We worked six hours.

Her crew owned the station.

I owned the survey question.

At one point I wanted the branch held at fifty percent for a test.

Sera said forty.

“Why?”

“Upper bearing ran hot this morning.”

“Not in the log.”

“Night hand told me at shift change.”

I looked at the shift sheet.

Written there.

Her new handoff system.

Forty was enough.

We tested.

Found the fault.

No argument.

At the end I returned the gauge.

Signed it in.

Sera said, “You can keep a district key to the cage if Halden approves.”

I looked at her.

“Why?”

“You use it enough.”

“Thought you liked paperwork.”

“I like knowing who can open things.”

Different.

I understood.

Mostly.

“Thank you.”

Sera nodded.

I almost apologized for the Dera sentence.

Months late.

Still possible.

“Also, I was an ass about the chamber access.”

“Yes.”

“I was going to apologize.”

“You did.”

“That was not enough response.”

“What do you want?”

“Something difficult.”

“No.”

I laughed.

There it was.

I did not need Sera to become Dera.

I needed East Four to remain a station after the person who taught me it left.

That required someone new enough to change the locks.
