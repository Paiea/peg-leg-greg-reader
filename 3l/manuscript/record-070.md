## RECORD 070

## THE ACCOUNTANT

The first time I put a price beside displacement, I wanted to erase it.

Eren Pol would not let me.

Good accountant.

Bad comfort.

River Four had exposed a problem bigger than one deferred repair.

We had no shared method for comparing unlike risks.

Structural urgency.

Service interruption.

Injury probability.

Household displacement.

Lost wages.

Replacement cost.

Emergency response time.

Political pressure, which everyone pretended was not a category while behaving as though it was.

Regional office wanted a standard worksheet.

I objected.

“Worksheets become truth.”

Eren said, “So do instincts.”

Annoying.

Nessa sided with him.

Of course.

“Your instincts already rank things,” she said.

“I know.”

“Then write the ranking down where other people can argue.”

That was the point.

Not objective numbers.

Visible assumptions.

We built the first version in a room with four professions and no agreement.

Eren: finance.

Tova: structure.

Nessa: operations.

Me: field review and excessive concern.

Pellin attended one meeting, realized nobody needed historical translation, and escaped.

Wise man.

We started with easy numbers.

Repair cost.

Replacement cost.

Estimated downtime.

Number of connected buildings.

Alternate capacity.

Crew availability.

Then harder.

Expected injury consequence.

Not probability alone.

Consequence if failure occurred.

Minor.

Severe.

Multiple casualties.

I hated the boxes.

Tova hated them too.

Still useful.

Then displacement.

How many households might need temporary relocation if service failed during winter?

For how long?

Cost per household?

There it was.

People becoming numbers.

Eren wrote twelve silver average temporary housing and transport per household for a one-week event.

I said, “That's obscene.”

“The cost?”

“The sentence.”

“Should I remove housing?”

“No.”

“Transport?”

“No.”

“Then?”

I did not have a better arithmetic language.

That was the discomfort.

If we refused to price displacement because human life resisted numbers, budgets would price it as zero.

Worse.

We kept the line.

Then added notes for nonfinancial burden.

School interruption.

Care disruption.

Accessibility.

Season.

Whether residents had realistic alternatives.

Eren said, “You are making the worksheet longer than the repair reports.”

“Yes.”

“Expected.”

The first model produced nonsense.

Good.

A high-revenue market branch outranked three residential supports because downtime losses were easy to price and household harm was conservative.

Nessa looked at the result.

“No.”

Eren said, “The arithmetic is correct.”

“The model is wrong.”

“How?”

“Because it thinks lost merchant revenue and a cold apartment are comparable only through money.”

Eren nodded.

He was not defending the result.

Good accountant.

We separated categories.

Safety.

Essential service.

Displacement burden.

Economic loss.

Asset cost.

Recoverability.

No single total score at first.

That annoyed administrators.

They wanted ranking.

Of course.

We eventually created one.

But the component scores remained visible.

A project could rank high because of injury risk or because many low-income households lacked alternatives.

Different reasons.

Same queue position sometimes.

Important.

Then came political value.

Not named that.

Visibility.

A failure at the royal market produced more public attention than the same outage in Rope Street.

Everyone knew.

Nobody wanted it in the model.

Eren said, “If we exclude it, decision-makers add it later invisibly.”

Dera, attending that meeting, said, “Then make them write it separately.”

Brilliant.

We created OVERRIDE FACTORS.

Any final ranking change outside the model required written reason.

Festival.

Royal visit.

Military need.

Legal obligation.

Political commitment.

Whatever.

Not forbidden.

Named.

Sunlight by paperwork.

Some officials hated it.

That suggested usefulness.

The first live use ranked nine projects.

River Four repair came second.

Market Seven archive access seventh.

A school heating branch first because failure probability moderate but winter consequence severe and no alternate heat.

Nobody objected much.

Suspicious.

Then a trade district project ranked sixth despite enormous economic value because alternate capacity was strong.

Merchants objected loudly.

Also useful.

They argued lost production should count more.

They were not wrong.

We adjusted economic recovery assumptions upward based on actual closure data.

Project moved to fifth.

Not first.

Compromise through better numbers.

The model never became neutral.

That was important.

Weighting severe injury above asset loss is a value judgment.

Weighting displacement burden above temporary commercial inconvenience is a value judgment.

Counting lost wages is a value judgment.

Not counting unpaid household labor would also be one.

We added it imperfectly.

Nessa insisted.

“If schools close, somebody stays home with children.”

“Can we price that?” Eren asked.

“Poorly.”

“Better than zero?”

“Yes.”

That became the standard.

Poorly but visibly can be better than absent.

One afternoon Eren showed me two versions of a district budget.

Same projects.

Same total money.

Version A maximized expected financial loss avoided.

Version B maximized combined safety and essential-service risk reduction.

Different winners.

“Which is correct?” he asked.

“B.”

He smiled.

“Why?”

I stopped.

Trap.

“Because I value those outcomes more.”

“Good.”

Not mathematically correct.

Morally chosen.

Then defendable.

That lesson mattered later more than I knew.

Not because this worksheet became the machine that hurt people decades later.

It did not.

Different tools.

Different people.

Do not make every early system secretly guilty of the future.

This one often helped.

But it taught me how easily numbers inherit values while pretending only to describe reality.

A clean table can hide an argument better than a speech.

So we kept the arguments visible.

Version history.

Weights.

Override reasons.

Data source.

Confidence where meaningful.

Unknown where not.

The worksheet spread.

Three regions adopted it.

Two modified it.

One removed the displacement category because data collection was too difficult.

Nessa sent them a letter sharp enough to cut skin.

They restored a simplified version.

Influence.

Messy.

Years later, people would ask me when I first understood that budgets were safety systems.

There was no single moment.

Maybe River Four.

Maybe the grant.

Maybe payroll.

Maybe my mother's medicine.

Money kept showing up wherever values became recurring obligations.

Eventually even I noticed.
