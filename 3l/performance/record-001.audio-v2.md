# RECORD 001 AUDIO PERFORMANCE V2

Status: experimental listening spike. Canon prose remains `../manuscript/record-001.md`.

## Voice contract

- GREG includes narration and Greg dialogue: `deep`.
- DRAGON: `fancy`, then pitch-shift `-3.25` semitones with tempo `1.0` and formants preserved.
- Every block is generated speaker-pure. Never generate mixed-speaker takes.
- `pause_after_ms` is inserted by the assembler after the block. It is not spoken punctuation.
- Same-speaker continuation blocks may use 70-90 ms. Normal speaker turns use roughly 140-360 ms. Dramatic beats are longer.

## Blocks

### 001 GREG · pause_after_ms=240

The dragon yawns while I am explaining how many people are going to die.

It is not a small yawn. The head lifts from the shelf of black stone, gold catches behind the teeth, and heat rolls across the floor.

I stop talking. Not because I am afraid. I am afraid, obviously. I'm just also offended.

“Am I boring you?”

One eye opens. It is larger than the shield I left outside. I left the shield because carrying one into a dragon's den felt optimistic to the point of disrespect.

### 002 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=220

“You have been speaking for seven minutes.”

### 003 GREG · pause_after_ms=200

“About a kingdom collapsing. Millions of people. That was the part where you were supposed to care.”

### 004 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=360

“I heard the number. No.”

### 005 GREG · pause_after_ms=220

The voice does not boom.

I expected booming. Stories always make dragons boom. Fire, a roar, rocks falling, somebody pisses himself.

This one speaks quietly enough that I have to listen. Which may be worse.

I look toward the way I came. The passage is narrow, dark, uphill for half a mile, then downhill for three, through sulfur vents, then across a shelf where the wind tried very hard to remove me from the mountain.

“Do you know what happens if the western anchors fail?”

### 006 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=200

“No. I heard you describe the Line. I do not know what happens if the anchors fail.”

### 007 GREG · pause_after_ms=220

That gets me.

“The entire western Line collapses. Then the inner stations overload. Then you get pressure events from here to the coast. Two point three million people live between here and there.”

### 008 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=220

“You told me two point one. I assume someone counted again.”

### 009 GREG · pause_after_ms=220

“Spring census.”

I stare at it. It isn't fucking with me. It actually does not care.

I had spent months imagining this conversation. In some versions I died before the cave. In others, shortly after reaching it. One particularly stupid version involved making a good enough argument that the dragon respected me.

I blame exhaustion for that one.

“Cities disappear. Farms go with them. Trade stops. People starve.”

### 010 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=280

“I understand. Cities disappear. Farms disappear. Trade stops for a time. Many people starve.

You are describing consequence as though its size obligates me.”

### 011 GREG · pause_after_ms=240

I had expected greed. Pride. Threats. Maybe an ancient grudge I could use. Even the bad versions had involved an argument.

I had not planned for indifference.

The dragon shifts one foreclaw. Stone grinds beneath it.

I have killed things large enough that their skulls became tavern decorations.

This thing could use one as a ring.

“Do you understand what a kingdom is?”

### 012 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=180

“Do you?

You speak of your kingdom as though it is a mountain. Before it, there were three others in the valley you call its heart. One lasted eighty-one years. One lasted four hundred and twelve. The oldest lasted somewhat longer than yours has so far. It was larger. Its people were certain this indicated permanence.

They cut roads through mountains. They redirected a river. They built seven towers where your capital now stands.”

### 013 GREG · pause_after_ms=180

I don't answer.

“There aren't seven towers.”

### 014 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=340

“No. The next kingdom used two as granaries and left the others empty. Their grandchildren pulled them down for stone.

People needed walls more than monuments.”

### 015 GREG · pause_after_ms=180

I look toward the mouth of the cavern.

“You're saying let everybody die because somebody else will build houses there eventually.”

### 016 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=180

“I said no such thing. You are upset because you believe the size of the consequence obligates me.

Why?”

### 017 GREG · pause_after_ms=180

I laugh once. I can't help it.

“Because millions of people dying is bad. To them. To me.”

### 018 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=320

“Good. To you. That is more useful.”

### 019 GREG · pause_after_ms=220

I stop.

Something changes in the dragon's look. Not interest yet. Maybe the possibility of it.

That is more than I had five minutes ago.

“I didn't climb a mountain so we could discuss my feelings. The Line is failing. You can stop it.”

### 020 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=280

“Can I? That is different from you thinking I can.

Why do you think so?”

### 021 GREG · pause_after_ms=70

There it is. The first opening.

I straighten a little. My back hurts.

Fifty-nine is not old enough to make every movement a negotiation, but I've had help. The left knee. Three ribs that set badly. A shoulder that clicks when the weather changes. Two fingers that never closed properly after a gate crushed them.

B-class. Ranker. King's commendation. Five named contracts.

Sounds impressive.

It is impressive.

Just not to me.

### 022 GREG · pause_after_ms=420

I used to be better. Enough better that sometimes I wake from dreams with the old body's instincts and spend half a second confused by how slow my hand moves.

“The western Line wasn't built as a wall. The anchors don't keep pressure out. They move it. West to east at first, then down.

The lower channels haven't been maintained because nobody knows they exist anymore. The ninth station is failing because the eastern sink is full. Rebuild every surface anchor and it'll still break again.”

### 023 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=360

“Continue.”

### 024 GREG · pause_after_ms=200

I almost do.

Then I remember why I am here.

“No. You help me with the Line. Then I'll tell you the rest.”

### 025 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=160

“The rest of what?”

### 026 GREG · pause_after_ms=160

“What I know. About the channels, and other things.”

### 027 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=180

“You believe you possess information I desire.

How many other things?”

### 028 GREG · pause_after_ms=360

I shrug.

“That depends what you know.”

The dragon is quiet again. Longer this time.

I shift my weight off the bad knee. Then back. Then off again.

There is a stone near the wall that looks almost like a seat if I am willing to gamble on etiquette.

I remain standing.

### 029 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=180

“You are unusual. You are negotiating from a position in which I can kill you, and you have nevertheless withheld information.

You assume I cannot extract it?”

### 030 GREG · pause_after_ms=220

I decide lying would be insulting.

“I assume you might. But if you kill me, you may lose something you want.”

The sensible version of this conversation disappeared a while ago.

“I came anyway because I thought you would listen.”

### 031 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=450

“That is the important part.

The kingdom is what you wish me to act upon. It is not what brought you here.

You believed I would listen because you possess knowledge you should not possess.

That belief existed before the Line began failing.”

### 032 GREG · pause_after_ms=70

For the first time since I entered the cavern, I don't have an answer ready.

My mouth goes dry.

The dragon lowers its head toward my level. It still has to stop far above it.

I had known the dragon existed. Roughly where. I knew the old records were wrong about its color. I knew fire would not be the primary danger. I knew not to take the northern shelf. I knew where the sulfur vents were.

I had known enough to get here.

Forty years ago, I had known more.

### 033 GREG · pause_after_ms=70

Or maybe I had only thought I did.

Memory is funny when you get old enough.

People imagine important things stay sharp. They don't.

Sometimes I can remember the pattern on a woman's cup but not what she said when she left. I can remember the smell of a room I slept in twice and lose an entire winter. I remember techniques that took me years to learn with a clarity that still lives in my wrists.

### 034 GREG · pause_after_ms=420

Names go first. Dates get slippery. Pain stays. Faces stay longer than they should.

And some things just sit there forever for no reason you can explain.

Like the number of steps between the third and fourth gates of a city that doesn't exist yet.

Or didn't.

Or won't.

Depending how you count.

### 035 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=200

“You knew the lower channels exist. The surface builders did not. The present keepers do not. The kings who inherited them did not.

You are not old enough to have known those who did, and you are not powerful enough to have entered the places where that knowledge remained.

What rank are you?”

### 036 GREG · pause_after_ms=180

“That's rude.”

The dragon waits.

“B. Low B, depending who is doing the paperwork.”

### 037 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=150

“And you came here alone?”

### 038 GREG · pause_after_ms=180

“Mostly. The horse made it pretty far.”

The dragon stares at me.

I wait.

Nothing.

“Tough room.”

### 039 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=140

“What happened to the horse?”

### 040 GREG · pause_after_ms=140

“Cliff.”

### 041 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=180

“Ah.

Who taught you about the channels?”

### 042 GREG · pause_after_ms=140

That may be the closest thing to sympathy I get.

“Nobody.”

### 043 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=120

“That is false.”

### 044 GREG · pause_after_ms=180

“No. It's incomplete. Help me with the Line and I'll complete it.”

### 045 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=420

“No. You have mistaken the transaction.

I do not require what you know about the Line. The Line is old. Old things interest me. That does not mean your knowledge purchases my attention.

You believe the information is valuable because it is hidden. Hidden things are common. Age creates hidden things without effort.

The channels are old. Their secrecy is ordinary.

You are not.”

### 046 GREG · pause_after_ms=180

I don't like the way it says that.

Its gaze moves over me. Boots ruined from the climb. Coat patched twice. Rank pin still attached because apparently I am vain enough to keep wearing it in front of a creature that watched kingdoms turn into quarries. Sword at my side. One cheap knife. One expensive knife. A pack with three days of food left.

A body that used to know how to do things this one never learned.

“I'm fifty-nine. Approximately.”

The pupil tightens.

“Long story.”

### 047 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=80

“You favor the left knee when you stand. When I raised my head, you put that leg behind you and turned your right side away from my mouth. You did it before you thought. That is training your present body cannot properly execute.

You recognized the lower-channel structure from markings you should not understand.”

### 048 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=280

“Your coat carries sulfur from the second ridge, but your boots do not. You crossed above the vents. There is no white grit from the northern shelf.

You knew where not to walk.

Who brought you here before? Who described the route? Who showed you the Line beneath the Line?”

### 049 GREG · pause_after_ms=380

Fuck.

I keep my face still.

I had planned for greed. I had planned for threats. I had planned to trade information one piece at a time until the dragon decided saving a kingdom was cheaper than killing me.

I had not planned for it to care where the information came from.

There's a crack running through the stone between my boots. Very old. Probably older than my kingdom. Maybe older than people.

### 050 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=160

“Greg.”

### 051 GREG · pause_after_ms=220

My head snaps up.

I never gave it my name.

Then I glance down.

Right. Rank pin. Name under it.

“Fuck you.”

### 052 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=160

The dragon smiles.

“Greg. Who told you how the Line works?”

### 053 GREG · pause_after_ms=150

I breathe out.

“Nobody.”

### 054 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=320

“Then how do you know?”

### 055 GREG · pause_after_ms=700

I look at the dragon. At the cave. At the thing I crossed half a kingdom and one very unfortunate horse to find.

There are answers that sound insane because they are insane. Others become worse when you try to make them reasonable.

I have had forty years to find a good way to explain this.

I never did.

So I give it the short version.

“I've seen it fail.”

### 056 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=300

“When?”

### 057 GREG · pause_after_ms=850

“Before.”

### 058 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=420

“The Line has not failed in your lifetime.”

### 059 GREG · pause_after_ms=350

The silence that follows is the first silence in the cave that feels like mine.

“I know.”

### 060 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=420

Then it asks, quietly:

“How?”

### 061 GREG · pause_after_ms=500

I smile despite myself.

Finally.

A question I know how to answer.

“I was there.”

### 062 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=500

The dragon does not blink.

“No. You were not.”

### 063 GREG · pause_after_ms=2000

“Not this time.”
