# RECORD 001 AUDIO PERFORMANCE V4 · PAST-TENSE LONG-TURN DRAGON

Status: experimental listening spike. Canon prose on `main` remains unchanged.

## Reproducible voice contract

- GREG includes narration and Greg dialogue: `deep`.
- DRAGON: `fancy`, then pitch-shift `-3.25` semitones with `tempo=1.0`, formants preserved.
- Every generated clip is speaker-pure. Never generate mixed-speaker takes.
- Dragon rule: silence/nonverbal observation or a substantial speech. No tiny conversational Dragon replies in this experiment.
- Narration is past tense.
- Pauses are assembler metadata, not spoken punctuation.
- Same-speaker technical continuation: 80 ms.
- Dramatic turn pauses are encoded per block below.
- Final settling tail is included in block 017 pause metadata (2000 ms).

### 001 GREG · pause_after_ms=320

The dragon yawned while I was explaining how many people were going to die.

It was not a small yawn.

Its head lifted from the shelf of black stone where it had been resting, and the cave changed shape around it. Gold caught somewhere behind the teeth. Heat rolled across the floor in a slow wave.

I stopped talking.

Not because I was afraid.

I was afraid, obviously.

I was just also offended.

“Am I boring you?”

One eye opened. It was larger than the shield I had left outside.

I left the shield because I had spent six days getting there and decided carrying one into a dragon's den was optimistic to the point of disrespect.

The eye studied me.

### 003 GREG · pause_after_ms=320

That was not the response I had prepared for.

“Millions of people.”

The dragon did not answer.

Its eye closed again.

The voice had not boomed. I had expected booming. Stories always made dragons boom. Fire. Roaring. Falling rocks. Somebody pissed himself.

This one spoke quietly enough that I had to listen.

Which might have been worse.

I looked toward the passage behind me. Six days in. Probably eight out, because my left knee had started complaining.

I tried again.

“Cities disappear. Farms go with them. Trade stops. People starve.”

Nothing.

The eye opened eventually.

“Do you understand what a kingdom is?”

### 004 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=620

Do you?

You speak of your kingdom as though it is a mountain. It is not.

Before it, there were three others in the valley you call its heart. One lasted eighty-one years. One lasted four hundred and twelve. The oldest lasted somewhat longer than yours has so far. It was larger. Its people were certain this indicated permanence.

They cut roads through mountains. They redirected a river. They built seven towers where your capital now stands.

The next kingdom used two as granaries and left the others empty.

Their grandchildren pulled them down for stone.

People needed walls more than monuments.

### 005 GREG · pause_after_ms=320

I did not answer for a second.

“There aren't seven towers.”

The dragon watched me.

No amusement. No anger.

Just waiting.

That shut me up more effectively than an answer would have.

I rubbed my face.

“So what? Let everybody die because somebody else will build houses there eventually?”

Still nothing.

That got under my skin.

“Because millions of people dying is bad,” I said.

Nothing.

“To them.”

The pupil tightened slightly.

I tried the part that suddenly felt more embarrassing.

“To me.”

That changed something.

Not much.

The head lifted half an inch.

But after enough time being stared at by something the size of a building, you noticed half an inch.

### 006 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=620

To you.

That is more useful.

A number cannot obligate me. A kingdom cannot obligate me merely because it has survived long enough for its people to mistake age for permanence.

But you crossed half a kingdom, ruined your boots, aggravated an already damaged knee, and walked into my home expecting me to listen.

That tells me something about you.

So stop telling me how many will die.

Tell me why you believed I would listen.

### 007 GREG · pause_after_ms=380

### 012 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=700

### 013 GREG · pause_after_ms=800

Not interested.

Still.

### 014 DRAGON · pause_after_ms=900

The Line has not failed in your lifetime. The lower failure you described has not occurred during the existence of your kingdom.

If you found an account of it, the account would require technical knowledge your own archives do not contain. If you reconstructed it independently, you would require access you do not possess. If another person taught you, that person would present the same problem you do.

You did not describe a prediction. You described maintenance failures with the irritation of someone remembering them.

So the useful question is not whether you have seen the Line fail.

It is when you saw it, and how.
