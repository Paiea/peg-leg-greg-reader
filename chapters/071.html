PEG-LEG GREG
ART
CHAPTER 71

THE COUNTER

The first question was stairs. This pleased me. Not because stairs were good. Because I knew the question. The Guild clerk looked at the spice-storage note.

“Ground floor.”

“Step?”

“One at front.”

“Height?”

He stared.

“Normal.”

“Useless unit.”

“Greg.”

“Fine. Chair?”

“Yes.”

“Table?”

“Yes.”

“Privy?”

“Rear yard.”

“Steps?”

“I don't know.”

There. Unknown. My chest tightened. Ridiculous. Privy step. Not drainage plate.

“Can someone retrieve stock?”

“The owner has a warehouse boy.”

“Do I need to move between shelves?”

“Second count is supposed to verify ledger against stock.”

“So yes.”

“Unless the boy brings stock marks to you.”

“Marks?”

“Lot tags. Seals. Weights where relevant.”

“Could he bring representative containers?”

“Ask owner.”

“Expected duration?”

“Two hours.”

“Pay?”

“Three copper.”

“Why Bronze?”

“Owner wants someone Guild-listed who can sign the discrepancy sheet. Literate Copper also accepted.”

“Why second count?”

“First count disagrees with ledger by enough that owner wants independent verification before accusing anyone of stealing cinnamon.”

“Cinnamon?”

“Among other things.”

Interesting. No. Relevant.

“Any lifting required?”

“Not in posting.”

“Could become required?”

The clerk looked at me.

“Anything could become required.”

There. My stomach tightened again. He did not mean anything profound. He meant warehouses. I knew that.

“What?”

he asked.

“Nothing.”

“You taking it?”

I looked at the note. Ground floor. One front step. Chair. Table. Rear privy unknown. Warehouse boy. Two hours. Three copper. No listed lifting. Enough? What about fire exits? Floor condition? Aisle width? Chair height? Distance to privy?

Whether owner would decide independent meant I personally touched every sack? Whether the warehouse boy was competent? Whether cinnamon dust made me sneeze while balancing? That last one was stupid. Probably.

“What if I inspect first?”

The clerk shrugged.

“Owner's there.”

“Job stays open?”

“Until someone takes it.”

Of course.

“How long?”

He shrugged again. The board did not owe me time. I looked at the posting. Then at my crutches.

“Where?”

“West Spice. Marrow Street.”

Closer than my lodging to Guild.

“Cart access?”

“Yes.”

Enough. Maybe.

“I'll inspect.”

“That's not taking it.”

“I know.”

The clerk put the slip back. Good. No ceremony.

*

Sera checked the wound before I left. I had hired Mira's boy again. Two copper to Guild. He had accepted one copper from Guild to take me onward to Marrow Street because he already had a delivery west. This felt like cheating. It was called routing.

People did it with freight constantly. Apparently I was freight now. No. Passenger. Better. Sera unwrapped the limb. The questionable margin looked less questionable. Still bruised. Still swollen. Still sutured. Still mine.

“No fever?”

“No.”

“Drainage?”

“Less.”

“Pain?”

“Same when moving. Less at rest.”

“Phantom?”

“Annoying.”

“Useful medical unit.”

“I learned from you.”

She pressed around the wound. One spot hurt sharply. I swore.

“Expected.”

“Bad expected.”

“Normal expected.”

“Worse.”

She looked at the skin edge.

“If this continues, I think we're past the highest revision concern.”

I stopped.

“Past?”

“Highest concern.”

“Not zero.”

“No.”

“Infection?”

“Still possible.”

“Breakdown?”

“Still possible.”

“Bone?”

“Stable.”

“Knee?”

“Good.”

There. I breathed.

“Can I work two hours seated?”

Sera looked at me.

“What work?”

“Inventory count.”

“Seated?”

“Mostly.”

“Travel?”

“Cart.”

“Lifting?”

“No.”

“Mostly?”

“Unknown.”

She stared.

“No lifting.”

“I can say no.”

“Yes.”

“Then?”

“Take breaks. Keep the limb protected. If pain rises significantly, swelling increases, dressing gets wet or dirty, you leave.”

“Define significantly.”

“No.”

“Sera.”

“You know what more pain means.”

“I know what different pain means.”

“Good. Use that.”

I disliked subjective thresholds. Unfortunately I owned a body.

“Crutches?”

“Yes.”

“Stairs?”

“One.”

“Fine.”

“Privy unknown.”

She looked at me.

“Then find out.”

Good. No prohibition. No blessing. Information.

“Can I take it?”

“I am not your employer.”

Excellent. I left.

*

West Spice Storage had one step. Normal. The clerk had been right. I hated him for it. The building was long and low with a painted red pepper over the door. No actual sign text. Inside smelled like twenty kitchens fighting. Pepper. Cinnamon. Clove. Dried citrus. Something bitter. Something smoky. My nose immediately itched. Fuck. Not prophecy. Dust. The owner was a woman named Mara Fen.

Late forties maybe. Gray at the temples. Sleeves rolled. No patience visible. She looked at the crutches. Then at the Guild badge. Then at me.

“You're the second count?”

“Maybe.”

She frowned.

“Maybe?”

“I want to see the work first.”

“Guild said you had first refusal.”

“Yes.”

“Do you want it?”

“Probably.”

“That is a terrible answer.”

“I know.”

Good. She pointed to a table near the front. Chair. High enough. Good. Ledger. First counter's sheet. Three narrow columns. Lot. Ledger amount. Physical amount. Variance.

“Problem?”

I asked.

“Five variances.”

“Large?”

“Two small. Three not.”

“Possible causes?”

“Receiving error. Issue not recorded. Wrong lot mark. Bad first count. Theft.”

“Order?”

“No.”

Good. Warehouse boy appeared. Sixteen. Maybe seventeen. Name Toma. Thin. Spice dust on sleeves. Mara said, “He can bring tags and open lots.”

“Can he lift?”

Toma looked offended.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

“Can you?”

Mara asked.

“No.”

There. Easy. Nobody died. Mara nodded.

“Then don't.”

Also easy. I looked around. Front table maybe twenty feet from door. Aisles behind. Packed shelves. Sacks low. Jars high. Crates. Floor mostly level plank. One warped board near second aisle. I noticed. Of course.

“Privy?”

“Back yard.”

“Steps?”

“Two down.”

“Rail?”

“No.”

There. Could manage? Three practice steps with rail. Two without. Not yet maybe.

“Alternative?”

Mara stared.

“For pissing?”

“Yes.”

“Bucket.”

Toma laughed. Mara did not.

“Private?”

“Back office.”

Fine. Not ideal. Workable.

“What else?”

Mara asked. I looked at the ceiling. No. Stop. Not mill frame. Spice count. I looked at table. Chair. Toma. Ledger. Front step. Bucket if needed. Two hours. Three copper.

“No lifting?”

“No lifting.”

“If I need to see a lot physically?”

“Toma brings it or you go look.”

“I may not be able to go look.”

“Then Toma brings it.”

“Independent count?”

“You tell him what to bring. He doesn't tell you what number to write.”

Good.

“What if tag is attached to something too large?”

“We move the tag if possible or you come later.”

Reasonable. I felt another question. Then another. Lighting. Scales. Seal integrity. Who had keys. Receiving schedule. Whether stock would move during count. That one mattered.

“Any stock movement while I count?”

Mara paused.

“Normally yes.”

“Can you freeze the five disputed lots?”

“Yes.”

“Do that.”

She nodded. Good question.

Then:

“Who has access?”

Mara's eyes narrowed.

“Why?”

“If we're checking theft.”

“We're not checking theft. We're checking count.”

Correct. I had expanded the problem.

“Right.”

“Taking it?”

I looked at the table. Three copper. Two hours. Maybe more. Could do. Not guaranteed. Enough.

“Yes.”

Mara held out the Guild slip. I signed. Gregory. Still objectionable.

*

The first variance was not interesting. This disappointed me. Ledger said twelve sealed jars. Physical count said eleven. Toma brought eleven jars. We counted. Eleven.

“Any broken?”

Mara checked receiving notes. One issued to kitchen supplier yesterday. Issue sheet not copied to main ledger. Solved. Not theft. Not first counter. Paper lag. I marked it. Second variance was two sacks of dried pepper. Ledger weight and physical weight differed by a small amount.

First counter had used gross sack weight. Ledger tracked net spice weight. Solved. I marked it. This was easy. Too easy. My body remained annoying. Sitting with the residual limb unsupported increased throbbing. I shifted. No good.

Chair too high for the limb to rest comfortably, but lowering myself was not possible. I put another chair sideways and rested my left thigh near the edge without pressure on the wound. Better. Mara noticed. Said nothing. Good. Third variance. Cinnamon. Of course. Ledger: eight small crates. Physical: eight. Weight: wrong. Toma brought the lot tags. All matched.

“Scale?”

I asked.

“Back aisle.”

Fuck.

“Can it come here?”

Mara said, “No.” Large platform scale. Of course.

“Can a smaller scale?”

“For spice?”

“Yes.”

“Not accurate enough at crate weight.”

Could Toma weigh and report? Independent? Maybe. I needed to see the reading. Could move table? No. Could move chair near scale? Yes. Could I get there? Aisle. Warped board. Twenty-five feet. Flat. Crutches. Possible. I stood. Mara said, “You don't have to.”

“I know.”

Good. Crutches. Hands. Right foot. The first ten feet fine. Then spice dust on floor. Not slippery exactly. I tested one crutch. Fine. Warped board. I stepped around. Toma watched too closely.

“Stop watching.”

“Sorry.”

“Don't be sorry. Carry the ledger.”

He took it. Useful. At the scale I needed somewhere to sit. There was a crate. Low. No.

“Chair.”

Toma brought one. Good. I sat. He loaded first cinnamon crate. Weight. Second. Third. By fourth, I was interested. All eight were under ledger weight by nearly the same amount. Not random loss.

“Same crate type?”

Mara said, “Yes.”

“Supplier?”

“Yes.”

“Received together?”

“Yes.”

“Ledger entered from supplier invoice or local weight?”

Mara stopped.

“Invoice.”

There.

“Receiving weight?”

“Usually spot-check.”

“This lot?”

She looked through receiving sheet.

“No mark.”

“Then maybe supplier crate nominal weight differs from your assumed tare.”

Mara frowned.

“Tare is standard.”

“Crates standard?”

“Toma.”

He brought an empty cinnamon crate from same supplier. We weighed it. Heavier than Mara's standard tare by enough to explain most of the difference. There. Not genius. Accounting.

“Why heavier?”

Mara inspected it.

“Different wood.”

“Supplier changed crates?”

“Apparently.”

“Then physical spice may match invoice net.”

“Need open weight.”

“Yes.”

She opened one sealed crate. Weighed contents separately. Close. Variance shrank to normal handling loss. Solved enough. Mara swore. Not at me. At supplier. Good.

“Update tare by supplier crate type,” I said.

“I know.”

Of course she did. I shut up. Fourth variance required me to look at shelf marks because two lots had nearly identical numbers. I could have sent Toma. I wanted to see. This was where pride hid. Not dramatic. Small.

“I'll go.”

Mara said, “Why?”

“Because I want to see the marks.”

“Toma can bring them.”

“Labels attached to shelf?”

“Yes.”

“Then I go.”

Could ask him to copy. Could ask Mara. Could move labels. No need. I could go. Was that reason enough? Maybe. I stood. My right thigh already tired. Hands wrapped. Shoulders sore. Twenty feet. Fine.

At the shelf, I saw the problem immediately. Not because I was brilliant. Because the two lot marks differed by one letter and the older ink had bled.

R7B.

R78.

Or R7B. Both.

“Who writes these?”

“Toma.”

He looked guilty.

“Not theft.”

Mara said, “Obviously.”

“Could use slash through seven.”

Toma said, “What?” I demonstrated on scrap. Different numeral form.

“Or separate letters from numbers.”

Mara nodded.

“Do that.”

Small change. Good. Then I turned. My crutch tip caught the edge of a pepper sack. Not hard. Enough to shift. My body tried left foot. Again. Hip dropped. I caught with hands. Toma grabbed my elbow. I froze. Not fall. Not wheel. Sack. Pepper. My heart went fast anyway.

“I'm fine.”

Toma released immediately. Good. I looked at the sack. It had been slightly into aisle. Normal warehouse. Could ask every sack moved. Could demand clear width. Could inspect entire floor. Could leave. My mind opened all doors at once. No. I moved the sack six inches with the crutch tip. Then stopped. Bad. Using crutch as tool could destabilize.

“Toma.”

He moved it.

“Keep this aisle clear while I'm here.”

“Yes.”

Proportional. Maybe. My hands shook a little. I waited. Mara watched.

“Done?”

“No.”

“Need break?”

“Yes.”

There. Easy word. We returned to table. I drank water. Five minutes. Nobody complained. The job waited. This was almost suspicious.

*

The fifth variance was the first counter's error. Probably. Ledger said twenty-four packets of clove. Physical count said twenty-six. We counted. Twenty-four. Toma counted. Twenty-four. Mara counted. Twenty-four.

“Why twenty-six?”

I looked at first sheet. Two lines below: dried orange packets, quantity two. Column alignment. The first counter had likely carried the two into the wrong line while copying. Could still be another explanation. But simplest.

“Who was first counter?”

Mara asked. I read the signature. Leni Voss. Copper. I did not know her.

“Doesn't matter.”

“It matters if she can't count.”

“She got four other lines right enough to expose actual ledger issues.”

Mara looked at me. Good.

“She probably copied wrong.”

“Probably?”

“Yes.”

“Can you sign that?”

“I can sign that second count is twenty-four and likely transcription error. I cannot sign her brain.”

Mara smiled for the first time.

“Fine.”

We finished in one hour and forty minutes. Not two. Good. I had spent maybe twenty minutes moving. Most seated. Still exhausted. This offended me. My right leg had done little. My arms some. My body was healing. That was work too. Unpaid. Annoying. Mara signed the Guild sheet. Three copper. She paid directly. I counted it. Of course.

“Would you do this again?”

she asked. I stopped. There. A future question.

“Maybe.”

She laughed.

“Terrible answer.”

“Yes.”

“Why maybe?”

I looked at the warehouse. Chair. Aisles. Sacks. Scale. Two steps to privy I had avoided by using bucket once. Not discussing that.

“Depends on the job.”

“That's how jobs work.”

Oh. Right. Not disability wisdom. Employment.

“Then yes. Depending.”

“Better.”

She turned to Toma.

“Fix lot marks.”

He groaned. Life continued.

*

The cart back to Guild was not waiting. I had not arranged one. Mistake. Not catastrophe. I could hire another. Marrow Street had carts. I sat on a crate outside West Spice while Toma went to find one. Dependence. Useful. Sun. Spice smell in my clothes. Three copper earned. Four copper transport today if same price. Net negative? No, Guild to Marrow had been partly covered.

Still. Work economics suddenly included mobility. Important. Not existential. Could negotiate future jobs with transport? Maybe. Could choose closer work. Could walk more as strength improved. Could Guild cover travel if assignment required? Maybe. Questions. Not today. Toma returned with a small passenger cart.

“One copper to Guild.”

Better. I paid. At Guild, I turned in the signed discrepancy sheet. The clerk looked at it.

“You took it.”

“Yes.”

“How?”

“What?”

“Job.”

“Seated mostly.”

“No, I mean how was it?”

“Oh.”

“Fine.”

“Owner happy?”

“Ask owner.”

“Any problems?”

I thought. Crutch caught sack. Fatigue. Privy. Scale location. Nothing reportable as Guild issue.

“No.”

He marked completion. That was it. One line. Three copper. No trumpet. Good.

“Want similar if posted?”

he asked. There. I almost said yes. Then no. Then maybe.

“Ask me.”

He frowned.

“What?”

“If something similar appears, ask me. Don't reserve it.”

“Fine.”

Perfect. Not a stream. Not role. Just ask.

*

Sera did not need to see me again that afternoon. Good. I wanted to go home. Temporary home. Storage room. Fine. Mira's boy was not available. I hired another cart. Two copper. This day was becoming expensive. At lodging, the keeper looked at my spice-stained shirt.

“You worked.”

“Yes.”

“Stupid?”

“Possibly.”

“Hungry?”

“Yes.”

She put stew on table. Good. I hooked crutches over chair back. Her system. Sat. My whole body hurt in the satisfying and unsatisfying ways simultaneously. Hands. Shoulders. Right thigh. Residual limb. Back. Brain. I ate.

“Pay?”

she asked.

“Three copper.”

“Cart?”

“Five copper total today.”

She laughed. I stared.

“Fuck.”

“First day.”

“Yes.”

“Could have stayed home and earned two copper.”

“That's not how arithmetic works.”

“It is today.”

I laughed. Pain. Worth it. Transport economics. Fine. Not every job viable. Also not every day required two Guild trips plus inspection. Tomorrow maybe no work. Good. Probably.

*

Jorren came after dark. I told him about cinnamon tare. He listened for almost a minute.

Then:

“You paid five copper to earn three?”

“Yes.”

He laughed so hard he had to sit.

“Fuck you.”

“You're a businessman.”

“It was not about profit.”

“That makes it better.”

“Guild trip was medical anyway.”

“So allocate cost.”

I stared.

“Don't.”

“What?”

“You accidentally made a good point.”

He looked pleased.

“Medical trip cost four copper round trip regardless. Job added one copper onward and maybe two back through Guild.”

“Then you made money.”

“Maybe zero.”

“Excellent business.”

“Shut up.”

We worked it out badly. Not exact. Because return routes overlapped. Because one cart was subsidized by Guild delivery. Because I would have traveled for wound care anyway. Actual marginal job transport maybe one to three copper. Three copper pay. Financially mediocre. Not necessarily stupid. Good. I liked that. Then hated that I liked it.

“What was work like?”

Jorren asked.

“Spicy.”

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

I told him. Not everything. The sack. The scale. Chair. Mara. Toma. Lot marks. He nodded.

“You liked it.”

“I liked parts.”

“Same.”

“No.”

“Fine.”

He looked at the calendar. I had not written the spice job. Completed already. No need.

“Stairs?”

he asked.

“No.”

“Training?”

“Three.”

“Still?”

“Cleaner.”

“Good.”

No pressure. He left for the green door again. This time he asked if I wanted anything brought back.

“Food?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

“Something sweet.”

“Money?”

I gave him one copper. He left. Normal.

*

I changed clothes lying down. Easier. Still annoying. Spice dust everywhere. My phantom foot felt hot. Not pain. Temperature. As if it had been near a stove. Maybe cinnamon. Ridiculous. I washed at the kitchen table. The keeper's used-water bucket was exactly where it needed to be. I did not improve it. Growth. No. Convenience.

I dumped the cloth water into it myself by sliding the basin close and tipping while seated. Some spilled. Not much. Good enough. Back in the storage room, I opened the notebook.

CRUTCH WRAP BEFORE BLISTER.

Done.

CART TO GUILD.

Expensive.

STAIRS.

Still three.

PELL.

Not mine.

Then:

WEST SPICE SECOND COUNT.

I wrote the five variances. Paper lag. Gross versus net. Wrong tare. Ambiguous lot mark. Transcription. Ordinary errors. No conspiracy. No theft proven. No hidden rot. Just work.

Under that I wrote:

ASK ENOUGH.

Stopped. Too philosophical. Crossed out ENOUGH.

Wrote:

ASK WHAT MATTERS.

Worse. Crossed that out too. The page looked stupid. I laughed. Fine. No slogan.

I wrote:

GROUND FLOOR. CHAIR. NO LIFTING. STOCK FROZEN DURING COUNT.

Facts. Those had been enough to decide. Not all facts. Relevant ones. Mostly. The sack had still been there. I had nearly lost balance. Nothing was ever complete. My chest tightened. Then I remembered Mara asking if I would do it again. Depends on the job. That's how jobs work. I put the pencil down. Maybe the world had always been like this.

Probably. I had simply gotten very impressed with myself for noticing. That thought was rude. I refused it. For now. The calendar still had SEE ALDEN. West Storehouse DEFERRED. Upstairs remained fourteen stairs away. Tomorrow I had no job. Good.

I was tired enough to mean it. Jorren returned with a honey cake wrapped in paper. He handed it through the door.

“You're still awake.”

“Barely.”

“Successful employment.”

“Financially questionable.”

“Best kind.”

He left. I ate half the cake. Saved half. No optimization. Almost. Then I moved it farther from the edge of the stool so I would not knock it over reaching for water at night. That was not optimization. That was common sense. Probably. I lay down.

My missing foot rested on the floor. The real one was under the blanket. One job done. Three copper earned. No revelation. Good.
