PEG-LEG GREG
ART
CHAPTER 91

THE BEGINNER

Hessa sent for me the next morning.

I did not assume this meant magic.

This was maturity.

It lasted until I reached her door.

Then I assumed it meant magic.

The note had been brief.

COME AFTER BREAKFAST.
BRING NOTHING.

Bring nothing was either very good or very bad.

If she wanted me to cast, I did not need equipment.

If she wanted to tell me I was still not casting, I also did not need equipment.

Efficient woman.

I knocked.

“Come.”

Hessa was at the long table by the window with three cups, a shallow ceramic dish, and a bowl of water.

No crystals.

No wire.

No chalk.

No measuring frame.

No Barrier plates.

Nothing interesting.

I looked at the table.

Hessa looked at me.

“No.”

“I didn't ask.”

“You arrived asking.”

“That is unfair.”

“Sit.”

I sat.

The chair was worse than mine.

This was now a category of knowledge I possessed.

Hessa pushed one of the cups toward me.

Tea.

I picked it up.

“Why am I here?”

“To answer a question.”

“Mine?”

“Eventually.”

She took a drink.

I waited.

This was another skill I had been developing.

Not well.

“What question?”

Hessa put her cup down.

“Does it still feel like your foot is there?”

“Yes.”

“How often?”

“Depends.”

“On?”

“Attention. Movement. Sometimes nothing I can identify.”

“Pain?”

“Sometimes.”

“What kind?”

I looked at the shallow dish.

Empty.

“Cold. Burning. Cramping. Pressure. Position.”

“Position?”

“Yes.”

“Explain.”

“Sometimes the foot feels planted. Sometimes twisted. Sometimes like the ankle is bent. Once I woke up and felt the whole foot hit the floor.”

“After it was gone.”

“Yes.”

“Any sensation while using magic?”

“No.”

She looked at me.

I corrected.

“I haven't used magic.”

“Good.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

“Then stop sounding offended.”

“I am not offended.”

“You are.”

“I am interested aggressively.”

“That is offended with paperwork.”

I drank tea.

She was improving.

Or I was easier to mock.

Probably both.

Hessa reached for the bowl of water.

She put one finger into it.

Then withdrew it.

“Cold.”

“I can see condensation.”

“Good.”

She slid the bowl toward me.

“Right hand.”

I put two fingers in.

Cold.

“Left.”

I switched.

Cold.

“Right foot.”

I looked at her.

She looked back.

“That seems difficult.”

“Your right foot, Greg.”

“Oh.”

I took off my right shoe.

The floor was cool.

Hessa moved the bowl down.

I put my toes in.

Cold.

She nodded.

“Left foot.”

I stared at her.

“No.”

“Correct.”

I stared harder.

She pulled the bowl back onto the table.

“What was that?”

“A question.”

“That was not a question.”

“It answered one.”

“Which?”

“Whether you would try.”

I opened my mouth.

Closed it.

Opened it again.

“That is insulting.”

“It is.”

“I would not put a nonexistent foot in water.”

“You nearly stood on one.”

“That was different.”

“You tried to step on one while kissing a girl.”

“That was private.”

“Nerin told me.”

“Of course he did.”

“Medical information.”

“Then why do you know about the kissing?”

“He enjoyed that part.”

I made a note to kill Nerin.

Later.

Hessa tapped the table.

“Your body can attempt an old movement before your attention corrects it.”

“Yes.”

“I wanted to know whether instruction could do the same.”

“And?”

“You noticed.”

“Obviously.”

“Good.”

She took another drink.

I waited.

The question I wanted to ask was sitting directly behind my teeth.

I let it stay there.

Hessa noticed.

Of course.

“Ask.”

“Can I cast?”

“No.”

There it was.

I had expected it.

Still unpleasant.

“When?”

“I don't know.”

“Why not?”

“Because I don't know what changed.”

“Channels?”

“Maybe.”

“Body map?”

“Maybe.”

“Neither?”

“Maybe.”

“That is not satisfying.”

“It is accurate.”

I put my shoe back on.

This took longer than it used to.

Not much.

Enough.

Hessa waited.

No assistance.

Good.

When I finished, she said, “I can check some things without having you cast.”

I stopped.

“What things?”

“Basic channel response.”

“Now?”

“Yes.”

That was magic.

Technically.

Not casting.

Still.

My pulse increased.

Nineteen was inconvenient.

Fifty-something years of memory and the body still reacted to permission like a dog hearing a cupboard open.

“What do I do?”

“Nothing.”

“That is becoming a theme.”

“Sit square.”

I adjusted.

She came around the table.

“Hands on your thighs.”

I did.

“Close your eyes.”

“No.”

“Greg.”

“Why?”

“So you stop trying to help.”

“I can stop trying to help with my eyes open.”

“No, you can't.”

Fair.

I closed them.

The room changed.

Not magically.

Sound.

Street outside.

Cart wheel over stone.

Someone calling something about onions.

Hessa's steps.

Then her hand touched my right shoulder.

Two fingers.

Light pressure.

Nothing.

She moved to my left shoulder.

Nothing.

Upper arm.

Forearm.

Wrist.

I knew this kind of examination only broadly.

Not enough to predict it.

Good.

She touched the center of my chest.

Something moved.

Not power.

Attention.

No.

That was too vague.

I felt the familiar internal readiness that came before drawing mana, except smaller and incomplete, like hearing the first note of a song and knowing the next one without singing it.

“Don't.”

“I didn't.”

“You started.”

“I thought.”

“You started.”

I opened my eyes.

Hessa's fingers were still against my shirt.

“What did you do?”

“Very little.”

“What?”

“Close your eyes.”

I did.

Annoying woman.

She repeated it.

Shoulder.

Arm.

Wrist.

Chest.

This time I did not follow the sensation.

It remained.

Faint.

Not imagined.

Probably.

She moved her fingers lower along the right side of my ribs.

The readiness shifted.

Then hip.

Right thigh.

Knee.

Nothing at the shin.

Ankle.

Nothing.

Foot.

Nothing.

Obviously.

She moved back up.

Left hip.

Left thigh.

Knee.

Then stopped.

I knew where her hand was from pressure through the trousers.

Below the knee there was no contact.

There could not be.

But my left foot felt cold.

I opened my eyes.

Hessa was standing beside me.

Her hand was nowhere near the residual limb.

“What?”

She withdrew.

“What did you feel?”

“Cold.”

“Where?”

“Left foot.”

Her face did not change.

That was worse than if it had.

“Did you do that?”

“I touched your left knee.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“No.”

“Did you use magic?”

“A little.”

“On the knee?”

“Near it.”

“What kind?”

“Not yours.”

“Obviously.”

“Then why ask?”

“Specificity.”

She returned to her chair.

I looked at my missing lower leg.

Nothing visible.

No glow.

No sensation now.

“Again.”

“No.”

“Hessa.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because once is information.”

“Twice is better information.”

“Sometimes.”

“Usually.”

“Not when the subject is already leaning into the result.”

“I am sitting.”

“You know what I mean.”

I did.

I hated that.

“What does it mean?”

“I don't know.”

“Does it mean the channel is still there?”

“I don't know.”

“Does it mean the body map carries channel expectation?”

“I don't know.”

“Does it mean your magic triggered phantom sensation without any channel below the knee?”

“I don't know.”

“Could you tell the difference?”

“Not today.”

I leaned back.

Bad chair.

My chair was better.

This was not relevant.

Hessa watched me.

“You're disappointed.”

“No.”

“Good.”

“I am furious.”

“Better.”

“Because this is useful.”

“Yes.”

“And you are stopping.”

“Yes.”

“Because I want to continue.”

“Partly.”

“That is vindictive.”

“Partly.”

I stared at her.

She smiled.

Very slightly.

I had not known Hessa could do that.

Disturbing.

“Other reason?”

“You have spent weeks learning what your body does when you aren't directing it.”

I said nothing.

“Today I need to know what it does when you aren't directing magic.”

I looked at the empty ceramic dish.

“What was that for?”

“Nothing.”

I looked at her.

She shrugged.

“Gives you somewhere to look.”

“That is evil.”

“It worked.”

I had spent the first five minutes trying to infer a test from a dish.

Fine.

Excellent.

“What did you learn?”

“That you can feel a basic channel prompt through the trunk.”

“I knew I could feel magic.”

“Before.”

“Yes.”

“Before is not now.”

Right.

She continued.

“You responded along the right side in a way I expected.”

“What does expected mean?”

“Broadly intact.”

My pulse increased again.

She raised one finger.

“Do not turn that into cleared.”

“I wasn't.”

“You were.”

“I was turning it into encouraging.”

“That is allowed.”

Good.

“And left?”

“Your response above the knee was present.”

“Broadly intact?”

“Broadly.”

“Below?”

“I did not test below.”

“You can't touch below.”

“I don't need to touch skin to test every channel response.”

That stopped me.

I knew that.

Of course I knew that.

But the sentence opened several doors at once.

Hessa saw it happen.

“No.”

“I have a question.”

“You have twelve.”

“Can channels persist through absent tissue?”

“I don't know.”

“Has anyone studied amputation?”

“Yes.”

That was new.

I sat forward.

“Who?”

“People.”

“Hessa.”

“Not here.”

“Where?”

“Different places.”

“What did they find?”

“Different things.”

“That is aggressively unhelpful.”

“It is also true.”

“Give me one.”

She considered.

“Some practitioners report changed flow after limb loss. Some report no meaningful change. Some cannot return to prior forms. Some can. Some develop pain with channel use. Some don't. Records are bad.”

“Why?”

“Because people lose limbs in wars, accidents, monsters, infections, failed magic, punishment, work. They are not all mages. The mages are not all trained the same. The injuries are not the same. The amputations are not the same. The follow-up is not the same. And most people care whether the patient can live, not whether a Barrier architecture remains elegant.”

I sat back.

That was fair.

Also rude.

“Below-knee specifically?”

“Some.”

“Barrier specialists?”

“I don't know.”

“Dynamic support?”

“I don't know.”

“Counter-architecture?”

“Greg.”

“Right.”

I stopped.

Barely.

This was the first time since the amputation that the problem had become larger than me in a useful way.

Not unique.

Not solved.

Not even especially well documented.

But other people had lost limbs and used magic afterward.

Some had returned.

Some had not.

Some differently.

That should have comforted me.

Instead it made me want records.

“Do not go looking for records.”

I looked at Hessa.

“I didn't say anything.”

“You leaned.”

“That is not speech.”

“It is with you.”

“Are there records in Carrow?”

“No.”

“Guild?”

“No.”

“Private?”

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

She let the silence sit.

I could feel my left foot again.

Not cold.

Present.

Heel on floor.

It was not on the floor.

I looked down.

The sensation weakened when I looked.

Interesting.

No.

Not interesting.

Not yet.

Hessa followed my gaze.

“Now?”

“Foot feels planted.”

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Position only?”

“Yes.”

“After the test?”

“Yes.”

“Did it start before you looked?”

“Yes.”

She nodded.

That was all.

No conclusion.

I wanted one badly enough that my teeth hurt.

“When do we test again?”

“Three days.”

“Why three?”

“Because I said three.”

“Physiological reason?”

“Scheduling.”

“Oh.”

She had work.

Of course.

“Same test?”

“Some of it.”

“Casting?”

“No.”

“Mana draw?”

“Maybe.”

I looked at her.

She pointed at me.

“Maybe means maybe.”

“I know.”

“You don't.”

“I do.”

“Say it.”

“Maybe means maybe.”

“Again.”

“Fuck you.”

“Good enough.”

I laughed.

I had not intended to.

Hessa's mouth moved again.

Almost smile.

She stood.

Done.

I stood too.

Hands to crutches.

Right foot planted.

Left knee bent.

The phantom foot tried to remain on the floor for half a second as my body rose.

It produced a strange stretching sensation through an ankle that did not exist.

I stopped.

Not because I was falling.

Because I wanted to notice.

Then I finished standing.

Hessa watched.

“Anything?”

“Old geometry.”

“Meaning?”

“Foot thought it stayed down while I stood.”

“Thought?”

“Felt.”

“Better.”

I adjusted the crutches.

“When you write this down, don't write ‘channel response below knee.’”

“I wasn't going to.”

“What will you write?”

She looked at me.

“Patient annoying.”

“That lacks diagnostic precision.”

“Patient consistently annoying.”

“Better.”

I went to the door.

“Greg.”

I turned.

“No casting.”

“I know.”

“No experiments.”

“I know.”

“No trying to reproduce what I did.”

“I don't know what you did.”

“That has never stopped you.”

Also fair.

I opened the door.

“Hessa.”

“What?”

“Three days?”

“Yes.”

I nodded.

That was enough.

Outside, Carrow continued without caring that my left foot had felt cold when a mage touched my knee.

A cart blocked half the street.

Someone had dropped onions.

A dog was eating one and regretting it.

I stood in the doorway for a moment with both crutches planted.

Three days.

Not clearance.

Not a theory.

Not even a result I could name safely.

But it was the first appointment about magic that ended with another appointment about magic.

I went home before I could improve it.
