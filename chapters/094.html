PEG-LEG GREG
ART
CHAPTER 94

THE SUBJECT

My hands were fine.

My residual limb was slightly more swollen than the morning before, but not enough to worry me and enough that Pessa would have worried me.

This created a scheduling problem.

Pessa had said come back if the leg was not more swollen.

It was more swollen.

Slightly.

Hessa had said three days.

Three days had happened.

I went to Hessa.

This was not choosing magic over training.

Pessa had given a condition.

My body had answered it.

I disliked this because it was reasonable.

Hessa opened the door, looked at me, and said, “You trained.”

“Yes.”

“With Pessa.”

“Yes.”

“Yesterday?”

“Yes.”

She looked down.

Not at the missing leg.

At the wrapping.

“More swollen?”

“Slightly.”

“Then why are you here?”

“You said three days.”

“I did.”

“Do you want me to leave?”

“No.”

Good.

She let me in.

The useless ceramic dish was gone.

I noticed immediately.

Hessa noticed me noticing.

“Disappointed?”

“It was a strong instrument.”

“It held almonds after you left.”

“That explains nothing.”

“It held almonds.”

The table had a narrow copper strip on it now.

Also three small stones.

Not crystals.

River stones, gray and ordinary, each about the size of the last joint of my thumb.

A cup of tea.

One chair.

I looked around.

“Where's yours?”

“I'll stand.”

“That seems aggressive.”

“Sit.”

I sat.

The chair remained bad.

Consistency.

Hessa picked up the copper strip.

It was thin enough to bend but not flimsy. One end had a small loop. The other was flat.

“What is that?”

“Copper.”

“I know copper.”

“Congratulations.”

“What does it do?”

“Very little.”

“Good. We are progressing.”

She put it down.

“First, leg.”

I looked at her.

“Not magic.”

“Fine.”

She checked the residual limb with the same practical lack of ceremony she used for everything else.

Wrapping off.

Skin.

Temperature.

Pressure.

Questions.

Any new pain?

No.

Drainage?

No.

Heat?

No.

Throbbing after training?

Some.

How long?

Settled by evening.

Swelling this morning?

Slightly above previous morning.

She pressed along the upper calf that remained.

Not hard.

Enough.

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Here?”

“No.”

“Here?”

“Tender.”

“New?”

“No.”

She nodded.

“Training today?”

“Pessa said not if more swollen.”

“Good.”

“I was going to tell her.”

“You were going to negotiate.”

“I was going to report.”

“And then?”

“Potentially negotiate.”

Hessa rewrapped the limb.

“Don't.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

She washed her hands.

Then she sat in the chair that had apparently existed behind me the entire time.

I looked.

“What?”

“You said you'd stand.”

“I changed my mind.”

“That is allowed?”

“For me.”

Excellent.

She put the three stones in a line.

“Tell me what they are.”

“Stones.”

“What kind?”

“I don't know.”

“Good.”

“Why good?”

“Because I don't want you identifying them.”

“Then why ask?”

“To see if you would pretend.”

“I do not pretend.”

Hessa looked at me.

“Professionally.”

She continued looking.

“Usually.”

She pushed the first stone toward me.

“Pick it up.”

I did.

“Anything?”

“Stone.”

“Greg.”

“Cool. Smooth on one side. Rougher on the other.”

“Magic.”

“No.”

“Put it down.”

Second stone.

Nothing.

Third.

Nothing.

She moved them around.

“Again.”

Nothing.

“Eyes closed.”

I closed them.

She put one in my palm.

Nothing.

Another.

Nothing.

Third.

Maybe.

I opened my eyes.

“No.”

“What?”

“You decided maybe after I changed the order.”

“I felt something.”

“What?”

“Expectation.”

“Good.”

“That is not magic.”

“No.”

She took the stones away.

“What are they?”

“Stones.”

“Yes.”

I stared.

“Completely ordinary?”

“Yes.”

“That was insulting.”

“It was necessary.”

“Why?”

“Because today you want a result.”

I said nothing.

She was right.

That was irritating enough without confirmation.

Hessa picked up the copper strip.

“This is also ordinary.”

“Why should I believe you now?”

“You shouldn't.”

Good answer.

She looped the copper around two fingers of her left hand and held the flat end toward me.

“Mana draw.”

My heart did the cupboard thing.

Again.

I hated that metaphor now.

“Mine?”

“Yes.”

“Casting?”

“No.”

“How much?”

“Less than you used to pull before deciding whether to cast.”

That was very little.

“From where?”

“Your normal draw.”

“That is not a place.”

“You know what I mean.”

I did.

The first movement inward.

Not a spell.

Not shaping.

Not Barrier.

Just drawing enough mana into active control that the channels woke.

I had done it thousands of times.

Maybe tens of thousands.

The remembered number was unreliable.

The body memory was not.

Probably.

Hessa saw me thinking.

“Stop.”

“I haven't started.”

“Exactly.”

I put both hands flat on my thighs.

“What does the copper do?”

“If I tell you, you'll try to make it do that.”

“That suggests it does something.”

“Very little.”

“Your favorite amount.”

“Yes.”

She held it out.

“Close your eyes.”

I did.

“Draw.”

I reached.

Nothing happened.

Not nothing.

I stopped.

“Again,” Hessa said.

“I did.”

“You anticipated the old sensation and stopped when it wasn't exact.”

I opened my eyes.

“How do you know?”

“You made the face.”

“What face?”

“The one where reality has disappointed you personally.”

I closed my eyes again.

Fine.

Draw.

This time I did not wait for the remembered shape.

Mana came.

Small.

Familiar.

Wrong.

I inhaled sharply.

“Hold,” Hessa said.

I held.

Not a spell.

No shaping.

Just presence.

The internal sensation was like standing in a room after someone moved the furniture.

Everything still existed.

Not everything was where my body expected.

The draw settled through my chest, shoulders, arms.

Down the right side.

Hip.

Thigh.

Lower leg.

Foot.

Left hip.

Thigh.

Knee.

Then my awareness continued.

That was the problem.

It continued into calf.

Ankle.

Foot.

All of which felt present.

Not physically present.

Magically?

No.

I did not know.

The left foot felt full.

Not cold.

Full was the wrong word.

Occupied.

“Release,” Hessa said.

I released.

The sensation vanished from both sides at once.

My phantom foot remained.

Ordinary phantom foot.

If ordinary was the word.

I opened my eyes.

Hessa was looking at the copper strip.

It had not changed.

“What did you feel?”

I told her.

All of it.

She asked me to repeat the sequence without interpretation.

Chest.

Shoulders.

Arms.

Right side.

Left side.

Knee.

Continued sense below the knee.

Foot.

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Burning?”

“No.”

“Cold?”

“No.”

“Pressure?”

“No.”

“Different from your normal phantom?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

I hated the question.

“More organized.”

“Meaning?”

“I don't know.”

“Good.”

“No, not good.”

“Accurate.”

She set down the copper.

“What did you see?”

“Nothing.”

“What did you feel?”

“I told you.”

“I mean me.”

“Oh.”

I had forgotten she was doing something.

Excellent subject behavior.

“What did you feel?”

“A draw.”

“Helpful.”

“Broadly clean.”

My pulse accelerated.

Hessa lifted a finger.

“Broadly.”

“Fine.”

“Your trunk response was normal enough.”

“Normal enough.”

“Right side too.”

“And left?”

“Above the knee, present.”

“Below?”

She looked at the copper strip.

“I don't know.”

I exhaled through my nose.

“Did the copper tell you nothing?”

“It told me something.”

“What?”

“That you drew mana.”

“I knew that.”

“I wanted independent confirmation.”

“Of whether I was lying?”

“Of whether what you experienced corresponded to a draw.”

I paused.

That was different.

“Did it?”

“Yes.”

“How does copper do that?”

“Very little.”

I stared.

She almost smiled.

“Again?” I asked.

“No.”

“Hessa.”

“No.”

“One draw.”

“You had one draw.”

“That is a terrible sample.”

“Yes.”

“So again.”

“Tomorrow.”

I blinked.

“Tomorrow?”

“Yes.”

“That is less cautious than three days.”

“No. The first test was whether anything obvious happened after prompting your channels. Nothing obvious did. Today was whether you could make a minimal draw without shaping.”

“I did.”

“Yes.”

The word landed harder than it should have.

I had drawn mana.

After the amputation.

I had drawn mana.

Not cast.

Not shaped.

Not tested Barrier.

But drawn.

I looked down at my hands.

No glow.

Of course.

“Greg.”

“I know.”

“What do you know?”

“That this is not clearance.”

“Good.”

“That it does not establish below-knee channels.”

“Good.”

“That the phantom sensation could be body map, channel expectation, attention, or some combination we haven't separated.”

“Good.”

“That one successful minimal draw does not tell us whether repeated draw causes pain, fatigue, asymmetry, instability, or delayed effects.”

Hessa nodded.

“That was annoyingly good.”

“Thank you.”

“And?”

I looked at her.

“And I drew mana.”

“Yes.”

There it was again.

I smiled.

I could not help it.

Hessa let me.

That mattered.

She did not immediately crush the moment into caution.

She gave me perhaps three seconds.

Then:

“No casting.”

“Fuck.”

“Good.”

“I knew you were waiting.”

“Yes.”

I laughed.

She handed me tea.

I drank.

My left foot felt normal.

Normal phantom.

Heel against floor.

Toes slightly curled.

I looked at the actual floor.

The sensation weakened.

“Foot?”

Hessa asked.

“Yes.”

“After release?”

“Yes.”

“Different?”

“Not now.”

“Write that later.”

“I thought I wasn't supposed to turn this into a research project.”

“You're the patient. Patients can remember symptoms.”

“Subject.”

“What?”

“Today I was the subject.”

“You were annoying in either role.”

Fair.

We sat for a minute.

I wanted to ask about Barrier.

I did not.

I wanted to ask whether a channel could terminate at the knee.

I did not.

I wanted to ask whether mana could follow remembered anatomy.

I did not.

This was almost physically painful.

Hessa watched.

“You can ask one.”

“One?”

“One.”

I considered.

Not the most interesting question.

The most useful.

“What would make you stop?”

She looked at me differently.

Good question, apparently.

“Pain that concerns me. Heat. New swelling that doesn't fit physical activity. Channel response I don't understand enough to repeat safely. Draw instability. Symptoms that persist after release. Anything that suggests shaping is happening when I didn't ask for shaping.”

“Why would shaping happen?”

“That was your one.”

“Cruel.”

“Yes.”

She drank tea.

I thought about stopping conditions.

This was familiar.

Not the magic.

The structure.

Define failure before the test.

Holl.

Fixtures.

West River.

No.

Not everything needed to become everything else.

Still useful.

“What happens tomorrow?”

“Same draw.”

“That's all?”

“Probably.”

“Probably?”

“If it is boring, we may vary position.”

“What position?”

“Seated. Standing if your leg is settled.”

I looked at her.

“You know Pessa has me training.”

“I know.”

“Do you talk?”

“Sometimes.”

“About me?”

“When relevant.”

This should have bothered me more than it did.

“Who owns tomorrow?”

“I own magic.”

“And Pessa owns movement.”

“Yes.”

“What if both want the same body?”

Hessa looked at me.

“You own the body.”

I went still.

She continued before I could turn it into something larger.

“So tell Pessa what happened. Tell me what Pessa does. Neither of us gets to pretend the other load doesn't exist.”

That was practical.

Good.

Less profound.

Better.

“Can I train tomorrow?”

“Ask Pessa.”

“Medically.”

“Your swelling decides more than I do.”

“I hate distributed authority.”

“I know.”

I finished the tea.

When I stood, the phantom left foot felt planted again.

I noticed before moving.

Both crutches.

Right foot.

Stand.

The phantom stayed where it was for a moment and then seemed to lift with me.

No stretching ankle this time.

Different.

I told Hessa.

She nodded.

No theory.

At the door she said, “Eat.”

“I eat.”

“You forget after magic.”

“I haven't done magic in weeks.”

“You forgot before.”

Fair.

“And no testing alone.”

“I know.”

“Not even draw.”

“I know.”

“Not even because you already did it once.”

“I know.”

She looked at me.

I sighed.

“I will not draw mana without you.”

“Good.”

I opened the door.

Outside, the street was bright enough to make me squint.

I had expected the first return of mana to feel larger.

Trumpets.

Revelation.

A clean answer about what my body was now.

Instead I had tea in my stomach, slightly swollen tissue under wrapping, sore hands from yesterday, and one successful draw I was forbidden to repeat.

I was absurdly happy.

I went to find Pessa.

Not to train.

To report.
