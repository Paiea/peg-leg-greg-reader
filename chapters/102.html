PEG-LEG GREG
ART
CHAPTER 102

THE PIVOT

Pessa made me walk in a circle.

This was insulting because I had come prepared to hold a shield.

Not physically prepared.

Mentally prepared.

Which was more dangerous.

I found her behind the storage shed the next morning. Same narrow strip of packed ground. Same wall. Same practice shield leaning against it.

I looked at the shield.

Pessa looked at me.

“No.”

“I did not ask.”

“You looked.”

“That is not asking.”

“It is with you.”

I shifted both crutches.

“My leg is baseline.”

“Good.”

“Hessa has no medical objection.”

“I don't care.”

“I know.”

That pleased me more than it should have.

Pessa pointed at the ground.

“Walk.”

“I walked here.”

“Again.”

“Where?”

She drew a rough circle in the dirt with the toe of her boot.

It was not a good circle.

“Seriously?”

“Yes.”

“With both crutches?”

“Yes.”

“No shield?”

“No.”

“No stick?”

“No.”

“Are you testing whether I can walk?”

“I am watching how you turn.”

Ah.

That was different.

I started.

The circle was perhaps twelve feet across.

Large enough that I did not have to pivot sharply.

Right crutch.

Left crutch.

Right foot.

Move.

The rhythm was familiar enough that I did not think about every placement.

Pessa stood inside the circle.

“Look at me.”

I looked.

That changed the next crutch placement slightly.

“Keep going.”

I kept going.

“Look at the wall.”

I turned my head.

My shoulders followed more than I wanted.

Right crutch landed wider.

“Ground.”

Head down.

Trunk forward.

Left crutch shorter.

“Me.”

Head up.

Right foot corrected.

“Stop.”

I stopped.

“What?”

“You tell me.”

I considered.

“Gaze changes placement.”

“We knew that.”

“Yes.”

“What else?”

I replayed the circle.

Dangerous.

Useful.

“My shoulders follow my head.”

“Sometimes.”

“Then the crutches compensate.”

“Sometimes.”

“Right foot corrects.”

“Sometimes.”

Pessa waited.

I hated sometimes.

“Again.”

Other direction.

This was worse.

Not dramatically.

Enough.

Turning left meant the inside of the circle was on my missing side.

My left crutch wanted to become the turn.

I planted it closer.

Then too close.

My right foot crossed slightly inward to follow.

Pessa said nothing.

I completed the circle.

“Again.”

“Why?”

“Because the first one was ugly.”

“That is not technical.”

“It was ugly.”

Second circle.

I tried to make it less ugly.

This made it worse.

I anticipated the left crutch placement and put it down early.

Then my right foot arrived where the crutch wanted to be.

I had to lift the crutch again.

Stutter.

Stop.

Pessa said, “You changed something.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“The first one was ugly.”

She stared.

Fine.

“I thought the left crutch was late.”

“Was it?”

“I don't know.”

“Good.”

“That word is doing inappropriate work.”

“Again. Don't fix it.”

Third circle.

I did not fix it.

This was difficult because I now knew where the ugly part lived.

At least I thought I did.

Left turn.

Head.

Shoulders.

Left crutch.

Right foot.

There.

A tiny hesitation.

Not the crutch.

Before it.

My trunk wanted to continue rotating while the right foot was still planted for the previous direction.

The left crutch arrived into a body already asking for the next support.

Then right foot had to catch up.

I finished.

“Stop.”

Pessa came closer.

“What?”

“I think I was wrong.”

She nodded.

This was not enough reaction.

“I said I think I was wrong.”

“I heard.”

“About sequencing.”

“Maybe.”

“No. The sequence is still involved. But I thought the crutch was late.”

“And?”

“The turn starts too early.”

Pessa's face changed slightly.

Not agreement.

Interest.

“Where?”

“Trunk.”

“Again.”

I walked.

This time she stood outside the circle.

“Slow.”

I slowed.

Slow made everything harder to hide.

At normal pace momentum helped carry the movement.

Slow removed charity.

Right crutch.

Left crutch.

Right foot.

Turn.

The trunk began rotating.

Left knee moved.

Not much.

Enough.

As if the missing foot could accept the turn and let the pelvis follow.

It could not.

My right foot then had to take the whole redirection.

I stopped.

“There.”

Pessa said, “What?”

“My left knee.”

“What about it?”

“It starts the old turn.”

“Maybe.”

“I felt it.”

“You felt something.”

I wanted to argue.

Did not.

Personal record.

Pessa crouched.

Not touching me.

“Do it once more. Smaller circle.”

She drew another line.

Smaller.

Of course.

I walked.

The tighter turn made the problem louder.

On the left arc, my pelvis tried to rotate across support that was not there.

My left knee moved outward and slightly forward.

My right foot twisted harder against the dirt.

The crutches became emergency geometry.

Not emergency.

That was dramatic.

Correction geometry.

I completed half the circle.

“Stop.”

I stopped.

Right calf working.

Hands fine.

Residual limb fine.

“What did you feel?”

I told her.

She nodded.

Then:

“Now right.”

Tight circle the other direction.

Different.

My intact leg was inside the turn.

Right foot could pivot under me.

But both crutches crowded the outside differently.

My left crutch wanted to cross behind.

I clipped it with the right crutch once.

Wood knocked wood.

“Again.”

I repeated.

Clipped again.

“Good.”

“How is that good?”

“Same mistake twice.”

“That is not usually praised.”

“Now I know you own it.”

I laughed.

Fine.

She took one crutch from me.

The left.

I stood with the right crutch in my right hand.

This was not the configuration from the shield work.

There had been shield left, right crutch.

Now the left hand was empty.

That changed more than expected.

“Circle.”

“One crutch?”

“Yes.”

“Which direction?”

“Both.”

I started right.

Bad.

One point of crutch contact instead of two meant every turn required more from the right foot.

My empty left hand lifted away from my body without permission.

Balance.

Counterweight.

Human answer.

I did not say it.

Right circle worked.

Left circle did not.

Not at first.

I planted the right crutch.

Turned left.

The crutch was on the outside of the turn.

My right foot pivoted.

Left knee moved into the missing support pattern.

I had nowhere useful to put the empty left hand.

This was not a real problem.

It felt like one.

I stopped.

Pessa said, “Why?”

“I didn't like it.”

“Good.”

“Human answer?”

“Yes.”

“I felt exposed.”

“Where?”

“Left.”

“That is a direction.”

“Exactly.”

She waited.

I tried again.

Left turn.

Right crutch outside.

Empty left side.

The absence was spatial, not merely anatomical.

Nothing between me and whatever was on that side.

No crutch.

No shield.

No leg below the knee.

The feeling arrived before any actual instability.

Interesting.

“Stop thinking,” Pessa said.

“I am walking.”

“You stopped.”

I had.

Fine.

Again.

This time she stepped into the circle.

Not at me.

Just there.

My body reacted anyway.

I turned my shoulders toward her.

Right crutch moved.

Right foot.

Left knee.

The old support request happened.

Then I corrected.

No hop.

Barely.

Pessa stepped away.

“What changed?”

“You.”

“That is not useful.”

“You occupied the space I was turning into.”

“Better.”

“I oriented toward you.”

“Yes.”

“Which changed the turn.”

“Yes.”

“So gaze.”

“Not only gaze.”

“Threat?”

“I was not threatening you.”

“Person.”

“Maybe.”

“Object?”

“Maybe.”

“You're enjoying this.”

“No.”

She was.

I could tell.

She gave me the left crutch back.

“Rest.”

I sat on the same ordinary chair from the shield session.

Right calf warm.

Hip mildly tired.

Hands fine.

Residual limb quiet.

No extra pressure.

Phantom foot felt angled for a moment.

Ordinary angled.

Not magical.

I ignored it.

Pessa handed me water.

I drank.

“What are we doing?”

“Turning.”

“I understand the literal answer.”

“Good.”

“No shield today?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because last time you almost fell when I moved sideways.”

“I did not almost fall.”

“You hopped.”

“Half an inch.”

“You measured?”

“Approximately.”

She stared.

“Fine. I hopped.”

“You blamed the crutch being late.”

“I said maybe.”

“After I made you.”

“Yes.”

“And now?”

“Now I think the movement begins before the crutch problem.”

“Maybe.”

I exhaled.

Pessa drank from her own cup.

“The shield made everything louder,” she said.

“Yes.”

“It also gave you too many things to blame.”

I stopped.

There.

That was good.

“So you removed it.”

“Yes.”

“To isolate turning.”

“No.”

I frowned.

“What?”

“To watch you turn.”

“That is the same thing.”

“No. You keep trying to isolate things that aren't isolated when you use them.”

I looked at the dirt circle.

Head.

Shoulders.

Pelvis.

Right foot.

Left knee.

Crutches.

Person in the space.

Speed.

Direction.

Fatigue.

“Fine.”

“Good.”

“What next?”

“Stand.”

Of course.

We did circles again.

Then not circles.

Pessa pointed to three places in the dirt.

“Go there. Then there. Then back.”

No pattern.

That mattered.

I could not prepare the turn as easily.

First point.

Second.

Third.

She changed them.

Again.

Sometimes left.

Sometimes right.

Sometimes shallow.

Sometimes almost back on myself.

I got better at one thing and worse at another.

If I moved the crutch early, I sometimes blocked the right foot.

If I turned the trunk first, the missing-side support request appeared.

If I kept the trunk too square, I made the turn in several ugly pieces.

If I looked at the destination too early, my shoulders followed.

If I stared at the ground, I arrived without knowing what I was arriving at.

No solution.

Inventory.

Again.

At one point Pessa said, “Behind you.”

I turned too fast.

Right crutch tip skidded.

My right foot hopped.

More than half an inch.

Maybe two.

Pessa caught my upper arm.

I stayed up.

We both froze.

Residual limb?

Fine.

Right ankle?

Fine.

Hands?

Fine.

Heart?

Annoyed.

Pessa released me.

“What happened?”

“I hurried.”

“Why?”

“You said behind me.”

“And?”

“It sounded urgent.”

“Was it?”

“No.”

“Again.”

I stared at her.

“You intentionally changed the urgency.”

“Yes.”

“That is almost an attack.”

“No.”

“It manipulated my response.”

“Yes.”

“Social combat.”

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

Again.

She said, “Behind you.”

I turned slower.

Too slow.

“Dead,” she said.

I looked at her.

“You said not fighting.”

“We aren't.”

“You just killed me.”

“I said a word.”

“This is legally questionable.”

She pointed.

“Again.”

Third time.

“Behind you.”

I turned.

Not fast.

Not slow.

Right crutch moved.

Right foot pivoted.

Trunk followed.

Left knee still made the old request, but smaller.

I saw the point behind me.

Stopped.

Stable.

Pessa said nothing.

I waited.

Still nothing.

“Well?”

“Again.”

Of course.

By the end, my right calf was tired, hip warm, hands beginning to complain, residual limb still quiet.

Pessa stopped before I asked.

That was irritating.

“How long?”

“Forty minutes.”

“That's all?”

“You're tired.”

“I can continue.”

“Yes.”

I waited.

She waited.

“Tomorrow exists,” I said.

“Apparently.”

I hated callbacks when other people used them.

She erased the dirt circles with her boot.

“Next time shield?”

“Maybe.”

“Movement?”

“We moved.”

“You know what I mean.”

“Yes.”

“Alden?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because I don't need him yet.”

Yet.

I heard it.

She saw me hear it.

“Do not make that word bigger.”

“Hessa said something similar.”

“I don't care.”

I laughed.

“Good.”

I stood.

Both crutches.

Right calf objected.

Nothing serious.

At the edge of the yard, I turned back.

Not because I needed to.

Because I wanted to see whether I did it differently.

Head.

Destination.

Crutch.

Foot.

Trunk.

Or maybe not.

I could already feel myself trying to turn the walk home into data.

I stopped.

Then I turned however I turned.

It was ugly.

It worked.

I left.
