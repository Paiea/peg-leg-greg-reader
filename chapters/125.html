PEG-LEG GREG
ART
CHAPTER 125

THE TEST CASE

Hessa had two threads. I disliked this immediately. They hung from the same wooden frame. Dark. Thin. One over the glazed tile beside the place my right forearm would go. The other farther away.

Not much farther. Enough. The gray cloth was folded on the side table. The copper was already out. Hessa was writing. I sat. She kept writing. I looked at the threads.

“No.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“I was observing.”

“You were reconstructing.”

“I can do both.”

“Not today.”

She finished the line.

“How are your hands?”

“Normal.”

“Right calf?”

“Normal.”

“Residual limb?”

“Baseline.”

“Phantom?”

“Ordinary.”

“Thumb?”

“Nothing.”

“Fingers?”

“Nothing.”

“Any urge to curl them?”

“No.”

“Wrist?”

“Normal.”

She held out her hand. I gave her mine. Right. She checked grip, thumb, fingers, wrist. Then left. Then residual limb.

Then the ordinary questions again in slightly different words because Hessa enjoyed catching lies I was not telling.

“Yesterday?”

“Theatre.”

“No magic?”

“No.”

“Day before?”

“Lyssa.”

“No magic?”

“No.”

“You understand that theatre using magic is not you using magic.”

“Yes.”

“Good. I asked because your face did something.”

“There was a window.”

“Greg.”

“I did not touch it.”

“Excellent.”

She went back to the table. I stared at the two threads.

“Why two?”

“Because one annoyed me.”

“That is not a scientific reason.”

“It is an excellent medical reason.”

“Hessa.”

She looked at me.

“The first test told me less about external effect than I wanted.”

“Yes.”

“It told me more about your control than I expected.”

“Yes.”

“That does not make the external result better.”

“I know.”

“You are saying that too quickly.”

“I still know.”

She pointed at the farther thread.

“This one is not for you.”

“Then why is it there?”

“For me.”

“That is irritating.”

“Good.”

She rolled my right sleeve above the elbow. The charcoal mark today was not the same. Still wrist-side. Slightly higher. A short line. Not the circle. Not the old line. New enough. I noticed.

Hessa noticed me noticing.

“Do not optimize.”

“I am not.”

“You are.”

“Internally.”

“That still counts.”

She drew a second charcoal line on the glazed tile. One beneath each resting thread. The near thread hung approximately where the first had. The far one hung perhaps four inches beyond it. Both moved slightly while she worked.

One turned. Stopped. The other shifted sideways. Stopped. Hessa waited. I waited. She opened the door. Both moved. She closed it. Waited. She crossed the room heavily. The far thread trembled. Near one moved less.

She wrote.

“Table vibration,” I said.

“Maybe.”

“You walked near the far leg.”

“Yes.”

“On purpose?”

“Yes.”

She opened the window a finger width. Both threads shifted. Near first. Far second. She closed it.

“Air.”

“Yes.”

She stood beside the apparatus without touching it. The near thread drifted slightly toward her sleeve. Then away.

“Heat.”

“Maybe.”

“Static.”

“Maybe.”

“Why are we doing this again?”

“Because I am not asking the same question.”

I looked at the two threads.

“You are comparing them.”

“Yes.”

“That is the same question with a control.”

“No.”

“It is literally a control.”

Her mouth moved. Almost smile. Dangerous.

“What is the question?” I asked.

“Whether the thread closest to the altered region behaves differently from a second thread exposed to as much of the same room as I can manage.”

“That is a control.”

“Yes.”

“I said that.”

“You said it was the same question.”

“It is related.”

“Everything is related if you talk long enough.”

I considered the horse. Did not say it. Good. Hessa put the copper in position.

“Rules.”

“Minimal draw.”

“Yes.”

“Right forearm only.”

“Yes.”

“Small localized alteration at the marked region.”

“Yes.”

“Maintain underlying draw.”

“Yes.”

“If instructed, REACH from existing altered region only, smallest extension toward the near thread.”

“Yes.”

“No release.”

“Yes.”

“No push.”

“Yes.”

“No casting.”

“Yes.”

“No Barrier.”

“Yes.”

“No hand, wrist, finger movement.”

“Yes.”

“No increase in internal alteration.”

“Yes.”

“Stop if it feels like force.”

“Yes.”

“Stop if it broadens.”

“Yes.”

“Stop when you say stop.”

“Yes.”

“Far thread is not a target.”

“Yes.”

“No attempt to influence both.”

“Yes.”

“No attempt to compensate for room movement.”

“Yes.”

I paused.

“That last one was not in the first test.”

“No.”

“Because now there are two.”

“Because now you know enough to be dangerous in a boring way.”

“That is rude.”

“That is medicine.”

She had me breathe normally for several minutes. I watched. Near thread moved. Far thread moved. Sometimes together. Sometimes not. Once both shifted left almost simultaneously. Once the near thread moved toward my empty chair before I sat.

Useful. Annoying. Hessa wrote everything.

Then:

“Sit.”

I sat. Right forearm beside the near thread. Three quarters of an inch. Approximately. I did not measure. Hessa would kill me.

“Do not stare at the thread.”

“Where should I look?”

“My hand.”

She held two fingers above the copper.

“That is worse.”

“For you.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

I breathed. Ordinary. Phantom left heel present. No relevance. Right hand loose. No thumb. No fingers.

“Draw.”

I did. Minimal. Still strange. Less strange than months ago. More dangerous than it felt. Copper answered. Not strongly. Enough that Hessa watched it. Underlying draw stable.

“Shape.”

Small. Right forearm. Charcoal line. The altered response appeared. Localized. Slightly broader than the mark on the upper side. I reported it.

“Hold.”

I held. No motor. No thumb. No left change. Hessa watched.

“How certain?”

“High.”

“Not maximum?”

“No.”

“Good.”

She waited. Long enough that I started wondering whether this was the entire test.

Then:

“REACH.”

Smallest extension. Toward the near thread. Not force. Not push. Not more. Different. The near edge became more distinct. As before. The alteration felt asymmetric. Toward the thread.

“Near edge sharper,” I said.

“Continue same amount.”

Same amount was impossible. I attempted not to change what I was doing. Copper shifted. Hessa's eyes moved. Not to me. Past me. Probably thread. I did not look.

“Hold.”

I held.

“RETURN.”

Immediately. I reduced the altered response. Near edge softened. Center dimmed. No elbow shift. No urge. No wrist. Underlying draw remained.

“Hold.”

I held.

“Release.”

I released. Ordinary arm. Hessa touched my wrist.

“Open.”

I opened.

“Close.”

Closed.

“Thumb.”

Moved.

“Again.”

Fine. Then she let me look. Near thread rested slightly right of its charcoal line. Far thread rested slightly left of its line.

“What happened?”

“You tell me.”

“No.”

“Hessa.”

“You were not watching.”

“That was intentional.”

“Yes.”

“So tell me.”

She checked my forearm again before answering.

“During baseline, both moved repeatedly.”

“Yes.”

“During your draw, both moved once.”

“Together?”

“Near first. Far followed.”

“Air.”

“Maybe.”

“During shape?”

“Neither enough to record.”

“REACH?”

“The near thread moved toward your arm.”

My chest did something. Small. I hated that she saw it.

“The far thread?”

“Did not move at that moment.”

I stared. Hessa raised one finger.

“Do not.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“What happened next?”

“While you maintained the same instruction, the near thread stopped. Then the far thread moved left.”

“Near?”

“Stayed.”

“So differential.”

“One observation.”

“Yes.”

“You are doing it again.”

“I said one.”

“You said it like a man trying to build a cathedral from one brick.”

“I would need more bricks.”

“That is not comforting.”

I looked at the near thread. It did not move. Of course.

“What does that mean?”

“Today?”

“Yes.”

“It means the nearer thread showed one movement toward your arm during REACH that the farther thread did not show at the same moment.”

“That is better.”

“Than what?”

“Than both moving.”

“Only if the question is whether they moved together.”

“The question is whether the near one behaved differently.”

“Yes.”

“It did.”

“Once.”

“Yes.”

“Then that is data.”

“Yes.”

“Useful data.”

“Yes.”

“Hessa.”

“It is more useful than the first test.”

There. I waited. She did not continue.

“And?”

“And I still cannot attribute it to mana.”

I exhaled. The far thread moved. Hessa pointed at it.

“That.”

I looked. It settled.

“Fuck.”

“Yes.”

“Could be breath.”

“Yes.”

“I was farther from that one.”

“Yes.”

“Could be convection.”

“Yes.”

“Static.”

“Yes.”

“Table vibration.”

“Yes.”

“Thread twist.”

“Yes.”

“Could be mana.”

“Yes.”

I looked at her. She looked back. That yes was worse than no.

“Then what changed?”

“My confidence that the test can produce interpretable information.”

Not my confidence that you moved it. Different. I knew. Still annoying. She tapped the farther thread lightly with a wooden stick. It spun.

“Last time, one thread moved and we had no local comparison.”

“Yes.”

“Today I saw a moment when the near thread behaved differently from the far thread.”

“Yes.”

“I also saw other moments when they behaved differently without magic.”

“Yes.”

“That matters.”

“Yes.”

I pointed at the apparatus.

“Why not put another thread the same distance on the other side?”

“Other side of what?”

“My arm.”

“Then it would not be exposed to the same local conditions.”

“It would be closer.”

“To different skin, different room position, different heat pattern, different movement.”

“Then put one on each side of the same altered region.”

“With what space?”

I looked. Not much.

“Smaller frame.”

“And now you have changed the frame.”

“That is solvable.”

“Yes.”

“So?”

“So I may do that later.”

I hated later. Hessa continued.

“Controls are not magic truth machines. They remove some explanations and leave others.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

“Then say what today's second thread cannot tell us.”

I looked at it.

“It cannot tell us whether the near movement was mana.”

“Good.”

“It cannot fully match local heat.”

“Yes.”

“Or static.”

“Yes.”

“Or tiny air differences.”

“Yes.”

“Or thread-specific twist.”

“Yes.”

“Or whether the two threads respond identically to the same disturbance.”

“Yes.”

“So it is a bad control.”

“No.”

I frowned.

“It is an incomplete control.”

“That sounds like a polite bad control.”

“It is a control for some questions and not others.”

Of course. Everything with Hessa eventually became jurisdiction. Even thread.

“You are angry.”

“No.”

“You are disappointed.”

“Yes.”

“Better.”

“I would prefer established external effect.”

“I would prefer a patient who does not build conclusions out of wishes.”

“I did not.”

“You almost did.”

“That is different.”

“Barely.”

She covered the apparatus with the gray cloth. I objected.

“With my face?”

“With your entire body.”

“You said the test was more interpretable.”

“Yes.”

“Then repeat it.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“One supervised external attempt today.”

“You did not say one before.”

“I am saying it now.”

“That is cheating.”

“I am the doctor.”

“You use that too much.”

“I use it exactly enough.”

She returned to the desk. I sat. My forearm felt normal. Which was good. Boring. Good.

“Hessa.”

“No.”

“I have not asked.”

“You want to know when.”

“Yes.”

“I don't know.”

“Tomorrow?”

“No magic tomorrow.”

I stared.

“One day?”

“One day.”

“Then?”

“Assessment.”

“External?”

“Assessment.”

“You are doing that deliberately.”

“Yes.”

I thought about arguing. I did not. Not wisdom. I had already lost. She wrote.

“Thirteenth successful minimal supervised draw.”

There was the count.

“Ninth supervised deliberate shaping attempt?”

“Yes.”

“Second supervised external attempt?”

“Yes.”

“External effect?”

“Unestablished.”

I hated the word. It was correct.

“Internal control?”

“Still encouraging.”

“Encouraging.”

“Yes.”

“Not improved?”

She looked up.

“You established a small altered region at a shifted target, attempted one bounded REACH, RETURNed promptly, maintained the underlying draw, and showed no motor recruitment, broadening, thumb symptom, elbowward shift, or unusual left response.”

“Yes.”

“That supports repeatability of the control sequence under a slightly changed setup.”

“Yes.”

“That is improvement in confidence.”

“Not effect.”

“Correct.”

“Fine.”

“You sound like you are trying to punish the word.”

“I am.”

“Tomorrow you live.”

“I have been doing that.”

“Continue.”

“What exactly are you protecting against now?”

She considered me for a moment. Not because she did not know. Because she was deciding how much to say.

“Accumulation.”

“Of what?”

“Unknowns.”

“That is not a symptom.”

“No. It is why I do not stack tests.”

She pointed at my right forearm.

“You have had repeated small draws, repeated shaping, a new external instruction, and now a second external attempt. Your immediate exams are reassuring. That does not mean I know every delayed cost.”

“There hasn't been one.”

“So far.”

“Yes.”

“Good. Keep helping me preserve that sentence.”

I did not like this either. It was difficult to argue against a strategy whose main evidence was that nothing bad had happened.

“You could just say you are cautious.”

“I have.”

“Many times.”

“And yet.”

Fine. She gave me ten minutes. No magic. Ordinary hand movement. Cup. Grip. Crutch. She watched me pick up the cup.

“Stop checking your thumb.”

“I wasn't.”

“You checked.”

“I moved it.”

“You checked by moving it.”

“That is how thumbs work.”

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

At the door she said:

“Do not buy thread.”

I stopped.

“I own thread.”

“Do not hang it from anything.”

“I was not going to.”

“Do not ask the theatre for theirs.”

I stared at her.

“How do you know about theatre thread?”

“I know about thread.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“Go live.”

I went. The morning had become late enough that the Guild was busy. I did not have a reason to go there. I went anyway. Jorren was eating an apple on the front steps.

Of course. He looked at me.

“Hessa?”

“Yes.”

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

“Boring.”

I sat beside him. Not gracefully. He did not help. Correct. He took another bite.

“What happened?”

“Nothing established.”

“That sounds expensive.”

“It was free.”

“Then you got your money's worth.”

“No.”

“No?”

“I got one movement.”

“You said nothing established.”

“Correct.”

“Then you got one unestablished movement.”

“That is not a thing.”

“It is clearly a thing. You have one.”

I looked at him. He bit the apple again.

“What did the other movement do?”

I stopped.

“What?”

“You said one movement.”

“I did not say there were two threads.”

“There were two threads?”

“Yes.”

“Then this story is already too complicated.”

“It is not complicated.”

“There are twice as many threads as yesterday.”

“Two days ago.”

“Worse.”

I almost explained the control. He held up the apple.

“No.”

“You asked.”

“I regret it.”

“You cannot ask a technical question and then refuse the answer.”

“I just did.”

“That is socially irresponsible.”

“Eat something.”

I looked at him. He offered the apple. I took it. One bite. Returned it. He frowned.

“You eat like a thief.”

“You offered.”

“I offered generosity. You took geometry.”

“What?”

“That bite was too large.”

“No.”

“It changed the apple.”

“That is what biting does.”

He inspected the missing section.

“Damage.”

I almost explained the two-thread control. Did not. Not because Jorren would not understand. Because I did not want to. I wanted lunch.

“Are you working?” I asked.

“Later.”

“What?”

“Warehouse.”

“Which?”

He looked at me.

“No.”

“Fine.”

He smiled. Good. We sat. People went into Guild. People came out.

Merrin passed carrying three ledgers and said:

“Gregory.”

“Merrin.”

No offer. No task. Good. A courier I did not know argued with a clerk about a damaged seal. A woman in a red coat shouted that somebody had moved her cart. Someone had.

The cart was across the street. The argument continued anyway. Jorren finished the apple.

“You going theatre?”

“I don't know.”

“Lyssa?”

“I don't know.”

“Pessa?”

“I don't know.”

“You have a demanding schedule.”

“Yes.”

“What are you doing now?”

“Lunch.”

“You don't have lunch.”

“I am about to.”

“That is not lunch.”

“It will become lunch.”

He stood.

“I have work.”

“Later.”

“Later became now.”

I looked at the sun.

“That is poor scheduling.”

“That is time.”

He left. No theft. Unusual. I bought stew. Sat near the Guild wall. A cart passed. Then another. No ancient beast. No dangerous authority. No impossible old-world recognition. Just Carrow. I ate. The thread entered my head after three bites.

Near moved. Far did not. Then later far moved. Could be mana. Could be room. Could be me. Could be nothing. I wanted another test. Tomorrow contained no magic. Fine. The theatre was west.

Lyssa might be anywhere. Pessa might be busy. Work existed. I finished the stew. For once, I did not decide what the rest of the day meant. I stood. Then waited because my right crutch tip had landed on a cabbage leaf.

I moved it six inches. Better. Current state. Continue.
