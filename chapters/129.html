PEG-LEG GREG
ART
CHAPTER 129

THE LIMIT

The threads were gone. I noticed before I sat down. The frame was gone too. No tile. No copper beside it. No white sleeve. No gray sleeve. Hessa had a cup of tea. This was worse.

“You stopped.”

“Sit.”

“You stopped the thread test.”

“Sit.”

I sat. She checked my hands first. Palms normal. No tenderness from yesterday. Right calf normal. Residual limb quiet. Phantom foot present in the usual impossible way, mostly heel this morning. Thumb fine. Fingers fine.

Wrist fine. Grip. Again. Fine. Hessa wrote. I waited. She drank tea.

“You stopped.”

“Yes.”

There. I did not like how quickly she said it.

“Why?”

“Because I reviewed the three attempts.”

“And?”

“The apparatus is not giving me enough interpretable signal to justify spending more of your safe attempts on it right now.”

I stared at the empty table.

“One failed replication.”

“One failed replication after two ambiguous attempts.”

“The second was better than ambiguous.”

“It was more interesting.”

“That is not the same word.”

“Correct.”

“The near thread moved toward me during REACH while the far thread didn't.”

“Yes.”

“That is a differential observation.”

“Yes.”

“Then the next test should repeat it.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because we did.”

“Once.”

“Yes.”

“That is not enough.”

“No.”

“Then repeat again.”

“No.”

I hated this. Hessa put down her cup.

“What answer are you trying to get?”

“Whether I moved it.”

“Can this apparatus tell us?”

“Eventually.”

“Maybe.”

“Then why stop?”

“Because your attempts are not free.”

“I am fine.”

“You are fine after the attempts we have done.”

“Yes.”

“That does not make the next ten free.”

“I said one.”

“You will say one after that.”

“Only if it is useful.”

“You currently think another thread attempt is useful.”

“It is.”

“I don't.”

There it was. Jurisdiction. Annoying. Clean. I looked at the empty table again.

“So what did the three tests establish?”

“That thread moves.”

“Excellent.”

“Hessa.”

“You asked.”

She ignored me.

“First, they established that suspended thread in this room is sensitive to more things than we can cheaply isolate.”

“Yes.”

“Air movement. Heat. vibration. twist. static. position. breathing. room traffic.”

“Yes.”

“Second, they established that two threads exposed to much of the same room can still behave differently without your magic.”

“Yes.”

“Third, we observed one movement during REACH that was interesting enough to repeat.”

“Yes.”

“We repeated it.”

“It didn't happen.”

“Correct.”

“That does not disprove it.”

“Correct.”

I stopped. She waited. That was the problem. She was not saying impossible. She was saying not enough. Harder to argue with.

“Fourth,” she said, “the reliable part of all three external attempts was not external.”

I looked at her.

“Your control.”

“Yes.”

“You established the small alteration where instructed. You maintained the underlying draw. You attempted a directional change without broadening into the older response. You did not recruit your hand. You did not chase sensation when the apparatus did something interesting. You RETURNed when told.”

“Mostly.”

“What does mostly mean?”

“I argued afterward.”

“Afterward is allowed.”

Good.

“Your internal sequence is becoming more repeatable than the external observation.”

“Yes.”

“So that is what we test today.”

I looked at the empty space where the frame had been.

“Did you throw it away?”

“No.”

“Where is it?”

“Cabinet.”

“Why?”

“Because if I leave it out, you will spend the entire assessment looking at it.”

“I am looking at where it was.”

“Yes.”

“That proves nothing.”

“It proves I know you.”

I did not answer. She pulled the three old pages from beneath a newer sheet. I recognized the white and gray labels in the margins.

“You kept the notes.”

“Obviously.”

“Show me.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because you will argue with my abbreviations.”

“I can read.”

“That has not protected me before.”

“Hessa.”

She turned the first page toward herself, not me.

“Attempt one. Single thread. Baseline movement. One movement toward arm during bounded external question, another later movement with multiple room confounds. No attribution.”

“Two movements.”

“I said one during the bounded question.”

“The other was still during maintained draw.”

“Which is why it is written down.”

Second page.

“Attempt two. Paired threads. Baseline differential behavior demonstrated before draw. During REACH, closer thread moved toward arm while farther did not at that same moment. Later farther moved independently.”

“Yes.”

“Attempt three. Same general setup. During shape, closer moved away, farther moved shortly after. During REACH, no recorded movement from either.”

“Yes.”

She stacked the pages.

“If I only owned the second page, I would repeat.”

“You did.”

“If I owned the first two, I would repeat.”

“You did.”

“If I own all three, I have to ask whether a fourth attempt is the best use of your current tolerance.”

“And you decided no.”

“Yes.”

“What would make you return to it?”

“Better reason.”

“That is vague.”

“A stronger external signal from some other bounded question. Better control. More mature shaping. A reason to think the thread's sensitivity is useful instead of merely noisy.”

“Or time.”

“Possibly.”

“So not abandoned.”

“No.”

That helped more than it should have. I did not want the thread. I wanted the possibility to remain alive. It did. Annoyingly. I looked around.

“No apparatus.”

“Your arm is the apparatus.”

“That sounds medically threatening.”

“It should.”

She brought out the copper. Relief was embarrassing. Not because copper did anything important. Because it belonged to the sequence I knew. Hessa noticed.

“You expected it.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“You always use copper.”

“Not always.”

“Recently.”

“Yes.”

“What does that tell you?”

“That you are predictable.”

“Good.”

“No, that was an insult.”

“I know.”

She set it down well away from the old thread position. I watched her choose the place.

“Is that placement part of the test?”

“No.”

“Then why there?”

“Because my tea is here.”

I looked at the tea. Reasonable constraints continued ruining elegant interpretation.

“What question?”

“Whether you can produce the same bounded directional change and RETURN at a different right-forearm target without an external object to pull your attention.”

“That is less interesting.”

“To you.”

“Yes.”

“I know.”

“Why remove the target?”

“Because I want to know whether REACH is currently an instruction you can execute internally, or whether the thread is part of what makes the response happen.”

I sat straighter. That was better. Not thread. Still a question.

“What direction?”

“Not toward the old thread position.”

“Why?”

“Because you have practiced that direction three times.”

“Two.”

“Three external attempts.”

“The first geometry differed.”

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

She marked my forearm. Charcoal. Small target closer to the upper outer side than the recent wrist-near work. Not elbowward. Not the familiar broad direction. Different. I looked.

“Outward?”

“Toward that mark.”

“There is no object.”

“The mark is an object.”

“External object.”

“No suspended target.”

“You know what I mean.”

“Yes.”

“Can I look at it?”

“Before draw. Not during.”

“Why?”

“Because I want your report, not you steering with your eyes.”

“That is not how mana works.”

“Good. Then it should not matter.”

I disliked that answer. She checked position. Right forearm supported. Hand relaxed. Fingers visible. Copper was present after all. On the other side of the table. Not part of a thread apparatus. Just copper.

Familiar. Hessa placed two fingers above the new charcoal mark.

“Question is small.”

“Yes.”

“One draw.”

“Yes.”

“One shape.”

“Yes.”

“One directional instruction.”

“Yes.”

“One RETURN.”

“Yes.”

“If the response broadens, changes direction, recruits hand or wrist, produces thumb or finger symptoms, produces unusual left response, pain, cold, numbness, weakness, or anything you cannot describe, you stop.”

“Yes.”

“No external effect question.”

“Yes.”

“No casting.”

“Yes.”

“No spell.”

“Yes.”

“No Barrier.”

“Yes.”

“No proving anything.”

I looked at her.

“That one was unnecessary.”

“No.”

“Fine.”

“Draw.”

I did. Minimal. Copper answered. Right forearm. Stable. No hand. No thumb. No left.

“Report.”

I did.

“Shape.”

Localized. The altered region came slower than last time. Not much slower. Enough to notice. I said so.

“Continue only if stable.”

I waited. It settled. Small. Near the charcoal mark but not on it.

“Location?”

I described it. Hessa touched the skin outside the region. No change.

“Hold.”

Held. Then she moved her fingers. Not toward the mark. Away. Two inches farther up and outward. I looked at her.

“That is not the mark.”

“No.”

“You said toward the mark.”

“I changed the instruction.”

“That seems bad.”

“Can you follow it?”

Maybe. That was different. Not REACH toward something. REACH from the altered region toward a direction Hessa indicated in space over my arm. No thread. No fixed destination. Just direction.

“Smallest extension,” she said. “Toward my fingers. No force. Maintain draw.”

I did. Or tried. The near edge sharpened. Wrong edge. Toward the charcoal mark. I stopped.

“Hessa.”

“Report.”

“It went toward the mark.”

“Did you intend that?”

“No.”

“Stop directional instruction. Hold.”

Held. Interesting. Bad. Interesting. She moved her fingers away.

“RETURN.”

I returned. The alteration reduced. Underlying draw remained. No hand. No thumb. No broadening.

“Hold.”

Held.

“Release.”

Released. Hessa checked everything. Again. Fine. I looked at the charcoal mark.

“You tricked me.”

“No.”

“You established a target and then changed it.”

“Yes.”

“That was intentional.”

“Yes.”

“So you were testing whether I was following the instruction or the prepared target.”

“Yes.”

“That is a trick.”

“It is a test.”

“You could have told me.”

“That would change the test.”

I hated that she was right. Mostly.

“What did I do?”

“You shaped near the prepared target.”

“Yes.”

“When I redirected the directional instruction, your first altered edge sharpened toward the prepared target instead of toward my indicated direction.”

“Yes.”

“So I failed.”

“You did not execute the changed direction as instructed.”

“That is failure.”

“For that question, yes.”

Clean. Again. I appreciated it. Also hated it.

“Did the altered region move at all?”

“The edge changed.”

“Toward the prepared mark.”

“By your report.”

“You did not feel it?”

“No.”

“Copper?”

“Changed with draw and shape. Nothing I would distinguish during the directional instruction.”

“So no independent confirmation.”

“No.”

“Could I have simply reported what I expected?”

“Yes.”

I looked at her.

“That is a problem.”

“Yes.”

“You believe me?”

“I believe you are trying to report accurately.”

“That is not the same.”

“No.”

Good. More problems. I had missed having enough problems.

“Can you touch the edge while I do it?”

“Not today.”

“Why?”

“Contact changes sensation.”

“Could still be useful.”

“Yes.”

“Then next time.”

“Maybe.”

“You hate commitments.”

“I make them when I have enough information.”

“That is a lie. You committed to no magic tomorrow very quickly.”

“That decision has excellent evidence.”

I laughed despite myself. She wrote something.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You wrote because I laughed.”

“I wrote because your hand stayed relaxed while you laughed.”

I looked at my right hand. Still open on the support.

“That matters?”

“Maybe.”

“You just did it.”

“What?”

“Made everything information.”

“Everything is information.”

“That is convenient.”

“Only if I don't care what it says.”

I recognized that. She knew I recognized it. Neither of us mentioned the last time.

“What does that mean?”

“I don't know yet.”

“Hessa.”

“It may mean the prepared target strongly organizes what you are doing.”

“Yes.”

“It may mean changing directional instruction after shaping is harder than beginning with the intended direction.”

“Yes.”

“It may mean you anticipated the old sequence.”

“Yes.”

“It may mean my instruction was poor.”

I looked at her.

“Again?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“One attempt.”

“I am fine.”

“One attempt.”

“But this is internal.”

“Yes.”

“And it failed.”

“Yes.”

“So repeat.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because failure is data before it is an invitation.”

That sentence was offensive. I leaned back.

“Tomorrow?”

“No magic.”

“Again?”

“Yes.”

“How many days?”

“One.”

“Then assessment?”

“Yes.”

“What are you going to test?”

“I don't know.”

“You absolutely know.”

“I have questions.”

“Which?”

“No.”

“Hessa.”

She picked up her tea. Cold now, probably.

“Today I learned something I did not know.”

“That I follow charcoal instead of fingers.”

“Possibly.”

“That sounds stupid.”

“It sounds like learning.”

“I knew the charcoal was the target.”

“Exactly.”

I stopped. She drank. I thought about it. I had built the shape around the mark before she changed the instruction. Of course the response favored it. Maybe. Or I had simply failed to redirect.

Or REACH was not direction at all and I had been interpreting noise. Or the thread had mattered. Or Hessa's fingers were a terrible target. Many possibilities. I started listing them. Hessa held up a hand.

“No.”

“I have four.”

“Keep them.”

“I can tell you.”

“I know.”

“You do not know mine.”

“I know enough.”

“That is arrogant.”

“Yes.”

Fair. I looked at my forearm. Charcoal still there. Nothing else.

“Are we farther from external effect than we were?”

“That is not how I would describe it.”

“How would you?”

“We stopped spending attempts on a poor measurement.”

“That is farther.”

“It is different.”

“It feels farther.”

She considered that.

“Yes.”

Good. At least she allowed that. I expected the feeling to disappear once named. It did not. Three days ago I had been staring at a thread that might have moved because of me.

Today Hessa had put the thread in a cabinet and made me fail to follow two fingers. Objectively, that felt like retreat. Also objectively, my arm had done something small on command and then stopped when told.

Again. The second fact was less exciting. It was also the one Hessa trusted. I knew which one I preferred. That did not change which one she trusted. I rubbed neither arm. Kept hands still.

Not because instructed. Habit.

“Am I getting better?”

“At the parts we can currently observe reliably.”

“That is a Hessa answer.”

“Yes.”

“Which parts?”

“Minimal draw. Localized shaping. maintaining draw while altering. RETURN. avoiding the older broad response. avoiding motor recruitment.”

“Directional control?”

“Unclear.”

“External?”

“Unestablished.”

“Casting?”

“No.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

“Barrier?”

“No.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

I sighed. She smiled. Actually smiled. Small. Cruel woman.

“Go live tomorrow.”

“I lived yesterday.”

“You were a messenger.”

I froze. Hessa looked at me.

“How do you know that?”

“Teren's sister buys herbs from the woman downstairs.”

I stared.

“What?”

“She mentioned the theatre had acquired a tall messenger on crutches who forgot his letter.”

I closed my eyes. No. Absolutely not.

“That was rehearsal.”

“I assumed.”

“I was not acquired.”

“I assumed.”

“I am not a messenger.”

“You were yesterday.”

“For thirty minutes.”

“That seems sufficient.”

“This city is too small.”

“Carrow is not small.”

“Then people need to stop talking.”

“No.”

I stood. She pointed at my forearm.

“Wash the charcoal off later.”

“Yes.”

“No magic tomorrow.”

“Yes.”

“No practicing directional attention.”

I stopped.

“What does that mean?”

“You know exactly what it means.”

“I cannot stop looking at things.”

“You can stop sitting at a table trying to recreate today's task without mana.”

“That would not be magic.”

“It would be rehearsal.”

I stared. She raised an eyebrow. I refused to give her that.

“No.”

“Good.”

I left. Outside, the morning had become bright. Not warm. Bright. I walked three streets before realizing I was heading toward the theatre. I stopped. Hessa tomorrow was no magic. Theatre was allowed. That was not why I had turned.

Probably. I looked west. Then east. Then at the street ahead. No test. No conclusion. No thread. My right forearm felt completely ordinary. I went to buy lunch.

At the bread stall, the seller said:

“Messenger.”

I nearly left Carrow.
