LIGHTART
CHAPTER 202

THE NEIGHBOR

There was still nothing under the cup.

I checked once.

Lyssa saw me do it and said nothing, which was worse than saying good.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You had a face."

"I have several."

"That one was waiting for paper."

"I was checking."

She tied a small bundle with cord and pulled the knot tight with her
teeth.

"Still nothing."

"Correct."

"Good."

I pointed at her.

"There it is."

She picked up the bundle.

It was not narrow.

I had checked.

"Third bell?" she asked.

"Third bell."

"Then why are you dressed?"

I looked down at myself.

I was wearing the same shirt I would have worn if I had not been going
anywhere.

"Because I own clothes."

"You've been ready for half an hour."

"I live here."

Lyssa smiled and left with the bundle.

I had time.

Again.

The problem with having a schedule was that it created areas around the
schedule where nobody had told me what to do.

I washed the bowl from last night's accidental soup. I swept the room
badly, then swept the part where the dirt had gone after I swept it
badly. I checked the leather on my crutch grip with my thumb and did not
go to the cobbler because nothing was wrong with it.

Then I sat down.

This lasted longer than I expected.

When I finally left, the third bell was still not close enough to
justify the theatre, so I walked the longer way through Carrow.

A fish seller was throwing water across the stones in front of his
stall. Two dogs objected. A woman had set six clay cups in a row on a
cloth and was arguing with a customer who wanted the seventh cup, which
was apparently not for sale despite being visibly a cup. A man on a
ladder painted the upper half of a sign while another man below
criticized every letter.

I bought a small heel of bread.

I did not buy cheese.

This was restraint.

The third bell began while I was finishing the bread.

I reached the hall before the last strike.

Rinna looked at me from the front table.

"Again."

"What?"

"On time."

"I've become dependable."

"No."

She pushed a folded paper toward me.

I looked at it.

"What's this?"

"Tomorrow."

"That is a day."

"West courtyard. Havel house."

I waited.

Rinna did not continue.

"Am I working?"

"I just told you where."

"That was a location."

"Tomorrow. Second bell. Here first."

"That's also a time."

"Good."

"How much?"

Rinna looked at me.

I smiled.

She looked down at the seat markers.

"Private supper. Four copper for you if you work the whole thing."

I stopped smiling.

Four copper was not silver.

It was also four normal local days arriving at once.

"Four?"

"Yes."

"What am I doing?"

"Whatever survives tomorrow."

"That sounds less specific than the pay."

"It usually is."

She pointed toward the board.

"Today first."

Today said:

BROKEN GATE: VEYA / EVIN / HARA / GREG

WIDOW: VEYA / NESSA / HARA

SHORT: SELLEN / MAREK

GREG: NEIGHBOR / SET

The Neighbor again.

I had been him before.

Four lines, depending on whether we counted the wife.

I preferred to count the wife.

She had the best line and did not have to appear.

"Same Broken Gate?"

"Yes."

"Local?"

Rinna stared.

"Ask Teren."

"I was going to."

"You were asking me."

"I was passing through."

She pointed rearward.

I passed through.

The hall was already half prepared.

Marek was carrying two lamps. Sellen had his own coat today and looked
healthier for it. Pell and Jori were arguing over a length of rope that
neither of them appeared to need yet.

Nessa had three costume pieces laid across a bench and was inspecting a
clasp.

Davin was beside her holding a chair.

It was a very good chair.

That was the problem.

It had a carved back, polished arms, and legs without visible repairs.

I stopped.

"Whose is that?"

"No," Rinna said from behind me.

I turned.

"I didn't ask to use it."

"You were about to."

"I asked whose it was."

"Also no."

Davin said, "Havel."

"The tomorrow Havel?"

"Yes."

"Why is their chair here?"

"Sample."

This raised more questions than it answered.

Rinna came around me and put one hand on the polished back.

"They want their own chairs in the playing space."

"People usually do."

"They want one used in the second piece."

Teren appeared from the rear carrying pages.

"No."

Rinna said, "I told them no."

"Good."

I looked between them.

"Why?"

Davin tapped the chair leg.

"Because it costs more than you."

"Individually?"

"Probably."

"That's insulting."

"It isn't ours."

Teren set the pages down.

"The courtyard has tables on three sides. We play the open side. No
painted door. No smoke. Two comic pieces before sweets."

There it was.

Tomorrow had become a job.

Rinna added, "And nothing sad."

Veya, passing with a folded shawl, said, "Then don't let Pell speak."

Pell called from the floor, "I heard that."

"That was the sad part."

Rinna ignored them.

"Havel's man brought the chair so we could see the height. We are not
taking it. We are not touching their furniture during the performance.
We are not moving their tables. We are not using smoke under the
awning."

I raised a hand.

"No."

"I wasn't going to ask about smoke."

"What?"

"Food."

Rinna closed her eyes.

"Workers eat after."

"Included?"

"If you don't irritate the kitchen."

"That's a dangerous condition."

"Then bring bread."

Four copper and possible food.

Tomorrow had become financially significant.

Teren picked up the pages.

"Broken Gate."

"Same version?"

"Yes."

"Four lines?"

"Yes."

"Entrance?"

"Same."

"Good."

Teren looked suspicious.

"That's all?"

"I know the role."

"Then why are we talking?"

Excellent question.

We rehearsed once because the gate had been repaired again.

Not repaired because it was broken.

Repaired because the lower painted brace had started separating from the
frame.

This meant it stood a little straighter than the last time I had
complained beside it.

I took my usual place.

Teren said, "Too close."

"I stood here last time."

"The gate didn't."

I moved six inches.

That was the rehearsal.

My wife still wanted the cart moved.

The municipal drainage was still guilty.

Nothing had improved.

Afterward I carried the gate off with Pell.

He took the heavier side without comment.

My wrist did nothing.

This pleased me enough that I did not mention it.

The private supper began spreading through the hall without becoming a
meeting.

Nessa rejected two costume baskets because tomorrow's pieces only needed
one.

Davin measured the width of the narrow painted table, then rejected it
because the Havel courtyard already had tables and Rinna had just said
we were not moving them.

Jori asked about lamps.

Rinna said the household had wall lamps, but they were bringing four of
their own.

Pell asked whether that meant four lamps total or four extra.

Rinna said, "Four ours."

Pell wrote something on a scrap of board.

I watched him.

"Did you just make a list?"

"No."

"You wrote four lamps."

"That's not a list."

"What is it?"

"Four lamps."

This was the strongest argument I had heard all day.

The customer wanted two funny pieces.

The King was requested.

Of course it was.

Someone at Havel house had apparently seen it before and wanted it
again.

The second piece was still being argued over.

Teren wanted Broken Gate because it traveled light.

Rinna wanted something shorter because the host wanted both pieces
before sweets.

Veya wanted to know how much the host cared about the schedule.

Rinna said, "Enough to pay us."

That ended that part.

Tomorrow's work was not prestige.

It was somebody with a courtyard and money wanting to laugh before
dessert.

I understood this kind of art.

Before today's house opened, we discovered that one of the lamp tins was
nearly empty.

Not tomorrow's lamp tin.

Today's.

Pell shook it beside his ear.

"Enough."

Nessa took it from him, shook it once, and said, "No."

"Same sound."

"Different answer."

"How much is in it?"

"Not enough."

Jori went for oil.

Pell watched him leave.

"I could have done that."

Nessa handed Pell a basket.

"You can do this."

He looked inside.

"What's in it?"

"Things."

He carried it away.

I had begun to think theatre was mostly assigning objects to the person
least able to continue a conversation about them.

Food was scattered.

Marek's dried apples had survived one day and were now almost gone.

Sellen had bread and something that might have been cold beans wrapped
in a leaf.

Hara arrived with no food and stole one dried apple before Marek could
stop her.

"You brought them for us," she said.

"I brought them."

"Yes."

"From north."

"Generous."

Marek looked at me.

I ate my own bread.

"I have no opinion."

"Good."

I almost objected.

I was improving.

Rinna came through with tomorrow's folded paper again.

"Second bell here."

"I know."

"Bring your crutches."

I looked down.

"That was the plan."

"Someone will forget something."

"Why would it be my legs?"

"It won't be now."

She moved on.

Sellen watched her go.

"Four copper?"

I looked at him.

"How do you know?"

"She told me."

"How much are you getting?"

"More than you."

"How much?"

"Enough."

"Everyone here has become Rinna."

"Occupational."

The house opened.

Normal people came in.

A woman with a red scarf sat too close to the aisle and had to move her
basket twice. Two men in the back argued softly until the first piece
started. A child dropped a wooden horse and then spent several minutes
trying to retrieve it without his mother noticing.

I worked the right side for a few minutes, then went back for the
Neighbor coat.

Nessa handed me the brown one.

I put it on.

"Wrong."

I froze.

"You gave it to me."

"I changed my mind."

"This is abuse."

She pulled it off me and handed me a dull green coat.

"Why?"

"Brown catches the gate."

I looked at the sleeve.

There was a loose hook.

"Then fix the hook."

"Later."

"That seems inefficient."

"Green."

I put on green.

This was not my mistake.

My mistake happened two minutes later when I picked up the wrong cup.

There were two cups on the prop table.

One belonged to Broken Gate.

One belonged to Widow.

They were both brown.

One had a chip.

I remembered that the chipped one was ours.

This was true.

Both were ours.

I carried the chipped cup toward the wing.

Nessa said, "Other."

I stopped.

"Of course."

"Chipped is Widow."

"Since when?"

"Today."

"Why?"

"Because I said."

I exchanged them.

No one died.

The correct cup went onstage.

The chipped cup remained for Widow.

I did not ask why Widow needed a damaged cup.

I was becoming a professional.

Broken Gate began.

The gate stood straighter.

Evin's cart remained imaginary.

Veya was irritated before I entered, which was useful because my entire
function was to add one more person with a problem.

"Whose cart is blocking my lane?"

Evin said, "Not mine."

"It has your name on it."

"Then someone has stolen my name."

The laugh was smaller than last time.

Fine.

I continued.

"My wife says move it."

"Tell your wife I am considering it."

"I did. She said consider faster."

Better laugh.

I left.

Backstage, Pell whispered, "Wife is good."

"She has range."

He looked at me.

"Don't."

"That wasn't magic."

"I know. Still."

Apparently Hessa had damaged all of us.

Later the gate came down.

Not actually.

We laid the upper frame at an angle so it looked collapsed from the
house.

I returned.

"I leave for ten minutes."

Hara said, "It was twelve."

"Then this is my fault."

The audience laughed.

The blame moved from Hara to Evin to drainage.

The city survived.

I came offstage and immediately started helping Jori move the frame
because tomorrow it might be going with us.

Teren said, "Maybe."

I stopped.

"Maybe what?"

"Gate tomorrow."

"I thought Broken Gate was the second piece."

"So did I."

"What changed?"

"Nothing yet."

"Then why did you say maybe?"

"Because tomorrow exists."

I hated planning.

Widow went next.

The chipped cup mattered because Hara was supposed to notice it and
accuse Nessa's character of using the dead woman's things.

This made the chip visible from exactly no part of the house.

Veya still made the accusation work.

Nobody needed the chip.

I felt vindicated privately.

The strongest moment belonged to Nessa.

Not because of the cup.

Near the end, Veya demanded to know why the room had been left untouched
for years.

Nessa, playing the landlady, had a line about rent.

She said it simply.

Then she started folding a shawl while Veya continued arguing.

The folding was not in the line.

It was not dramatic.

By the time Veya reached the accusation, Nessa had folded the shawl into
a small square and put it on the dead woman's chair.

The room went quiet.

Hara looked at the shawl.

Then at Nessa.

Her next line was softer.

The audience stayed with them.

I was behind the flat holding the unchipped cup from the previous piece
because I had forgotten to put it away.

This was less useful.

I put it down carefully.

The short piece went last.

Marek and Sellen spent most of it arguing over a missing hat that both
men were wearing at different times.

The audience saw the second hat before Marek did.

The laughter built until he finally touched his head.

He took the hat off.

Looked at it.

Looked at Sellen.

Sellen said, "Found it."

That was enough.

After the house cleared, tomorrow became physical.

Nessa set one costume basket aside.

Then a second smaller one.

Then took three things out of the first and put them in the second.

Davin leaned the gate frame against the wall.

Teren still had not decided whether it was traveling.

Jori marked four lamp tins.

Pell found a length of rope that he actually needed.

Rinna stood at the front with a list.

A real list.

I showed Pell.

He looked offended.

"That's different."

"How?"

"More things."

Fair.

Rinna pointed at me.

"Blue bundle."

"Short or little?"

"Little."

I found the little blue bundle.

It contained folded cloths.

I put it beside the costume basket.

"Tomorrow?"

"Yes."

"What are they?"

"Tomorrow."

"That's not an object."

"It is now."

I accepted this.

I carried two lamp tins to the travel pile.

Then a box of cups.

Then the wrong stool.

Davin stopped me halfway across the room.

"Not that one."

I looked at the stool.

"Why?"

"Loose foot."

"It was in the pile."

"It was near the pile."

I put it back.

This was my second object error of the day.

I decided not to count the cup because Nessa had changed the system.

The stool was mine.

Davin handed me another.

"That one."

I carried it.

No lesson.

Rinna paid me one copper for today.

"Neighbor. Set."

"Private-job preparation."

"Included."

"In today?"

"In your life."

"That is not accounting."

"Tomorrow four."

I looked at the coin in my palm.

Then at tomorrow's pile.

Four lamps.

One costume basket.

Maybe gate.

Little blue bundle.

Cups.

A stool with all its feet.

Four copper.

I could buy several meals with four copper.

I could also fail to earn four copper by spending it in my head before
tomorrow.

I put today's coin away.

"Second bell?"

"Here."

"Not third."

"Correct."

"Schedule changed."

"That's what schedules are for."

This seemed wrong.

I went home.

I bought nothing on the way.

This was easier because I had already eaten bread and because four
imaginary copper had made every real copper feel temporarily suspicious.

Lyssa was not home.

There was still nothing under the cup.

I checked once.

Still mail.

I washed my hands.

There was green paint on the side of my shirt.

I had no idea where it came from.

I rubbed at it with water.

This made the green larger.

I stopped.

Lyssa came in while I was standing beside the basin staring at the
damage.

She looked at my shirt.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"You were going to."

"I was going to say this happened at work."

"Yes."

"Can it come out?"

"Maybe."

"That's not confidence."

"It's paint."

She put down a parcel.

I did not ask whether it was narrow.

"Private job tomorrow," I said.

"Where?"

"West courtyard. Havel house."

"Who are they?"

"I don't know."

"What are you doing?"

"Whatever survives tomorrow."

"Useful."

"Four copper."

That got her attention.

"Four?"

"Whole job."

"What kind of job?"

"Two funny pieces before sweets. No smoke. Don't touch their chairs."

Lyssa nodded as if these were normal conditions.

"They feed you?"

"After."

"So you'll eat there."

"I'll be working."

"That wasn't my question."

I looked at her.

"Rinna said after."

"Then eat after."

"This household has become very controlling about meals."

"You forget them."

"Once."

Lyssa waited.

"Several times."

She took my shirt sleeve and looked at the paint.

"Nessa doesn't dress you?"

"For the show."

"Then stop painting your own clothes."

"I wasn't painting."

"Strong defense."

We ate the last of the accidental soup with more water and the heel of
bread Lyssa had brought.

It had become less soup and more memory.

No Hessa note came.

No one moved any paper.

Tomorrow I was due at the hall at second bell, which meant the
third-bell schedule had lasted exactly long enough to become adjustable.

The Havel house had a courtyard, expensive chairs, an awning that
disliked smoke, and enough money to make four copper sound ordinary.

I did not know what I would play.

I did know what I would carry.

Probably too much.

That was a job.
