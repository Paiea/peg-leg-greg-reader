CHAPTER 212

THE USHER

Antonius Vale found me before breakfast.

Not personally.

That would have been alarming.

A boy in a brown vest knocked while Lyssa was scraping the last beans
from last night's pot into a smaller bowl.

I opened the door.

He looked at me.

"Greg?"

"Usually."

He handed me a folded note.

"Vale."

Of course.

I stared at the note.

The boy held out his hand.

"For reply."

"I haven't read it."

"I can wait."

This was apparently a threat.

I opened it.

GREG,

YOUR DEBT REMAINS ACTIVE.

TABLE WORK.

DELIVERY ACCOUNTS.

TOMORROW, FIRST BELL.

ONE HOUR.

TRANSPORT FROM VALE OFFICE IF REQUIRED.

A. VALE

I read it again.

The debt had not changed while I was busy becoming a servant, nephew,
paper problem, and possible Guild criminal.

Rude.

Lyssa leaned against the table.

"Vale?"

"Yes."

"Debt?"

"Apparently."

"You knew."

"I knew quietly."

The boy was still waiting.

I looked at him.

"What reply does he need?"

"Yes or no."

"What happens if I say no?"

The boy shrugged.

"Then I bring back no."

Not a threat.

Worse.

Administration.

I turned the note over and wrote YES with Lyssa's charcoal.

Then added:

FIRST BELL IS OFFENSIVE.

I considered it.

Crossed that out.

Then wrote:

YES.

The boy took it.

"Tomorrow."

"Apparently."

He left.

I closed the door.

Lyssa put the smaller bowl in front of me.

"You thought he'd forgotten."

"I hoped his records had suffered."

"He's a lender."

"Records can suffer anywhere."

"Not there."

Probably not.

I ate cold beans.

The old arrangement had not survived my leg.

That part had been obvious.

Antonius had stopped treating me like a body he could send wherever a
body needed to go, which was considerate mostly because a missing leg
had made the alternative ridiculous.

The debt had survived.

So had the newer arrangement.

Table work.

Some room work.

No roads.

No pretending I could carry a warehouse because a ledger said I owed
somebody money.

I had done invoice comparisons for him before.

Two copper of debt credit.

Then nothing for a while.

Apparently Antonius did not require regular proof that a debt existed.

He had a ledger.

I hated that I could hear him saying it.

"Delivery accounts," Lyssa said.

"Yes."

"Carts?"

"Probably."

She looked at me.

I looked at her.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"You did with your face."

"I was thinking about carts."

"Exactly."

"Vale has carts."

"So does half Carrow."

"That is what makes it interesting."

"That is what makes it carts."

I ate another bean.

She went back to her work.

Nothing sat under the cup.

No Hessa note.

That one had already arrived.

Tomorrow had become crowded without asking me.

First bell, Antonius.

After midday, Hessa.

Theatre had not yet voted.

I left the Vale note on the table long enough to remember the time, then
folded it into my coat.

By third bell, the theatre cart still had its wheel.

The chalk mark still crossed pin and axle in one unbroken line.

Jori was crouched beside it anyway.

"Moved?"

"No."

"Then why are you there?"

"Checking."

"Didn't you just check?"

He looked at me.

I moved on.

Rinna had today's board in one hand and a short list in the other.

The short list was not theatrical.

BREN?

LEFT GLOVE.

CART YARD.

PAID ONCE. MORE.

That was all.

No strings.

No map.

No dramatic circle around the name.

Good.

She folded it before Pell could see.

I said, "Vale found me."

Rinna looked up.

"Who?"

"Antonius Vale."

That got recognition.

Not surprise.

"Creditor?"

"That is an ugly way to describe our relationship."

"Accurate?"

"Unfortunately."

"When?"

"Tomorrow, first bell. One hour."

She looked at the board.

I added, "Guild after midday."

"I remember."

"You told me to tell you."

"Now I remember twice."

"Efficient."

She looked at the board again.

"Tomorrow, Vale. Guild. Come after Guild if you're still useful."

"That's a very open condition."

"Then don't make it worse."

"So no morning theatre."

"No."

"Will you survive?"

"We did yesterday."

I had no answer to that.

Rinna pointed at today's board.

KING: MAREK / HARA / EVIN

LETTER BOX: VEYA / SELLEN / NESSA

SHORT: PELL / EVIN

GREG: HOUSE / SET

"No role?"

"No."

"Again?"

"You've had several."

"I've developed expectations."

"Kill them."

She handed me the ticket box.

Apparently today I was the usher.

I stood at the front door while the hall warmed behind me.

Pell and Jori argued over whether the rear cart being farther inside
made the passage safer or merely more annoying.

Nessa had three costumes hanging from the side rail.

Davin was repairing a lamp hook that had nothing to do with Havel.

Marek was eating something that smelled like garlic.

Normal.

A woman arrived first and paid for one.

Then two men together.

Then an older couple.

Then a broad man in a dark coat came up the street and looked directly
at the door.

My body noticed before my brain did.

Shoulders tight.

Good foot planted.

Crutch angle changed.

He stopped in front of me.

Large.

Dark coat.

No close-cut hair.

No glove.

He looked at the board beside the door.

"How much?"

I stared.

"For what?"

He stared back.

"The show."

Right.

"One copper."

"For both?"

"Each."

"That's robbery."

I almost laughed.

Did not.

"Then you may remain financially secure outside."

He frowned.

Then paid two copper.

His wife came up behind him carrying a wrapped bundle.

He had been waiting for her.

That was all.

I put the coins in the box.

The man said, "King first?"

"Yes."

"Good."

He went inside.

I watched his hands.

Both ordinary.

I disliked myself a little.

Rinna appeared behind me.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Then sell tickets."

"That's what I'm doing."

"Do it less suspiciously."

"Apparently I have a face now."

"You've always had one."

Unfortunate.

Before the house opened fully, a familiar produce woman stopped at the
door with a basket on one hip.

She did not buy a ticket.

She pointed past me at Rinna.

"Her."

Rinna came over.

The woman said, "Your cart still inside?"

"Yes."

"Good."

Rinna waited.

The woman waited too.

Apparently Carrow had developed competitive waiting.

Finally the woman said, "Heard about Talla."

Rinna's face stayed flat.

"From?"

"Not your business."

"Then neither is mine."

The woman snorted.

"Fair."

She shifted the basket.

"No one came to me. Before you ask."

"I wasn't."

"You were."

I liked her.

She looked at me.

"Guild man?"

"No," Rinna and I said together.

The woman blinked.

"Fine."

She left without buying a ticket.

Rinna looked at me.

"That rumor is annoying."

"I agree."

"Do not improve it."

"I haven't."

"Keep doing that."

"Finally, a task suited to me."

She went back inside.

That was the entire threat for the afternoon.

A woman with turnips had heard something.

No new victim.

No new man.

No broken object.

The rumor remained small enough to fit in a produce basket.

I preferred it that way.

More people came.

A carpenter with sawdust on one sleeve.

Three young women who argued over who had paid last time.

A boy who tried to give me half a copper and confidence.

An old woman who said she would sit near the back because Marek
projected spit.

I did not verify this.

By the time the King started, we had twenty-six.

I counted twice.

Not because of scraping noises.

Because Rinna had made me count twice.

Progress could also be externally imposed.

I closed the door and carried the box back.

The King needed one platform, two chairs, the narrow table, and the red
cloth.

I brought the red cloth.

Wrong red cloth.

There were apparently two.

Nessa took it from my hand.

"Other."

"How many colors are secretly plural?"

"All of them."

I found the darker red cloth.

The first one belonged to Short.

No lesson.

The King started.

Marek was clean today.

Hara had a petition that was not upside down.

Evin had both shoes.

For several minutes, nothing improvised.

Then a fly landed on Marek's forehead.

He ignored it.

The fly remained.

Hara watched it.

The audience began watching Hara watching it.

Marek kept speaking.

Finally Hara said, "Your Majesty."

"What?"

"You have an adviser."

Marek's eyes narrowed.

"Where?"

Hara pointed at his forehead.

The audience laughed.

Marek held perfectly still.

"Is he wise?"

"He has stayed through the entire petition."

"Then wiser than most."

Bigger laugh.

Evin stepped closer, examined the fly, and said, "He disagrees."

"With what?"

"Everything."

The fly left.

Marek watched it go.

"Dismissed."

That was the strongest moment.

No chair moved.

No paper fell.

No one needed me.

Good.

Between pieces, I changed the table and nearly carried the wrong red
cloth back again.

Nessa made a sound.

I stopped.

"Right."

"Which?"

I looked at both.

"Darker stays."

"Good."

I carried the other.

Competence.

Letter Box played normally.

Veya found the key.

Sellen lied about a letter.

Nessa made the audience laugh by sitting down before Sellen finished
inviting her to sit.

The hall forgot about Bren because the hall did not know Bren existed.

I forgot for most of it too.

That was better.

At the rear, Pell whispered, "Vale?"

I looked at him.

"How do you know?"

"You said it near Rinna."

"I regret your hearing."

"Antonius Vale?"

"Yes."

"You owe him money?"

"Debt."

"That's money."

"Not currently."

"How much?"

"No."

He looked offended.

"Nessa gets to do that."

"Do what?"

"Say no when I ask how much."

"Learn from her."

"I refuse."

Short was next.

I moved the correct red cloth.

This was my contribution to art.

After the show, the broad man in the dark coat came back to the door.

My shoulders tightened again.

He held out a small wooden token.

"My wife dropped this."

I looked at the lost-things cup.

The matching token sat inside.

I handed it to him.

He nodded.

"Good show."

"Thank you."

"King was better last month."

"I'll tell him."

"Don't."

He left.

Ordinary people were exhausting.

Cleanup took longer because one bench had shifted enough to wobble on
the floor.

Davin fixed it with a thin scrap under one foot.

I did not ask whether this was another secret system.

I knew.

Jori checked the cart.

Chalk straight.

Rinna counted tickets.

One copper came to me.

Normal local pay.

I took it.

She said, "Tomorrow."

"Vale first bell. Hessa after midday. Then here if useful."

"Correct."

"You want me after Guild?"

"If Hessa says you can work."

"She'll say no lifting if I look tired."

"Then don't lift."

"What do I do?"

"We'll discover whether you have other skills."

"Harsh."

"Go."

I did.

At the pay table, I unfolded Vale's note again.

Rinna noticed.

"You're reading the same words."

"They may improve."

"They won't."

"Antonius writes efficiently."

"Then why read twice?"

"Hostility."

She took the note before I could stop her.

Not snatched.

Simply removed from my hand with the confidence of somebody accustomed
to props being in the wrong place.

"Delivery accounts," she read. "One hour."

"Private correspondence."

"You showed me the time."

"I showed you the time."

"Now I've seen the rest."

She handed it back.

"Debt credit?"

"Yes."

"How much?"

"Whatever the work earns against the debt."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the arrangement."

"How much debt?"

"No."

Rinna raised one eyebrow.

I had apparently learned Nessa's technique after all.

"Does Vale know about the men?" she asked.

"No."

"Are you going to tell him?"

"I don't know."

That answer bothered me because it was true.

Rinna folded today's ticket count.

"Don't tell him for us."

"I wouldn't."

"If it comes up, it comes up. But the hall didn't hire Vale."

"Vale would probably charge the hall for being rescued."

"Then definitely don't."

I put the note away.

That settled one thing.

If Antonius heard about Bren, it would not be because I marched into his
office and offered him the theatre's problem like an unpaid invoice.

On the way home I passed a Vale warehouse.

Not one I had worked in recently.

Two men rolled a barrel through the wide door while a clerk stood beside
a board and argued about a number.

A cart waited outside.

Its driver had one foot on the wheel hub.

I looked at the pin.

Present.

Of course.

Antonius's world was full of carts.

Warehouses.

Deliveries.

People who owed money.

People waiting to be paid.

People arguing over whether three jars had arrived broken or two.

Bren's world, as far as I knew, was two men at a theatre door and a
missing pin.

The overlap was not a connection.

It was Carrow.

That mattered.

I kept walking.

Lyssa was home before me.

She had changed out of the dark work wrap and was cutting the bad edge
off an onion.

Not my onion.

We had become onion-rich by accident.

"Vale?" she asked.

"Tomorrow."

"You didn't go?"

"Note."

"Ah."

I put the note on the table.

She read it.

"Delivery accounts."

"Yes."

"One hour."

"Yes."

"Transport covered."

"I can walk."

"That's not what it says."

"I know."

She gave me a look.

My leg had made certain arguments less philosophical.

"If he sends transport, use it," she said.

"I enjoy arriving under my own power."

"You enjoy being difficult."

"Also true."

She put onion into a pan with a little fat.

"What happened at theatre?"

"Nothing."

She waited.

"Actual nothing," I said. "Cart pin still there. Bren did not arrive.
Nobody threatened anyone."

"Good."

"There was a suspicious man."

Her hand stopped.

"He wanted tickets."

She resumed cutting.

"Greg."

"I discovered that."

"Before or after you stared at him?"

"During."

"Good."

"Why does everyone keep saying that?"

She ignored me.

I told her about the fly.

That got more interest than Vale.

Correctly.

Then I told her tomorrow's schedule.

"First bell Vale. After midday Hessa. Theatre after if I'm useful."

"That's three."

"Two and a possible."

"Still three places."

"Only if the third place wants me."

She put the knife down.

"Vale debt still alive."

"Yes."

"You knew."

"People keep emphasizing that."

"Because you keep acting surprised."

"I was surprised by timing."

"Why?"

"I've been busy."

"So has he."

That was fair.

Antonius Vale had not disappeared because I had joined a theatre.

My debt had not dissolved because Bren had walked through a door.

Hessa's paper did not care about either of them.

Tomorrow, I would spend an hour looking at somebody else's delivery
accounts, then sit behind glass and ask folded paper to move away from
me at two distances.

After that, if my arm still belonged to me, Rinna might find something
for me to carry without carrying it.

Lyssa put food down.

Onion.

Bread.

A little fat.

No beans.

"Luxury," I said.

"Eat."

I did.

The Vale note stayed beside Hessa's note.

One debt.

One experiment.

Neither had anything to do with Bren.

Probably.

I left it there.

The lamp burned steadily while we ate.

That was my household scale now: not wealth, not safety, just enough oil
that the room did not flicker every time the wick reconsidered its
profession.

I looked at the two notes again.

Lyssa said, "Stop."

"I wasn't touching them."

"You were thinking loudly."

I ate the rest of the bread.

Tomorrow could arrive without further preparation.

This was apparently allowed.
