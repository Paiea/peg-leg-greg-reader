PEG-LEG GREG
LIGHTART
CHAPTER 213

THE COUNTER

Antonius sent a cart.

Not a carriage.

That distinction mattered because one had cushions and the other had a
sack of nails under the bench.

The boy from yesterday sat beside the driver.

He looked at my crutches.

"Vale said transport."

"I can walk."

"He said transport."

Lyssa stood behind me in the doorway.

"Get in."

I looked at her.

She looked at the cart.

I got in.

The sack of nails shifted under my good foot as we started.

Luxury.

Carrow at first bell belonged to people who had already decided the day
was late. Bakers had doors open. Carts occupied corners as if streets
had been built around them. A man carried three empty baskets on his
head and insulted somebody I could not see.

Vale's office was attached to one of the smaller warehouses, which meant
it was still larger than our room and contained fewer useful onions.

The driver stopped near the side door.

The boy said, "One hour."

"I remember."

He shrugged.

Apparently he was not responsible for whether I did.

Inside, a clerk pointed me toward the back table.

Antonius Vale sat there with three narrow stacks of paper and a cup he
was not drinking from.

He looked almost exactly as he had the last time I had seen him.

This was unreasonable.

I had lost a leg, joined a theatre, moved paper with mana, acquired
several kinds of employment, and accidentally convinced an extortionist
I might be dangerous.

Antonius had changed coats.

"I thought you'd forgotten me," I said.

"I have a ledger."

There it was.

I sat.

He looked at my crutches leaning against the chair, then at me.

"You move better."

"I've practiced."

"Walking?"

"Mostly falling less."

He slid the first stack toward me.

"You are harder to schedule."

"I have employment."

"You have several fragments of employment."

"That's still plural."

"Unfortunately."

The work was exactly what the note had promised.

Delivery accounts.

No hidden message.

No criminal code.

No secret list of men named Bren.

Three warehouse receiving sheets. Four cart departure tallies. Six
invoices. Two repair charges. One complaint from a customer who had
apparently believed a late barrel of lamp oil had ruined a wedding.

Antonius tapped the piles.

"These should agree."

"They don't?"

"If they agreed, I would not need you."

"Maybe you enjoy my company."

"No."

Good.

I started with dates.

Then cart marks.

Then loads.

The first disagreement was boring.

A clerk had copied the previous day's cart mark onto the next line. The
receiving record had the correct one because the driver had signed
beside it.

I put the two papers together.

"This is copied."

Antonius looked.

"Why?"

"Same mark two days. Different driver. The second receiving sheet has
the other cart."

"Which means?"

"Clerk copied yesterday before writing today."

"Probably."

"You don't sound impressed."

"I pay clerks to create fewer problems than they solve."

"I could create more."

"I know."

He made a small mark in his ledger.

The second problem took longer.

Three delivery invoices had deductions for late arrival.

Two matched the receiving times.

The third did not.

The invoice said the load arrived after second bell because of a wheel
repair.

The warehouse receiving sheet had it before first bell.

The repair charge existed.

Wrong day.

I spread the papers.

"This repair belongs to the next run."

Antonius looked at the repair slip.

"Why?"

"Cart mark."

"Same cart."

"Yes. But the repairman wrote the warehouse destination in the margin.
This one went south. The delayed invoice went west."

He took the slip.

The margin was faint.

"Useful."

"I prefer praise with more emotion."

"No."

He moved the corrected papers aside.

Most of the rest was ordinary mess.

A receiving clerk counted nine jars where the invoice said ten. A note
attached to the invoice said one jar had cracked while loading, which
solved the mystery unless the cracked jar had developed a commercial
afterlife.

A driver had written his own name differently on two sheets.

A repair charge for a wheel rim was real and correctly placed.

One late delivery was simply late because the driver had left late.

I found no conspiracy.

This was comforting.

Then I reached a receiving sheet marked for a yard near Mason's Cut.

I stopped.

Only for a moment.

Antonius said, "What?"

"Nothing."

"That was not nothing."

I looked at the sheet again.

It was not Talla's yard.

At least not the name she had given Rinna.

It was a receiving point two streets off Mason's Cut.

Ordinary.

"Place," I said.

"What place?"

"Mason's Cut."

Antonius waited.

I considered Rinna's instruction.

Do not tell Vale for us.

Fair.

"I am not bringing you a problem from the theatre."

"I did not ask for one."

"Good."

"Now tell me why you stopped."

I hated efficient people.

"Two men came to the hall."

Antonius's expression did not change.

"They wanted a small share."

"Of what?"

"Us."

"That is not an amount."

"Apparently it becomes one after you agree."

That got the smallest pause.

I continued.

"They mentioned Havel work. Said carts lose wheels. Lamps disappear.
Doors stop opening. Rinna refused."

"And?"

"Later somebody pulled the retaining pin from our handcart."

"Did you see them do it?"

"No."

"Then somebody pulled a pin."

"Yes."

He waited.

"Another cart yard near Mason's Cut got the same kind of visit," I said.
"They paid once."

"And?"

"More."

Antonius leaned back slightly.

"Of course."

"You say that like it's obvious."

"It is obvious after it happens."

That was annoyingly close to my opinion of plans.

"The man is probably called Bren," I said.

"Probably?"

"Someone at the other yard heard another man call him that."

"Bren what?"

"No surname."

"Then you have half a name."

"He has one glove."

Antonius looked at me.

"That is less than half a name."

"Left."

"Still."

"He may have something wrong with the hand."

"May?"

"I made him think I knew."

That finally changed Antonius's face.

Not much.

His eyes stayed on me a little longer.

"Why?"

"Because I didn't want him to break anything."

"Did it work?"

"He left."

"Then what?"

"He said he'd come back."

Antonius looked down at the delivery sheet.

"That is not the same as working."

"I know."

I did not tell him about the broom shadow.

Or Sellen's coat.

Or paper.

Especially paper.

He tapped the Mason's Cut sheet once.

"Did Rinna send you to tell me this?"

"No."

"Did she ask for help?"

"No."

"Do you?"

"No."

The answer came quickly enough that he looked at me again.

"I don't know what you would do," I added.

"Good."

"That sounded insulting."

"It was practical."

He stacked the Mason's Cut sheet with the others.

I said, "You don't care?"

"I care when people interfere with repayment."

I stared.

Antonius continued, "If the theatre pays you, and you pay me in labor or
coin, damage to the theatre can affect me."

"That is the nicest thing you've said to me."

"It was not intended kindly."

"Still counts."

"No."

I smiled despite myself.

He did not.

"Small share," Antonius said.

"Yes."

"After visible better work."

"Havel."

"And the other yard?"

"Good runs. Good coin."

He nodded once.

"The first amount tests whether payment is easier than refusal."

"And if they pay?"

"They have answered the question."

That was all.

No lecture.

No criminal map.

No revelation that Bren had once borrowed money from Antonius's cousin's
warehouse keeper.

Just arithmetic.

I looked at the papers.

"The cart yard paid once."

"And was asked for more."

"Yes."

"Then they answered clearly."

The clerk near the door called that first bell had passed.

Antonius checked the small sand timer on the table.

My hour was nearly gone.

He pointed at the corrected stacks.

"Two copper credit."

"Against the debt."

"Obviously."

"Cash would be more emotionally satisfying."

"You owe me."

"People keep remembering."

"I have a ledger."

"Yes."

He recorded the credit.

I did not see the remaining total.

I did not ask.

That felt like maturity or cowardice.

Possibly both.

As I stood, Antonius said, "If you get the other half of the name, tell
me."

"Why?"

"Because I asked."

"That isn't a reason."

"It is from this side of the table."

I put my crutches under me.

"Are you going to do something?"

"I have half a name and a story from my debtor about somebody else's
theatre."

"When you say it like that, it sounds weak."

"It is weak."

"Good."

"If it becomes relevant to my business, it may stop being weak."

There he was.

Not future Antonius.

Not yet.

But I could see the shape of the man who would eventually understand
that money moved through people before it moved through ledgers.

Or maybe I was cheating because I already knew.

"Do not invent relevance," Antonius said.

I blinked.

"What?"

"You are doing something with your face."

"I hate that everyone can see my face."

"Then acquire a better one."

I left.

The Vale cart took me only as far as the central market because I told
the driver I had time.

He did not care.

Hessa's appointment was after midday.

I bought nothing.

This felt financially sophisticated.

At the Guild, the gray-coated woman saw me in the corridor.

"Paper Man."

"Comparative Paper Man."

She stopped.

"That is worse."

"I've been told."

Hessa had the same room prepared.

Same pale board.

Same chair mark.

Same glass.

Near remained marked.

Intermediate remained marked.

Far remained marked.

No one had improved the paper.

I sat.

Hessa looked at my hands.

"What did you do this morning?"

"Accounts."

"Physical."

"Sat."

"Before that?"

"Cart."

She looked up.

"Rode in one."

"Good."

"Lyssa made me."

"Also good."

"I dislike consensus."

She checked wrist rotation.

Fine.

Arm lift.

Fine.

Forearm.

Boring.

Shoulder.

Boring.

"Water?"

"Some."

She gave me more.

"This experiment has a lot of water."

"Your body continues to have a lot of water."

Apparently that joke had survived peer review.

She showed me today's page.

INTERMEDIATE / FAR.

AWAY ONLY.

SAME APPARATUS.

"We are not testing farther," she said.

"We are literally testing far."

"We are comparing the established intermediate position with the
existing far position."

"Which is farther."

"Do you want to go home?"

"No."

"Then be precise."

I drank water precisely.

She had already written the order on a folded slip and placed it face
down.

"You chose before I came?"

"Before you attempt."

"Why?"

"So I do not choose based on what happens."

That seemed unfair to outcomes.

She turned the slip.

INTERMEDIATE.

FAR.

FAR.

INTERMEDIATE.

"Four?"

"If your body stays boring."

"My body is deeply committed to boring."

"We will see."

Baseline first.

The thirtieth successful supervised minimal draw came without surprise.

The gathering was familiar now.

Small heat behind the sternum.

An inward pull that still felt less like grabbing something and more
like noticing a current already moving.

The wrist-side unevenness remained.

No increase.

No sudden reservoir.

No hidden second Greg.

Hessa watched.

"Enough."

I released.

She marked it.

Then the twenty-sixth supervised deliberate shaping attempt.

The gathered mana crowded toward the wrist side.

I tried to give it the rough deliberate form we had been using.

The shoulder wanted to join.

I lowered it.

The shape softened.

I did not chase.

It held badly for two breaths.

Then less badly for one.

Then it was gone.

Hessa wrote.

"Better?" I asked.

"Comparable."

"I miss praise."

"You have theatre."

Cruel.

External attempt thirty-one.

Intermediate.

Fresh matched fins.

Target and control.

Glass down.

Same chair.

Same foot.

Same hands.

Same request.

AWAY FROM GREG.

I gathered.

Shaped.

Kept it small.

Requested away.

Nothing.

Then the lower edge moved.

Tiny.

Clean.

Less than the width of my smallest fingernail.

Away.

Control unchanged.

Hessa checked.

"Response."

She wrote:

31. INTERMEDIATE.

TARGET DISPLACED AWAY.

SMALL.

CONTROL UNCHANGED.

I looked at Far.

She said, "Don't."

"I am allowed to look."

"Apparently not productively."

Fresh pair.

Attempt thirty-two.

Far.

The extra distance was not dramatic.

That was the annoying part.

The paper was still right there.

Reachable if I leaned.

Not reachable under the rules.

I gathered.

The shape was rougher.

I backed off.

Requested away.

Nothing.

I waited.

Nothing.

A shadow shifted.

I kept my head still this time.

Still nothing.

Hessa checked both fins.

"No observed response."

She wrote it.

I exhaled.

"Far remains rude."

"Far remains far."

"That was almost poetry."

"No."

Rest.

Water.

Hessa read another page while I sat with my hands open.

Someone outside asked for a frame.

Someone else said Soren had it.

A third person said Soren did not have it.

The frame developed a social life without us.

My forearm felt used.

Not tight.

Wrist fine.

Shoulder quiet.

Hessa checked anyway.

Then attempt thirty-three.

Far again.

Fresh pair.

Same everything.

I gathered.

The shape came cleaner at first.

Then tightened.

I let it become smaller.

Requested away.

For a breath, nothing.

Then the top edge turned.

Not much.

The lower corner shifted.

I stopped breathing.

Hessa leaned closer.

"Do not add."

I didn't.

The fin settled.

She checked from one side.

Then the other.

The target had moved.

Barely.

But the far corner now sat farther from me than it had.

The control had not changed.

Hessa looked at the marks.

Then at the paper.

"Observed."

My chest tightened for an entirely different reason.

"Far."

"One far response."

"Still far."

"Yes."

"At far."

"Yes."

I smiled.

She pointed at me.

"Do not make it larger."

"I haven't said anything."

"You are about to."

I was.

She wrote:

33. FAR.

SMALL ROTATIONAL RESPONSE.

NET DISPLACEMENT AWAY OBSERVED.

CONTROL UNCHANGED.

ONE OBSERVATION.

I read the last two words.

"You wrote that aggressively."

"I wrote it legibly."

Attempt thirty-four.

Intermediate.

Fresh pair.

My forearm was more obviously used now.

Still no pain.

Hessa made me rotate the wrist.

Fine.

Lift.

Fine.

"Last."

"I know."

"Say it."

"Last."

Same position.

Same request.

The gathering came.

Shape came rough.

I let it stay rough.

Requested away.

Nothing happened immediately.

Then the target slipped.

Small.

Straight enough that even I did not need to negotiate with the angle.

Away.

Control unchanged.

Hessa checked.

Then stopped the session.

No fifth attempt.

I did not ask.

This was growth.

Possibly.

She wrote for a while.

I drank water and tried not to turn one far movement into a kingdom.

Eventually she turned the page toward me.

I pointed.

"So it reaches far."

"No."

"It moved at far."

"Once."

"That is a response at far."

"Yes."

"So it reaches far."

"No."

She stared back.

"Your problem," I said, "is language."

"My problem is you."

Fair.

"What can I say?"

"That a response was observed once at the far position in the current

"That sentence needs food."

"It is accurate."

"Paper moved farther away than before."

"Paper at the far position responded once today."

"Somewhat less close to me."

She considered.

"Still bad."

"Progress."

"No."

All of them.

No independent draw.

No independent shaping.

No paper.

"Forearm used."

"Wrist?"

"Fine."

"Fine."

I left with counts that had become:

thirty successful supervised minimal draws.

Twenty-six supervised deliberate shaping attempts.

The afternoon house had already started when I reached the hall.

I checked the cart once.

Chalk straight.

Rinna saw me.

"Body?"

"Fine. No lifting."

She pointed at a stool beside the front table.

"Sit."

"I have become management."

"No."

There were six late arrivals during the next piece.

I took their copper.

One tried to argue the show was already partly over and therefore should
cost less.

I told him the remaining actors were still full size.

He paid.

Rinna heard.

She did not praise me.

I worked the front until the house ended.

No carrying.

No set.

No acting.

No Bren.

No broken pin.

No suspicious glove.

At close, Rinna gave me one copper.

I looked at it.

"For sitting?"

"For tickets."

"Vale gave me two copper credit for sitting."

"Go work for Vale."

"I owe him money."

"Then this seems difficult."

I took the copper.

At home, Lyssa had bread and a small bowl of cooked greens.

She looked at me.

"Vale?"

"Two copper off the debt."

"Good."

"Paper?"

"Four tries."

"How many?"

"Three responses."

She waited.

"Two intermediate. One far. One far did nothing."

"Far moved?"

"Once."

"Does that mean far works?"

I pointed at her.

"You've been talking to Hessa."

"No."

"It means a response was observed once at far."

Lyssa smiled.

I hated everyone.

"Theatre?"

"One copper."

"Bren?"

"No."

That was the whole day.

Debt moved.

Paper moved.

Tickets moved.

Bren did not.

For once, I was willing to count all four.
