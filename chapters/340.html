CHAPTER 340

THE MOVING LINE

Hessa's note arrived folded around a piece of string.

Not chalk this time.

String.

I distrusted variety.

The note said:

GREG

MIDDAY

SOUTH WALK

BOTH CRUTCHES

NO EQUIPMENT

HESSA

Underneath, smaller:

WEATHER IF STUPID

I read that twice.

Then looked out the window.

Carrow had produced one of those bright gray days where the clouds could not decide whether they were threatening rain or merely being dramatic.

Wind moved the laundry lines across the neighboring roofs.

Not hard.

Enough.

Lyssa looked up from the sleeve she was repairing.

"Hessa?"

"Apparently weather may be stupid."

"Then you'll fit in."

"Household support remains excellent."

I left the oval home.

That felt strange.

Not difficult.

Just noticeable.

The rest stone stayed boxed.

The holdfast stayed shelved.

One copper stayed in my purse because Hessa had specifically said no equipment, not no money, and I refused to become the sort of person who interpreted training notes as tax law.

South Walk turned out to be exactly what it sounded like.

A long covered stone arcade along the southern edge of an old civic yard.

Roof overhead.

Open arches on one side.

Wall on the other.

Wind came through anyway.

Of course it did.

Hessa was standing halfway down the arcade with a coil of thin white string, six chalk marks on the floor, and a shallow wooden tray.

No bead visible.

That was suspicious.

"You're late," she said.

"By how much?"

"You're not."

"Then that was hostile."

"Useful test."

"Of what?"

"Whether you'd waste time defending punctuality."

I stared at her.

"I did."

"Yes."

Terrible teacher.

I liked her.

The South Walk was empty except for us and one old man reading under the farthest arch.

He did not look up.

Good.

Hessa pointed at my bag.

"No equipment."

"Money."

"Keep it."

"Generous."

"Put the bag against the wall."

I did.

She looked at my crutches.

"Any shoulder issue from theatre?"

That got my attention.

"How do you know theatre?"

"You live in a city."

"That is not an answer."

"Any shoulder issue?"

"No. Ordinary fatigue. Fine today."

"Right leg?"

"Fine."

"Left knee?"

"Fine."

She nodded.

No ceremony.

No explanation for how she knew.

Annoying.

She pointed at the first two chalk marks.

"Minimal draws. Walking."

I blinked.

"Walking while drawing."

"Yes."

"Mana?"

"No. Decorative breathing."

"You could be clearer."

"I could."

There were six chalk marks in a straight line, each perhaps four normal steps apart.

For me, more complicated.

Crutch.

Right foot.

Crutch.

Swing.

Set.

Not exactly that every time.

The body found its own sequence when I stopped naming it.

Hessa took the string.

Tied one end loosely to a small brass ring set into the wall.

The other end she held.

The string crossed the walkway at waist height, then sagged slightly.

"What's that for?"

"Later."

"I hate later."

"Walk."

I started at the first chalk mark.

Minimal draw.

No shaping.

No external effect.

Just the smallest supervised pull I had been doing since Hessa decided my instincts were a civic hazard.

First attempt.

Clean enough.

Second chalk mark.

I moved.

Drew again while the left crutch was forward.

The draw came rough.

Not failed.

Uneven.

Third.

I timed it after right-foot contact.

Cleaner.

Fourth.

Mid-swing.

Bad.

The draw broke almost immediately.

Hessa said nothing.

Fifth.

I waited for both crutches to settle.

Clean.

Sixth.

Tried before the right foot landed.

Thin and ugly.

Hessa raised one finger.

"Stop."

I stopped.

"Six," she said.

I nodded.

Current total forty-seven minimal draws.

I would write it later.

Probably.

Maybe.

No, I would.

Counts mattered here.

"What changed?" Hessa asked.

"Body contact."

"Too broad."

"When I draw during a stable contact phase, cleaner. During swing or transition, worse."

"Which contact?"

I thought.

"Not sure. Both crutches planted was best. Right foot planted also good. Left-side body loading feels different because there is no foot contact."

"Good."

Not praise.

Confirmation.

She moved the tray into view.

Inside was the same plain white ceramic bead from Chapter Three Hundred Thirty-Three.

I recognized it.

"You kept it."

"It belongs to me."

"Emotionally?"

"Legally."

"Colder."

She set the bead on the floor near the wall.

"No external effect yet. Shaping only."

Nine deliberate shaping attempts.

She made me walk between the chalk marks while holding a tiny bounded shape at different points in my gait.

Not far.

Not clever.

No target.

Just sustain something while the body moved underneath it.

First three were awful.

One collapsed when my right crutch landed harder than expected.

One overcorrected because I felt the shape thin while my shoulders shifted.

One lasted almost two steps, then smeared when I changed pace.

Hessa said, "You're treating every body change as mana change."

"It feels like mana change."

"Of course it does."

"Helpful."

"Your body is moving. Your perception is moving with it. The field may not be doing what you think."

That landed.

Not profound.

Annoyingly practical.

Attempts four through six improved when she made me choose one gait phase and only shape there.

Crutches planted.

Draw.

Hold.

Move.

Release.

Again.

The fourth held for half a breath.

Fifth held a little longer.

Sixth was clean enough that Hessa said, "Again differently."

Of course.

Seven through nine she made me shape during movement instead of at stable contact.

Not full continuous control.

Just start after push-off and release before landing.

Attempt seven failed almost immediately.

Eight held, thin.

Nine held through one full body transition without the late shove.

I smiled.

Hessa said, "Don't."

"I'm allowed facial expressions."

"Not that one."

"Which one?"

"The one before you chase a result."

I stopped smiling.

Rude.

Accurate.

Nine deliberate shaping attempts.

Total fifty.

That number felt suspiciously clean.

I refused to care.

Hessa picked up the bead.

Then the string finally became relevant.

She tied the bead to it.

Not through the bead.

The bead sat in a tiny cloth loop that hung from the string like a bad ornament.

"Moving target," I said.

"Not yet."

She pulled the free end of the string.

The bead slid along the waist-high line from one wall point toward her.

Slow.

Very slow.

"That's a moving target."

"The target moves. You do not."

"Pedantic."

"Necessary."

She reset the bead near the wall.

"External effect. One grain toward you. Same magnitude as last session when possible."

"On the hanging bead?"

"Yes."

"While it moves?"

"Eventually."

First she left it still.

Attempt one.

Near enough.

Tiny movement.

Hard to judge because hanging bead wanted to swing.

Hessa had anticipated that.

Of course.

The cloth loop touched a second lower thread that acted as a guide.

Not rigid.

Enough to limit side swing.

"Again."

Attempt two.

Clean one-grain-ish movement along the string.

"Good."

Then she moved the bead.

Slow slide.

Attempt three.

No contact.

Attempt four.

I reached too late.

The bead had moved through the place I was holding in my head.

Attempt five.

I chased it.

Bad.

The effect smeared behind the bead like I was trying to push yesterday.

Hessa stopped the string.

"What are you targeting?"

"The bead."

"No."

"The moving bead."

"Still no."

I stared.

She waited.

I hated teachers who waited.

"A predicted position?"

"Better."

"The point where the bead will be when the effect lands."

"Better."

"That's the same thing."

"It isn't."

Unfortunately, she was right.

A target was not always an object.

Sometimes it was an intersection between object and time.

I did not say that out loud because it sounded like something I'd write on a wall and regret.

Attempt six.

Slow bead.

I targeted ahead.

Too far.

Nothing.

Attempt seven.

Ahead, less.

The bead twitched.

Maybe one grain.

Hessa said, "Count it as contact, not clean."

Good.

Attempt eight.

Clean.

Tiny movement toward me while the bead was already moving sideways along the string.

The vectors made my brain itch.

The effect did not stop the slide.

Just added a little inward bias.

"There," I said.

"Yes."

She kept moving the bead.

Attempt nine.

Failed.

Attempt ten.

Clean enough.

Attempt eleven.

Overstrong.

Two or three grains equivalent.

Hessa's face changed.

Not alarm.

Disapproval.

"You corrected late."

"I felt it miss."

"And then?"

"Pushed."

"Why?"

"Because I wanted contact."

"Yes."

There it was again.

Greed.

Not money.

Result greed.

The same ugly little instinct wearing different clothes.

Attempt twelve.

She slowed the bead further.

I let one pass without acting.

That surprised me.

"You skipped it," Hessa said.

"Bad timing."

"Good."

Then on the next pass I acted.

Clean small movement.

She stopped the bead.

"Twelve external effects."

I nodded.

Total one hundred three.

Forty-seven minimal.

Fifty deliberate shaping.

One hundred three external.

I repeated them silently.

Hessa untied the bead.

I thought we were done.

Wrong.

She moved the chalk marks.

Not all of them.

Three.

She placed one near the wall, one halfway across the arcade, one under the outer arch where wind moved through.

Then put the bead on the floor at the middle mark.

"Now you move. Target does not."

I exhaled.

"You enjoy separating axes."

"You enjoy combining them and naming the failure one thing."

Fair.

No new external attempt yet.

She first had me walk past the bead three times without mana.

Just route.

Crutches.

Right foot.

Body angle.

Where I looked.

Where the bead entered and left peripheral vision.

The first pass I watched it too hard and clipped my right crutch tip against a chalk edge.

No fall.

Just ugly contact.

Hessa said, "Again."

Second pass I looked ahead.

Better.

Third pass I let the bead live in peripheral vision.

Best.

"Why?" she asked.

"Because if I stare down, I ruin walking geometry."

"And?"

"I don't need visual fixation to hold target position."

"Do you know that?"

I paused.

"No. I suspect it."

"Better."

We did not add counts because no magic.

Then she allowed three more deliberate shaping attempts while walking past the stationary bead.

I objected.

"You said nine shaping attempts."

"I said nothing of the kind."

She had not.

I had counted nine because she stopped after nine.

Annoying.

So total would change again.

Attempt ten shaping.

Failed during turn.

Eleven.

Held through one pass.

Twelve.

Held while I did not look directly at bead.

Interesting.

Total deliberate shaping fifty-three.

Then Hessa said, "No external effect."

I looked at her.

"Why set this up?"

"To see if you would ask."

"I am asking."

"Good."

"And?"

"You're done."

I stared at the bead.

Static.

Right there.

I wanted one attempt.

Badly.

Not because it mattered.

Because the experiment had created the question.

Could I move while affecting a stationary target?

Everything in me wanted the answer immediately.

Hessa watched me want it.

"No," she said.

"I didn't ask."

"You were about to."

"Maybe."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because you did enough today to create a cleaner question."

"And now we stop before answering it."

"Yes."

"Cruel."

"Useful."

I looked down the arcade.

The old man at the far arch turned a page.

The weather had remained stupid but not malicious.

Wind pushed a little rain through the open side and dotted the stone near the outer chalk mark.

Hessa picked up the bead.

"What did you learn?"

"Moving target is not the same as far target."

"Obvious."

"Body movement changes my perception of control."

"Better."

"Stable contact phases are easier for minimal draw than swing phases."

"Today."

"Today."

"Good."

"I chase moving targets by correcting behind them."

"Yes."

"Prediction matters."

"Maybe."

"You hate declarative statements."

"Only unsupported ones."

"That's most of mine."

"Yes."

I considered.

"I can produce a small clean effect on a slowly moving target under supervision."

"More precise."

"At near range."

"Good."

"Using one-grain-ish magnitude."

"Good."

"With at least two clean or clean-enough contacts today."

"Three, depending how strict you are."

"You decide counts."

"No. You record your standard."

I hated responsibility.

"And we did not test me moving with external effect."

"Correct."

"Deliberately."

"Correct."

"Because you are evil."

"Unrelated."

I smiled.

She did not tell me to stop.

Progress.

Then she said, "One more thing."

Of course.

She pointed at my crutches.

"When you felt the shape thin during movement, where did you correct from?"

I thought back.

Shoulder?

Hands?

Mana sense?

"Usually after the right crutch loaded."

"Why?"

"Because that's when I felt the drop most clearly."

"Or when your upper body changed most clearly."

There.

Not conclusion.

A split.

Mana instability versus body-sensation contamination.

"How do we separate those?"

"Later."

I hated later.

She handed me the piece of string from the note.

"What's this for?"

"You can keep it."

"Why?"

"So you stop thinking every object I hand you is instruction."

I looked at the string.

Plain white cord.

Nothing magical.

Probably.

"This is psychological warfare."

"Go home, Greg."

I went home.

Not directly.

The rain had strengthened enough to make the shortest route unpleasant, so I took the covered market arcade.

Different route.

More people.

A fruit seller had moved half her table three feet inward because water was coming through one broken gutter.

A courier ran past with a waxed shoulder cape.

Two children were floating folded paper boats down the curb channel and arguing over whether touching a boat disqualified the race.

Normal city magic remained absent.

Normal city nonsense did not.

I liked that.

At home, Tavi immediately saw the string.

"Mine?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Hessa gave it to me."

"Magic?"

"No."

"Then mine?"

Strong market pressure.

I considered.

The point, apparently, was to stop treating every object as instruction.

Giving it to Tavi might count.

"You may have half."

"Why half?"

"Reserve."

He accepted this immediately, which was concerning.

I cut the string.

He took his half to the spoon carrier.

Of course.

Lyssa asked, "How was training?"

"I learned walking is harder when I think about walking."

"Advanced."

"Also moving targets are inconvenient."

"Very advanced."

Cala called from the kitchen, "Did he fall?"

"No."

"Then good."

Household standards remained excellent.

I went upstairs with my half of the string.

The oval was where I had left it.

The rest stone box remained closed.

The holdfast sat on its high shelf.

Three artifacts.

None relevant.

That felt healthy.

I took out the narrow scrap.

Counts first.

41 / 41 / 91 became:

47 MINIMAL

53 DELIBERATE SHAPING

103 EXTERNAL EFFECT

Then I added:

MOVEMENT CHANGES CONTROL PROBLEM

Crossed out CONTROL PROBLEM.

Too broad.

Wrote:

BODY MOTION + MANA PERCEPTION MAY BE MIXED

Then:

STABLE CONTACT PHASE EASIER TODAY

Then:

MOVING TARGET: STOP CHASING WHERE IT WAS

I stared at that one.

Too wise again.

I almost crossed it out.

Did not.

Useful enough.

Then:

NO TEST YET: GREG MOVING + EXTERNAL EFFECT

That was the clean future question.

Not answer.

Question.

I put the scrap away.

The one copper stayed in my purse.

The rest stone stayed boxed.

Possible theatre work stayed possible.

Magic stayed supervised.

For once, the number of unfinished things in my life did not feel like failure.

It felt like inventory.

I disliked that thought less than expected.
