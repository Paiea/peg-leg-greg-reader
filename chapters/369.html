CHAPTER 369

THE CARRIER

The harness became useful because Lyssa made too much cloth.

Not personally.

That would have been alarming.

She had finished Marra's first lot.

Twelve aprons.

Twelve sleeve pairs.

Eight carry wraps.

Which, when folded, became three bundles large enough to make the table look employed.

I stood beside them wearing the porter harness.

Lyssa looked at me.

Then the bundles.

Then me again.

"No."

"I haven't said anything."

"Your face has."

"My face is innocent."

"Your face bought an old harness yesterday and has been waiting for excuse."

That was unfair.

Mostly.

Cala was tying the second bundle with gray cord.

"Light carry," she said.

"Yes."

"Buckle not trusted past twelve pounds."

"Ten to twelve."

"That is not improvement."

"Precision is always improvement."

Lyssa put both hands on hips.

Charcoal coat hung from chair behind her.

She was not wearing it.

Work morning.

Old shirt, sleeves rolled, hair tied badly because she'd redone it twice and stopped caring.

Her fingers had two tiny red marks where needle had won arguments.

"You're carrying one," she said.

"Two side loops."

"One."

"Harness has ambitions."

"Harness can develop them slowly."

Cala nodded.

Household conspiracy.

I accepted.

One bundle.

Not the largest.

The eight carry wraps went together, narrow folded stack tied twice.

Cala lifted it with both hands and judged weight.

"Maybe six pounds."

Lyssa lifted too.

"Seven."

"How did you gain one pound by touching it?" I asked.

"Experience."

"Magic."

"Cloth."

We fitted bundle into right-side loop.

Not elegant.

Harness side loop had been made for documents, tools, narrow parcels.

This was a narrow parcel if generous with definition.

I shortened lower strap until bundle sat behind elbow line.

Crutch clear.

Upper edge pressed ribs lightly when I leaned left.

I adjusted again.

Better.

Lyssa crouched in front of me and pulled cord.

"Too loose."

"Harness strap or bundle tie?"

"Bundle."

She retied.

I watched because her hands were faster than mine and because bending over tied parcel while balancing on one leg and two crutches was possible but stupid.

There were many things I could do.

Useful category was smaller.

Tavi came downstairs.

Stopped.

"You're wearing cloth."

"Carrying."

"Looks like wearing."

"It's a harness."

"Still wearing."

He had me there.

He walked around once.

"Can I put sword in it?"

"No."

"What if wood sword?"

"No."

"What if spoon?"

"Why spoon?"

"Sword size."

I looked at Cala.

She was smiling into cord.

Traitor.

Lyssa lifted the other two bundles, one under each arm, then immediately put one back.

"That's stupid."

"Growth," I said.

"Don't."

She took largest apron bundle against chest and left sleeve pairs for Cala to carry to door.

Then reconsidered.

"Actually."

She looked at me.

I waited.

"Could second fit over left shoulder bag?"

"Probably."

"Safely?"

Better question.

I tested empty geometry with bundle held against bag.

No.

Left side made crutch swing tight.

"Not comfortably."

"Then no."

Easy.

No heroic cargo optimization.

We had bodies.

Bodies had limits.

We also had streets and hands and possibility of making two trips.

Cala said, "I'll walk to corner with third. You take it from there after you set first down at Marra's."

Lyssa looked at clock.

"That adds maybe ten minutes."

"Yes."

"Fine."

Nobody died from logistics.

A lesson civilization learned repeatedly.

We left after first bell.

I carried wraps on right harness.

Lyssa carried aprons.

Cala carried sleeves as far as East Cloth corner, where Jessa's younger cousin happened to be passing with empty handcart.

Not fate.

Carrow.

He knew Cala, knew Marra's green door, and offered cart corner-to-shop because he was going two doors past anyway.

Cala said yes before I could turn coincidence into philosophy.

Sleeve bundle went into cart.

No fee.

No debt.

Just someone going same way.

This city was dangerous that way.

You could start expecting people.

We walked.

The harness worked.

That was almost disappointing in simplicity.

No glow.

No hidden maker mark.

No old porter spirit waking because I bore honest labor.

Leather creaked.

Bundle stayed against right side.

Weight sat lower than shoulder bag.

My right crutch cleared.

On steeper curb I had to rotate torso slightly so front corner didn't bump thigh.

Fine.

One actual constraint.

One useful object.

I could live with that.

Lyssa glanced over twice.

Second time she said, "Stop looking pleased."

"I'm walking."

"Pleasedly."

"Not word."

"Is now."

She adjusted apron bundle higher.

"How are hands?" I asked.

"Fine."

"Actually?"

She flexed right thumb.

"Sore. Not bad."

"How long total?"

"Two long days. One short evening. Yesterday morning for finishing and checking."

"So maybe two and a half."

"Roughly."

"And you still like sewing?"

"I like some sewing."

"Important distinction."

"I don't like twelve identical apron ties."

"Shocking."

"By nine, I hated ties personally."

"What did ten do?"

"Apologized. Eleven was fine. Twelve got cocky again."

Messier when tired.

Good.

I liked it.

Not because every sentence needed performance.

Because it sounded like her after three days of cloth.

At Marra's green door, cousin's handcart arrived maybe half minute before us.

He had already set sleeve bundle just inside.

Jessa stood over it checking cord.

"You tied this?"

Lyssa said, "Yes."

"Ugly."

"Secure."

"Still ugly."

"Your face is ugly."

Jessa considered.

"Fair."

Greg remained gravitational center of dry comeback register in theory.

In practice, apparently everyone was allowed one.

I did not write that down.

Marra came from rear room wiping chalk from fingers.

She looked at three bundles.

Then at Lyssa.

"All?"

"All."

"Finished when?"

"Last wrap yesterday. Checked this morning."

"Any defects returned?"

"One apron front had a thin place near lower edge. I marked it and left unfinished like card said. It's in top of apron bundle."

Marra nodded once.

Good.

No praise yet.

Work first.

She pointed to long front table.

"Open by type."

Lyssa set aprons down.

Jessa brought sleeves.

I reached for harness bundle.

Marra said, "You can put that on floor first."

"Table offended?"

"Table clean. Street harness not."

Correct.

I lowered wrap bundle to clean board Marra put down.

Then removed harness.

No one applauded.

Trade remained cruel.

Marra started with aprons.

Not every piece.

First three.

Then random fifth, eighth, twelfth.

She checked seam width against marked strip.

Pulled ties.

Turned corners.

Ran thumb along hem.

Looked at one stitch line twice.

Lyssa stood with arms folded.

Not defensive.

Trying to look not defensive.

Different.

Jessa leaned against shelf until Marra said, "If you're going to hover, hover while sorting fasteners."

"I was observing history."

"History can sort fasteners."

Jessa sorted.

Marra checked sleeve pairs.

Four sets.

Then wrap bundle.

First.

Third.

Seventh.

She unfolded eighth completely.

Stopped.

Lyssa's shoulders tightened.

Marra touched closure loop at one end.

"This is old position."

Lyssa stepped forward.

"Position on marked cut."

"Yes."

Marra pulled work card from shelf.

Compared.

Then opened narrow message strip clipped behind it.

Read.

Her mouth changed.

Not much.

Enough.

"Hall changed loop offset yesterday."

Lyssa stared.

"Yesterday when?"

"Second bell."

"I finished wraps before midday."

"I know."

"Did you send change?"

"No."

"Why?"

Marra looked at strip.

"Because I read it, updated shop batch, and failed to remember your lot was already outside shop."

There.

Actual mistake.

No hidden test.

No cleverness.

Just work.

Lyssa looked down at wrap.

"How many wrong?"

"All eight if same."

"They're same."

"Then all eight."

Silence.

My brain immediately started building solutions.

Move loops.

How long?

Who pays?

Material damage?

Could maybe cut and resew.

Not my work.

I shut up.

Marra looked at Lyssa.

"My written-mark error category."

Lyssa exhaled through nose.

"Good."

Not happy.

Clear.

Marra said, "Your finishing is accepted. I own rework."

"You don't want me to move them?"

"Not under first-lot terms."

"I can."

"I know."

"If you pay it."

Marra's eyebrows moved.

There was Lyssa.

"Separate offer," Marra said.

"How much?"

Jessa stopped sorting fasteners without pretending not to listen.

Marra inspected one loop attachment more closely.

"Eight loops. Existing stitch holes may remain visible if moved. Hall spec says service goods, not presentation. I need to confirm whether patch line acceptable before anyone cuts thread."

Lyssa nodded.

"So not offer yet."

"Correct."

Good.

No invented number.

No automatic scope creep.

Marra set wrap aside.

Then continued inspection.

That mattered.

One problem did not turn whole lot into failure.

She found one sleeve cuff with a three-stitch section slightly tight.

"This yours."

Lyssa leaned close.

Pulled fabric gently.

"Yes."

"Would pass household. Not mine."

"Fine."

"Can fix here."

"How long?"

"Five minutes."

Lyssa looked at me.

Not for permission.

Just because I existed.

"You mind?"

"I am carrier."

"That wasn't answer."

"No."

She took sleeve pair to side bench.

Jessa handed her seam pick without comment.

Then said, "Don't bleed on it."

"I haven't."

"Yet."

"You are deeply supportive."

"Professionally."

Lyssa fixed three stitches.

Not dramatic.

Five minutes was generous.

Marra checked again.

Accepted.

Then she counted pieces against card.

Twelve aprons.

Twelve sleeve pairs.

Eight wraps.

One marked material defect unfinished.

Marra had supplied defect.

No penalty.

She wrote on card.

FIRST LOT RETURNED

FINISH ACCEPTED

1 MATERIAL DEFECT HELD

8 WRAPS REQUIRE CLIENT-SPEC LOOP REVISION

SHOP RESPONSIBILITY

Then she signed below.

Lyssa watched every word.

"Six?" she asked.

Marra looked up.

"Six."

No ceremony.

She went to cash drawer.

Counted six copper onto table.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Lyssa looked at money.

Did not touch immediately.

Not because amount enormous.

Because money had been maybe for four days.

Now it was work already done.

Different thing.

Marra said, "Count it."

Lyssa did.

Six.

Into charcoal coat pocket she was not wearing.

No.

Wrong.

She had left coat home.

She used small purse from trouser pocket instead.

Important not to let coat teleport because convenient.

I would have laughed if anyone knew why.

Marra turned page in ledger.

"Hall confirmed second lot this morning."

Lyssa's hand stayed on purse.

"How large?"

"Sixteen aprons. Sixteen sleeve pairs. Twelve wraps. No heat-cloths yet."

"Rate?"

"Nine copper accepted return. Materials supplied. Five days."

Lyssa did not answer.

Good.

Marra continued before silence became pressure.

"Same error categories. No delivery penalty passed through. I update your card if Hall changes written spec. If I fail to transmit after lot leaves, that's mine."

The wrap mistake had already changed process.

Small.

Real.

Lyssa asked, "Any bench use included?"

"No. If you want it, separate."

"Do I need answer now?"

"Tomorrow midday."

Lyssa nodded.

"I'll answer tomorrow."

Marra nodded back.

No sales pitch.

No speech about future.

Work existed.

Offer existed.

Decision could wait.

Then Marra looked at eight wraps.

"If Hall accepts visible patch line, I'll offer you rework separately because you know your own stitches. If they require clean relocation, I may do it here."

"Fine."

"I'll send note."

"Fine."

Jessa said, "She means actually send note this time."

Marra looked at her.

Jessa held up both hands.

"Process improvement."

"Fasteners."

"History is sorting them."

Marra almost smiled.

Almost.

We left with less cloth and more money.

Not my money.

My harness was empty.

That felt wrong enough that Lyssa noticed.

"You look disappointed."

"Carrier without cargo."

"You carried."

"Past tense identity crisis."

"You'll survive."

Outside green door, she stopped under awning.

Rain had started lightly.

Not blue hour.

Just weather.

She took purse out.

Counted six again.

Then put it away.

"You don't have to tell me what you're thinking," I said.

She looked at me.

"That's new."

"Growth."

"You keep saying that and making it less true."

Fair.

We started home.

After half block she said, "Nine is better."

"More than six."

"Thank you, scholar."

"Available for advanced arithmetic."

"But second lot is bigger."

"Yes."

"Five days instead of four."

"Yes."

"First one took me about two and half days actual work. Maybe a little more if I count checking."

I waited.

This was her thinking out loud.

Not necessarily invitation.

"I was faster by end," she said. "Aprons got easy. Sleeves fine. Wraps boring."

"Mm."

She looked over.

"You can talk."

Permission.

"Nine for larger lot may still be better if pace holds. But larger quantity might eat more than extra day, and you don't know if repeating it every week makes you hate it."

"Yes."

"Also you now know Marra pays when work accepted."

"Important."

"And when her mark changes, she owned it."

"More important."

She walked several steps.

"I thought she'd try to make me move loops for free."

"She didn't."

"No."

"Would you have?"

"No."

Fast answer.

Then slower.

"Maybe first month ago I would have. Because I would've thought refusing meant I was difficult. Or not grateful. Or not good enough."

There.

Not speech polished for audience.

Thought still arriving while mouth moved.

"Now?" I asked.

"Now I did work she asked for. Correctly except three stupid stitches. She paid six. Her client changed thing after. That's not gratitude problem."

"No."

"And if she wants me to fix loops, she can pay me."

"Yes."

"Stop agreeing like priest."

"I can object."

"Don't."

We walked.

Rain strengthened enough for awnings to matter.

We took covered route along spice shops.

Harness straps darkened slightly where drops hit.

Oiled leather handled it fine.

No crisis.

At one crossing a delivery boy nearly backed into my empty right loop with crate corner.

I turned sideways.

He apologized without stopping.

Useful equipment also occupied space when empty.

Noted mentally.

No ledger needed.

At home Cala was at stove.

Tavi had spoon sword.

Of course.

He pointed at empty harness.

"Where cloth?"

"Delivered."

"Then why still wearing?"

I looked down.

Fair.

I removed it.

Cala looked at Lyssa.

Not purse.

Face.

"Accepted?"

"Yes."

"All?"

"My work, yes. One material defect. Hall changed wrap loop position after cut. Marra owns rework."

Cala nodded.

"Paid?"

Lyssa pulled purse and shook once.

"Six."

Tavi said, "Can I see?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because it's money."

"I know what money looks like."

"Then you don't need to see mine."

He thought about this.

"Okay."

Surprisingly effective.

Cala asked, "More work?"

"Second lot offered. Nine copper. Bigger. Five days. Answer tomorrow."

Cala stirred pot.

"You want it?"

Lyssa opened mouth.

Stopped.

"I don't know yet."

Cala nodded.

"Then know tomorrow."

That was Cala.

No optimization model.

No speech.

Know tomorrow.

Lyssa sat.

I hung harness on chair back.

Not artifact chest.

It was leather.

Tavi touched one side loop.

"Spoon fits."

"No."

"You didn't test."

"No."

"That's not science."

I stared at him.

Someone had taught child wrong lessons.

Possibly me.

Lyssa laughed hard enough to bend forward.

Not elegant laugh.

Tired laugh.

She covered face.

"Don't encourage him," I said.

"I can't help it."

Cala said, "Spoon stays at table."

Tavi returned spoon.

Democracy restored.

Later, after dinner, Lyssa spread blank scrap beside work chest.

Not Marra's card.

Her own.

She wrote numbers.

I did not lean over.

This was advanced relationship technique.

She noticed anyway.

"You can look."

I moved closer.

FIRST LOT

6 C

2 B BENCH

ABOUT 2.5 DAYS WORK

3-STITCH FIX

MARra ERROR ON WRAPS

She crossed out lowercase mistake in Marra and rewrote.

Then below:

SECOND LOT

9 C

BIGGER

5 DAYS

NO BENCH INCLUDED

Then:

DO I WANT TO DO THIS EVERY WEEK?

She stared at last line.

"That's different question," I said.

"I know."

"Second lot doesn't necessarily mean every week."

"I know."

"Then why write it?"

She chewed inside cheek.

"Because if I say yes twice, people start thinking pattern. Including me."

Good answer.

"You can still stop after two."

"I know."

"You can also ask future weekly terms before third."

"I know, Greg."

There it was.

Too much.

I stopped.

She looked at me after moment.

"Sorry."

"No need."

"There is. You weren't doing anything wrong. I just have thread in brain."

Messy phrase.

Good phrase.

"Thread brain sounds terminal."

"Probably."

She tapped pencil against paper.

"I think I want second lot."

"Okay."

"That is all?"

"You said you think."

"I do."

"Then think until tomorrow."

She narrowed eyes.

"Cala infected you."

"Plan."

From kitchen Cala called, "I heard that."

Lyssa laughed again.

I went upstairs later and wrote my own notes.

Not Lyssa's six.

Not her nine maybe.

Mine.

CASH: 9 C

VALE: 23 C

PORTER HARNESS

RIGHT-SIDE LIGHT PARCEL: WORKS

EMPTY LOOP STILL CATCHES SPACE

BUCKLE PIN STILL NOT CHECKED FOR HEAVIER LOAD

Then I stopped.

Almost wrote about Marra accepting Lyssa's lot.

Did not.

Not my ledger.

Still mattered.

That was inconvenient.

Some of best things happening in my life refused to become my property, income, skill, or problem.

I was getting better at letting that happen.

Maybe.

Carrier.

Today that had been enough.
