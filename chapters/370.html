CHAPTER 370

THE STUDENT

Hessa found me teaching Tavi how not to fall off a chair.

This was less hypocritical than it sounds.

He was not on chair yet.

Important distinction.

The chair stood in middle of kitchen because Cala had asked him to wipe upper cabinet doors.

Tavi had interpreted this as permission to become architecture.

I had intercepted at chair plus footstool.

"One chair," I said.

"I can't reach."

"Then ask taller person."

"You are taller."

"Excellent."

"But you have crutches."

"Less excellent."

Lyssa, at table, did not look up from scrap where she had written Marra's second-lot numbers again.

"Ask me."

Tavi looked at her.

"You're busy."

"I can wipe cabinet."

"Cala told me."

There.

Work ownership.

Apparently everyone had contracts now.

"Then one chair," I said. "Flat. No stool. Move chair instead of reaching sideways."

"That takes longer."

"Falling also takes time."

"Not much."

"Recovery does."

He considered this with annoying seriousness.

Knock came at door.

Cala opened it.

Hessa stood outside.

Of course.

She looked past Cala at me.

Then at Tavi.

Then at chair.

"What are you teaching?"

"Gravity."

Tavi said, "Chair."

Hessa nodded.

"Chair is harder."

I disliked immediate alliance.

"Why are you here?"

"Shoes."

I looked down.

I had one.

"Cruel."

"Get ready."

No note.

No appointment.

No greeting worth keeping.

Hessa remained Hessa.

Lyssa looked up.

"Magic?"

"Probably," I said.

Hessa said, "Yes."

"See?"

Lyssa looked at her scrap again.

"I'm taking Marra's second lot."

There.

Resolved.

No council.

No ceremony.

"Nine?" I asked.

"Nine. Five days. Same terms. I am going after breakfast."

"Good."

She pointed pencil at me.

"That's all."

"I know."

"No face."

"I have to keep face."

"Neutral face."

I attempted.

She narrowed eyes.

"Worse. Go do magic."

Hessa had already stepped back outside.

Apparently dismissed by household.

I ate enough bread that Cala did not call me back.

No artifacts.

No porter harness.

No oval.

I left with crutches, ordinary shoulder bag, notebook, charcoal.

Hessa checked bag anyway.

"Still insulting," I said.

"Still useful."

We did not go to South Walk.

That was first warning.

We went east.

Past cloth lanes.

Past narrow square where two fish sellers were arguing over drain water.

Past a shrine wall with seven little brass cups set into stone.

Then down a lane I knew only as route toward old dye yards.

Most had moved farther south years ago.

Too much water use.

Too much colored runoff.

Too many neighbors discovering opinions about purple gutters.

One yard remained active.

Another had become storage.

Hessa turned into third.

Gate open.

No sign.

Inside, long brick court held three empty dye troughs, a roofed side gallery, and lines strung overhead between posts.

Hundreds of little pale cloth tabs hung from them.

Not laundry.

Practice markers.

Wind moved them.

Not much.

Enough.

I stopped.

"No."

Hessa kept walking.

"Good morning to you too."

"Moving targets?"

"No."

I looked at tabs.

They moved.

"Those are philosophically moving."

"They are wind markers."

Ah.

Different problem.

Hessa set small wooden case on dry trough edge.

"Body."

"Fine."

She waited.

"Right leg fine. Left knee fine. Shoulders slightly tired from yesterday carrying cloth. Hands fine. Sleep good."

"Magic since last session?"

"No."

"Artifacts?"

"Three Reeds once for neighbor emberwings. Short activation. No mana from me."

"Anything else?"

"No."

"Work?"

"Theatre route instruction. Auction. Carried Lyssa's work yesterday."

"Fall?"

"No."

"Near fall?"

I thought about auction.

No.

Harness curb.

No.

"No."

Hessa nodded.

Then pointed to a chalk path already drawn through court.

Not straight.

It bent around first empty trough, narrowed between two posts, widened near back wall, then returned along other side.

A loop.

"Walk."

I walked.

No mana.

First turn easy.

Narrow post gap required shorter crutch placement.

Back wall had rough brick seam that made right-foot landing slightly uneven.

Return side broad.

I completed.

"Again."

Second.

"Again."

Third.

I stopped at start.

"You changed route."

"Yes."

"Last moving-caster result was familiar straight route."

"Yes."

"So today is route complexity."

"No."

I hated her.

"Then why route?"

"Because you keep trying to name lesson before taking it."

That landed harder than deserved.

Probably because true.

I had spent first life learning fast partly by predicting instructors.

By thirty, that was useful.

By fifty, most people teaching me either had narrow specialty I wanted or were wasting my time.

Current life had apparently decided to fix personality defect by making me nineteen and Bronze.

Hessa opened wooden case.

One bead.

Same size as before.

Dark blue this time.

She did not hang it.

She set it on narrow stone shelf built into back wall.

Stationary.

Farther from route than prior South Walk bead.

Not much.

Maybe one pace.

There.

Distance axis.

I said nothing.

Hessa looked at me.

"Good."

"I hate praise for silence."

"Then stop earning it."

I almost answered.

Did not.

Worse.

She pointed to start.

"Six minimal. Walking full loop. Draw only on broad return side."

"Why broad return?"

"Because I said."

Not satisfying.

Enough.

Attempt one.

I walked first bend.

Shortened through posts.

Right foot over brick seam.

Return side.

Both crutches planted.

Minimal draw.

Clean.

Released before turn.

Attempt two.

Clean enough.

Three smeared because I drew while right crutch was still settling.

Four clean.

Five broke early.

Six clean.

Hessa marked nothing visible.

"Count."

"Fifty-nine minimal."

"Good."

Six added to fifty-three.

No miracle.

Body remembered enough.

"Again," she said.

"Minimal?"

"No mana."

Of course.

I walked loop twice with no draw.

Hessa moved from trough to side gallery.

Then told me to walk again.

I did.

At broad return she said, "Where is bead?"

I looked.

Back wall.

"There."

"You looked."

"You asked."

"Again."

I walked.

"Where is bead?"

This time I kept eyes on route.

"Back wall, left of brick seam, about one pace farther than old near range."

"Better."

"You don't want visual correction."

"I want you to stop hunting target with head every pass."

Different.

"Peripheral?"

"Enough to know where it is. Not enough to rebuild route around it."

There.

The cloth tabs overhead shifted with breeze.

One line moved left to right.

Another barely stirred.

Wind markers.

Not target.

They told me court air changed between passes.

Potential noise.

Hessa had chosen outdoor yard deliberately.

I almost said that.

Then remembered lesson about naming lesson.

Student.

Annoying role.

She pointed at start.

"Four external at old distance first."

I looked at bead.

"It's farther."

Hessa walked to wall, picked bead up, moved it to short iron peg closer to route.

Old near range approximately.

Baseline.

Good.

I said nothing.

"You can stop performing silence," she said.

"You created this."

"Walk."

Attempt one.

Full loop.

No draw until broad return.

Target entered peripheral field.

I initiated after right-foot contact.

Tiny effect.

Bead clicked against peg.

Late enough that I felt shoulder chase.

Hessa said, "No."

Attempt two.

Nothing.

Attempt three.

Small clean movement.

No shoulder correction.

Still moving.

Attempt four.

Another movement, uglier.

Hessa stopped me.

"One clean enough."

"Three moved."

"One clean enough."

"I know."

"You said three."

"I was mourning."

She moved bead back to wall shelf.

Farther.

"Now twelve."

There.

Distance axis.

Same moving caster.

Stationary target.

Same route after repeated familiarization today.

One variable stretched.

Mostly.

Outdoor air still existed.

Hessa pointed up.

"Tabs."

"Wind."

"If lower line jumps, skip pass."

"Because cross-draft changes bead?"

"Because I want less noise."

Good.

Attempt one.

I walked.

Lower tabs shifted hard near return.

Skipped.

"Not counted," Hessa said.

Attempt one actual.

Draw.

Nothing.

Distance was not dramatic.

Mana did not care about my feelings.

Attempt two.

Nothing.

Three.

Tiny bead movement.

Too late.

My torso had already passed clean geometry and I reached mentally backward.

Hessa said nothing.

Worse.

Attempt four.

I tried earlier.

Nothing.

Five.

Skipped because wind tabs jumped.

Not counted.

Five actual.

I felt shape settle during broad return.

Held target location without staring.

External push.

Bead moved.

Small.

Clean enough that I knew before Hessa spoke.

"Keep walking."

I did.

No smile.

Mostly.

At start she said, "One."

"One."

"Do not reproduce it."

"You say that like malicious curse."

"It works."

Attempt six failed because I reproduced it.

I swore.

Hessa did not smile.

Her eyes did.

Attempt seven.

Nothing.

Eight.

Bead moved, but wind line had started shifting just before effect.

Hessa said, "Discard."

"It moved before big gust."

"Discard."

"You enjoy waste."

"I enjoy not lying."

Fair.

Attempt eight still counted as attempt.

Result discarded as evidence.

Nine.

No effect.

Ten.

Tiny clean movement.

Not as much as five.

But timing clean.

No wind jump.

No late shoulder.

Hessa raised hand.

Stop.

"That is ten," I said.

"Yes."

"You said twelve."

"I changed mind."

"There are two left."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because I have two clean-enough farther results and you are beginning to chase success."

I opened mouth.

Closed.

She was right.

Worse.

"So ten counted farther attempts."

"Yes."

"Two clean enough."

"Yes."

"One other moved late. One moved under wind noise and gets discarded as evidence."

"Yes."

"Four baseline attempts first, one clean enough."

"Yes."

"External total becomes one hundred twenty-nine."

Hessa paused.

I counted again.

One hundred fifteen before today.

Four near baseline.

Ten farther.

Fourteen.

One hundred twenty-nine.

"Correct," she said.

"Minimal fifty-nine. Shaping fifty-three."

"Correct."

I leaned on crutches.

Not exhausted.

Hands slightly warm from route repetition.

Shoulders working.

Brain louder.

"So distance improved."

Hessa looked at me.

I sighed.

"No."

"Try again."

"Today, on this route, after familiarization, I produced two clean-enough small external effects on a stationary target about one pace farther than prior near-range moving-caster work. Two out of ten counted farther attempts."

"Better."

"One clean baseline out of four at old distance."

"Yes."

"Which means I was not especially reliable even before distance changed."

"Yes."

"You could say something encouraging."

"You noticed."

That was apparently encouragement.

I sat on dry trough edge.

Hessa gave me water.

I drank.

Overhead tabs fluttered.

"Why this yard?" I asked.

"Available."

I stared.

"That's all?"

"No."

Of course.

She pointed to tabs.

"South Walk is too clean."

There.

"You wanted environmental noise visible."

"Some."

"But not moving target."

"Correct."

"And route has turns so I cannot make one perfect straight-line timing ritual."

"Partly."

"What else?"

"You tell me."

Trap.

I looked at court.

Three troughs.

Narrow posts.

Rough seam.

Broad return.

Target farther.

Wind visible.

No crowd.

No real consequence if effect failed.

"It makes me move like I actually move. Not marching lane."

Hessa nodded once.

"Closer."

"Current body route changes. Crutch placement changes with width and surface. You want effect to survive some ordinary geometry without adding target movement or speed increase yet."

"Yes."

There.

I felt pleased.

Hessa ruined it.

"But I did not count that as separate variable."

"Why not?"

"Because today is not proof of route-general skill. You learned this loop first."

Right.

"So this is still one practiced route."

"Yes."

"Just less artificial than South Walk."

"Yes."

"You are deeply committed to making every result smaller."

"Small results fit through doors."

That sounded like something Hessa had been waiting years to say.

"Was that metaphor?"

"No."

"It was."

"Drink water."

I drank.

She packed bead.

I watched hands.

Young hands.

Competent.

That still did something strange to me sometimes.

Not because Hessa was young exactly.

I was young now too.

That remained rude.

In first life I had taught people older than her.

Fought beside people younger.

Taken orders from specialists who knew things I didn't.

Age had never made expertise simple.

But being nineteen again put old reflex in wrong room.

I wanted to skip explanation because I remembered categories.

Wanted to infer next drill because I knew training structures.

Wanted to turn every correction into system before system had enough evidence.

Hessa kept making me do stupid thing instead.

Be student.

Not pretend ignorance.

Not erase first life.

Just let somebody else own lesson when it was theirs.

Annoying.

Probably useful.

"Why are you making face?" Hessa asked.

"Personal growth."

"Stop."

Reasonable.

She closed case.

"No independent moving external work."

"Yes."

"No farther target practice alone."

"Yes."

"No artifact combination."

"Yes."

"No using Three Reeds to create controlled visual field for casting."

I looked at her.

"I hadn't thought of that."

"Now you have."

"This is your fault."

"Still no."

"Cruel."

"Useful."

We left dye yard.

At gate I asked, "Next axis speed?"

Hessa kept walking.

"Maybe."

"Distance again?"

"Maybe."

"Stationary casting at farther range to separate movement cost?"

She looked at me.

That one apparently less stupid.

"Maybe."

"You have three maybes."

"I have more."

"Can I have one?"

"No."

Teacher.

Terrible species.

We split near East Cloth.

Hessa went north without goodbye.

I went toward Marra's green door because Lyssa had said she was answering after breakfast.

Not to supervise.

Mostly because route home passed within half block.

I saw her before I reached door.

She came out carrying one folded work card and no cloth.

"You took it," I said.

"I said I would."

"You said you thought you would."

"Now I did."

"Nine copper. Five days."

"Yes."

"Same categories?"

She gave me look.

I stopped.

"Sorry."

"Thank you."

We walked together.

After ten steps she said, "Yes, same categories. Marra wrote client-update line onto card this time."

"Good."

"I know."

"Any wrap rework?"

"Hall accepts visible patch line. Marra offered me one copper to move all eight loops. I said yes. Separate from second lot. I can do it this afternoon."

There.

New money.

Her money.

New work.

Her work.

"Good," I said.

She pointed at me without looking.

"One good. That's all."

"Understood."

"How was magic?"

"Hessa made me student."

"Were you not already?"

"Hostile question."

"Did anything move?"

"Yes."

"That sounds good."

"Careful. Hessa might hear."

Lyssa looked around.

"Is she in wall?"

"Tavi believes possible."

"Then definitely."

At home Tavi had cleaned cabinet doors.

Mostly.

One had a wet streak shaped like hand.

Cala inspected.

"You missed top left."

Tavi looked at chair.

Then at me.

Then at Lyssa.

"Can someone tall do it?"

I pointed at him.

"Learning."

Cala handed cloth to Lyssa.

Lyssa wiped top left in two seconds.

Tavi looked offended by efficiency.

"I could've done that."

"You did rest," Cala said.

"Most."

"Most is why we're here."

He accepted this with visible reluctance.

I went upstairs.

No money movement for me.

Nine copper.

Twenty-three Vale.

I wrote:

Then I almost wrote:

I KNOW WHAT HESSA IS DOING.

I stopped.

Crossed blank space where sentence would have gone.

That was old habit.

Knowing shape of lesson before lesson finished.

Sometimes useful.

Sometimes just another way of trying to stand where teacher stood.

I wrote instead:

NEXT: HESSA DECIDES.

Stared at it.

Hated it.

Left it.

Student.

Apparently restraint counted there too.
