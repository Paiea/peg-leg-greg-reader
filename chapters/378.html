THE USHER

The theatre sent for me because somebody else had fallen down.

Not badly.

This mattered.

NEED HOUSE ACCESS COVER TONIGHT.
NOT ROUTE TEACHING.
NOT LAMP WORK.
DOORS / WEST GALLERY / LATE EXIT.
2 C.

Five to seven.

If I accepted.

"Yes," I said.

No negotiation.

Not every known rate required performance.

"Go."

"Brenna finishing."

"I live here."

Right.

Concerning.

Not main-stage crowd.

Good.

"Don't let people stack bags or stools in aisle. Seat mobility aids without creating trip hazard. If someone needs slower stair, hold flow. If someone cannot use gallery stair safely, offer floor section if available. Don't decide for them. Ask."

That last part landed.

"Late exit?"

"Mask scene uses dark transition. We don't release west gallery until aisle lamps return. People always think they can see enough. They cannot."

"And doors?"

"Open at first bell. Count only capacity marks, not people identity. Red mark full. No standing behind back rail."

House work.

Simple.

Until people arrived.

People make every simple system creative.

"Then youngest needs space somewhere," I said.

Father said, "He'll be on lap."

Child was maybe eight.

Large eight.

Mother looked tired enough to leave family at door.

"Five bodies need five capacity marks," I said.

"He's not using seat."

"Capacity isn't seat count."

Father started argument.

Mother said, "Buy five."

Father bought five.

Marriage survived.

Second problem: woman with cane wanted west gallery because sister already there.

Stair narrow.

I asked, "Want arm support, slower clear stair, or floor alternative?"

She looked at me.

Then crutches.

"Slower stair. Don't touch me unless I ask."

"Good."

I held incoming flow at bottom.

She climbed.

Slow.

Fine.

People behind waited thirty seconds and somehow survived.

Third problem: boy put folded stool in aisle.

"Move it."

"My grandmother needs it."

Grandmother said, "I need it under me, idiot."

Boy moved stool.

Different family survived.

By first bell west gallery almost full.

I liked ushering less than route teaching.

No.

Different.

Nessa passed once.

"Status?"

"Three marks open. Aisle clear. One cane user upstairs by choice. Maro still victim of gravity."

"Good."

Maro from stool said, "Hostile workplace."

Nessa kept walking.

Performance began.

Mirror-mask rehearsal from Three Reeds day had become actual scene.

I noticed because glare did not hit west gallery.

Theatre's replacement shade apparently arrived.

Good.

My artifact not needed.

Also good.

Three Reeds had solved temporary need, not captured institution.

Actors argued inheritance under masks.

One still breathed before lies.

Nessa had kept it.

Audience laughed first time.

Oh.

Maybe actor was right.

Art remained suspicious.

Not dramatic.

Then more.

She stood.

I opened rear door quietly.

Her companion followed.

Outside landing she leaned rail.

"Air," she said.

"Want water?"

"No. Just air."

I did not diagnose.

Companion rubbed back.

I smelled.

Nothing.

Then faint.

Not theatre smoke.

Hot dust?

Oil?

No visible haze.

Could be costume lamp.

Could be street.

Could be imagination.

"Stay here," I said.

To companion: "I'm getting house lead."

Nessa was across room.

I did not cross audience.

"Report."

Face changed slightly.

"Yes."

He looked up.

Not out.

Flicker.

"Jori is checking."

Question.

Authority.

"You can leave now if you want. Stair is lit. If house needs broader exit, we'll direct it."

She nodded.

They left down stair.

No panic.

Jori returned with Nessa and small red cloth marker.

"Rear lamp feed collar heating," Jori said. "Not flame. We are isolating west rear lamp. Front aisle lamps stay."

Nessa asked, "Can gallery remain?"

Jori: "Yes if rear fixture dark and smell clears. If not, clear gallery."

Nessa looked at me.

"Late exit rule changes. Rear lamp unavailable. At mask dark transition, west gallery holds until front aisle lamps full and runner confirms stair landing lit from lower fixture."

"Understood."

There.

Actual consequence.

No artifact.

No heroics.

System degraded one piece and adapted.

Jori isolated lamp.

Rear corner darker.

Hot smell faded.

Audience mostly never noticed.

Performance continued.

Maro whispered, "You get all interesting shifts."

"You fell carrying paper."

"Exactly."

At interval, west gallery emptied partially.

Cane user came down slowly.

Same choice.

I held flow.

A man behind her sighed loudly.

"You want go first?"

"Then breathe quieter."

I liked her.

Not because dry comeback.

Because she had earned it.

I looked.

Rail widened around decorative carved joint.

For my crutch use, irrelevant.

For cane plus rail grip, maybe not.

"Want me to pass that on?"

"Yes."

Different voice.

"West gallery holds. Aisle lamps first."

Of course.

"Hold."

Rear remained dark.

Landing lit.

"West gallery clear. Slow at turn."

No fall.

No rush.

No problem.

Which is what good house work looked like.

Capacity wasn't furniture.

Good.

First life had not made me patient.

Second body maybe did.

No.

Second life did.

"Correct."
