THE OUTSIDER

Dema Orr hated ramps.

Not all ramps.

Important distinction.

"This one," she said, striking North Market viewing terrace ramp with cane tip, "was designed by somebody who thinks wheels and legs both enjoy scenic detours."

I looked up.

Ramp began twelve feet from stairs, doubled back twice, passed behind a festival lantern storage cage, then reached terrace entrance approximately six years later.

"It's gentle."

"Yes."

"Wide."

"Yes."

"Rail both sides."

"Yes."

"You hate it."

"Deeply."

Dema was shorter than Lyssa, broader through shoulders, maybe late thirties. Dark hair cut at jaw because wind had apparently offended her personally. Forearm cane on left. Market licensing sash under rain coat.

She walked quickly enough that I had already learned not to assume cane meant my pace.

"Why?"

She pointed with cane.

"Lantern cage gets moved there every Glasswake because storage permit says cage can't block east fire lane. Then vendors stack delivery crates beside cage because ramp landing isn't technically stall frontage. Then people using ramp have one shoulder width through. Every year somebody reports. Every year festival ends. Cage goes away. Problem becomes next year's problem."

There.

Not ramp.

"No."

"Because we're here?"
