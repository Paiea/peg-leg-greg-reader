CHAPTER 309

THE CAMPER

The wagon north of Glass Cut had suspension designed by someone who hated kidneys.

Kel disagreed.

"It has suspension."

"Technically."

"That's what suspension means."

"Then it has hostile suspension."

Dask, across from me, looked interested.

"Hostility is difficult to measure."

Sori said, "Don't."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were."

"I was considering variables."

"That's how you start."

Vessa sat beside driver and ignored us.

Maro had fallen asleep.

Actually asleep.

Head against rolled tarp.

Mouth slightly open.

River-light specialist defeated by wagon.

I respected him more.

North road climbed away from Cut through low pine and pale stone.

Different country again.

Alder Basin had been wet work and ropes.

Glass Cut had been stone pretending eyesight was optional.

Here road ran dry between scrub, small farms, and occasional black marker posts with white ceramic caps.

I asked what they were.

Kel said, "Old flood return marks."

Dask opened mouth.

Kel added, "That is what everyone calls them."

Dask closed mouth.

Growth.

The road itself carried Glasswake residue in little ways.

Some wagon ruts shone when shadow crossed them.

Not in sun.

Shadow.

A fence nail blinked blue once as we passed.

One puddle reflected the wagon five seconds after wagon had left it.

I turned around to watch.

Sori caught me.

"Passenger again?"

"Research."

"With your mouth open?"

I closed mouth.

"Advanced research."

We reached marker thirty-two before midday.

Washout had eaten outer half of road.

Signed detour went uphill through orchard track.

Sign was good.

Rare luxury.

Kel stopped anyway.

"Wagon width."

Vessa got down.

We all did because apparently field work meant leaving perfectly good seats whenever terrain became interesting.

Detour track was narrow but firm.

Apple trees on one side.

Drop into muddy drainage on other.

Not cliff.

Enough to ruin wheel.

Kel measured with his body first.

One pace between inner rut and safe outer shoulder.

Then used folding stick.

"We fit."

Vessa: "Margin?"

"Enough if nobody gets creative."

I looked at Dask.

He said, "Why me?"

"No reason."

We walked first bend while Kel drove wagon empty of passengers.

Fine.

Second bend tighter.

Still fine.

Third had fallen apple branch across inner side.

Kel stopped.

I looked.

Branch maybe four inches thick at base.

Could move it.

Probably.

Then farmer appeared from orchard carrying saw.

"Leave it."

There.

Local person.

He cut branch.

We waited six minutes.

No Greg.

Retirement fund thriving.

Farmer said washout happened two nights before Glasswake, not because of it.

Important because people liked blaming magic for weather too.

Sori wrote route note.

Kel gave farmer half dried pear from lunch bag.

Farmer accepted like this was normal road tax.

Maybe it was.

Past orchard, road widened.

We rode.

By second bell I was bored enough to count ceramic caps.

Twenty-seven.

Then lost count because Dask asked why.

"Because I was bored."

"You could have asked me."

"For what?"

"Something to count."

"That's worse."

He looked genuinely offended.

Maro woke.

"Where are we?"

Kel said, "North."

Maro stared at him.

"I hate you."

"Good nap?"

"Yes."

Travel.

I liked it.

Nothing happened for almost an hour.

That felt new enough to matter.

Then we saw first lock tower.

Far away.

Black against afternoon sky.

Not West Locks proper yet.

Outer relay tower.

Tall narrow structure with three horizontal arms like someone built windmill and forgot wheel.

White cloth strips hung from upper arm.

None moved.

Wind definitely existed.

I looked at Maro.

He was already looking.

"Normal?"

"Sometimes."

Dask said, "Define sometimes."

Maro closed eyes.

Sori patted his knee.

"Don't kill him before camp."

We passed tower without stopping.

Vessa said it belonged Locks maintenance, not our current station.

Good.

Not every weird thing belonged to us.

Road camp sat another two miles north.

Calling it camp was generous.

Three roofed platforms.

One stone fire ring.

A hand pump.

Two privy sheds.

A low lockbox hut with survey seal.

And enough flat ground for wagon, which immediately made Kel happier than architecture deserved.

West Locks themselves were not visible.

But I could hear them.

Deep mechanical knocks every few minutes.

Not regular.

Water moving somewhere beyond hills.

Camp keeper was a woman named **Pella Dorn** with shaved sides of head, long braid, and one thumb missing at first joint.

She checked Vessa's papers.

"Six."

"Six," Vessa confirmed.

Pella looked at me.

"You the record hand?"

"Unfortunately."

"Then you get dry platform."

Dask said, "Why does he get dry platform?"

"Because last one ruined three days of paper sleeping under drip."

Dask considered.

"Reasonable."

Sori said, "I also have paper."

"You look mean enough to keep rain away."

Sori smiled.

"I like her."

Camp rules.

Water pump tested before use.

No washing dishes in drainage trough.

Food hung in iron box after dark because glass-mice came up from lock channels.

There.

"Glass mice?"

Pella looked at me.

"Mice. Eat glass salts. Teeth shine. Steal soap."

"Magical?"

"They're mice."

"That didn't answer."

"It answered enough."

Excellent.

No open flame after second night bell if east wind because lock vapor sometimes carried.

No sleeping against white marker stones.

"Why?"

Pella: "Cold."

"Just cold?"

"Usually."

Maro said, "Listen to her."

I would.

Vessa assigned camp.

Kel wagon and mule.

Sori food inventory.

Dask equipment check.

Maro evening light read.

Greg water and sleeping platforms.

I stared.

"Promotion."

"You wanted adventure."

"I wanted at least one sword."

"You have pump."

Cruel.

Pump handle heavy but functional.

I filled two buckets.

Carried one at time because two plus crutches would be stupid.

Pella had shoulder yoke hanging nearby.

I looked at it.

Pella followed my look.

"Bad match with crutches. Needs both hands clear at the turns."

"One bucket at a time."

"Yes."

Six trips.

Could have optimized.

Didn't.

Water existed at end.

Then bedding.

Survey bedrolls went dry platform.

Mine against inner post, not white marker stone.

Dask placed his exactly three feet from equipment cases.

Sori moved it while he wasn't looking.

He came back.

Stopped.

Looked at bedroll.

Looked at her.

"Why?"

"Testing environmental awareness."

"Move it back."

"You move it."

He did, muttering.

Crew life.

There.

Not coworkers in office anymore.

People who knew who snored soon.

Kel cooked.

This surprised me.

Not badly either.

Pot of beans, smoked meat, onion, some bitter green Pella had hanging by hut.

Maro chopped.

Sori claimed supervisory authority and was ignored.

I sat on platform edge cutting bread because knife plus seated body was job I could do without turning dinner into accessibility seminar.

Dask tried to calculate portions.

Kel took ladle away.

"People aren't gauges."

"Food is measurable."

"Not by you."

Pella ate with us.

Vessa asked Locks status.

Pella chewed before answering.

"West Three running slow. West Four closed for tooth replacement. Five open cargo only. Foot route east gallery."

"Glasswake effects?"

"Two false gate tones last night. One this morning. Maintenance says no physical movement with either. East gallery had floor shine at dawn. Gone by noon."

Maro: "Black pockets?"

"None seen."

Dask: "Observed none?"

Pella pointed spoon at him.

"I will put you outside iron box."

Sori laughed hard enough to cough.

Dask looked at Vessa.

"Threat?"

"Boundary."

I liked camp.

Pella continued.

"Road north after Locks open. Marsh shelf wet. Two wagons came through. One says reed hounds near Mile Reed."

There.

Finally.

"Reed hounds?" I asked.

Pella looked delighted to have new idiot.

"Hounds made of reeds."

"Actually?"

"No."

Kel said, "Don't encourage her."

Pella grinned.

"Long-legged marsh dogs. Tan. Hunt rats and little deer. Sometimes follow camps for scraps. Not monsters unless you're sausage."

"I am partly sausage now."

"Sleep high."

Maro said, "They're usually shy."

Pella: "Usually."

I pointed at her.

"You enjoy that word."

"Keeps people alive."

Fair.

After dinner Vessa posted watches.

Not because danger high.

Because equipment, animals, road camp, normal field procedure.

Kel first.

Greg second.

Maro third.

Vessa fourth.

Sori and Dask off because Sori had dawn custody prep and Dask had first-light gauge calibration.

I said, "Favoritism."

Sori said, "Competence allocation."

"I hate administration."

"No you don't."

True.

Second watch began sometime after moon rose.

Kel woke me by tapping boot against platform.

"Quiet."

"Good quiet?"

"Mostly. Heard hounds once east. Lock knocked twice. Pella says normal."

"Anything glowing?"

"Dask's teeth when he sleeps."

I looked toward Dask.

"Really?"

Kel walked away.

Asshole.

I got up.

Harness.

Crutches.

Blanket around shoulders.

Camp at night felt smaller.

Fire down to coals.

Wagon dark shape.

Mule asleep standing.

Survey cases locked in hut except overnight gauge box under platform.

Deep lock knocks came through ground more than air.

Thunk.

Long pause.

Thunk-thunk.

Water answered somewhere far off.

I sat near fire ring.

Watch did not require standing dramatically.

Good.

I listened.

First life watches had been different.

Sometimes six people asleep behind me because if I failed they died.

Sometimes monsters close enough I could smell them.

Sometimes enemy adventurers.

Sometimes nothing for eight hours except cold and thoughts.

This was closer to last one.

I liked last one now.

At nineteen, again, I had learned boredom could be luxury.

Something moved near food box.

Tiny.

Scratch.

I looked.

Two points of pale blue light.

Then four.

Then six.

Glass mice.

Excellent.

Three little gray bodies emerged from drainage grass.

Normal mice except incisors glowed faint blue-white when mouths opened.

One climbed iron food-box leg.

Reached grease collar.

Slid down.

Tried again.

Persistent.

I whispered, "Respect."

Another mouse went toward soap bag hanging from peg.

Pella had warned.

I tapped crutch shaft lightly against stone.

Mouse froze.

Ran.

Others followed.

No spell.

No battle.

Bronze adventurer defeats mice with noise.

S-class legacy secure.

Five minutes later they returned from other side.

Smarter than expected.

I moved soap bag higher.

Victory through logistics.

Then east dark made sound.

Not mouse.

Soft footfall.

Pause.

Soft footfall.

I looked toward wagon.

Mule's ears were forward.

There.

Animal noticed too.

I stood.

Not locked in.

Attention sharpened one notch.

Tan shape at edge of moonlight.

Long legs.

Narrow chest.

Dog-like head.

Reed hound.

One.

Maybe.

It stood beyond drainage trough, watching camp.

I watched back.

No weapon in hand.

Crutch could become weapon.

Knife at belt.

Old habits offered distance, angle, approach line, mule position, sleeping crew, food box, wind.

Quietly.

No need to spend more.

Hound sniffed.

Took one step.

I said, normal voice, "No."

It stopped.

Good dog.

Maybe.

Another shape moved behind first.

Smaller.

Then third.

Three.

Not attack posture.

Interested.

Probably food.

Pella's partly sausage problem.

I picked up small stone.

Threw it to land in dirt between us.

Not at animal.

Hounds jumped back.

One barked once.

Weird bark.

Almost cough.

Maro woke behind me.

"What?"

"Three reed hounds. East."

He sat up slowly.

Looked.

"Don't feed."

"Hadn't planned."

"They'll follow wagon tomorrow if they learn camp gives food."

"Pella said shy."

"Those are young."

One hound edged sideways.

Maro stood.

No hurry.

He took empty pot lid.

Hit it once with spoon.

CLANG.

All three vanished.

I stared at him.

"Advanced river-light technique?"

"Yes."

"Teach me."

"S-class prerequisite."

Fuck him.

He sat beside me.

"I'll stay until they don't come back."

"Your watch is next."

"I know."

"Then sleep."

"I'm awake."

"Your problem."

We sat.

Quiet.

After maybe ten minutes he said, "You miss home?"

There.

Not technical.

"Yes."

"Want to go back?"

I thought.

"Not yet."

He nodded.

"Good answer."

"Yours?"

"This is home-ish."

"Six miles west."

"Near."

"Dask was right."

"Don't say that."

We listened to locks.

Maro eventually said, "When I was younger I thought leaving meant choosing somewhere else."

Longer speech from him.

Rare.

"Then I left. Came back. Left again. Places don't care about your categories. You can belong somewhere and still be gone. You can hate a place and know every drain. You can love people and not want their street today."

I looked at him.

"That sounded rehearsed."

"It was not."

"Worse."

"I regret talking."

"Good."

He smiled.

Then pointed east.

One hound had returned.

Farther away.

Watching.

Maro lifted pot lid.

It left before clang.

Learning.

"Smart," I said.

"Enough."

There.

Enough.

I understood word differently now.

Not mediocre.

Not settling.

Enough could be exact amount required.

Enough strength.

Enough money.

Enough attention.

Enough Greg.

Reserve stayed reserve until enough stopped being enough.

Maro took watch early because awake.

I went back to bed.

Slept badly for first half hour because Dask did, in fact, grind teeth.

They did not glow.

Kel lied.

Morning arrived gray.

Pella had coffee substitute already boiling.

Tasted like burnt root and punishment.

I drank two cups.

Vessa had route sheet open.

"West Three slow. Four closed. Five cargo only. We take east gallery on foot, cases through Five freight cradle, regroup north landing."

There.

More infrastructure.

"Then?" I asked.

"Marsh shelf road. Mile Reed. North station by evening if pace holds."

"Hounds?"

Maro said, "Greg made friends."

"I did not."

Pella: "You feed them?"

"No."

"Then not friends."

Good.

Dask emerged looking terrible.

Sori said, "Your teeth are loud."

He stopped.

"What?"

"You grind."

"No."

Five people looked at him.

Dask became offended by consensus.

"That is not measurement."

Camp packed.

Bedrolls rolled.

Food box checked.

Soap survived.

Glass mice had eaten tiny corner of one label on empty salt packet.

Sori stared at damage like personal betrayal.

Pella said, "Told you."

We loaded wagon for short run to Locks approach.

I looked north.

First lock tower stood much closer now.

Behind it, two more arms rose above ridge.

Water knocked deep under ground.

Road camp already felt temporary.

One night.

One dinner.

One watch.

One stupid mouse campaign.

Enough to know where Dask slept, how Kel cooked, that Sori laughed hardest when someone threatened Dask, that Maro got philosophical when awake too long, and Vessa drank coffee substitute without making face because she was either stronger than all of us or dead inside.

Accumulation.

Small.

Real.

Camper.

I had slept somewhere that wasn't home.

Again.

This time it didn't feel like interruption.

It felt like route.
