CHAPTER 328

THE PRACTITIONER

Dask stared at my notebook like it had personally insulted him.

"You did what?"

"Moved tag."

"On purpose?"

"Yes."

"How far?"

"Half pace. Maybe less."

"Moving target?"

"Sliding."

"Single attempt?"

"Yes."

"And you didn't tell me?"

"I wrote it down."

He looked at notebook.

Then me.

"That is not telling me."

Sori, sorting custody sheets at other end of table, said, "It is literally telling everyone forever."

"Not same."

"Better, actually."

North station had one main room, one narrow records room, and apparently enough opinions for six people.

Outside, mast arm two moved again.

Slow.

Wrong direction for wind.

Nobody panicked.

Station keeper, **Rett Morn**, leaned out door and watched.

"There."

Dask abandoned me instantly.

Good.

He ran outside with gauge board.

Maro followed.

Vessa pointed at me.

"Records."

"I know."

"Good."

Normal work.

Excellent.

I took station log from Rett.

Arm two movement first observed twenty-three minutes before our arrival.

Single low tone through mast shortly before that.

No weather change recorded.

No freight bell scheduled.

No lock command due.

West Locks false tone earlier separated by more than an hour.

Do not connect because both tones.

I wrote exact times where known.

Approximate where not.

Rett watched my page.

"You always write uncertainty like accusation?"

"It deserves representation."

"Huh."

He had one of those faces built for weather.

Deep lines, red nose, gray beard cut short.

Probably fifty.

Maybe forty and north wind had opinions.

He tapped station log.

"Arm moved twice before you came. Once after. Never did that last year."

"How old mast?"

"Older than station roof. Younger than stone base."

"Useful."

"You asked."

Fair.

Outside, Dask called, "Greg!"

I went.

Mast stood thirty feet from station on square stone plinth.

Three black arms mounted on vertical shaft.

Arm one east-west.

Two angled northwest.

Three nearly vertical.

Wind came south.

White thread tied by Rett to arm two fluttered south-to-north.

Arm itself crept west.

Not with thread.

Maro had brass disk out.

Dask had two rulers, because of course.

"Record," Vessa said.

I did.

Arm moved maybe two finger-widths over forty breaths.

Then stopped.

No tone.

No visible shaft movement on arms one or three.

Maro said, "Could be residual return in mast body."

Dask: "Could be bearing creep."

Rett: "Could be haunted."

Sori came outside.

"Official category?"

"Very."

Rett smiled.

Vessa said, "No climbing. No disassembly. Station sends notice to regional relay desk. We compare record, then move on schedule."

There.

Bounded.

North station was not our new home.

Dinner was station stew.

I asked what was in it.

Rett said, "Food."

I stopped asking.

Dask did not.

He identified turnip, barley, smoked bird, and one herb Rett refused to name on principle.

Then Dask returned to me.

"Four attempts."

I ate.

"Hello to you too."

"We agreed."

"We tentatively agreed to controlled comparison after future work if conditions allow."

He stared.

"You remembered wording."

"I know you."

"Four attempts. Same target. Controlled. Not while job active."

Vessa was eating nearby.

Dask looked at her.

"Permission?"

She chewed.

Swallowed.

"When?"

"After meal. Before dark. Flat ground behind station. No field equipment. One target. Four attempts. Greg stops if pain, instability, unusual spill, or anything I dislike."

"You dislike everything," Sori said.

"Not measurements."

Vessa looked at me.

"Want to?"

There.

Important distinction.

"Yes."

I did.

Not because emergency.

Not because proving.

Because two practical successes changed value of information.

And maybe there was difference between draining retirement account and checking interest rate.

Vessa nodded.

"Twenty minutes. Then done. This does not become nightly ritual unless we decide later."

"Agreed."

Dask looked offended by limit and delighted by permission simultaneously.

After meal we went behind station.

Flat packed dirt.

Low stone wall.

No public crowd.

No field gear nearby except Dask's notebook and one measuring cord.

Target was not brass disk.

Dask chose wooden button.

I objected.

"Different material."

"Exactly."

"Then not comparison."

He paused.

Looked at me.

"Correct."

Sori, who had apparently come to watch, said, "He admitted error. Mark date."

Dask ignored her.

He fetched small brass washer from personal repair tin.

Close enough to disk/tag material family, though shape different.

We set it on smooth board.

Stationary.

Distance one pace.

I sat.

Crutches laid beside me.

No twisting reach.

No urgency.

Dask marked line.

"Four attempts. Same instruction each time. Move washer right. Not distance target. Any visible displacement counts separately from direction quality."

"How much rest?"

"Thirty breaths minimum between attempts. More if you want."

"Good."

Vessa stood ten feet away.

Maro leaned on wall.

Kel had somehow appeared with cup.

Sori had papers she was pretending to read.

Great.

Private science.

Attempt one.

I looked at washer.

Not magic theory.

Not grand memory.

Same small internal reach I had used for tag.

Push.

Nothing.

Maybe sensation.

No movement.

"No observed movement," I said.

Dask wrote.

Thirty breaths.

Attempt two.

I changed nothing deliberately.

Same posture.

Same target.

Same intent.

Push.

Washer twitched.

Tiny.

Left.

Wrong direction.

Dask made noise like injured scholar.

"Movement observed, direction wrong."

"Yes."

"Approximately two millimeters."

"Observer?"

He glared.

"Estimated two millimeters by board marks."

Better.

Thirty breaths.

Attempt three.

I wanted to correct.

That was trap.

One result did not justify technique change.

Same setup.

Push right.

Washer moved.

Clean.

Maybe half inch.

Right.

Stopped.

There.

Nobody spoke for second.

Then Dask said, very carefully, "Visible displacement right. Approximately one-half inch."

Maro: "No other observed movement."

Sori: "Board stayed."

Vessa looked at me.

Not impressed face.

More interested.

I smiled.

Couldn't help.

Three.

Not mastery.

But three clean practical-ish successes now across two days? No.

Careful.

Ch385 spontaneous disk.

Ch393 deliberate moving tag.

Now one controlled stationary washer.

Different contexts.

Useful.

Thirty breaths.

Attempt four.

Same.

Push.

Washer vibrated.

Did not travel.

Dask crouched.

"Movement?"

"Vibration only," Maro said.

"Direction?"

"No net displacement I can see."

Sori: "Same."

Dask wrote.

Done.

I sat back.

No pain.

Slight pressure behind eyes.

Not headache.

Fatigue maybe.

I said it.

"Mild pressure behind eyes after fourth. No pain."

Dask wrote that too.

Vessa asked, "Could you do more?"

Probably.

"Yes."

"Should you?"

There.

"No reason."

She nodded.

Good.

Dask looked at results.

Attempt one none.

Two movement wrong direction.

Three clean right.

Four vibration/no net displacement.

"Three of four show something," he said.

"Two of four show displacement."

"One clean intended."

"Yes."

"Combined with tag and disk..."

"Careful."

He stopped.

Actually stopped.

Then nodded.

"Different contexts."

Growth.

I liked him slightly.

Sori said, "Disgusting."

"What?"

"Both of you learning restraint."

Kel drank from cup.

"Won't last."

Maro said, "Probably not."

We logged all four as **controlled external attempts**.

External ledger one-thirty to one-thirty-four.

No shaping.

No minimal.

No rank.

No breakthrough fanfare.

Just better evidence.

I felt good anyway.

That was allowed.

Maybe more than good.

Hungry.

Not for food.

There.

Dangerous.

Old feeling.

One clean result made next attempt imaginable.

Next ten.

Next hundred.

Train until twitch became inch, inch became reliable, reliable became useful under movement, pressure, distance.

I knew that staircase.

I had climbed versions of it before.

Retirement account had door.

I put hand on knob.

Did not open.

Not tonight.

But now I knew lock still worked.

That mattered.

We slept in station loft.

No camp watch required because Rett had mast watch and locked yard.

Dask wanted to discuss washer until midnight.

Sori threatened to smother him with folded blanket.

He lasted another six minutes.

Morning route notice came before first bell.

North road split.

Main freight line continued northwest toward **Harrow Fen**, larger marsh settlement and relay market.

Survey route turned north-east along old levee toward **Three Posts station**, then farther toward upper West Locks return beds.

Vessa read notice.

"We take levee."

Good.

New road.

Rett handed her regional relay response about mast.

Not answer.

Instruction:

KEEP ARM TWO IN POSITION.
DO NOT RESET MANUALLY.
RECORD TONES / MOVEMENT.
MAINTENANCE TEAM DISPATCHED FROM HARROW FEN.
NO ROUTE CLOSURE.

Bounded again.

No conspiracy.

No us.

We left after breakfast.

North station shrank behind us.

Levee road ran raised above marsh on packed stone and old timber matting.

Water both sides.

Tall reeds.

Wide sky.

No wagon at first.

Kel led pack mule because levee width narrowed under repair sections.

We walked.

My hands felt yesterday.

Not bad.

Grip hot spot wrapped.

Shoulders okay.

Magic pressure gone.

I did not test while walking.

Important.

Dask asked once.

"Later?"

"Not while moving."

"Agreed."

Look at us.

Professionals.

At second mile, levee passed little roadside shrine.

Not religious maybe.

Three iron hooks set into stone post with bits of ribbon, old coins, broken ceramic, one tiny cracked mirror.

I slowed.

Maro said, "Travelers leave things when levee holds through flood."

"Does it help?"

"Levee?"

"Offerings."

"Depends who you ask."

Dask said, "No controlled data."

Sori said, "Nobody asked you."

I had copper.

Could leave one.

Didn't.

Not because cynical.

Because road had held for me one mile.

Maybe earn ritual first.

Farther on, marsh opened enough to see Harrow Fen in distance to west.

Low roofs on raised berms.

Tall relay tower.

Market awnings bright red and yellow.

Big enough to tempt.

Not our route.

Greg wanted to go.

Of course.

Artifacts.

People.

Food.

A place named Fen sounded promising.

Vessa saw me looking.

"Not our route."

"I know."

"Return route?"

"Maybe."

Good enough.

Around midday levee dipped to old stone bridge over drainage cut.

Bridge narrow.

One rail missing.

Local repair sign said PASS ONE AT TIME / NO CART.

Fine.

Kel took mule around shallow ford below.

We crossed individually.

Maro first.

Vessa.

Sori.

Dask.

Me last.

Right crutch planted.

Left.

Right foot.

Halfway, something pale moved under water.

Long.

Too long for fish I knew.

I stopped because body stopped.

Not fear exactly.

Recognition without name.

Shape turned.

White back.

Four dark fins? Legs?

Then gone under bridge shadow.

I said, "Large pale animal under cut. Unknown."

Maro came back to rail end.

"How large?"

"Maybe six feet. Long body. Four dark side shapes. One pass."

"Direction?"

"Downstream."

He looked water.

Nothing.

"Could be mud sailer."

Dask from other side said, "Could be several things."

Maro: "Yes."

No one climbed down.

Good.

We continued.

Three Posts station appeared late afternoon exactly as advertised.

Three posts.

Actually four.

I hated immediately.

One was broken.

Fine.

Station itself small timber shelter beside raised signal frame.

No keeper visible.

Door open.

Vessa called.

No answer.

There.

Different pressure.

Not catastrophe yet.

Kel checked mule.

Maro looked signal frame.

Sori looked posting board.

Dask looked everything.

I looked ground.

Tracks.

Several.

Boots.

Cart? No.

One drag mark toward north reeds.

Could mean anything.

Vessa said, "Nobody spread."

Good.

We stayed together.

Inside station, kettle warm.

Not hot.

One chair tipped.

Ledger open on table.

Last line half written.

`SECOND TONE FROM WEST. ARM THREE...`

Then nothing.

There.

Reserve stirred.

Not open.

Not yet.

Vessa looked at me.

"Greg."

"Locked?"

She held my eyes.

"Not yet. Record first."

There.

Correct.

I took notebook.

Practitioner.

Magic, yes.

But mostly practice of knowing when not to become something larger too soon.
