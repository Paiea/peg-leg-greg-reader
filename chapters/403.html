CHAPTER 334

THE OBSERVER

White Span looked different in wind.

Good.

If it had looked exactly the same, I would have been annoyed on principle.

We left Westbank while the inn sign still had both fake suns painted black against night.

Half mile.

Easy.

Cold enough to make shoulder complain before movement warmed it.

Not injured.

Just reminding me it existed.

Lessa waited at west rise with two lanterns, one broom, one dented wash basin, and no coffee.

"Fenner forgot coffee."

"Again?"

"He remembered basin."

"Tradeoffs."

She pointed toward bridge.

"Wind's here."

It was.

North to northeast.

Not strong.

Enough.

Lume's surface carried small diagonal ridges where yesterday had been broad calm sheet.

No mirror.

Not yet.

Dask was already happy.

I could tell because he had become quieter.

Dangerous sign.

Fenner came up hill carrying timing board under one arm and coffee under other.

Lessa looked betrayed.

"You remembered?"

"I contain multitudes."

"You contain one cup."

"Mine."

She took it.

"Now none."

Local command structure.

Efficient.

Vessa checked positions.

Same as yesterday where possible.

I took witness post.

Dask sight/timing.

Maro bank/weather.

Sori master record.

Kel bridge traffic and practical effects.

Fenner local sequence.

Lessa utility and insulting everyone.

Good team.

The basin sat on a waist-high stone table beside sight frame.

Clean enough.

Metal dull, not polished.

Filled from Lume before first light.

Water inside trembled slightly when gusts hit rim.

Not ideal isolated still surface.

Still useful.

Dask wanted to shield it.

Fenner said no.

"Same sky. Same wind. Different container. Don't improve test until after we see test."

Dask looked at me.

"He's right."

"I know."

"You looked upset."

"I am capable of being right and upset simultaneously."

There.

I liked him again.

My slate plate stayed wrapped in bag.

For now.

Fenner had approved supplemental use later if needed, but not before first observation pass.

Good.

No new toy contaminating baseline.

The east brightened.

Real sunrise came like yesterday.

Slow.

Normal.

Respectable.

Physical sun broke ridge.

Gold touched upper bridge.

We watched downstream of arch four.

Nothing.

One breath.

Ten.

Thirty.

Wind moved chop diagonally across river.

No false band.

Dask marked.

Fenner said nothing.

Lessa whispered, "Don't sulk at water."

"I'm not."

"You are."

Minute one.

Nothing broad.

Then Maro said, "There."

Not center.

Closer to west bank.

A narrow patch behind one projecting stone shoulder where surface chop weakened.

Pale gold.

Wrong direction.

Small.

Maybe six feet across.

It appeared in protected water while exposed water beside it stayed ordinary gray-gold from real east sun.

There.

New.

Not answer.

Dask's pencil moved.

My brain moved too.

Calm patch.

Shelter.

False reflection present locally.

Exposed chop no broad band.

Do not say requires calm.

Not yet.

The patch widened along sheltered edge maybe another three feet.

Stopped.

Wind gust hit.

Surface broke.

False light fragmented.

Not vanished.

Split into little flashes between ripples.

Then rejoined when water flattened for two breaths.

Good.

Very good.

Not because solved.

Because behavior.

I wrote:

**FALSE REFLECTION PRESENT IN PARTIALLY SHELTERED WEST-BANK WATER WHILE EXPOSED ADJACENT WATER REMAINED CHOPPED AND DID NOT FORM PRIOR BROAD BAND.**

Then:

**WITHIN SHELTERED PATCH, STRONGER RIPPLE FRAGMENTED VISIBLE BAND INTO DISCONTINUOUS FLASHES. LESS RIPPLE RESTORED MORE CONTINUOUS APPEARANCE.**

Sori read over once.

Nodded.

Dask said, "That suggests surface coherence threshold."

"Suggests," I said.

"Yes."

He actually used word correctly.

Progress was disgusting.

Basin.

I looked.

Nothing false.

It reflected east sky normally.

Metal rim vibrated slightly in wind.

Water inside had small ripples.

No west-facing gold.

Fenner saw too.

"Basin's boring."

"Useful boring," Sori said.

"Still boring."

Then Lessa said, "Wait."

We waited.

She crouched beside basin without touching.

One corner of basin sat behind sight-frame post.

Tiny sheltered wedge.

Water there was calmer than rest.

For maybe one second I thought pale gold flashed.

Maybe.

Then gone.

Dask inhaled.

I said, "No claim."

He exhaled.

Lessa said, "I saw something."

"So did I."

Sori: "What?"

"Possible pale flash in sheltered basin corner. Less than one breath. Could be real-sun glint, observer angle, rim reflection, or false phenomenon."

Dask said, "Record ambiguous."

"Yes."

There.

We did.

No miracle.

No clean lab answer.

Good.

False patch in river lasted longer than I expected.

Not six minutes broad.

Closer to four, depending whether you counted fragmented flashes as continuous event.

That became argument.

Dask wanted two timings.

Fenner wanted one operational time.

Sori solved it.

"Primary visible interval and continuous-band interval separately."

There.

Primary false-light presence: about four minutes fifty breaths by board.

Continuous sheltered band: about three minutes twenty breaths before stronger chop broke it mostly into intermittent fragments.

Then fragments continued perhaps another minute and change before nothing distinguishable remained.

No one liked approximate wording.

Reality remained rude.

Kel crossed bridge during event again.

No footing issue.

No visibility loss.

He reported false patch harder to notice from bridge deck today because wind broke surface.

Practical consequence.

Fenner reopened ferries sooner than yesterday because broad distracting band never formed.

That mattered.

Administrative hold shortened.

Not because danger lower proven.

Because distraction lower observed.

Toma Grell took first ferry.

Berrit complained he should have.

Toma told him spiritual carp could wait.

Good.

After false light vanished, Fenner approved my slate plate.

Not for anomaly.

Current.

We went down west bank to safe shelf.

Riva's plate set flat at waterline.

Maro helped choose position.

Wet.

Film pulled along grooves southeast with slight cross-bias eastward under north wind.

Matched local float board generally.

Good.

Useful object remained useful object.

No glowing.

No secret reaction.

Dask looked almost disappointed.

I was pleased.

"Current reader reads current," I said.

"Revolutionary."

"Write paper."

Sori said, "Don't."

We packed.

Then route changed.

Fenner had message from Ira Fen by noon rider.

Outside witness obligation complete after two mornings.

Continue extension **east along Lume** to **Bellweather Crossing** if crew willing and Vessa approves.

Reason was not another miracle waiting on schedule.

It was infrastructure.

Bellweather maintained a line of old river mirrors and ferry sight posts used for dawn navigation before modern markers.

Regional office wanted comparison because White Span phenomenon might depend on local geometry, local river bend, or broader river reach.

Not proof.

Comparison.

Bellweather was roughly nine road miles east.

One overnight stop possible at **Low Fen Orchard** halfway.

There.

World opened again.

Vessa read message twice.

"Optional extension through Bellweather, return routing after unless new operational reason."

Sori checked stamp.

"Valid."

Kel said, "Road?"

Fenner said, "East bank road dry. One old ferry stair washed out, detour marked."

Maro looked interested.

"Bellweather mirrors still there?"

Fenner shrugged.

"Some. Half used for hanging laundry last I saw."

Dask looked offended on behalf of mirrors.

Excellent.

Vessa looked around crew.

"We can stop here. Obligation met. Or continue."

No pressure.

Good.

I looked east.

Lume bent broad and bright through grasslands.

Nine miles.

Another place.

Another comparison.

Another bed not mine.

Home remained south.

Still there.

I wanted it.

Also wanted east.

Apparently this was my problem now.

"Continue," I said.

Maro nodded.

Kel: "Fine."

Sori: "If paperwork stays clean."

Dask said, "Bellweather has historical mirror infrastructure."

Vessa: "That is not a vote."

"Continue."

"Better."

We left White Span after midday meal.

No ceremony.

Fenner shook Vessa's hand.

Lessa gave Dask his own pencil cord back because he had left one yesterday.

Berrit told me fish had definitely been confused this morning.

Toma told him fish had definitely been fish.

I recorded neither.

Westbank faded behind.

The Two Suns sign visible longer than village because yellow paint was offensively bright.

Then bend took it.

East road followed Lume but stayed thirty to a hundred yards inland depending bank.

New grammar.

River always nearby.

Sometimes visible.

Sometimes hidden behind willow-like scrub and low white bluffs.

Sound came and went.

Flat boats moved downstream with square dark sails.

Road traffic more commercial than north basin.

Barrels.

Fruit carts.

Net bundles.

One wagon carrying six enormous ceramic jars tied individually in straw.

Dask wanted to know contents.

Driver shouted, "VINEGAR!"

Dask asked, "Why six jars?"

Driver shouted, "BECAUSE SEVEN BROKE THE AXLE!"

Good answer.

Shoulder did well first two miles.

Then began low ache.

I shifted bag.

Kel noticed.

Didn't ask.

At next shade stone he stopped mule for water.

Coincidence.

Probably not.

I took ten breaths.

Nobody counted aloud.

Crew had learned me.

That was strange.

Nice.

The road climbed away from Lume near afternoon.

Below, river surface flashed real sun correctly.

East afternoon light.

No second reflection.

Normal river could still be pretty after becoming evidence.

Important.

Around fourth mile we found first old river mirror.

Not at Bellweather.

A roadside remnant.

Tall stone post with rusted iron frame holding oval silvered plate tilted toward river.

Clouded almost black.

One corner cracked.

Laundry not present.

Dask stopped.

Vessa said, "Thirty seconds."

He approached without touching.

Stamp at base:

**BW-R3 / SIGHT 12**

Bellweather Reach 3 maybe.

Maro said, "Old dawn sight marker. Boatmen lined reflection with notch to read approach in fog."

"Magic?" I asked.

"Mirror."

Dask said, "Silver treatment sometimes directional."

Maro: "Mirror."

I liked Maro.

The plate reflected us badly.

Six stretched people.

One mule.

My head looked long.

Unacceptable.

No false sun.

No anomaly.

We moved.

Low Fen Orchard appeared near dusk.

Not much settlement.

An orchard on slightly raised island of dry ground, rows of low black-barked trees with pale green fruit, one long farmhouse, two worker sheds, road shelter, hitch rail.

Sign:

**LOW FEN ORCHARD / ROAD BEDS / PEAR BRANDY / NO CREDIT**

Promising.

Owner **Hedda Morn** met us with pruning knife still in hand.

Broad woman, gray braid, left knee stiff.

"Survey?"

Vessa showed stamp.

"Beds six?"

"Four beds. Two hammocks."

Sori opened mouth.

Hedda said, "Hammocks count if you sleep."

Sori closed mouth.

Interesting.

Vessa said, "Assignment coverage?"

"Road rate, yes. Dinner extra if not listed."

It was listed.

Good.

We had stew with pears in it.

I expected terrible.

It wasn't.

Dask hated it.

This made meal better.

After dinner Hedda poured tiny finger of pear brandy for herself, none for us unless paid.

I did not buy alcohol.

Mostly because shoulder and dawn schedule.

Also because one copper for bath had been smarter.

Maybe later.

Outside orchard, Lume glimmered through trees half mile away.

Tomorrow Bellweather.

Old mirrors.

Different river geometry.

Comparison rather than answer.

That felt right.

I checked bag before hammock.

Quietener.

Slate reader.

Tavi rock.

Renewal paste.

Thirteen copper.

No magic attempts.

No fights.

No solution.

But we knew more.

Wind chop mattered to visible form.

Shelter mattered enough to produce local patch.

Isolated basin did not clearly reproduce effect, though one ambiguous flash existed.

Current stayed current.

Ferries changed procedure based on observed distraction.

And route moved east.

Observer.

Not solver.

For now, better.
