CHAPTER 389

THE BIDDER

Auctions were dangerous because they turned not buying something into losing.

That was bullshit.

Very effective bullshit.

I knew this before breakfast.

Knowing did not help.

Sori found me in Dockhouse Seven common room with the Dock Eight auction notice flattened beside my bowl.

"No."

"Good morning."

"You're studying it."

"Reading."

"Same thing when you want something."

"I don't know if I want anything."

She sat across from me.

"Worse."

The notice listed twelve lots.

Frames A, B, and C were not twelve separate lots because auctioneers understood temptation better than engineers.

**LOT 4: BK-12 TRANSFER FRAME A, RESTRICTED OPERATIONAL, COMPLETE**

**LOT 5: BK-17 TRANSFER FRAME B, REPAIR / PARTS**

**LOT 6: BK-17 TRANSFER FRAME C, SERVICEABLE UNLOADED, LOADED RECERTIFICATION REQUIRED**

Then smaller component lots.

Guide rollers.

Jaw assemblies.

Stop pins.

Spring housings.

Two old lateral collars from unrelated frames.

One box labeled **MIXED BK HARDWARE, UNVERIFIED**.

There.

Box.

Always box.

Sori followed eyes.

"No."

"You don't know what I'm looking at."

"Mixed hardware."

"How?"

"You like boxes where seller admits ignorance."

That was unfair.

Accurate.

Still unfair.

Kel came down carrying bread.

"Auction?"

"Apparently."

"I'm going."

Sori looked at him.

"Why?"

"Frame C."

"You're buying a transfer frame?"

"No. Want to see what it sells for."

There.

Normal person.

I could learn.

Vessa entered behind him.

No field coat.

That changed her more than expected.

Still Vessa.

Just woman having breakfast instead of route authority.

"Annex has nothing for us," she said. "We're off assignment unless we accept something new."

Dask appeared from stairs.

"Mirror guild opens at second bell."

Sori pointed at him.

"Also no."

"I didn't ask you."

"Practicing."

Maro came last.

He looked rested.

Annoying.

"Duskport packet leaves fourth bell," he said.

I looked at him.

Sori closed eyes.

"Of course you asked."

Maro shrugged.

"Route board was there."

"Cost?" Kel asked.

"Three copper passenger deck. Five with bunk allotment if space. Freight separate."

There.

Duskport had price now.

Three copper.

Not much.

Which made it more dangerous.

"How long?" I asked.

"One day and one night if river behaves. Arrives next morning or midday."

"How far?"

"Packet clerk said about thirty river miles. Road longer."

Thirty.

Carrow got farther away without moving.

Sori watched me look at the route board.

"Duskport?"

"Maybe."

"Market?"

"Probably."

"Idiot."

"Sometimes."

Vessa poured tea.

"We decide after auction. No rush. Daily packet means daily."

Good.

No artificial deadline.

That made choice more real.

I hated it.

Dock Eight public viewing started before bell two.

Auction yard looked different with buyers.

Yesterday it had been work.

Today everything had prices waiting to happen.

Dealers wore chalk boards on belts.

Repair shops sent apprentices with measuring sticks.

Two freight companies had men in clean coats pretending not to know each other.

A woman with three gold rings inspected frame feet by tapping them with knuckle.

Bran Tey stood near rail with arms folded.

Same jaw stripe.

Same folded ear.

He saw us.

"Morning."

Longest thing he'd ever said.

Kel nodded toward C.

"Viewing open?"

"Yes. Don't cycle."

"Wouldn't."

Bran looked at me.

"You bidding?"

"Maybe."

"Don't buy B."

I laughed.

He didn't.

"I wasn't planning to."

"Good."

That was probably illegal auction advice.

Or friendship.

Hard to tell with Bran.

Frame A drew serious attention.

Complete operational hardware had value even restricted.

Frame C too.

B attracted scavengers.

People cared less about whole failed frame than which pieces could be certified separately.

I cared about pieces too.

That was problem.

I walked lots once without touching.

Then again with Kel.

Lot 8 held two lateral collars.

Neither from B.

Different lots.

One had a stop tooth chipped.

Other looked intact but retirement reason said **UNSTABLE RETURN UNDER COLD LOAD, NOT REPRODUCED WARM**.

Interesting.

Not useful enough.

Lot 9: jaw assemblies.

Heavy.

No.

Lot 10: guide rollers and mounts.

Maybe useful if I owned transfer frame.

I did not.

Lot 11: stop pins and springs.

Generic enough to be useful to someone.

Not me.

Lot 12.

Mixed BK hardware.

Wood crate maybe two feet long.

Contents tagged individually where known.

Four worn guide shims.

Three spring seats.

A brass center-index plate.

Two small counterweight pucks.

One intact hand-sized **load-memory tongue** from older BK-9 series.

There.

I stopped.

Kel noticed.

"That?"

"Maybe."

Load-memory tongue was not memory like thoughts.

Old industrial term.

On certain transfer frames, tongue held last neutral bias long enough for operator to see whether frame returned to same resting center after unload.

Diagnostic aid.

Not active control.

Not power source.

Not valuable alone to most people because modern frames built index directly into collar housing.

Old-life me had used similar things.

Not this make.

Same idea.

I crouched as much as current geometry allowed.

Tag:

**BK-9 INDEX TONGUE / RETIRED / RESPONSE UNKNOWN / NO MATCHING FRAME IN LOT**

No price.

Auction.

Sori appeared at shoulder.

"What does it do?"

"Shows whether old frame returns to same neutral bias after unload."

"Do you own old frame?"

"No."

"Then no."

"Could be bench useful."

"For what?"

There.

Good question.

I looked again.

Tongue was brass strip with weighted dark tip and three tiny witness marks along base.

Mounting holes.

Mechanical bias.

Could possibly be adapted as repeatable displacement indicator.

Possibly.

That word had cost me money before.

"I don't know yet."

Sori smiled.

"Then no."

"Maybe cheap."

"Still no."

Kel crouched.

"Counterweight pucks useful."

"For what?"

He pointed at me.

"See?"

Fuck.

We moved on.

Public auction began second bell exactly.

Auctioneer **Ressa Vale** stood on low platform.

Sori froze.

Ressa looked at Sori.

"No."

Sori's mouth opened.

Ressa grinned.

"Everyone asks."

I nearly fell over.

Sori pointed at me.

"Not one word."

"I have several."

"Die quietly."

Ressa Vale was maybe fifty, broad shoulders, red scarf, voice capable of moving freight without crane.

"Terms! Copper unless announced silver. Deposit immediate. Full payment before lot leaves yard. Restricted hardware stays restricted. Certification does not transfer because you feel lucky. If you buy something stupid, congratulations on your property."

Excellent.

Lot 1 went fast.

Old dock winch housing.

Seven copper.

Lot 2, chain guides.

Four.

Lot 3, mixed iron feet.

Three.

Then A.

Frame A opened twelve copper.

Jumped fifteen.

Eighteen.

Twenty-two.

Twenty-six.

I stopped thinking about owning whole frame around twelve.

Repair shop and freight company fought to thirty-one.

Sold thirty-two copper.

Kel whistled.

"More than I thought."

"Complete hardware."

"Still."

Frame B opened two.

Nobody.

One parts dealer offered two.

Another three.

Gold-ring woman four.

Dealer five.

Sold five.

There.

Failed frame still worth five as metal and components.

Rational.

Frame C opened eight.

Quickly twelve.

Fifteen.

Eighteen.

Then slowed.

Same-lot note and loaded recertification mattered.

Gold-ring woman took it for twenty-one.

Exactly my purse.

I could have bought it.

Technically.

And then owned twenty-one copper of transfer frame I had nowhere to put, no reason to use, and no money.

Beautiful demonstration of difference between affordability and sense.

Sori leaned close.

"You could've."

"Yes."

"You didn't."

"Yes."

"Growth."

"Stop."

Lots 7 through 11 went mostly to shops.

Prices low enough to tempt, high enough to remind me other people also understood hardware.

Then mixed box.

Lot 12.

Ressa lifted inventory sheet.

"Mixed BK hardware. Unverified. No matching frame. Scrap and bench use. Opening one copper."

One.

A man behind me said, "One."

Ressa pointed.

"One. Two?"

I knew man.

Not personally.

He'd bought B.

Parts dealer.

He wanted box because parts.

Reasonable.

I should let him.

"Two," I said.

Sori inhaled through nose.

Dealer looked at me.

"Three."

Three.

Box probably worth three in metal/components alone.

Maybe more.

Load-memory tongue alone might be useful to repair shop with BK-9 work.

I had no matching frame.

No immediate use.

But diagnostic indicator could be adapted.

Could.

Not would.

Auction brain whispered losing.

Fuck auction brain.

I set ceiling before next bid.

Four.

Not because I had twenty-five.

Because four was price where mixed box remained tolerable experiment and salvage, even if tongue useless to me.

"Four," I said.

Dealer said, "Five."

Immediately.

There.

Done.

I shut mouth.

Ressa looked around.

"Six?"

Silence.

She looked at me.

I did nothing.

"Five once. Five twice. Sold."

Dealer got box.

My body felt stupid little loss.

Like I'd failed.

I had not.

I had succeeded at not paying above my number.

Harder than it sounded.

Sori said nothing for several seconds.

Then:

"Good."

"Don't."

"You stopped."

"I had ceiling."

"You had ceiling before bidding?"

"Yes."

She looked genuinely impressed.

Rude.

Kel said, "I would've gone six."

"Why?"

"Pucks."

"You don't know what you'd use them for."

He stared at me.

"Fuck you."

Excellent.

Auction ended before third bell.

I spent zero copper.

Twenty-five survived.

That should have felt boring.

It did not.

I had bid.

Lost.

On purpose.

Different skill.

Bran caught us leaving.

"Good ceiling."

I looked at him.

"You heard?"

"Everyone heard."

"Auction. Right."

He nodded toward dealer loading B.

"He'll make money."

"Probably."

"You wouldn't."

"Probably not."

"Good."

Bran walked away.

Five words of approval.

Basically speech.

Outside Dock Eight, Sable Quay was fully awake.

Cranes moving.

Bells.

Carts.

Packet horns.

River traffic stacking into lanes.

We had no assignment.

That still felt strange.

Vessa gathered us near public board.

"Decisions."

No clipboard.

No authority voice.

Almost.

"I'm staying Quay one more night," she said. "Annex asked if I'd review a route form this afternoon. Personal choice, not crew order."

Kel said, "I'll stay. Dock work tomorrow maybe."

Dask said, "Mirror guild."

Everyone ignored him.

Maro looked at river board.

"I might take packet east."

There.

I turned.

"Duskport?"

"Never been."

"You said you'd seen Lume years ago."

"Not lower than Quay."

Interesting.

Sori folded arms.

"I have to decide whether my records duty ended when annex accepted packet or whether I am voluntarily following idiots."

"Which idiots?" I asked.

"Plural was deliberate."

Dask said, "If Duskport has lower-river optical guilds, I could compare fabrication methods."

Sori looked at him.

"Three idiots."

Kel said, "I'm staying."

"One sensible."

Vessa looked at me.

No order.

No job.

No route reason.

Twenty-five copper.

Three-copper deck passage.

One day and one night.

Thirty river miles.

Daily packet, so not now-or-never.

Home south and west.

Duskport east/downriver.

I thought about Cala.

Tavi.

Lyssa.

Theatre.

Vale office.

Carrow streets.

Not because I wanted to leave them.

Because I could feel exact direction they were from here.

Home had become compass point.

That was new.

I could go back.

Not today perhaps, but deliberately.

Route existed.

Money existed.

I wasn't trapped.

That mattered.

Maybe more than distance.

"Tomorrow packet," I said.

Sori stared.

"You decided?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Good question.

Not assignment.

Not mystery.

Not emergency.

Not money.

"Because I want to see it."

There.

Simple.

Maro smiled.

Dask said, "Good."

Sori looked offended by lack of argument.

"That's it?"

"That's it."

"No elaborate justification?"

"Would you prefer one?"

"No."

"Then excellent meeting."

Vessa nodded once.

"Packet office before close. Buy tomorrow passage if you mean it."

There.

Commitment should cost something.

Good.

We walked to lower packet office.

Maro came.

Dask came.

Sori came while claiming she had not decided.

Packet clerk sat behind brass grille.

Sign:

**DUSKPORT DAILY**

**DEPART 4TH BELL**

**PASSENGER DECK 3C**

**BUNK ALLOTMENT 5C, SPACE LIMITED**

**MEALS SEPARATE**

**PERSONAL BAG INCLUDED**

**LARGE FREIGHT WEIGHED**

I asked, "Crutches count personal?"

Clerk looked at me.

"Are they attached to you emotionally?"

"Deeply."

"Personal."

"Good."

"Deck has benches. Covered middle section. Two stairs to lower washroom. Crew can store crutches during sleep if you want."

"I don't."

"Then don't."

Excellent transport policy.

"Tomorrow deck," I said.

"Three copper."

I paid.

Twenty-five to twenty-two.

Clerk stamped small blue ticket.

**SABLE QUAY -> DUSKPORT**

There.

Choice became object.

Maro bought deck too.

Dask hesitated.

Not money, I thought.

Guild.

"Tomorrow?" I asked.

"Mirror guild opens second bell. Packet fourth."

"You can do both."

He looked relieved.

"Yes."

Bought ticket.

Sori stood at grille.

Clerk said, "Travel?"

She looked at me.

"I hate all of you."

"Three copper," clerk said.

She paid.

Four idiots.

Kel and Vessa stayed Quay.

That changed crew.

Not breakup.

Not drama.

Just people choosing differently.

Good.

We told them outside.

Kel shrugged.

"Bring back something stupid."

"No."

"You will."

"Probably."

Vessa looked at four tickets.

Then at me.

"You're not my crew tomorrow."

That landed.

"I know."

"If something goes wrong, you make your own call."

"I know."

"Don't lock in because you think that's what I'd ask."

There.

Interesting.

"Okay."

She held gaze.

"Greg."

"I heard you."

"Good."

Then she offered hand.

I took it.

No ceremony.

"You were useful," she said.

"High praise."

"It is."

Kel clapped my good shoulder carefully.

"If you come back missing other leg, I'm charging repair labor."

"Fair."

Sori said, "Why is that the standard?"

"He has pattern."

"One is not pattern."

Dask said, "Statistically, sample size is inadequate."

Everyone told him to shut up.

Perfect.

Afternoon opened.

No work.

No auction left.

Ticket in pocket.

Twenty-two copper.

Vale twenty-three.

One copper difference again.

Funny.

I could have paid five for bunk.

Didn't.

Deck was fine.

I could have bought PF-3.

Didn't.

Still maybe wanted it.

That was okay.

Wanting did not create obligation.

Neither did bidding.

Neither did money.

Neither did road.

I spent rest of afternoon doing something radical.

Nothing strategic.

Walked Quay.

Ate fried dough for bits.

Watched crane crew move six barrels without dropping any.

Bought paper and charcoal for bits because I was low.

Sat on river wall.

Tavi's striped rock came out of bag.

I rolled it in palm.

Carrow object.

Duskport ticket beside it.

Two directions.

Not enemies.

I wrote home.

Not full route report.

Cala would hate that.

Short.

**Still safe. Work ended at Sable Quay. I chose to keep going downriver tomorrow with Maro, Sori, and Dask. Duskport, one day and one night by packet. This part is my choice. Shoulder almost normal. Crutch repaired. I have money. I miss you. Tell Tavi his rock is now farther from home than he is. Tell Lyssa I am telling her because I chose something.**

I stopped.

Read last line.

Kept it.

There was public post west at evening bell.

Not fast.

Good enough.

I paid postage in bits.

No copper.

Then sat until sun lowered.

Lume turned gold.

Correct direction.

Respectable river.

Tomorrow I'd ride it thirty miles farther away.

Not because bridge failed.

Not because Survey extended contract.

Not because strange light needed witness.

Not because anyone needed me.

Because I bought ticket.

That was different.

Maybe stupid.

Maybe good.

Usually same ingredients.

Ticket stayed in shirt pocket.

I checked twice.

Then third time because growth had limits.
