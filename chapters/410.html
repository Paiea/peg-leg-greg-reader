CHAPTER 383

THE PROSPECT

Duskport had stairs.

Of course it did.

The sea had apparently not improved civilization.

Bright Gull tied south quay eight minutes before second bell.

Harven Coil was standing before gangplank touched stone.

Captain Iven pointed at him.

"You fall in, hearing gets worse."

"Understood."

"Wait."

He waited.

Barely.

Mira Sol had both bird cages ready.

One bird made noise like old hinge.

"Shut up," she told it.

It did not.

Rell Koss got freight lane clear.

Gangplank down.

Harven went first after crew.

Not running.

Fast enough to make running jealous.

Mira followed with porter and cages.

At bottom Harven turned.

"Quarantine runner. South gate?"

"Blue arch," Mira said.

"I remember."

"You better."

Then he disappeared into Duskport.

No ending.

Good.

Or terrible.

Same thing sometimes.

Mira looked at me before leaving.

"If you want word about the boy, packet office posts notices from upriver."

"Good to know."

Fucking face had traveled thirty miles and developed reputation.

Then she left.

Iven stood by rail while passengers emptied.

I offered hand.

"Thanks."

"For what?"

"Not killing us."

"Low standard."

"Reliable one."

She shook once.

"Duskport packet office is Pier Four if you need westbound. Bright Gull returns tomorrow afternoon unless freight changes."

There.

Return route.

I liked knowing.

Didn't mean taking.

Maro came down behind me.

Dask had already stopped halfway down gangplank because he could see something reflective across basin.

Sori shoved him gently between shoulders.

"Move."

"Those are signal mirrors."

"Move while knowing that."

We entered Duskport.

First thing city did was almost run me over with fish.

Cart.

Fish cart.

Important distinction.

Driver yelled.

I moved.

Current body could move fast enough for fish.

Progress.

South quay was not a street.

It was six streets arguing over same stone.

Porters.

Carts.

Rope sellers.

Customs runners.

Fishmongers.

Sailors carrying things that should have required three sailors.

Children with message tubes.

Three languages in first hundred yards, one I knew poorly, one not at all.

Salt on everything.

Not river salt.

Sea.

I could smell it under tar and fish and smoke.

I wanted to go see water.

Dask wanted mirrors.

Sori wanted lodging before we became four idiots paying emergency rates after dark.

Maro wanted food.

Democracy failed immediately.

Sori won.

"Beds first."

"Sea is large," Maro said. "Will probably remain."

"Probably?" Dask asked.

Maro looked at him.

"For Greg."

I hated all of them.

Cheap lodging near south quay existed if cheap meant relative to Duskport.

First place wanted two copper each.

No.

Second wanted one copper plus bits, shared loft, wash basin extra.

Maybe.

Third had sign:

**ANCHOR'S END**

**BEDS 1C**

**FLOOR 4 BITS**

**NO FIGHTING INSIDE**

Inside.

Important qualifier.

Sori inspected loft.

I inspected stairs.

Twelve.

Wide.

Rail both sides.

Acceptable.

Innkeeper **Pell Varo** looked at crutches.

"Loft bed near stair?"

"Yes."

"Same price."

"Good."

"If you fall, scream after landing. Wakes fewer people."

"Thoughtful."

We took four beds.

I paid one copper.

Twenty-two to twenty-one.

There.

Vale twenty-three.

Back under debt by two.

Still fine.

Bag locked in foot chest with my small brass lock.

Quietener.

WS-F/9.

Tavi rock.

All there.

Crutches stayed with me.

Obviously.

Then sea.

We walked west through market lane because Dask said mirror district was west and Sori said if he vanished alone she would sell his coat.

"You cannot."

"I can sell coat."

"It's mine."

"Market decides."

Duskport widened as we moved.

River basin on right.

Warehouses gave way to chandlers and workshops.

Then shipyards.

Masts everywhere.

Not decorative.

Working forest.

Ropes thicker than wrist.

Anchor chains.

Sails stretched for repair across frames like giant skins.

Magic lived here too.

Not fireworks.

Blue seam-lines along one drydock wall that pushed seep water toward drains.

Wind vanes with tiny charged fins correcting dock cranes.

A warehouse door whose iron threshold flashed dull green when sealed cargo crossed.

Dask pointed at all of it.

I told him to conserve finger.

Then we reached seawall.

There.

Ocean.

No river bank opposite.

No far wall.

Gray-blue under pale morning.

Coastal ships small against it.

First-life me knew oceans.

Second-life me stood on two crutches and looked anyway.

Body did not care what memory knew.

Chest opened a little.

Wind hit face.

Salt.

Gulls.

Distance.

Maro stood beside me.

"Worth three copper?"

"Yes."

Sori said, "Don't tell packet office."

Dask was looking backward.

Of course.

"There's a mobility shop."

I turned.

Across seawall road, between sailmaker and rope-repair yard, hung wooden sign shaped like boot.

Except boot ended at shin.

Under it:

**HARK & VENN**

**BRACES / ARTICULATED SUPPORTS / WORK LIMBS / MARINE FITTINGS**

I looked longer than intended.

Sori noticed.

Of course.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"That's a lie."

Maro read sign.

Then looked at left leg.

Not rude.

Direct.

"Have you ever tried one?"

"Work limb?"

"Peg."

There.

Word.

Simple.

I looked down.

Left knee.

Nothing below.

Crutches had become normal enough I sometimes forgot they were equipment until city gave me twelve stairs and fish cart.

"Not current life."

Sori frowned.

"You had one first life?"

"No. Had leg."

"Right."

Maro said, "Could help."

I knew.

Obviously.

But knowing abstractly and shop existing across street were different things.

Dask said, "Depends socket, stump tolerance, alignment, terrain, gait training, whether knee control is good, whether they use passive foot or rigid end, weight, load transfer, skin shear, and probably six other things I don't know."

Sori stared.

"That was almost useful."

"I read."

"Terrifying."

I looked at shop again.

A man came out carrying wooden lower leg.

Not pirate peg.

More complex.

Leather socket above.

Shaped shin.

Broad split foot with dark rubber-like sole.

Brass side stay.

He handed it to woman in dock-worker trousers.

She sat on bench, rolled trouser leg, fitted it over right below-knee stump, tightened straps, stood between parallel rails outside shop.

Then walked.

Not perfectly.

Better than crutches for carrying crate with both hands.

That mattered.

Very much.

I had two hands.

Crutches usually rented both.

Peg could buy one or both back depending task.

Mobility not replacement.

Option.

Options were expensive.

Sori said, "Go ask."

"We just got here."

"Shop is here now."

"I can ask later."

"You can."

She paused.

"You won't."

Rude.

Maybe true.

Maro said, "Ask price. Doesn't buy it."

Auction lesson returned.

Wanting did not create obligation.

Neither asking.

Fine.

We crossed.

Hark & Venn smelled like leather, cedar shavings, oil, and hot metal.

Walls held braces.

Splints.

Work hooks.

Artificial feet.

Jointed knees for above-knee loss.

Rigid pegs.

Marine legs with wide soles and drainage holes.

Some beautiful.

Some ugly.

All practical.

Woman behind bench looked up.

Maybe sixty.

Dark skin.

White hair cut short.

Three fingers missing from left hand.

She looked at me once.

Not crutches first.

Gait.

Shoulders.

Left knee.

Hands.

Then crutch tips.

Professional.

"Below knee."

"Yes."

"How long?"

"Current body?"

She paused.

"Interesting answer."

Sori covered face.

"Long enough healed," I said.

"Pain?"

"Not usually."

"Skin breakdown?"

"No."

"Knee range?"

I flexed.

She watched.

"Good. Sit."

"I'm only asking."

"Then sit while asking."

I sat.

Her name, according brass plate:

**MASTA VENN - LIMBWRIGHT**

Venn.

No.

I did not ask.

Growth.

She pulled stool.

"What do you want?"

There.

Not what happened.

What do you want.

I looked at doorway.

Dock worker outside walking rails.

"More hands."

Masta nodded.

"Good answer."

"Not speed."

"Speed comes later if it comes."

"I want to carry something without using mouth."

"Also good."

Sori said, "He does use mouth."

"Sometimes."

Masta ignored her.

"You travel?"

"Apparently."

"Work?"

"Mixed. Field. Theatre. Carrying. Survey recently."

"Fight?"

I looked at crutch tooth repair.

She followed eyes.

"Yes," I said.

"Don't fight on first fitting."

"Wasn't plan."

"People say that."

She asked permission before touching knee/stump.

I said yes.

Quick assessment.

No drama.

Pressure points.

Scar position.

Knee control.

Hip strength.

Standing balance between rails.

She had me put crutches aside and stand on right leg with hands on rails.

Then shift pelvis left into padded temporary support block under stump without loading full.

Body understood possibility immediately.

Not walking.

Just line.

Weight could go somewhere that was not right foot or hands.

Interesting.

Very.

Masta stepped back.

"You could use work leg."

Sori leaned forward.

"How much?"

I glared.

Masta did not.

"Depends."

Of course.

"Basic rigid dock peg, leather socket, no articulated ankle, indoor/flat-yard use, three fittings and gait rail sessions. Eighty-four copper if wood ordinary and no rush."

Silence.

Dask said, "Oh."

Good contribution.

Masta continued.

"Travel work limb with split foot, replaceable sole, better wet grip, side stay, adjustable socket because bodies change. One silver and eighteen copper."

There.

Silver.

Actual silver.

No conversion stated.

Did not need one.

More than twenty-one copper.

Much more.

"Marine drainage foot and salt-resistant fittings starts one silver thirty-six. Articulated ankle higher. Charged balance assist higher than that. I don't recommend charged assist first limb unless you enjoy paying me to watch you fall creatively."

I liked her.

Hated prices.

"How much higher?" Dask asked.

Masta looked at him.

"Are you buying?"

"No."

"Then enough."

Sori smiled.

I asked, "Basic peg would help carrying."

"Yes. Also teach bad gait if you pretend it's travel leg. Flat work. Short durations first. Skin checks. You keep crutches."

"Travel one?"

"Better for what you're describing. Still keep crutches. Limb is tool, not miracle. Some days stump says no. Some ground says no. Some stairs say go fuck yourself."

There.

Perfect.

"Timeline?"

"If I take you."

I blinked.

"If?"

"I have eleven active fittings. Three shipyard injuries this month. Two children outgrowing braces. One sailor who keeps breaking same foot because he believes instructions are personal attack."

"Fair."

"Deposit gets slot."

"How much?"

"Travel leg, twenty copper deposit. Balance due before final hardware leaves shop. Fittings over maybe eight to twelve days if you're local and stump behaves. Longer if not."

Twenty.

I had twenty-one.

Technically could place deposit.

Then one copper.

Food.

Lodging.

Travel.

Vale.

No.

Not impossible.

Worse.

Possible enough to hurt.

Sori knew arithmetic.

Her face changed.

No teasing.

"You could reserve it," she said.

"And have one copper."

"Yes."

"No."

Masta nodded.

"Good."

I looked at her.

"You don't want deposit?"

"I want clients who can eat between fittings."

There.

Again.

Human being.

"Any cheaper used?"

"Sockets are body-specific. Feet and stays sometimes reused. I have two salvage feet. Neither fits your load well. Could make something cheaper and worse. Won't."

"Payment plan?"

"For locals with guild guarantor. You're not local."

"Work trade?"

Masta looked at me again.

Different.

"What work?"

Good question.

I had no answer ready.

"Depends what you need."

"I need money."

"Same."

Maro coughed laugh.

Masta leaned back.

"You said Survey."

"Temporary regional route crew. Glasswake prep, field observation, load work, records."

"Records?"

"Yes."

"Mechanical?"

"Some."

"You know load frames?"

"Enough to be dangerous if unsupervised."

"Better answer than expert."

She tapped bench with three-fingered hand.

"My brother runs **Venn Marine Recovery** on East Hook. Salvage diving, wreck lift, quay recovery. They lost condition clerk two weeks ago. Not dead. Married someone in Gullhaven and left with no notice, which my brother considers worse."

Dask said, "Condition clerk?"

"Recovered hardware gets logged before yard tears it apart. Salt exposure. Charge residue. deformation. ownership marks. whether object should be touched. whether object should be left in fucking bucket until someone qualified arrives."

I looked at her.

She looked at me.

There.

"Pay?"

"Ask him."

"Would it cover leg?"

"Not in afternoon."

"Few days?"

"Depends whether you're useful."

Good.

"Name?"

"Hark Venn."

I looked at shop sign.

Hark & Venn.

"You're Venn."

"Yes."

"He's Hark?"

"No. Hark died twelve years ago. My brother is also Venn. Shop name stayed because changing sign costs money."

Sori laughed.

Masta pointed at her.

"You understand business."

"Unfortunately."

I asked, "You'd refer me?"

"I said ask him. Referral is expensive word."

"Fair."

"Tell him Masta said you know enough to answer questions without pretending. He'll hate that less than most things."

That was not referral.

It was something.

I looked at travel leg hanging wall.

One silver eighteen copper.

No.

Not yet.

But now price existed.

Worker existed.

Work existed.

Path existed.

Different from wanting impossible thing.

Dangerous.

I asked one more question.

"If I earn enough, you can make me one that actually fits how I move? Not generic dock peg."

Masta's expression sharpened.

"If you do fittings. If you listen. If you stop when skin says stop. If you don't ask me to build weapon because you fought dogs once."

"How did you know about dogs?"

"You have repaired bite marks on crutch and face of man waiting to tell story."

Fuck face.

"Then yes," she said. "I can build you work leg. Maybe travel leg. We find out what body tolerates before we decide what ego wants."

There.

I wanted it.

Badly.

Not because title.

Not because pirate joke.

Because I had spent nineteen years of second life adapting around missing lower leg and months making crutches into extensions of hands.

I was good on them.

Very good.

That did not mean I had to stay on them forever.

A peg would not give leg back.

It would give option.

Hands.

Standing.

Carrying.

Different fights maybe.

Different work.

Different city grammar.

Stairs still assholes.

But fewer things rented both arms.

Twenty-one copper in purse.

One silver eighteen copper on wall.

I did not know exact conversion here.

Didn't matter.

Not enough.

For first time in a while, that felt useful.

Not enough meant target.

Sori saw it happen.

"Oh no."

"What?"

"You have work face."

"Apparently I have many faces."

"This one's worse."

Maro said, "East Hook?"

Masta pointed through back wall.

"Follow seawall east past drydock bells. Big red recovery crane. Yard sign missing R because storm. Says VEN MARINE RECOVE Y. Can't miss it."

Dask said, "That seems missable."

"Then get lost."

We left.

Sea still there.

Duskport still huge.

Mirror district still waiting for Dask.

Harven's daughter somewhere in court.

Mira's birds somewhere in quarantine line.

Tessa's son upriver with fever, maybe.

World did not reorganize around my leg.

Good.

But my day did.

I stood on seawall with both crutches under arms and looked east.

Red recovery crane visible above roofs.

Missing-letter sign probably below it.

Twenty-one copper.

One silver eighteen.

No job.

Maybe job.

I could go sightseeing.

Could shop.

Could spend day at sea.

Could turn west tomorrow.

Instead I adjusted shoulder bag.

"East Hook," I said.

Sori groaned.

"You just arrived."

"Yes."

"You were supposed to wander."

"I am wandering toward employment."

"That's not wandering."

Maro started walking.

"Depends how bad interview goes."

Dask looked west toward mirrors.

Then east toward us.

Pain.

Actual pain.

"Go," Sori told him. "Mirror guild. Meet Anchor's End tonight."

"You're sure?"

"Yes."

He vanished west.

Three of us went east.

I looked once more at shop sign behind us.

Work limbs.

Not miracle.

Tool.

Expensive tool.

Good.

I knew what to do with expensive.

Become worth more.
