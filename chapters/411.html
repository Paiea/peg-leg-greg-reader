CHAPTER 411

THE HAND

East Hook smelled worse than south quay.

Achievement.

Less fish.

More rope rot.

Hot pitch.

Wet iron.

Old bilge.

Something chemical enough that Sori stopped breathing through nose.

"Charming."

Maro said, "Working harbor."

"That's excuse people use for smell."

Red recovery crane rose above roofs exactly where Masta said.

Big enough that I saw pulley block before yard.

Sign below:

**VENN MARINE RECOVE Y**

Missing R.

Masta had not exaggerated.

Dask would've enjoyed this.

Good he was elsewhere.

Yard occupied wedge between seawall and drydock cut.

Two recovery barges.

One narrow diving tender.

Stacks of chain.

Winches.

Floats.

Hooks.

Three open-sided sheds.

A fenced quarantine pen with warning boards.

And people.

A lot of people missing pieces.

Not everyone.

Not even most.

Enough that my eye stopped catching it as exception.

Man hauling line with left hand and iron work hook where right should be.

Woman on short rigid peg, thigh intact, moving crate labels with both hands.

Older diver with two natural legs but brace from hip to ankle.

Boy maybe seventeen with three fingers on one hand and no apparent concern.

Foreman on articulated below-knee limb, broad marine foot dark with wet grit.

There.

Not specialty shop because story wanted Greg to find one.

Specialty shop because harbor ate people slowly.

Sometimes quickly.

Winch cable.

Crane load.

Propeller.

Dock crush.

Gangrene after bad cuts.

Cold-water damage.

Wreck collapse.

Sea did not care whether body came home complete.

And Duskport was big enough to turn recurring disaster into industry.

Repair bodies.

Repair braces.

Repair tools.

Keep people working.

Maro followed my eyes.

"Makes sense."

"Yeah."

Sori said nothing.

Maybe because there wasn't joke.

At gate, clerk stopped us.

"Business?"

"Work. Maybe. Masta Venn sent me to ask."

"Sent or mentioned?"

Good harbor question.

"Mentioned. Specifically refused word referral."

Clerk smiled.

"That's Masta."

He shouted across yard.

"TAV! MASTA SENT A MAYBE!"

Man near recovery cradle shouted back.

"I'M BUSY!"

"HE HAS ONE LEG!"

"SO DO HALF OUR CUSTOMERS!"

Not half.

Point stood.

Clerk looked at me.

"He'll come."

"Tav?"

"Tavren Venn. Masta's brother."

There.

Name.

Tavren came three minutes later.

Mid-fifties.

Broad chest.

Dark skin like Masta.

Hair mostly gray and tied low.

Right ear missing top third.

Both hands present.

He wiped them on cloth already dirtier than hands.

Looked at me.

Crutches.

Bag.

Sori.

Maro.

Then back me.

"What'd she promise?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

"Said your condition clerk left."

His face changed.

"Married."

"Yes."

"Gullhaven."

"Also yes."

"No notice."

"Apparently."

"Bastard."

"She said similar."

"What can you do?"

There.

No résumé.

Good.

"Records. Field condition. Load systems enough to ask right specialist. Survey work. Some salvage-adjacent hardware. I don't know your yard."

"Magic?"

"Bronze. Not enough to sell you magic expertise."

One eyebrow.

"Good. Diving?"

"No."

"Swimming?"

"Yes. Not with this setup in working water."

"Good. Rigging?"

"Basic load sense. Not harbor-qualified."

"Ownership marks?"

"Can record. Don't know Duskport codes."

"Charge residue?"

"Observe with proper tools. Not universal appraiser."

"Useful answer."

Masta family trait.

Tavren pointed to fenced pen.

"Trial. Half day. Two copper if you finish records clean enough I don't have to redo them. One copper if I keep any useful portion but have to rebuild most. Nothing if you lie, touch quarantined hardware, or decide being clever is substitute for asking."

Two copper.

Not peg money.

Not nothing.

"What am I recording?"

"Today's shallow haul. Four pieces. Yard diver already cleared biological hazard. Charge status mixed. Ownership unresolved on two. You work under Nali."

"Nali?"

"Condition lead until I replace clerk."

"Then why need clerk?"

"Because Nali is also diver, rigging inspector, and only person here who can read old south-coast wreck marks without inventing letters. I prefer she does those jobs."

Reasonable.

"Two copper. Half day."

"If useful."

"Done."

Sori said, "Do you need records witness?"

Tavren looked at her.

"What are you?"

"Records/legal. Not applying."

"Then no."

Sori smiled.

"Excellent."

Maro asked, "Visitors allowed?"

"From rail. Don't cross yellow rope."

They left me to become employed or embarrassed.

Both educational.

Nali was woman I'd seen on articulated marine leg.

Forty maybe.

Short black curls.

Left BKA.

Her work limb had broad split foot, salt-dark fittings, leather socket covered in waxed canvas, side stay.

Basically Masta's expensive category in motion.

She walked on wet planks without drama.

Not natural gait.

Good gait.

Different.

She carried clipboard and pry bar.

Both hands.

Again.

I wanted one.

Badly.

Nali saw me looking at leg.

"Masta?"

"Yes."

"How much did she quote?"

"One silver eighteen."

Nali laughed.

"Cheap mood."

"That's cheap?"

"Mine cost more. Marine foot. Reinforced salt stay. Employer paid part after wreck."

"Recovery yard does that?"

"Sometimes. Harbor guilds too. Depends how you lost it, where you work, whether someone owes you, whether forms survive accident."

There.

Infrastructure.

"Duskport has enough demand?"

Nali looked around yard.

"You serious?"

"I'm new."

She leaned clipboard against rail.

"Port has three limbwright shops I trust, two I wouldn't let fit stool, one brace guild, shipyard clinic, sailors' mutual fund, dockers' injury pool, divers' burial fund, and a charity that mostly buys children braces and argues with adults about drinking."

"Three shops?"

"Hark & Venn does working limbs. South Arch does fancy articulated city legs and noble children. Brine Street does cheap pegs and repairs while-you-wait. Masta gets sailors, dockers, salvagers, mill hands, anyone who needs body to survive wet work."

"So not unusual."

"Losing limb? Still bad day. Getting fitted? Tuesday."

Good line.

She picked clipboard up.

"Harbor machines don't forgive sleeves, hands, feet, or timing. Sea adds infection. Before clinics got better, more people lost limbs from cuts that should've stayed cuts. Now fewer. Still enough."

"And sailors?"

"Rope crush. capstan. cargo. wrecks. boarding if they're idiots or unlucky. Storm damage. Frostbite north routes. Some come here already missing pieces because Duskport has specialists and work that will still hire them."

That last part mattered.

Not just harbor creating amputees.

Harbor attracting them.

Because adaptations existed.

Work hook.

Marine peg.

Modified ladders.

Harness jobs.

Dock unions that knew forms.

An ecosystem.

Nali tapped clipboard against my chest lightly.

"History lesson costs time. Trial started."

Right.

Work.

Yellow-rope pen held four recovered objects on draining racks.

All tagged temporary numbers.

A: iron cargo latch assembly, barnacle scars, twisted hinge.

B: brass-and-wood signal housing with cracked faceplate.

C: compact black ceramic cylinder wrapped in copper cage.

D: length of articulated chain with three pale glass inserts.

I looked at C longest.

Nali noticed.

"Don't."

"I wasn't touching."

"Good."

"What is it?"

"Your job says what it is physically, what marks are visible, what condition can be observed, what tests are authorized. Not what your imagination wants."

I liked Nali too.

Duskport problem.

Nali gave me forms.

Sections:

Recovery location.

Water depth.

Diver clearance.

Biological clearance.

Visible ownership marks.

Material.

Deformation.

Corrosion.

Charge-screen result.

Leak/heat/motion.

Handling class.

Disposition pending.

Lots of blank space.

"Do I fill recovery data?"

"Already entered from dive slate. Copy exact. Don't improve wording."

Good.

A came from old quay collapse at East Hook Three, six feet water.

B and C from same wreck pocket near **Merrin's Jaw**, twelve feet.

D snagged independently on harbor sweep line.

Nali demonstrated charge screen.

Portable forked brass frame placed near object without contact.

Three positions.

Green dull.

Amber pulse.

Red sustained.

"Green means safe?" I asked.

"No."

Good.

"Green means this screen did not detect threshold charge in this position."

"Better."

"Amber?"

"Some response. Stop and call me. Red?"

"Stop faster."

"Excellent."

A was easy.

Iron latch.

Ownership stamp partly visible under corrosion.

I copied characters I could see and bracketed uncertain segment.

Nali checked.

"Why not complete likely yard code?"

"Because likely isn't visible."

"Good."

B looked simple until faceplate shifted when rack vibrated from crane outside.

I stopped.

Nali looked.

"Why?"

"Movement not from me. Faceplate loose. Could expose interior if rack shifts again."

She nodded.

Moved rack wedge.

Authorized visual from side only.

Inside wood darkened with water.

Copper contact strips green-white at edges.

Charge screen green from front, green left, amber faint right.

I stopped.

Nali came.

Ran screen herself.

Amber again.

"Good. Mark localized residual response. No opening. Specialist later."

Not mystery.

Not my job to solve.

C black ceramic cylinder.

Copper cage.

No obvious corrosion except one dark seam.

Ownership marks none visible.

Charge screen from first position gave nothing.

Second nothing.

Third...

Fork hummed.

Not amber light.

Mechanical hum.

I froze.

Nali's hand came up immediately.

"Back."

I backed.

She looked at fork.

No light.

Hum stopped.

"Did screen touch?"

"No."

"Distance?"

"About two fingers from cage."

"Movement?"

"None observed."

"Heat?"

"Didn't touch. No visible vapor."

"Sound source?"

"Could be fork. Could be cylinder. I can't separate."

Nali looked at me.

"Good."

She put red isolation tag on rack.

Not because red screen.

Because unknown response.

Called **Torrin Glass**, charge specialist.

He arrived annoyed.

Older man with missing left eye.

Not limb.

Harbor still diversified injuries.

"What?"

Nali pointed.

"Screen hum position three, no lamp. Greg observed."

Torrin looked at me.

"Touch it?"

"No."

"Good."

He used different instrument.

Long insulated probe held near cage.

Waited.

Nothing.

Moved.

Tiny vibration.

Again.

"Resonance," he said. "Cage responding to screening fork frequency. Not stored charge. Could still have stored charge below probe threshold. Leave isolated until dry."

There.

Explanation partial.

Not secret treasure.

I wrote exact.

D chain was more annoying.

Three glass inserts, one cracked.

Ownership mark on every fourth link but two different stamps appeared.

I almost assumed repair splice.

Stopped.

Nali asked why.

"Two stamps. Could be repair, reused chain, or mixed assembly."

"Good. What do you write?"

"Both stamps, positions, no ownership conclusion."

"Good."

This work fit brain.

Not glamorous.

Useful.

By midday my forms were done.

Nali took them away without praise.

Excellent sign.

I ate dock bread and smoked fish in bits while waiting.

Maro had left rail at some point.

Sori remained because apparently watching employment interview counted entertainment.

She sat beside me.

"How bad?"

"I like it."

"That's bad."

"Very."

She looked toward Nali's leg.

"I get shop now."

"Yeah."

"Three trusted shops."

"Apparently."

"Harbor destroys people."

"And employs them after."

Sori chewed bread.

"That's darker."

"Also better."

"Both."

There.

Human problem without needing speech.

System hurts.

System adapts.

People keep working because rent exists.

Some because they want work.

Some because sea is only thing they know.

Some because body changed, not person.

Nali came over.

"Tavren wants you."

"Good wants or bad wants?"

"He always looks like bad wants."

Tavren was in shed office with my forms.

He had marked three spots in charcoal.

"A. You wrote ownership stamp partial. Why not infer full?"

"Couldn't see full."

"B. Amber right. Why no probable source?"

"Didn't know."

"D. Two ownership stamps. Why no likely repair note?"

"Could be repair. Could be reused links. I didn't inspect joins under authority."

He stared.

Then nodded once.

"Nali says same."

He put two copper on desk.

There.

Pay.

"Useful."

I picked up copper.

Twenty-one to twenty-three.

Equal Vale again.

"More work?"

Tavren leaned chair back.

"Maybe."

Of course.

"Tomorrow we have wreck lift outside breakwater. Old coastal courier **Merry Wake**. Sank eight years ago. Owner finally sold salvage rights after insurance fight. We recover two cargo-room safes, steering hardware, whatever loose fittings remain. Divers bring up. Yard records."

"Condition clerk work?"

"Partly. More crowded. Ownership tags matter because salvage contract excludes personal effects and temple cargo. Anything marked passenger cabin goes separate custody. Anything with temple seal stays underwater until temple observer arrives."

There.

Human conflict seed already.

"Pay?"

"Three copper full day if you're records only and useful. Four if you also do shore-side load tally and don't slow riggers. Five if Nali signs you for both plus evening custody inventory."

Better.

Not silver tomorrow.

Several days could matter.

Living costs matter too.

"How many days work?"

"Merry Wake maybe three if weather holds. Then East Hook quay collapse. Then whatever sea breaks next."

"I want several."

He looked at my crutches.

Then at me.

"For leg?"

Masta told him.

Or obvious.

"Yes."

"Masta prices mean you're short."

"Very."

"Good. Desperate workers lie. Don't."

There.

"I won't."

"Also don't work hungry to save faster. Nali will notice and send you home."

Masta family.

Annoying.

"Understood."

Tavren pushed form across desk.

Temporary yard hand.

Three-day trial.

No guaranteed hours beyond posted recovery.

Base 3c full useful records day.

Add-ons only assigned and signed.

Personal gear liability mine.

Yard covers assigned protective equipment.

No diving.

No live-load riding.

No unsupervised charge handling.

No salvage claim simply because I noticed value.

Excellent clause.

I signed.

Sori read before I did because she was Sori.

"Fine," she said.

Tavren looked at her.

"You his lawyer?"

"No."

"Good. Lawyers expensive."

"So are mistakes."

He considered.

"You want work?"

"No."

"Good."

We left yard with two new copper and tomorrow's start before first bell.

East Hook looked different now.

Still smelled terrible.

But I could see network.

Limbwright shop west.

Recovery yard east.

Dock clinic behind crane row.

A man with work hook buying noodles.

Nali crossing wet plank on marine foot while carrying clipboard and pry bar.

No one applauded.

No one stared.

Duskport had enough broken bodies that adaptation had become ordinary commerce.

Sad reason.

Useful result.

I thought about Carrow.

Could I have gotten fitted there eventually?

Probably some version.

Maybe imported.

Maybe general brace maker.

But here, harbor generated enough need for people to get very good at specific problem.

Wet grip.

Salt fittings.

Drainage.

Deck angle.

Rope work.

Ladders.

Sockets that had to survive sweat and rain and twelve-hour shifts.

Specialization followed repeated pain.

Civilization did that sometimes.

Turned injury into profession.

Not beautiful.

Still better than pretending injury was rare because it made people uncomfortable.

Sori nudged me.

"You're doing face again."

"Which?"

"Math."

I counted.

Twenty-three copper.

Travel limb one silver eighteen.

Tomorrow three to five if useful.

Bed one copper if Anchor's End still same.

Food bits mostly.

Vale twenty-three waiting back home.

No conversion.

No shortcut.

Just days.

"Three-day trial," I said.

"I heard."

"Maybe more after."

"I heard."

"Could get there."

Sori looked at me carefully.

"Eventually."

"Yes."

Not soon.

Good.

Soon would've been cheap.

We walked back toward city center.

Sea wind behind us.

Recovery crane ahead of memory.

Tomorrow wreck.

Tomorrow other people's property under eight years of water.

Tomorrow workers who knew things I didn't.

I had two copper more than morning.

And a job for three days if I stayed useful.

Peg still impossible today.

Less impossible than yesterday.

That was enough.
