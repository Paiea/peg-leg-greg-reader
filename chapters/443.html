CHAPTER 435

THE WAYFARER

The broken pin had failed correctly.

Good for everyone except broken pin.

Harbor chain house posted result before breakfast.

**WEST CLUTCH SHEAR PIN / WITNESS DRY**

**RATED FAILURE CONFIRMED**

**NO EARLY-CRACK INDICATION**

**ALIGNMENT INSPECTION ROUTINE / NOT EMERGENCY**

That meant yesterday had been maintenance debt and bad timing.

Not hidden structural doom.

Comforting.

Also boring.

Good.

I ate bread under harbor awning while two fishermen argued whether committee should now order four pins or six.

Neither asked me.

Excellent.

Old Merrow looked different dry.

Rain had polished everything.

Stone terraces pale.

Roof channels still dripping.

Cistern markers bright.

Green-black ridge above town steamed where sun hit wet grass.

Rain tongue stretched flat between its white arms.

Silent.

I had no reason to stay harbor.

That was new problem.

No Sori paperwork.

No Dask instrument.

No Maro bird.

Just me.

Route board stood beside bakery lane.

Yesterday I had walked past because wet stairs and stranger brain.

Today I read slower.

**RIDGE CART / FIRST & FOURTH RING / 1C**

**WEST CATCH / UPPER FARMS / QUARRY SPLIT**

**WEST BAY FOOT ROAD / DRY WEATHER ONLY**

**PACKET WEST BAY -> TERNHAVEN / TOMORROW THIRD RING / 3C IF RUNNING**

There.

Road.

Not home road.

Other side of island.

Ternhaven meant nothing to me.

Perfect.

I asked bakery woman, "West Bay how far?"

"Ridge cart to split. Then two bells down if you're walking normal."

She looked at crutches.

"Longer for you."

"Thank you for advanced mathematics."

"You're welcome."

"Inn?"

"One cheap bunk house. One not cheap."

"Packet tomorrow actually reliable?"

She laughed.

Question answered.

"What's Ternhaven?"

"Town."

"Useful."

"You asked what, not why."

Old Merrow people apparently enjoyed accuracy.

I paid bakery loft nothing further because night done.

Packed.

Trial leg still wrapped.

Lot 67 dry.

No leak singer.

No broken chain souvenir.

Good.

Fourth ring cart gave me morning to climb ridge slowly instead of taking it.

Bad idea maybe.

Then I remembered cart cost one copper.

Very good idea.

I bought seat.

Thirty-four to thirty-three.

Cart wasn't passenger coach.

Flat freight wagon with two side benches and rain tarp rolled overhead.

Cargo:

empty produce crates.

three sacks lime.

coiled irrigation hose.

five people.

Me.

No assigned seats.

Driver pointed rear left.

"Less bounce."

"Why?"

"Axle hates right side."

Important information.

I chose left.

Other riders:

woman with two baskets green beans.

young man smelling quarry dust.

old farmer with one eye and no interest in anyone.

mother carrying sleeping child.

boy maybe sixteen with rolled copper gutter strip.

Nobody cared who I was.

Excellent.

Fourth ring.

Cart climbed.

Old Merrow terraces narrowed below.

Water system became clearer from above.

Not one cistern network.

Hundreds.

Roof catch.

Lane catch.

Farm catch.

Overflow channels feeding lower rings.

Every terrace borrowed water from weather before giving remainder downhill.

Useful social structure hidden inside plumbing.

I did not write essay.

Growth.

At second switchback cart stopped because boy with copper strip jumped off.

Driver shouted, "Wrong ring!"

Boy froze.

Looked uphill.

Looked downhill.

"Fuck."

Driver laughed.

"Third, not second. Get back."

Boy climbed.

I felt better.

Stranger mistakes universal.

Upper ridge farms appeared after half bell.

Stone walls.

Low trees bent west.

Terraced beds under stretched mesh.

Not grain fields.

Herbs, beans, dark-leaf greens, something red growing close ground.

Between plots stood tall ceramic poles capped with shallow bowls.

Rain gauges maybe.

One farmer got off.

Cart continued.

At next stop woman with beans left.

Then mother.

Me, quarry man, old farmer.

Quarry split sat near ridge saddle.

Sign:

**WEST CATCH -> LEFT**

**MERROW QUARRY -> RIGHT**

**WEST BAY -> LEFT THEN DOWN**

Driver said, "West Bay?"

"Maybe."

"Then off here. Cart turns quarry."

There.

Decision.

Could go quarry.

Could go west catch.

Could go back.

No companion preference to lean against.

I got off.

Left.

West Bay.

Simple.

Driver said, "Rain channel path slippery after yesterday. Use upper farm road until marker nine, then cut down."

"Thank you."

"Don't use blue stones."

"Why?"

"Those are water crew paths."

Useful.

Cart left.

Now actually alone.

Road dry enough.

Grass wet edges.

Black stone center.

Crutches good.

Wind smelled green instead of salt.

I walked.

Slow.

No hurry.

Marker one.

Two.

Three.

Farm dogs barked behind walls.

Four.

At five, road widened beside empty stone shed.

Flat packed ground.

Dry.

No people.

Body felt good.

This was trial-leg opportunity.

Not test because chapter needed one.

Because road ahead mostly level to nine and both hands would help with pack straps.

I sat shed step.

Shell on.

Medium foot.

Crutches primary.

Started watch by sun position plus rough count.

Not Lot 67.

No false precision.

Walked.

Stable road.

Short steps.

Crutches light, not absent.

After maybe ten minutes, I stopped at wall gap and retightened pack chest strap with both hands standing.

There.

Ordinary.

Then moved.

At marker seven road had shallow cross-drain.

I did not step over unsupported.

Crutches down.

Good.

Marker eight.

Hip fatigue mild.

No socket heat.

Marker nine came around twenty-two minutes maybe.

I stopped.

Not because failure.

Because plan.

Shell off behind low wall.

Upper pink mild.

Lower front clear.

No pain.

At twenty-minute skin check pink gone.

Note:

**Old Merrow ridge farm road. ~22 min. Packed dry stone. Crutches light/primary. Two-handed pack adjustment standing. Cross-drain with crutches. Mild hip fatigue. Upper pink cleared. No lower-front mark.**

Good.

Baseline moving.

Not miracle.

I ate hard bread.

Then did something I had not done in too long.

Magic.

Not because artifact.

Not because crisis.

Because quiet road and dry wall gave clean conditions.

I picked a dead bean leaf from roadside.

Light.

Dry.

Placed it on flat stone one pace away.

Wind minimal behind wall.

I waited.

Old instinct wanted complicated.

No.

External effect.

Small.

Known target.

No timing claims.

No Lot 67.

I set crutches stable.

Breathed.

Reached with sense I had been rebuilding badly and slowly for months.

Not grabbing.

Pressure.

Direction.

Leaf edge lifted.

Could be air.

Stopped.

Waited.

Second attempt would contaminate first if I started wanting result.

I moved leaf to different stone.

Checked wind with finger.

Almost none.

Tried again.

Leaf slid maybe half finger.

Stopped.

There.

Small enough to insult first-life me.

Useful enough current me smiled.

External attempt one hundred thirty-six.

Not breakthrough.

Not rank.

Not proof of range increase.

One light dry leaf.

Sheltered wall.

Maybe half finger.

I wrote exactly that.

**External 136. Dead dry bean leaf, sheltered wall, ~1 pace. Minimal wind by hand check. Visible slide ~half finger after directed pressure. No timing measurement. Do not generalize.**

Then did not repeat.

Important.

Training brain wanted ten.

Road brain wanted west.

Road won.

Good.

I packed leaf nowhere.

Not artifact.

Just leaf.

Path down west side changed fast.

East side green terraces.

West side darker.

Rock exposed.

Water channels cut deep beside road.

At first overlook I saw West Bay.

Tiny harbor.

Maybe thirty roofs.

One pier.

No tall beacon.

Beyond, sea open and gray.

Far horizon had low island shape.

Ternhaven maybe.

Or something else.

Closer, west catch system occupied whole slope.

Not cisterns.

Huge stepped stone basins.

Rainwater collected in broad terraces, settled mud, then spilled cleaner water down through reed beds.

Industrial watershed.

Beautiful in practical way.

And damaged.

One upper basin brown.

Workers clustered around breach in side wall.

Water running where shouldn't.

Yesterday rain consequence.

I stayed road.

Nobody hired me.

Good.

Then woman on road ahead waved.

At me? Maybe.

I looked behind.

Nobody.

Me.

She carried red staff and wore yellow water-crew sash.

"West Bay?"

"Yes."

"Main path closed below basin six. Mud slide."

"Alternative?"

"Farm contour path. Longer."

"How much?"

She looked at crutches.

"For you? Maybe bell and half."

Fuck.

"Any cart?"

"Crew cart after sixth ring if space. Two bits to bay."

There.

Choice.

Walk longer.

Pay bits.

Wait.

"When sixth?"

"About half bell."

Easy.

"I'll wait."

She nodded.

No bravery points for exhausting myself downhill after trial wear.

Good.

I sat on dry wall outside crew lane.

Workers moved mud baskets.

One basin spill gate opened.

Brown water diverted into reed trench.

Nobody explained.

I watched enough to infer some.

Not enough to pretend.

Crew cart came late.

Of course.

Bench mostly tools.

Driver shifted two shovels.

"Two bits."

I paid.

Copper unchanged.

Ride down rough.

Very glad not walking.

West Bay reached near evening.

Smaller than sign implied.

Or sign implied nothing.

One packet office.

One fish shed.

One long bunk house.

Rain barrels everywhere.

Route board:

**TERNHAVEN / THIRD RING / 3C / WEATHER CHECK SECOND**

**SOUTH RETURN OLD MERROW / CART FIRST RING / 1C**

**NORTH SKIFF / KELP ISLES / CARGO ONLY**

Interesting.

Cargo only.

Not today.

Thirty-three to thirty-two.

Dinner bits.

One old fisherman.

One me.

One empty.

No familiar voices.

Good.

Water worker across room asked, "You from Duskport?"

I looked.

"Eventually."

"Crutches look harbor-made."

"Carrow first. Duskport recently."

"Long way."

"Getting longer."

He nodded like that was answer.

Didn't ask why.

I liked him.

Later rain started light on roof.

Not enough for tongue maybe.

West Bay had smaller warning instrument outside bunk house.

Three hanging reed plates.

Wind made one click.

Then two.

Different town.

Same weather.

I counted money.

Thirty-two copper.

Twenty-three Vale.

Road eating capital.

Leg still expensive.

But today I had crossed island by cart, leg, crutches, water crew wagon, and stupidity avoidance.

Also moved a leaf half a finger.

Maybe.

Enough.

Tomorrow Ternhaven if weather allowed.

Three copper.

Another island or mainland? Didn't know.

I should ask.

Then didn't.

Could ask morning.

Wayfarer.

Not because I knew way.

Because I kept taking one.
