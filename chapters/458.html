CHAPTER 357

THE WAYFINDER

Lakeward ferry posted green at first ring.

That was inconvenient.

I had spent last night hoping weather would make decision for me.

Very mature.

South Larken board:

**LAKEWARD EAST / 4C / CLEAR WINDOW / LOAD CALL FIRST-AND-HALF**

Cash fourteen.

Ten-copper floor.

Four copper fare.

Which would leave exactly ten.

Then lodging would require violating my own rule before I had even found bed.

No.

Reedmark north remained three copper.

Tollen two.

Crown Vale was now west behind ridge.

All sensible.

Lakeward still pulled hardest.

Glass works.

Orchard road.

East country.

Different.

I stared at board until clerk said, "You buying or courting it?"

"Financially incompatible relationship."

"Then move."

Fair.

One small job had appeared below route board.

**LANDING THREE / DEPARTURE TOKEN CHECK / 1C / ONE BELL / SEATED**

Not enough to be interesting.

Exactly enough to be useful.

I took it.

No heroic labor.

No half-day delay.

One bell at a little table beside boarding lane.

Job was stupidly simple.

Passenger handed token.

I matched stamped route notch to clerk sheet.

Green basket Lakeward.

Blue Reedmark.

Yellow Tollen.

Wrong basket went back to clerk.

No fare disputes.

No identity judgments.

No upgrades.

Just stop people getting onto wrong boats before wrong boat became expensive geography.

I caught three.

Woman with Tollen token in Lakeward line.

Man with yesterday's void Reedmark chit.

One child carrying mother's token while mother held child's.

That one technically would have solved itself.

Still counted.

One copper.

Fourteen to fifteen.

Now Lakeward fare left eleven.

One-copper floor bed would leave ten.

Thin.

But not fake.

I bought east crossing.

Fifteen to eleven.

No joy.

A ferry ticket should feel like possibility.

Mine felt like subtraction.

Good.

Trial leg stayed wrapped.

It had rested yesterday, but wet deck, cross-lake fog possibility, and unknown landing surface made crutches correct.

I boarded **Glass Heron** through lower ramp.

Broader than lower-lake boats.

Two shallow hulls joined under a wide deck? No. Not twin hulls. One broad hull with side outriggers? Keep simple.

Broad flat-bellied hull, low cabin, high reed-slat wind screens.

At bow, two bottom-change plates hung folded upward for open water.

Fog bowl mounted aft under hood.

Pilot **Rian Kett**.

Deck crew three.

Passengers maybe twenty.

Freight mostly orchard crates, glass cullet sacks, lamp oil, seed baskets.

I found bench against wind screen.

No one knew me.

Still nice.

Then somebody said, "Peg-Leg Greg?"

Damn it.

I looked.

Teenage boy with apple crate.

"No."

He grinned.

"My aunt came through High Shelf two days ago. Said there was tall one-legged man with trial limb heading lake road."

"That's circumstantial."

"She said he talks annoying."

"Could be anybody."

He laughed.

Nickname had beaten mail somehow.

Fine.

"I don't even have peg."

"Yet."

Kett Arlo's disease spreading by trade routes.

Boy's name **Perrin Sol**.

Orchard apprentice from east side.

He was returning with rootstock.

"Lakeward?" I asked.

"Then east road."

"How far?"

"Home? Half day cart if road dry."

"Where?"

"Morrow Orchard."

New place.

"Big?"

"Enough."

Good answer.

Glass Heron left South Landing.

Shore fog was lifting, but lake still held gray bands farther east.

Open water changed sound.

Sea ferries had constant salt chop and long swell memory.

Larken had shorter motion.

Wind-made.

Patchy.

Surface dark in one direction, silver in another.

Caro's line returned.

Lake wind lies.

Pilot Rian watched water like it was handwriting.

Bottom plates stayed up.

Fog bowl aft dark.

No need.

For first half bell nobody talked much.

Then older woman across aisle started telling story to two children.

Not performance.

Something they had clearly heard before and wanted again.

"Tell the cup one."

"You know cup one."

"Tell it."

She sighed like storyteller defeated by market demand.

"Fine."

I listened because sitting did not require moral justification.

Story was called **The Woman Who Returned the Water**.

Not ancient-god proclamation.

A woman named **Iria** lived in orchard country during a dry year.

Spring above orchards thinned.

People fought over channels.

Upper farms closed gates.

Lower farms accused them of theft.

Village measurers argued whether spring itself was failing or water was being held.

Iria's grandmother told her there was older water path before the cut road.

Not road exactly.

Sequence of places.

Lakeward shore.

White Reed.

Sleeping Stone.

Three Sisters Hollow.

Mirror Spring.

Iria carried a plain cup of lake water uphill.

At each place she poured some out.

Not because lake water magically fed spring.

Because the path marked where water changed.

At White Reed, soil stayed wet though channel was dry.

At Sleeping Stone, moss lived on downhill face.

At Three Sisters, one hollow still held frogs.

At Mirror Spring, she found spring mouth blocked by fallen root and stone after storm.

She cleared enough for water to move.

Then carried a black spring pebble back down and placed it in lake.

Children asked why.

Old woman said, "Because story says water remembers road both ways."

One child said, "Rocks don't remember."

"Then remember for rock."

Good.

That line landed.

I expected ending.

Didn't get one.

Woman continued.

On way home Iria met three farmers arguing at lower gate.

They wanted to know who caused dry channels.

She told them storm blocked spring.

Upper farmer said that proved he had not stolen.

Lower farmer said upper still closed too much gate.

Third said everybody would lie again next drought.

So they set marker stones at channels and agreed each family had to walk spring path once before accusing another.

Children hated administrative ending.

"That's boring."

"That's why you still have apples."

Excellent.

Perrin beside me had gone quiet.

"Your orchard?" I asked softly.

He looked.

"What?"

"Morrow. Is that in story country?"

His expression changed.

Not dramatic.

More careful.

"Yeah."

"Mirror Spring real?"

"Depends which old person you ask."

"That's not answer."

"There's spring called Mirror. Path isn't used now. Cut road goes around north shoulder."

I looked back toward storyteller.

She was correcting child pronunciation of Three Sisters place-name.

"Still water problem?"

Perrin stared at crate.

"Not like story."

There.

Somebody wanted something badly maybe.

"What's like story?"

"Nothing."

Then, after a while:

"East channels are low."

"Drought?"

"Maybe."

"Upper gates?"

"Maybe."

"Spring?"

"Maybe."

"You have excellent evidence."

"I know."

He sounded irritated.

Not at me exactly.

Good.

"Why were you west buying rootstock if channels low?"

"Because my mother already paid."

Human answer.

"And because if water comes back, we need it."

"If?"

He rubbed crate edge.

"District reader says wait three days. Water office wants another channel measure. Upper orchards say lake fog changed recharge. Lower orchards say upper gates are cheating. My grandfather says somebody needs to walk old path."

"And what do you say?"

"I say grandfather can't walk it."

There.

Quest arrived quietly.

Not mine.

Somebody else's.

"You?"

"I have work."

"That's convincing."

He glared.

"You don't know my family."

"Correct."

I shut up.

Good.

Glass Heron crossed gray band.

Fog bowl aft lit pale.

Crew did not panic.

Pilot adjusted east by maybe small angle.

A deckhand called marker number.

Bottom-change plates lowered as shore shelf approached.

Water under us darkened green over weed beds.

One plate gave faint line.

Pilot shifted north.

Layered imperfect evidence.

Worked.

Lakeward emerged through fog in pieces.

First glass towers.

Not towers like castle.

Tall kiln chimneys with pale exhaust.

Then shoreline cranes.

Then long low buildings with roofs of blue-gray tile.

Orchard carts everywhere.

Apples, pears, long yellow fruit I didn't know.

Freshwater reeds bundled by docks.

East side smelled like wet wood and hot glass.

No salt.

Still strange.

We docked.

Perrin lifted rootstock crate.

Too heavy for him alone.

Deckhand helped.

I did not volunteer with crutches on wet ramp.

Growth.

At landing square, story woman disappeared into market.

Perrin waited near road board.

I looked too.

**ORCHARD ROAD / MORROW / 2C CART / SECOND BELL**

**GLASS ROAD / EAST WORKS / 1C CART**

**NORTH SHORE / REEDMARK / 3C FERRY**

**LOWER LAKE / TOLLEN / 2C**

Morrow Orchard.

Two copper.

I had eleven.

Could not pay and preserve floor.

Not without work.

Again.

But now there was reason beyond novelty.

Not obligation yet.

Reason.

Perrin saw me read Morrow line.

"Don't."

"Don't what?"

"Make face like you're thinking."

"My face does that."

"Grandfather will hire anybody who listens to story."

"That's poor hiring practice."

"Exactly."

I almost laughed.

"Hire for what?"

"Walk."

"Old path?"

"He wants somebody to go from White Reed to Mirror Spring and mark wet signs before district crew finishes arguing."

"Why not you?"

He got angry this time.

"Because I have two younger sisters, mother, six rows already stressed, and I am apprentice under east graft house. If I disappear three days because grandfather wants story walk, we lose more than water."

There.

No clean answer.

"How long is path?"

"Nobody knows now."

"That's bad."

"Yes."

"Passable?"

"Grandfather says yes."

"That is not evidence."

"I know."

He was learning my language fast.

"District maps?"

"Old path crosses landslide cut beyond Three Sisters. New road goes around."

"So grandfather wants stranger with one leg to inspect abandoned footpath."

Perrin looked at crutches.

"I said hiring practice bad."

Fair.

I should walk away.

Actually I should crutch away.

Cash eleven.

Ten floor.

No room.

Unknown path.

Unknown lodging.

Unknown work.

Borrowed trial leg.

No reason to inherit orchard family's problem.

Then Perrin said:

"He pays eight copper if somebody reaches Mirror Spring and comes back with route notes. Twelve if they also bring sealed spring sample to district office."

Oh.

Real money.

Not miraculous.

Several days risk for eight to twelve.

Could matter.

Could fund road.

Could maybe feed leg money indirectly.

Could also cost more than paid.

"Why twelve for sample?"

"District office wants it. Grandfather says if we're walking anyway, bring sample."

"Who provides bottle?"

"District."

"Authority?"

"Sample only. No gate authority."

Good.

"Why hasn't district sent somebody?"

"They will. Eventually."

"How eventually?"

"Three days maybe. Five if reader team stays north channels."

"And orchard?"

"Lower rows start losing fruit if channels stay this low another two or three days."

There.

Clock.

Not world ending.

Fruit.

Work.

Family.

Money.

Old story path.

Modern measurement delay.

Somebody needed something.

I hated how well it fit.

"Grandfather name?"

"Teren Sol."

"Morrow Orchard?"

"Yes."

"Does he know you are recruiting one-legged strangers at ferry docks?"

"No."

"Excellent."

"He will like you."

"That worries me."

I still did not agree.

Important.

First lodging.

Lakeward cheap board showed:

**GLASS YARD FLOOR / 1C / WORKERS + TRAVELERS**

I paid.

Eleven to ten.

Exactly floor.

No discretionary copper.

There.

Adventure officially required income.

I put pack at Glass Yard floor.

Dry stable stone.

Trial limb had rested all day? No wear today. Could use later, but not necessary. Keep rest.

Then I went to district water office.

Not because quest accepted.

Because check claims before becoming idiot.

Office clerk **Aven Rusk** found Morrow notice after some searching.

"Low east channels."

"How low?"

"Enough for watch, not emergency."

"Mirror Spring sample requested?"

"Requested if field team reaches source."

"Can private person bring one?"

"Sealed district bottle, witness mark at fill, accepted as supplemental. Not authoritative source reading by itself."

Good.

"Old path?"

Aven made face.

"Story path."

"Real?"

"Parts."

"Passable?"

"Depends what you call path."

"Accessible with crutches?"

She looked at me properly.

"I would not promise that."

Finally.

"Landslide?"

"Three Sisters north shoulder. New road bypasses. Old footpath may cross lower bench. We do not maintain it."

"Any map?"

"Public archive sketch. No current condition."

She pulled thin sheet.

White Reed.

Sleeping Stone.

Three Sisters Hollow.

Mirror Spring.

Same sequence.

Story was map memory.

Not exact map.

But map.

Hawaiian-story nudge in world's own grammar: story holds geography, practical observation, social rule.

I copied route names.

"Bottle?"

"We don't issue unless someone signs supplemental sample chain."

"Can I see form?"

She handed.

Simple enough.

Collector name.

Route.

Fill point.

Witness if available.

Seal number.

Observed conditions.

No claim of source flow rate.

No claim of cause.

Good.

"Fee?"

"Deposit one copper on bottle. Refunded when returned intact, sample or empty."

I had zero discretionary.

Of course.

"Can orchard post deposit?"

"Yes."

Interesting.

I returned form.

"Not signing yet."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"Because people sign before asking where Mirror Spring is."

"I asked."

"That's why good."

I left.

Perrin was waiting by market fountain.

"You went office."

"I enjoy ruining stories with paperwork."

"Grandfather is at Morrow tomorrow morning."

"I have no money for cart."

"He'll pay cart if he wants you."

"That sounds suspiciously like free fare."

"It's employment travel."

Damn technically legitimate economics.

"I haven't accepted."

"I know."

He stopped pushing.

Good.

"Can he come here?"

"He won't leave rows."

Human problem again.

"Then I either spend work time going to him or don't."

"Yes."

"Any work here tomorrow early?"

"Glass yard always has something."

Not guarantee.

I went work board.

**GLASS RETURN CRATE PAD / 2C / FIRST RING / SEATED OK**

There.

Not exciting.

But enough to restore floor margin before deciding.

I marked it mentally.

No claim yet.

At Glass Yard floor, kiln glow painted ceiling orange through high windows.

I opened notebook.

Wrote:

**Potential actual job: Morrow Orchard, Teren Sol. Old story route White Reed -> Sleeping Stone -> Three Sisters -> Mirror Spring. 8c route notes; 12c with sealed supplemental spring sample. District confirms low east channels + sample accepted as supplemental only. Old path unmaintained; current accessibility unknown; landslide near Three Sisters. Lower rows may lose fruit in 2-3 days.**

Then:

**This is somebody else's problem.**

I stared.

Added:

**Which is usually how adventure starts.**

Annoying.

No quest accepted.

Not yet.

But for first time since leaving Carrow, tomorrow had something more specific than next town.

A person.

A place.

A path.

A reason to reach end and come back.

That felt different.

Wayfinder.

Not because I knew way.

Because story did.

Maybe.
