CHAPTER 364

THE TRAILBLAZER

The bottle arrived before breakfast.

Adventure had paperwork.

Good.

The district runner set a padded wooden case on Morrow packing-shed table and made me read the seal form back to her.

Collector.

Route.

Fill point.

Observed conditions.

Witness if available.

Seal number before fill.

Seal number after fill.

Do not rinse bottle in source.

Do not touch inside neck or stopper.

Do not fill from standing pool if flowing source can be reached safely.

Do not alter source to obtain sample.

That last one mattered.

I signed receipt.

The Morrow cooperative had posted the one-copper bottle deposit already.

No money moved from me.

Cash twelve.

Vale twenty-three.

Protocol fifty-nine, fifty-three, one-thirty-eight.

Still boring.

Excellent.

The runner left.

Garrick Sol inspected the case like he expected bureaucracy to have hidden a second bottle inside out of spite.

"That's all?"

"Bottle, seal, form."

"They used to send a reader."

"They still are. Later."

"Later is useful word when your trees aren't thirsty."

Helena Sol was tying my food roll at the other end of table.

"Do not start before he leaves."

Garrick looked at her.

"I said one sentence."

"It was the first stair."

Jonah Sol came in carrying two water flasks.

That name was new.

Better.

I had spent part of last night looking at the names in my own notes and realizing the road had developed a disease.

Perrin.

Sera.

Lio.

Rusk.

Names I had already heard elsewhere, or names close enough to old ones that my notebook was starting to look like one family had colonized the continent.

So I fixed the notes before they hardened.

Jonah Sol.

Garrick Sol.

Helena Sol.

Ada and June.

Celia Brohm at Lakeward water office.

People deserved names that did not sound like I had drawn them from the same small sack of syllables.

Jonah put one flask beside my pack.

"Mine. You give it back at White Reed."

"Planning to."

"Grandfather says keeper has water."

"Grandfather says many things."

From table, Garrick said, "I can hear you."

"Good."

Helena smiled into knot.

We left after first ring.

Jonah carried his own small day bag.

I carried contract gear.

Crutches primary.

Trial limb wrapped against side of pack.

I had considered wearing it on maintained farm track.

Then looked at morning fog, damp stone, and several hours of unknown effort ahead.

No.

Save it.

The first stretch out of Morrow barely felt like quest.

Orchard rows.

Gate boxes.

Workers.

Cart ruts.

Low walls.

People called to Jonah by name.

Nobody called to me.

Good.

Water channel followed road uphill.

At Morrow it had been thin.

Above first terrace it was wider.

Not dramatically.

Enough to notice.

"Upper gate?" I asked.

Jonah pointed ahead.

"Shared split. Morrow branch takes left. Halden road takes right."

"Do we cross it?"

"Yes."

At split, wooden gates stood in stone throat.

Both open partially.

A chalk board under little roof showed morning marks.

I copied them.

Did not interpret.

Jonah watched.

"You could just say there's more water here."

"There is more water here."

"See?"

"That does not tell us why less reaches Morrow."

"I know."

"Then why are we fighting?"

"Because you make every sentence wear boots."

I liked that.

"Useful on trail."

"Terrible at dinner."

We kept climbing.

Maintained road narrowed after last packing yard.

Orchards broke into patches.

Stone walls older.

Some trees abandoned or feral.

Fruit small and hard.

Fog burned away slowly.

East wind had not started.

At a bend Jonah stopped.

"White Reed from here."

I saw pale stripe below us.

Not white exactly.

Silver-yellow reeds packed along shallow basin where two channels met.

A squat keeper house sat above ford.

Beyond it, road continued east as cart track.

Old path did not.

Old path climbed north beside reed basin and vanished into trees.

There.

Line between transport and adventure.

Of course there was a house at it.

Civilization liked putting paperwork at thresholds.

Keeper was **Mabel Crowe**.

Sixties maybe.

Short gray braid.

Mud boots.

One sleeve rolled higher than other because right forearm was wrapped in old burn scar.

She looked at Jonah.

"Garrick finally bullied somebody into walking it?"

"Contracted," I said.

She looked at me.

Then crutches.

Then pack.

"Worse."

Jonah said, "Morning, Mabel."

"You going too?"

"Only here."

"Good. Your mother has sense."

"That isn't why."

"Still good."

Different voice.

No dry-comeback academy. She spoke like conclusions had been waiting before we arrived.

I showed contract and district bottle receipt.

Mabel read slowly.

Twice.

"They put Three Sisters failure pay in writing."

"I insisted."

"Smart. Garrick would've paid you in regret."

Jonah said, "He's my grandfather."

"Then you know."

Maybe one dry line. Fine.

Mabel handed papers back.

"Ford first. Then you can decide whether old path deserves your remaining leg."

"Missing one already."

She stared.

"Yes. I counted."

Fair.

White Reed ford was not ankle deep.

Not knee either.

Mid-shin on Jonah.

Current modest but visible.

Bottom rounded stone with green film.

Bad crutch surface.

Old path crossed directly through it.

New keeper footbridge stood thirty paces downstream.

Narrow timber.

Rail one side.

"Why would I ford?" I asked.

Mabel pointed at route copy.

"Because old path did."

"Why would I care?"

Jonah laughed.

Mabel did not.

Then she smiled slightly.

"Good. Use bridge."

There.

Story route was not commandment.

The place mattered.

The exact historical foot placement did not.

I inspected bridge.

Dry enough.

Boards intact.

One-sided rail meant crutches still workable.

No trial limb.

Crossed slowly.

Fine.

On far side I went back toward ford bank without entering water.

White Reed soil.

Story said wet even when channel dry.

Current channel was not dry.

So story condition did not reproduce.

Still, something interesting.

Reed basin held water higher than incoming visible side channel suggested.

Could be groundwater.

Could be backwater geometry.

Could be seep.

Could be me not understanding basin.

Mabel crouched and pushed thin marked rod into mud near reed edge.

"You measuring?"

"Every morning."

"What?"

"Mud-water line in three holes. Not official. Mine."

"Why?"

She gave me look like question silly.

"Because reeds tell you after they're unhappy. Holes tell me before."

Good.

"Trend?"

"Lower than last week. Higher than channel makes people think."

"Meaning?"

"Meaning exactly that. Don't make me your clerk."

I wrote:

**White Reed: visible channel low relative to keeper expectation; shallow basin substrate remains wet. Keeper Mabel Crowe tracks three informal mud-water holes daily; says lower than last week, still higher than channel appearance suggests. No causal claim.**

Jonah read over shoulder.

"Boots."

"Excellent boots."

Mabel said, "Story people always liked this place because water hides here."

"Hides?"

"Not magic. Ground's full of old gravel under mud. Water moves sideways. My father called it hiding because he was better with children than geology."

That was exactly what I wanted.

Story language preserving observation without being technical report.

"Does White Reed feed Morrow?"

"Some years. Some ways. Ask district if you want percentages they don't have."

"Does Mirror Spring feed this basin?"

"Partly. Probably. There are side seeps. Rain shelf. Fog drip. Old stone channels nobody maps because half collapsed before my mother was born."

Messier.

Better.

"So strong Mirror sample doesn't prove strong Morrow supply."

"Of course not."

Jonah sighed.

"Can anybody in this district answer one thing with one thing?"

Mabel looked at him.

"Water doesn't."

That landed.

We ate early bread outside keeper house.

Jonah had to turn back soon if he wanted graft house by second ring plus lateness margin.

He kept looking at old path.

"I remember Sleeping Stone," he said.

"You were twelve."

"I know."

"You don't have to prove you remember."

"I know."

He sounded like he did not know emotionally.

Mabel cleaned mud rod.

"Your grandfather took you once."

"Twice."

"Second time you cried because thorn beetle got in your shirt."

"I was twelve."

"You said that already."

Jonah looked at me.

"I could take you farther."

There.

Temptation.

Local memory.

Extra body.

Company.

And his actual life waiting downhill.

"No."

"Sleeping Stone is only maybe two bells."

"Maybe."

"I know first climb."

"You have work."

"Grandfather said he'd cover half."

"If White Reed runs long. Not if you convert yourself into expedition staff."

"That's not your decision."

True.

I stopped.

"No. It's yours."

He looked surprised.

Good correction.

"My contract does not require you. Your family needs you. Your placement matters. If you decide to come anyway, I'm not going to pretend I own you. But I am not asking."

He stared up path.

Long time.

"Mom will be furious."

"Relevant."

"Grandfather will be pleased."

"Suspiciously relevant."

"Graft master will dock me."

"Very relevant."

He kicked pale reed stalk.

Mabel said, "Don't kick my reeds because adulthood is disappointing."

Jonah stopped.

Then laughed despite himself.

"I'm going back."

"Good," Mabel said.

He frowned at her.

"You could let me feel noble for one second."

"No."

He gave me his route memory anyway.

After White Reed, stay uphill of old stone channel until split oak.

At split oak, left path goes abandoned charcoal cut.

Right follows dark slab ridge.

Sleeping Stone visible only after last turn.

There was once a marker pile at split.

Maybe gone.

"If you hit two burned stumps, wrong way," Jonah said.

"How old are those instructions?"

"Nine years."

"Wonderful."

"The stumps were already old."

"Improving rapidly."

Mabel added current information.

"Split oak still standing last spring. Charcoal cut washed deeper. Don't take it. I haven't walked beyond first rise this year."

Current enough.

I copied separately:

**Jonah memory, age 12 / nine years old: uphill of stone channel; split oak; right toward slab ridge; old marker pile uncertain; burned stumps indicate charcoal cut/wrong route.**

**Mabel current-ish: split oak standing last spring; charcoal cut more washed; first rise only this year; no condition knowledge beyond.**

Different evidence ages.

Good.

Jonah handed me his flask.

"Keep it."

"You said return at White Reed."

"Mabel has water."

"You need one going back."

"I can fill here."

Mabel said, "He can. Take flask."

Useful ordinary loan.

Not magical gear.

"I'll return it."

Jonah shrugged.

"If you don't, Grandfather will add it to contract somehow."

Then he left.

Actually left.

Downhill.

No dramatic turn at path.

No last-minute party formation.

Within five minutes orchard trees swallowed him.

Mabel watched me watching.

"You nervous?"

"Yes."

"Good."

"Everybody here likes that answer."

"People who say no before old road are either lying or young."

"I have been both."

She pointed at pack.

"Too heavy?"

"Manageable."

"That's not answer."

Apparently disease spread.

I adjusted shoulder strap.

"Heavy enough uphill will matter. Not heavy enough to leave required gear."

"Better."

I checked crutch tips.

Wrap.

Straps.

Water.

Bottle case secure high in pack.

Trial limb wrapped.

Weather.

Fog mostly lifted.

East wind beginning in reed tops.

Dry forecast still.

Old path entrance was narrow dirt line above basin.

First twenty paces easy.

Then roots.

Then slope.

I stopped after maybe fifty.

Looked back.

Mabel keeper house below.

White Reed silver around water.

Maintained bridge.

Farm road.

Orchards beyond.

Could still turn around and lose nothing but dignity, which had never been expensive.

I continued.

The first rise was harder than map implied.

Maps rarely included shoulders.

Crutches bit dirt well where dry.

Roots created step problems.

I used old technique.

Not magic.

Not S-class anything.

Three points.

Set.

Move.

Breathe before needed.

Don't wait for fatigue to announce itself.

Rest on schedule rather than pride.

Pack changed balance backward.

I shortened stride.

At one steep patch I sat and scooted three body lengths rather than pretend standing method superior.

Adventure dignity remained cheap.

At top of first rise, Mabel was no longer visible.

Only reed basin through branches.

Isolation arrived quietly.

No music.

No monster.

Just nobody close enough to hear normal voice.

I sat on flat stone and drank.

Heart working.

Arms warm.

Right shoulder fine.

Left residual limb fine because no socket.

Crutch palms beginning pressure but normal.

Good.

Old stone channel appeared beside path.

Half buried.

Not carrying surface water.

Moss inside.

Damp at bottom.

Story path and water path overlapping again.

I did not assume connection to Mirror.

I wrote.

Then heard stone click uphill.

One click.

Pause.

Another.

Not falling continuously.

Movement.

I froze.

Old me came closer.

Not locked-in fully.

Just attention changing shape.

Wind east.

Leaves moving right-to-left.

Click came against wind side.

Something stepping.

Could be person.

Could be animal.

I moved off center of path to wider patch beside channel wall.

Back protected by stone enough.

Crutches planted.

No stupid weapon improvisation.

Wait.

A long-bodied animal came through brush uphill.

Not monster.

Deer-sized maybe smaller.

Six legs.

Gray-brown hide.

Flat antler-like plates sweeping backward from skull.

It stopped when it saw me.

I stopped harder.

We regarded each other.

I knew broad category from first life.

Shelf grazer.

Not exact local species.

Usually flight animal.

Usually mattered.

Its left rear leg was caught in trailing vine? No. Thin old wire.

Ah.

Old route infrastructure again.

Wire loop dragged from leg into brush.

Animal shifted.

Wire tightened.

It jerked.

Bad.

I was not touching that.

Could maybe cut wire if animal restrained.

Animal was not restrained in useful sense.

It had six legs and several opinions.

I backed one crutch-length.

Slow.

Grazer lowered head.

Plates forward enough to make geometry obvious.

Not attack yet.

Pressure.

I gave path.

More space downhill.

Animal pulled uphill.

Wire caught branch.

It panicked.

Now action.

It lunged sideways, not at me exactly, but path was narrow and I occupied part of available world.

Old me calculated before current body finished objecting.

Do not retreat straight downhill on crutches.

Drop center.

I sat hard behind channel wall.

Not graceful.

Grazer crossed where chest had been, plates clipping leaves.

Wire snapped branch.

Animal stumbled, recovered on too many legs, dragged loose wire uphill, disappeared.

Silence.

My heart upgraded contract price without authorization.

I stayed seated.

Count.

Hands.

Elbows.

Hip.

Residual limb.

Pack.

Bottle.

All fine.

One crutch had fallen across path.

Retrieved from seated position.

No chase.

No rescue attempt.

No magic.

I inspected where wire had caught.

Old copper line, green-black with age, partly buried along channel wall.

Maybe old signal? Water marker? Fence? Unknown.

Animal had pulled several lengths free.

I did not follow.

I marked hazard:

**First rise above White Reed: old thin wire exposed along path/channel edge, partly buried, now pulled loose by local six-legged shelf grazer. Entanglement hazard. Animal departed uphill with some wire trailing. Do not assume wire function.**

Useful route note.

Also:

**Path narrow enough animal passage required yielding. Wider refuge at channel-wall break approx. first rise.**

That was adventure.

Not glorious.

I preferred glorious in retrospect anyway.

I waited another few minutes.

Then stood carefully.

The path continued uphill toward split oak.

Sleeping Stone somewhere beyond.

White Reed no longer visible.

Jonah already headed back to work.

Mabel below keeping water holes.

Garrick waiting for evidence.

Helena waiting for me not to be stupid.

Twelve copper waiting at theoretical successful end.

And somewhere above, water had gone somewhere.

I adjusted pack.

Set crutches.

Continued.

Trailblazer was generous title.

The trail already existed.

Mostly.

My job was to find out how much of it still did.
