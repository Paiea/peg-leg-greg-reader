CHAPTER 391

THE SURVIVOR

The split oak was dead.

That was inconvenient mostly because Jonah had described it as difficult to miss.

Alive trees had leaves.

Dead trees had opinions.

This one had both trunks split wide over the old channel, one half gray and bare, the other carrying three stubborn branches of yellowing leaves.

Good enough.

I stopped under it and checked route copy.

White Reed behind.

Sleeping Stone ahead.

Charcoal cut left after split oak.

Washed out.

Avoid.

Old water wall should stay on right until path climbs away.

I looked.

One branch left dropped into darker trees.

One continued uphill beside broken channel stone.

Right wall.

Take right.

Simple.

I hated simple.

The old path had narrowed enough that crutch placement became constant work.

Root.

Stone.

Dry dirt.

Another stone.

Left crutch tip on flat.

Right foot through.

Pack settle.

Repeat.

No trial leg.

Wrong terrain.

My shoulders had started complaining before my lungs did.

Current body knew the route was uphill even if old brain wanted to call it moderate.

I took a drink from Jonah's flask.

Small.

Water supply mattered now.

The old channel beside trail kept changing character.

At White Reed it had been damp and mossed.

Here it was mostly dry.

Then fifty paces later, wet again.

Not flowing.

Just black stone with tiny beads of moisture in cracks.

Story said Sleeping Stone had moss on downhill face.

That didn't mean anything yet.

Everything shaded had moss.

I wrote:

**Above split oak. Old channel intermittently damp. No continuous surface flow. No claim source connection.**

Then kept climbing.

The charcoal branch appeared exactly where Mabel said.

Almost.

The problem was somebody had stacked three small stones at its mouth.

Not old marker stones.

Too clean.

Recent enough edges hadn't weathered.

Human trace.

I stopped.

No name needed.

No person visible.

Charcoal branch descended left through washed dirt.

Fresh boot prints?

Maybe.

Two partial depressions.

Could be worker.

Could be Jonah-sized memory invented by wish.

Don't overclaim.

I marked:

**Three-stone stack at charcoal branch mouth. Appears deliberate/recent relative to surrounding weathering. Two possible tread impressions, uncertain.**

Could mean someone had marked danger.

Could mean lunch.

Could mean absolutely nothing.

I stayed right.

Good.

Path climbed around shoulder.

Wind changed.

East wind.

Later than weather board predicted? Maybe.

Not useful.

Cooler.

Leaves turned silver underside.

I could hear water now.

That stopped me.

Not channel beside me.

Farther uphill.

Thin rush.

Intermittent.

Then gone when wind moved.

Could be stream.

Could be trees.

Could be old brain wanting quest soundtrack.

I listened another half minute.

Nothing.

Moved.

The trail leveled briefly onto narrow shelf.

Dry.

Flat enough I considered trial limb.

Then looked ahead.

Shelf lasted maybe forty paces before dropping into roots and broken stone again.

Not worth fitting.

Crutches stayed.

At far end, route vanished.

Not metaphorically.

The old channel wall continued.

Path did not.

A wedge of earth and brush had slid down from uphill sometime recently enough that roots still showed pale where torn.

Not huge.

Maybe twenty feet across.

Enough to erase trail.

There was room below channel wall, but slope fell steep into trees.

Above slide, ground looked rough but possible.

I stopped well back.

This was not Three Sisters.

No contract turnaround yet.

Just route problem.

I took off pack.

Set it behind stable stone.

No point making balance worse while scouting three paces.

Crutches planted.

I tested edge with right foot.

Firm.

One pace.

Another.

Slide material mostly dry crumb and root mat.

No standing water.

Across it, old wall visible again.

Could traverse high edge where roots remained anchored.

Maybe.

Could descend around below.

Bad with crutches.

Could climb above and rejoin.

Steeper but tree roots.

Old me ranked options automatically.

Not because genius.

Because I had spent decades deciding which bad ground would kill me slower.

High route.

But before committing, I heard the sound again.

Water.

Closer.

A hollow knock under dirt.

Then another.

Not water exactly.

Stone taking load.

I froze.

The slide face gave tiny twitch.

Loose dirt ran down two inches.

Oh.

Not stable.

Back.

I shifted weight onto crutches.

One step.

Then the uphill root mat tore.

Not dramatic at first.

A crack.

Brush jerked.

Then half the slide started moving.

Action became simple.

Not fast.

Correct.

I could not run.

So don't try.

Pack behind me.

Stable wall to right.

Channel wall knee-high.

Big root clump coming down left-center.

Two fist-sized stones bouncing first.

I dropped my center.

Right crutch forward.

Left crutch folded close.

Turn sideways behind wall.

A stone skipped past where my shin had been.

Dirt hit boots.

Root clump struck edge of old channel wall and broke apart.

Then something larger moved above.

Flat slab.

About door-sized.

Sliding, not rolling.

Slow enough to see.

Fast enough to matter.

Its path would hit the wall section I was using.

No time to climb.

No room downhill.

Could retreat three paces to thicker wall if I moved now.

Current body said three paces on crutches through sliding dirt was expensive.

Old brain said staying cost more.

Move.

Right foot.

Plant.

Crutch.

Drag pack strap? Pack still behind stable stone farther back, not on me. Good.

Second step.

Dirt shifted under right foot.

I went low, almost knee.

Left stump safely clear inside wrap? No trial leg. Residual limb above ground.

Crutch tip slid.

Locked-in mode arrived without permission.

Sound narrowed.

Stone.

Dirt.

My breath.

Slab scrape.

Distance.

Angle.

I had done this with two legs once.

Body did not care about nostalgia.

I shoved through right arm and intact leg.

Third pace.

Behind thicker wall.

Slab hit.

Old channel exploded sideways in dust and broken black stone.

A fragment came over top.

Head-sized.

Too late to duck fully.

Magic.

Not test.

Need.

I pushed.

No shaping sequence.

No careful breath count.

Just old command into current weak channel.

Away.

The fragment kicked sideways.

Not far.

Enough.

It clipped my pack-free shoulder instead of face and struck dirt behind me.

Pain.

Sharp.

Not catastrophic.

I was already down.

More earth came.

I curled behind wall and covered head.

Crutches tucked.

Wait.

Stone hissed.

Leaves snapped.

Then less.

Then only pebbles.

Then quiet.

I counted five breaths.

Ten.

Locked-in stayed too long.

Everything wanted action.

No.

Stay.

Listen.

Nothing moving uphill.

No second crack.

Shoulder hurt.

Right hand working.

Left arm working.

Neck fine.

Head fine.

Chest fine.

Right leg fine.

Residual limb untouched.

Crutches?

Right crutch under elbow.

Left beside me.

I checked shafts.

No visible crack.

Tips intact.

Good.

I sat another full minute.

Then laughed once.

Bad sign.

"Adventure," I told dirt.

Dirt declined comment.

I stood carefully.

Shoulder pain deepened when I lifted arm overhead.

Could still bear crutch load?

Test gently.

Yes.

Painful but functional.

Not smart to pretend nothing.

I stepped back toward pack.

Pack had taken dust but stayed behind original stable stone.

Sample bottle inside padded wrap.

Check.

Seal empty intact.

Good.

Food intact.

Trial limb wrap dusty, not damaged.

Good.

I sat and wrote before memory became heroic.

**Route loss above split oak, before Sleeping Stone. Recent/active earth slide approximately twenty feet across old trail. While scouting edge, upper root mat released and slide moved again. Old channel wall section struck by large flat slab and partially collapsed. I retreated behind thicker wall. One stone fragment deflected and clipped right shoulder. No head strike. Crutches intact. Sample bottle intact. Shoulder painful but load-bearing. Trail crossing not attempted after release.**

Then magic line.

This mattered.

**External attempt/use 139: emergency deflection, no setup, stone fragment at close range. Visible lateral change sufficient to alter strike from face/head line to shoulder line. No measurement. No repeat. High-stress context.**

Protocol.

59 minimal.

53 shaping.

139 external.

That was a real result.

Ugly.

Useful.

Expensive.

My head buzzed slightly from locked-in focus more than magic.

Temper followed.

I was angry at slope.

At old path.

At Garrick Sol for saying day up like calendar owed him terrain.

At myself for scouting too close after hearing load noise.

That one deserved anger.

I had heard warning.

I had still stepped onto edge.

Old experience was not permission to be clever.

Good lesson.

Now route.

Trail erased.

Slide active.

Could turn back.

Contract guaranteed nothing below Three Sisters.

But safety mattered more.

Could also find bypass without crossing slide face.

I was before Sleeping Stone.

Old channel likely continued around shoulder.

Topographic option uphill through trees.

Need assess without entering unstable zone.

I moved back twenty paces to where slope above was intact.

Looked uphill.

Trees spaced enough.

Ground steep but rooted.

Could climb around slide crown and descend to trail beyond.

Shoulder pain made crutch climbing worse.

Could wait.

Could rest and see if pain settled.

I did.

Half bell maybe.

Food.

Water.

Cold cloth from flask on shoulder.

No swelling obvious through shirt.

Tender outer shoulder.

Could lift arm to chest height comfortably.

Overhead hurt.

Crutch loading used downward push mostly.

Manageable.

Turnaround threshold wasn't only broken bone.

Three-day quest did not require turning one bruise into damage.

I considered returning White Reed.

Honestly.

Not because cowardice.

Because route had just tried to bury me before first shelter.

Then I looked uphill bypass.

Stable root network.

No exposed slide scar until far side.

Could take slow.

Could stop if shoulder worsened.

No need to cross active debris.

Proceed.

Not prove.

Proceed conditionally.

I repacked.

Tightened shoulder strap differently so pack sat more on left side/waist harness.

Crutches.

Climb.

Ugly.

Ten paces felt like fifty.

I used roots as handholds only after testing.

Sometimes sat and moved pack first.

No dignity.

Excellent survival method.

At top of bypass, I found something interesting.

Old cut stone.

Not channel wall.

Two squared blocks sunk into hillside with iron loops rusted almost gone.

Anchoring point maybe.

Old maintenance line?

Could relate to exposed wire below.

Do not invent.

I wrote:

**Two squared anchor blocks above slide crown, iron loops heavily rusted. Possible old route/channel maintenance infrastructure. Function unknown.**

No conspiracy.

No treasure.

I kept moving.

Far side descent easier.

Trail reappeared beside channel after maybe hundred paces.

I sat.

Shoulder still functional.

No worse.

Good.

Then I noticed channel.

Water.

Actual surface water now.

Thin ribbon.

Not enough to cover bottom.

Running downhill.

I stared.

Had slide exposed it?

Could be seep from disturbed slope.

Could be local branch entering above.

Could be Mirror system.

No claim.

I marked exact location relative to slide and tasted nothing because I wasn't idiot.

District sample bottle remained for source.

My normal flask still had enough.

Continue.

Path curved.

Trees opened.

And there it was.

Sleeping Stone.

Story name was annoyingly accurate.

Long black slab jutted from hillside over shallow recess, rounded top like shoulder under blanket.

Downhill face covered in moss.

Of course.

But not evenly.

Upper half dry gray.

Lower third dark green.

Under overhang, old soot stained stone.

Human shelter use.

Recent?

Maybe.

There was also a small stack of cut orchard twine in back corner.

Not ancient.

Definitely not nine years old.

Somebody had used shelter more recently than Garrick's memory suggested.

No person.

No name.

I checked ground.

One cold fire ring.

Ash old enough dry.

Boot marks too confused to date.

Twine cleaner than rest.

I wrote.

Then sat under stone.

Shelter.

Finally.

I unwrapped shoulder enough to inspect.

Bruise already forming.

No deformity.

Movement limited by pain but present.

Could continue tomorrow if no overnight worsening.

Today?

No.

Enough.

Quest had given me route slide, shoulder strike, bypass climb, emergency magic, and Sleeping Stone.

I had earned stopping.

I set blanket.

Food.

Water.

No fire yet.

East wind reached overhang but weak.

Story said dry unless east wind.

Story got that part partly right too.

Not prophecy.

Observation preserved.

I looked at moss.

Downhill face green.

The old story had called it sign.

But moss here made sense from shade and seep.

There was a wet line behind stone.

Tiny.

Coming from uphill crack.

Not source proof.

Still water sign.

I wrote:

**Sleeping Stone reached. Downhill/lower face mossed; upper face dry. Small seep from uphill crack behind overhang. Story-memory matches a real persistent wet micro-site, but cause/local connection unknown.**

Good.

Then the action replayed in my head.

Fragment.

Push.

Shoulder.

Old brain had not asked permission.

That was interesting.

External 139 had worked under ugly conditions better than some clean tests.

Maybe because need narrowed intent.

Maybe because adrenaline changed output.

Maybe because fragment already moving and tiny lateral change required less than moving static object.

Maybe because I got lucky.

All valid.

Do not build theory tonight.

I wrote all four possibilities.

Then stopped.

No second test.

No celebratory pebble pushing under shelter.

The magic had done its job.

Let it be useful.

Dark came early under trees.

No signal from Morrow.

No Jonah.

No keeper.

No road bells.

For first time this trip, I was sleeping somewhere nobody had sold me floor space.

That felt more like adventure than getting nearly flattened.

Barely.

I checked route copy.

Sleeping Stone.

Next:

Three Sisters Hollow.

Landslide country.

Wonderful.

My shoulder throbbed.

I put notebook away.

Survivor.

Bit dramatic.

I had survived a small slide, not war.

But titles had been generous before.

And old me knew survival was usually less impressive from inside it.

Mostly it looked like hearing the wrong sound, moving before pride finished talking, and sitting down when sitting down was the smartest thing available.

I lay under Sleeping Stone listening to the tiny seep tick into dirt.

Tomorrow I would decide whether my shoulder still belonged to the expedition.

Tonight it did.

That was enough.
