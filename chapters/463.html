CHAPTER 367

THE CROSSER

The stream was lower in the morning.

Not dramatically.

Enough to matter.

I stood above southern descent before first ring with shoulder already warm from packing and watched water move over the flat stone ford.

Yesterday's line had reached around my ankle.

Now lower.

Yesterday's surprise pulse had pushed hard enough against both crutches to make retreat correct.

This morning I watched for one bell.

No pulse.

That did not mean none coming.

It meant one bell without one.

Better than zero bells.

Shoulder test first.

Right arm forward.

Side.

Load through crutch.

Ache.

No sharp change.

Bruise darker.

Range still limited overhead.

Functional.

Food count next.

Not enough for another cheerful three-day loop.

Enough for today and controlled return if I stopped pretending portions were suggestions.

I split remaining bread and cheese into travel and emergency.

Dried fruit already less than I wanted.

Water good.

Bottle empty.

Twelve copper.

Twenty-three Vale.

Four copper fallback waiting behind me if I returned with useful notes.

That mattered because now continuing was expensive in ways that had nothing to do with coin.

Time.

Food.

Shoulder.

Return distance.

And the increasingly irritating possibility that I might actually find what I came for.

I descended southern steps.

Recent branch cuts looked the same as last night.

Not useful.

At stream I ignored cracked plank.

Good decisions deserved repetition.

The shallow ford showed full stone bottom through clear water.

I walked bank upstream and down once.

No hidden deeper slot apparent.

No alternate dry crossing.

Current strongest toward center.

I set rules.

No trial limb.

Pack tightened high.

Bottle padded center.

One crutch moves at a time.

Right foot only after both crutches planted.

If water rises above yesterday's mark or shoulder sharpens, reverse immediately.

No pride adjustments halfway.

Then I entered.

First crutch.

Second.

Right foot.

Pause.

The water was cold enough to make concentration easy.

Second move.

Third.

Shoulder complained on right-side plant but held.

I shortened reach.

Less efficient.

More stable.

Center water pushed harder against crutch shafts.

No pulse.

Halfway.

I stopped.

Not because danger.

Because planned pause.

That distinction mattered.

Breathing normal.

Pack stable.

Bottle silent.

Move.

One.

Two.

Three.

Far bank close.

Then water increased.

Not yesterday's sudden shove.

A gradual rise.

Half finger around right boot.

Maybe normal variation.

Maybe beginning of pulse.

Decision.

Forward bank three placements.

Backward bank six.

Continue.

I moved faster without becoming sloppy.

Plant.

Plant.

Foot.

Plant.

Plant.

Foot.

Right crutch slipped half an inch on algae.

My body corrected before thought finished.

Left crutch loaded.

Hip low.

Right foot stayed planted.

Shoulder flashed pain.

No fall.

I reset tip farther apart.

One more sequence.

Far bank.

I sat on stone immediately.

No celebration.

Just inventory.

Shoulder worse for maybe thirty seconds.

Then settled back to angry baseline.

Crutches intact.

Pack dry above bottom seam.

Bottle intact.

Right boot soaked.

Crossing successful.

I recorded approximate water condition and that level rose slightly during crossing without abrupt pulse.

No cause claim.

No hero language.

Then I looked at route.

The southern shelf continued east under trees.

Recent cuts continued too.

One branch shaved chest high.

Another sapling bent and tied aside with plain fiber cord.

That was current use.

Not necessarily current today.

But intentional.

The shelf curved below Three Sisters scar, staying under mature trees and outside fresh bare slide face.

Old retaining wall held sections.

Other sections had become root shelf and dirt.

I moved slowly.

Shoulder made every right crutch plant a reminder.

About half bell later, route climbed.

Three old stone steps.

Then six.

Then the opposite intact lower bench appeared above.

Rejoin.

There it was.

Southern bypass connected across the cut.

Not safely proven for everyone.

Not maintained road.

But passable this morning for me under current conditions.

Useful.

I marked junction with notebook sketch.

No cairn.

No changing route.

Not my authority.

Opposite bench ran east with old water wall beside it.

Three Sisters receded behind.

Mirror Spring ahead somewhere beyond next ridge.

For first time I had crossed the contractual failure point.

Eight copper if I reached source and returned notes.

Twelve with sample.

Money surfaced in thought.

I disliked that.

Then accepted it.

Money was part of why I was here.

Pretending otherwise would be another kind of vanity.

The old bench climbed.

Not steep.

Relentless.

I rested often.

Shoulder more limiting than leg now.

Interesting reversal.

Trial build remained dead weight in pack.

Could have left it Morrow.

Maybe.

But future stable ground might justify carrying it.

Today did not.

Late morning I reached first strong human trace beyond Three Sisters.

Not person.

A low side channel had been cleared of leaves recently.

Too recently for years.

Mud along edge showed rectangular tool scrape.

Someone had maintained water movement here.

Why?

Could be orchard family.

Could be hunter using drainage.

Could be district.

Could be nobody connected to Morrow.

No names.

No conspiracy.

I wrote exact.

Then found something more useful.

A small wooden marker wedged under wall stone.

No words.

Three shallow cuts on one face.

One deep cut on other.

Could be route marker.

Could be maintenance tally.

Could be scrap.

Leave.

I was getting better at not turning objects into plot just because I found them.

The route narrowed under white cliff seam.

White.

There.

Garrick's description:

black-stone catch wall below white cliff seam.

Mirror Spring likely near.

I stopped.

Not because exhausted.

Because objective proximity changed behavior.

From now on, do not contaminate bottle.

Do not touch water systems casually.

Do not alter source.

Observe before approach.

The white seam ran across cliff face perhaps two hundred paces ahead.

Below it, trees thick.

I could hear water.

More than Three Sisters stream.

Steady.

Not roaring.

Enough.

Path descended slightly.

Shoulder liked downhill less because crutches took more braking load.

I sat twice and moved on rear rather than force it.

Old me would have mocked current me.

Old me also had two legs.

He could shut up.

Then I smelled something.

Wet stone.

Cold mineral.

And something sweet-green from moss.

No salt.

Still no salt.

Carrow felt absurdly far.

The path opened.

Mirror Spring.

Not magical mirror.

At least not visibly.

A broad black-stone catch wall sat under pale cliff seam.

Water emerged from three places.

One main spring mouth at center under stone lip.

Two thinner side seeps.

Catch basin below fed narrow channel running west and south through old stonework.

The basin surface reflected cliff in broken silver-black patches.

Mirror enough.

I did not move closer yet.

First observation from distance.

Main flow present.

Strong by eye compared to thin channels below.

But I did not know normal.

Side seep left active.

Right side weak.

Catch basin not empty.

No obvious giant root blocking mouth.

No story reenactment.

Good.

Then I saw why recent route cuts existed.

Someone had been here.

Recently.

Not person now.

Evidence.

Fresh boot or shoe impressions? Careful. Ground damp. Several tread-like marks near side wall, overlapping, not readable enough for count.

A coil of new fiber cord hung from peg under old catch roof.

One small hand shovel leaned against wall.

And one gate.

Not orchard-style major gate.

A narrow wooden diversion flap in secondary channel below basin.

Newer than surrounding stone.

Currently half closed.

That mattered.

I stayed still.

Contract explicitly said no altering gates.

Good rule.

Water left basin through two branches.

Main lower channel.

Secondary east-cut channel controlled partly by new flap.

Where did east branch go?

Unknown.

Could feed upper orchards.

Could feed maintenance pool.

Could be entirely unrelated to Morrow.

Do not accuse.

No trial limb.

Crutches careful.

Shoulder painful now.

I checked district form.

Fill point.

Observed conditions.

Seal.

Witness if available.

No witness.

Allowed blank if none? Form had witness if available according to Celia. Fine.

Need valid sample from source.

Where exactly counts source?

Main mouth or catch basin?

Celia had said fill point recorded. Bottle accepted supplemental, not authoritative.

Best minimally contaminated point without altering source.

Main outflow immediately below mouth accessible from flat stone.

Good.

Before opening bottle, inspect hands.

Dirty.

No washing with source then touching rim carelessly.

I had clean cloth wrap section.

Bottle instructions likely keep interior sterile by not touching lip/inside. Fine.

I unpadded bottle.

Seal intact.

Opened only when positioned.

Shoulder made two-handed handling awkward.

I sat.

Crutches beside.

Right foot braced.

Bottle low.

I filled from falling stream below main lip without touching stone or basin.

Water clear.

Not proof of anything.

Cap.

Seal strip.

Press.

Record.

**Mirror Spring main center outflow, below lip, no contact with basin stone. Main visible flow present. Two side seeps present, right weaker than left by visual observation only. Newer secondary diversion flap below catch basin half closed. No gate changes made. Recent human-use traces present. No witness available.**

Twelve copper condition maybe met if sample survived return and office accepted chain.

Not money yet.

Then I did something harder.

I looked at spring without wanting answer.

Why were lower Morrow channels low?

Spring visibly running.

That made one hypothesis less comfortable.

Not source dry.

But visual flow could be below normal.

Diversion could matter.

Side branch could matter.

Losses between here and White Reed could matter.

Upper gates could matter.

Fog recharge could still matter.

Everything still alive.

Correct procedure had not solved social problem.

Of course.

I inspected from allowed areas.

New diversion flap had fresh wood grain.

Peg hardware newer iron.

A small scratched marking on side, not words, maybe maker tally.

No owner sign.

Main channel stone showed wet line higher than current water by maybe two fingers.

Recent? Could be yesterday. Could be seasonal stain. Record as visible higher wet/stain line, timing unknown.

Right seep area had pale mineral crust and damp moss.

No blockage obvious.

Left seep stronger.

Main mouth clear enough visually.

No fallen root.

No boulder.

Legend not literal.

Good.

Then sound uphill.

Human.

A cough.

I froze.

Not combat readiness exactly.

Attention sharpened.

Another sound.

Foot on stone.

Somebody above catch roof.

I moved away from gate and sample handling area so first impression was not stranger tampering with water.

Crutches planted.

Hands visible.

A person stepped down from narrow upper path carrying bundled brush and short pruning blade.

Middle-aged man perhaps.

No need name yet.

He stopped when he saw me.

Then saw my bottle.

Then crutches.

"Who are you?"

Not friendly.

"Greg. Private Morrow field contract. District supplemental bottle."

"Morrow sent you?"

"Morrow cooperative hired me. District provided bottle procedure. I don't represent district."

His eyes moved to sealed bottle.

"You touched gate?"

"No."

"Why are you here?"

"Lower Morrow channels are low. Contract is route notes plus source sample."

His mouth tightened.

Human conflict arrived at source.

"Of course they sent somebody."

"They didn't send me to accuse you."

"You don't know who I am."

"Correct."

That almost broke tension.

Almost.

He set brush bundle down.

"That flap isn't theirs."

"I didn't say it was."

"They'll say it is."

"Maybe."

"They always do."

There.

Somebody wanted something badly.

I stayed quiet.

He continued because people often did when silence refused to fight back.

"East branch feeds ridge nursery and fire cistern. Has for years. We replaced rotten flap after spring thaw. Morrow knows."

Claim.

Not fact yet.

"Who is we?"

He hesitated.

Name threshold.

This person mattered.

Need registry check before naming, but prose already drafting. Avoid naming until future chapter/state can check. He can say ridge growers without personal name.

"Ridge growers," he said. "Upper shoulder holdings."

"How much does flap divert?"

"Depends basin."

"Today?"

"Less than quarter."

"Measured?"

He bristled.

"By marks."

"Where?"

He pointed to side post with carved notches.

I hadn't noticed.

Five horizontal cuts.

Water near second.

"Old share heights."

"Old how?"

"Before me."

Not enough.

Maybe.

"Morrow says lower rows losing water."

"Morrow closes their own orchard gates badly and blames mountain."

Maybe.

"Spring lower than usual?"

"A little."

"How little?"

"One mark maybe."

"This time last year."

"You can inspect my notes if you want."

Risk.

But useful.

"Why?"

"Because I'm writing your claims as claims. If you think I misheard, better now than after I walk back."

**Main visible flow present. Visual normal unknown. Secondary east flap half closed. Identity/authority unknown.**

"I can add how you know."

"How?"

"Better."

"Still not measurement."

"I know."

No sarcasm.

Just tired.

"After slide. Upper route killed a mule two years ago. We use lower shelf to reach spring."

That mattered.

"Why isn't Morrow using it?"

"Because they stopped coming."

"Garrick Sol walked old route nine years ago."

Recognition.

"Garrick."

Not warm.
