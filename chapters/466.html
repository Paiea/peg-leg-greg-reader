CHAPTER 463

THE SEEKER

I slept late.

Not dramatically.

One bell later than useful people.

Still counts.

Morrow packing loft had morning light through slats and fruit crates below making small wooden noises as workers moved them.

My shoulder remained bruised.

Less stiff than yesterday.

Better.

Not good.

I tested it anyway.

Forward lift.

Fine.

Side lift.

Ache.

Overhead.

Still insulting.

Crutch load.

Stable.

Trial limb remained wrapped.

No reason to prove anything to anybody.

Cash twenty-four copper.

Vale twenty-three.

Bits unspecified.

Ten-copper floor.

Fourteen copper of room.

That felt strange.

Not wealth.

Choice.

Choice was expensive enough that I noticed when I had some.

Below, Morrow had already started counting fallen fruit.

Actual counting.

Baskets by row.

Chalk marks.

Helena's idea survived sunrise.

Good.

I did not join.

My contract was done.

This mattered too.

Not every problem became mine because I understood it a little.

Garrick was at lower gate with two workers when I came down.

Gate had been restored one notch as agreed.

He saw me.

I expected argument.

Got a nod.

Small.

Tired.

Enough.

"Leaving?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Lakeward?"

"Probably."

"Probably is bad road planning."

"I've improved."

He almost smiled.

Almost.

Then he looked toward rows.

"District reading cart comes before noon."

"Good."

"You aren't staying for it."

Not question.

"No."

He looked at me again.

"Good."

Interesting.

Maybe he didn't want me there.

Maybe he understood what contract ending meant.

Maybe both.

"Route held," he said after a while.

"Parts did."

"Enough."

There was old path pride in that sentence.

I let him keep it.

Helena gave me a paper-wrapped breakfast from packing shed.

Paid? She waved away question.

"You made us twelve copper worth of trouble. Eat."

"I thought trouble was included."

"Apparently not."

Jonah arrived carrying Mabel's route-note copy under arm.

"I'll take this down."

"Thanks."

"She'll complain about handwriting."

"It's legible."

"That's not same thing."

Fair.

He looked at pack.

"You're actually leaving."

"That was the idea."

"Where?"

"Lakeward. Then I don't know."

"That's still terrible plan."

"Consistent branding."

He grinned.

Then became serious.

"Come back sometime."

Not dramatic.

No friendship oath.

Good.

"Maybe."

"That's not answer."

"You'll survive."

He rolled eyes.

I left Morrow by midmorning cart.

Two copper.

Twenty-four to twenty-two.

Still twelve above floor.

No anxiety spike.

Progress.

Danton Pike was not driving this one. Good. Fewer named people.

Driver was driver.

Cart rolled west through orchards.

Water boards had people around them now.

Not shouting.

Mostly looking.

Better use of eyes.

At one shared box, district gray coat crouched by scale marks while two growers waited.

I did not stop.

Morrow became rows.

Rows became scattered trees.

Lakeward glass roofs appeared near afternoon.

Shoulder liked cart more than crutches.

Everything liked cart more than crutches.

At Lakeward gate I had three immediate choices.

Cheap floor again.

Better room.

Or keep moving before shoulder convinced me otherwise.

I picked cheap room anyway.

Not because broke.

Because bed was bed.

**GLASS YARD BUNK / 2C**

Twenty-two to twenty.

Still ten above floor.

Luxury apparently was paying twice the floor rate and feeling smug.

I dropped pack.

Then did something I had not been able to do last time.

Went to market without needing job board first.

Dangerous behavior.

Lakeward afternoon market was louder than I remembered.

Glass workers ending shifts.

Orchard carts coming in.

Reed bundles.

Kiln heat from east works.

Fresh bread.

Metal polish.

Wet rope.

No salt.

Still no salt.

I noticed every time now.

At district office, sample file had not returned yet.

Expected tomorrow or later.

Fine.

I left home note at post counter.

Bits.

No exact count.

**Morrow job done. Reached Mirror Spring by old route through White Reed, Sleeping Stone, and Three Sisters. Route partly broken but a lower maintenance bypass still exists under some conditions. Brought sealed sample back. District accepted it for transport. Water dispute not solved. Contract paid 12c. Cash now 20c after travel and bunk, Vale 23c. Shoulder bruised from route accident but improving. Trial build healthy, not used on rough path. Not heading home yet. I liked the trip more than I expected. - Greg**

I stared at last sentence.

Could remove it.

Didn't.

Post clerk took note.

That was that.

Then market.

No objective.

I could afford curiosity now.

Not purchase, necessarily.

Curiosity.

Important difference.

Artifact stalls near glass quarter were better than South Larken.

Not richer exactly.

More specialized.

Kiln tools.

Fog glass.

Heat-mark tiles.

Retired orchard charms that mostly seemed designed to tell farmers things farmers already knew.

One stall had old route hardware.

Bell pins.

Channel tags.

Glass markers.

A dented brass frame shaped like half a wheel.

I stopped.

Seller looked up.

Older man, unnamed because not yet important.

"Broken," he said.

"Good sales technique."

"Saves time."

I pointed.

"What was it?"

"Road memory frame. Maybe."

Maybe.

Good word.

"What does that mean?"

"Means estate lot said road memory frame and I didn't argue with dead man's nephew."

Excellent provenance.

I leaned closer without touching.

Half-wheel brass frame.

Three slots.

One still held cloudy glass strip.

Tiny tooth track around inside rim.

No obvious charge.

No dramatic glow.

"Price?"

"Nine copper."

I laughed.

He didn't.

"Broken maybe-road thing for nine?"

"Brass is worth three. Old glass another two if cutter wants it. Teeth are fine. Nine if you want whole question."

That was actually better pricing logic than most sellers deserved.

Market could see salvage value.

Not dumb.

"Where from?"

"Estate lot out of east ridge. Cartman's family."

"Which road?"

"Didn't ask."

"Maker mark?"

He turned frame with rag.

Tiny stamped shape near hinge.

Not letters.

Three short lines over a circle.

I did not know it.

"Recognize?"

"No."

"Then why nine?"

"Because you stopped."

Fair.

I moved on.

Nine copper was not impossible now.

That was dangerous too.

But I didn't know enough.

Good.

A second stall sold old orchard measuring pieces.

Nothing pulled.

Third had retired ferry depth tongues.

Interesting, not needed.

Fourth had a glass case with small black stone disk under wire.

Label:

**ECHO STEP / INCOMPLETE / NO PAIR / 18C**

I stopped harder.

Seller noticed.

Different seller.

Woman with magnifying lens on cord.

"Incomplete," she said.

"I can read."

"Most interested men can't."

"What does echo step mean?"

"Old trail pair."

"Pair of what?"

She tapped case.

"Two stones. Set one at known place, carry one. Under charge they answer distance? Direction? Delay? Depends maker and generation. This one's missing mate. No useful response alone that I know."

Interesting.

Not miracle.

Not useful enough.

"Why eighteen?"

"Maker school."

She pointed underside mark through glass.

Same symbol.

Three short lines over circle.

There.

Connection.

"That mark."

"Old east-road school."

"Name?"

"Depends who you're arguing with. Lakeward catalog calls them **Sunwheel Roadworks**. Orchard people call them **Three Rays**."

Not a person name.

Good.

"How old?"

"Old enough that complete paired pieces sell to collectors before workers."

"This one?"

"Incomplete. Eighteen because mark is clean and stone body isn't cracked."

Market knows provenance.

Price reflects collector value.

No bargain yet.

"What roads used them?"

"East ridge mostly. Older orchard roads before current post glass. Some north passes."

I thought of half-wheel frame.

"Road memory frame?"

Her eyebrows moved.

"Where?"

"Other stall."

"Brass half-wheel?"

"Yes."

"Three slots?"

"Yes."

Now she was interested.

"That might be marker reader."

"Might?"

"Three Rays made too many things and labeled almost none."

"What would it read?"

"Old road disks maybe. Or station strips. Depends teeth."

"Useful now?"

"If you have matching route pieces."

"Which I don't."

"Then decorative question."

Good phrase.

I liked her.

Still didn't name her.

"Any surviving matching route?"

She leaned back.

"Maybe east beyond orchard country."

There.

"Where?"

"You buying information?"

"Depends price."

"One bit."

Fair.

I paid one bit.

She said:

"Old **Sunroad Cut**. East of Lakeward, past Bracken Vale road split. Most modern traffic goes south around ridge now. Old cut closed after bridge wash years ago. Collectors still find Three Rays bits there sometimes."

New place.

"Closed how?"

"Depends section. Old bridge gone, upper road partly walkable from west according to people who like making bad decisions recreationally."

"That seems specific."

"I sell to collectors."

"Any reason to go besides broken artifacts?"

"View."

"Terrible reason."

"Then don't."

I looked at Echo Step disk.

"Story?"

She smiled slightly.

"There's always story."

Of course.

"What one?"

"Old orchard saying. Sunroad never lost a traveler who kept three shadows."

I waited.

"That's it?"

"Mostly. Some versions say road could remember where you were if you carried the right stone. Some say bridge bells answered before fog. Some say people made all that up after road closed because broken infrastructure is more romantic than maintenance budgets."

Excellent.

"Which do you believe?"

"I believe complete Three Rays route sets sell for silver."

Best answer.

Silver.

There was economic pull.

Not free artifact.

Not cheap miracle.

A real collector market.

"How complete?"

"Reader plus matched route disks or strips. Working pair sometimes more. Depends condition and provenance."

"You seen one?"

"Complete? Once."

"Price?"

"Two silver and change."

I whistled.

Leg money territory.

Above leg money.

Potentially.

"Did it work?"

"Enough that buyer paid."

"What did it do?"

"Reader held strip. Disk placed across hall. Needle moved to one of three bands when charge pulse sent. Seller claimed route-position confirmation. Buyer claimed more. I claimed commission."

Good.

No omniscient artifact reveal.

Market-visible capability existed.

"Sunroad Cut still has pieces because?"

"Because if everybody knew where complete ones were, I wouldn't be telling you for one bit."

Fair.

Half-wheel still there.

Nine copper.

Now I knew likely school.

Possibly marker reader.

Still incomplete.

Still question.

Could buy.

Cash twenty.

Nine would leave eleven.

Only one above floor.

Bad.

No.

"Nine."

"Eight."

"Nine."

"You came back."

Painful.

Good.
