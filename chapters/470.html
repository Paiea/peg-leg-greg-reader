CHAPTER 374

THE CALIBRATOR

Ten copper looked different after I wrote thirty at the top of a page.

Before, ten had been safety.

Now it looked like the bottom of a hole.

Not a deep hole.

Still a hole.

I counted anyway.

Ten copper.

Twenty-three with Vale.

Bits in the small pouch, enough that I did not need to turn breakfast into a philosophical crisis.

Work-limb deposit already paid.

Everything else was either equipment, obligation, or wishful thinking.

I closed the purse.

Lakeward morning came through the bunkhouse shutters in thin gray bars.

My shoulder had improved again overnight, though improvement had apparently decided to become a slow administrative process.

Forward lift was fine.

Side lift pulled.

Overhead still produced a sharp enough warning that I stopped before proving anything.

Right knee scrape had dried clean.

No swelling.

No reason to dramatize it.

The reconstructed Three Rays demonstration opened after midday.

That gave me half a day.

Old Greg, meaning me three weeks ago or possibly three months ago because my timekeeping had become criminal, would have wandered the market until something happened.

Current Greg had a page.

**BODY**

**MAGIC**

**MONEY**

**TOOLS**

**INFORMATION**

**PEOPLE**

I hated how much it looked like work.

Good plans often did.

Body first.

Not training.

Assessment.

The trial limb had been rested through the Sunroad push and shoulder fall. Skin had been healthy last check. Lakeward had dry paved courtyards and benches.

But using it while shoulder was sore could actually reduce crutch load if I kept the session short and flat.

Or it could make me stupidly confident because both hands became available and I forgot I was still testing an unfinished shop leg.

I took it to the bunkhouse rear court.

No audience except a woman hanging sheets who looked once, decided I was not interesting enough, and continued working.

Perfect.

Socket.

Liner.

Temporary ash shaft.

Medium test foot.

Everything looked intact.

I fitted slowly.

Standing changed the room.

Still.

Every time.

Crutches under hands, weight mostly right, then a little through left.

No immediate pressure complaint.

I waited.

Then one step.

Then another.

Dry flat stone.

The body map came back faster than before.

Not natural.

Familiar.

Different thing.

I walked the length of the courtyard once with both crutches.

Turned.

Came back.

Shoulder tolerated it.

Then I put one crutch against wall and stood with the other in right hand.

Left hand free.

That was the whole damned point.

Not walking pretty.

Not looking restored.

A hand.

One available hand changed support work immediately.

Carry a signal lamp.

Hold a line.

Open a latch.

Use an artifact.

Catch somebody.

Point without putting a crutch under armpit and hoping gravity respected optimism.

I picked up an empty wash basin from bench.

Light.

Both hands for three steps.

Set it down.

No wobble beyond normal test-leg awkwardness.

Again.

Five steps.

Stop.

No reason to turn progress into punishment.

I checked time.

Twelve minutes.

I could have done more.

That was exactly why I stopped.

Socket off.

Immediate skin check.

Mild pink at upper contact.

No point heat.

No pain.

Twenty minutes later, mostly clear.

Thirty, clear.

Notebook:

**Lakeward dry court. 12 min. Both-crutch walking stable. One-crutch standing brief. Empty basin two-hand carry 3 + 5 steps. Mild upper pink immediate, mostly clear 20, clear 30. Shoulder tolerated. Stopped early intentionally.**

Then beneath it:

**BODY TARGET: not duration. usable hands.**

That felt better.

The old support version of me had never cared how impressive a movement looked if it failed to create options.

Good.

One lane aligned.

Money next.

Harder.

I went to Lakeward district contracting board instead of general work board.

Different building.

Different wages.

Also different assumptions.

General board had washing, loading, carrying, sweeping, sorting.

Contract board had things like:

**ROUTE CONDITION COPY / NORTH REED / 6C / TWO DAYS / OWN TRAVEL**

**FERRY LOSS WITNESS / VARIABLE / LICENSED OR DISTRICT REFERENCE**

**WAREHOUSE CLAIM INVENTORY / 8C / TWO REFERENCES**

**FIELD MARKER AUDIT / 7C / ROUTE EXPERIENCE / MAP COPY REQUIRED**

**PRIVATE SALVAGE CATALOG / RATE BY LOT / PRIOR APPRAISAL REFERENCE**

There.

Money did not magically appear because I wanted more of it.

The higher-paying work had gates.

References.

Records.

Proof.

Sometimes licenses.

Annoying.

Also useful.

I copied categories and requirements.

A clerk at side desk watched me.

"Looking for work?"

"Looking for what work pays more than two copper."

He nodded like this was a common disease.

"Usually work where being wrong costs somebody more than two copper."

That was good enough that I wrote it down.

"How does somebody get onto those without already being on them?"

"Reference."

"From?"

"Someone who paid you for comparable work. District office. Registered shop. Merchant house. Road office. Depends on listing."

Mirror Spring.

Bracken hazard report.

Morrow route notes.

Maybe.

"Does completed district supplemental sample work count?"

He looked up.

"If chain accepted and hiring office signs field completion."

"It was."

"Then get a completion slip."

Simple.

I had receipt proving sample entered transport.

Not completion reference.

Different paper.

"Road hazard report?"

"If ridge office confirms useful and identifies you. Better if closure or repair action followed."

Sunroad had closed.

That mattered.

"Artifact screening?"

"Registered appraiser reference."

I had Sori.

Maybe.

Not assume.

The clerk pulled a blank card.

"You trying to build a field file?"

"Apparently."

"Then stop collecting stories about work and collect signatures."

Less poetic than grandmother route lore.

Probably more profitable.

I thanked him.

No name.

Didn't need one.

My people audit had been too emotional yesterday.

Who would answer a letter.

Who cared.

Important.

But support networks also had institutional forms.

Who could verify what I had actually done?

That was different.

I added a second people list:

**REFERENCE CAPACITY**

Morrow district field completion.

Bracken ridge hazard usefulness.

Sori commercial/artifact judgment if earned.

Hark & Venn limb/travel reliability, probably not field contracting.

Theatre work reliability maybe Carrow local.

Hessa magic work absolutely not something I was turning into employment paperwork without asking.

Good.

People were not resources.

References were specific things people might choose to provide.

Difference mattered.

I spent no copper.

Already improving.

By midday, the east museum storehouse had a line.

Not large.

Eight people.

Three looked like collectors.

One looked like a teacher with four children attached by moral force.

I paid three bits and entered.

The Three Rays reconstruction occupied a long room with shutters on both east and west walls.

Not impressive at first glance.

Three waist-high iron frames sat on a wooden track about twelve paces long.

Each frame held a cloudy glass strip.

At the center of each was a black disk mounted behind the glass.

Brass toothed arcs allowed the disk assembly to rotate through limited positions.

On the wall above them:

**RECONSTRUCTION FROM SUNROAD RECOVERY RECORDS**

**REPLACEMENT FRAMES / REPLACEMENT GLASS IN TWO STATIONS**

**ONE ORIGINAL DISK PAIR USED FOR DEMONSTRATION**

**BEHAVIOR ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT CERTIFIED TO HISTORIC FIELD PERFORMANCE**

Good museum.

A staff demonstrator waited until group settled.

Also unnamed.

"This reconstruction is intended to show one plausible operating principle supported by surviving instructions and component wear. It is not a claim that every Sunroad station functioned identically."

I liked her too.

The children did not.

One whispered, "Does it do anything?"

His teacher whispered back, "That's what she's explaining."

"But does it?"

Strong question.

The demonstrator opened west shutters.

Afternoon light entered low enough to strike first shade glass.

She rotated brass arc two teeth.

The cloudy strip darkened most of the light except one narrow band.

Behind it, black disk showed a pale crescent.

She walked to second station.

Different frame position.

Different disk.

Another pale crescent.

Then third.

At the end she held up a small white board with three drawn crescent shapes.

"The paired disks carry numbered notch families. Under this reconstruction, a traveler or road keeper could compare the observed light shape at one station against the expected paired shape at another. If the pair and frame orientation agreed, the station could provide a directional or timing confirmation even when distant landmarks were obscured."

One collector raised hand.

"So navigation."

"Possibly route confirmation. Not general navigation."

Good.

"Fog?"

"Direct heavy fog would reduce useful light. Surviving notes suggest shade or broken fog conditions may have been relevant. We do not know operating thresholds."

"Magic?"

"No charged material has been identified in recovered standard components."

Interesting.

Mechanical optical support infrastructure.

Maybe.

The demonstrator changed one thing.

She swapped the second black disk with another from tray.

Same size.

Different stamped number.

Ran light again.

The crescent did not match the board.

"This is why the surviving instruction fragment `do not read single disc` matters. A disk alone does not tell us enough. The relationship between paired markings, frame orientation, and expected light condition appears to have carried the useful information."

There.

The orphan disk at eighteen copper became even worse as a purchase.

Not useless.

But context-dependent.

A component without pair or station knowledge.

Collector bait unless you had matching infrastructure.

The child whispered, louder now:

"So it tells you if you're where you think you are?"

The demonstrator paused.

"That is one plausible simplified description."

The child nodded.

Solved.

I wrote:

**Three Rays support function candidate: externalized confirmation. Not tell traveler where to go. Help detect when route/timing/orientation doesn't match expectation. Reduces confident wrongness.**

That was support.

Deeply.

Not power.

A system designed around people making fewer expensive mistakes.

I understood why it bothered me in a good way.

Old me would have loved it.

Not because artifact.

Because it moved uncertainty out of one person's head and into shared infrastructure.

You didn't need the best navigator on every cart.

You needed stations, paired components, expected patterns, maintenance, and people trained enough to compare.

That made ordinary travelers better.

Support at civic scale.

I stayed for second demonstration.

Not because first was unclear.

Because I wanted to watch failure.

I asked staff afterward whether they demonstrated misalignment deliberately.

She did.

Frame faced slightly wrong.

Same pair.

Same light.

Crescent shifted enough to miss expected board.

Then she partially shaded window.

Signal weakened.

Not binary.

Interpretation got harder.

Good.

Constraints.

"Could somebody fake a match by rotating frame until shape looked right?" I asked.

"In reconstruction? Yes."

"Historic system?"

"If frame orientation was uncontrolled, presumably that would defeat part of purpose. Installed frames show anchor points and wear consistent with limited set positions. Whether there were seals, stops, inspection rules, or other controls is unresolved."

Another support lesson.

Systems only worked if people couldn't casually tune evidence to answer they wanted.

Morrow again.

Different object.

Same species.

I left without buying anything.

That was becoming a skill.

At the archive desk, I asked whether reconstructed-set notes could be copied.

Two bits for public summary.

I paid.

Bits, not copper.

Fine.

The summary added no miracle.

Paired-number families.

Three density bands.

Frame orientation west at some documented stations.

Return/home marks in damaged text.

Installed tooth wear suggesting repeated limited positions.

No charged material in standard recovered parts.

Possible optical route/timing confirmation.

Unresolved regional variation.

Enough.

Three Rays had moved from `maybe buy weird brass` to `understand support architecture`.

That changed tool priority.

I crossed **orphan Three Rays component** off acquisition list.

Then wrote:

**Desired tool function: independent position / condition confirmation that works without full station network.**

Different.

Maybe another artifact could do it.

Maybe current reader plus maps plus ordinary markers already covered some.

Don't buy history when you need capability.

That sentence hurt collectors everywhere.

Late afternoon, I went back to contracting board.

Not to take work.

To look again with new eyes.

Seven copper marker audit.

Two references.

Route experience.

Map copy.

I had route experience.

Could get references.

Not today.

Eight copper warehouse claim inventory.

Two references.

I had done custody/evidence work around artifacts and field samples, but not enough formal warehouse claim inventory to pretend equivalence.

Private salvage catalog.

Prior appraisal reference.

Sori maybe.

Again, maybe.

The money lane was becoming concrete.

Not `find better job`.

Build proof that unlocked better categories.

I wrote:

**MONEY PROGRESSION IS CREDENTIAL + REPUTATION + CAPITAL, NOT ONLY HOURS.**

Then underneath:

**Immediate: get Morrow completion reference. get Bracken hazard confirmation. wait for Sori reply.**

Thirty copper stopped looking impossible.

Still not close.

Different.

At post counter, no replies.

Of course.

I had sent letters yesterday.

The universe remained committed to distance.

I almost laughed at myself.

People network required patience.

Terrible mechanic.

I bought dinner with bits and returned to bunkhouse.

Shoulder tired but not worse from twelve-minute limb session.

That was useful data.

I checked skin again hours later.

Clear.

Right knee scrape boring.

Best possible condition for knee scrape.

I opened notebook to six headings.

BODY:

Twelve-minute stable session. Hands target clarified.

MAGIC:

Waiting Hessa. No test.

MONEY:

Ten copper. Thirty target. Higher-pay categories identified. Need references.

TOOLS:

Three Rays system useful conceptually. Orphan component poor acquisition. Capability gap reframed.

INFORMATION:

Demonstration confirms plausible paired optical route-check principle, reconstruction only.

PEOPLE:

Separate relationship from reference. Ask specifically. Don't assume.

That was a better day than earning two copper.

Financially, I had earned nothing.

That part still annoyed me.

But I had moved earning ceiling.

Potentially.

No applause.

No stat screen.

No satisfying blue light announcing **FIELD CONTRACTOR UNLOCKED**.

Just paperwork I didn't have yet.

Which meant tomorrow needed action.

Not combat.

Progression action.

Get the references moving.

Find out whether Lakeward had a paid task where one existing accepted field completion could qualify me, even if rate wasn't eight copper yet.

Maybe take something that built the second reference rather than merely paid lunch.

That was the shift.

A two-copper job could still be worth taking if it bought access to seven-copper work later.

A five-copper job could be bad if it taught nothing, proved nothing, and stranded me somewhere useless.

Money wasn't just amount.

It had direction.

Same with magic.

Same with tools.

Same with people.

Support was never one big thing.

It was a stack of small advantages arranged before somebody needed them.

I had forgotten that because I'd spent so long being the person who needed the advantages.

That was fair.

Reset had taken almost everything.

Body.

Power.

Money.

Status.

Infrastructure.

People who knew what I could do.

I had treated survival as proof I was rebuilding.

It was proof I was alive.

Different category.

Now I had a trial leg that could give me both hands for five careful steps.

One hundred thirty-nine external attempts, with a couple small results worth respecting and one emergency result worth not lying about.

Ten copper.

A strange little support kit.

A notebook full of roads.

People scattered across enough places that letters had become a real tool.

And, apparently, the beginning of a field file.

Pathetic compared with S-class.

Excellent compared with nothing.

I could work with excellent compared with nothing.

I turned to a clean page.

At top:

**BUILD THE STACK.**

Then, because I knew myself:

**DO NOT TURN LIFE INTO A CHECKLIST.**

Important safety note.

I closed notebook.

Tomorrow I would go make somebody willing to sign something useful.

After that, maybe the next road.

This time I intended to know what I was taking with me.
