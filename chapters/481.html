CHAPTER 411

THE SUBSTITUTE

The theatre came back for me after breakfast.

Not metaphorically.

There was a knock on the door while Lyssa was trying to convince a customer that a sleeve could not be both tighter and easier to move in.

The customer said, "I only mean here."

Lyssa said, "That is where your arm bends."

"Yes."

"So if I make it tighter there, bending becomes worse."

"I understand that."

"No, you don't."

Knock again.

I looked at door.

Lyssa pointed at it with two pins.

"Useful."

Apparently I had a household role.

I opened the door.

The man outside wore theatre black, though his shirt had faded enough that one shoulder was almost brown.

I knew his face.

Not name.

He knew mine.

"Greg."

"Unfortunately."

He looked relieved anyway.

"We came by."

"Lyssa mentioned."

"You're home."

"Observant institution."

He looked past me.

Lyssa said from table, "If you want him, take him."

The customer said, "Can I move now?"

"No."

The theatre man looked back at me.

"We need a substitute this afternoon."

There.

Not summons.

Not destiny.

A shift.

"What kind?"

"Prompt side."

That mattered.

"Not rail?"

"No."

"Not load?"

"No."

"Not trap?"

"No."

"Not running stairs?"

He looked at crutches.

"No."

Good theatre.

I said, "What happened?"

"Renn is sick."

A name I knew enough to place.

Prompt-side support.

Not friend.

Not stranger.

"How sick?"

"Enough that he sent word instead of arriving."

Reasonable threshold.

"What do you need covered?"

He had answer ready.

"Rehearsal reset, entrance holds, prop table, cue copy. Second half into evening if we keep schedule."

"Scene changes?"

"Light ones. Anything heavy gets two other hands."

"Who owns calls?"

"Stage lead."

"Who owns props?"

"Prop mistress until handoff."

"Who owns fly?"

"Fly crew."

"Then why me?"

He looked genuinely confused.

"You've done the room."

Right.

Not because I was secretly the best support specialist alive.

Because I had worked there before.

Useful distinction.

"Pay?"

"Three copper if you stay through evening close. Two if we release before supper."

There it was.

Three copper.

Not silver.

Not leverage.

Not life-changing.

Also not one-copper desperation work.

And I was twelve copper with a ten-copper floor.

Three mattered.

I said, "Food?"

"Staff pot if you're there through supper."

Better.

"Anything I need to bring?"

"Yourself. Pencil if you distrust ours."

"I distrust everyone's pencils."

"Then bring one."

Lyssa said, "He'll come."

I looked back.

She was pinning sleeve.

"You negotiate for me now?"

"I want you out of room before you explain elbows."

Customer said, "I would listen."

"No, you wouldn't."

The theatre man waited.

I thought about Hark & Venn.

Trial socket gone to shop.

Liner home.

Two crutches.

Shoulder improved.

No new skin problem.

No magic issue.

No reason not to work.

Also no reason to pretend this was capital strategy.

Sometimes three copper was three copper.

And sometimes returning to a place that had known you before you left was useful for reasons that did not fit under MONEY.

"I'll come."

"Second bell after midday."

"I know."

He nodded.

"Good."

Then left.

I closed door.

Lyssa said, "Three?"

"Three if evening."

"Good."

"You heard all of that."

"Small room."

"Privacy remains theoretical."

"Pay rent somewhere larger."

Cruel economic policy.

I got my notebook.

Not the road one.

The theatre pages were older.

Folded cue copies.

Prop notes.

One page with a stain I still hoped was soup.

I found enough blank paper.

Lyssa saw.

"You're smiling."

"No."

"You are."

"Bad lighting."

"It's morning."

"Exactly."

She smiled without looking up.

I hated that.

The walk to theatre was familiar enough to be annoying.

Carrow streets had already resumed demanding things from my body.

Uneven curb.

Delivery cart blocking half lane.

Two boys carrying a board sideways like civilization had not invented turning.

I arrived early because old habits had survived amputation, travel, capitalism, and several poor decisions.

The theatre smelled right.

Dust.

Lamp oil.

Old cloth.

Hot rope.

Paint.

A little mildew where the back wall always lost an argument with rain.

Nobody had fixed that while I was gone.

Comforting.

The front house was dark except for work lamps.

Onstage, three actors were arguing over a bench.

Not emotionally.

Literally.

One wanted it two feet left.

One wanted it where it was.

Third was sitting on it and refusing to participate in the geometry.

A stage lead I recognized by role more than friendship stood in aisle with cue book.

She looked at me.

"You're alive."

"Mostly."

"Good enough."

Excellent welcome.

She pointed prompt side.

"Renn's table."

I went.

The table was a crime scene.

Cue copy.

Two chalks.

Bell pull.

Prop handoff sheet.

Cup with three pencils.

One pencil broken.

A little wooden marker carved with ACT TWO.

And half an apple.

I looked at apple.

Stage lead said, "Not his."

"That creates more questions."

"Later."

Good.

I sat on the tall prompt stool.

Then got off immediately.

Too high for crutches.

I pulled a low chair instead.

That was the first adaptation.

No speech.

No dignity loss.

Chair.

The stage lead came over.

"Need anything?"

"Clear path from here to prop table. No loose cable across it."

She looked.

There was loose cable.

"That wasn't there yesterday."

"Still here today."

She kicked it aside, then stopped.

"Not enough."

Called a hand.

They rerouted it properly against wall.

Good.

I asked, "What changed?"

She opened cue copy.

"Act two entrance order. Third scene now starts with Nella already onstage. Door two sticks again. Bench moves after supper scene, not before. Bell cue seven moved under line instead of after it. We lost one lantern because somebody dropped it."

From stage, an actor yelled, "It slipped."

Stage lead yelled back, "Gravity remains undefeated."

The actor said, "I maintain the handle participated."

Different room.

Same species.

I wrote changes.

Not all theatre work needed mystical significance.

Sometimes the job was knowing which door was broken.

Rehearsal started.

For the first twenty minutes, my contribution was almost nothing.

Hold door two.

Mark missed entrance.

Reset letter prop.

Hand bell rope to stage lead when she came over.

Write down that the bench had moved too far downstage because one leg caught a floor seam.

Then the room became a room.

Actor in blue coat missed entrance because he was still arguing with wardrobe.

The woman playing Nella entered early because she had been told yesterday's order and nobody had reached her before call.

Prop cup appeared on wrong table.

One scene started without the red scarf because somebody had moved it to drying rail.

Nobody died.

Nobody was incompetent.

The system had changed in six small places and people were carrying different versions in their heads.

Ah.

There it was.

I knew that problem.

The stage lead stopped scene.

"Again from bell seven."

Actor on bench said, "From line?"

"From bell."

"Before line?"

"Under line."

"Which line?"

The stage lead closed her eyes.

I said, "Your line about the widow's garden."

He looked at me.

"Oh."

Nella said, "That is not my line."

"It is his."

"I know. I'm just saying nobody calls it that."

Actor said, "I call it that now."

Stage lead pointed at me.

"Write widow's garden in margin."

I did.

The cue copy became marginally worse and operationally better.

Good theatre.

At second run, Nella held entrance correctly.

Door two stuck.

I braced one crutch, pulled latch with free hand, and got it open.

Not elegant.

But I could do that with two crutches because I was not moving at same time.

Hands target.

There.

The work limb was absent and still changing how I thought about work.

I did not narrate that aloud.

Progress.

A prop hand arrived with red scarf.

"Where?"

"Second hook until supper scene."

"Why?"

"Because first hook is letter now."

He stared at hooks.

"There are three."

"Correct."

"Which is second?"

I looked.

Someone had removed little chalk numbers.

Of course.

I marked wall.

One.

Two.

Three.

Prop hand said, "That seems obvious."

"It becomes obvious after someone does it."

He hung scarf.

Thirty seconds later another hand tried to put cup on hook two.

I stopped him.

"Table."

"Renn uses hook."

"Not today."

"Why?"

"Scarf."

He looked at scarf.

"Who put scarf there?"

The first prop hand yelled from across room, "Me."

"Why?"

"Because Greg told me."

Now several people looked at me.

I raised pencil.

"Temporary government."

Nobody respected it.

Good.

By midday, the room had stabilized.

Not because I had solved theatre.

Because the current cue copy and current physical room finally matched.

That was enough.

We ate staff pot behind scene shop.

Beans.

Onion.

Something green that had surrendered identity.

Free.

I accepted.

One actor sat near me and talked for most of meal.

Not to me specifically.

To the air around us.

"I think the problem is the widow's garden line is too early because if I say it there then Nella's reaction belongs to information she has not emotionally received yet, which I know sounds like nonsense because she hears the line at same time, but emotionally the scene hasn't caught up, and yes I understand the bell is practical, but practical things still have dramatic consequences, and I am not saying move bell, I am saying maybe I need to arrive at the line differently."

He ate one spoonful.

Continued.

I said, "Tell the stage lead."

"I did."

"What did she say?"

"She said stop arriving differently every run."

Fair.

He frowned into beans.

"She may have a point."

"Terrible."

"I know."

He kept talking.

I listened enough.

Not every conversation needed winning.

After meal came scene-change rehearsal.

This was where my body mattered more.

The old version of my theatre work had involved carrying things because carrying things was available.

Current version did not.

I owned that before someone else had to.

Stage lead said, "Bench reset."

Two hands moved bench.

I did not.

"Lantern stand."

One hand reached for it.

I said, "I can place marks."

Stage lead nodded.

I chalked where feet belonged.

Crutch.

Bend carefully.

Mark.

Stand.

Slow.

Still useful.

One stagehand glanced at me, then at bench.

"You want lighter side?"

"No."

"You sure?"

"Yes."

He shrugged and kept moving.

That was good too.

No inspiration speech.

No pity contest.

The job changed.

At one point a narrow side table needed to shift only a foot.

I could have done it by hooking one hand under edge and hopping.

I did not.

Because that was stupid.

I called, "Table."

A hand moved it.

Then I reset papers on it.

Shared work.

Theatre did not require every person to prove maximum independence.

By late afternoon, the stage lead had stopped checking whether I knew what she meant by reset.

She would call:

"Garden."

I reset letter and bench marks.

"Door."

I checked latch.

"Seven."

I set bell rope and cue line.

"Red."

Scarf.

Compact language.

Earned language.

Not universal sarcasm.

Good.

The rehearsal itself got better.

That mattered.

The actors stopped tripping over changed entrances.

The bench stopped fighting floor seam.

Nella's early presence changed the scene in a way I actually liked.

She sat before the others entered and mended something small while listening to conversation she was pretending not to hear.

That gave the whole scene weight.

Not tactical weight.

Not foreshadowing weight.

Theatrical weight.

The audience would understand that she had been there before anyone acknowledged her.

Nice.

At one point I forgot to watch cue sheet because I watched her performance.

Bell seven came late.

Stage lead looked at me.

I rang.

Too late.

Scene stopped.

She said, "Greg."

"My fault."

Actor in blue said, "I thought it was emotionally interesting."

Stage lead said, "You think weather is emotionally interesting."

"It is."

"Again."

I wrote **WATCH THE DAMN CUE** beside bell seven.

Theatre remained capable of punishing abstraction.

Second attempt clean.

Before evening run, wardrobe sent down a coat with one cuff pinned wrong.

Not my department.

It became my problem because the actor wearing it was due through door two in less than a minute and the loose pin was aimed directly at his wrist.

I said, "Stop."

He stopped.

Good actor.

I pointed.

He looked down.

"Oh."

Wardrobe hand came over at speed, saw pin, and said, "Who did that?"

Actor said, "I assumed you."

She looked offended enough that I almost apologized on his behalf.

"I pin inward."

"Apparently someone pins politically."

"Hold still."

She fixed cuff in twelve seconds.

Then looked at me.

"Thank you."

"Selfishly motivated. Blood complicates props."

She did not laugh.

The actor did.

Door cue came.

He went.

No injury.

No lesson.

Just one more tiny failure that became nothing because someone noticed before it became expensive.

Evening close happened after lamps came on.

No audience.

Just rehearsal.

But the room had shape again.

Stage lead signed the day's call sheet.

Then counted three copper into my hand.

No ceremony.

Twelve became fifteen.

She said, "Renn may be back tomorrow."

"Good."

"You available if he isn't?"

There.

Potential repeat.

Not entitlement.

I said, "Send word."

"Here or home?"

"Home."

She stared.

"I know where that is."

"Apparently everyone does now."

"Your woman explained."

Of course.

"What did she say?"

"That if we lost you again, we should charge postage to your pay."

I nodded.

"Fair."

The theatre man from morning was coiling a cable nearby.

He said, "She said more than that."

"I don't need all evidence."

"Growth."

I looked at him.

He grinned.

Traitor institution.

I put three copper away.

Fifteen.

Five above floor.

Still twenty-three Vale.

Still no debt payment.

Still nowhere near clearing work-limb balance safely.

But the cash floor had room again.

More importantly, I had worked a known place without turning myself back into who I had been before leaving.

I had not carried the bench.

I had not climbed fly stairs.

I had not hidden the crutches.

I had not made the room accommodate me through vague goodwill either.

I had asked for clear path.

Used chair.

Taken prompt-side work.

Called other hands for moving things.

Done the parts I could do well.

That was not lesser theatre.

It was just current theatre.

On walk home, my shoulders were tired.

Not injured.

Hands a little sore.

Right leg had done a full day of city standing and short movement.

Residual limb ordinary.

No trial wear because shop had hardware.

Protocol unchanged.

Fifty-nine.

Fifty-three.

One hundred thirty-nine.

No magic.

No artifact.

No clever deal.

Three copper for one skilled substitution.

Fine.

At home, Lyssa was sewing my coat.

She looked up.

"How much?"

"Three."

"Good."

"How was your sleeve war?"

"She wanted it tighter."

"Did you make it tighter?"

"No."

"Victory."

"She comes back tomorrow."

"War continues."

Lyssa bit thread.

Then looked at my face.

"You had fun."

"No."

"You did."

"I was paid."

"That isn't answer."

I put purse on table.

"Rehearsal improved."

There.

She smiled.

Not huge.

Enough.

"What were you doing?"

"Prompt side."

"Meaning?"

"Stopping adults from putting red cloth in wrong places."

"Heroic."

"At scale."

She threw a scrap at me.

I caught it.

With one hand.

Because the other was on crutch.

I looked at scrap.

Then at her.

She said, "Do not make that meaningful."

"Wasn't going to."

"Liar."

Maybe.

I sat.

Fifteen copper.

Twenty-three with Vale.

Bits unspecified.

Ten floor.

Five above.

Hark & Venn working offscreen.

Theatre maybe tomorrow, maybe not.

Home still home.

I opened notebook.

Wrote:

**THEATRE. PROMPT-SIDE SUBSTITUTE. 3C. CURRENT CUE/ROOM ALIGNMENT. NO HEAVY SHIFT.**

Then:

**Useful work did not require pretending old body.**

I considered that.

Too neat.

Crossed it out.

Wrote instead:

**Door two still sticks.**

Better.

That was enough.
