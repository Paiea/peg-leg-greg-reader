CHAPTER 426

THE INTERRUPTER

Hessa's note arrived folded around one sentence.

**COME SHOW ME WHAT YOU DID, NOT WHAT YOU THINK YOU DID.**

Comforting.

There was a second line underneath.

**BRING THE NOTEBOOK. DO NOT PRACTICE FIRST.**

Less comforting.

I showed Lyssa.

She read both lines.

"She knows you."

"Everybody is becoming hostile."

"Maybe you're consistent."

"That is not defense."

"It wasn't."

I had fifteen copper.

Twenty-three with Vale.

Bits unspecified.

Ten floor.

Five above it.

Hark & Venn had the trial socket, temporary shaft, and test foot.

I had the liner.

Theatre might want me again if Renn stayed sick.

Nobody had sent word.

Good.

One day could contain one thing.

Apparently revolutionary.

I packed the magic notebook.

Not the road notebook.

They had become separate mostly because the road notebook was fat enough to injure somebody.

The magic pages were thinner and somehow more embarrassing.

Numbers had that effect.

Fifty-nine supervised minimal draws.

Fifty-three deliberate shaping attempts.

One hundred thirty-nine external-effect attempts.

The last number had become dangerous in my head.

Not because it was high.

Because one of those attempts had mattered outside a room.

Emergency.

Bad footing.

Bad timing.

A motion I had not wanted continuing.

I had pushed.

Something had moved enough differently that I stayed upright.

Useful.

Also terrible evidence.

Hessa's favorite kind of problem.

I left home on two crutches.

No trial limb.

No magic on the way.

No private warm-up in an alley because I had been specifically told not to be stupid in writing.

Growth.

Hessa's workroom looked almost unchanged.

That was reassuring until I noticed the table had moved.

Half a pace left.

I stopped in the doorway.

"Table."

"What about it?"

"It moved."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because I moved it."

Excellent investigation.

Hessa held out a hand.

"Notebook."

I gave it to her.

That always hurt.

She opened near the back.

Not the old marked-strip pages.

The newer pages.

Travel.

Attempts.

Failures.

The emergency entry.

She read in silence.

I stayed standing until she pointed at chair.

"Sit."

I sat.

She kept reading.

No greeting.

No how was the east.

No congratulations for not dying.

Good specialist.

After a while she said, "One hundred thirty-nine."

"Yes."

"Why is one hundred thirty-nine not supervised?"

"Because the hill refused scheduling."

She looked at me.

"Again."

"Bad footing. Load shifted. I was already moving. I used the external effect because falling was worse."

"Target?"

"Not a target in the room sense."

"Greg."

"My own relation to the moving load and crutch plant."

"That is still not a target."

"I know."

"What did you intend?"

There.

Harder.

"Interrupt the motion."

"Which motion?"

"The part that was continuing wrong."

"That is a sentence you wrote after surviving."

Fair.

I thought.

The actual moment had been faster.

No philosophy.

No protocol board.

Crutch tip moved.

Weight went somewhere it should not.

Body followed.

Then external effort.

"Rightward continuation," I said. "Relative to where I needed the plant."

"Better."

"Not good?"

"Better."

She turned notebook around.

My emergency entry was narrow.

Good.

No heroic paragraph.

No claim of force.

No claim of distance.

No claim that magic saved me by itself.

She tapped it.

"You wrote deflection."

"Yes."

"Do you know it deflected?"

"No."

"Do you know your body corrected?"

"Yes."

"Do you know the magic contributed?"

I hated this one.

"I know I attempted it."

"Correct."

"And the outcome changed."

"Many things changed."

"Also correct."

She looked pleased.

Terrible woman.

"You have been thinking about this for weeks."

"Yes."

"How often?"

"Enough."

"Have you tested it?"

"No."

"Good."

That sounded like praise.

I distrusted it.

She got up.

The old marked strip was not on the main table.

Instead there was a shallow wooden track about an arm long, set on two blocks.

At one end sat a small round wooden shuttle with a metal washer fixed into its center.

No glow.

No runes.

No theatrical significance.

I pointed.

"New toy?"

"Old drawer."

"What does it do?"

"Rolls badly."

"Specialist equipment."

"Very."

She lifted one end of the track by a finger width.

The shuttle rolled down.

Slow.

Straight enough.

Hit a padded stop.

She reset it.

"Today is not about making that move."

"Good."

"Today is not about stopping it."

Less good.

"Today is not about your emergency."

"Then why am I here?"

"Because you keep naming outcomes before you know which condition matters."

Hostile.

Accurate.

She put a chalk mark halfway down the track.

Another near the end.

Then a small upright card beside the middle.

"External only."

"So one-forty."

"If you attempt."

"Minimal and shaping stay fifty-nine and fifty-three."

"Yes."

Good.

Numbers.

Rules.

I liked rules when they were trying to stop me from lying.

Hessa said, "First condition. Shuttle at rest. Try to move it sideways."

"How much?"

"Enough that I can see it."

"Direction?"

"Toward window."

"Distance?"

"I said enough that I can see it."

Annoying.

I planted crutches beside chair and sat forward.

Shuttle at rest.

External effect had always been the strange lane.

Not touching.

Not shaping a held thing.

Not drawing something into myself.

Changing something out there.

Sometimes.

Badly.

I settled.

Looked at shuttle.

Attempted.

Nothing.

Hessa watched shuttle.

Not me.

Good.

"Count it."

"One-forty."

She wrote.

"Again?"

"No."

I looked up.

"One attempt?"

"Different condition."

Of course.

She reset track level.

"At rest taught us what?"

"Nothing visible under one attempt."

"Good."

She lifted track.

Shuttle rolled slowly.

"Do not touch yet."

It passed first chalk mark.

Stopped at padded end.

"Now what?"

"Same direction. Toward window. Attempt only after it passes middle mark."

Motion already happening.

There.

She reset.

Lift.

Roll.

The shuttle crossed first line.

Middle.

I attempted.

Nothing obvious.

It reached pad.

Hessa crouched.

Looked at track.

"One-forty-one."

"Nothing?"

"Did I say nothing?"

"No."

"Then don't help."

She reset.

This time she put a thin line of charcoal along the shuttle's near edge.

"Why?"

"Because eyes lie."

"Mine?"

"Everyone's."

Better.

She ran it again without magic.

The charcoal edge stayed nearly parallel to track.

Tiny wobble from bad wheel.

Baseline.

Again.

"One-forty-two. Same timing."

I waited.

Middle line.

Attempt.

The shuttle ticked.

Small.

Not dramatic.

Its leading edge angled maybe a finger toward window before padded stop.

I froze.

Hessa did not.

She marked where it struck padding.

Then ran finger along track.

"Again."

"You saw that."

"I saw a different end position."

"That's what I said."

"No. You said that with your face."

Rude.

Reset.

"One-forty-three."

Same track.

Same lift.

Same direction.

Middle line.

Attempt.

Nothing.

Straight to pad.

I exhaled.

Hessa wrote.

"Good."

"That was failure."

"Excellent observation."

"You said good."

"Because now you have two different outcomes under similar conditions."

"Which is worse."

"Which is information."

There was Hessa.

I leaned back.

Shoulders fine.

No body strain worth naming.

Magic effort had that familiar internal thinness.

Not pain.

More like reaching with something that had never learned anatomy.

Hessa watched me.

"Any change?"

"Small fatigue."

"Where?"

I pointed vaguely at center chest, then corrected.

"No. Not chest exactly. General."

"Write general."

I did.

She took a small wedge and changed track angle slightly.

Steeper.

"Why?"

"Speed."

"Same timing?"

"Same mark."

"So less time remaining after attempt."

"Yes."

"Direction?"

"Toward window."

She reset shuttle.

"One-forty-four."

It rolled faster.

Middle.

Attempt.

The magic felt late.

Not because I had missed line.

Because the thing already had more certainty.

That was not technical.

I hated the word.

The shuttle reached end.

Maybe touched padding slightly left of baseline.

Maybe not.

Hessa marked it as **unclear**.

Good.

"No clean claim."

"No."

She flattened track again.

Then did something I did not expect.

Turned the entire track around.

Now shuttle would roll the opposite direction through room.

I looked at her.

"Window direction changes relative to track."

"Yes."

"So you want same room direction?"

"No."

"What direction?"

"Right of travel."

There.

Different reference.

Not toward window.

Relative to moving object.

"Why?"

"Because your emergency description was relative to motion, not room."

That made my spine straighten.

Hessa noticed.

"Do not become excited."

"Impossible instruction."

"Try."

She ran baseline once.

Shuttle rolled opposite direction.

Same poor little wobble.

Reset.

"One-forty-five. After middle. Right of travel."

I waited.

Middle.

Attempt.

Nothing.

Pad.

Hessa marked.

Again.

"One-forty-six."

"How many today?"

"Until I stop."

Strong ethics.

Roll.

Middle.

Attempt.

The shuttle gave a tiny sideways jerk.

Not enough to leave track.

Enough that charcoal edge changed.

Right of travel.

I stared.

Hessa caught it before I said anything.

"Do not celebrate."

"Wasn't."

"You inhaled like a gambler."

"Breathing remains medically necessary."

"Reset your face."

I did not know how.

She measured no distance.

Good.

There was no useful ruler claim here.

Only visible deviation.

She marked **clean visible lateral change / moving / right-of-travel condition**.

One clean.

One.

Out of what?

Not enough.

She reset track level.

Then set shuttle at rest again.

"One-forty-seven. Right relative to what?"

I looked at stationary shuttle.

"Nothing."

"Exactly."

"Room right?"

"Choose."

"Room right."

Attempt.

Nothing.

Hessa wrote.

She folded hands.

"Now tell me what happened."

Trap.

I knew trap.

"Across eight external attempts today, two moving trials showed clean visible lateral changes?"

She raised eyebrow.

I counted.

140 rest no response.
141 moving no obvious.
142 moving clean.
143 moving none.
144 faster moving unclear.
145 reversed moving none.
146 reversed moving clean.
147 rest none.

Eight.

"Two clean moving deviations," I said. "One unclear at higher speed. Rest attempts no visible response. Other moving attempts no visible response."

"Good."

"That suggests motion matters."

"No."

I closed eyes.

"That suggests motion might matter."

"Better."

"Could be timing."

"Yes."

"Could be attention."

"Yes."

"Could be track vibration."

"Yes."

"Could be random variation in whatever the hell I'm doing."

"Yes."

"Could be target construction."

"Yes."

"You enjoy this."

"Immensely."

I believed her.

She pulled the marked strip from a drawer.

There it was.

Old enemy.

The strip from sessions where left, right, whole had behaved badly enough to create several weeks of restraint.

She set it down.

I looked.

"Are we using that?"

"No."

"Then why bring it out?"

"Comparison."

She opened older page.

Attempt seventy-six.

Left marked region, no response.

Seventy-seven.

Right marked region, clean small rotation, right end away relative to left.

Seventy-eight.

Whole strip, no response.

Hessa tapped page.

"What did you want seventy-seven to mean?"

"That I could affect part of object."

"What did it prove?"

"One clean rotation happened under right-marked attempt."

"Anything else?"

"No."

"What did you want one hundred thirty-nine to mean?"

"Emergency deflection."

"What did it prove?"

"That I attempted external effect during a bad motion and the situation ended differently than it might have."

She waited.

I sighed.

"Which proves almost nothing about mechanism."

"Good."

"Today?"

"Today gives me a reason to test moving targets again."

Not Greg.

Her.

Hessa's protocol.

Important.

"You have a next protocol."

"I have a candidate."

I smiled.

She pointed at me.

"That expression is why I use words carefully."

"Candidate protocol."

"Moving target. Fixed track. Known baseline. Variable timing later, not today."

"Direction reference?"

"Both room-fixed and travel-relative eventually."

"Speed?"

"Later."

"Mass?"

"Much later."

"Practical object?"

"No."

"Application?"

"No."

"Very hostile."

"Alive."

I looked at shuttle.

Small wooden disk.

Washer.

Bad wheel.

Two clean deviations.

Eight attempts.

No practical use.

And still.

It was first time in a while the external lane had felt like it was narrowing toward something I recognized.

Not force.

Not throwing.

Not invisible hand.

Interruption.

Maybe.

A motion exists.

A bad line exists.

Change enough that somebody gets another second.

A cart rolls wrong.

A blade travels wrong.

A falling tool misses foot.

A door swings.

A person loses balance.

No.

Too fast.

Too many applications.

I caught myself.

Hessa saw that too.

"What are you doing?"

"Ruining it with usefulness."

"Correct."

"I was imagining cases."

"Stop."

"Stopped."

"Your problem is you have sixty years of uses waiting to colonize one clean event."

"Fifty-nine."

"What?"

"Old age."

She stared.

I stared back.

Then she said, "That was not the important number."

Fair.

I looked at notebook.

External total now one hundred forty-seven.

Minimal fifty-nine.

Shaping fifty-three.

Protocol changed numerically.

Capability did not change nearly that much.

That was healthy.

"Do we call it interruption?"

"No."

"Chapter title problem."

"What?"

"Nothing."

She took notebook back and wrote one line beneath results.

**NEXT CANDIDATE: MOVING-TARGET EXTERNAL TEST. DO NOT GENERALIZE TO STOPPING, DEFLECTION, FORCE, OR PRACTICAL USE.**

Then another:

**NO INDEPENDENT TESTING.**

I said, "You really know me."

"I know literacy is imperfect."

"Cruel."

"Useful."

She closed notebook.

Session over.

That surprised me.

"We're done?"

"Yes."

"Eight attempts."

"Yes."

"I can do more."

"I know."

"That usually isn't reason to stop."

"It often is."

There it was.

Same lesson as work limb.

Same lesson as roads.

Same lesson as money, apparently.

Capacity did not require immediate expenditure.

Terrible universe.

I stood.

Crutches.

Body fine.

Magic fatigue light.

No dizziness.

No headache.

No hand tremor.

No visible residual effect.

Hessa watched the stand anyway.

"Shoulder?"

"Improved."

"Leg?"

"Shop has trial hardware."

"Good."

"You don't know details."

"I don't need details to know you aren't wearing it."

Fair.

"Final work pending."

"Then leave it pending."

Everyone in my life had formed conspiracy against acceleration.

I took notebook.

At door, Hessa said, "Greg."

I turned.

"One hundred thirty-nine."

"Yes."

"Emergency use was not permission."

"I know."

"Do you?"

I thought about hill.

Bad plant.

Motion going wrong.

The fact I would do it again if choice was fall or attempt.

Honest answer.

"If the same emergency happened, I might use it again."

Hessa nodded.

Not approval.

Not anger.

"Then write that as your rule, not mine."

That stopped me.

Institutional boundary.

Ethical boundary.

Evidence boundary.

She was not going to pretend she controlled what I did alone.

And I was not going to pretend my choices were her authorization.

"Fair."

"Good."

I wrote before leaving:

**Outside-session restriction remains. Emergency exception is my judgment/risk, not Hessa clearance. If used, record narrowest possible facts.**

She read over shoulder.

"Better."

"Still hostile?"

"Always."

I left.

Carrow afternoon was ordinary enough to insult magic.

A bread cart blocked half street.

Someone had hung sheets between windows and created a public tunnel.

Two men argued over a wheel.

A child tried to drag a stick longer than he was tall.

No one needed external interruption.

Good.

I bought lunch with bits.

No copper moved.

Fifteen stayed fifteen.

At home, Lyssa had three lengths of brown cloth across table and a customer who was explaining sleeves with both arms.

I stopped inside door.

Customer said, "I want it to fall naturally but still hold shape."

Lyssa said, "Those are different requests."

"I know."

"No, you keep combining them."

"I mean naturally here."

She grabbed his wrist.

"Your arm is not naturally here. You are posing."

He lowered arm.

"Oh."

Lyssa looked at me.

"Magic?"

"Two clean things happened."

Her face became suspicious.

"Out of?"

"Eight."

"That sounds less impressive."

"Thank you."

"What things?"

"Small moving target changed sideways."

"Useful?"

"Hessa says I'm not allowed to ask that yet."

Lyssa nodded.

"I like her."

"Everyone does."

"I didn't say I like you."

Customer looked between us.

"Are you together?"

Lyssa said, "Unfortunately."

I put notebook away.

Not behind road reference.

Not beside Hark & Venn liner.

Different drawer.

Magic could remain its own problem.

I checked the numbers once before dinner.

Fifty-nine.

Fifty-three.

One hundred forty-seven.

That last number was bigger.

The claim was smaller.

Two clean visible changes on a moving wooden shuttle out of eight attempts under controlled conditions.

Rest attempts did nothing visible.

One faster trial unclear.

No stopping.

No force measurement.

No practical use.

No independent testing.

Candidate next protocol existed now.

That was progress.

Annoyingly good progress.

I had spent weeks wanting magic to become useful again.

Maybe useful did not begin with stronger.

Maybe it began with knowing what kind of mistake it was willing to interrupt.

I crossed out **interrupt**.

Too early.

Wrote:

**Motion may matter.**

Hessa would approve.

Which was unbearable.

Cash fifteen.

Vale twenty-three.

Bits unspecified.

Protocol fifty-nine, fifty-three, one hundred forty-seven.

No income.

No expense in copper.

No limb update.

No theatre update.

No artifact.

No debt payment.

Home in Carrow.

One candidate protocol.

Two clean little deviations.

And absolutely no permission to turn either into a spell.

Good.
