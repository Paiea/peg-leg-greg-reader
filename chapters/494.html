CHAPTER 383

THE BIDDER

Duskport gave me three days to wait.

This was dangerous.

Not because city.

Because waiting with money was historically how I acquired problems.

I woke at Anchor's End before breakfast and checked my left leg.

Skin clean.

Shoulders normal.

Right calf normal.

No reason to improve anything.

Excellent.

I had become boring enough for prosthetics.

Pell was behind counter.

"You working today?"

"No."

He stopped wiping cup.

"Sick?"

"Client."

"Still?"

"Apparently status lasts more than one day."

He considered that.

"Dangerous."

Correct.

Breakfast cost ordinary money.

I paid it.

Then sat with tea and no job board.

That lasted seven minutes.

Not bad.

A man at next table unfolded a printed sale sheet.

I did not read over shoulder.

Growth.

Then he turned it around to show Pell.

Growth ended.

**DUSKPORT HARBOR SURVEY RETIREMENT SALE**

There.

Not artifact sale.

Better.

Retirement sale meant people knew what things were supposed to do.

Usually.

The man tapped bottom.

"They moved viewing to North Instrument Hall. Roof leak at old office."

Pell said, "Again?"

"Again."

"They should retire roof."

The man laughed.

I drank tea.

Did not ask.

Ten seconds.

Then:

"Public?"

Both looked at me.

Pell said, "There he is."

"What?"

"Client died."

"Temporarily."

The man slid sheet across table.

Public viewing today.

Bids close tomorrow at fourth bell.

Collection within five days.

Deposit required on lots above one silver.

Items sold as retired, damaged, obsolete, incomplete, or surplus unless specifically certified otherwise.

Excellent language.

No miracles promised.

I read lots.

Mostly instruments.

Range rods.

Chain reels.

Signal mirrors.

Two cracked wind-bowl throats.

A tide recorder missing float.

Three brass housings without internal assemblies.

One sealed lot of survey notebooks with public-copy restriction.

No.

Then:

**LOT 31. VEYR COASTAL RESPONSE COMPASS, EARLY PATTERN. INTERMITTENT NORTH HOLD. HOUSING SOUND. GLASS CHIPPED. RETIRED AFTER REPEATED CROSSWIND ERROR. WITH CASE. RESERVE 4S.**

I stopped.

Veyr.

Maybe.

Name did something.

Not enough.

I read again.

Veyr.

First life memory was not library.

It was attic after earthquake.

Boxes.

Dust.

A captain swearing because somebody had thrown away old navigation junk worth more than house.

Veyr instruments.

Later ones.

Military collectors loved them.

No.

Not collectors.

Navigators.

Something about response glass.

Early pattern.

Crosswind error.

I knew shape of importance without exact fact.

Dangerous kind of knowing.

Four silver reserve.

Not cheap.

Good.

The market was not asleep.

I finished tea.

Pell watched.

"Road face?"

"Purchase face."

"Worse."

"Probably."

North Instrument Hall sat three streets uphill from harbor office.

Duskport remained committed to stairs as economic policy.

I used cargo ramp behind cooper row.

Longer.

Better.

No one congratulated me.

Inside hall, twenty-eight people were already looking at thirty-seven lots.

Excellent.

Public opportunity.

Not mine.

A clerk at entrance handed me bid card after I showed coin enough for deposit.

Not all money.

Enough.

"Inspection only," she said. "No opening sealed housings. No activation beyond posted test. Questions to survey appraiser, not clerks."

"Posted test?"

She pointed.

Each lot had card.

Good system.

I started at one.

Not thirty-one.

This was discipline.

Lot one was chain reel.

Lot two mirror.

Lot three another mirror.

By lot six discipline had become theater.

I went to thirty-one.

Compass sat in open walnut case.

Brass body about palm width.

Thick.

Ugly by later standards.

Glass chipped near edge exactly as listing said.

Inside, ordinary needle floated above pale circular plate.

Around plate was narrow gray-green ring.

Not decorative.

Probably.

A woman beside me held loupe.

She looked at my crutches, then compass, then back to loupe.

No conversation.

Good.

Posted card:

**CURRENT TEST: stable north hold indoors at rest. Under fan crosswind, indicated heading drifts 8-14 degrees and may recover late. No certification for navigation. Response ring intact by visual inspection. No guarantee of magical function beyond observed behavior.**

There.

Seller knew.

Four silver reserve for a compass that failed in wind was still aggressive.

Unless housing, maker, ring, or parts had value.

I asked appraiser.

He was gray-haired, missing tip of left ear, and offended by everyone equally.

Promising.

"Lot thirty-one. Why four?"

"Veyr housing. Early response ring. Case original enough."

"Enough?"

"Latch replaced. Lining isn't."

"Ring useful?"

"If you repair heading error."

"Can it be repaired?"

"If I knew cheaply, reserve would not be four."

Excellent answer.

"Parts value?"

"Two to three to instrument shop. More if someone needs exact early ring dimensions."

"Collector value?"

He shrugged.

"Maybe. Duskport likes things that work."

Also excellent.

No idiot.

I looked again.

Veyr.

Crosswind error.

Something tugged.

First life.

Not famous compass.

Famous failure.

Maybe.

I closed eyes.

Bad habit in public.

Memory came sideways.

A lecture I had not attended willingly.

Old officer with scar across mouth.

Talking about directional artifacts.

Early Veyr rings were too responsive to moving air?

No.

That sounded like current listing pretending to be memory.

Reject.

What did I actually remember?

Veyr later built storm instruments.

Something used on skyships?

Wrong century maybe.

Response glass that did not point north.

It pointed...

No.

I opened eyes.

The woman with loupe had moved to hinge.

She asked appraiser, "Ring removable without cutting housing?"

"Not cleanly."

"Then no."

She left.

Useful.

Another buyer arrived.

Young man in blue coat.

Instrument shop badge.

He did not look at compass face first.

He looked underneath.

Then case.

Then card.

Market awake.

Good.

I asked, "Can I see posted crosswind test?"

Appraiser pointed at schedule.

Lot demonstrations every half bell.

Next in twelve minutes.

I waited.

Did not touch other things.

Mostly.

Lot thirty-two was a cracked tide lens.

I touched with eyes.

Allowed.

At demonstration, clerk placed compass on marked table.

Needle settled north.

Then another clerk turned hand fan apparatus.

Not magic.

Leather vanes.

Steady side wind.

Needle drifted.

Nine degrees maybe.

Then eleven.

Stopped.

Wind stopped.

Needle returned slowly.

Bad compass.

Everyone saw bad compass.

I saw something else.

Not because genius.

Because the ring moved first.

Barely.

Gray-green response ring under plate brightened on windward side before needle drifted.

Then brightness traveled around rim.

Needle followed late.

I leaned.

Appraiser said, "Line."

I was over floor mark.

I moved back.

"Does ring always do that?"

"Do what?"

"Brighten windward before heading moves."

He looked at compass.

"Ring responds before needle, yes. That's part of defect."

Part of defect.

My brain became unpleasant.

"How early?"

"Depends wind."

"Consistent?"

"Enough that old survey notes complain about it."

"Can I read notes?"

"Lot file. Clerk has copy."

There.

Not secret.

I got copy.

Three pages.

Retirement history.

Compass had served eleven years.

Crosswind drift worsened over last four.

Repairs attempted twice.

Needle pivot replaced.

Housing reseated.

Response ring not replaced because early Veyr dimensions no longer standard.

Then one line:

**Observed ring flare precedes indicated drift by approximately one to three breaths under strong lateral gust. Considered nuisance artifact; not actionable for heading correction.**

I read it again.

One to three breaths.

Before indicated drift.

Not north compass.

Wind-change warning.

Maybe.

No.

Observation first.

Attribution later.

Ring flare preceded compass error under test.

That was established.

Could be useful as crosswind indicator.

Road bowls already did similar job with sound.

But portable?

If ring reacted to lateral airflow before needle movement, maybe it was sensing change rather than direction.

Maybe current defect was output.

Maybe later Veyr storm instruments came from exactly this failure.

Memory sparked.

There.

A museum plaque.

Not sure where.

**VEYR PRECURSOR RESPONSE RINGS.**

I had seen one behind glass in first life.

Or read about one.

Could be false memory assembled from current words.

Annoying brain.

He looked at me like I had asked why spoon wasn't boat.

"Because it is a compass."

"Bad compass."

"Correct."

"Ring changes before drift."

"Correct."

"Could that be useful separately?"

"Maybe. If you want to carry bad compass to learn wind exists."

Fair.

Duskport had skin.

Road bowls.

Flags.

Sails.

A portable one-to-three-breath warning mattered only in certain conditions.

Maybe high crosswind.

Maybe enclosed work.

Maybe projectile timing.

Hessa.

No.

Not independent testing.

Do not annex artifact into magic experiment because both interesting.

I hated memory sometimes.

Blue-coat buyer had returned.

He wrote something on bid card.

Clerk stamped.

First bid.

I did not ask amount.

Board updated five minutes later.

**LOT 31 CURRENT HIGH: 4S2C.**

There.

Market.

I had enough.

Probably.

Four silver plus travel plus future limb balance.

Still not poverty.

Still large.

Object might be worth two or three silver in parts if wrong.

Potential downside bounded but ugly.

Could maybe resell.

Could maybe not.

No exact purse conversion.

Good.

This time actually.

Survey chain reel, underpriced maybe half silver.

Could resell.

Boring.

Useful.

No special edge.

Lot thirty-four was sealed brass housing with no internals.

Beautiful trash.

High bid 4s2c.

A second bid appeared.

4s5c.

Not blue coat.

Someone else.

Good.

Deposit one silver once bid exceeded four.

Refunded if lost.

I put down one silver deposit.

Real money left hand.

**4S7C.**

Clerk stamped.

No music.

No ownership.

Just current high bid.

I hated auctions immediately.

At noon, somebody made it 4s9c.

I ate bread outside hall because leaving felt strategically irresponsible.

This was stupid.

Bids closed tomorrow.

I had an entire day.

Still I sat where board visible through door.

"Buying?"

"Trying."

"What?"

"Bad compass."

"Good city for that."

Duskport civic identity remained strong.

4s9c.
