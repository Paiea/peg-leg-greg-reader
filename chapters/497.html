CHAPTER 386

THE CORRESPONDENT

Mail found me at breakfast.

This was suspicious.

Pell held envelope between two fingers.

"Greg?"

"Unfortunately."

"Carrow."

I took it.

Lyssa's handwriting on front.

Not Lakeward.

My body relaxed before I decided whether that was reasonable.

Pell noticed.

Of course.

He went away without comment.

Growth everywhere.

I opened envelope.

Two pages.

First line:

**Tavi says if you can carry soup now, you owe him soup.**

I laughed.

There.

News traveled faster than dignity.

Then:

**I told him you carried water and one drop escaped. He says water is easier because soup has consequences. I don't know what this means. Neither does he.**

Good.

Next paragraph was not about me.

Better.

Lyssa had picked up two extra evening shifts because a woman named Sera had gone home to help sick mother.

Tavi had been staying with neighbor after school hours.

One of market awnings tore in wind and nearly took three baskets of onions with it.

Antonius had apparently argued with cooper over invoice and won by becoming quieter.

That sounded correct.

Theatre had put up notice for a short harbor comedy next week.

Lyssa might go if shifts allowed.

No promise.

No Greg-shaped hole in every sentence.

I read slower.

Then request.

**Can you check whether Duskport has the narrow blue binding cord you bought last winter? Carrow seller hasn't had it for two weeks. Don't buy a crate. I need maybe six arm lengths. If it's expensive, leave it.**

There.

Task.

Not rescue.

Not confession.

Cord.

I knew exactly shop.

Maybe.

Dangerous phrase.

I finished letter.

Last line:

**Tell Masta I support soup science only if she does.**

I folded page.

Then unfolded.

Read Tavi line again.

I could write entire fitting.

Rail lengths.

Pressure.

Posterior relief.

Foot alignment.

Wrong table.

One drop.

No take-home.

Two days.

I could explain why it mattered.

How hands felt.

How old solution under arms stopped feeling like defeat.

How carrying two cups had somehow been larger than first unsupported step.

No.

Letter did not need essay.

I ate eggs.

Then went looking for cord.

Not immediately.

After tea.

Important distinction.

Duskport textile quarter sat inland from sailmakers.

Not really quarter.

Two streets and alley pretending to be district.

Blue binding cord existed in first shop.

Wrong blue.

Too bright.

Second shop had right color but thick.

Third had narrow cord in five colors.

One looked correct.

I did not buy crate.

Heroism.

"Six arm lengths," I said.

Shop woman looked at my arms.

"Whose?"

Fair.

"Twelve feet."

"That's not six of yours."

"I revised standard."

She measured.

Price three bits.

I had no idea if expensive relative to Carrow cord.

Lyssa had said if expensive leave it.

Three bits for twelve feet felt not expensive.

Dangerous intuition.

"Usual?" I asked.

"For dyed narrow cotton? Yes."

"Any cheaper undyed?"

"Half."

"Need blue."

"Then you need blue price."

Excellent economy.

I bought it.

Not crate.

Cord fit pocket.

I considered sending immediately.

Post office could.

But I would likely return Carrow after next fitting unless Masta changed plan.

Two days.

Could carry myself.

No urgency stated.

I wrote reply at Anchor's End.

**Lyssa,**

Then stopped.

Too formal.

Crossed out.

New sheet.

**Found cord. Narrow blue, twelve feet, three bits. If wrong blue I will claim Duskport lighting fraud.**

Good.

**Fitting went better. Masta still has leg. I carried two cups with water across room after adjustment. One earlier attempt ended on wrong table because I started reaching for support and pressure changed. Masta said putting it down was correct. Next fitting in two days.**

Enough.

I wanted to write:

**It felt good.**

I wrote it.

Then stared.

Why was that harder than posterior pressure report?

Annoying.

I left it.

**Tell Tavi soup remains advanced certification.**

Then ordinary things.

Anchor's End food decent.

Pell insults clients equally.

Duskport still vertical.

I had subscribed to an instrument broker's sale notices after losing an auction for broken compass.

That sentence looked bad.

I added:

**I did not buy the broken compass. This is important context.**

Better.

No Lakeward paragraph.

Nothing had happened.

She already knew facts I had disclosed.

Silence was not update.

No need to send anxiety as information.

I folded reply.

Then remembered cord.

Could send cord with letter.

Would cost postage.

Could carry in two days.

Her request was maybe six arm lengths because Carrow seller out two weeks.

If I waited two days, still useful.

But what if fitting extended stay?

Unknown.

Three bits cord.

Small packet postage maybe worth option.

I went post counter.

Asked.

One bit to Carrow with letter at this weight.

Fine.

Sent.

There.

Task complete.

I walked harbor afterward.

Not because chapter needed event.

Because afternoon existed.

A crew was replacing warped plank on loading ramp.

I watched from outside work area.

One man carried long board with both hands.

Normal.

He turned sideways through gap.

Normal.

I noticed his feet.

Then stopped noticing.

Important maybe.

Not every two-handed carry was prophecy.

I bought fried fish.

Both crutches made carrying paper tray annoying.

Same as yesterday.

I tucked one crutch under arm, held food, moved badly three steps to wall, then ate standing.

No lesson.

Just logistics.

Could have asked vendor carry it.

Did not need.

Could have waited for table.

Did not want.

Body remained body.

At second bell, rain started.

Duskport rain came sideways because city had opinions.

I went back Anchor's End.

Pell was moving stools away from leaking window.

I helped by holding door while he carried one.

One hand.

Crutch under other.

Nothing transformative.

Then another guest came downstairs and took second stool without asking.

Pell said, "Thanks."

Not to me specifically.

Good.

We moved three things.

Rain stopped.

Entire crisis.

At dinner no mail.

Of course.

I had sent letter six hours ago.

Brain still checked.

Stupid.

I went upstairs.

Liner folded on chair.

Crutches against bed.

Tomorrow was empty day.

Then fitting after.

Could find work.

Could find another sale.

Could visit Merrin.

Could manufacture importance until calendar surrendered.

Instead I opened notebook.

Wrote:

**CORD SENT.**

Then:

**NO LAKEWARD WORD.**

Then crossed second line out.

No word was not event.

That distinction had become annoying enough to matter.

I closed notebook.

Downstairs somebody laughed.

Pell yelled something about wet boots.

Carrow continued without me.

Duskport continued around me.

Masta was probably working on somebody else's problem.

Lyssa would get blue cord tomorrow or next day.

Tavi would eventually demand soup.

Nothing required me tonight.

Strange.

I slept anyway.
