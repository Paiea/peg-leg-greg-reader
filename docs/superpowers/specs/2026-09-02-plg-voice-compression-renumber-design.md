# PLG Voice, Compression, and Renumber Design

## Goal

Make the existing manuscript sharper, shorter, and more character-specific without losing the accumulated-life engine that makes Peg-Leg Greg distinct.

This pass has three coupled jobs:

1. create durable per-character voice authority for recurring speakers;
2. audit the recent manuscript for repeated chapter architecture, redundant explanation, and unnecessary procedural length;
3. permit structural consolidation, including chapter deletion/merging and renumbering, when a cleaner book materially improves.

## Editorial authority

Manuscript prose remains canon authority for what happened. This pass has explicit author permission to change chapter boundaries and numbering when the underlying events, character outcomes, magic evidence, money facts, body continuity, relationships, and protected uncertainty are preserved.

Renumbering is allowed. It is not mandatory.

The editor may:
- cut paragraphs, scenes, or whole chapters whose distinct value is already delivered elsewhere;
- merge neighboring chapters when they are one movement artificially split into two;
- move a small beat into an adjacent chapter when that preserves continuity better than keeping a weak standalone chapter;
- renumber later chapters after approved structural consolidation;
- substantially rewrite dialogue and paragraph architecture for voice and rhythm;
- preserve shared Carrow colloquial speech where it feels culturally or relationally earned.

The editor may not:
- invent replacement plot to justify a cut;
- change magic results/counts;
- alter money facts or debt facts;
- erase disability/material constraints;
- change relationship status or scene outcome;
- collapse distinct people merely to reduce cast size;
- turn a voice guide into catchphrase enforcement.

## Voice architecture

Create `state/voices/` as a durable voice library.

`state/voices/INDEX.md` explains how workers use the folder and lists the priority recurring cast.

Each recurring-character page contains:
- core cognitive lens;
- spoken rhythm and sentence shape;
- humor profile;
- disagreement / refusal style;
- information behavior: what they explain, assume, hide, or ignore;
- relationship-specific changes, especially with Greg;
- canonical dialogue evidence where available;
- non-canon voice-test lines clearly marked as tests, never canon;
- anti-flattening warnings;
- overlap allowed with other speakers;
- phrases/constructions to watch when they begin sounding too much like Greg or Hessa.

## Tagging rule

Dialogue attribution should become more relentless when speaker ambiguity exists.

Use simple `said` / `asked` freely. Do not force action beats merely to avoid tags.

In two-person exchanges, untagged runs are fine when voice and sequence remain effortless. In group scenes, default slightly toward over-clarity rather than making the reader solve speaker bookkeeping.

Voice distinctness is not a substitute for attribution.

## Voice-separation principle

Do not neutralize Greg to make everybody else distinct. Greg remains Greg.

Move recurring characters away from the shared house voice through cognition and relationship:
- what they notice first;
- what they consider a finished answer;
- whether they pursue an argument or refuse it;
- whether they explain mechanism, procedure, consequence, feeling, or nothing;
- sentence length and interruption patterns;
- certainty tolerance;
- humor motive;
- what they regard as disrespect, waste, risk, or intimacy.

Shared slang, dry humor, workplace shorthand, and phrases learned from one another are allowed. Distinct voices need not be maximally different.

## Recent-run compression audit

Primary structural audit window: Chapters 260-299, while reading neighboring material as needed.

Each chapter receives one disposition:
- **KEEP**: distinct chapter-level value; prose may still tighten.
- **COMPRESS**: chapter remains, target substantial internal reduction.
- **MERGE CANDIDATE**: material may belong with a neighbor.
- **CUT CANDIDATE**: chapter adds too little unique value to justify its boundary.

Audit dimensions:
- unique event / relationship movement;
- unique world or magic information;
- unique emotional movement;
- repeated chapter architecture;
- repeated epistemology / qualification language;
- repeated body checks or procedural steps already established;
- repeated `this was growth`, `not my job`, `not a profession`, `good`, `maybe`, or lesson-after-lesson narration;
- dialogue characters slipping into shared Greg/Hessa cadence;
- endings that explain what the scene already proved.

## Compression targets

Do not impose a universal percentage.

Typical target:
- strong distinctive chapter: 5-15% shorter;
- useful but repetitive chapter: 20-35% shorter;
- procedure-heavy chapter with established mechanics: 30-45% shorter;
- weak standalone chapter: merge or cut rather than polishing 3,000 redundant words.

Preserve spacious chapters whose value is social presence, leisure, intimacy, atmosphere, comedy, or lived-world texture. Compression is not permission to turn PLG into plot notes.

## Structural priority

Preserve chapters that materially change Greg's relationship to usefulness, belonging, desire, or agency, especially recent chapters where he participates without needing a job or claim.

Do not cut ordinary-life chapters merely because nothing dramatic happens. Cut repetition, not quietness.

## Recent craft problem to correct

Hessa's evidence discipline has productively changed Greg, but the whole city must not sound as though it attended Hessa's seminar.

Other workers may be empirical without sharing her rhetoric. Increase:
- intuition;
- trade shorthand;
- superstition;
- local sayings;
- incomplete explanations;
- confident-but-partial rules;
- people who know a procedure but not the mechanism;
- people who are wrong;
- people who simply do not care about Greg's distinction.

## Execution order

1. Establish voice folder and update editorial rules.
2. Build 260-299 structural compression map before rewriting those chapters.
3. Perform a pilot heavy edit on a small recent batch using the map and voice pages.
4. Compare rhythm, word count, continuity, and voice separation.
5. Expand across 260-299.
6. Only after the structural choices settle, perform any renumbering and update all manuscript indexes/state/reader links atomically.
7. Continue backward/forward heavy editing with the same voice authority.

## Renumber safety

Never renumber incrementally across accepted authority while structural decisions remain unsettled.

When renumbering is executed:
- freeze the chosen structural map;
- create an old -> new chapter-number mapping;
- update exact manuscript chapter headings;
- update chapter titles/indexes;
- update state references where chapter number is necessary;
- update reader navigation/anchors;
- preserve historical editorial files as historical rather than pretending their old numbers never existed;
- verify no dangling references before merge.

## Success test

The pass succeeds when:
- the recent run reads faster without feeling thinner;
- recurring speakers remain understandable without tags over several turns, while tags still protect clarity;
- Hessa sounds uniquely Hessa;
- Lyssa does not sound like Greg's concise analyst;
- Marek does not sound like Greg with extra jokes;
- practical workers have domain-shaped speech rather than universal evidence language;
- Greg explains fewer already-landed moments;
- chapter boundaries feel earned;
- quiet life remains pleasurable;
- canon and accumulated residue survive.
