# Vale Leverage + Economy Normalization Design

Status: **AUTHOR APPROVED**

## Goal

Repair Peg-Leg Greg's economic continuity without changing the story's core beats by combining two corrections:

1. Antonius Vale's lending model is intentionally high-interest, leverage-seeking, relationship-based finance rather than ordinary amortized lending.
2. Greg's economic progression must widen from cheap copper labor into silver, tens-of-silver, and occasional gold-scale value as his judgment, risk, coordination, reputation, and capital access improve.

The preferred repair is **numbers first, arithmetic second, narrow explanatory prose third, story structure last**.

## Canonical character/economic interpretation

### Greg's early financial problem

Greg is not financially ignorant.

He is financially arrogant.

His old-life instincts were formed at a much larger wealth scale. Silver feels smaller to him than it objectively is to nineteen-year-old Greg because old Greg was accustomed to spending gold-scale and plausibly platinum-tier resources without treating every expenditure as consequential.

Therefore early Greg plausibly:
- borrows silver aggressively;
- treats expected future earnings as if they are nearly cash;
- gambles silver;
- finances specialist testing;
- buys training and equipment quickly;
- funds curiosity;
- accepts ugly financing because he believes he will simply out-earn it;
- repeatedly discovers that young Greg's earning capacity is not old Greg's earning capacity.

His recurring realization can be comedic and blunt: **he understood the terms and was still dumb enough to take them.**

Do not rewrite early Greg into a prudent poverty character.

## Antonius Vale lending model

Antonius's terms are genuinely predatory on paper.

The Chapter 2 short-term rate of **35% over eight days** remains a useful canonical signal. The running account may also accumulate fees, advances, financed purchases, specialist costs, and other agreed charges.

Antonius knows these terms create balances that many borrowers will not cleanly repay.

That is not an accident.

### What Antonius is buying

Antonius often lends where conventional lenders would refuse.

He is not merely buying interest income. He is buying **optionality and durable leverage over potentially useful people**.

A borrower who cannot clear the account may owe him:
- bounded labor;
- first look at opportunities;
- information;
- introductions;
- appraisal or specialist help;
- occasional commercial favors;
- availability for specific tasks;
- a continuing reason to stay in Antonius's orbit.

This is ethically gray and materially self-interested.

Do not sanitize Antonius into a secret philanthropist.

He likes leverage. He knows what the terms do. Some borrowers may have legitimate reasons to resent him.

But he also does take risks on people others will not fund, and many borrowers may receive real opportunities because he does.

### Collection behavior

Antonius generally does **not expect** risky borrowers to repay every line of the accrued interest cleanly in cash.

That expectation is part of the model. The ugly interest preserves leverage.

He may accept labor, information, access, introductions, and usefulness over time because those are economically valuable to him even if the face ledger remains ugly.

However, for Greg specifically, Antonius does **not waive the final interest** once Greg decides to clear the account.

That is the stronger outcome.

Antonius is willing to let the leash persist almost indefinitely because he does not expect full repayment. Greg surprises him by paying the whole face balance anyway.

Greg therefore bears the full monetary consequence of the terrible terms he knowingly accepted.

## Vale ledger normalization model

### Early account

The Chapter 2 loan becomes a composite running account by Chapter 5.

Durable components may include:
- original principal;
- high short-term interest;
- later advances;
- financed specialist/material costs;
- guild-note expenses;
- gambling/working-capital advances;
- Tere gauge seller financing;
- fees;
- partial payments;
- labor credits.

The early ledger should be allowed to become absurd on paper.

### Tere gauge 5s / 10s contradiction

Preferred repair:

Do **not** silently choose either 5s or 10s.

Preserve Chapter 7's **5s negotiated purchase price** as principal.

Where Chapter 10 currently says Greg will own it once he produces **10s**, normalize the language so the higher number is explicitly the **current amount attached to the purchase/account after Vale's brutal short-term financing and/or rolled charges**, not a retconned purchase price.

This turns an error into character-consistent financial damage.

### Late verification

The late Vale verification should reveal **one collectible face balance**: the ugly nominal amount after principal, advances, fees, labor credits, payments, and accumulated interest are all reconciled.

There is no friendship discount and no settlement waiver.

The balance should be large enough that Greg's reaction is essentially:

**I understood the terms. I kept borrowing anyway. I am dumb.**

Antonius can agree without cruelty.

The exact normalized amount should be chosen only after the representative later earnings are normalized, so that Greg can plausibly repay it in full over the existing late sequence without an economic reset.

Target shape: **high tens of silver**, not pocket copper and not a number so large that closure requires rewriting the plot.

### Why full repayment is interesting

Antonius does not expect Greg to pay all the interest.

That is precisely why Greg doing so matters.

The account was designed to become a long leash. Greg eventually becomes economically capable enough to cut the leash by honoring the exact terrible agreement in full.

Antonius is more impressed because:
- he expected usefulness, labor, information, and continued orbit more than perfect cash collection;
- Greg had every incentive to keep living under the flexible account;
- Greg instead becomes independently productive enough to eliminate the entire balance;
- the wager produces more than Antonius expected.

Antonius should not become sentimental or refuse payment.

If Greg says he is paying the account, Antonius takes the money and closes it.

## Late relationship consequence

Chapter 491's drinking scene remains structurally correct and becomes stronger under this model.

Before closure, jokes with Antonius always have an account underneath them.

After closure, Greg can ask **"Drink?"** with no ledger reason.

Antonius is allowed to be quietly impressed that the overconfident idiot who took absurd financing actually paid the entire compounded account.

The relationship remains acquaintance / friend-adjacent, not suddenly intimate.

## Broader compensation normalization

### Core rule

**Pay follows value created, moved, protected, unlocked, coordinated, or risked.**

Greg's purse does not determine what his work is worth.

### Simultaneous tiers

Cheap copper work remains valid for:
- manual labor;
- short errands;
- token theatre pay;
- ordinary clerical comparison;
- small social payments.

Silver should be ordinary skilled/commercial operating money for:
- independent witness work;
- sourcing commissions;
- useful specialist judgment;
- bounded coordination;
- commercial record work where failure costs money.

Tens of silver should increasingly appear for:
- multi-day multi-crew organization;
- serious specialist brokerage;
- meaningful gray/illegal logistics;
- high-value commissions;
- urgent management/coordination;
- dangerous or institutionally exposed work where Greg has legal alternatives.

Gold-scale value or compensation should remain occasional and tied to genuinely large underlying value:
- rare artifacts;
- major contraband;
- ownership/capital positions;
- organized high-risk logistics;
- specialist judgment protecting or unlocking a gold-scale position.

No universal copper/silver/gold conversion is introduced.

## Minimal prose-repair strategy

Do not rewrite dozens of chapters merely to explain the economy.

Preferred prose changes are concentrated at:

1. **Early Vale setup / running-account clarification**
   - preserve Chapter 2 high-interest terms;
   - clarify composite-account growth where useful;
   - repair Tere 5s/10s contradiction by distinguishing principal from current financed balance.

2. **Representative later earning scenes**
   - change nominal transaction/pay numbers;
   - recalculate dependent purse figures;
   - adjust Greg's reaction only where the old copper-scale reaction becomes wrong.

3. **Late Vale verification / full repayment**
   - reveal absurd but collectible face balance;
   - have Greg recognize his own stupidity/arrogance;
   - make clear Antonius did not really expect every interest line to be paid;
   - have Greg insist on clearing the entire amount anyway;
   - preserve Greg making a large first payment while protecting enough working capital to remain operational;
   - preserve later final closure becoming administratively simple after Greg's earning power rises;
   - Antonius takes the money rather than waiving it.

4. **Chapter 491 relationship scene**
   - preserve structure;
   - strengthen Antonius's impressed calibration lightly;
   - preserve understated tone.

## Non-goals

Do not:
- make Antonius secretly benevolent;
- make all debt harmless;
- erase Greg's actual cost for bad borrowing;
- waive the final interest for Greg;
- make Greg financially ignorant;
- make every job silver/gold;
- invent a denomination conversion;
- turn Antonius into a therapist;
- turn the economy into a rigid RPG wage table;
- rewrite plot beats that already work simply to display new numbers.

## Success criteria

The repaired manuscript should make all of these simultaneously believable:

- Greg spends/borrows silver early because old-life wealth damaged his intuition.
- Greg can be broke despite moving silver because liquidity and net economic activity differ.
- Antonius's rates are genuinely brutal.
- Antonius still plausibly helps people and invests in them.
- Antonius benefits from keeping borrowers in orbit.
- Antonius does not expect most risky borrowers to pay every accrued interest line in cash.
- Greg's running Vale balance becomes large without requiring a new plot.
- Greg eventually pays the **full** reconciled face balance anyway.
- Antonius is genuinely impressed because Greg does something the lending model was not designed to require.
- the final closure means freedom from leverage, not triumph over pocket change.
- later skilled/high-risk work visibly pays more than early manual labor.
- silver becomes normal operating money, tens of silver become leverage/risk money, and gold remains a higher tier.
