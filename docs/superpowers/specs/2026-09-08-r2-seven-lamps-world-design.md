# R2 Seven Lamps / World-Iceberg Design

Status: **APPROVED DESIGN / NON-CANON ARCHITECTURE**

## Goal

Deepen Greg, Again / R2's magic and world architecture without turning the local chapter engine into a lore queue.

Preserve a large hidden magical reality, incomplete human understanding, magical ecology, trade/economy, and myth-shaped stories that can exist independently of Greg.

## Core design

### 1. Seven Lamps as backend magical architecture

Use the seven-lamp conceptual set derived from the prior Architecture / Exposition experiment as deep backend magic:

- Sacrifice
- Truth
- Power
- Beauty
- Life
- Memory
- Obedience

These are **not** ordinary visible spell schools, elemental types, or a public character-selection taxonomy.

They are different fundamental ways magical reality can be made to produce results.

Different Lamps may reach superficially similar outcomes through genuinely different mechanisms.

Example shape only: several traditions may all prevent an incoming strike, but one may oppose force, one may redirect through truth/geometry, one may bind a rule, one may spend something valuable, one may restore a remembered state, and another may use living/adaptive response. Do not canonize these example mappings as exact Lamp mechanics before story evidence earns them.

### 2. Human magic is an incomplete cultural model

Human civilization can contain skilled warders, healers, fighters, artificers, schools, Guild practice, high-rank adventurers, and Rankers while still understanding only fragments of the deeper system.

Human traditions may:

- know one Lamp well but call it something else
- partially use another without realizing it
- combine multiple Lamps and think they are one school
- produce similar effects through different underlying principles
- mistake practical rules for fundamental truth
- maintain correct techniques with incorrect explanations
- discover useful interactions centuries before understanding why they work

Do not let characters casually know the complete Seven Lamps architecture merely because backend story state does.

The iceberg must remain real.

### 3. Greg's old expertise remains enormous but incomplete

Old Greg was one of seven S-class adventurers on the continent and an elite Barrier/support practitioner.

That means he had extraordinary practical mastery of the human magical world he lived inside.

It does **not** mean he understood all magical reality.

Current Greg may therefore possess unfairly deep practical knowledge while still encountering beasts, ruins, artifacts, traditions, or phenomena that exceed Old Greg's theory.

Barrier remains strongly associated with the practical grammar:

> **PLANE. DIRECTION. LOAD.**

Do not prematurely assign Barrier to one exact Lamp. It may prove to be a partial human model, hybrid practice, or culturally misclassified use of deeper principles.

Preserve the author fact that Old Greg's route into Barrier began from a birth-control context. Exact mechanics remain unestablished.

### 4. Magical ecology is not a bestiary stat ladder

The world should contain magical beasts, strange ecologies, rare shapeshifters, territorial entities, magical plants, altered landscapes, underground life, and phenomena that do not care about Guild taxonomy.

Creatures should have ecology where useful:

- habitat
- food
- migration
- mating / nesting
- territorial behavior
- predators and prey
- symbiosis
- seasonal pressure
- mana interaction
- learned human avoidance or exploitation
- trade value
- religious / cultural meaning

Do not reduce all magical beings to Bronze/Silver/Gold/Ranker equivalence.

Guilds may still use practical danger categories or contract language, but those are human management systems, not cosmic truth.

### 5. Other people's myths

R2 may translate structural beats from prior Hawaiian / moʻolelo-inspired story quarry into this fantasy world without literally importing Hawaiʻi, Hawaiian identity, or surface cultural cosplay.

Useful quarry shapes include:

- retrieval journeys that become emotionally complicated
- hospitality involving powerful strangers
- rare shapeshifters
- water guardians or place-bound dangerous beings
- impossible construction or landscape-scale labor
- intimate conflict producing geographic consequence
- local prohibitions outsiders misunderstand
- rival powers
- difficult returns
- old lovers or family history complicating apparently simple work
- supernatural situations that can be funny, petty, romantic, familial, tragic, or practical rather than automatically grim

Preserve **causal and emotional architecture**, not literal names, sacred material, or transplanted cultural markers.

Most important rule:

> **GREG DOES NOT HAVE TO OWN THE MYTH.**

He may arrive through a contract, road, trade route, party job, accident, shelter, salvage dispute, creature problem, or invitation and find that a much older story is already moving.

He may witness it, assist one part of it, make one crucial contribution, misunderstand it, profit from it, be injured by it, or leave before it resolves.

The people whose story it is continue after Greg leaves.

### 6. Trade and magic reinforce each other

Magic should deepen R2's money / trade engine rather than replacing it.

Possible economic consequences include:

- creature materials
- dangerous-route premiums
- salvage ownership
- artifact appraisal
- repair / recharge / maintenance
- poaching and protection
- specialist transport restrictions
- legal provenance
- cross-school mispricing
- regional magical techniques
- strange-market arbitrage
- insurance-like risk allocation
- seasonal markets
- contracts whose value depends on magical ecology

Greg may know that an item, maker, creature product, location, or technique later becomes valuable while remaining wrong or incomplete about the present causal chain.

Sometimes he should get an unfairly good trade.

Sometimes he should lose money or buy the wrong thing.

### 7. Causality pays both directions

The prior `LET GREG WIN` correction remains, but it is not positivity doctrine.

Use:

> **LET CAUSALITY PAY BOTH DIRECTIONS.**

Good decisions often help.

Bad decisions often hurt.

Good decisions can meet bad luck.

Bad decisions can occasionally get lucky.

Greg's decades of expertise change odds in domains he truly understands, but do not grant narrative protection.

Do not append compensating failure to every win.

Do not protect Greg from earned loss merely because the engine was recently corrected toward competence.

## Local inheritance

The local chapter writer should **not** boot with a Seven Lamps textbook.

It should inherit only compact pressures:

- magic is deeper than current human theory
- different traditions may solve similar problems differently
- high human mastery is not total magical understanding
- magical ecology can exceed Guild classification
- myth-scale stories may already belong to other people
- money, trade, law, ecology, and magic should interact
- wins and losses both follow causality

The chapter writer still begins with:

> **WHAT SHOULD ACTUALLY HAPPEN NEXT?**

Do not turn the next chapter into a Seven Lamps reveal, magical-beast showcase, moʻolelo-derived episode, new school explanation, or artifact market scene merely because this design exists.

## Long-range rehearsal instruction

Run a new multi-year forward/backward rehearsal with this deeper architecture active.

Vary:

- how much of the Seven Lamps iceberg Greg encounters directly
- whether human schools remain locally practical or begin theoretical breakthroughs
- creature/ecology prominence
- trade/artifact prominence
- travel/underground prominence
- other-people's-myth frequency
- party formation
- rank progression
- theatre/no theatre
- early/middle/late leg loss
- warrior persistence

Extract recurrence rather than selecting plots.
