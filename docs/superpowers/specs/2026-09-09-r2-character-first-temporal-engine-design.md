# R2 Character-First Temporal Engine Design

Status: **APPROVED DESIGN / EXPERIMENT BRANCH ONLY**

Branch: `experiment/r2-character-rebuild`

## Purpose

Restore the useful temporal/distributed-generation leverage of later R2 and the current Falling Off a Cliff rolling-horizon system without restoring the chapter grammar that flattened R2.

The engine must make it cheaper to explore future story pressure while keeping character-specific life stronger than causal/system neatness.

The calibration sample is reconstructed Chapters 27–31 and its post-revision evaluation.

## Core principle

> **Temporal leverage may increase search depth. It may not lower the character bar.**

Old R2 asked primarily whether future material still survived causally.

The rebuilt engine must also ask:

> **Does this future still belong specifically to these people, and does the scene discover something that another competent-work chapter could not reveal as well?**

## Authority boundary

Public/current R2 on `main` remains historical evidence.

The character-first rebuild remains branch-only editorial material until explicitly promoted through a later publication decision.

No temporal experiment path may directly alter or feed public:

- R2 written authority
- chapter routes / reader surfaces
- registry/data
- audio
- images

Historical later R2 remains quarry/rehearsal/evidence, never chronology authority for the rebuild.

## Horizon model

### A — Current hard run

- 3–5 connected chapters
- full-fidelity character-first prose
- numbered because it is directly adjacent to the reconstructed frontier
- fresh-read exact current rebuild prose before writing
- may become accepted rebuild authority after connected reader/challenger evaluation
- no public publication implied

### B — Next hard run

- 3–5 scene/chapter-equivalent units at high fidelity
- real production, not throwaway ideation
- must obey accepted A facts
- should remain replaceable until A reconciliation
- may use provisional scene labels instead of fixed chapter numbers when numbering would create false chronology pressure
- promotes only through fresh re-performance/reconciliation

### C — Warm speculative rehearsal

- character-clock or relationship-pressure cluster
- scene-complete or compressed where useful
- chronology remains soft
- preserve desires, decisions, social residue, money, work, body, geography, magic, objects, obligations, and relationship motion
- prose exists only where embodiment improves judgment

### D — Cold speculative rehearsal

- cheap far-edge reconnaissance
- pressures, independent clocks, possible intersections, ordinary-life consequences, and backward obligations
- no requirement to occupy the slot
- empty D is allowed
- avoid brittle exact chapter numbers and exact dates unless the current story has already created them

## Character-first survivor gate

Causal survival is necessary but insufficient.

Before a scene or cluster can promote toward A/B, retrieve the established people involved and test:

1. What does each person want now?
2. What are they doing when Greg is absent?
3. What does Greg notice first?
4. What is Greg avoiding noticing?
5. What is Greg likely to get wrong specifically because he is Greg?
6. What does another character choose that Greg does not own?
7. What does the scene discover that another work/procedure scene would not discover as well?
8. If names and obvious biography are stripped, do these people remain identifiable?
9. Does the scene leave useful residue, or sterilize itself into a conclusion?
10. Is this shape being selected because the current people earned it, or because the generator already knows how to write it?

If a speculative event survives but the scene fails this gate, preserve only the discovery/pressure and re-perform later.

## Independent clocks

The temporal engine should actively track independent motion for recurring people and systems, but only compactly inside `HORIZON_STATE.md`.

Useful clocks include:

- Greg: body, money, magic, work, home, desire, obligations
- Mara: Vey/Halden work, correspondence, ambition, availability, relationship pressure
- Jorren: roads, employers, money, injuries, contacts, independent friendships
- Noll: Lower Rook life, food/work, resources, relationships Greg does not mediate
- Arlo/Ivena/Dena: production, customers, service, labor, money, disagreement
- Sella/Guild: work availability and administrative reality
- Tavin: freight geography, correspondence, existing young-Greg relationship

Do not create a giant permanent simulation ledger. Record only clocks that can plausibly affect the current A/B/C/D horizons.

## Scene selection pressure

A future scene earns advancement by changing at least one meaningful state:

- desire
- attachment
- resentment
- misunderstanding
- trust
- expectation
- money
- body
- capability
- responsibility
- belonging
- reputation
- geography
- home
- work ownership
- magic understanding
- relationship language
- self-story

A purely procedural chapter may still earn itself, but only if the procedure produces a distinctive human discovery, cost, choice, or consequence.

## Greg friction gate

Do not optimize Greg into perfect restraint.

Allow earned:

- jealousy
- vanity
- wanting admiration
- social misreading
- defensiveness
- avoidance
- lust/desire
- loneliness
- premature grief
- overattachment
- control disguised as advice
- resentment toward defensible choices
- bad timing
- technically correct but socially poor behavior

Do not manufacture a flaw quota. Greg may also make genuinely good decisions without punishment.

## Repetition gate

Before A acceptance and before B promotion, read the connected run rather than chapters in isolation.

Challenge:

- `problem -> professional -> boundary -> useful action -> lesson`
- evaluator fragments used by habit
- `Not X. Y.` reversals used by habit
- identical joke placement
- dialogue followed by explanation of what it proved
- tidy Greg-principle endings
- supporting adults becoming uniformly reasonable
- work dominating chapter selection merely because work is reliably writable
- anti-escalation becoming its own formula
- compressed summaries that skip the scene readers actually want

Variance is diagnostic, not a quota.

## Re-performance rule

No C/D wording promotes directly.

No B wording is protected merely because it is high fidelity.

When material approaches A:

1. fresh-read exact accepted rebuild prose
2. fresh-read relevant character references / recent relationship scenes
3. read surviving B/C/D discovery material
4. discard invalidated assumptions
5. ask `WHAT SHOULD ACTUALLY HAPPEN NEXT?`
6. re-stage at full R2 Shared Greg Surface quality
7. keep old wording only where it still wins

## Historical later-R2 quarry

Historical later R2 is an additional rehearsal source, not an extra horizon.

It may provide:

- people
- jobs
- jokes
- objects
- places
- relationship discoveries
- economic consequences
- magic evidence
- body progression
- world facts

It may not dictate sequence, timing, or survival.

## Storage

Keep all new temporal material under:

`r2/editorial/character-rebuild/`

Required compact control files:

- `HORIZON_STATE.md` — current A/B/C/D routing and relevant clocks

Horizon prose/rehearsal lives under horizon-specific folders inside the same experimental tree.

Do not add a separate doctrine hierarchy, permanent speculative database, or public dependency.

## First activation

The first activation begins immediately after the verified 27–31 calibration sample.

- A starts at reconstructed Chapter 32 and contains 3–5 connected full-fidelity chapters.
- B contains the next 3–5 high-fidelity scene/chapter-equivalent units, provisional until A reconciliation.
- C and D are unnumbered character-clock rehearsals.
- Historical later R2 may be quarried only when current pressure makes a specific discovery relevant.

The first A run must not default to another new professional subsystem merely because Chapter 31 ends with Jorren leaving.

Immediate live pressures include:

- Tavin's still-unsent reply
- Mara's correspondence on her own Halden/Vey clock
- Jorren away for four or five days on his own client relationship
- Noll's increasingly undernamed friendship and outside life
- Arlo/Ivena/Dena operating filtration without Greg's centrality
- Greg wanting roads while also accumulating a home
- Greg's support assessment still ahead

## Success criterion

The engine is succeeding if it produces more usable future material **without making the novel more predictable**.

A healthy future backlog should increase options, not obligations.

The accepted front must continue to feel like people living close enough to change one another, not like a system demonstrating that it understands its own doctrine.
