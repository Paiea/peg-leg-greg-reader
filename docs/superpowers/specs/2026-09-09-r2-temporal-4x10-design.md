# R2 Temporal 4×10 Experiment Design

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL / REVIEW TARGET / NO PUBLIC AUTHORITY**

Branch: `experiment/r2-temporal-4x10-043-082`

Base authority: accepted character-rebuild state through Chapter 42, `Tax Clerk`.

## Goal

Test whether R2 improves when four independent temporal writers explore forty chapter-equivalents at once instead of advancing only a short near-edge run.

Each horizon attempts ten substantial chapters. Each chapter aims for roughly **3,000 words**, making each horizon roughly **30,000 words** and the full experiment roughly **120,000 words**.

The target is not 120,000 words of authority. The target is enough embodied prose to expose character, setting, plot, action, relationship, pacing, and future-story possibilities that summaries cannot discover.

## Experimental override

For this branch only, this design overrides the earlier `SPEC.md` prohibition on a giant temporal backlog and the existing `TEMPORAL_ENGINE.md` 3–5 chapter A / compressed B/C/D horizon sizes.

It does **not** alter accepted Chapters 1–42, public R2, audio, images, reader routes, or the standing character-first north star.

## Four simultaneous horizons

All four horizons begin from the same accepted truth through Chapter 42.

| Agent | Provisional territory | Target | Trust |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A | 43–52 | ~30,000 words | nearest candidate future |
| B | 53–62 | ~30,000 words | serious speculative future |
| C | 63–72 | ~30,000 words | serious farther future |
| D | 73–82 | ~30,000 words | widest search / least chronological trust |

The numbers are provisional coordinates, not promises. B/C/D may not assume that another future horizon happened exactly as drafted. They may use only accepted truth through 42 plus durable world/character evidence and quarry discoveries that survive independently of chronology.

A also remains non-authoritative until review.

## Simultaneity rule

The experiment must behave as four writers working at once, not A writing a ten-chapter outline that B extends.

Operationally:

1. Give all four agents the same Chapter-42 state and character/world constraints.
2. Give each a distinct temporal distance and pressure question.
3. Draft across horizons in rounds rather than completing A and then extending it into B/C/D.
4. Do not import newly invented A facts into B/C/D during the first draft.
5. Do not import B facts into C, C facts into D, or vice versa.
6. After all four runs exist, cross-read them and classify discoveries, contradictions, false inevitabilities, and portable survivors.

## Chapter length

Aim for **3,000 words per chapter**.

Healthy variation is allowed. A chapter that finishes at 2,500–3,500 words can still succeed. Do not pad to hit a counter. However, repeated sub-2,000-word chapters count as a failure of this experiment because the point is to give scenes enough room to breathe, think, move, and accumulate physical detail.

## Greg interiority and action

Greg is allowed to think extensively. Long thought is not a defect when it remains attached to a body, task, room, road, person, or immediate sensory problem.

Critical rule:

> **Greg's thoughts and actions do not need to agree.**

He may believe he is calm while gripping something too hard. He may decide not to interfere and then interfere. He may insist he is not jealous while asking an obviously jealous question. He may intellectually accept an absence while arranging his day around it. He may want a road and behave like someone building a home. He may reach the correct conclusion only after his body or behavior already exposed the opposite desire.

The narration should not stop to explain every contradiction. Let behavior reveal it.

## Physical-action requirement

Turn up physical action without turning every chapter into combat.

Action includes:

- walking, running, climbing, lifting, hauling, carrying, repairing, cooking, cleaning, packing, dressing, washing, eating, riding, crossing water, navigating crowds, working beside another person
- sword work, magic use, dangerous labor, pursuit, accidents, environmental hazards, fights, monsters, or emergencies when earned
- touch, proximity, bodily hesitation, fatigue, soreness, hunger, heat, cold, wet clothing, poor sleep, sex/desire when earned
- objects and environments resisting people

A chapter should usually contain multiple meaningful changes in physical state or location. Ten chapters cannot become ten conversations in rooms.

## Action escalation

R2 should have more kinetic chapters than the immediately preceding rebuild run. This does not mean constant spectacle.

Across each ten-chapter horizon, seek a mix of:

- ordinary movement and labor
- travel or geographic change
- one or more genuinely dangerous sequences when earned
- magic used under pressure rather than only discussed
- social choices made while bodies are occupied with something else
- consequences that leave marks on clothing, money, gear, fatigue, injury, timing, or relationships

## Character constraints

Preserve current independent clocks and relationship languages.

Greg must not become the dispatcher for everyone.

Mara, Jorren, Noll, Tavin, Arlo, Ivena, Dena, Sella, and other established people may make choices Greg does not see or approve.

Old Greg's future knowledge should produce emotional distortion as often as informational advantage: false familiarity, grief, dread, premature attachment, temptation, annoyance, nostalgia, inappropriate certainty, or fear of losing a future that has not happened.

## Setting requirement

Every horizon must widen or deepen the lived world. Carrow cannot become an abstract hub between conversations.

Use streets, river traffic, inns, guild rooms, workshops, markets, road surfaces, weather, light, smells, housing, food, transport, labor systems, local superstition, nonhuman ecology, magic infrastructure, and class/economic friction as active scene material.

New setting details must create choices or constraints rather than functioning only as decorative lore.

## Plot requirement

Each horizon should create a coherent ten-chapter movement without pretending its ending is destiny.

A movement can arise from overlapping independent clocks rather than one quest. It should still contain acceleration, consequences, reversals, and enough causal linkage that the run reads like story rather than ten vignettes.

Avoid the old default grammar:

`problem -> competent professional -> Greg finds boundary -> narrow useful action -> sensible outcome -> Greg summarizes lesson`

## No sunk-cost authority

Word count buys **discovery**, not survival.

A 3,200-word D chapter can be killed completely. A failed B chapter may preserve one bodily habit, one setting, one joke, one character choice, or one conflict shape. Future prose does not make its invented facts true merely by existing.

## Audit

After drafting all forty chapters, measure and review:

1. **Word count** — per chapter, per horizon, total, mean, median, range, and number below 2,500 / within 2,500–3,500 / above 3,500.
2. **How the engine works** — whether concurrency produced useful divergent futures, contamination, contradictions, or surprising portable discoveries.
3. **Prose** — voice, cadence, repetition, evaluator fragments, abstraction, sensory specificity, scene breathing room, endings.
4. **Character** — Greg contradictions, supporting-character autonomy, distinct relationship languages, emotional mess, anonymous-character test.
5. **Setting** — physical specificity, geographic breadth, local systems, ecology/magic, whether place changes action.
6. **Plot** — momentum, causal linkage, reversals, unresolved residue, whether horizons become vignettes or conveyors.
7. **Action** — physical verbs, movement, labor, danger, magic under pressure, fights/hazards when earned, and whether thought remains embodied.

The audit must be candid. Its job is to decide whether the 4×10 engine is worth retaining, modifying, or killing.

## Promotion rule

Nothing from this experiment automatically publishes.

After audit, any winning near-edge material must be freshly reconciled against the newest accepted R2 authority before promotion. B/C/D wording has no direct path to canon.

## North star

> A man who remembers history better than he remembers life is forced to live closely enough that life starts replacing history as his primary source of truth.

The 4×10 engine succeeds only if temporal scale gives Greg **more life**, not merely more plot.
