# 3L Opening Records 001-010 Design

## Status

Approved story-development design for the first ten Records of **The Third Leg**. This is development authority, not manuscript prose authority. Exact names, world terminology, mechanics, and scene details may still change when prose proves a better solution.

## Core opening problem

Older Greg, roughly fifty-nine, is a capable but diminished version of the man he once remembers being. In his First Life he reached S-class. In his Second Life he never returned to that height. He is still renowned by ordinary human standards, roughly B-class / low-Ranker scale as a working hypothesis.

A kingdom-scale civilizational system is failing. The human stakes may reach hundreds of thousands or millions. Nobody sends Greg to negotiate with a dragon. Greg independently decides that the dragon may be able to solve the problem, and he believes he possesses First-Life knowledge valuable enough to force a conversation.

The dragon does not care about the kingdom merely because the casualty number is large. It has watched civilizations rise, collapse, and rebuild. This is ancient-scale detachment, not cartoon nihilism. The dragon understands Greg's moral concern without sharing Greg's assumption that the kingdom is the most important thing in the room.

Greg's impossible knowledge is what changes the conversation. His claims about S-class-level realities, places, beings, techniques, structures, or ancient facts are close enough to truth that the dragon recognizes Greg should not know them. Greg's memory may be fuzzy after decades, but it is broadly experiential and credible rather than a perfect archive.

The dragon's curiosity shifts from the kingdom problem to Greg himself: how does this current man know what only a much more capable person should know? Greg eventually reveals that he has lived before.

## Hidden emotional spine

Greg believes he has come to solve a large external problem and may also believe he wants to repair one terrible loss from Life Two. The deeper truth, not revealed immediately, is that Greg wants another beginning. He has started treating lives as iterative.

Book-level emotional movement remains:

**normal life -> love -> loss -> revenge -> rebuilding**

Life Two matters because it becomes a real ordinary life, not because it is a more efficient climb back to S-class.

The dragon frame should eventually expose the tension between Greg judging the dragon for treating civilizations as replaceable and Greg treating his own lives as drafts that can be redone.

## Dragon character doctrine

The dragon is ancient, dangerous, patient, intelligent, self-interested, and genuinely curious. It is not Greg's therapist and not a generic wise mentor.

It may be unmoved by a million abstract deaths while becoming fascinated by one impossible human because rarity, anomaly, truth, and particularity matter differently to it.

The dragon does not share human time pressure. Weeks of conversation are not inherently burdensome.

It should talk substantially more than Greg at selected moments. Greg often answers briefly. The dragon can be expansive because time does not cost it what it costs Greg.

The dragon should occasionally be surprised, corrected, annoyed, or taught something by Greg. The relationship must not become professor/student.

## Greg character doctrine

Greg is practical, transactional, observant, experienced, occasionally arrogant, and capable of making very large decisions from grounded personal logic.

At age fifty-nine he is genuinely competent. Reaching the dragon should feel like something a lesser person likely could not do.

At nineteen, Greg begins Life Two believing he knows how to become himself again. He expects to use First-Life knowledge to reach S-class faster, cleaner, and with fewer mistakes.

The story gradually demonstrates that memory provides leverage without providing complete causal understanding.

## Setting engine

Working concept: an ancient civilizational infrastructure system, temporarily called **the Line**.

The Line is not merely magic scenery. It shapes settlement, trade, agriculture, roads, insurance, ranker work, maintenance professions, law, pricing, migration, and political stability.

Humans maintain and administrate the system without fully understanding its oldest foundations. At nineteen, Greg experiences it as ordinary infrastructure and work. By fifty-nine, multiple failures threaten kingdom-scale collapse.

Exact terminology and mechanics remain provisional until prose/worldbuilding proves the strongest version.

Setting rule:

**SETTING creates PRESSURE -> Greg makes CHOICES -> consequences alter POSITION -> new position creates new PRESSURE.**

## Temporal / narrative form

The account is literary first-person retrospective prose.

**Young Greg owns perception. Old Greg owns selection.**

Remembered scenes use what Greg at that age could perceive, believe, want, and misunderstand. Older Greg controls what is selected, compressed, omitted, emphasized, or recalled uncertainly.

Dragon-frame present may interrupt normal Life-Two Records when the interruption materially changes interpretation, reveals relationship movement, challenges Greg's compression, or advances the bargain.

Dragon presence in ordinary Records should generally live around a loose **10-15% neighborhood**, but this is not a quota. Some Records may use little or none; others may use more.

Prose is story authority. A separate performance/audio script may be derived from prose with explicit speaker routing. Do not make screenplay formatting the canonical manuscript.

## Record 001 - THE PETITIONER

Age ~59, frame present.

Greg deliberately reaches the dragon because the kingdom-scale system is failing. He presents casualty and civilizational stakes. The dragon is sincerely unmoved by scale alone.

Greg uses one piece of First-Life knowledge as leverage. The dragon finally becomes attentive, but to Greg rather than the kingdom.

Primary turn: **Who told you that?**

## Record 002 - THE CLAIMANT

Greg believes his leverage worked and tries to return to negotiation. The dragon instead probes provenance.

Greg supplies additional knowledge associated with S-class-level experience. Some details are fuzzy or slightly wrong after decades. Enough is correct that coincidence becomes implausible.

The dragon looks at current Greg and recognizes a mismatch between the man present and the experience claimed.

Primary turn: Greg reveals that the impossible experience did not happen **this time**.

## Record 003 - THE BARGAINER

First Life and Second Life emerge explicitly.

Greg reached S-class in First Life. Life Two produced the current, lower-ranked older Greg.

Greg repeatedly tries to return to the kingdom crisis. The dragon repeatedly returns to the anomaly of Greg.

The dragon asks what happens after the kingdom is saved, creating the first small crack in Greg's stated motivation.

They negotiate the account. Life Two becomes the spine. First Life enters whenever its memory materially caused Life-Two behavior.

The dragon rejects highlight-reel compression but does not require equal page time for every day. Compression is allowed; erasure is dangerous.

Primary transition into the remembered life: **Where you became different.** Exact final wording may change in prose.

## Record 004 - THE RETURNED

Age ~19.

Greg wakes/returns with decades of memory. Ordinary bodily differences matter immediately: pain absent, youth restored, living people present again, hunger/body unfamiliarity.

Greg possesses S-class knowledge but not an S-class body. An early attempt to use advanced capability should demonstrate this gap materially.

Initial belief: **I know the route. I can do it better this time.**

## Record 005 - THE ASPIRANT

The ranker economy/world becomes concrete through jobs, licenses, risk, reputation, contracts, wages, and social position.

Greg remembers an early First-Life route that eventually contributed to S-class success, but also remembers its waste, danger, injury, exploitation, or inefficiency.

He chooses to skip a painful early step and takes a more local, practical job connected to the civilizational infrastructure.

He believes he is optimizing the same destination.

## Record 006 - THE HAND

Greg works inside the setting rather than touring lore.

Infrastructure maintenance, local danger, money, food, repairs, travel, ranker labor, and ordinary institutions reveal how the world actually functions.

Greg is mentally overqualified and physically underdeveloped.

He begins becoming locally useful and socially legible.

## Record 007 - THE ADVISER

Greg successfully exploits First-Life memory.

He predicts a disruption, shortage, breach, contract problem, or similar setting-specific event and positions himself or another person advantageously.

The memory advantage should genuinely work.

A second person changes a decision because of Greg, creating the first meaningful secondary divergence.

## Record 008 - THE RESCUER

Greg remembers a serious outcome from First Life but only knew it retrospectively.

Being physically present reveals that he remembers the outcome more clearly than the mechanism.

The real cause is embedded in infrastructure, rules, maintenance, incentives, institutions, or old magical systems.

Greg must solve the present problem rather than simply recall the answer.

He changes another person's fate. The survivor's continued presence creates ordinary downstream consequences.

## Record 009 - THE STAYER

Time compresses across weeks or months while ordinary life accumulates.

Greg gains routines, work shorthand, possessions, recurring people, social expectations, and the first sense that this life is becoming locally his.

An opportunity appears that would move him closer to his remembered First-Life route.

Greg stays for reasons created by Life Two itself. He tells himself the delay is temporary.

This is the first meaningful choice driven by attachment to the new life rather than optimization of the old one.

## Record 010 - THE STRANGER

A person important to First-Life Greg appears or passes through. Greg recognizes them instantly. They do not know him because the relationship originated on the path Greg skipped.

At the same time, a remembered public event still occurs but differs in a small, causally plausible way because of earlier Life-Two changes.

Greg realizes both personal and public history are no longer waiting exactly where memory left them.

He does not collapse. His initial response is confidence: he will adjust.

## First-ten function

Records 001-003 establish the Greg/Dragon relationship, impossible knowledge, worldview conflict, and account.

Records 004-010 begin Life Two and show the first movement from remembered optimization toward a life that develops its own value.

The first ten should not rush love, loss, revenge, or rebuilding. They should open the door to normal life and demonstrate that Greg's supposedly repeatable route is already becoming something else.

## Prose baseline

Target roughly **2,500 words per Record** as a useful baseline, not a law. A Record may be shorter or longer when scene pressure demands it.

Prioritize life per word, causal setting, specific work, accumulated relationship, and distinct voice over hitting an exact count.

## Audio / performance rule

Canonical manuscript remains literary prose.

Derived audio workflow may create a speaker-routed performance document:

- Greg narration/dialogue -> Greg voice
- Dragon direct speech -> Dragon voice
- Other characters remain inside Greg's audiobook performance unless later authority deliberately expands the cast
- Dragon may use the same core voice source at slower natural playback speed as currently being explored

The performance layer may adjust pauses, chunk boundaries, and delivery markup but must not silently become story authority.
