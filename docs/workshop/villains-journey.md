# Villain's Journey / Reverse Hero's Journey

> **WORKSHOP NOTE ONLY — NOT AN ENGINE RULE**
>
> This document preserves a structural idea for future workshop/reference. It is not a PLG writing rule, outline, required trajectory, beat sheet, or scene generator. Do not change prose or manufacture story beats to satisfy this theory.

## The old idea

An older structural theory: take the Hero's Journey and explore it in reverse order or direction. "Villain's Journey" does not necessarily mean a journey about a villain. The useful idea is the reversal of conventional heroic progression: beginning from something like the completed heroic state and moving counter to the expected order.

## Possible relationship to Peg-Leg Greg

PLG may have developed an interesting accidental relationship to this idea.

Greg begins with qualities many protagonists acquire near the end of a heroic story: extreme experience, former power, survival history, knowledge, scars, and an already-completed legendary life. His present progression can sometimes move in the opposite external direction:

- legend toward person
- exceptional toward ordinary
- world-scale importance toward local importance
- followers toward peers
- destiny toward obligation
- adventure toward home

The external heroic trajectory can move backward while Greg's human trajectory still moves forward.

A useful shorthand:

> **Greg is not climbing toward becoming extraordinary. He already did that. He may be climbing toward having an ordinary life that matters to him.**

That can make apparently small gains meaningful. A job, a route, a favor, a key, a coworker, a reputation for being useful, or somewhere people expect Greg to return can function as genuine progression without needing to escalate him toward conventional heroic importance.

A related possible inversion is scale. A conventional heroic story often widens importance from home to village to kingdom to world. Greg's movement can sometimes contract from world-class importance toward city, neighborhood, workplace, individual relationships, and specific practical obligations while the meaning of those things increases rather than decreases.

One possible endpoint implied by the idea is not that Greg finally becomes capable of leaving the ordinary world for adventure. He has already done that. It may be that he becomes capable of having an ordinary world he does not want to leave.

## Guardrail

Do not retrofit the manuscript to prove this theory. Do not force every arc to invert a Hero's Journey beat. Do not use this note as an automatic prompt for new scenes. If PLG naturally contradicts the theory later, PLG wins.

Keep this as a workshop lens only. It may be useful when thinking about large-scale structure, diagnosing an arc, or searching for a different direction during future workshop sessions.
