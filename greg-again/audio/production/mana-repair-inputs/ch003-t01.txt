Future knowledge was much easier to spend before I tried to buy anything with it.

The east market gave me three possibilities in the first hour.

One was wrong.

One was real but useless for another twenty years.

The third was a pile of gray stone chips beside a masonry stall.

Kestrin shale.

I stopped.

Cheap. Brittle. Mildly mah-nah-reactive. Mostly used where nobody cared if it cracked.

Later, it mattered.

I knew that.

Probably.

There was that word again.

I remembered pale filtration housings stamped with the Arwick mark. Old Kestrin waste binding alchemical residue under heat. Finished filters cheap enough that nobody important bothered to think about them.

That was all.

No formula. No inventor. No exact year.

I had the ending of a story and none of the useful middle.

Still better than two copper.

The stall owner wanted two silver for the cart.

I had two copper.

Right.

A bucket was one copper.

I bought the bucket.

Growth.

The old me would have bought the cart because scale felt like confidence.

Current me had just been hit in the ribs by Jorren and nearly walked into vegetables while practicing footwork.

A bucket was enough to be wrong with.
