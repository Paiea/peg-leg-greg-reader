A month later, Sella stopped asking for my guild token.

That was how I noticed the month.

Not the calendar. Not the tally marks beside my bed. Not the increasingly complicated page where I had attempted to track training, debt, food, mah-nah, shale, and several financial decisions that became less defensible when arranged chronologically.

Sella looked up when I entered, reached beneath the counter, and slid the yard register toward me before I had finished reaching for the token.

Habit.

Mine and hers.

That was time.

Jorren was leaning against the end of the counter in new boots.

That was time too.

I noticed the boots because the old pair had split along the inside heel three weeks ago and he had complained about the repair cost for most of a spar-ring session.

"You got the escort."

He looked down.

"You remember that?"

I had forgotten his name twice during our first week.

Apparently my memory had priorities.

The north-road caravan left in nine days.

Jorren had spent the month earning enough contract credit to get taken on. He had done this while I was busy proving that future knowledge became substantially less impressive when converted into rent, training fees, experimental ceramics, loan interest, and food.
