TEXT READER · CHAPTER 103

THE SHAPER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Hessa had two copper strips.

This seemed aggressive.

I looked at them.

She looked at me.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You are already making theories.”

“I am identifying inventory.”

“Sit.”

I sat.

The chair remained bad.

I appreciated that some things in life had standards.

My right calf was mildly sore from Pessa.

Not pain.

Work.

Hip similar.

Hands fine.

Residual limb baseline.

I told Hessa before she asked.

She asked anyway.

“What did Pessa do?”

“Made me turn.”

Hessa waited.

“That is the medically relevant summary.”

“Fall?”

“No.”

“Near fall?”

“One skid. One hop. She caught my arm.”

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Swelling after?”

“No.”

“Today?”

“Baseline.”

“Hands?”

“Fine.”

She nodded.

No questions about circles.

No questions about left turns.

No questions about my theory.

Good.

Different jurisdiction.

Still not saying it.

Hessa examined the residual limb.

Nothing interesting.

Then she sat across from me.

Two copper strips on the table.

One straight.

One bent into a shallow curve.

I stared.

“Those are different.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because they are.”

“That is not enough information.”

“It is for now.”

I leaned back.

“What are we doing?”

“Drawing.”

I tried not to look disappointed.

Failed.

Hessa saw.

“First.”

My pulse improved.

“First?”

“Minimal draw. Same as before.”

“Then?”

“If it is boring, maybe something else.”

There it was.

Maybe.

My least favorite word.

I closed my eyes.

Hessa used the straight copper strip.

“Draw.”

Mana came.

Easier again.

Chest.

Shoulders.

Arms.

Right side.

Left thigh.

Knee.

Below the knee, nothing unusual.

Ordinary phantom foot present.

No magical extension I could distinguish.

Hold.

Release.

Done.

Boring.

Beautiful.

Hessa asked the usual questions.

No pain.

No heat.

No cold.

No pressure.

No cramping.

No unintended shaping.

Residual limb unchanged.

Hands unchanged.

She wrote.

I waited.

She put the straight copper aside.

Picked up the curved one.

My heart began doing something embarrassing.

“Hessa.”

“Eyes open.”

I opened them.

She placed the curved strip in front of me.

Not touching.

Just there.

“What is it?”

“Copper.”

“I know.”

“Then we're making progress.”

I refused to reward that.

“What does the curve do?”

“Nothing useful.”

“Then why?”

“Because you have never trained with it.”

I went still.

There.

The shaping test.

Maybe had become now.

Hessa watched my face.

“This is not casting clearance.”

“I know.”

“It is not permission to shape outside this room.”

“I know.”

“It is not permission to repeat whatever happens.”

“I know.”

“Say it.”

“I am allowed to attempt exactly what you instruct while you supervise. Nothing else.”

“Good.”

I looked at the copper.

“What am I doing?”

“Draw the same small amount.”

“Yes.”

“Hold it.”

“Yes.”

“Then move the smallest amount you can deliberately distinguish into the pad of your right thumb.”

I blinked.

“That is shaping?”

“It is deliberate alteration.”

“Routing.”

“Maybe.”

“You hate categories.”

“I hate categories before evidence.”

Fair.

“Why thumb?”

“Right side is intact. Thumb is small. It is not part of any spell form I know you trained as a distinct first step.”

“What if it is?”

“Is it?”

I searched memory.

Barrier.

Pulse.

The ugly little fire-light thing.

Early academy exercises.

No.

Not specifically thumb pad.

“No.”

“Good.”

“And the curved copper?”

“I am going to hold it near your thumb.”

“To detect the change?”

“To give me another witness.”

“Does the curve matter?”

“No.”

“Then why curve it?”

“So you do not unconsciously treat it as the same test.”

I stared.

“That is manipulative.”

“Yes.”

Excellent.

She held the curved copper near my right hand.

Not touching.

“Draw.”

I drew.

Small.

Familiar.

Hold.

“Now,” she said, “do not make a spell. Do not reach outward. Do not increase the draw. Put only enough mana into the pad of your right thumb that you can tell you changed something.”

This sounded easy.

That was the problem.

I reached for the change.

My body knew what to do.

Too well.

Mana shifted down the arm.

Not to the thumb.

Across the palm.

Wrist rotated slightly.

Forearm tightened.

Something in my chest opened.

Barrier.

Not the spell.

The beginning.

The old setup.

I stopped.

Hessa said, “Release.”

I already was.

Mana dropped away.

My breath came faster.

Nothing hurt.

Hessa put the copper down.

“Report.”

“I failed.”

“Report.”

Fine.

“Draw stable.”

“Yes.”

“I attempted to move a small amount toward right thumb.”

“Yes.”

“Instead the mana spread into the palm and wrist.”

“Yes.”

“Forearm engaged.”

“Yes.”

“My wrist rotated.”

“Yes.”

“Then I felt the beginning of Barrier setup.”

“Did you shape Barrier?”

“No.”

“Did mana leave the body?”

“No.”

“Did you increase draw?”

“I don't think so.”

“I didn't observe a meaningful increase.”

“Copper?”

“Responded.”

“To what?”

“The redistribution.”

My pulse jumped.

“So I shaped.”

Hessa raised one finger.

“You altered.”

“That is shaping.”

“Maybe.”

“Hessa.”

“You deliberately attempted one alteration. The result was not the alteration I asked for.”

I looked at my thumb.

Normal thumb.

Traitor.

“Again.”

“No.”

Of course.

“One attempt tells us almost nothing.”

“It told us something.”

“That old pattern is automatic.”

“Maybe.”

“I felt Barrier.”

“You felt something you recognized as Barrier setup.”

“That is the same thing.”

“No.”

“It is very close.”

“Yes.”

“So again.”

“No.”

I leaned back.

Bad chair.

Excellent enemy.

“What did you see?”

Hessa took her time.

“Stable draw. Then a broader shift through the right hand than requested. Wrist and forearm movement. Channel change consistent with deliberate alteration. No external release. No spell completion. No concerning physical response.”

“And Barrier?”

“I am not inside your head.”

“You know my Barrier form.”

“I know parts of your casting habits from before.”

“Did it look like Barrier?”

“It looked familiar.”

There.

Good enough.

Bad enough.

I smiled.

Hessa frowned.

“Why are you happy?”

“I shaped mana.”

“You failed the instruction.”

“I shaped mana badly.”

“That is true.”

I laughed.

She let me.

Not long.

Then she checked my hand.

Temperature.

Pulse.

Grip.

No tremor.

Residual limb.

No change.

Phantom foot?

Normal.

No organized fullness.

No extension.

Nothing relevant.

Good.

The left leg had contributed nothing unusual.

I appreciated its restraint.

“What now?”

“Tea.”

“That is not a magical progression.”

“It is today.”

She poured.

I took the cup with the right hand.

My thumb worked.

This felt absurdly meaningful.

“Does this count as clearance?”

“No.”

“I know. I wanted to hear you say it.”

“Why?”

“Consistency.”

She drank.

I looked at the curved copper.

“Why did it respond if I did the wrong thing?”

“Because it was not checking success. It was helping me confirm that something changed.”

“So it cannot tell thumb from palm.”

“No.”

“Could you?”

“Broadly.”

“Broadly.”

“Yes.”

I hated that word less now.

“What would success have looked like?”

“To you?”

“Yes.”

“A smaller localized change with less accompanying wrist and forearm pattern.”

“And if I can't?”

“We find out.”

“Could the old pattern be impossible to suppress?”

“We do not know.”

“Could it be useful?”

“Later question.”

“Could relearning involve replacing old forms?”

“Later.”

“Could old forms be adapted?”

“Later.”

“Could Barrier eventually come back?”

She looked at me.

That was the real question.

I had hidden it badly.

“Hessa.”

“Maybe.”

I exhaled.

Not because I expected more.

Because maybe was not no.

Dangerous.

She saw that too.

“Do not build a future out of one failed thumb.”

“That is a cruel sentence.”

“It is accurate.”

I drank tea.

The excitement did not go away.

Neither did the irritation.

Good combination.

“What is next test?”

“Probably the same instruction.”

“Same thumb?”

“Probably.”

“Same position?”

“Probably seated.”

“Same copper?”

“Maybe.”

“Why not tomorrow?”

“No.”

“I didn't ask when.”

“You were about to.”

“When?”

“Day after tomorrow.”

I stared.

“You are obsessed with boring days.”

“I am interested in delayed effects.”

“There were none before.”

“Before was draw only.”

Right.

This was different.

Tiny.

Still different.

“Can I train with Pessa tomorrow?”

“Medically, if you remain at baseline, I do not object to you asking her.”

“Same answer.”

“Same question.”

“Can I tell her I shaped?”

“You can tell her what happened.”

“She will say she doesn't care.”

“Probably.”

I smiled.

“Can I tell Jorren?”

Hessa looked confused.

“Why are you asking me?”

“I don't know.”

“You may tell your friend you failed to move mana into your thumb.”

“That wording is hostile.”

“It is accurate.”

I thought about Jorren's response.

Probably:

Good.

That was somehow irritating in advance.

“Can I write it down?”

“Yes.”

“Paragraphs?”

“Yes.”

“No table?”

She stared.

“Sorry.”

I finished the tea.

Hessa checked my hand once more.

Then:

“Stand.”

Both crutches.

Right foot.

Push.

No problem.

My right thumb tingled.

I froze.

“Hessa.”

She was immediately attentive.

“What?”

“Thumb tingling.”

“How much?”

“Very mild.”

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Numb?”

“No.”

“Cold?”

“No.”

She touched the thumb.

Then fingers.

Had me squeeze.

Release.

Touch each fingertip.

Normal.

“Still?”

“Yes. Less.”

She waited.

We waited.

The tingling faded.

“Gone.”

She wrote.

I looked at the notebook.

“Concerning?”

“Not currently.”

“Relevant?”

“Yes.”

“Delayed effect?”

“Immediate minor effect.”

“Could be from gripping the crutch.”

“Yes.”

“Could be from shaping.”

“Yes.”

“Could be attention.”

“Yes.”

“Useful.”

“Maybe.”

I laughed.

She did not.

“Greg.”

“I know.”

“Report if it returns.”

“I will.”

“No self-test.”

“I will not.”

“No thumb experiments.”

“I will not.”

“No Barrier rehearsal.”

“I will not.”

“No trying to reproduce the wrist movement without mana as a magical exercise.”

I paused.

“That is very specific.”

“Because I know you.”

Fair.

I left.

Outside, I looked at my right thumb.

Not because I was testing it.

Because it was attached to my hand.

Important legal distinction.

I had shaped mana.

Badly.

Into the wrong place.

With an old reflex trying to take over.

Possibly.

One tiny deliberate failure.

I was so happy I nearly walked into a barrel.

The barrel did not care.

Carrow remained consistent.
