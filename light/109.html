LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 109

THE STOPPER

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Hessa drew a line on my forearm.

Not a dot.

Progress.

“Charcoal again?”

“Yes.”

“You have limited artistic range.”

“I am devastated.”

The line was short.

Maybe the width of my little finger.

It sat on the inside of my right forearm, several inches above the wrist.

I looked at it.

Then at Hessa.

“New target.”

“Not exactly.”

“What does that mean?”

“It means today I care less about reaching the mark.”

I stopped.

That was different.

My right hand had stayed quiet since the second tingling episode.

No recurrence after the successful shaping attempt.

No recurrence during cards.

No recurrence overnight.

No pain.

No weakness.

No numbness.

No cold.

Residual limb baseline.

Hands fine.

Right calf fine.

Everything boring enough that I had become suspicious of boredom again.

Hessa had examined me.

Then forearm.

Then hand.

Then drawn the line.

“What's the question?”

“Whether you can stop.”

“I stopped last time.”

“You released.”

“At your instruction.”

“Yes.”

“That is stopping.”

“Not the same thing.”

I leaned back in the bad chair.

“Explain.”

“No.”

“Hessa.”

She put the straight copper strip on the table.

Folded towel.

No ceramic dish this time.

The charcoal nub remained in her hand.

She added a second small line farther up the forearm.

Two marks now.

I stared.

“That is worse.”

“Why?”

“Two marks imply a choice.”

“Yes.”

“Which one?”

“You don't know yet.”

“I dislike this.”

“Good.”

“You are enjoying this.”

“No.”

She set the charcoal aside.

“Minimal draw first.”

“Yes.”

“Then I will tell you which line.”

“During the draw?”

“Yes.”

“And I alter toward it?”

“Yes.”

“How far?”

“Until I tell you stop.”

There.

My pulse changed.

“Then?”

“You stop altering.”

“Hold the draw?”

“Yes.”

That was the new part.

Not release.

Stop the alteration while keeping the draw stable.

I sat more upright.

Hessa noticed.

“Do not get excited.”

“That instruction has never worked.”

“One attempt.”

“Yes.”

“If you feel the familiar broad hand-and-wrist pattern, stop and release.”

“Yes.”

“If you feel pain, tingling, weakness, cold, numbness, or anything you think is wrong, stop and release.”

“Yes.”

“If your wrist begins moving.”

“Stop and release.”

“If I say stop.”

“Stop altering but keep the draw.”

“Yes.”

“For how long?”

“Until I tell you release.”

“That introduces timing.”

“I know.”

“How long?”

“You do not.”

“Cruel.”

“Useful.”

Maybe.

No.

Not saying that word.

I looked at the two lines.

One nearer the wrist.

One farther up.

Both on intact right forearm.

No left side.

No phantom question.

Good.

“What if I cannot tell whether the alteration stopped?”

“Report that.”

“What if I overshoot?”

“Report that.”

“What counts as overshoot?”

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

I put my right forearm on the towel.

Palm up.

The lines faced me.

Hessa rotated my arm slightly so they faced more toward her.

“Do not use vision once we start.”

“You drew them where I can see them.”

“Now you know where they are.”

“Approximate.”

“Enough.”

She raised the copper.

“Ready?”

I breathed.

No rehearsal.

No trying to pre-build the sensation.

Just ready.

“Yes.”

“Draw.”

I drew.

Mana came.

Small.

Stable.

Chest.

Shoulders.

Arms.

Right side.

Left thigh.

Knee.

Ordinary phantom foot.

Nothing unusual below.

Right hand quiet.

Forearm quiet.

“Stable?”

“Yes.”

“Far line.”

I almost looked.

Did not.

Alter.

Toward the farther line.

This was not hand.

Forearm felt different.

Less precise.

Or I was less familiar with attending to it.

Mana did not move like water.

Again.

Stop using water.

Something shifted.

A narrow band?

No.

A patch.

Longer than last time.

Along the inside of the forearm.

Moving upward.

Not physically.

Magically.

Probably.

I wanted to know where it was.

No visual check.

Hessa said, “Stop.”

I stopped.

Or tried.

The alteration continued for a fraction.

Maybe.

Then held.

My entire attention narrowed.

Draw still present.

Alteration no longer moving.

I thought.

Then the patch widened.

Fuck.

Not upward.

Sideways.

Broader across the forearm.

I had stopped direction, not change.

“It's spreading,” I said.

“Release.”

I released.

Everything went.

My forearm remained on the towel.

Hessa lowered the copper.

“Report.”

“I failed.”

“Report.”

“Draw stable.”

“Yes.”

“You called far line.”

“Yes.”

“I made a localized change moving toward it.”

“Continue.”

“When you said stop, I stopped the movement toward the line.”

“Think.”

“I attempted to.”

“Better.”

“I think it stopped moving upward.”

“Yes.”

“Then the altered area widened sideways.”

“Yes.”

“I did not intend that.”

“Yes.”

“No wrist movement.”

“I saw none.”

“No hand spread.”

“I saw none.”

“No Barrier-like setup.”

“I saw none.”

“Pain none. Tingling none. Numbness none. Cold none. Weakness none.”

“Below knee?”

“Ordinary phantom only.”

“Did you feel the widening before you reported it?”

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“Very short.”

“That is not a duration.”

“You trained me.”

“No.”

I looked at the forearm.

No visible change.

Only charcoal.

“Copper?”

“Responded during the alteration.”

“During the widening?”

“Yes.”

That mattered.

“Then it was real.”

“It was observable.”

“Hessa.”

“Yes.”

“Fuck you.”

“Consistent language.”

I laughed.

She wrote.

I waited.

This time I did not ask immediately.

Personal record.

Five breaths.

Maybe six.

Not timing.

She finished.

“What did you see?”

“A more elongated alteration than the prior hand target.”

“Toward far line?”

“Yes.”

“Did it reach?”

“No.”

“How far?”

“I am not giving you inches.”

“Coward.”

“Yes.”

“Then after stop?”

“The forward progression reduced.”

“Reduced?”

“Yes.”

“Not stopped?”

“Not cleanly enough for me to call it stopped.”

That hurt.

Fine.

“And widened.”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

“Enough to distinguish.”

“So I did not stop.”

“You changed what it was doing.”

I looked up.

There.

“What?”

“The alteration did not continue in exactly the same way after the stop instruction.”

“But it kept changing.”

“Yes.”

“So I partially controlled it.”

“Maybe.”

I groaned.

“Use your own word.”

“I am.”

“What is the question now?”

“Whether you can stop deliberate shaping without releasing the draw.”

“And answer?”

“Not cleanly from this attempt.”

“Because it widened.”

“Yes.”

“But it changed direction.”

“Perhaps.”

“Perhaps?”

“Your report says forward movement stopped. My observation says the pattern changed. That does not prove you deliberately redirected it. It may have broadened because control degraded.”

That was worse.

Also better.

I hated both.

“So the failure is specific.”

“Yes.”

“I can initiate a small unfamiliar alteration.”

“We have some evidence for that.”

“I can respond to a stop instruction enough to change the pattern.”

“We have one observation consistent with that.”

“But I cannot yet hold it unchanged.”

“We do not know if you can. You did not today.”

There.

Clean.

I nodded.

No breakthrough.

No collapse.

A narrow failure.

Useful.

“What would happen if you told me stop earlier?”

“I am not testing that today.”

“Of course.”

“One attempt.”

“Yes.”

“Do you understand why?”

“Because repetition trains.”

“And?”

“Because widening was unintended.”

“And?”

“Because you want delayed observation.”

“Yes.”

“And because you hate me.”

“No.”

“Unproven.”

She examined my forearm.

Temperature.

Skin.

Hand.

Grip.

Wrist.

Normal.

Then waited.

I waited too.

This part had become familiar in the worst possible way.

The bad chair.

The wall crack.

Nerin moving in the outer room.

Someone talking quietly.

Street noise.

No thumb tingling.

No forearm tingling.

No pain.

Nothing.

I looked at the two charcoal lines.

“Can I wash them off?”

“Not yet.”

“Again?”

“Yes.”

“What if charcoal is causing the magical effect?”

Hessa stared.

“I wanted to see if you had limits.”

“There they are.”

“Good.”

I leaned back.

“What does this do to casting prognosis?”

“Almost nothing.”

“Almost?”

“It gives me another piece of evidence that you can deliberately initiate alteration.”

“Yes.”

“And another piece that control after initiation is not yet clean.”

“Yes.”

“That is unsurprising.”

“Rude.”

“It is early.”

There.

Early.

Not impossible.

Not recovered.

Early.

I liked that word too much.

“What about spells that don't require holding?”

“Later.”

“What about spells where release is the end?”

“Later.”

“What about Barrier?”

“Later.”

“Could releasing have been the correct stop?”

“For a spell, perhaps. Not for today's question.”

“Because you wanted separation between altering and drawing.”

“Yes.”

“And the ability to stop one without ending the other.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

She paused.

I waited.

Actually waited.

Then she answered.

“Because if every shaping change can only end by dropping the entire draw, you have less control than I want before testing anything with an external effect.”

That shut me up.

External effect.

Not next.

Not promised.

But category.

There.

I did not smile.

Probably.

Hessa saw anyway.

“No.”

“I said nothing.”

“You heard future.”

“I heard criteria.”

“You heard future.”

“Both can be true.”

She sighed.

“What would external mean?”

“Not today.”

“I know.”

“Then do not make me define a test I am not planning.”

“Fine.”

We waited.

No symptoms.

Eventually Hessa said, “Move fingers.”

Normal.

“Wrist.”

Normal.

“Grip.”

Normal.

“Stand.”

Crutches.

Right hand grip.

Up.

No tingling.

Walk to door.

Back.

No symptoms.

She let me wash the charcoal off.

Two gray streaks.

Gone.

“Tomorrow?” I asked.

“No magic.”

I closed my eyes.

“You have only one tool.”

“It works.”

“Day after?”

“Come back.”

“Assessment.”

“Yes.”

“Stop test again?”

“Maybe.”

“Different target?”

“Maybe.”

“Draw only?”

“Maybe.”

“You have ruined that word.”

“You keep coming back.”

Fair.

I stood.

At the door:

“Hessa.”

“What?”

“If I had held the change after stop, would that have counted as success?”

She considered.

“For today's instruction, yes.”

“Good.”

“You did not.”

“I know.”

I smiled.

That confused her slightly.

Good.

Failure with a shape.

Better than fog.

I left.

Outside, I had no dramatic desire to tell everyone.

That surprised me.

I still wanted to tell Lyssa.

And Jorren.

Maybe Alden.

But not immediately.

The result was less shiny.

More technical.

I liked it anyway.

I went to the Guild.

Merrin looked up.

“Work?”

I thought.

Maybe.

No.

I wanted something else.

“What is everyone doing?”

“That is not a work request.”

“I know.”

He pointed toward the yard.

“Alden and Lio are arguing about distance.”

Good.

I went.

Not training.

Watching.

The yard was active.

Pessa had three people moving between marked lines.

Alden stood near the wall with Lio, both looking at an arrow stuck in a target.

Alden saw me.

“Settle this.”

“No.”

“You don't know the question.”

“Preventive.”

He looked offended.

Lio laughed.

“What is it?”

Alden pointed.

“That's twelve paces.”

Lio said, “Eleven.”

I looked.

“Whose paces?”

Silence.

Good.

I had them.

Alden said, “Normal.”

“No such thing.”

“Fuck.”

Lio walked it.

Eleven of his.

Alden walked it.

Twelve of his.

They both stared.

I sat on a barrel.

“Congratulations.”

“That is stupid,” Alden said.

“Yes.”

“We need a measure.”

“Yes.”

Lio looked at me.

“You have one?”

“No.”

“Then you're useless.”

“Correct.”

This felt excellent.

Pessa glanced over.

Saw me.

“Training?”

I checked.

Did I want to?

Body baseline.

Hand fine.

Magic test done.

Hessa had not forbidden movement.

But I had already spent attention today.

The stop test had taken more out of me mentally than physically.

I wanted to sit.

“No.”

Pessa nodded.

Nothing else.

Alden said, “Coward.”

Octavia had spread too.

“Apparently.”

I watched them argue about how to standardize distance without a measuring cord.

They eventually used the length of a practice spear laid repeatedly on the ground.

Not precise.

Better.

Lio's “eleven” and Alden's “twelve” became something like six-and-a-bit spear lengths.

They were both dissatisfied.

Good.

The target did not care.

Alden shot.

Missed left.

Lio shot.

Hit outer ring.

Alden blamed the distance change.

Pessa said, “You were standing in the same place.”

Jorren appeared from nowhere with an apple.

Of course.

“Magic?”

he asked.

“Failed.”

“Good.”

Alden lowered the bow.

“What?”

I had made a mistake.

Now there was an audience.

“Not failed magic. Failed instruction.”

“What instruction?”

“Stop.”

Jorren bit apple.

“You failed stopping?”

“Yes.”

“That sounds like you.”

“Fuck you.”

Alden handed bow back to Lio.

“Explain.”

I did the short version.

New forearm target.

Shape toward.

Hessa says stop.

Hold draw.

Alteration stops going forward but widens.

Release.

No symptoms.

No spell.

Alden frowned.

“So you shaped again.”

“Yes.”

“And failed.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Everyone had one word.

I hated all of them.

Lio said, “Could you feel it widen?”

“Yes.”

“Did it hurt?”

“No.”

“Could you move it back?”

“Not tested.”

“Why not?”

“Hessa.”

He nodded immediately.

Professional reputation.

Useful.

Pessa had listened without appearing to.

She said, “Hand fine?”

“Yes.”

“Then I don't care.”

Perfect.

Jorren handed me apple.

I took it.

“Knife?”

I asked.

He showed the dark handle at his belt.

“Always.”

“You are becoming a person who carries a food knife.”

“I carry food.”

“That is not true.”

“I intend to.”

Alden said, “Can we go back to distance?”

“No,” Jorren said.

“It was better.”

I laughed.

Then watched.

No theory.

No notebook.

Just arrows.

Alden adjusted.

Lio corrected something in his shoulder.

Pessa corrected both.

I ate apple.

At one point, my phantom left foot felt planted.

Ordinary.

I noticed.

Did nothing.

Later it was gone.

No connection.

Good.

Lyssa found me near evening.

Not because I had summoned her.

She came through the Guild yard looking for someone else first.

A woman from the freight side waved to her.

Lyssa crossed over, spoke briefly, handed her a small wrapped bundle, accepted coins, then finally looked around and saw me.

Independent business.

Good.

She came over.

“You're sitting.”

“Apparently suspicious.”

“To everyone.”

“Yes.”

She looked at the practice target.

“Training?”

“No.”

“Magic?”

“Earlier.”

Her eyes changed.

“How?”

“I shaped and failed to stop.”

“That sounds complicated.”

“It was.”

“Did it hurt?”

“No.”

“Are you allowed?”

“One attempt. Supervised.”

“Hessa?”

“Yes.”

“Did she look angry?”

“Normal face.”

Lyssa smiled.

“Then good.”

There it was.

Again.

I told her the short version.

She listened.

Not pretending to understand channel mechanics.

“What happens next?”

“No magic tomorrow.”

“You hate that.”

“Yes.”

“Then?”

“Assessment.”

“More stopping?”

“Maybe.”

She laughed before I could react.

Cruel.

“I hate that word.”

“I know.”

We walked out together.

Not a date.

Not declared.

She had finished work.

I was done watching arrows.

We were both hungry.

That was enough.

At the first food stall, she chose something with too much sauce.

I chose the same.

Bad strategy.

Good food.

We ate standing for her and sitting for me on a low wall.

Lyssa got sauce on one finger and wiped it on the paper wrapping.

“You're staring.”

“At the food.”

“You're terrible at lying.”

“I had a whole card chapter about this.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

She took another bite.

I looked at her, then at the street.

People were closing shutters one at a time. A cart rolled past with one wheel making a soft repeated click. Someone farther down the road was arguing about whether a basket belonged inside or outside a doorway.

Carrow did not care that I had shaped mana.

Good.

Lyssa finished first.

“You want mine?”

“Yes.”

“You answered before I finished.”

“You were offering food.”

“I could have been asking whether you wanted the paper.”

“I would still have said yes eventually.”

She handed me the last two bites.

My hand stayed normal.

My residual limb stayed normal.

No spell had happened.

I had shaped mana and failed to stop it cleanly.

That was progress.

Not because failure was secretly success.

Because it was finally specific enough to be wrong.

I liked that.
