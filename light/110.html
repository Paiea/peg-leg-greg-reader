TEXT READER · CHAPTER 110

THE PARTNER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

I wanted to train.

This was inconvenient.

Not because anything hurt.

Nothing hurt.

Hand normal.

Thumb quiet.

Residual limb baseline.

Right calf fine.

Shoulders fine.

Hessa had forbidden magic for the day, which left an entire category of bad decisions unavailable.

So after breakfast I went to the Guild yard and found Pessa.

She was carrying six short wooden stakes.

“Training?” she asked.

“Yes.”

She handed me two stakes.

I stared at them.

“What are these?”

“Stakes.”

“I can see that.”

“Good.”

Then she kept walking.

I followed.

Two crutches.

Two stakes.

Problem.

I put both stakes under my left arm, which made the left crutch awkward. One slipped.

Pessa did not turn around.

I picked it up.

Eventually we reached the back area near the storage shed where we had done the shield work.

Alden was not there.

Lio was not there.

No audience.

Pessa took the stakes back.

“Did Hessa say you could?”

“She said no magic today.”

“That was not my question.”

“She has no medical objection to me asking you about training if everything remains baseline.”

“Everything?”

“Hand normal. No tingling. Residual limb baseline. No new pain.”

“Yesterday?”

“Magic assessment. No injury. Mentally annoying.”

“Today?”

“I want to train.”

She nodded.

That was apparently enough to begin.

She drove the stakes into packed dirt.

Not a line.

A shape.

Four points.

Irregular.

One near the wall.

One farther out.

One slightly behind me.

One close enough that I could touch it with a crutch tip if I leaned.

“What?”

“Walk.”

“Pattern?”

“No.”

“Order?”

“No.”

“Destination?”

“I'll tell you.”

I looked at her.

“Same turning problem.”

“Maybe.”

There it was.

I started.

“Far stake.”

I went.

Not fast.

Right crutch.

Left.

Right foot.

Swing.

Again.

At the stake:

“Wall.”

Turn.

Left turn.

My head moved first.

Shoulders followed.

Pelvis started.

Preserved left knee flexed.

There.

The old request.

Missing support.

I caught it before the right foot hopped.

Maybe.

Or the crutch caught it.

Or speed was low enough that nothing dramatic happened.

I reached the wall stake.

“Behind.”

I turned right.

Crutches crowded.

Left tip clipped right shaft.

I stopped.

Pessa said nothing.

“Again?”

“Close stake.”

I went.

This was familiar.

Not the route.

The frustration.

Every turn contained too many things.

Gaze.

Shoulders.

Pelvis.

Right foot.

Two crutches.

Speed.

Destination.

Surface.

The missing part my body sometimes still included in the plan.

I wanted one variable.

Pessa wanted me to move.

“Far.”

I moved.

“Behind.”

Turn.

Better.

“Wall.”

Left.

Bad.

The left knee flexed toward support that did not exist.

My right crutch landed late.

I corrected.

“Close.”

I reached.

“Stop.”

I stopped.

“What happened on wall?”

“Old left-side support request.”

“What else?”

“Right crutch late.”

“What else?”

I replayed.

Head.

Shoulders.

“Looked at the stake too early.”

“Maybe.”

“You saw it.”

“I saw your head.”

“Not the same thing.”

“No.”

Good.

Annoying.

“Again.”

We did it again.

Then she changed where she stood.

Not threatening.

Just occupying space.

“Far.”

I started.

Her body blocked the easiest line.

I adjusted around her.

“Wall.”

I turned.

She stepped.

Not into me.

Into where I had expected to put the left crutch.

I stopped hard.

“What the fuck?”

“You stopped.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“You were there.”

“Good.”

“That is not a training insight.”

“No.”

I went around.

She moved again.

This time I chose a wider line before reaching the stake.

“Behind.”

Right turn.

Crutches crowded less because I had space.

“Close.”

I moved.

She stepped away.

The route opened.

My body accelerated without permission.

Not much.

Enough.

Right foot landed farther forward.

Hands took more load.

I slowed.

“Why faster?” Pessa asked.

“Space opened.”

“Did I tell you faster?”

“No.”

“Again.”

This was worse than stakes.

People changed geometry.

Obviously.

But obvious was not the same as having to move through it.

“Far.”

Pessa stood still.

I went.

“Wall.”

She moved left.

I moved right.

“Behind.”

I turned.

“Stop.”

I stopped.

No skid.

No hop.

Ugly.

Stable enough.

“Again.”

After maybe twenty minutes my hands warmed.

Not bad.

Right hip working.

Calf beginning to notice.

Residual limb quiet.

Pessa moved one stake.

“Why?”

“Because it was there.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is literally the answer.”

She put it closer to the wall.

“Far.”

I went.

“New wall.”

Turn.

Less room.

My right crutch hit the wall before I expected.

The tip did not slip.

The shaft knocked wood.

I froze.

Pessa said, “Continue.”

I had to back the crutch out.

Right foot reposition.

Then move.

Awful.

“Again.”

“Can we identify what we're testing?”

“No.”

“You know.”

“I know what I'm watching.”

“That is different.”

“Yes.”

I hated this profession.

“Far.”

Again.

After another round she said, “One crutch.”

I looked at her.

“Which?”

“You choose.”

That mattered.

I chose the right crutch.

Left hand empty.

Same as one of the Ch102 configurations.

The first sensation returned almost immediately.

Exposed.

Not unstable yet.

Spatially exposed.

As though the missing left-side support had become more obvious because there was no left crutch occupying that side either.

“Far.”

I moved slower.

“Normal speed.”

“This is normal.”

“No.”

Fuck.

I increased.

Not fast.

Just less careful.

“Wall.”

Left turn.

My left hand lifted.

For balance?

For protection?

For nothing?

The preserved left knee flexed.

Pelvis started the old turn.

Right crutch planted.

Right foot pivoted.

I made it.

“Behind.”

Right turn.

Easier at first.

Then the single right crutch crossed too close to my right foot.

I shortened the step.

“Close.”

Pessa stepped into the open left side.

I changed direction away from her too sharply.

Right crutch planted wide.

My body leaned.

Right foot hopped.

Small.

Maybe an inch.

Pessa did not catch me.

I recovered.

“Stop.”

I stopped.

Breathing harder.

Not from distance.

From attention.

“What happened?”

“She entered the side that felt open.”

No.

“She entered my left side.”

Pessa waited.

“I moved away before I knew where I was moving to.”

“Better.”

“Right crutch went wide.”

“Yes.”

“Right foot hopped.”

“Yes.”

“Left knee tried to do something.”

“What?”

“Don't know.”

“Good.”

I looked at her.

“You enjoy that answer now.”

“I enjoy you not making one up.”

Fair.

She gave me the left crutch back.

Two again.

Immediately less exposed.

Also more crowded.

Trade.

Of course.

No solution.

We rested.

Actually rested.

I sat on the same ugly ordinary chair from before.

Pessa drank water.

I drank water.

Neither of us explained recovery.

Alden appeared at the edge of the yard.

Of course.

He saw me.

Saw Pessa.

Saw stakes.

His face lit up.

“No,” Pessa said.

“I didn't ask.”

“No.”

“I can stand here.”

“No.”

“This is discrimination.”

“Go shoot.”

“I already shot.”

“Shoot again.”

Alden looked at me.

I shrugged.

He left.

Mostly.

He went far enough away to plausibly be doing something else.

“Persistent,” I said.

“So are you.”

“Different.”

“No.”

We rested longer.

My hands cooled slightly.

Right calf still fine enough.

Residual limb quiet.

Pessa crouched and moved two stakes again.

“Shield?” I asked.

“No.”

“Why?”

“Do you need one to make this difficult?”

“No.”

“Then no.”

Good.

Disappointing.

Both.

Second block was shorter.

Two crutches only.

Before we started, Pessa put one stake on its side.

I looked at it.

“Why?”

“Because it's on its side.”

“You did that.”

“Yes.”

“So it is deliberate.”

“Yes.”

“Then it represents something.”

“It represents a stake on its side.”

I hated her.

“Walk.”

The fallen stake made the route narrower without actually blocking it.

I could step over it with the right foot.

Probably.

But the crutches complicated that.

A tip could land beyond it.

A shaft could catch.

The left knee could not contribute a foot that did not exist.

I went around.

Pessa said nothing.

Next pass, she called the destination that put the fallen stake directly between me and the shortest route.

I went around again.

“Why?”

“Safer.”

“Than?”

“Stepping over it.”

“Did you try?”

“No.”

“So you don't know.”

“I know enough.”

“Maybe.”

I stopped.

“Do you want me to step over it?”

“I want you to choose.”

“That is not helpful.”

“It is to me.”

Fine.

Next pass I tried.

Right crutch beyond stake.

Left crutch near side.

Right foot lifted.

Too much attention went down.

Destination vanished.

I cleared the wood.

Then the left crutch shaft caught the end as I moved.

The stake rolled.

Not far.

Enough to make me freeze.

“Continue,” Pessa said.

I continued.

Ugly.

At the destination I looked back.

“Going around was better.”

“Today.”

“Stop stealing my words.”

“No.”

The third time I went around.

Faster.

Cleaner.

Then Pessa stepped into that route.

I laughed.

Actually laughed.

“You're an asshole.”

“Move.”

I took the step-over route again.

This time I knew the crutch shaft could catch.

I placed the left crutch farther out.

Right foot over.

Destination still mostly visible.

No roll.

Better.

Not good.

Better.

Pessa picked the stake up.

“Why now?”

“Because I'm done with it.”

I had no response.

She won.

Pessa stopped calling destinations in predictable rhythm.

Sometimes immediately.

Sometimes after I had stood at a stake long enough to settle.

Sometimes while I was still arriving.

“Behind.”

Turn.

“Far.”

Move.

“Wall.”

I was halfway through correcting the crutches when she called it.

I tried to reverse.

Bad.

Everything stacked.

Right foot planted.

Left crutch too far back.

Right crutch forward.

Trunk already turning one direction.

New instruction opposite.

I stopped completely.

Pessa said, “Why?”

“Could not reverse cleanly.”

“Could you reverse badly?”

“Yes.”

“Then why stop?”

Because stopping felt safe.

There.

“I chose stop.”

“Good.”

I blinked.

“That was allowed?”

“I did not say it wasn't.”

“You wanted me to reverse.”

“I wanted to see what you did.”

“You are impossible.”

“Move.”

I reset.

She called another.

This time when the new direction arrived mid-turn, I did not reverse.

I finished the turn.

Then turned again.

Slower overall.

More steps.

But no tangled crutches.

Pessa watched.

“Why?”

“Because reversing inside the turn was worse.”

“Today.”

“Yes.”

There.

Not rule.

Today.

Next time I tried to finish first again.

It failed differently because the wall was closer.

My right crutch struck it.

I had to stop.

Pessa smiled.

Not kindly.

“Fuck you.”

“Again.”

We went until my right calf was clearly tired and both palms had that hot pressure that came before stupid choices.

Near the end she stood directly behind me.

Not close.

Close enough that I knew.

I was at the far stake.

She said nothing.

I waited.

Still nothing.

I turned my head.

“Do I have a destination?”

“No.”

“Then what am I doing?”

“Standing.”

“That seems beneath both of us.”

“Does it?”

I shifted weight through the right foot and crutches.

Left knee relaxed.

Phantom foot briefly felt as if the heel were touching dirt.

It was not.

I looked forward again.

Pessa walked around me once.

I tracked her with my eyes.

Not my body.

Then she moved behind me again.

The urge to turn came before any instruction.

Interesting.

I stayed.

“Why didn't you turn?” she asked.

“You didn't tell me to.”

“Would you in the yard?”

“If I thought someone behind me mattered.”

“Do I matter?”

“Unfortunately.”

She snorted.

Then, sharper:

“Behind.”

I turned.

Moderate.

Not the fast Ch102 mistake.

Not slow enough to make the cue meaningless.

Right crutch planted.

Head and shoulders moved.

Pelvis followed.

Left knee started its old contribution.

I did not solve it.

I kept moving.

Right foot pivoted.

Left crutch came around.

No skid.

No hop.

Not clean.

Pessa was already somewhere else when I finished.

“Again?” I asked.

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because you're tired.”

“I can do one more.”

“Yes.”

That was all she said.

Which was worse than no.

Pessa stopped.

I did not argue.

Personal growth.

Or fatigue.

Probably fatigue.

I sat.

She pulled the stakes.

“What did we learn?”

I hated that question.

“Nothing.”

“Good.”

I stared.

She continued.

“You moved.”

“That is not nothing.”

“You want a rule.”

“Yes.”

“There isn't one yet.”

“Maybe there is and we haven't found it.”

“Maybe.”

I considered the session.

Two crutches gave support and crowding.

One right crutch gave space and exposure.

People changed routes.

Open space changed speed.

Walls changed crutch placement.

Urgent direction changes made clean sequencing impossible.

Stopping was sometimes a choice.

Finishing one turn before starting another worked once and failed once.

No universal answer.

“What are you watching?” I asked.

Pessa pulled the last stake.

“Whether you can keep making choices after the first plan stops working.”

I went still.

That sounded larger than movement.

Dangerous.

I refused it.

“In this yard.”

Pessa looked at me.

“Yes. In this yard.”

Good.

Jurisdiction.

She carried the stakes away.

I stayed seated.

Alden reappeared instantly.

“Shield?”

“No.”

“Fight?”

“No.”

“One crutch?”

“Some.”

“Did you fall?”

“No.”

“Almost?”

“Not interestingly.”

He looked disappointed.

“You are a terrible storyteller.”

“Correct.”

“Pessa said I can't help.”

“She is wise.”

“You looked less terrible than last time.”

I looked at him.

“That sounded almost kind.”

“Don't make it weird.”

“What improved?”

He shrugged.

“You stopped less.”

That was observationally interesting.

Also from Alden.

Dangerous source.

“Maybe.”

“And you didn't almost fall when she yelled behind.”

“I did not almost fall last time.”

“She caught you.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“It looked like the same thing.”

I decided friendship had limits.

“She said not yet.”

I looked at him.

“Did she?”

“Yes.”

That was different from a promise.

I knew that now.

“What are you doing?”

“Lunch.”

“That's not training.”

“Correct.”

“Can I come?”

I thought.

“Yes.”

He looked surprised.

So was I.

We found Jorren before food.

Or Jorren found us.

He had bread.

Knife out.

Naturally.

“You two look sweaty.”

“Alden is not sweaty.”

“I trained emotionally.”

“No,” I said.

Jorren cut the bread.

“With the knife?” I asked.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because I own it.”

Alden nodded.

“Valid.”

Traitor.

We bought stew.

Sat outside.

My right calf relaxed.

Hands complained less once they were not carrying me.

Residual limb stayed quiet.

No thumb tingling.

No magic.

Hessa would be pleased by the absence of everything.

I did not tell her.

That would require going there.

No.

Alden talked about arrows.

Jorren talked about a woman at the fish market who had accused him of choosing a fish based on personality.

“Did you?” I asked.

“Yes.”

“Which personality?”

“Confident.”

“How do you identify confidence in a dead fish?”

“Eyes.”

“The eyes are dead.”

“Confidence persists.”

Alden nearly choked.

I laughed hard enough my side hurt.

Not injury.

Just stew and stupidity.

Later Alden left for actual training.

Jorren had somewhere to be and did not explain.

Good.

I went home.

At the lodging, the keeper had moved the ugly kitchen stool.

I noticed because it was no longer where I expected.

I almost asked why.

Did not.

Not my stool.

Upstairs took the usual time.

Fourteen steps.

Rail.

Pause.

No proof.

In my room I sat in the repaired tavern chair.

Tomorrow Hessa.

Today Pessa.

No magic.

No shield.

No fight.

No system.

I had spent an hour turning badly around wooden stakes while a woman deliberately stood in inconvenient places.

I wanted to do it again.

That was probably enough.
