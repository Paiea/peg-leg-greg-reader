TEXT READER · CHAPTER 111

THE BRAKE

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

My hands were mildly offended by yesterday.

Not injured.

Offended.

The distinction mattered because Hessa asked.

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Numbness?”

“No.”

“Weakness?”

“No.”

“Thumb?”

“Normal.”

“Residual limb?”

“Baseline.”

“Calf?”

“Tired yesterday. Fine this morning.”

“Palms?”

I opened both hands.

“Warm yesterday. Fine now.”

Hessa inspected them anyway.

She pressed the right thumb pad.

Then the palm.

Then wrist.

Then forearm.

Nothing.

She checked the residual limb because she was Hessa and because apparently no part of me was allowed to become irrelevant merely because today's question concerned my arm.

“Pessa?”

“Stakes.”

Hessa looked up.

“That is not an activity.”

“It was when she did it.”

“What did you do?”

“Walked around them badly.”

“Fall?”

“No.”

“Near fall?”

“Small hop once. No catch.”

“Hands?”

“Hot by the end.”

“Stopped?”

“She stopped me.”

“Good.”

I did not tell her that I had wanted another repetition after she stopped me.

It was not medically relevant.

Probably.

Hessa sat back.

“Today we draw.”

I tried not to look disappointed.

Failed.

She noticed.

“Do you want me to choose a test because your face is irritating?”

“No.”

“Good.”

“Are we shaping?”

“I have not decided.”

That was worse.

She prepared the table.

Folded towel.

Copper.

Today the strip was straight again.

No charcoal on my hand.

No charcoal on my forearm.

I stared at the absence.

Hessa said, “You are already trying to infer the test from what I did not put on the table.”

“Yes.”

“Stop.”

“I cannot stop thinking.”

“That is not what I asked.”

Fair.

I sat.

Right forearm on towel.

Left hand loose.

Right foot planted.

Residual limb supported.

Crutches against the wall where I could see them.

“Minimal draw,” Hessa said.

“Nothing else?”

“Nothing else.”

I drew.

Familiar now.

Not easy.

Familiar.

Mana gathered through the routes I knew without becoming ordinary.

Trunk.

Arms.

Right side.

Left thigh to knee.

Nothing unusual below it.

Ordinary phantom left foot somewhere under the table, heel angled slightly inward.

Not magical.

Probably not even worth noting unless asked.

“Stable,” Hessa said.

I held.

Copper responded.

No thumb sensation.

No wrist movement.

No shaping.

“Release.”

I released.

Hessa waited.

I waited.

She checked pulse.

Hand.

Thumb.

Grip.

“Fine?”

“Yes.”

She wrote.

I watched.

“You said draw.”

“Yes.”

“You did not say draw only.”

“No.”

“So now?”

“Now I know you can come in the day after Pessa makes your hands hot, perform a minimal draw, and not immediately produce a hand symptom.”

“That seems unsurprising.”

“Good.”

I hated when boring pleased her.

She looked at me.

“Do you know why I care?”

“Because the previous thumb symptom happened in a hand that also bears crutch load, and yesterday was the first substantial movement session since before the shaping tests.”

“Good.”

“So this is not a shaping test.”

“No.”

“Are we done?”

“Maybe.”

I leaned back.

Hessa kept writing.

I did not ask again.

This lasted approximately nine seconds.

“What would make it not done?”

She sighed.

“Your inability to tolerate silence.”

“That is not medical.”

“No.”

She set the pen down.

“Tell me what you think happened when I said stop last time.”

I had thought about this.

Not practiced.

Thought.

Allowed.

“I reduced the forward change.”

“Know or think?”

“Think.”

“Why?”

“Subjectively it stopped moving toward the line.”

“And then?”

“It widened.”

“Did you choose widening?”

“No.”

“Did you choose not to widen?”

I paused.

“That sounds like the same question.”

“It isn't.”

I replayed the sensation.

The stop cue.

The effort to stop.

The broadening.

“I was trying to stop changing it.”

“Everything about it?”

“Yes.”

“Were you trying to hold the shape you had?”

“Yes.”

“Were you trying to return it to the original draw?”

“No.”

“Were you trying to release?”

“No.”

She nodded.

“What did your body do?”

“No wrist rotation.”

“I know what I saw.”

Right.

My report.

“Forearm felt tense.”

“Muscular?”

“Maybe. Not strongly.”

“Hand?”

“Quiet.”

“Breathing?”

I frowned.

“I don't know.”

“Good.”

She wrote that.

“You are delighted by ignorance.”

“I am delighted when you stop replacing it.”

She picked up a charcoal stick.

My attention sharpened.

Hessa saw that too.

“Do not celebrate.”

“I am not.”

“You are nineteen. Your entire face is celebrating.”

She drew one short line on the inside of my right forearm.

Farther from the wrist than last time.

Then another line closer to the elbow.

Two again.

I looked at her.

“One attempt?”

“If we do it.”

“If?”

“I can still decide not to.”

I shut up.

Miracle.

Hessa placed the copper.

“Different question,” she said.

“Good.”

“That was not praise.”

“What question?”

“Last time I told you to stop altering while holding the draw.”

“Yes.”

“You interpreted that as freeze the alteration.”

“Yes.”

“Today, if we proceed, I may ask something narrower.”

I waited.

She pointed to the nearer charcoal line.

“You will draw. I will tell you whether to alter toward the near or far mark. You will begin one small alteration. Then I will say enough.”

“Instead of stop.”

“Yes.”

“What does enough mean?”

“End the deliberate alteration and allow the mana to settle back toward the underlying draw without releasing the draw.”

I stared.

“That is harder.”

“Maybe.”

“It is also different.”

“Yes.”

“Last time I was trying to hold the altered state.”

“Yes.”

“Now you want me to stop maintaining it.”

“I want to know whether you can stop feeding the deliberate change without dropping everything.”

That was cleaner.

Also terrifying.

“External effect?”

“No.”

“Barrier?”

“No.”

“Left?”

“No.”

“Second attempt if I fail?”

“No.”

Of course.

“What counts as failure?”

“I will tell you afterward.”

“Hessa.”

“No.”

“You know.”

“I know some things I would stop for. I do not know every way you can fail before you do it.”

Fair.

I hated fair.

She examined my right hand one more time.

“Any tingling now?”

“No.”

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Numbness?”

“No.”

“Cold?”

“No.”

“Ready?”

“Yes.”

“Minimal draw.”

I drew again.

The second draw of the day felt slightly less crisp.

Not weaker.

More aware.

That could have been attention.

I said nothing.

“Stable?”

“Yes.”

Hessa watched.

Copper answered.

“Far mark.”

I began.

Small.

Not thumb.

Not hand.

Forearm.

The alteration gathered as a narrow pressure of awareness, not physical pressure, moving along the inside of the right forearm.

Not a line exactly.

More like a region with a direction.

It approached the far mark.

“Enough.”

I stopped feeding it.

That was the instruction.

Not freeze.

Not reverse.

Not release.

Stop feeding.

For half a second nothing happened.

Then the altered region softened.

Not disappeared.

Softened.

The edge nearest my wrist faded first.

The center remained.

Then the whole thing seemed to collapse inward.

My wrist twitched.

Tiny.

I felt it.

Hessa saw it.

“Release.”

I released immediately.

Mana gone.

The forearm sensation vanished with it.

My heart was beating harder than the test deserved.

Hessa touched my wrist.

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Tingling?”

“No.”

“Numbness?”

“No.”

“Weak?”

“No.”

“Cold?”

“No.”

“Barrier?”

I thought.

“No.”

“Anything familiar?”

“The wrist twitch.”

“Familiar how?”

“Movement. Not the setup.”

“Do not improve the answer.”

“I am not.”

She checked grip.

Fingertips.

Thumb.

Normal.

Then she sat.

I waited.

She wrote.

I waited longer.

This was abuse.

Finally:

“You stopped feeding the alteration.”

I almost smiled.

She raised a finger.

“And then the pattern changed.”

Smile gone.

“How?”

“I saw the response diminish rather than broaden. Copper response reduced with it. Then your wrist moved.”

“Did I succeed?”

“You performed the instruction more closely than last time.”

“That is not yes.”

“No.”

“Is it no?”

“No.”

I stared.

“Hessa.”

“You want a category before I have one.”

“I stopped shaping.”

“You stopped deliberately feeding the change. I am reasonably confident of that.”

That landed.

“Reasonably?”

“Yes.”

“And it settled?”

“It changed toward less altered. That is what I observed.”

“Without dropping the draw.”

“Yes.”

There.

Something in my chest lifted.

Hessa continued before I could turn it into a parade.

“You also had an involuntary or at least unrequested wrist movement at the end.”

“Tiny.”

“Yes.”

“No Barrier pattern.”

“Not one I recognized.”

“So.”

“So I have one attempt where the alteration broadened after a stop instruction and one attempt where a differently framed instruction produced diminishing alteration followed by a wrist twitch.”

“That is better.”

“It is different.”

“It can be both.”

Hessa looked annoyed that I had said something reasonable.

I enjoyed this.

“Does this change external effect?”

“No.”

“Casting?”

“No.”

“Next test?”

“I don't know.”

My joy survived anyway.

That annoyed her more.

“You are not cleared to practice this.”

“I know.”

“You are not cleared to draw.”

“I know.”

“You are not cleared to shape.”

“I know.”

“You are not cleared to reproduce the wrist movement.”

“I know.”

“Tomorrow no magic.”

I groaned.

“One day?”

“One day.”

“Then?”

“Come back the day after tomorrow.”

“Are we doing this again?”

“I don't know.”

Of course.

I stood.

No tingling.

Hand normal.

Palms normal.

Residual limb quiet.

I took the crutches.

Hessa said, “Greg.”

I stopped.

“You trained yesterday.”

“Yes.”

“You tested today.”

“Yes.”

“Tomorrow does not need to contain Pessa because it cannot contain me.”

“I know.”

She watched me.

I thought about lying.

Did not.

“I might ask Lyssa to do something.”

Hessa blinked.

“That sounds medically preferable.”

“Rude.”

“Go.”

I went.

Outside, Carrow was offensively normal.

Carts.

Bread.

Mud.

Someone shouting about lamp oil.

A dog asleep under a bench.

I had shaped mana.

I had maybe stopped shaping mana.

Reasonably.

Under a different instruction.

Once.

In a small supervised test.

Without a spell.

I wanted to tell everyone.

Instead I went to find Lyssa.

She was working.

So I waited.
