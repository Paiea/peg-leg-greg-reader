LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 122

THE REACHER

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Hessa had covered the tray.

That was new.

Not dramatic.

A square of gray cloth over the part where the copper usually sat.

I noticed before I sat down.

She noticed me noticing.

“Hands first.”

“That is not copper.”

“Hands.”

Right thumb.

Normal.

Fingers.

Normal.

Wrist.

Normal.

Forearm.

Normal.

She pressed along the inside of the arm, then checked grip, fingertips, wrist movement.

“Yesterday?”

“Theatre.”

“Magic?”

“No.”

“Symptoms?”

“No.”

“Training?”

“No.”

“Fall?”

“No.”

“Near fall?”

“No.”

“Residual limb?”

“Baseline.”

“Phantom?”

“Ordinary.”

“Anything unusual below the left knee?”

“No.”

“Hand urge?”

“No.”

“Thumb?”

“No.”

She wrote.

I looked at the cloth.

She did not.

This was deliberate cruelty.

“Do you want to know what is under it?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

I waited.

She wrote another line.

“Hessa.”

“Yes.”

“You are enjoying this.”

“No.”

“You are lying.”

“Possibly.”

She closed the notebook.

Then removed the cloth.

Copper.

Of course.

Also a narrow glazed tile.

A small upright wooden frame.

And from the top of the frame hung a single dark thread.

The thread ended above the tile.

Nothing touched it.

I stared.

Hessa watched my face.

“That is external.”

“It is a thread.”

“It is outside my body.”

“Yes.”

I looked at her.

She looked at me.

“Is this clearance?”

“For one question.”

I stopped breathing for a moment.

Not intentionally.

Hessa's expression sharpened.

“Breathe.”

I did.

“One question,” she repeated. “One attempt. Under my instruction. This is not casting clearance. It is not Barrier clearance. It is not independent external work. It is not permission to reproduce the test.”

“Yes.”

“Say the rest.”

“Left shaping remains excluded.”

“Yes.”

“No independent draw.”

“Yes.”

“No independent shaping.”

“Yes.”

“No independent external attempt.”

“Yes.”

“No spell.”

“Correct.”

I looked at the thread again.

It was extremely unimpressive.

Perfect.

“What is the question?”

“Whether you can deliberately extend a small altered response beyond the boundary of the right forearm without releasing the underlying draw.”

I looked back at her.

“Extend.”

“Yes.”

“Not release.”

“Correct.”

“Not push.”

“Correct.”

“Not cast.”

“Correct.”

“Not Barrier.”

“Correct.”

“How far?”

She pointed.

The thread hung less than a finger-width from where my right forearm would rest beside the frame.

“That far.”

“That is not a measurement.”

“It is enough of one.”

“Hessa.”

“I measured before you arrived.”

I looked at her.

She smiled.

Cruel woman.

“How far?”

“Three quarters of an inch from skin to thread when your arm is in position.”

Better.

“Why thread?”

“Light. Visible. Harmless. If it moves, we can see direction. If it does not move, we have a clean absence to discuss.”

“Air.”

“I know.”

“Breathing.”

“I know.”

“Table vibration.”

“I know.”

“Crutch movement.”

“You will be seated.”

“Door.”

“Closed.”

“Window.”

“Closed.”

“Your sleeve.”

“Rolled above the elbow.”

“My hand.”

“Still.”

“Your hand.”

“Not near it.”

“Copper response.”

“Separate observation.”

I stared at the apparatus.

“You thought about this.”

“Yes.”

That should not have been surprising.

It was.

I had spent weeks wanting an external test.

Hessa had apparently spent at least some portion of that time deciding what one would need to mean.

The thread did not look like magic.

It looked like sewing.

Good.

Probably.

“What counts?”

Hessa pointed at the tile beneath it.

“There is a charcoal line directly below the thread's resting position.”

I leaned closer.

There was.

“If the thread moves visibly away from rest while you are holding the requested state, I record direction and duration.”

“How much movement?”

“Visible.”

“That is subjective.”

“Yes.”

“Can we mark distance?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because your first external question is whether there is any reproducible-looking change worth measuring more precisely later.”

“Reproducible-looking is dangerous language.”

“It is.”

“Because one attempt cannot establish reproducibility.”

“Yes.”

I liked her less when she agreed quickly.

“And if it moves once?”

“We record that it moved once.”

“External effect?”

“We record an observed external movement during the attempt.”

“That is cowardly.”

“That is accurate.”

“And if it does not move?”

“We record no observed external movement.”

“Failure.”

“No.”

“It is literally failure to move the thread.”

“It is failure to move the thread if moving the thread is the goal.”

“It is.”

“The goal is to learn whether your current control can produce an observable external change under this instruction.”

“That is moving the thread with extra words.”

“No.”

I knew this argument.

I was going to lose it.

“What stops the test?”

“Any thumb tingling. Any urge to curl or spread fingers. Wrist movement. Weakness. Cold. Numbness. Pain. Familiar broad pattern. Any left-side change that is not ordinary phantom sensation. Any sense that you are trying to complete Barrier. Any urge to release forcefully. Any change you cannot describe well enough for me to decide whether to continue.”

“That last one is hostile.”

“Yes.”

“One attempt.”

“One.”

“If nothing happens, no second attempt?”

“No.”

“If I know what I did wrong?”

“Especially no.”

I sat.

She positioned my right forearm on a folded cloth beside the frame.

Not touching.

The thread hung near the inside of the forearm.

Three quarters of an inch suddenly looked enormous.

I could bridge it with a fingertip.

Not allowed.

My hand rested open on the table beyond my forearm.

Hessa adjusted the copper on the other side.

“Before draw.”

The thread was still.

Mostly.

Nothing in a room was perfectly still.

Tiny tremor.

Maybe air.

Maybe vision.

The charcoal line gave me a reference.

“Breathe normally,” Hessa said.

“That will move it.”

“Then we observe baseline first.”

We did.

I breathed.

The thread moved almost imperceptibly once.

Toward me.

Then back.

“Baseline movement,” Hessa said.

“Yes.”

“Again.”

I breathed.

Nothing obvious.

Again.

Tiny movement.

Different direction.

Good.

Annoying.

“Do not hold your breath during the attempt.”

“I know.”

“I am telling you anyway.”

She waited.

Then:

“Minimal draw.”

I drew.

Small.

Familiar enough to recognize.

Not easy.

Not automatic.

Copper responded.

Right forearm.

Ordinary phantom left foot.

No unusual extension.

No thumb.

No fingers.

“Hold.”

I held.

The thread remained thread.

“Small alteration. Right forearm. Toward the charcoal mark on your skin.”

There was a small mark near the wrist-side area again.

I shaped.

Localized.

Not perfect.

Small.

Copper changed.

“Report.”

“Alteration present. Small. Slightly broader toward elbow than the mark, but not shifting.”

“Motor?”

“No.”

“Thumb?”

“No.”

“Left?”

“Ordinary.”

“Good. Hold.”

I held.

The thread did nothing.

This was not yet the external instruction.

My heart had accelerated anyway.

That was inconvenient.

“Hessa.”

“Yes.”

“Heart rate.”

“I can see.”

“Does that matter?”

“It may matter to you. Not enough to stop.”

Fine.

She watched my hand.

Then the copper.

Then the thread.

“Now listen carefully.”

I did.

“Do not release.”

“Yes.”

“Do not increase the internal alteration.”

“Yes.”

“Do not try to move your hand, wrist, or fingers.”

“Yes.”

“Keep the underlying draw active.”

“Yes.”

“From the existing altered region only, attempt the smallest extension you can toward the thread.”

I stared at the thread.

“Extension.”

“Yes.”

“Across the gap.”

“Yes.”

“Stop if it feels like force.”

“Yes.”

“Stop if it becomes broad.”

“Yes.”

“Stop if I say stop.”

“Yes.”

She waited.

“Reach.”

I tried.

Nothing happened.

Not to the thread.

Internally, the alteration changed.

I felt it narrow.

No.

Not narrow.

Lengthen?

Dangerous word.

The edge nearest the thread became more distinct.

I did not feel anything outside the skin.

I did not know what outside should feel like.

The thread remained still.

“Report,” Hessa said.

“Near edge sharper. Maybe narrower behind it.”

“External sensation?”

“No.”

“Motor?”

“No.”

“Continue only the same instruction. Do not increase force.”

I tried again.

Small.

The internal altered region seemed to lean toward the gap.

That was metaphor.

No.

It changed asymmetrically toward the thread.

Better.

Copper response changed.

The thread moved.

Tiny.

Toward my arm.

I froze.

Wrong.

Not release.

Not stop draw.

Hessa said, “Hold.”

I held.

The thread returned toward the charcoal line.

My heart was loud.

“Did you breathe differently?”

“I don't know.”

“Good answer. Hold.”

The alteration remained.

No motor.

No thumb.

No cold.

“Again. Same extension. No more.”

I tried.

The thread did not move.

Nothing.

I wanted to try harder.

Did not.

“Report.”

“Internal asymmetry toward thread. No external sensation. No movement I can see.”

“I saw none.”

“First movement?”

“I saw it.”

“Cause?”

“Unknown.”

“Yes.”

I hated that.

“Continue.”

“One attempt.”

“This is the same attempt.”

Fair.

I maintained draw.

Maintained alteration.

Tried the same smallest extension.

The thread moved again.

Not much.

This time sideways.

Not toward me.

To my left.

Then stopped.

Hessa immediately said:

“RETURN.”

External question over.

I obeyed.

Not release.

Reduce alteration.

The thread settled.

I did not watch it.

Right forearm.

RETURN.

The altered region diminished.

Wrist-side edge softened.

Center dimmed.

No motor urge.

No elbowward shift I could detect.

“Report.”

“Reducing. No motor. No thumb.”

“Continue.”

I did.

The alteration became uncertain.

“I cannot tell if residual remains.”

“Underlying draw?”

“Still present. Higher confidence.”

“Hold.”

I held.

Hessa watched copper.

Then my hand.

Then thread.

“Release.”

I released.

She took my right hand immediately.

Open.

Close.

Thumb.

Fingers.

Wrist.

Grip.

No symptom.

“Cold?”

“No.”

“Numb?”

“No.”

“Pain?”

“No.”

“Urge?”

“No.”

“Left?”

“Ordinary phantom.”

“Anything external now?”

“No.”

She waited.

I waited.

The thread hung above its charcoal line.

I wanted to accuse it of something.

Hessa wrote.

I could not read upside down from this angle.

Probably intentional.

“Well?”

“Sit.”

“I am sitting.”

“Then continue.”

Hostile.

She wrote longer than usual.

“What did you see?”

“I will tell you.”

“When?”

“When I am done writing what I saw before hearing what you want me to have seen.”

That was fair.

Horrible.

I looked at the thread.

First movement toward my arm.

Could be breath.

Second sideways.

Could be air.

Could be table.

Could be nothing.

Could be magic.

The last possibility was too large.

I tried to make it smaller.

Observed movement.

Unknown cause.

Good.

Hessa put the pencil down.

“Your report first.”

I gave it exactly.

Minimal draw.

Small alteration.

Near edge became more distinct after REACH.

Internal response became asymmetric toward thread.

No external sensation.

First visible thread movement toward arm.

I did not know whether breathing changed.

Second extension produced no observed movement.

Third produced a small sideways movement.

Then RETURN.

Alteration reduced.

No unwanted motor response.

No thumb.

No unusual left phantom.

Release normal.

Hessa nodded.

“My observation is close.”

“Close how?”

“Copper changed when you attempted extension.”

“That is expected.”

“Not necessarily.”

“Fine.”

“The internal altered response appeared to change directionally.”

“You can see that?”

“I can see enough through copper response to say it changed. Direction is partly your report.”

Important.

“The thread moved twice during the extension portion.”

“Yes.”

“Once toward you. Once sideways.”

“Yes.”

“It also showed small baseline movement before draw.”

“Yes.”

“So?”

“So I cannot attribute either movement to you.”

Disappointment arrived cleanly.

Not devastating.

Just there.

“Then no external effect.”

“No established external effect.”

“That is different wording.”

“Yes.”

“Did the test fail?”

“No.”

“Hessa.”

“No.”

“What did it establish?”

“That I can ask you to attempt a bounded extension without you immediately releasing, broadening into the familiar pattern, recruiting your hand, or losing the underlying draw.”

I stopped.

She continued.

“And when I ended the external question, you returned the alteration without the previous unwanted responses.”

“That is internal control.”

“Yes.”

“So we learned internal control during an external test.”

“Yes.”

“And the external result is unknown.”

“Correct.”

I looked at the thread.

“You planned this.”

“I hoped for interpretable information.”

“You got the wrong information.”

“I got information.”

“Practitioner.”

“Yes.”

I leaned back.

“Am I cleared for external testing now?”

“You were cleared for this external test.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“I know.”

“Can we repeat it?”

“Not today.”

“Tomorrow?”

“No.”

“Day after?”

“Maybe.”

“How many living days?”

“Two.”

I stared.

“Again?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because this was your first supervised attempt to deliberately extend an altered response beyond your body.”

“And nothing happened.”

“Unknown.”

“That is worse.”

“No. It is less satisfying.”

Same thing.

Not same thing.

Fine.

“Two full no-magic days?”

“Yes.”

“Then assessment?”

“Yes.”

“Could the next assessment repeat the thread?”

“Yes.”

“Could it use something else?”

“Yes.”

“Could it return to internal control?”

“Yes.”

“Could you decide external testing was premature?”

“Yes.”

“You are impossible.”

“Yes.”

I looked at the apparatus.

“Can I ask whether the sideways movement is more interesting than the movement toward me?”

“You can ask.”

“Is it?”

“Not enough to matter.”

“Because breath would more likely move it away?”

“Do not build airflow theory from your chair.”

“I know airflow.”

“You do not know this room's airflow well enough.”

“Window closed.”

“Door closed.”

“Still convection.”

“Yes.”

“Body heat.”

“Yes.”

“Your movement.”

“Yes.”

“Table vibration.”

“Yes.”

“Thread twist.”

“Yes.”

“Static?”

She paused.

“Possibly.”

I hated the universe.

“So the thread can move for six reasons.”

“More.”

“And magic is one candidate.”

“Possibly.”

“Possibly possibly.”

“That is where we are.”

I wanted a clean event.

A spark.

A twitch that happened exactly when I reached and nowhere else.

A thread leaping sideways three times in identical direction.

Something I could own.

Instead I had two tiny movements and a woman professionally refusing to let me have them.

Good.

No.

Accurate.

Probably good later.

Still irritating now.

Hessa uncovered the charcoal reference under the thread and checked where it rested.

“Back at baseline?”

“Approximately.”

“Approximately?”

“Thread has twist.”

I laughed.

She looked at me.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Gregory.”

“The first external magical test of my life may have been defeated by sewing.”

“It was not defeated.”

“Thread twist.”

“Is real.”

“I know.”

“That is why we test small things.”

I looked at her.

“Because small things reveal all the ways measurement is annoying.”

“Among other reasons.”

She removed the frame.

That felt abrupt.

The external world disappeared from the tray.

Copper remained.

I watched her put the thread apparatus on a shelf.

“Can I touch it?”

“No.”

“I meant later.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because you will inspect the thread.”

“Yes.”

“And the frame.”

“Yes.”

“And the tile.”

“Yes.”

“And then attempt to determine airflow.”

“Yes.”

“And then arrive next time with a theory.”

I considered lying.

Did not.

“Yes.”

“No.”

Fair.

She made me sit another ten minutes.

Ordinary hand movement only.

No ritual.

She caught me checking the thumb once.

“Did it feel wrong?”

“No.”

“Then stop.”

I drank water.

Reached for the cup.

Normal.

Grip.

Normal.

Crutches.

Normal.

Three ordinary steps.

No tingling.

No finger urge.

No wrist movement.

No strange left phantom.

Hessa watched.

“Two days.”

“Yes.”

“No magic.”

“Yes.”

“Living.”

“Poorly specified.”

“Consistently effective.”

“That is not established.”

She pointed at the door.

I left.

Outside, Carrow had not changed.

This was offensive.

I had just attempted to affect something outside my body with mana.

Maybe.

Technically I had attempted.

The something had moved.

Maybe because of me.

Maybe because rooms contained air.

A cart passed.

Someone shouted about fish.

Two children were arguing over a hoop.

I stood near Hessa's doorway and tried to decide what I felt.

Pleased.

Annoyed.

Hungry.

That seemed manageable.

Theatre was west.

Guild another direction.

Lyssa would be working or not.

Pessa had her own day.

Tomorrow had no magic.

The day after also had no magic.

Then Hessa.

I could spend forty-eight hours thinking about thread.

Absolutely not.

I started walking.

West.

Then stopped.

Not because theatre was wrong.

Because I was hungry and the food stall I wanted was east.

I turned east.

This was not symbolic.

It was lunch.

Probably.
