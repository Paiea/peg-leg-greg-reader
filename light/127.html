LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 127

THE REPEAT

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Hessa had the same two threads. I checked. Same frame. Same glazed tile. Same copper. Same dark thread. Same spacing. Probably. I looked closer.

Hessa said:

“Sit down.”

“I am checking whether you changed it.”

“I changed one thing.”

I stopped.

“What?”

“Sit.”

I sat. She checked me first. Hands. Right calf. Residual limb. Phantom. Thumb. Fingers. Wrist. Grip. No surprises. The phantom foot was mildly cold despite not existing. Ordinary. Hessa pressed along my right forearm.

“Yesterday?”

“No magic.”

“Thread?”

“No.”

“String?”

“No.”

“Cord?”

“No.”

I looked at her.

“You are expanding the prohibition.”

“You went to Guild.”

“Yes.”

“Courier?”

“Yes.”

“Did you tie anything to anything?”

“No.”

“Good.”

“That question is absurd.”

“You are creative when annoyed.”

“I repaired a horse with wood.”

“Not yesterday.”

“No.”

“Good.”

She went to the apparatus.

“What changed?”

She pointed at the two threads. Small paper sleeves covered the upper inch of each. One white. One gray.

“That.”

“Why?”

“So I stop thinking of them as near and far while I record.”

I stared.

“You know which is which.”

“Yes.”

“That does not work.”

“It helps.”

“How?”

“It makes me write white and gray instead of the conclusion I expect.”

“That is not blinding.”

“No.”

“Barely even pretending.”

“Yes.”

“Then why?”

“Because perfect is not available.”

She moved the frame. Not far. Half an inch. Then put it back against two charcoal marks on the table. I noticed those too.

“You marked the feet.”

“Yes.”

“To preserve position.”

“Yes.”

“That is a second change.”

“The marks were there last time.”

I looked. They were faint. I had missed them. Annoying. Hessa adjusted the tile.

“Question today is replication.”

I liked the word. She saw that.

“Do not.”

“I did nothing.”

“You liked it.”

“Yes.”

“Stop.”

“No.”

She ignored me.

“Same bounded sequence. Same target region. Same approximate geometry. Same near comparison and farther comparison. One attempt.”

“Same charcoal mark?”

“Re-drawn from my notes.”

“So not exactly.”

“No.”

“Same thread?”

“Yes.”

“Same cup?”

“What cup?”

“Nothing.”

She looked at me. I looked back. Horse stayed out of this room. Good.

“What counts as replication?” I asked.

“Nothing by itself.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the answer you need.”

“Hessa.”

“If the closer thread again shows a movement toward your arm during REACH while the farther thread does not at the same time, that would be another observation of the same general pattern.”

“Yes.”

“If it does not, that is also information.”

“Yes.”

“If both move together, information.”

“Yes.”

“If the farther moves and the closer does not, information.”

“Yes.”

“If neither moves, information.”

“Yes.”

“So everything is information.”

“Correct.”

“That is convenient.”

“It is only convenient if you do not care what the information says.”

I did. Obviously. She opened the door. Waited. Both threads moved. White more. Gray less. She wrote. Closed door. Waited. Neither. She walked past the table. Gray trembled. White did not. She wrote. Opened the window a finger width.

Both moved left. Not together. White first. Gray second. She wrote. Closed it. We waited. The room settled. Mostly. Hessa moved my chair into position. Right forearm beside the closer thread. I could tell which sleeve was closer.

White. So could Hessa. This was not blinding. Still. She had changed how she wrote. Maybe that mattered. I breathed normally. White moved slightly away from me. Gray rotated. Hessa wrote. White returned near its line.

Gray stopped left of its line.

“Baseline is worse today,” I said.

“Different.”

“More movement.”

“So far.”

“Humidity?”

“Maybe.”

“Wet yesterday.”

“Outside.”

“Could affect room.”

“Yes.”

“Thread moisture.”

“Yes.”

“Air.”

“Yes.”

“Heat.”

“Yes.”

“Static less likely if damp.”

“Less likely is not absent.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

I wanted to ask how long we were going to watch thread before doing anything. I did not. Five minutes. Maybe six. Long enough that my right shoulder became aware of the chair. Not pain.

Position. Hessa moved the chair back.

“Reset.”

“What?”

“You shifted.”

“I breathed.”

“You also leaned.”

“I did not.”

“You did.”

“How much?”

“Enough that I noticed.”

“That is not measurement.”

“No.”

She made me stand. I did. She reset the chair against a floor mark I had not noticed. Of course there was a floor mark. I sat again.

“Do not hold yourself rigid.”

“I wasn't.”

“You are now.”

“Because you said I moved.”

“Normal posture.”

“That is impossible after instruction.”

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

I tried to forget my spine. Failed. Eventually succeeded enough. Hessa waited another two minutes.

“Draw.”

Minimal. Right forearm. Copper answered. Small. Stable. No thumb. No finger. No wrist. No left response.

“Shape.”

Same region. Or as close to same as memory and charcoal allowed. Localized alteration. Near edge slightly more distinct. Upper edge faint. Center changed. I reported each.

“Hold.”

Held. White thread moved. Small. Away from my arm. I saw it. Hessa had not told me to look away yet. My chest sank. Interesting. Disappointing. Useful. Probably. Gray moved a moment later. Toward the window.

Hessa wrote.

“Do not interpret.”

“I did not.”

“Your face did.”

“Everyone says that.”

“REACH.”

I looked at her hand. Two fingers above copper. Same as last time. Smallest extension. Toward the closer thread. No force. No push. Maintain draw. The near edge sharpened. Asymmetry toward target. Familiar now.

Not comfortable. Familiar.

“Near edge sharper.”

“Continue same instruction.”

I did. Nothing else internally. No broadening. No motor. No urge. No thumb. Hessa watched past me. I watched her fingers. One breath. Two. Three.

“Hold.”

Held.

“RETURN.”

I returned. Altered region softened. Center diminished. Upper edge faded. Underlying draw stayed.

“Hold.”

Held.

“Release.”

Released. Hessa was at my wrist immediately. Open. Close. Thumb. Wrist. Grip. Again. Fine. She checked the forearm. Then the left. Then made me wait. I looked at the threads. White rested almost on its charcoal line.

Gray sat a little right.

“What happened?”

Hessa wrote first. That was cruel.

“Hessa.”

“White moved during shape.”

“I saw.”

“Away from your arm.”

“Yes.”

“Gray moved shortly after.”

“Yes.”

“During REACH?”

I waited. She finished the line.

“Neither moved enough for me to mark.”

I stared.

“Neither.”

“Correct.”

“Nothing.”

“Nothing I recorded.”

“During the entire REACH?”

“Yes.”

“White didn't move toward me.”

“No.”

“Gray?”

“No.”

“So it did not replicate.”

“The specific differential movement from last time did not replicate today.”

There was the sentence. I disliked it. It was clean.

“Could still have been mana last time.”

“Yes.”

“Could be mana today without movement.”

“That question is not useful.”

“It is possible.”

“Many things are possible.”

“Could the shape have changed the thread during the movement away?”

“You had not begun REACH.”

“I was shaping.”

“Yes.”

“That is still mana.”

“Yes.”

I looked at her. She raised one finger.

“No.”

“You cannot just exclude it.”

“I am not excluding it.”

“You are.”

“I am refusing to move the question because today's answer disappointed you.”

I stopped. That landed. I hated when she did that.

“The test was REACH.”

“Yes.”

“The movement during shape was not the target observation.”

“Correct.”

“But it happened.”

“Yes.”

“And could matter.”

“Yes.”

“Then record it.”

“I did.”

Fine.

“What does it mean?”

“I don't know.”

“Useful.”

“Yes.”

“You use that phrase badly.”

“I use it accurately.”

She sat. I remained in the test chair.

“Last time,” she said, “closer thread moved toward your arm during REACH while farther did not at that moment.”

“Yes.”

“Today, it did not.”

“Yes.”

“That lowers my confidence that we are seeing a repeatable external response under this setup.”

There. Actual movement. Down. Not up. I did not like it. I also trusted it more because I did not like it. Probably dangerous thought.

“What about internal control?”

“Still good.”

“Same?”

“Good.”

“That is not a number.”

“No.”

“Better confidence?”

She looked at her notes.

“Fourteenth successful minimal supervised draw.”

I waited.

“Tenth supervised shaping attempt.”

“Yes.”

“Third supervised external attempt.”

“Yes.”

“You established the localized alteration at the intended shifted region.”

“Yes.”

“You maintained underlying draw.”

“Yes.”

“You attempted one bounded REACH without broadening, force sensation, motor recruitment, thumb symptom, wrist movement, elbowward shift, or unusual left response.”

“Yes.”

“You RETURNed promptly.”

“Yes.”

“So yes. My confidence in that sequence increases again.”

I nodded. External down. Internal up. Annoying. Balanced. Probably.

“What next?”

“No magic tomorrow.”

I closed my eyes.

“One day.”

“Yes.”

“Then assessment?”

“Yes.”

“External?”

“Assessment.”

“You enjoy that.”

“A little.”

I opened my eyes.

“Are you going to use the same threads again?”

“I don't know.”

“You must have a plan.”

“I have several.”

“Which?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because I want to decide after I review all three attempts, not while you are sitting here trying to make one of them win.”

“I am not trying to make it win.”

“You are arguing that movement during shape should count.”

“It should be recorded.”

“It was.”

“Then yes.”

She almost smiled.

“Good.”

I looked at the white sleeve.

“Why keep the paper labels if you know which is near?”

“Because I noticed something in my own notes.”

“What?”

“I wrote ‘near moved’ more quickly than I wrote the actual direction.”

I looked at her.

“That is bad.”

“Yes.”

“You are admitting error.”

“Yes.”

“Are you ill?”

“No.”

“Should I check your grip?”

“Greg.”

“Fine.”

She tapped the page.

“Near is already interpretation. White is an object.”

“That seems excessive.”

“Probably.”

“Then why do it?”

“Cheap.”

I had no answer. She won. Again. I pointed at the gray cloth.

“Are you changing the apparatus next time?”

“I said I don't know.”

“The comparison is imperfect.”

“Yes.”

“Local heat differs.”

“Yes.”

“Distance differs.”

“Yes.”

“Air around my arm differs.”

“Yes.”

“Thread behavior differs.”

“Yes.”

“So improve it.”

“How?”

“Put a second target at the same distance from another part of me.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because then you are changing what you are doing internally.”

“Not necessarily.”

“Yes.”

“I could shape two regions.”

“No.”

“Not simultaneously.”

“No.”

“Separate trials.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because I am not doubling your shaping to improve my thread experiment.”

That was irritatingly reasonable.

“Then use a barrier between the threads.”

“That changes airflow.”

“Shield both.”

“Changes the room.”

“Enclose the frame.”

“Heat.”

“Larger enclosure.”

“Greg.”

“Yes.”

“You are trying to design the perfect apparatus.”

“Yes.”

“There isn't one.”

“There can be a better one.”

“Yes.”

“Then why not?”

“Because better for one confound can be worse for another, and because I also care whether the same simple setup ever gives the same result twice.”

I looked at the covered frame.

“That is why you repeated it unchanged.”

“Mostly unchanged.”

“Paper sleeves.”

“Those are for me.”

“Still changed.”

“Yes.”

“You hate that.”

“Yes.”

Good. At least the universe remained stable.

“What if the next test is different?”

“Then I will tell you the question before you do anything.”

“And if it is the same?”

“Same.”

“One attempt?”

“Probably.”

“No magic after?”

“Depends.”

“Hessa.”

“Assessment.”

I hated that word almost as much as unestablished. She gathered the copper and set it beside her notes. I noticed three pages. One for each external attempt. The first page had more writing than the other two.

Of course it did. Messier question. Messier notes.

“What are you looking for when you review?”

“Patterns.”

“That is vague.”

“Symptoms first.”

“None.”

“So far.”

“Then control sequence.”

“Yes.”

“Then apparatus behavior before, during, after.”

“Yes.”

“Then whether anything I thought mattered was actually just memorable.”

I stopped.

“What?”

She tapped the first page.

“Two movements are memorable.”

“Yes.”

“Memorable is not the same as diagnostic.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

The second page.

“One differential movement is memorable.”

“Yes.”

“The third page refusing to repeat it is also memorable.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

She stacked them.

“That is why I am reviewing all three.”

Not to decide which story she liked. Fine. I understood. Still wanted the thread to move. Hessa covered the frame. Gray cloth. Threads gone.

“No magic tomorrow.”

“Yes.”

“No thread.”

“Yes.”

“No hanging cord.”

“Yes.”

“No clever substitutes.”

“I am not going to move dust.”

She stared.

“I was making a joke.”

“Do not move dust.”

“Hessa.”

“Go.”

I went. Outside, I stood for a moment. The result should have disappointed me more. It did disappoint me. I wanted the thread to move. Again. Same direction. Same timing. I wanted a clean line through three observations and a conclusion at the end.

Instead:

First test ambiguous. Second test suggestive. Third test did not repeat the suggestive part. That was not failure. It was not success. It was science being an asshole. I walked. Not Guild. Not theatre.

Not Lyssa. At the first corner I nearly turned toward Hessa again. Not to argue. To ask one more question. Different problem. Same behavior. I kept going. A baker had set two trays near the open window.

One sweet. One not. I could smell the sweet one from across the street. That was enough information to make me cross. No. Stop. I bought a pear instead because I had already committed to not turning lunch into a system.

The pear seller gave me the softer of two without asking. I noticed.

“Why that one?”

“You'll eat it now.”

“How do you know?”

“You're holding it.”

“That happened after you chose it.”

She looked at me.

“Do you want the hard one?”

“No.”

“Then go away.”

I went away. Carrow remained hostile to inquiry. I bought a pear. Sat on a low wall. A dog stared at me. I stared back. The dog looked away first. Established external effect. No.

I ate the pear. A cart went past. Not Sevren. A woman carrying flowers stopped to retie one bundle. Two boys argued about whether a roof tile had fallen today or yesterday. Nobody knew.

The dog came back. Sat near my right crutch. Brown. One white paw. Its left ear folded at the tip. It watched my pear with professional attention. I shifted the core from my right hand to my left.

Its eyes followed. That was an external effect. No. That was a dog. I did not test anything on it. Good. I put the pear core on the wall beside me instead. The dog looked at it.

Then at me. Then at it. This was already a better experiment than thread because the dog clearly wanted something. Also worse because dogs had opinions. It stood. Took one step toward the wall.

Stopped. I did nothing. The dog sat again.

“Your controls are terrible,” I told it.

Its tail moved once. Could be wind. I laughed before I could stop myself. A man passing with a sack looked at me.

“Dog won't eat pear.”

“I can see that.”

“He'll eat sausage.”

“I don't have sausage.”

“Then you've designed badly.”

He kept walking. I stared after him. Everyone in Carrow had become Hessa. Unacceptable. My forearm felt normal. Hands normal. Residual limb quiet. Phantom toes appeared briefly when I shifted. Ordinary. Tomorrow no magic.

Fine. I finished the pear. The dog watched the core. I held it out. It sniffed. Did not take it. Non-replication.

“Fuck you too.”

The dog left. I laughed. Then went home.
