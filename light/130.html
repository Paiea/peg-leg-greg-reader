TEXT READER · CHAPTER 130

THE EXTRA

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The bread seller called me Messenger again. I ignored him. Mostly. Then I went to the theatre. That proved nothing. The door was open. Inside, nobody needed a messenger. This was disappointing. I had not admitted I wanted to do it again.

Apparently my body had. I stood just inside. Teren was arguing with a page. Not a person. The page. He had three versions open across his knees.

The recurring woman was onstage with the servant actor and another woman I had seen twice but never spoken to. The soot-shirt worker was halfway up a short ladder adjusting something above a painted doorway.

Nobody looked at me. Fine. I went to my usual chair. Basket of fake fruit. Again. I moved it. Teren looked up.

“Gregory.”

“Teren.”

“Chair is wrong.”

“It is the same chair.”

“Not for today.”

“Why?”

“Sightline.”

He pointed two chairs left. I moved. No argument. That felt suspicious. The servant actor noticed.

“Messenger.”

“No.”

“You came back.”

“Yes.”

“For messenger.”

“No.”

“Liar.”

The recurring woman said:

“He forgot the letter. Of course he came back.”

“I did not forget it today.”

“You don't have one.”

“Exactly.”

She smiled. Then went back to the scene. I watched. No messenger. Apparently the play contained other events. Unreasonable. The scene involved three people at a supper table. Actual table today. Not chalk. Wood.

Three cups. One bowl. A plate with nothing on it. The servant actor was supposed to enter late. He kept entering too early. I enjoyed this.

Teren said:

“Again.”

Servant actor returned to the wing.

The woman at the table said:

“You're enjoying this too much.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

They reset. The scene ran. The servant actor entered. Late this time. Better. Then he sat in the wrong chair. The other woman stopped.

“That is mine.”

“Not in the scene.”

“It is now.”

“You have two.”

“I have one body.”

The recurring woman said:

“Keep going.”

They did. The servant actor shifted the cup instead of the chair. The other woman took the cup back. He took the bowl. She took the bowl too.

The scene became a silent war over table objects while the recurring woman delivered a serious line about inheritance. Teren stopped them.

“None of that.”

The servant actor said:

“She started it.”

The other woman said:

“He sat wrong.”

The recurring woman said:

“You're both children.”

Teren said:

“Again.”

Before they reset, a woman from backstage appeared holding the missing belt from earlier.

“Whose?”

Three people answered. Different names. She stared at them.

“Excellent.”

She hung it over the blue door.

The soot-shirt worker shouted:

“Not there.”

“Then come get it.”

“No.”

The belt stayed. The servant actor looked at me.

“Professional environment.”

“Yes.”

“Highly disciplined.”

“Obviously.”

“Do you feel inspired?”

“Deeply.”

He went back to his mark. The belt remained on the door for the next three runs. Nobody who had claimed it took it.

At the fourth run, the woman at the supper table reached the doorway, saw the belt, and used it. She put it around her waist mid-scene. Nobody stopped. The servant actor's eyes widened. She ignored him.

After the scene, somebody backstage shouted:

“That's mine.”

The table woman removed it.

“Then why was it on my door?”

No answer. She handed it to soot-shirt worker. Soot-shirt worker threw it backstage without looking. Someone caught it. I had no idea who owned it. This was apparently less important than everyone had claimed.

I laughed. Nobody cared. Good. The soot-shirt worker climbed down. She had a strip of painted cloth in one hand.

Someone behind her said:

“That's mine.”

She kept walking. The voice followed.

“That's the west-wall strip.”

“It is brown.”

“It is west-wall brown.”

She stopped. Turned. A narrow man with paint on both wrists came out from behind the doorway. He pointed at the strip like she had stolen a child.

“That matches the alley set.”

“It matches this.”

“It absolutely does not.”

They held the strip against the doorway. I looked. Same. Probably. The paint-wrist man looked at me.

“You.”

“No.”

“Which?”

I looked again.

Soot-shirt worker said:

“Don't recruit civilians.”

“He is not civilian anymore.”

“I am absolutely civilian.”

The servant actor called from stage:

“Messenger.”

“Fuck you.”

Paint-wrist man held the strip higher.

“Door or alley?”

I compared. There was a tiny warmer red under the doorway brown. The strip had more gray.

“Alley.”

He pointed at soot-shirt worker.

“Thank you.”

She looked at me.

“Traitor.”

“You asked indirectly.”

“I did not ask.”

“Good.”

She took a different strip from his arm. He objected. She ignored him. This appeared to be a supply system. I liked it. Mostly because nobody was winning. The soot-shirt worker finally turned to me.

“Gregory.”

“Yes.”

“Hold.”

I held. Seated. Learning. She compared the cloth to the painted doorway. Different brown. Slightly. Maybe.

“Wrong?”

I asked.

“Yes.”

“They look the same.”

“No.”

I looked again. One warmer. One grayer.

“Close.”

“Close is wrong under front light.”

“Why?”

She stared at me. I stopped. Not a lesson day. She took the cloth.

“Need darker.”

Went away. I watched rehearsal. No messenger. After maybe twenty minutes, Teren handed me a page. I looked at him.

“What?”

“Read the landlord.”

“Why?”

“Because I need to hear the cut.”

“Where?”

“From chair.”

I looked. Six lines. Older man. Angry about rent. Good. I could do angry about money. Probably. The recurring woman stood at the table. She read opposite me. I started. Too quiet.

“Louder,” Teren said.

Of course. I started again. Louder. Too much. The servant actor covered one ear.

“Landlord is furious.”

“Fuck you.”

Teren said:

“Less.”

I tried. The line sounded normal. The recurring woman answered. I missed the next line because I was looking at the page and not listening. Teren tapped his own copy. I found it. Again.

Second pass better. Third pass, Teren cut half my second line while I was saying it.

“Stop after ‘three months.’”

I stopped. The recurring woman continued. My next line now came sooner. I missed it.

“Again.”

We did. This was not messenger. Still fun. Then Teren changed the cut back.

“Why?”

“Testing.”

“That is rude.”

“Yes.”

The servant actor whispered:

“Hessa.”

I looked at him.

“How do you know Hessa?”

“I don't.”

“Then why?”

“You made the face.”

Everyone had the face now. Fine. We ran the little landlord exchange again. I got through it. Teren took the page.

“Good.”

That meant done. I wanted another. Apparently I had standards now. I sat. The recurring woman went back to the table scene. Someone backstage dropped something metal. Loud. Everyone onstage flinched. The recurring woman did not stop.

She continued the line. The other woman did too. The servant actor turned the flinch into looking toward the doorway. For half a second it looked intentional.

Then Teren said:

“Again from before the drop.”

The metal thing clattered again because somebody kicked it while retrieving it.

The servant actor called:

“Commitment.”

Someone backstage shouted:

“Fuck off.”

Good theatre. The scene restarted. I found myself listening for the drop. It did not happen. Disappointing. At break, the servant actor came over with two cups. One had water. One smelled like something worse.

He gave me water.

“Landlord.”

“No.”

“Messenger Landlord.”

“No.”

“Gregory of Rent.”

“No.”

He sat on the floor. I looked at him.

“Why floor?”

“Chair is cursed.”

“It is a chair.”

“Exactly.”

I drank.

He said:

“You keep coming.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

I looked at him. He waited. Not serious enough to be dangerous.

“It's funny.”

He nodded.

“Mostly.”

“That is not what I said.”

“I know.”

He drank the bad cup. Made a face.

“What is that?”

“Tea.”

“No.”

“Exactly.”

We sat. Around us, people moved furniture. The soot-shirt worker returned with darker cloth. The blue window was not blue. Nobody cared yet. Someone argued about a missing belt. Someone else said the belt was under the red bench.

There were two red benches. Apparently this building had learned nothing from blue doors. I smiled. The servant actor saw.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Dangerous.”

He stood. Break over. I expected to keep sitting. Instead the recurring woman pointed at me.

“Gregory.”

“Yes?”

“Stand there.”

She pointed near the painted doorway. I stood.

“What am I?”

“Person.”

“That is broad.”

“You can manage.”

The servant actor said:

“Debatable.”

She ignored him.

“When she crosses, move aside.”

The other woman stood at the table.

“Why?”

“Because she needs the path.”

“That is not acting.”

“No.”

Good. I stood. The scene began. The other woman crossed. I moved aside. Too early. Also wrong direction. I had moved toward the painted doorway because it was closer. She had planned to cross behind me.

Now I was in the path twice. She stopped.

“Why are you following me?”

“I am avoiding you.”

“You are extremely bad at it.”

“I noticed.”

“Again.”

Teren did not even say it. She did. Apparently everyone had authority over again now. We reset. The second attempt I waited too long. She had to angle around my right crutch. Not dangerous.

Ugly. She finished the cross anyway.

Then turned at the table and said:

“Your crutch is louder than you are.”

“That seems false.”

“Not from here.”

Teren looked up.

“She's right.”

“About volume?”

“Yes.”

“This is becoming personal.”

The servant actor said:

“Landlord Messenger Furniture has a sound problem.”

“I will kill you.”

“Not in this scene.”

“Again.”

I went back. She changed path. I moved again. Now I was in her way. She stopped. Looked at me.

“Choose.”

“I was getting out of the way.”

“Once.”

Right. Again. This time I waited until she committed to a path. Moved once. Fine. Then the servant actor entered from behind me and nearly hit my crutch. He avoided it. Kept scene.

Afterward he said:

“Your leg is in my kingdom.”

“Crutch.”

“Same problem.”

“Then use another door.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Mine.”

Of course.

Teren said:

“Gregory, half a step left.”

I moved. Servant actor tried entrance. Clear.

“Again.”

We repeated. That was all. I had become scenery with opinions. Excellent.

Then the recurring woman added:

“When I say ‘bring him in,’ cross to table.”

“Who?”

“You.”

“As what?”

“Person.”

“That is not enough.”

“It is today.”

“What do I do at table?”

“Stand.”

“Why?”

“Because I am talking to you.”

“Do I have lines?”

“No.”

The servant actor said:

“Messenger has been demoted.”

I looked at him.

“Landlord outranks messenger.”

“Person is below both.”

Unclear hierarchy. We ran it.

The woman said:

“Bring him in.”

I crossed. Too fast.

“Again.”

Too slow.

“Again.”

I stopped at wrong side of table.

“Again.”

I looked at Teren. He was not smiling. Not annoyed either. Just pages. Again. I crossed. Stopped where she could see me without turning away from the other actor. She said her line to me.

I had no response. I looked at her. She continued. That was apparently correct. Strange. Next run she stood closer. I stopped in the old place. Now I was too far away. She looked at the distance between us.

Then at me. I stayed. She said the line louder.

Afterward:

“Why didn't you move closer?”

“You moved.”

“Yes.”

“You said stop there.”

“I said stop where I can talk to you.”

“That is not a location.”

“It is today.”

I looked at Teren. He was writing. No help.

“So I choose?”

“Yes.”

“Based on what?”

“Where I am.”

“That changes.”

“Yes.”

“That is terrible.”

She smiled.

“Again.”

I moved closer next time. Too close. She stepped back while continuing the scene. I noticed and adjusted half a step. Teren did not stop us. That was apparently acceptable. Not correct. Acceptable. The distinction was becoming irritatingly useful.

Next pass I nodded. She stopped.

“No.”

“What?”

“Don't.”

“Why?”

“Because then I have to answer the nod.”

“That makes no sense.”

“It makes enough.”

I stared. She smiled.

“Again.”

Fine. No nod. Just stand. Harder than expected. My face wanted to do something. My body wanted to acknowledge. Instead I stood. She spoke. I listened. Then she turned away. Scene moved. Teren did not stop.

That felt like success. Tiny. Embarrassing. Good. Later, the soot-shirt worker needed the path. I moved before she asked. She noticed.

“Learning.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

She carried the darker cloth up the ladder. The painted doorway changed almost imperceptibly. Under the front lamp, the patch disappeared. I could not see where old brown ended and new began. That was satisfying.

Not magic. Paint. Good. The afternoon kept going. No messenger. I stopped caring. Mostly. I read landlord twice more when Teren needed it. I stood as Person during three runs. One time I forgot to move when the other woman crossed.

She shoulder-checked me lightly. Kept going. I apologized after.

She said:

“Move next time.”

“Yes.”

That was all. One time the servant actor came through his door too fast and caught the edge of the painted flat with his sleeve. The flat shifted. Soot-shirt worker grabbed it. The actor kept going.

I almost looked at the flat. Did not. Because the recurring woman was speaking to me. I stayed where I was. Afterward I looked. The flat was fine. Soot-shirt worker had already wedged it.

No one praised me for not turning around. Obviously. It was not impressive. That felt good too. The next run the flat did not shift. The run after that, the cup rolled off the table.

Different failure. The recurring woman caught it against her skirt with one hand and kept speaking. The other woman took it from her without looking. Servant actor reached for it one line later because his hand expected the cup to be there.

Nothing. He converted the reach into pointing. Nobody laughed. The scene continued.

Afterward he said:

“I saved that.”

The recurring woman said:

“You nearly murdered a cup.”

“I saved the scene.”

“You pointed.”

“Heroically.”

Teren said:

“Again.”

I laughed. Servant actor pointed at me.

“See? Audience.”

“I am not audience.”

“You laughed.”

“Against my will.”

“Counts.”

I did not know whether it counted. I did know I wanted the next run. At the end Teren collected pages.

“Tomorrow?”

“Hessa.”

He nodded. No after. Interesting. The recurring woman passed me carrying the empty plate. She stopped. Looked at me.

“You came back.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

That was all. Small. Ordinary. Somehow better than being needed. I liked that.

Then:

“Messenger.”

“I was not messenger.”

“Landlord?”

“Briefly.”

“Person?”

“Yes.”

She nodded solemnly.

“Promotion.”

“Fuck you.”

She laughed. I left. My palms were warm. Right calf tired. Not bad. Residual limb quiet. I sat on the low wall outside before walking home. Not because ordered. Because I wanted to. People passed.

Nobody called me Messenger. Excellent. Then the bread seller across the street saw me. He raised one hand. I raised a finger. He laughed. I did too. No magic today. Tomorrow Hessa. Today I had been a landlord, a person, and occasionally furniture.

None of them mattered. I wanted to go back.
