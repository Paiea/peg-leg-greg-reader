LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 137

THE SHAPE

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Hessa had paper. Too much paper. Not notes. Separate sheets. Blank. I stopped in the doorway.

“No.”

She looked up.

“What?”

“You have paper.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Sit.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the next event.”

I sat. Morning baseline first. Throat normal. Good. Hands normal. Right calf normal. Residual limb quiet. Phantom heel present if I looked for it. No thumb. No fingers. No wrist. No unusual left response.

No cold. No weakness. No pain. Hessa wrote all of that. Then slid one blank sheet closer. I looked at it.

“No magic drawing.”

“No.”

“Good.”

She put the sheet back.

“Today there is no direction.”

I looked at her.

“At all?”

“At all.”

“That seems regressive.”

“Yes.”

“You agree?”

“With the word you chose? No.”

“You just said yes.”

“I agreed it seems that way to you.”

Terrible woman. I rested my right forearm on the folded white towel. Same broad outer-forearm region. No charcoal. No thread. Copper farther up the table. No target. No indicated direction. Nothing.

“What is the question?” I asked.

“What does the localized shape do before we ask it to do anything else?”

I stared at my arm.

“That is boring.”

“Yes.”

“You are spending an attempt to characterize geometry.”

“Yes.”

“Not direction.”

“No.”

“Not external effect.”

“No.”

“Not progression.”

She looked at me.

“Gregory.”

“Fine.”

“One draw.”

“Yes.”

“One shape.”

“Yes.”

“Describe it at fixed points.”

I looked at the paper again.

“Then draw after?”

“Maybe.”

“You said maybe yesterday.”

“I say many things.”

“Structured self-report.”

“Yes.”

“Still not independent.”

“Yes.”

“Why paper?”

“To preserve your report with fewer words.”

“That sounds like drawing.”

“After release, if I decide the verbal report was stable enough to make the drawing worth doing.”

I thought.

“Why after release?”

“Because drawing during the attempt recruits the hand.”

Obviously. Good.

“Could I use the left?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because that is a stupid way to introduce a second motor task into an assessment about right-forearm sensation.”

Fair. She moved the paper away from me.

“Before draw, I am going to name four edges.”

“Wrist, elbow, upper, lower.”

“Yes.”

“Upper relative to what?”

“Your arm position now.”

“That is imprecise.”

“Useful enough.”

She pointed without touching. Wrist side. Elbow side. Upper side, away from the table edge. Lower side, toward the table edge. Not anatomical superior/inferior. Table geometry. Fine.

“At each checkpoint,” she said, “you report center and four edges.”

“That is a lot.”

“Yes.”

“You told me not to overreport.”

“I told you not to invent precision you do not have.”

Different.

“Do I report shape before size?”

“Whatever you can reliably distinguish first.”

“Could change.”

“Yes.”

“Useful.”

She smiled once. Barely. I had become infected.

“Draw.”

I did. Minimal. Narrow. Familiar. Not easy. Familiar. No hand. No thumb. No left. Stable.

“Report.”

“Minimal draw. No other response.”

“Hold.”

Held.

“Shape.”

No direction. That was harder now. Not physically. Mentally. I had spent several assessments waiting for an instruction to point the shape somewhere. Now there was none. I found the localized alteration. Center first.

Then boundary. Small. Uneven.

“Report when stable.”

I waited. The region settled.

“Center changed.”

“Yes.”

“Size around two fingers across. Maybe slightly longer along the arm.”

“Direction?”

“No instructed direction.”

“I asked what you perceive.”

I checked.

“Long axis maybe wrist-elbow.”

“How sure?”

“Moderate.”

“Edges.”

“Wrist side clearer than elbow.”

There. Again. Hessa did not react.

“Upper?”

“Softer.”

“Lower?”

“Clearer than upper.”

“Center?”

“Stable.”

“Shape?”

“Oval. Not symmetric.”

“Which edge most distinct?”

“Wrist.”

“Second?”

“Lower.”

“Elbow?”

“Third.”

“Upper?”

“Least.”

“How confident in that ordering?”

I waited.

“Wrist and upper, high. Middle two less.”

She wrote.

“Hold.”

I held. This was uncomfortable in the scientific sense. Not physically. I wanted direction. Not because I wanted magic. Because direction would give the asymmetry a reason. Without instruction, the shape was just asymmetric.

That was worse.

“Checkpoint two.”

I checked.

“Center stable.”

“Size?”

“Same.”

“Wrist edge?”

“Still strongest.”

“Elbow?”

“Maybe sharper than before.”

“Upper?”

“Still soft.”

“Lower?”

“Same.”

“Shape?”

“Oval. Maybe less uneven.”

“Maybe.”

“Yes.”

“Hold.”

Held. No broadening. No motor. No external sensation. Nothing dramatic. The shape existed. Or the report existed. Same old problem.

“Checkpoint three.”

I wanted the wrist edge to have changed. It had not.

“Center stable. Size same. Wrist strongest. Lower maybe second. Elbow close. Upper weakest.”

“Any spontaneous directional change?”

“No.”

“Any extension?”

“No.”

“Any drift?”

I checked.

“No.”

“Anything else?”

“No.”

Hessa wrote. Then waited. Longer. I looked at her.

“Are we done?”

“Not yet.”

“Why?”

“Time.”

“What about it?”

“I want one longer hold without direction.”

“That increases exposure.”

“Yes.”

“How long?”

“Short.”

“That is not a number.”

“Longer than the previous interval. Shorter than I would permit if this were perfectly boring.”

I smiled.

“Perfectly boring.”

“Do not make me regret phrasing.”

I held. The localized region stayed. Wrist edge still distinct. Then something changed. Small. Not magic outward. Inside. The upper edge sharpened slightly. I thought. I waited. It stayed.

“Hessa.”

“Report.”

“Upper edge clearer.”

“Compared to?”

“Before.”

“Wrist?”

“Still strongest.”

“Lower?”

“Same.”

“Elbow?”

“Same.”

“Center?”

“Stable.”

“Did you intend upper?”

“No.”

“Expect upper?”

“No.”

“Confidence?”

“Moderate.”

She wrote.

“Anything else?”

“No.”

“Hold.”

I held. The upper edge remained clearer. The shape was now more balanced. Still not symmetric. Maybe.

“RETURN.”

I reduced. Interesting. Which edge went first? I had not thought about that. The upper softened quickly. Then lower. Wrist stayed distinct longest. Elbow faded with center.

“Report.”

I did.

“Wrist persists longest?”

“Yes.”

“How sure?”

“Moderate.”

“Any movement?”

“No.”

“Any motor?”

“No.”

“Left?”

“No.”

“Hold.”

The region diminished.

“Gone?”

“Almost.”

Wait.

“Gone.”

“Draw?”

“Still.”

“Release.”

I released. Clean. Nothing chased it. No hand. No thumb. No fingers. No left. No external anything. Hessa checked everything. Normal. Then she put the blank paper in front of me.

“Now draw.”

I looked.

“With which hand?”

“Right.”

“That seems obvious now.”

“Yes.”

“From memory.”

“Yes.”

“Not exact anatomy.”

“No.”

“Just the shape.”

“Yes.”

I took the charcoal. Not magic. Ordinary. My right hand felt normal. I drew an oval. Stopped. Too smooth.

“That is wrong.”

“Then fix it.”

I flattened the wrist side slightly. Made upper edge softer. Lower edge a little more distinct. Then added a second faint outline for the later upper-edge sharpening. Hessa watched.

“Labels.”

I wrote:

WRIST STRONGEST UPPER SOFT, LATER CLEARER LOWER MODERATE ELBOW MODERATE CENTER STABLE I looked.

“That is ugly.”

“Yes.”

“Scientifically.”

“Yes.”

She took the charcoal.

Under the drawing she wrote:

POST-RELEASE STRUCTURED SELF-REPORT FROM MEMORY. NOT INDEPENDENT OBSERVATION. Good.

“You really enjoy ruining things.”

“I enjoy labeling them.”

“What does this tell you?”

“Several things.”

I waited. She did not continue.

“Hessa.”

“You want the directional answer.”

“Yes.”

“Today was not a directional test.”

“I know.”

“So I am not going to use it as one.”

I stared.

“Fine. What did today tell you?”

“That your neutral localized shape was not reported as symmetric.”

“Yes.”

“That the wrist-side boundary was the most consistently distinct edge across the observation period.”

“Yes.”

“That the upper edge changed without an instruction toward it.”

“Yes.”

“That your reported geometry changed slightly over time while the center and gross size remained stable.”

“Yes.”

“That RETURN was clean.”

“Yes.”

“And that a post-release drawing may be useful for preserving your self-report across sessions.”

I looked at the drawing.

“Useful enough to repeat?”

“Possibly.”

“Again tomorrow?”

“No.”

“One no-magic day.”

“Yes.”

“Then assessment.”

“Yes.”

“Neutral shape again?”

“I have not decided.”

“You absolutely have.”

“No.”

“What are your options?”

She considered.

“Repeat neutral shape.”

“Yes.”

“Repeat elbowward with a neutral pre-report first.”

“Yes.”

“Repeat wristward with a neutral pre-report first.”

“Yes.”

“Do neither and return to simple localization.”

“That seems less useful.”

“Possibly.”

“Could compare drawings.”

“Yes.”

“Could rotate arm position.”

“No.”

I stopped.

“Why?”

“Too many changes.”

Right.

“Same position first.”

“Probably.”

“Then later position.”

“Maybe.”

I leaned back.

“This is becoming geometry.”

“It was always geometry.”

I looked at her. She looked back. That sounded dangerously like theory. She did not expand. Good. I looked at my drawing again. Uneven oval. Not impressive. No glow. No thread movement. No object moving across a room.

A shape I could not see, drawn after it was gone. Progress. Maybe. I hated maybe.

“Can I keep the drawing?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Notes.”

“I made it.”

“During my assessment.”

“Communist.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

She put the paper with the notes. I rolled my sleeve down. Throat normal. Arm normal. Everything normal. We had spent an hour proving that something invisible might be slightly lopsided. Excellent.

At the door Hessa said:

“Tomorrow no magic.”

“Yes.”

“No drawing it again.”

“I already drew it.”

“No redrawing from memory.”

“That is not magic.”

“It is assessment work.”

“You have become very broad about that.”

“Yes.”

“Fine.”

“No comparing the shape to your sword.”

I froze.

“What?”

She looked at me.

“The herb seller.”

I closed my eyes.

“Who told the herb seller about the sword?”

“Apparently everyone.”

“This city is evil.”

“No.”

“Small.”

“Not small.”

“Then explain this.”

“People talk.”

I left. The bread seller saw me. I pointed before he could speak.

“No.”

He opened his mouth anyway.

“OVAL.”

I stopped. He grinned.

“How?”

“Hessa buys bread.”

Of course she did. I considered murder. Did not. No magic tomorrow. The theatre was west. Lyssa might be south. Guild somewhere else. Home behind me. I stood at the corner longer than necessary.

Then went west. Not because of the sword. That lie had become unusable. Fine. I wanted another run.
