LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 147

THE REHEARSAL

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

I went to work. Then I missed my entrance. Not by much. Enough. Iven was onstage being poor at paying debts when my cue came through the opening in the wall. Clear. Perfectly clear. I heard it. I knew it. I waited because in Carrow there had been another line before mine. There was not another line here. Teren had cut it ten minutes earlier while I was eating bread. Iven waited. The hall waited. I realized. I moved. Too fast. My right crutch hit the edge of the wing.

The sound was enormous. Not actually enormous. It felt enormous. I entered late with one crutch still vibrating. Iven looked at me.

"Master, before you become unreasonable---"

"I already am."

That was not the line. Iven paused. I knew the correct line. He knew I knew it.

"Good," he said. "Then perhaps we can skip ahead."

Someone laughed backstage. Not Teren. Teren said, "Stop." We stopped. He was in the house with three pages in one hand and a pencil in his mouth.

"Again."

"From where?"

"Entrance."

"I didn't know you cut---"

"Now you do."

That was my acting note. I went back. The local worker standing near the wall opening moved aside for me. I positioned my crutches. Waited. Iven started. This time I entered correctly. Then I overplayed the first laugh. Not deliberately. That made it worse.

"You've had eleven days to improve it," I said.

I waited. Not long. Long enough. There was no audience. No laugh. Only an empty hall, local workers, Teren, Serra in the opposite wing, and someone hammering upstairs despite Coln's apparent objection to anyone using upstairs. The pause sat there. Dead. Iven continued.

"You wound me."

"No. Then you would owe me for the floor."

I did it again. The shape of the old laugh. Waiting for something that was not there. Teren said, "Greg."

"Yes."

"Don't."

"What?"

He looked at me. Then at the pages. Then toward the front doors, where Coln's agent had appeared again.

"Don't do that."

He walked away. Excellent. Iven waited.

"What am I doing?"

"The thing."

"You said that before."

"Yes."

"What thing?"

He shrugged.

"Waiting for ghosts."

I looked at him. That was irritatingly specific.

"Again."

We started. This time I rushed. Better than waiting. Worse in another direction. Iven interrupted me in the middle of a line.

"No."

"What?"

"You're stepping on me."

"I am not near you."

"Words."

"I know what stepping on a line means."

"Then stop."

I took a breath.

"Again."

"From?"

"Beginning."

He looked toward the house. Teren was gone.

"Fine."

We started again. It was mediocre. That was all. Not secretly good. Not almost there. Mediocre. I knew because I had felt the scene work before. This did not. When we finished, Iven said, "Food?"

"I just ate."

"I didn't."

He left. I remained onstage. Serra crossed behind me carrying the wooden crown.

"Was that bad?"

"Yes."

"Good."

"Why good?"

She stopped.

"I didn't say good for you."

Then she kept going. Fine. The rehearsal moved on without solving me. Marek came down from upstairs wearing a different coat. Not gray. Blue. Coln saw him.

"Why were you upstairs?"

Marek stopped at the bottom of the stairs.

"Changing."

"I said you do not go upstairs."

"You said that before I needed trousers."

"There is a dressing room downstairs."

"Occupied."

"By whom?"

Marek looked toward the women's room. Coln looked too. Nessa's voice came from inside.

"Don't."

I turned. Nessa was here. So was Rinna. I had not seen them arrive. A cart must have come while I was failing. Three costume trunks blocked half the corridor. Rinna stood over one with her hands on her hips. Pell was inside the dressing room. Serra had vanished. Nessa stepped out holding the mourning dress. The hem looked normal. I had never seen it before the cut, so this meant nothing. Coln said, "Who are you?"

"Nessa."

"That room is not included."

Nessa looked behind her.

"At what?"

"In your hire."

She stared.

"It's a room."

"Upstairs rooms are excluded."

"This is downstairs."

Coln pointed toward a different door.

"That one."

Nessa followed his finger.

"Then why are you telling me?"

Coln looked at Teren. Teren was not there. This seemed to happen often. The agent stepped between them.

"Master Coln means the upper wardrobe and two rear rooms are not part of the present agreement."

Nessa said, "Fine." No argument. She turned.

"Pell. We don't have wardrobe."

Pell's voice came from inside.

"Fuck."

Rinna said, "Wagon." Nessa said, "Too far." Rinna said, "Old kitchen." Edda shouted from somewhere, "No." Nessa shouted back, "Temporary."

"No."

"Corner."

"No."

"Under table."

"No."

Nessa looked at Rinna.

"Green room."

A local worker passing by said, "No." Nessa closed her eyes. The mourning dress remained over one arm. This problem had nothing to do with me. I enjoyed it for almost thirty seconds. Then Rinna pointed at me.

"Greg."

"No."

She blinked.

"I didn't ask yet."

"I know."

"Hold this."

She handed me the mourning dress. Of course. I held it. Nessa and Rinna went to inspect the green room. Pell came out carrying a box of pins. He saw me with the dress.

"Don't let it touch the floor."

"I know."

"You don't."

"I can infer."

"Keep the hem up."

"I am."

"Other side."

I lifted the other side.

"Higher."

"Pell."

"What?"

"I have one free hand."

He looked at my crutch.

"Oh."

He took the dress.

"Thanks."

That was the end of my involvement. Good. I went looking for Teren. Not because of the costume room. Because my Shopkeeper was bad. I found him in the house near the back row speaking with the ledger woman.

"No," she said.

"Why?"

"Sound dies under balcony."

"There is no balcony."

She pointed upward. A shallow gallery projected from the rear wall. Teren looked at it.

"That is a gallery."

"Yes."

"Different."

"No."

He rubbed his face. I waited. The ledger woman said, "If you put musicians there, they lose stage."

"We don't have musicians."

"Then don't."

"Excellent."

She walked away. Teren saw me.

"No."

"I haven't asked."

"Good."

"Shopkeeper is bad."

"Yes."

I stared.

"You watched?"

"Some."

"What am I doing?"

"Too much."

"That is not specific."

He looked toward the stage. Someone shouted his name. Then someone else shouted it from backstage. Teren pointed at me.

"Stop trying to make it good."

He turned. I said, "That is terrible advice."

"Then ignore it."

He left. I stood among empty benches. The hall owner walked past me carrying a ledger. Not the ledger woman's ledger. Different ledger. He did not look at me. I went back to the stage. A rehearsal had started without me. Not my scene. Marek and Serra. King and Princess. The Sword was not in it. I stayed in the wing. Marek crossed downstage. Serra stopped him.

"No."

"What?"

"Too far."

"I am at the mark."

"Wrong mark."

She pointed with her toe. There were three chalk marks near each other. Marek looked down.

"Who did that?"

A local worker said, "Me."

"Why three?"

"Chair. Table. You."

"Which is me?"

The worker pointed. Marek moved. Serra restarted. No Teren. They ran the scene themselves. Marek missed a turn. Serra corrected him without stopping. He adjusted. She changed her path around the chair because the wing was shallower. He changed his next cross to give her space. Neither discussed it. They had done this longer than I had. That was obvious. Halfway through, Serra skipped a line. Marek answered the line she should have said anyway. She stopped.

"No."

"You skipped."

"I know."

"Then why stop?"

"Because you covered."

"Yes."

"Don't."

"Why?"

"I need to know I skipped."

Marek considered.

"Fine."

They went back. That was interesting. Not for me. For them. I stayed quiet. They ran it again. Better. Then Serra's yellow-scarf woman appeared in the house. She stood near the back. Serra saw her. Missed her next line. Marek waited. Serra recovered. The woman in yellow did not react. When the scene ended, Serra walked offstage without looking at Marek. She went directly into the house. The yellow-scarf woman met her halfway. They spoke. Too far for me to hear. Serra folded her arms.

The woman said something. Serra looked away. Marek came into the wing. He saw them. His expression changed.

"You know her."

He looked at me.

"Yes."

"Who is she?"

"No."

"That is not an answer."

"It is."

He picked up a cup. Empty. He put it down.

"Is she family?"

Marek looked at me. Then at Serra.

"No."

"Friend?"

"Greg."

"What?"

"Not yours."

I stopped. He was right. I disliked how often that happened. The yellow-scarf woman touched Serra's arm again. Serra pulled away this time. Not violently. Clearly. The woman said something else. Serra left through the front doors. The woman remained. Marek watched. I watched Marek. He noticed.

"Don't."

Fine. I went to find food. Edda had onions in the old kitchen. Still.

"How many onions are there?"

"Too many."

"What are they for?"

"Tonight."

"Are we eating here?"

"Yes."

"Where are we sleeping?"

She looked at me. I had not asked Teren. I had forgotten.

"Not here."

"Useful."

She handed me an onion.

"What?"

"Peel."

"I am an actor."

"Congratulations."

I peeled onions. This was better than Shopkeeper. Edda cut them. I peeled. Another local woman came in and started washing a pot. They talked over me. Not to me.

"Did he come back?"

"No."

"He said he would."

"He says."

"You going?"

"No."

"You said that last time."

"I went last time."

"Exactly."

"That's why no."

The second woman laughed. Edda did not. I peeled another onion. I had no idea who they meant. Good. After five onions, my eyes watered.

"This is hostile."

Edda said, "Actor."

"Yes."

"Use it."

"For what?"

She smiled. I hated everyone. Rinna came in.

"Need table."

"No," Edda said.

"Five minutes."

"No."

"Two."

"No."

Rinna looked at me. I shook my head. She left. Edda handed me another onion. The rehearsal resumed after the costume rooms were reorganized into some arrangement I did not understand. Nessa had taken half the green room. The local crew hated this. Edda had successfully defended the old kitchen. Marek was no longer wearing blue. Serra had returned. The yellow-scarf woman had not. Nobody mentioned it. Teren called Shopkeeper. I wiped onion from my hands. Iven appeared from nowhere.

"Again?"

"Yes."

"You smell."

"Onions."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"Debt."

"That makes no sense."

He entered. I waited in the farther wing position. The cue came through the wall. I entered. Correctly. That was the only part I did correctly. The first exchange was flat again. Then Iven changed a line. Not much.

"Master, before you become unreasonable, I have a very good explanation."

Very good. Different. I answered.

"You've had eleven days to ruin it."

Wrong line. Not written. Iven smiled.

"Ten."

"What?"

"It was ten yesterday."

I stared. He stared back. Oh.

"Then you've wasted today too."

Somebody in the house laughed. Not an audience. Crew. I did not wait. We continued. Iven changed another line. I answered differently. The scene loosened. Then I got greedy. I reached for the consultation line because I knew it worked. I set it up. Too clearly. Iven saw me doing it. He refused.

Instead of asking whether I was threatening him, he said, "You are a deeply unpleasant man." I had nothing. My prepared answer died. I stared. Iven waited.

"Pay me."

He laughed. Actually laughed. Not acting. Teren said, "Good. Stop." I looked toward the house. He was halfway down an aisle with Coln.

"That was good?"

"Enough."

"Why?"

He kept walking. Three seconds. Iven came beside me.

"You tried to make me say it."

"Yes."

"No."

"I know."

"Good."

He left. I stood there. Mediocre first. Better second. Still not solved. Fine. The Sword rehearsal was worse. Serra was different after whatever had happened with the yellow-scarf woman. Not worse. Different. Quieter. Her Princess entered without the earlier sharpness. I expected the version from Carrow. Wrong. My first line came too hard. She looked at the sword. Not at me. The silence after my line was wrong. I filled it. Also wrong.

"Do you intend to carry me like that all evening?"

Serra turned.

"That isn't your line."

"I know."

"Then why did you say it?"

I had no answer. Teren shouted from the house, "Keep going." We kept going. Badly. I tried to find the rhythm. Serra did not give me the old rhythm. She moved slower. Held the sword differently. At one point she sat instead of crossing. I missed the cue because I was waiting for the cross. Silence. Serra said, "Sword?"

"Yes."

"That was your cue."

"I know."

"Apparently not."

Marek laughed backstage. I said, "Fuck you." Teren said, "Sword doesn't say fuck you."

"I wasn't Sword."

"Good."

We restarted. Second time, I watched Serra. Too much. I followed every movement. That made me late in a different way. She stopped.

"Greg."

"What?"

"I'm not going to do the same thing tonight."

"I know."

"No. You know now."

That hurt. Fair.

"Why?"

She looked at me. Not angry. Tired.

"Because I don't want to."

That was all. Teren called from the house.

"Again."

Serra took her starting place. I did too. Third time, I stopped trying to predict her. Not completely. Enough. She entered. Picked up the sword. Held it. I waited for the line. Not the movement. The line came. I answered. Better. Then I missed another cue later anyway. Not everything improved at once. Good. We finished. Serra left immediately. No note. No conversation. She went backstage, took her coat, and disappeared through the side door. I looked at Marek. He was changing a belt.

"Don't."

"I didn't ask."

"Your face."

"I have one face."

"No."

Davin said that too. Conspiracy. Teren climbed onto the stage. Everyone who was nearby looked at him. Not because he was important. Because he had the schedule.

"Tonight," he said. "Doors at seven. First show tomorrow afternoon. Second tomorrow night."

Someone said, "Both tomorrow?"

"Yes."

Nessa said, "No." Teren looked at her.

"Why no?"

"Red dress isn't done."

"Which red dress?"

Nessa stared. Teren corrected.

"Right. Red dress. When?"

"Tomorrow morning."

"Good."

"No. Noon."

"Show afternoon."

"I know."

"Can it be morning?"

"No."

"Can the show be evening?"

Coln said from the house, "No." Nobody had known he was there. Teren closed his eyes. Nessa said, "Then she wears blue." Rinna said, "Blue is court." Nessa looked at her.

"Not anymore."

That solved something. Or created something. Teren continued.

"Tonight is setup and open rehearsal. No full show."

I raised my hand. He ignored me.

"Food here. Sleeping at the River House, three streets west. Rooms are arranged."

I lowered my hand. That had been my question.

"Greg."

I looked up.

"Yes?"

"Shopkeeper tomorrow afternoon. Sword both."

"Shopkeeper night?"

"Maybe Meren."

"He's coming?"

"Maybe."

"When will you know?"

Teren looked at me. I knew.

"If it changes me."

Pell laughed from the wing. Teren frowned.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

He moved on.

"Upstairs is closed except costume changes with permission."

Marek raised his hand.

"No," Teren said.

Marek lowered it.

"Do not argue with Coln. Do not move his benches. Do not touch the front lamps. If you break anything, tell Davin before Coln sees it."

Davin said, "Tell me before you break it."

"Better."

Teren looked around.

"Questions?"

Everyone spoke at once. Not me. Nessa wanted time. Rinna wanted storage. Pell wanted to know which blue dress. Iven wanted food. Orin wanted the room list. A local worker wanted our tower moved. Coln wanted the tower out of the corridor.

Davin said the tower was only there because Coln would not let him put the long box against the wall. Coln said the box should be flat. Davin said, "No." That continued. I stepped offstage. Nobody noticed. Outside, late afternoon had turned the river bright between buildings. I had time. Maybe. Ten minutes to the municipal hall. Ten back. Unless rehearsal started again. Unless someone needed me. Unless Teren changed something. I looked inside. Everyone was arguing. Good. I went to the old kitchen. Edda was stirring the pot.

"Municipal hall."

She pointed with the spoon.

"Front, left. Fountain, right. Uphill."

"I remember."

"Then why tell me?"

"I wasn't asking."

She looked at me.

"Actor."

I took the bread she offered. Then I went outside. For the first time since arriving, nobody gave me anything to carry. I turned left.
