LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 149

THE AUDIENCE

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Then we went inside. Nobody was ready. This did not prevent people from watching. The front doors stood open to the river street, and the house had perhaps thirty people scattered through it. Not seated properly. Scattered.

Three children occupied the front bench and were arguing over the middle. Two women sat halfway back with baskets at their feet. A man in a work apron had chosen an aisle seat and was eating something from folded paper. Several local crew members had stopped pretending they were not watching. Mara was there. I stopped. Municipal Mara. Ink cuffs. She sat near the back with her father. He saw me.

"Roof boy!"

The entire hall did not turn. Enough people did. I closed my eyes. Serra looked at me.

"Roof?"

"Don't."

Her mouth moved. Not a smile. Worse. The old man raised one hand. I did not wave. Mara did. Traitor. Someone backstage shouted, "Sword!"

"I know!"

Serra was already moving.

"Princess!"

"I know!"

We went opposite directions. That felt appropriate. The backstage corridor had become narrower because it contained more people, more clothing, and one large painted wall that had not been there when I left. I stopped. The wall stopped me too.

"Why is this here?"

A local worker behind it said, "Move."

"I can't."

"Then back."

I backed. The wall advanced. I flattened against the real wall. It passed. Davin came behind it holding the bottom edge.

"Where is it going?"

"Stage."

"Why?"

"Wall."

"That isn't---"

He was gone. Fine. I found the men's dressing room. My bag was under the stool. Iven was not asleep on it. Improvement. Marek was buttoning a brown vest while Pell knelt beside a trunk looking for something.

"Have you seen the narrow belt?" Pell asked.

"No," Marek said.

"You wore it."

"When?"

"Carrow."

"That was days ago."

"Yes."

"Then no."

Pell looked at me.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"Good."

I put my crutches against the wall long enough to check my coat. Apple. Still there. Less round. I took it out. One side had a bruise where I had apparently leaned against something. Marek reached. I moved it away.

"No."

"Food."

"Mine."

"Where did you get an apple?"

"Roof."

He stopped.

"What?"

"Nothing."

I put it in my bag. The apple had survived architecture. It did not need theatre. Teren appeared in the doorway.

"Greg. Sword first."

"What about Shopkeeper?"

"Later."

"How much later?"

"After Sword."

"That is not---"

He was gone. Marek said, "Good talk."

"Fuck you."

"Better."

Pell found the belt under Marek's coat. He stared at it. Marek looked down.

"Oh."

Pell hit him with it. Not hard. They continued dressing. I went to the wing. The house had grown. Maybe forty now. People entered without ceremony. Some paid something at the front. Some apparently did not. A woman came in, saw someone she knew, crossed two rows to sit beside her, and immediately began talking. This was not an audience. It was a town temporarily facing the same direction. Teren stood in the center aisle.

"Start with the road scene."

Someone onstage said, "Which road?"

"The second."

"We cut the second."

"No, we cut the first half of the second."

"So the third?"

Teren looked at his pages.

"No."

A child in front laughed. Teren looked at him. The child stopped. Then Teren smiled. The child smiled back.

"Second road," Teren said. "From the toll."

Actors moved. No curtain. No announcement. A local worker carried a chair across the stage while the scene began. The audience did not care. Or they understood. I did not. The road scene started. Orin entered wearing half a costume. Proper coat, ordinary trousers. Iven followed with a hat I had never seen. They spoke. Teren stopped them after four lines.

"No. Bench is wrong."

A local worker said, "Bench is there."

"I know."

"You asked there."

"Yesterday."

"This is today."

The worker stared. Teren pointed.

"Two feet."

The worker and another man moved the bench. The audience watched. The children in front whispered. Orin stayed in character while the bench moved around him.

"You see," he told Iven, "this is what happens when public works receive funding."

A few people laughed. Teren said, "Don't add." Orin said, "Of course." They restarted. He did not add it. That impressed me more than the joke. The scene ran. A woman entered late and sat beside Mara. Mara's father said something to her. She laughed. Not at the play. Fine. Nobody was obligated. Teren stopped the scene twice. Once for blocking. Once because Iven's hat fell off. Iven picked it up. Teren said, "Again from bridge." Iven said, "There is no bridge."

"Line."

"Oh."

They continued. The audience stayed. Mostly. The man with folded-paper food left halfway through. A mother came in with a baby. Two boys left. Three older men took their seats. The room changed. Not dramatically. Enough. Then Teren called Sword. My stomach tightened. That was stupid. I had performed for more people at East Market Hall. This was not a performance. That made it worse. At a performance, mistakes belonged to the show. Here, Teren could stop me. Everyone would know why. Serra came into the wing.

She wore the blue dress. Not Princess blue. Different blue. Nessa was behind her adjusting a sleeve.

"Arm."

Serra lifted it.

"Other."

She changed arms. Nessa pinned something. Serra looked at me.

"You look ill."

"I am fine."

"Good."

Nessa said, "Don't move."

"I am not moving."

"To her."

"Oh."

Serra's face remained neutral. Nessa finished.

"Don't catch this on him."

She pointed at my crutch. Serra looked at the sleeve. Then at me.

"Move."

"I am not even near you."

"Future."

Nessa left. Serra rolled one shoulder. I said, "Are you doing it the same?" She looked at me. I knew immediately that this was the wrong question.

"No."

"Right."

"Greg."

"What?"

"Listen."

"I know."

She stared.

"No. You know now."

I hated callbacks when I deserved them. Teren shouted, "Princess!" Serra entered. I stayed behind the wall opening. Could not see her. Good. Maybe. The scene began before the Sword entered. Her voice carried through the opening. Different again. Not as quiet as afternoon rehearsal. Sharper. But not East Market sharp. I tried not to build a version of her before my cue. That required more effort than it should have. Line. Line. Movement I could hear but not see. Then my cue. I spoke.

"Careful."

Too serious. I knew it as soon as it left me. Serra paused. The audience did too. Then she said, "You object?" Not the written line. I answered.

"I prefer thieves with steadier hands."

A laugh. Small. Front somewhere. My body noticed it. Immediately. Dangerous. Serra said, "You prefer thieves?"

"I have limited experience."

Another laugh. More. There. I felt myself reach. Not for a line. For the feeling. The room had opened slightly. I wanted to keep it open. Serra moved. I heard fabric. Her next written line came. I answered too quickly. Stepped on the end. The laugh vanished. Good. Punishment immediate. Serra continued. I settled. For three exchanges, we were fine.

Then she crossed somewhere I could not see and changed the way she held the sword. I knew because my next line expected the blade to tilt. It did not. Silence. Not cue silence. My silence. I had used movement again. Fuck. Serra waited. The audience waited. I said the line late. Teren called, "Stop." There it was. Everyone watched. Not everyone. The baby did not. Mara's father was scratching his beard. A woman in the third row was whispering. Still. Enough. Teren stood.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"You can't see her."

"I know."

"Then why are you watching her?"

"I am not."

Serra looked toward the wing. I could feel it. Teren said, "You're waiting for movement."

"Yes."

"Don't."

"I know."

"Good. Again."

That was it. No lecture. The audience heard all of it. Excellent. We restarted. A child in front said, "He can't see her?" His mother whispered something. The child said, louder, "Why not?" Teren said, "Because he's a sword." The child considered.

"Oh."

We continued. This time I listened. Actually listened. Not for the cue I remembered. For Serra. Her breath came through the opening before one line. The scrape of her shoe before another. Not useful. Then words. Useful. I answered the words. Better.

We reached the place where the Princess threatened to return the Sword to the King. At East Market Hall, I had looked toward Marek as King, waited, and said no. Big laugh. I remembered it. Of course I remembered it. Serra said, "Perhaps I should return you to His Majesty." There. The old opening. Marek was onstage. I could not see him. I could imagine him picking at his sleeve. I almost did the old thing.

"To him?"

A few people laughed. Not many. I heard Marek make a small offended sound. Different. I waited. Too long. Ghost. Fuck. Then a man in the audience coughed. The laugh was gone. I said, "No." Nothing. Dead. Not catastrophic. Dead. Teren did not stop us. Worse. We continued. I could feel my face heating though nobody could see it. Good. Sword. No face. Serra changed the next line.

"You seemed more certain a moment ago."

That was for me. Not Princess. Both. I answered, "I had less information." A laugh. Not large. Real. I did not wait.

"Most kings improve with distance."

Marek said, "I can hear you." That got a bigger laugh. I answered immediately.

"That is unfortunate."

Bigger. There. Again. The room opened. I wanted it. I knew I wanted it. Marek stepped in.

"I could have you melted."

Written? No. Probably not. I said, "Into what?" He paused. I heard the audience shift. Marek said, "Something useful." Good. Very good. I had an answer. Three answers. All bad because I was choosing. The pause lengthened. Serra saved us.

"A spoon."

The front children exploded. Not polite laughter. Actual children losing control. One slapped the bench. Marek looked at her. I could not see it. I heard his silence. Then he said, "Why a spoon?" Serra answered, "He would finally serve someone." The adults joined. I laughed. Sword should not. I did. Teren said, "Stop." We stopped. The laughter faded. Teren pointed at Serra.

"Keep spoon."

Marek said, "Absolutely not."

"Maybe."

"Then why say keep?"

"So I remember."

"That is not what keep means."

Teren pointed at me.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"Don't laugh."

"I know."

"Also don't wait for the old no."

"I know."

"Good."

Three seconds. He sat. Serra came offstage. I moved out of her way. She looked at me.

"Spoon."

"Fuck you."

"Not mine."

"Might be."

She smiled. Not much. Enough. Then the yellow-scarf woman entered the hall. Serra saw her. The smile disappeared. Not because of me. Good. The woman took a seat near the side wall. Serra looked away. Nessa caught Serra before she could leave.

"Sleeve."

"Now?"

"Yes."

Serra raised her arm. Work continued. I went backstage. Marek followed.

"Spoon."

"No."

"You would make a lovely spoon."

"I hope Coln throws you upstairs."

Marek's expression changed. Tiny. I had stepped somewhere. Not terrible. Still.

"Sorry."

He shrugged.

"Don't be. He'd have to catch me."

Then he walked away. Maybe fine. Maybe not. I did not follow. The open rehearsal moved to scenes I was not in. I sat in the green room. Half of it was costumes now. Nessa had won. Or compromised aggressively. Rinna stood over a trunk sorting fabric by a system I could not identify. Pell held the mourning dress up against a local lamp.

"No."

Nessa said, "What?"

"Still wrong."

"Where?"

He pointed at the hem. Nessa crouched. Rinna said, "It's the shoe."

"No," Pell said.

"It was the shoe yesterday."

"Different wrong."

"What different?"

Pell touched the fabric.

"Pulls."

Nessa looked.

"It does."

Rinna said, "Because you shortened it." Pell looked at her. Nessa said, "Not now." Rinna continued sorting. I sat. Nobody asked why. Good. My shoulders hurt. Not badly. The right one had tightened from the hill and rehearsal. My hands were tired. I flexed them. Rinna noticed. Noticed, not cared.

"Your bag's under the stool."

"I know."

"Need stool."

I moved my bag. She took the stool. That was all. I leaned against the wall. Someone onstage forgot a line. Teren supplied it from the house. Someone else argued. A local worker came into the green room looking for a red sash. Nessa said, "No." He said, "Hall sash."

"This is ours."

"Red."

"All red belongs to you?"

"Here, yes."

Rinna threw him a different sash. He left. Pell said, "That was mine." Rinna said, "Not anymore." I closed my eyes. Only for a moment. When I opened them, Orin was sitting beside me. I had not heard him arrive.

"You sleep?"

"No."

"You looked asleep."

"I was resting my eyes."

He nodded.

"Old person phrase."

"You are the old person."

"Exactly."

He held out a small cup. Water. I took it.

"Thank you."

He had another. We drank. From the stage, Marek said something that made the audience laugh. Orin tilted his head.

"He's hunting."

"What?"

"Nothing."

"No, what?"

Orin drank.

"You'll see."

"Is he waiting for ghosts?"

"What?"

"Iven said---"

"I don't care what Iven said."

He stood.

"Where are you going?"

"My scene."

He left. Excellent. I had learned nothing. The house filled further as evening went on. Not full. Maybe sixty at the most. People came and went. A group of apprentices stayed for one scene, laughed at Marek, and left before Orin entered. Mara disappeared. Her father remained. The flower seller from my wrong-fountain mistake appeared near the front and did not recognize me until I came onstage later. Edda sat for exactly one scene. Then left because something in the kitchen required her.

Coln stood at the rear for most of it. He did not laugh. At anything. Possibly principle. Teren called Shopkeeper. Iven appeared beside me.

"You tired?"

"No."

He looked at me.

"Yes."

"Fine."

"Don't make it slow because you're tired."

"I won't."

"Don't make it fast because I said that."

"I hate you."

"Good."

We took places. The audience was larger now than during Sword. Different people. That mattered immediately. I entered correctly. Good. Iven began.

"Master, before you become unreasonable, I have an explanation."

I answered.

"You've had eleven days to improve it."

Nothing. Not one laugh. I had expected something. Not the East Market laugh. Something. Nothing. I continued. Iven said, "You wound me."

"No. Then you would owe me for the floor."

One person laughed. Late. I almost waited for it. Did not. Better. The scene moved. This audience did not care about debt yet. Or me. Fine. Iven's uncle line came.

"Your uncle has become very expensive for a man I have never met."

Two laughs. Small. The children liked that one for some reason. Then Iven changed.

"You're not going to follow me."

Old opening. Danger. I said, "No." The room waited. I did not.

"You're going to come back."

Iven blinked. Not written.

"Why?"

"Because I have your coat."

He looked down. He was wearing a coat. That was luck. Or observation. He grabbed the lapels.

"When did you take my coat?"

"I didn't."

The audience laughed. Iven stared.

"Then how do you have it?"

"I don't. But now you're checking."

Bigger. Iven laughed. I did too. Fuck. Not Sword. Fine. Maybe. He said, "This is my coat."

"Then pay me while you're here."

The children laughed again. Different scene now. Teren did not stop us. We continued. The old almost-standing exchange never happened. Neither did consultation. I noticed. I missed them. That was strange. The scene worked anyway. Not as well as East Market. Different. Near the end, Iven deliberately backed toward the chalked bad board. I saw it. He did not.

"I wouldn't."

He stopped.

"What?"

"Another step and the floor charges interest."

He looked down. Saw the X. The local crew laughed hardest. Of course. Iven moved.

"Even your building is extorting me."

"Not mine."

That got almost nothing. Fine. We finished. No big ending. No callback. Teren said, "Good. Again from uncle." The audience stayed. We went back. Second time was worse. Of course. I tried to reproduce the coat thing. Not exactly. Close enough. Iven did not wear the moment the same way. The laugh was smaller. Then I tried to improve it. Bad. Teren stopped us.

"No."

"I know."

"Good."

Iven said, "Ghost."

"Fuck you."

The front children laughed. Teren pointed at them.

"Don't encourage him."

They laughed more. We reset. Third time, I let the coat go. The scene was fine. Not magical. Fine. Teren moved on. That was all. Backstage, Iven said, "First was better."

"Yes."

"Second bad."

"Yes."

"Third fine."

"Yes."

He looked disappointed.

"What?"

"You agreed too fast."

"I am tired."

"Ah."

He left. I sat on a crate. Davin immediately said, "Need crate." I stood. He took it. Of course. Later, the long iron-cornered box finally moved. Not opened. Moved. Two local workers and Davin carried it through the corridor toward the rear storage room. Coln saw.

"No."

Davin did not stop.

"Not there."

Davin kept moving. Coln followed.

"That room is not included."

Davin stopped. Slowly. He looked at Teren. Teren was in the house. Davin shouted, "Teren." Teren shouted back, "What?"

"Box."

"What about it?"

"Room."

"What room?"

Coln said, "Not included." Teren closed his eyes from twenty yards away. The audience watched. This was apparently part of the entertainment now. Teren walked backstage. Davin waited. Coln waited. The two local workers holding the box waited. One said, "Heavy." Davin said, "Yes." Teren arrived.

"Why does it need that room?"

Davin said, "Dry." Coln said, "No." Teren looked at the box. Then at Coln.

"How much?"

Coln named a number. Teren stared.

"For a room?"

"For use."

"For one box?"

"Room."

Davin said, "Dry." Teren paid. Not happily. The audience applauded. Actual applause. Teren turned toward the house.

"Fuck all of you."

More applause. Even Coln almost smiled. Almost. The box went into the room. Still unopened. Good. The open rehearsal ended without ending. People simply began leaving when Teren stopped calling scenes. Some stayed to talk. Some came backstage despite apparently not being allowed. Nessa chased two children out of the costume area. Marek disappeared. Orin found someone he knew. Iven found food. Serra was still in the house with the yellow-scarf woman. They sat several seats apart at first. Then closer. I did not watch. Much.

I went to get my bag. The apple was still inside. Bruised more. I took it. A bite was missing. I stared. Marek appeared in the doorway chewing.

"You."

"What?"

"My apple."

He looked at the apple.

"Evidence unclear."

"There is a bite."

"Could be old."

"It was whole."

"Roof apples decay quickly."

I threw the apple at him. He caught it.

"Thank you."

"That was not---"

He bit it again. I considered murder. Too tired.

"River House," he said.

"I know."

"You know where?"

"Three streets west."

"Which west?"

I stopped. Marek smiled.

"Come on."

Outside, the air had cooled. People spilled from the hall into the river street, talking about scenes that had not actually been performances. A child passed us saying, "Spoon." Marek pointed at me.

"See?"

"That was Serra."

"You are the spoon."

"I was going to be melted."

"Details."

Serra came out behind us. Alone. She heard.

"Spoon?"

Marek said, "He's famous."

"I hate both of you."

Serra looked tired. More than before. She also looked amused. Both could be true. We started toward River House. Marek ate the rest of my apple. I let him. Mostly because taking it back would require effort. Behind us, the hall remained lit. Davin and local crew were still working. Nessa and Pell were still working. Teren was still inside with Coln. Edda had not left. The day was not over because I was done. That was becoming normal. I shifted my grip on the crutches. My hands hurt.

My shoulders hurt. Tomorrow afternoon there would be a real audience. Not people wandering in and out. Not Teren stopping scenes. Not a rehearsal where a bad line could simply be started again. I should have been worried about that. I was. Marek said something to Serra that I missed. She shoved him toward the gutter. He nearly went in. I laughed. Then we kept walking.
