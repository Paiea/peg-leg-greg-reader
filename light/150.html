LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 150

THE WORK

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

River House had six rooms, one bathtub, two washbasins, three chairs that matched, and a woman at the front desk who had already decided she hated actors.

"We are not actors," Marek told her.

She looked at him.

"What are you?"

"Traveling cultural labor."

Serra said, "He's an actor." The woman handed Marek a key.

"Two."

"Two what?"

"Room."

"Which room?"

"Two."

Marek looked at the wooden tag. It said 2.

"Excellent."

I was too tired to enjoy this properly. The common room smelled like beer, wet wool, onions, and whatever had been cooked three hours earlier. Four men played cards beside the hearth. None of them looked up when we entered. A woman near the window was reading a letter. Someone laughed upstairs. The theatre people immediately scattered. Not all toward beds. Orin knew one of the card players. Of course he did. He stopped, said, "You're dead." The man looked up.

"So are you."

They shook hands. Iven asked the desk woman where food was. She pointed at a covered pot. He went there. Serra handed her key to Marek.

"Which room?"

"Three."

"You have three?"

"No. I have three."

"That is what I asked."

She stared at him. He gave the key back. She left. Not upstairs. Front door. Gone. I did not ask. Marek watched the door for a moment. Then he went upstairs. I stood in the middle of the common room with my bag. Nobody told me where I was sleeping. Fine. The desk woman looked at me.

"Name?"

"Greg."

She checked a page.

"Gregory?"

"Yes."

"Room one."

"Who else?"

"Pell."

"Anyone else?"

She checked.

"Maybe."

"What does maybe mean?"

"It means Teren paid for beds, not certainty."

"That is not how beds work."

She looked past me.

"Next."

There was no next. I went upstairs. Room one was at the end of the corridor and had two narrow beds, one window, one chair, and a washstand with a basin that leaned slightly toward the wall. Pell was not there. His bag was. Thread was. A shoe was. One sock hung over the chair. The other did not exist. I took the bed nearest the door. Not because I was selfish. Because the other required moving around the chair. Practical. I sat. Then I did nothing.

For perhaps a minute. Maybe five. My hands rested on my thighs. My shoulders lowered slowly. The room was quiet enough that I could hear someone downstairs arguing about cards. I took off my coat. Boot. Right boot. One. Efficient. Then trousers enough to deal with the wrapping on my left leg. The skin was warm. More irritated than morning. Not damaged. A little red where the wrapping had shifted during the day. I cleaned it. Dried it. Waited. Rewrapped. No drama. No revelation.

I was simply tired enough that tying cloth neatly took two attempts. The first looked wrong. I stared at it. Then almost redid it. No. It was secure. Comfortable. Fine. I left it. That felt more difficult than it should have. Someone knocked.

"Occupied."

The door opened. Marek looked in.

"That isn't what occupied means."

"I am half naked."

He looked down.

"Yes."

"Leave."

"Food."

"I ate."

"When?"

I thought.

"Bread."

"That's not food."

"It is literally food."

"Downstairs."

He left the door open. I threw the washcloth. Missed. It landed in the corridor.

"Fuck."

I had to get up. By the time I came downstairs, Pell had returned. He was carrying the red dress. Or part of it. Nessa followed carrying the rest. They did not appear to be staying at River House so much as colonizing it. Rinna had taken one end of the common table. Fabric covered half of it.

The card players had moved their game six inches and were pretending this had nothing to do with them. Orin was now playing.

"Thought you were dead," the man across from him said.

"Still winning."

"You owe me twelve."

"Dead men don't pay."

"That isn't the rule."

"It is where I come from."

I sat with Iven. He had found stew. Marek had found beer. Iven had both.

"Where?"

He pointed. I got stew. It was mostly potato. Good. I ate. Marek sat opposite me.

"Tomorrow."

I looked up.

"What?"

"Show."

"I know."

"You look worried."

"I am eating."

"You can do both."

"Apparently."

He drank. I said, "How much do we actually do this?"

"Eat?"

"Shows."

He thought.

"Depends."

Everyone in theatre answered everything with depends.

"On what?"

"Town. Money. Weather. Who's sick. Who hates Teren. Who Teren hates. Whether the hall is standing. Whether anyone comes."

Iven said, "Sometimes once."

"Once a day?"

"Once in a town."

Marek said, "Sometimes twice a day for six days." I stopped eating.

"Six?"

"Maybe."

"That is twelve shows."

"Yes."

"The same show?"

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

Iven said, "Sometimes they pay for another." Marek said, "Sometimes someone dies." Iven looked at him.

"In the play."

"Usually."

I said, "When do you rehearse?" They both looked at me. Iven shrugged.

"When we can."

"That cannot be the system."

Marek leaned back.

"It is a very good system."

"No."

"Sometimes we get a week."

Iven said, "Luxury."

"Sometimes three days."

"Normal."

"Sometimes Teren says, 'You're the priest now.'"

I stared. Marek pointed at me.

"Shopkeeper."

"That was different."

"No."

"I had fourteen lines."

"You had fourteen lines because Meren wasn't there."

"Yes."

"Exactly."

Iven tore bread.

"Once I learned a duke during intermission."

I looked at him.

"During the show?"

"Yes."

"How many lines?"

"Thirty-something."

"That is not possible."

"I was bad."

Marek laughed. Iven said, "Very bad."

"What happened?"

"Second half."

"That is not an answer."

"That is what happened."

Marek leaned forward.

"That's the work."

I waited. He did not turn it into wisdom. Good. Iven said, "If you know the shape, you can fill."

"The shape?"

"Scene."

Marek said, "Who wants what. Who enters. Who leaves. Which line somebody actually needs." Iven said, "Don't invent too much." Marek said, "Unless they deserve it."

"Who is they?"

"Everyone."

I ate stew. This was horrifying. Also--- I understood why nobody had treated Shopkeeper as remarkable. They had not discovered I could act. They had needed a Shopkeeper. I had been nearby. That was less flattering. Better.

"Do you get paid every show?"

"Yes," Iven said.

Marek lifted his beer.

"Which is why tomorrow matters."

"Because money."

"Because money."

Iven raised his cup too.

"Money."

I had water. I raised it.

"Money."

Marek looked offended.

"That is water."

"I know."

"Tomorrow night, not water."

"Why?"

He stared.

"Money."

Iven nodded.

"If both shows go."

Marek said, "Both shows go."

"If nobody dies."

"In the play."

"Preferably."

I smiled. There it was. Work. Not rehearsal. Not learning. Tomorrow afternoon, we did the show and got paid. Then we did it again. Then apparently people drank. Maybe a lot. That seemed reasonable. Across the room, Nessa said, "No." Pell said, "It will hold."

"No."

"It held today."

"Today nobody wore it."

Rinna said, "She wore it."

"For six minutes."

"Still wore."

Nessa pulled the red fabric toward her. A card player lifted his beer before the dress swept through where it had been. Nobody apologized. The front door opened. Teren came in. Everyone noticed. Not immediately. A few seconds. Then Marek lifted his cup.

"Money?"

Teren looked exhausted.

"Yes."

Marek stood. Actually stood.

"Money."

Several people cheered. Not the inn people. Mostly theatre. Teren held up one hand.

"Tomorrow."

Marek sat.

"Cruel."

"Two shows. Afternoon and night. Gate terms are done."

"Good terms?" Orin asked from cards.

"No."

"Bad?"

"Done."

That was apparently enough. Teren crossed to the pot. The desk woman stopped him.

"That costs."

"I paid food."

"You paid rooms."

"I paid food."

"Not here."

Teren stared. Then took out money. She took it. He got stew. Nobody rescued him. I said, "What about Meren?" Teren ate one spoonful before answering.

"No Meren."

"So I do Shopkeeper both?"

"Yes."

"Tomorrow night too."

"Yes."

"Both Sword."

"Yes."

"That is four---"

"Two shows."

"I know, but four roles."

"Two roles twice."

"Yes."

Teren ate. I waited. He looked up.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

Three seconds. He went back to eating. Marek pointed at him.

"See?"

"What?"

"Work."

Teren said without looking up, "If you're explaining work, stop." Marek stopped. For nearly a minute. Then he said, "After tomorrow night---" Teren pointed his spoon. Marek drank. Fine. I finished stew. People kept arriving. Davin came late and ate standing. He had grease on one sleeve.

A local man came with him, said something about a wheel, ate bread, and left again. Serra did not return. Nessa and Pell continued sewing. Rinna disappeared upstairs with two dresses and came back with neither. Orin lost money at cards. He claimed otherwise. Iven fell asleep in his chair. Not metaphorically. His spoon was still in his hand. The desk woman took the bowl away before he could drop it. Apparently she hated actors selectively. I lasted another half hour. Maybe.

Marek began telling a story about a show where a horse entered through the audience. I heard the beginning. Then the horse became two horses. Then I stopped believing him. Then I stopped caring. I went upstairs. Pell came in much later. I woke when the door opened. He moved quietly. Not carefully. There was a difference. He put something on the chair. Undressed. Lay down. I said, "Red dress?" He stopped.

"What?"

"Done?"

"No."

"Morning?"

"No."

"No?"

"No."

"What happens?"

"Blue."

"Oh."

He got into bed.

"Good night."

"Good night."

A moment.

"Pell."

"What?"

"How often do you actually finish costumes before shows?"

Silence.

Then:

"Yes."

"That is not---"

He was already breathing differently. Fine. I slept. Morning arrived because someone pounded on the door.

"Up."

I opened my eyes. Pell was gone. His bed looked as though nobody had slept in it. The pounding came again.

"Greg."

"Teren?"

"Food. Hall in forty."

I sat up.

"Forty?"

Teren had already moved to the next door.

"Up!"

Someone farther down shouted, "Fuck you!" Teren shouted back, "Paid today!" A cheer came through the wall. I laughed. Then stood. My body had opinions. Shoulders stiff. Hands better. Right hip tight. Left leg fine after sleep. I washed. Wrapped. Dressed. The sock on the chair was still there. Not mine. I left it. Downstairs, breakfast was bread, eggs, and chaos. Serra was there. I had no idea when she had returned. She sat with Rinna, drinking something hot. Marek had one eye open. Iven had neither.

Orin was already gone. Nessa was not there. Pell was not there. Davin was not there. Teren stood by the door with a list.

"Eat."

"I am."

"Faster."

"I just sat down."

"Then you have nowhere to go."

He moved on. Serra looked at me.

"You're Shopkeeper twice."

"Yes."

"You know?"

"Yes."

"Meren's not coming."

"I know."

"Good."

She drank. I waited. She did not explain why she was telling me something I knew. Maybe checking. Maybe tired.

"Are you doing Princess the same as last night?"

Her eyes narrowed. I smiled.

"Joke."

"Bad."

"Good."

She almost smiled. Then Rinna said something quietly to her. Serra turned. Conversation over. I ate. At the hall, there was no rehearsal. That was the first thing I noticed. People worked. Benches were set. Lamps checked. Floor swept. Costumes moved. Davin and two local workers brought the long box out of the dry room and carried it toward backstage. I watched. Still closed. Fine. Nessa was in the green room. Red dress across her lap. Still sewing.

"You said blue."

She did not look up.

"Go away."

"Right."

I went away. Teren was onstage.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"Shopkeeper entrance."

I went to the wing.

"Not run. Show me."

I positioned. He looked.

"Crutch."

I moved it.

"Other."

I moved the other.

"Fine."

"What about Sword?"

"Same."

"Spoon?"

He looked at me.

"What?"

"Serra's line."

"Oh. If she says it."

"So keep?"

"If she says it."

"What do I---"

He was gone. That was all the rehearsal I got. Iven passed carrying his debtor coat.

"Have my coat?"

"No."

"Good."

"Are you going to say it?"

"What?"

"The line."

"What line?"

I stared. He smiled. Then left. Fuck him. The hall filled slowly. This time people paid. That changed everything. Not the room. Us. Marek came backstage with two cups. He handed one to Orin.

"After."

Orin sniffed it.

"Now."

"After."

"Coward."

They drank anyway. Nessa slapped the cup out of Marek's hand when he came near the costumes.

"After."

"Everyone is obsessed with after."

"Because before you are useful."

Marek looked at me.

"See?"

I did. The show was not a test. It was not the culmination of rehearsal. It was the thing people had paid for. Everything else existed to get us here. And now we had to do it. Teren appeared backstage.

"Doors closed in five."

Someone said, "Already?"

"Yes."

"Where's Serra?"

"Here," Serra said from behind him.

"Where's Iven?"

"Here."

"Orin?"

"Dead."

"Here," Orin said.

"Nessa?"

"Busy."

"Pell?"

"Busy."

"Davin?"

No answer. Teren said, "Fine." That apparently meant Davin was where he needed to be. He looked at me.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"Don't wait for yesterday."

I frowned.

"That is almost advice."

"Don't get used to it."

He left. The house noise changed. People settling. Talking. Benches scraping. A cough. A child being told to sit. Someone calling to someone across the room. Then the front doors shut. Not silence. Different noise. Contained. Marek rolled his shoulders. Iven adjusted his coat. Serra stood with one hand on the Sword's scabbard. Nobody looked ceremonial. Nobody gave a speech. Nessa still had a needle in her mouth. Pell ran past carrying a shoe. A local worker swore because one lamp smoked. Teren shouted, "Places." People moved. So did I.

My Shopkeeper scene was later. Sword first. I went to the wall opening. Crutches positioned. Hands ready. I could not see the stage. Good. The opening scene began. No stopping. A line came wrong. Nobody stopped. Someone entered early. Nobody stopped. The audience laughed at something I had never thought was funny. Nobody stopped. The show kept going. I listened. My cue was coming. My heart was beating too fast. That was fine. Apparently that was work too. Serra's voice reached me through the wall. Different again. Of course.

I smiled. Then listened.
