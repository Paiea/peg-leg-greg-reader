LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 151

THE SHOW

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Then listened. Serra's line came. Not the line I expected. Close enough that I knew where we were. Different enough that I nearly answered the old one. I waited. One beat. Not for laughter. For her. She said, "And if I decide you're more trouble than you're worth?" My cue.

"You already stole me. Complaining now seems dishonest."

A laugh came from somewhere near the middle. Small. I let it go. Serra shifted. Fabric brushed wood.

"You call this stealing?"

"I was on a wall."

"You were unattended."

"I am a legendary weapon, not a pie."

That got more. I had not planned pie. Good. I heard Marek move somewhere beyond the opening. Too early. He was not supposed to enter yet. Serra heard him too.

"Your Majesty," she said.

That was not her line. Marek answered immediately.

"My dear."

He was in. Fine. Apparently the King was here now. The scene was supposed to give Serra another exchange with Sword before he entered. That exchange contained the information that she intended to use me against him. We needed that. I knew we needed that.

I also had no idea how to put it back without announcing that we had missed it. Serra did. She said to Marek, "I was just telling your sword what I intend to do with him." Marek said, "My sword?" There. Fixed. No pause. No panic. The missing information was back. I almost admired it enough to miss my next line. Almost.

"I object to the possessive."

Marek said, "You object to everything."

"Not everything."

"What don't you object to?"

I had nothing. Serra said, "Being stolen."

"I objected immediately."

The audience laughed. Marek crossed closer. I could hear his shoes.

"Give him back."

Serra said, "No." That was supposed to be later. Fine. Marek said, "You cannot simply take a royal weapon."

"I did."

"You cannot keep it."

"I am."

"You see what I endure?" Marek asked me.

"Yes."

I waited half a breath.

"You."

A bigger laugh. Marek made a wounded sound. The room opened. I felt it. Did not chase it. Serra continued. We reached the return threat differently. Not "Perhaps I should return you." Instead she said, "He seems to want you." Marek said, "Naturally." I said, "That is not what I heard." The laugh was smaller. Fine. Serra said, "Would you prefer I melt you?" There it was. Not Marek. Her. Different again. I said, "Into what?" She paused. I knew what she could say. Spoon.

The audience from last night might remember. Some of them were probably here. I waited for her answer. Serra said, "A chamber pot." The room broke. I did too. Not aloud. Almost. Marek said, "That is my sword." Serra said, "Then you may empty him." More laughter. I had no line. None. Three possible answers arrived. All of them wanted the laugh. I discarded them.

"Put me back on the wall."

That was enough. Not huge. Enough. We moved. The scene survived. Later, I missed a cue anyway. Not because I was watching Serra. Because someone backstage dropped something metal and my attention went there. Serra covered without looking toward me.

"Have you nothing to say?"

"Yes."

I spoke the line. Late. The audience never knew. Maybe. Then the scene was over. Serra came through the wing breathing hard. I said, "Chamber pot?" She said, "Spoon was yesterday." Then Nessa grabbed her.

"Stop."

"What?"

"Back."

Serra turned. A seam had opened beneath one arm. Not much. Enough. Nessa shoved a needle through fabric while Serra still wore the dress.

"Don't stab me."

"Then stop breathing."

"That seems excessive."

"Arm."

Serra lifted it. Marek came offstage behind us.

"Chamber pot?"

"Fuck off," Serra said.

"That was excellent."

Nessa said, "Hold this." Marek held fabric. He stopped being King immediately. I moved because someone said, "Move." A local worker passed carrying a lamp. The show continued. I could hear Orin onstage. Different scene. Different problem. He spoke a line. Silence. Then another. A woman answered. Wrong line. I knew because I had heard the scene enough. Orin answered the wrong line as though it were right. She froze. He walked toward her. Not threatening. Giving her something.

"Which is why," he said, "you sent for the physician before the letter arrived."

Her face changed. I could see them through a gap now. She understood.

"Yes," she said. "Before the letter."

Back. The scene found its road again. No one applauded the repair. They did not know it had happened. That was probably the point. I had time before Shopkeeper. Not much. I drank water. A boy from local crew ran past.

"Where's the blue jar?"

Nobody answered. He ran back.

"Blue jar?"

Pell shouted from the green room, "Broken."

"When?"

"Now."

"What do I use?"

"Brown."

"It's supposed to be poison."

"Then poisonous brown."

The boy disappeared. Iven arrived wearing the debtor coat. I looked at it. He saw me look.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face."

"Davin says that."

"Davin is right."

"Are we doing coat?"

"No."

"Good."

"Maybe."

"I hate you."

"Good."

Teren appeared between us.

"Shopkeeper. Two minutes."

"Anything changed?" I asked.

"Yes."

"What?"

"Shorten after constable."

"How?"

"Be shorter."

Then he was gone. Iven said, "Excellent direction."

"Shut up."

We waited. My hands were sweating. I wiped one on my trousers. The scene before ours went long. I knew because Iven sighed. Then someone skipped six lines and it became short. Iven looked at me.

"Go."

"That isn't my cue."

"It is now."

He entered. Fuck. I followed. Correctly enough. The audience changed when I stepped out. Not because of me. Because stage light was hotter than backstage and the house became a dark shape beyond it. Faces in the first rows. Bodies farther back. Sound. Someone coughed. A child whispered. Iven turned.

"Master, I can explain."

Not the eleven days line. Fine.

"How much money do you have?"

He stopped.

"That's not an explanation."

"It is the part I care about."

A laugh. Iven put a hand to his chest.

"You reduce friendship to coin."

"We are not friends."

"Then why have you extended me credit?"

"Professional error."

More laughter. Good. Iven paced. Different blocking. I adjusted. He said, "My uncle---"

"No."

"I haven't told you---"

"No."

"You don't even know which uncle."

"None of them."

"My mother's brother is extremely wealthy."

"Then he can afford your debt."

"He is also dead."

"Then he is late."

That landed. Bigger than I expected. I almost smiled. Did not. Iven did. Barely. Then he said, "You are a deeply unpleasant man." There. From rehearsal. I knew "Pay me" worked. The answer sat ready. Too ready. I said it anyway.

"Pay me."

The audience laughed. Good. Not as much as before. Fine. We continued. Iven did not give me the follow line from rehearsal. Instead he said, "I would, but you've taken my purse." I stared. He had a purse. Visible. I could say coat. No.

"You're holding it."

He looked down.

"So I am."

The audience laughed. He tucked it behind his back.

"Now I'm not."

"That is not how theft works."

"How would you know?"

"Because I charge for it."

Nothing. Dead. Bad line. Move. Iven moved.

"Then I will return tomorrow."

"With money."

"With dignity."

"Keep the dignity. It has no resale value."

Better. We found them again. Then the constable did not enter. His cue came. Nothing. Iven looked toward the entrance. I looked too. Bad. The audience saw. Iven said, "Well?" I said, "I was waiting for the law."

"Why?"

"I enjoy disappointment."

A laugh. Still no constable. We needed him because he broke the argument and delivered the notice that moved Iven into the next scene. I did not know the notice. Iven did. He walked toward the entrance.

"Perhaps I should fetch him."

I said, "If you find him, tell him you're under arrest."

"For what?"

I had nothing useful.

"Debt."

"That is not a crime."

"Today is improving."

The constable burst in. Late. Hat sideways.

"Stop!"

Iven turned.

"For debt?"

The constable had no idea what he meant.

"No."

The audience laughed. The constable recovered.

"For disturbing the peace."

I said, "Take him." Iven said, "Him?" The constable pointed at me.

"You."

That was wrong. Very wrong. I looked at him. He looked at me. Iven looked delighted.

"Excellent," he said.

The audience laughed. The constable realized.

"No. Him."

He pointed at Iven. Too late. I said, "Professional error." The audience laughed harder than the first time. Iven put out his hands. The constable delivered the notice. We were back. The scene ended. I got offstage. The constable actor grabbed my arm.

"Sorry."

"Why me?"

"I saw one leg."

"What?"

"I don't know."

Then he ran because he had another entrance. Iven came through laughing.

"You."

"Fuck you."

"You should have been arrested."

"For what?"

"Professional error."

I hated him. I was also laughing. Teren passed.

"Good."

"Which part?"

"Done part."

He kept going. That was apparently my review. The rest of the show happened around me. Not to me. That was different. I watched some. Missed more. Nessa repaired Serra again. Pell changed a hem while the actress wearing it stood on a box.

Davin appeared backstage with a hammer, disappeared beneath the stage, and three minutes later the loose board stopped making noise. I never saw what he did. The poisonous brown jar went onstage. A man drank from it. The audience accepted that brown was poison.

Marek forgot a name and called another character "my loyal friend" until someone supplied it. Orin came offstage furious.

"What?"

I asked.

"They laughed."

"At what?"

He looked offended.

"My death."

From the house came applause. Orin said, "Animals." Then went to change clothes. Serra passed me carrying the Sword.

"Your death was funny."

Orin shouted from the dressing room, "Fuck you." She smiled. The final act tightened. Not because anyone said so. People were tired. Lines moved faster. Entrances came closer. A scene that had dragged in rehearsal suddenly worked because nobody gave it room to drag. Then a chair broke. Marek sat. The chair folded. He vanished. The audience screamed. Then laughed. Marek, from the floor, said, "I have been betrayed." Serra said, "By furniture." He stood without the chair. The scene continued.

A local worker dragged the broken pieces off during the next entrance. No one mentioned it again. Then we were at the end. I knew because everyone backstage began gathering near the wings. Not sentimental. Ready. Final line. Pause. Applause. Real. Louder than open rehearsal. Not enormous. Enough to fill the room. We went out. I stood with crutches. Sword voice belonged nowhere now. Shopkeeper gone. Just me. People clapped. Mara's father shouted, "Roof boy!" Fuck. I bowed anyway. Then we left. The moment ended immediately.

"Water."

"Where's the red dress?"

"Don't sit there."

"Who has my shoe?"

"Move the bench."

"Gate count?"

"Later."

"Where's Teren?"

"Coln."

Of course. I drank water. My shirt stuck to my back. My hands shook slightly from work and heat. Serra sat on the floor while Nessa removed the damaged sleeve. Marek had a scrape on one palm from the chair. Iven was eating bread already. Orin was still offended by his death.

"How long?" I asked.

Iven looked at the wall clock.

"Three hours."

"Until?"

"Doors."

I stared. He tore bread.

"We do it again."

"Yes."

"Same show."

"Hopefully not."

I understood what he meant now. Not different play. Different show. Teren came backstage carrying a small leather purse and several folded slips.

"Pay."

People appeared. Fast. Faster than for notes. Marek first. Of course. Teren handed coins out by name. Not equal piles for everyone. I noticed. Did not ask. Different jobs. Different arrangements. Maybe debts. Maybe advances. Not mine.

"Gregory."

I went over. He put coins in my hand. I looked down. My first payment from the troupe. Not promised. Not theoretical. Money. For the show I had just done. Sword. Shopkeeper. One show. I counted. Teren said, "Don't."

"Why?"

"Because you're blocking everyone."

I moved. Counted elsewhere. Correct. Marek appeared over my shoulder.

"Money."

"Yes."

"Tonight, more money."

"Yes."

"Then beer."

"I am tired."

"Beer."

"I have another show."

"So do I."

"That argues against beer."

"It argues for beer after."

Serra, from the floor, said, "He's new." Marek nodded solemnly.

"True."

I put the coins away. Three hours. Eat. Fix. Drink water. Do it again. Nessa called, "Greg." I looked over. She threw something. I caught it badly against my chest. Thread.

"Pell."

"Where?"

She pointed. I took it. Apparently celebration was later. I went to find Pell.
