TEXT READER · CHAPTER 153

THE MONEY

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The first tavern was full. Marek opened the door, looked inside, and said, "No."

"You said first one."

"I was wrong."

"That was fast."

"I improve."

Iven walked into my back with the bottle.

"Why are we stopped?"

"Marek has standards."

"Since when?"

"Tonight."

Marek shut the door. A man inside shouted his name before it closed. Marek kept walking.

"You know him?" I asked.

"No."

"He knew you."

"Common problem."

The second tavern had music. Not good music. Still music. A fiddle, a drum, and a woman singing loudly enough that quality had become less important than commitment. The windows were open to the river. Light spilled across the street in yellow blocks. People stood outside with cups because there was nowhere left to sit. Marek stopped.

"Yes."

Iven said, "Too loud."

"Yes."

"That wasn't approval."

Marek went in. We followed. The room smelled like beer, hot bodies, fried onions, river damp, and smoke. Someone had moved two tables against a wall to make space near the musicians. Nobody was dancing exactly. Several people were failing to stand still. A woman near the door saw Marek.

"King!"

He bowed.

"Citizen."

"You owe me money."

He straightened.

"I have never seen you before."

"You borrowed four coppers from me three years ago."

Marek looked at her. Then at me.

"Local history."

She held out her hand. He paid her. Iven laughed so hard he had to lean against the wall. The woman bit one of the coins.

"Interest."

"No."

"Five years."

"You said three."

"Interest."

"That is not how---"

She took another coin from his hand while he argued. Then kissed his cheek.

"Good show."

She disappeared into the room. I stared. Marek wiped his cheek.

"Do not."

"I said nothing."

"Your face."

"Iven says that."

"Iven is right."

Iven was still laughing. We found a table because Orin already had one. Of course. He sat with the man from River House, two women I did not know, and a narrow man with silver rings on three fingers. Four cups and a deck of cards occupied the center. Orin pointed at us.

"Late."

"We left before you," Marek said.

"Yes."

"How?"

Orin shrugged. The man from River House moved his chair enough to make room for Iven. Nobody moved for me. I looked around. A stool stood near the wall. I got it. Marek shouted toward the bar, "Four!" I said, "Three." He looked at me.

"You are here."

"I can buy mine."

He considered.

"Five!"

"That is worse."

A server appeared carrying cups. Not because Marek had shouted. She was already coming. She put one in front of Orin, one in front of the silver-ringed man, and two at another table. Marek watched them go.

"Betrayal."

The server returned.

"What?"

"Four beers."

"Pay."

Marek pointed at me.

"No."

She looked at me. I said, "One."

"Coward," Marek said.

"I am buying one."

"That is how it starts."

The server named a price. I reached into my pocket. For the first time, I spent money I had earned by being a sword and a shopkeeper. The coin looked exactly like every other coin. Good. I gave it to her. She gave me change. Also ordinary. Better. Marek paid for his and Iven's. Orin already had one.

The server looked at the fifth empty place Marek had apparently ordered for. Marek said, "Future." She left. I drank. Beer. Not terrible. Not good. Cold enough. I had expected something. I did not know what. My first troupe money had purchased beer. There should have been a lesson. There was beer. I drank again. Orin's friend said, "Roof." I looked at him.

"No."

He grinned.

"Old man told half the street."

"Excellent."

Marek turned.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Roof?"

"Nothing."

Iven said, "He has another role."

"Fuck you."

Orin said, "Roof?" I drank. The silver-ringed man dealt cards.

"Playing?"

"No."

"Yes," Marek said.

"I said no."

"You don't know the game."

"Exactly."

"Best time."

"No."

The woman beside Orin said, "He's right."

"Which one?"

"Both."

Fine. I watched. For three hands. Then the silver-ringed man dealt me cards anyway. I looked at them.

"What am I doing?"

"Losing," Orin said.

That I understood. The game involved matching something, hiding something else, and lying. Possibly. Everyone lied constantly, including about the rules. I lost two coins. Then won one. Then lost three because Marek told me a rule that did not exist.

"That cannot be allowed."

"It happened."

"You invented it."

"Improvisation."

"No."

Orin said, "Don't use theatre words to cheat." Marek looked offended.

"You cheat."

"Professionally."

"That is worse."

"Yes."

The musicians finished a song. Half the room cheered. The singer drank from someone else's cup. Then started another before the fiddle player was ready. He joined on the second line. Nobody cared. A group from the show recognized Iven. One man pointed.

"Debtor!"

Iven raised his cup. The man shouted, "Pay him!" People around him laughed. Iven pointed at me.

"There."

Several faces turned. I became Shopkeeper for approximately two seconds. A woman near the bar shouted, "Arrest him!" The room laughed. I raised both hands.

"No law."

That got enough. Then they went back to drinking. Good. Marek leaned close.

"You could get free beer from that."

"I have money."

He stared at me.

"That is not the point."

"What is the point?"

"Free beer."

He left the table. Within five minutes he had free beer. I did not know from whom. I kept mine. The room grew louder. Not suddenly. Layer by layer. More people came in after the show. Some had been audience. Some had not. The musicians changed. The drummer left and another man sat down without discussion. The singer vanished. A boy with a wooden flute took her place for two songs. Iven joined a group near the window. Orin kept playing cards. Marek moved everywhere. He knew nobody and everyone.

At one point he was arm-wrestling a dockworker. At another he was telling three women a story with his hands. Then one of the women slapped his shoulder and walked away laughing. Then he was gone. I asked Orin, "Where did Marek go?" Orin looked around.

"No idea."

"You were looking at him."

"I stopped."

Reasonable. I played another hand. Lost. The silver-ringed man said, "You think too long."

"I am learning."

"That is why."

I looked at Orin. He shrugged.

"Sometimes."

I lost again. Fine. I stopped playing. The woman beside Orin had been quiet most of the time. She wore a green scarf around her hair and had a scar through one eyebrow. She said, "Sword." I looked at her.

"Yes?"

"You were better at night."

I waited. She drank. That was apparently the whole statement.

"Thank you."

"Shopkeeper worse."

"Thank you."

Orin laughed. She looked at him.

"You died badly."

Orin stopped laughing.

"I died correctly."

"You fell before the knife touched you."

"It was going to."

"Coward."

Orin turned to me.

"Do you see what I endure?"

I said, "Again." The woman laughed. Orin pointed at me.

"Traitor."

I was enjoying myself. That surprised me enough that I noticed. Then I stopped noticing. Someone dragged the table back two feet because dancing had become real. Nobody asked. The cards moved with it. A cup spilled. The silver-ringed man swore. A woman stepped onto a bench to sing. Not one of the musicians. Just a woman. People joined. I knew none of the words. Marek reappeared beside me.

"Stand."

"No."

"Dance."

"No."

"You have one leg."

"I am aware."

"Excellent excuse."

"For not dancing."

"For dancing badly."

He grabbed my wrist. I did not move. He pulled. Still no.

"Marek."

"Gregory."

"No."

He let go immediately.

"Fine."

Then danced badly beside me. Not mockingly. Just badly. He had both legs and apparently no plan for either. I laughed. A woman joined him. Then another man. Then Iven. Iven was worse. That helped. I stayed on the stool. My shoulders hurt less now that the crutches leaned against the wall. My hands wrapped around the cup. Someone bumped my right foot. Apologized. Someone else stepped on Marek. Did not apologize. The song sped up. Marek nearly fell. He recovered by grabbing Iven. Iven nearly fell. They both shouted.

The room cheered for reasons unrelated to them. I drank. A second beer appeared. I had not ordered it. I looked around. The server pointed toward the bar. The constable actor from the show raised his cup. I raised mine. Free beer. Marek had been right. Terrible. I drank that one too. Slower. The constable actor came over later.

"Sorry again."

"For arresting me?"

"Trying."

"You got it right tonight."

"Boring."

"Yes."

He leaned on the table.

"My brother saw afternoon. Told everyone I arrested the one-legged shopkeeper."

"You did."

"I know."

"He made it sound intentional?"

"Yes."

"Better story."

He nodded. Then someone called him. He left. No name. I realized I still did not know his name. I could ask later. Or not. The theatre had many people I knew by mistakes. That seemed normal now. The front door opened. Teren came in. The room noticed less than I expected. Our table noticed. Orin lifted his cup.

"Money?"

Teren looked at him.

"You already got paid."

"More?"

"No."

Orin lowered the cup. Teren went to the bar. He did not sit with us. He spoke to the tavern keeper. Then a man I did not know joined him. They talked. Not happily. Not angrily. Business. Marek saw.

"Tomorrow."

"What?"

He pointed.

"Booking."

"How do you know?"

"I don't."

"Then why---"

"Could be debt."

"Whose?"

"Yes."

I stopped asking. Teren eventually got a drink. One. He stood at the end of the bar with it. Nobody dragged him into the party. Maybe because he would refuse. Maybe because they knew better. After a while, Rinna came in. Alone.

She looked around, found Orin, said something in his ear, took his drink, finished it, and left. Orin stared after her.

"What was that?"

The green-scarf woman asked.

"Work."

"What work?"

"Not mine."

He dealt. I liked that answer. Davin arrived perhaps an hour later. Grease on both sleeves now. He came through the door, found us, and sat without removing his coat. Marek put a beer in front of him. Davin drank half.

"Box?" I asked.

He looked at me. I regretted it.

"Dry."

"Good."

He drank the rest. Marek said, "Stay."

"No."

"You just arrived."

"Yes."

"Where are you going?"

"Sleep."

"Coward."

Davin stood.

"Wheel at seven."

"What wheel?"

Davin left. Marek looked at me.

"Local history."

"You don't know either."

"No."

Good. No Serra. I noticed because I was not looking for her. That was probably a lie. I saw yellow at the door once and looked. Different woman. Fine. Lyssa came into my head unexpectedly when a couple near the wall began arguing. Not because they resembled us. They did not.

The woman was short and furious. The man kept eating while she spoke. I thought Lyssa would have hated Marek. Then I thought no, she might like him for twenty minutes. Then hate him. Then maybe like him again. I smiled into my cup. Marek saw.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Woman?"

"Yes."

He looked around.

"Which?"

"Not here."

He understood.

"Oh."

That was all. No teasing. Interesting. Then he stole one of my remaining coins. I slapped his hand.

"Fuck you."

"There he is."

I took the coin back. The music changed again. Slower. People paired off. Some left. Some moved closer. Iven was talking to a man by the window with both hands, serious now. Orin's River House friend had disappeared. The green-scarf woman leaned against Orin's shoulder while still beating him at cards. The silver-ringed man had acquired another deck somehow. Teren was gone. I had not seen him leave. Marek vanished again. This time with the woman who had slapped his shoulder earlier. Maybe.

I saw them go toward the back. Could have been anything. Not mine. I bought food. Fried potatoes. Too salty. Perfect. I paid with show money. That felt better than beer. I ate half. Gave the rest to Iven when he returned.

"Why?"

"I can't finish."

He took them. No ceremony.

"Tomorrow," he said.

"What?"

"Leave after noon."

"For Carrow?"

"No."

I waited. He ate.

"Where?"

"North."

"How far?"

"Day."

"Show?"

"Maybe."

"Maybe?"

"Teren's talking."

"Of course."

Iven ate another potato.

"Could be two nights. Could be nothing."

"When will we know?"

"Before we leave."

"That is not enough time."

"For what?"

I had no answer. Packing. Knowing. Preparing. Apparently none required much.

"What about tomorrow morning?"

"Sleep."

"That I can do."

"Then you're ready."

He left with the potatoes. There. Tomorrow. Not rehearsal. Maybe travel. Maybe another town. Maybe another show. I did not know. The uncertainty bothered me less than it should have. Or maybe the beer helped. I stood carefully. Two beers. Not drunk. Different. My head felt warm. My body was still tired beneath it. My hands had stopped hurting because I was not using them. That would change as soon as I picked up the crutches. I did. Yes. Still tired. Marek returned. His hair was worse.

"River House."

"You're leaving?"

"Eventually."

"You look terrible."

"Successful."

"Different word."

"Same profession."

He had lost money. I knew because he checked his pocket, frowned, checked another, then smiled as though this had been expected.

"How much did you spend?"

"No."

"How much?"

"No."

"Do you know?"

He thought.

"No."

I laughed. He pointed at me.

"New man laughs."

"Old man loses money."

"I am twenty-four."

"Ancient."

"Cruel."

We found Iven near the door. Orin refused to leave.

"Winning," he said.

The green-scarf woman said, "Losing."

"Strategically."

We left him. Outside, the river air hit cold. Good. The street had emptied some. Not much. People still moved between taverns. A cart rattled over stones. Somewhere upriver, somebody sang badly without accompaniment. Iven walked ahead. Marek walked beside me for half a block, then stopped to talk to a man sitting on a windowsill. I kept going. He caught up.

"Who was that?" I asked.

"No idea."

"You stopped."

"He knew me."

"Did you know him?"

"Now."

That might have been true. River House appeared after one wrong turn. Marek blamed Iven. Iven had not been with us for the wrong turn. He had gone the correct way. We found him at the front door.

"Idiots."

"Traitor," Marek said.

Inside, the desk woman looked at us.

"No singing."

"We are not singing," Marek said.

"No fighting."

"We are friends."

She looked at me. I said, "Probably."

"No breaking furniture."

Marek looked offended.

"That was the hall."

Her eyes narrowed. We went upstairs. Room one was dark. Pell was asleep. Or unconscious. Hard to tell. I moved quietly. Not carefully. Different. My purse went beneath my folded trousers. Then I took it back out. Counted what remained. Two show payments. Minus beer. Minus cards. Minus food. Still most of it. Good. I put it away. I washed my face. Cleaned up enough. Checked my leg. Fine. Too tired for anything more complicated. Pell spoke from the dark.

"Red."

I froze.

"What?"

"Dress."

"Are you awake?"

"No."

"Is it done?"

"No."

Then he snored. Fine. I lay down. The room moved slightly when I closed my eyes. Not spinning. Considering it. I opened them. Waited. Closed them again. Better. Downstairs, someone laughed. A door shut. Footsteps crossed the hall. Then more footsteps. The troupe had not all come home. Maybe some would not. Tomorrow after noon we might leave north. Maybe for another show. Maybe not. I had no idea what the town was called. That seemed like information I should possess. I could ask in the morning.

I thought about the money beneath my trousers. Not all of it. Most. Enough. I had spent some because I wanted to. Beer. Cards. Potatoes. Stupid things. Mine. I smiled in the dark. Then somebody in the next room began snoring hard enough to shake the wall. I threw a pillow at it. The wall did not care. Pell did not wake. I laughed once. Quietly. Then slept.
