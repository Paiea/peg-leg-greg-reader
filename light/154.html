LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 154

THE VISITOR

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Someone was hammering inside my skull. I opened my eyes. The hammer stopped. A second later it started again. Not my skull. Outside. Somewhere below the window, metal struck metal three times, paused, then struck twice more. Pell was already awake. That offended me.

He sat on the edge of his bed with the red dress across his knees and a needle between his teeth.

"What time is it?"

He took the needle out.

"Morning."

"Useful."

"You asked."

My mouth tasted like old beer and sleep. My shoulders had stiffened overnight. Both hands felt thick when I flexed them. Nothing terrible. Just everything reminding me it had been used yesterday. Pell bent over the dress.

"You came in loud."

"I did not."

"You argued with the wall."

"The wall was snoring."

"That was room two."

"Then room two was snoring through the wall."

Pell considered.

"Fair."

I sat up. The room moved exactly once. Then stopped. Good. Two beers. Apparently two beers had opinions. I drank from the water cup beside the bed. Warm. Excellent.

"Did you finish it?"

Pell looked down at the red dress.

"No."

"You were working on it when I went out."

"Nessa was."

"You were asleep when I came back."

"Yes."

"And now you're working on it."

"Yes."

"When do you stop?"

"When it stops being wrong."

"That sounds dangerous."

He put the needle back between his teeth. I got dressed. My residual limb was warm but normal. The wrapping had shifted slightly overnight. I redid it before standing. No problem. My hands objected to the crutches. They lost. Downstairs, River House had become a collection of consequences. Marek was facedown on a table. Not asleep. I knew because when I entered he lifted one finger.

"Water."

A pitcher sat six inches from his hand. I pushed it closer. He did not move.

"Closer."

"It is touching you."

"Cruel."

I poured a cup and put it beside his head. He turned enough to drink without sitting up.

"Thank you."

"You spent all your money?"

"No."

"How much is left?"

He closed his eyes.

"Do not attack me."

"I asked a question."

"Violence."

Iven sat by the window eating eggs. He looked fine. I disliked him for that.

"You drank more than I did."

"Yes."

"Why are you fine?"

"I am older."

"That should make it worse."

"Experience."

"No."

"Water."

"I drank water."

"More."

I got breakfast. Eggs, bread, something fried, more water. Orin was absent. Serra was absent. Teren was absent. Rinna came through the front door while I ate, carrying a small paper packet and three lengths of cord. She looked as though she had been awake for hours.

"Morning," I said.

She looked at me.

"Is it?"

Then she went upstairs. Marek lifted his head.

"Rinna."

She did not turn.

"Rinna."

Nothing.

"Rinna, I require medical aid."

From upstairs:

"Die quietly."

Marek put his face back on the table. Iven ate. The hammering outside stopped. Then Davin came in. So that explained nothing. He had grease on one cheek and a wooden wedge in his hand.

"Wheel?" I asked.

"Yes."

"Fixed?"

"No."

He took bread.

"Broken?"

"No."

I waited. He ate.

"Then what is wrong with it?"

"Wrong wheel."

I stared.

"You got up at seven for the wrong wheel?"

"No."

He left. Marek said into the table, "Davin conversations are very satisfying." I agreed. Teren arrived twenty minutes later with Serra. Not together exactly. They entered through the same door. Serra had her hair tied back and looked tired but clean. Teren looked like he had slept in a chair while negotiating with it. Neither explained where they had been. Good. Teren put a folded paper on the breakfast table.

"North after noon."

Iven kept eating. Marek raised his head.

"Show?"

"Tomorrow night."

"Money?"

"If we perform."

"That sounds conditional."

"It is."

"Why?"

"Because they have not seen the hall yet."

"Who?"

"We."

Marek frowned. Teren drank from the pitcher.

"The booking is offered. Hall is supposedly usable. We arrive tonight, look at it, perform tomorrow if it is not a barn."

"Barns pay," Iven said.

"Not this rate."

Marek sat upright. That appeared to hurt him.

"How much?"

Teren told him. Not me. Quiet enough that I missed it. Marek's face improved.

"Good hall."

"We haven't seen it."

"Excellent hall."

Teren ignored him.

"One day north. Two nights likely. Maybe three if tomorrow sells. We leave at one."

There. A job. Maybe. No rehearsal. No speech. I ate another piece of bread. Teren pointed at me.

"Gregory."

"Yes?"

"You coming?"

I looked at him. That was somehow still a question.

"Yes."

"Good."

He went upstairs. Serra took the chair opposite me. I said, "Morning."

"Is it?"

"Rinna said that."

"Rinna is wise."

She stole half my bread. Apparently this was universal. I did not ask where she had been. She did not tell me. Marek looked between us. Then thought better of whatever he was about to say. Progress for everyone. Packing began at ten. Not because anyone announced it. Things simply started leaving River House. Costume trunks went first.

Rinna and Pell carried one between them. I moved toward the other end. Rinna said, "No." I stopped. She pointed with her chin.

"Door."

I opened the door. Useful. The next trunk came. I held the door. Then another. I was excellent at doors. Outside, two wagons waited. One had a wheel removed. Davin sat beside it with three wheels. Three. I stared. He noticed.

"Don't."

"I said nothing."

"Your face."

Everyone said that now. He selected one wheel, rolled it upright, looked at the hub, and rejected it.

"What was wrong with the first wheel?"

"Nothing."

"Then why---"

"Other wagon."

I looked at the second wagon. It had four wheels. I decided not every wheel needed to become mine. Davin glanced up.

"Good."

I hated that he knew. The long iron-cornered box emerged from the hall later, not River House. Two local workers and Davin brought it. Still closed. Still upright. Still apparently more important than its contents. They loaded it against the inner wagon rail and tied it in place. I watched the knot. Recognized enough to know I should not touch it. Also progress. Coln stood outside the hall with Teren. They spoke briefly. No shouting. No visible murder. Teren handed him something. Coln counted it.

Marek saw Coln and immediately crossed to the far side of the wagon. I said, "Subtle."

"Learn from masters."

"Does he still hate you?"

"He has excellent taste."

"That does not answer me."

"It answered something."

Coln looked over. Marek waved. Coln did not. Serra walked past both of them carrying a basket. Neither stopped her. The yellow-scarf woman was not there. I noticed. Then stopped noticing. Mostly. By eleven, the red dress finally left River House. Not finished.

I knew because Nessa walked behind Pell while he carried it and said, "Don't fold that."

"I am not."

"You are thinking about it."

"I am carrying it."

"Like you intend to fold it."

"How does that look different?"

"It does."

They disappeared toward the hall. Marek watched.

"I am glad I am not a dress."

"You would be a terrible dress."

"Excellent legs."

"One of us has to be realistic."

He looked at mine. Then at me. I raised a crutch. He moved away. I packed my own bag. That part was easier now. Not easy. Less stupid. Shirt. Wrapping. Food. The few things I had brought. My money went into the inner pocket rather than the top of the bag. I tied my crutches to the wagon myself. Slipped hitch. Test. Release. Retie. Done. No one watched. Better. Then I put my bag in the wrong wagon. Rinna took it out.

"Other."

"Why?"

"This is costumes."

"There are boots in it."

"Costume boots."

"There is bread."

"Not ours."

I took my bag.

"Other wagon?"

"Yes."

I moved it. Veteran. At noon, Orin appeared. No coat. One shoe muddy. He carried a small wrapped parcel. Nobody asked where he had been. Marek did.

"Where were you?"

Orin looked at him.

"No."

"What happened to your coat?"

"No."

"Why is your shoe---"

"No."

"What is in the parcel?"

Orin put the parcel into his bag.

"No."

Marek turned to me.

"Local history."

Orin said, "I will kill you."

"See? History."

The green-scarf woman did not appear. Neither did his River House friend. Again, not mine. We were almost ready when the visitor arrived. I noticed him because he did not fit anything. Not rich exactly. Not poor. Not local crew. Not hall. Not inn. Not audience, as far as I remembered.

He was perhaps fifty. Maybe older. Thin gray coat despite the warmth. Dark gloves. A narrow face with one eyebrow that rose slightly higher than the other even when he was not making an expression. He carried no bag. That was the first odd thing. Travelers carried things. Workers carried things. People leaving inns carried things. He carried a walking stick he did not seem to need. He stopped across the street. Looked at the wagons. Looked at the hall. Looked at me. Not stared. Looked. Then away.

I kept tying my bag. A minute later he crossed.

"Excuse me."

I looked up. He was closer now. His coat had good stitching and bad buttons. One button was wood. The others horn.

"Yes?"

"You're the sword."

I waited.

"Sometimes."

His mouth moved slightly. Not a smile.

"You were the shopkeeper too."

"Yes."

"Twice."

"Yes."

"You changed him."

I did not know what he meant.

"Between shows."

"Oh."

He waited. I waited. The street moved around us. A cart passed. Somebody shouted from the hall. Marek laughed at something behind the wagon. The man said, "Which was closer?"

"To what?"

"The role."

I looked at him.

"I don't know."

"Good."

That irritated me.

"What is good?"

"That answer."

"Why?"

He glanced toward the hall.

"Most people would choose."

"I can choose if you want."

"No."

He looked at my crutches. Not my leg. The crutches. Then at the wagon knot. Then at my hands.

"Do you always play seated roles?"

"No."

"What else?"

"Sword."

"That is not seated."

"It is mounted."

That almost got the smile.

"Fair."

I said, "Who are you?"

"Lucan Drell."

The name meant nothing. He seemed unsurprised.

"Greg."

"I know."

That was less comfortable.

"From the show?"

"From several conversations."

"With who?"

"People."

"Useful."

This time he smiled. Small.

"There you are."

"What?"

"Nothing."

I disliked him. Not enough to leave. He asked, "How long have you been with them?"

"I am not sure I am with them."

He looked at the wagons. Then at my bag. Then at the crutches tied to the rail.

"Of course."

"That sounded like disagreement."

"It was observation."

"Those are usually worse."

He laughed once. Quietly. Marek appeared beside me. Not protectively. Curiously.

"Friend?"

"No."

Lucan looked at him.

"King."

Marek bowed.

"Sometimes."

Lucan said, "You were better at night." Marek's expression changed. Not much.

"You saw both."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I had tickets."

"That is suspicious."

"I bought them."

"Worse."

Lucan looked back at me.

"You listen more when you're tired."

I felt something tighten. Not fear. Annoyance. Maybe because it was too close to something I had been doing.

"I also miss more lines."

"Yes."

"That seems relevant."

"It is."

Marek said, "Are you hiring him?" I turned.

"What?"

Lucan's eyebrow rose.

"No."

"Then stop interviewing him."

"I wasn't."

"You're bad at it."

Lucan considered Marek.

"Possibly."

Marek leaned toward me.

"Strange man."

"He is standing here."

"I know."

Lucan did not seem offended. That made him stranger. Teren came out of the hall. Saw Lucan. Stopped. Not dramatically. Just stopped. Then continued toward us.

"Drell."

"Teren."

They knew each other. Of course. Marek looked delighted.

"Local history."

Teren ignored him.

"What do you want?"

"Nothing."

"Then you're expensive."

Lucan smiled again.

"I watched your shows."

"I heard."

"From whom?"

"Coln."

Lucan looked toward the hall.

"Still alive."

"Unfortunately."

"Still charging for air?"

"By the breath."

That was the most relaxed I had seen Teren with Coln's name involved. Not friendly. Familiar. Lucan tapped his walking stick once against the stones.

"You have the old box."

Everything near me seemed to get quieter. It did not. The street was still loud. But I heard that. Teren said, "Yes." Marek stopped smiling. Lucan looked at the wagon. The long box was partly visible behind the rail.

"Still upright."

"Davin."

"Of course."

"What do you want, Drell?"

Lucan looked at me again.

"Nothing today."

Today. I did not like that word. Teren did not either.

"You're not taking anyone."

"I didn't ask."

"Good."

Marek said, "Taking?" Nobody answered him. Excellent. Lucan said to me, "North?" I looked at Teren. Teren's face said nothing.

"I think so."

"Good road after the ridge. Bad before."

"Thank you."

"Don't thank him," Teren said.

"Why?"

"He'll think you owe him."

Lucan nodded.

"True."

I definitely disliked him now. He stepped back.

"Safe road."

Marek said, "No." Lucan looked at him.

"You don't get to arrive strangely, ask questions, know Teren, mention the box, stare at Gregory, and then leave."

Lucan considered this.

"Yes."

"That is exactly what you are doing."

"Yes."

"Who are you?"

"I told him."

"You did not tell me."

"Lucan Drell."

Marek waited.

"So?"

"So what?"

"What does that mean?"

"Usually that someone wants my attention."

Marek looked at me.

"I hate him."

"I was ahead of you."

Lucan actually laughed. Then he left. Just walked away. No carriage. No companion. No explanation. He went uphill and disappeared into the noon crowd. Marek watched him.

"Follow?"

"No," Teren said.

"I wasn't asking you."

"No," I said.

Marek sighed.

"Cowards."

Teren looked at me.

"If he speaks to you again, tell me."

"Why?"

"Because I asked."

"That is not a reason."

"It is the one you have."

He walked toward the wagon. I followed him two steps.

"Teren."

He stopped.

"Who is he?"

"Drell."

"I know his name."

"Then you're ahead."

"Is he theatre?"

"No."

"Guild?"

Teren looked at me.

"No."

That answer came fast enough that I believed it.

"What does he want?"

"If I knew, I would have told him no more specifically."

That sounded true.

"Why did you say he isn't taking anyone?"

Teren rubbed his face.

"Tired."

"That was not an answer."

"No."

He walked away. Fine. Not mine. Except apparently slightly mine. I hated that. At one, we left. Not exactly at one. One fifteen. Which counted. Marek slept almost immediately. He took the side bench, folded his arms, and disappeared. Iven sat opposite me with his hat over his eyes. Orin rode in the other wagon. Serra had somehow acquired an apple. I did not ask from where.

Pell and Nessa remained in a quiet argument about the red dress until the wagon moved, then both stopped because sewing on the road was apparently a form of suicide. Rinna counted coins into three separate cloth packets. One went into her bag. One into a costume trunk. One she gave to Pell. He did not count it. Davin drove. The correct wheel stayed on. I assumed. The town thinned. Stone became packed road. River stayed with us for a while, then bent away. North. The road climbed.

Lucan had said bad before the ridge. He was right. That annoyed me too. The wagon jolted hard enough to wake Marek. He opened one eye.

"Drell."

"What?"

"Who?"

"I don't know."

"Teren knows."

"Yes."

"Box."

"Yes."

"Taking."

"Yes."

We sat with that. Then Marek said, "Beer."

"No."

"Last night."

"Yes."

"Good."

"Are you changing subjects?"

"Yes."

"Thank you."

He went back to sleep. I looked out at the road. Fields. Low stone walls. A woman leading two goats. A boy sitting on a gate. Clouds collecting farther north. The wagon hit another rut. My shoulder hit the side. I swore. Serra handed me the apple. I looked at it.

"For me?"

"No."

"Then why?"

"Hold."

She retied her hair. I held the apple. Then she took it back. Fine. Hours passed. Not interesting hours. Good hours. We stopped once for water. Davin checked the wheel. The mysterious one. Or a different one. I did not ask. At the stop, Teren spoke with a courier heading south. The courier handed him a folded note. Teren read it. Swore. Marek woke instantly.

"Money?"

"No."

"Show?"

"Maybe."

"That was already maybe."

"Now differently maybe."

"What happened?"

Teren folded the note.

"Hall has a roof leak."

I laughed. Everyone looked at me.

"What?"

Marek pointed.

"Roof boy."

"Fuck you."

Teren said, "If the stage is wet, we don't perform."

"Can it be moved?" I asked.

"Maybe."

"To where?"

"Gregory."

"Yes?"

"Don't become interested."

"I am not."

"You are visibly interested."

"My face?"

"Yes."

Everyone was right about my face. We continued north. The rain started before the ridge. Not much. Enough to make everyone look at the sky. Teren looked at it longest. A hall with a roof leak. A maybe show. A strange man named Lucan Drell who had watched me twice, knew Teren, knew the box, and apparently thought I listened better when tired. I decided not to think about him. That worked for almost four minutes. Then the wagon hit another rut. Marek woke again.

"Are we there?"

"No."

"Wake me when money."

He slept. I laughed. Then looked north. Another town. Maybe another show. Maybe a wet stage. Apparently that was tomorrow's problem.
