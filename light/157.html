TEXT READER · CHAPTER 157

THE TABLE

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

There were three tables.

One held cups.

One held a crown, two folded letters, a wooden bird, and something that
might have been a dead fish.

The third was empty.

I put the sword on the empty one.

Pell appeared from nowhere.

"No."

"Fuck."

"Other."

"Which other?"

He pointed at the table with the fish.

"Why?"

"King."

"The King keeps his sword with a dead fish?"

"Not dead."

The fish moved.

I stared.

It was carved wood attached to a spring.

"Why does it move?"

Pell took the sword from me.

"Later."

"There is a later?"

He put it beside the crown.

"Hopefully."

Then he ran.

I had been offstage perhaps thirty seconds.

My Advocate coat was still pinned wrong under one arm. The broken order
was in Orin's possession. I had no idea where my pages were. Somewhere
onstage the Chancellor was being arrested for a crime I had nearly
transformed into public service.

Marek needed the sword.

I listened.

Wrong scene.

Not yet.

Good.

I leaned against the wall.

Iven came back.

"Why are you standing?"

"I am resting."

"Shopkeeper."

"When?"

He looked toward the stage.

"Two."

"Two what?"

"Scenes."

"That is not time."

"It is theatre."

He handed me a cloth cap.

I stared at it.

"Shopkeeper doesn't wear this."

"Tonight he does."

"Why?"

"Your Advocate hair."

I touched my hair.

It was hair.

Apparently it had become legally distinct.

I put on the cap.

Iven took it off.

"Wrong way."

"It's a cap."

"Yes."

He turned it and put it back.

I hated theatre.

The sword disappeared from the table.

Marek had apparently collected it without me noticing.

Good.

I started removing the Advocate coat.

Nessa caught the sleeve before I pulled.

"Pins."

"What?"

She reached inside and removed two pins.

"Do you want blood?"

"Not particularly."

"Then stop undressing like an animal."

She took the coat.

I stood in my shirt while she passed it to Pell.

"Shopkeeper coat."

Pell looked around.

Nessa said, "Pell."

"I know."

He did not know.

Neither did I.

The Shopkeeper coat was hanging behind the curtain because somebody had
moved the costume rail away from the leaking window. I found it after
Pell found something else.

He held up a gray coat.

"That is Marek."

"No."

"It was yesterday."

"Today it's Orin."

"Why?"

"Rain."

I stared at him.

He stared back.

Neither of us knew how rain had changed ownership of the gray coat.

Nessa shouted, "Shopkeeper!"

I found mine.

By the time I had it on, the audience was laughing.

I stopped.

Listened.

Still one scene before me.

Fine.

My body had begun doing something unpleasant where every sound from the
stage felt like a missed cue.

A door shut.

Was that mine?

No.

Audience laugh.

Mine?

No.

Serra spoke.

Definitely not mine.

I breathed.

I had done Shopkeeper four times.

This should have been easier than Advocate.

That thought was obviously a mistake.

Iven appeared in debtor clothes.

No coat again.

"Don't."

"I haven't said anything."

"You were looking at my coat."

"You don't have one."

"Exactly."

"Why are you telling me not to ask about something you don't have?"

"Because now you're going to."

"I wasn't."

"You are now."

Fuck him.

We waited.

The hall was quieter than the eastern-town night crowd. Not silent. More
attentive. They laughed, but they did not shout over the play. Wet
weather had apparently produced people willing to sit indoors and watch
carefully.

I preferred the drunk ones.

They were less likely to notice legal errors.

Iven looked toward the wing.

"Go."

"Not yet."

"Correct."

"Why did you say go?"

"Testing."

"Fuck you."

He smiled.

Then our cue came.

We went.

Shopkeeper began well.

Not brilliantly.

Well.

Iven entered complaining about debt.

I asked how much he had.

He said less than yesterday.

I almost said, "Under whose authority?"

The words reached my teeth.

I stopped them.

The pause was too long.

Iven looked at me.

The audience looked at me.

I said, "That is an impressive way to answer without using a number."

A few laughs.

Not many.

Move.

Iven offered half.

I said, "Half is what people offer when they have all."

That was written.

I think.

He said he had expenses.

I asked whether I was one.

He said, "Increasingly."

Better.

Then I used the word jurisdiction.

I do not know why.

"Your poverty is outside my jurisdiction."

Silence.

Iven blinked.

I heard someone backstage choke.

I said, "Pay me."

That worked.

Not huge.

Enough.

Iven leaned closer.

"What is jurisdiction?"

"Expensive."

That worked better.

Fine.

Apparently the Shopkeeper had attended law school between towns.

The scene moved.

No uncle.

No aunt.

No coat.

No constable error.

Different room.

Different people.

At the end Iven tried to leave.

I said, "Where are you going?"

"To find a cheaper creditor."

"Bring him here."

"Why?"

"I have jurisdiction."

The audience laughed.

I hated myself.

Iven did too.

I could tell because he was trying not to smile.

Backstage he said, "Never again."

"Agreed."

"Jurisdiction?"

"I have been poisoned."

"By advocacy."

"Apparently."

He handed me water.

I drank.

The next scene was already moving.

Marek came offstage without the sword.

"Where is it?"

"What?"

"Sword."

"Gregory."

"No. Actual sword."

"Oh."

He looked back toward the stage.

"On throne?"

There was no throne in this hall.

There was a chair.

I looked.

The sword was not there.

Pell ran past.

"Sword?"

"Which?"

"Actual."

"Prop."

"Yes."

"Table."

"Which?"

He kept running.

I found the table.

No sword.

The wooden fish jumped.

I nearly hit it.

"Stop that."

The fish did not.

Iven appeared.

"Sword."

"I know."

"Where?"

"I don't know."

"Good."

"How is that good?"

"Now two of us know."

Serra came offstage in the red dress.

"What are you doing?"

"Looking for a sword."

She pointed.

It was under Marek's chair.

Of course.

"Why?"

Marek, from onstage, shifted his foot.

He had kicked it.

I retrieved it.

Serra took it from me.

"You need to be Sword."

"I know."

"You are holding Sword."

"That is different."

She shoved the prop into Iven's hands.

"King."

Then to me:

"Wall."

I went to the wall.

My Sword position had changed because the left entrance was open again
but the bench was still moved. The mounting board stood farther right
than in the eastern hall. My crutches went behind the flat. My body went
where it fit.

Someone strapped the decorative belt across me.

Not tightly enough.

I said, "Again."

The local worker tightened it.

"Good."

The scene before Sword ended.

I heard applause.

Then movement.

Serra entered.

Red dress.

Different room.

Different Princess.

Not because she had decided to become a new person, I thought. Because
the dress changed how she moved.

Or maybe I was making that up because I was tired.

She took me from the wall.

The belt caught.

For half a second, Sword refused to be stolen.

Serra pulled harder.

I came free.

Audience laughed.

She looked at me.

"Stubborn."

I said, "Evidence."

Wrong role.

A laugh anyway.

Serra's eyes changed.

Barely.

I knew.

I had brought Advocate onto Sword.

I corrected.

"I mean legendary."

Nothing.

Worse.

I should have left evidence.

Too late.

Serra said, "You are badly mounted."

"I was resisting theft."

"Poorly."

"I am a sword."

"That is your defense?"

"No. Apparently I need an advocate."

The audience laughed.

Fuck.

I had done it again.

Serra let the laugh pass.

Then:

"I have one. He's terrible."

Bigger.

I laughed.

Actually laughed.

Wrong.

She waited.

I stopped.

We continued.

Marek entered with the actual prop sword.

For one horrifying instant there were two swords in the scene, one of
them me and one of them sharp-looking wood.

He was not supposed to have it.

I knew because Serra looked at it.

Marek looked at it.

Then he looked at me.

"I found another."

Serra said, "Keep that one."

Marek looked at me again.

"Why?"

"This one talks."

Marek considered the prop.

He raised it to his ear.

The audience laughed.

He said, "This one has better manners."

I said, "That one has no jurisdiction."

Serra closed her eyes.

Iven made a noise backstage.

I had promised never again approximately six minutes earlier.

Marek said, "Neither do you."

Correct.

The scene continued.

The extra sword should have ruined something.

Instead Marek carried it until a servant character entered, then handed
it over without explanation.

The servant accepted it.

Gone.

Problem removed.

Later Serra gave me a line I knew.

I answered with the next line instead.

She looked at me.

I knew immediately.

I had skipped the setup.

The line I had said required her to have threatened the King.

She had not.

Audience silence.

Serra said, "I haven't threatened him yet."

Laugh.

I said, "I know you."

Bigger.

Then she threatened him.

We continued.

Backstage, she unstrapped me.

"You owe me two things."

"Bridge and threat."

"Good."

"Do I get credit for remembering?"

"No."

She left.

The rain started again during Orin's next scene.

I knew because everyone backstage looked upward at once.

Not audience.

Company.

A few drops hit the confidence bucket behind the left curtain.

Ping.

Pause.

Ping.

The roof man, who had stayed for the show, stood from his place near the
rear wall.

He watched the ceiling.

Ping.

Nothing.

Nothing.

Ping.

He sat back down.

Confidence restored.

The audience did not care.

One child turned around to look.

Bessa turned the child back by pointing at Orin.

Orin was in the middle of accusing somebody of poisoning somebody else.

He forgot the poison.

Not the accusation.

The object.

He reached toward the prop table.

No cup.

No jar.

Nothing.

I could see his hand from the wing.

He could see the empty space.

Pell whispered, "Blue."

I whispered, "Where?"

"Blue."

That was not location.

Iven looked under the table.

Nessa looked behind a trunk.

Orin continued speaking with his empty hand.

The other actor said, "You have no proof."

Orin looked at his hand.

Then at him.

"I had proof."

Audience laughed.

"Where is it?"

Orin looked toward the wing.

We all froze.

He said, "Stolen."

The other actor said, "Convenient."

"Crime often is."

They continued.

The blue poison jar was found thirty seconds later inside a boot.

Nobody knew why.

Pell blamed Marek.

Marek was onstage and therefore unavailable to defend himself.

Reasonable.

My second Advocate entrance came too soon.

Everything came too soon.

I had changed out of Sword but still had the Sword shirt under the
Advocate coat.

Nessa looked at it.

"No time."

"Good."

"No, bad. Go."

I had the pages this time.

I could not take them onstage.

I looked once.

Bridge.

From.

Seal.

Magistrate already knew.

This second entrance was shorter after the cuts. I returned to challenge
the King's attempt to dismiss the case after the Chancellor's arrest.
The Advocate had to establish that the forged order had caused actual
loss and that arresting one man did not restore the wagons or the
undefended bridge.

I knew the facts.

I knew them so hard I forgot the character.

I entered on time.

Excellent.

"Your Majesty, the soldiers were diverted FROM the bridge."

Too loud.

Too early.

That was not my first line.

Marek stared.

Audience stared.

I had walked onstage and announced a preposition.

Somewhere backstage Iven died.

Marek said, "I had gathered that."

Laugh.

I held the order.

Still broken.

"Good."

Another laugh.

I had no idea why.

My actual first line returned.

"The Chancellor is arrested, but the merchants remain without their
wool, the crown remains without its bridge, and your seal remains
compromised."

Correct.

Flat.

Completely flat.

I had delivered it like a list of roof repairs.

Marek answered.

I said the next necessary thing.

Correct.

Flat.

The audience listened.

No laughs.

That was fine.

I wanted a laugh.

That was dangerous.

I did not reach for one.

The King tried to dismiss me.

I said the Merchant Council would seek restitution.

Marek said, "From whom?"

"From the crown."

"Which is me."

"Unfortunately."

Small laugh.

Fine.

He said the treasury was empty.

I nearly said pay me.

I did not.

Shopkeeper remained backstage where he belonged.

Instead I said, "Then your Majesty has discovered the merchants'
position."

That got something.

Not much.

Enough.

Orin entered early.

He was not supposed to be in this part.

I knew because Marek's face changed.

Orin crossed the stage carrying the blue poison jar.

Why?

No idea.

He put it on the King's table.

"There."

Marek looked at it.

"So there is proof."

"Different crime."

Audience laughed.

Orin turned to leave.

I said, "Magistrate."

He stopped.

I should not have stopped him.

I had no line for him.

He waited.

I waited.

The audience waited.

I said, "The bridge."

Orin said, "Still there."

Laughter.

"Empty."

"Less good."

He left.

I had accomplished nothing.

Marek said, "Are all your legal arguments this efficient?"

"No."

"Good."

We finished.

Backstage Iven was leaning against the wall.

He looked ill.

Not Lorn ill.

Me ill.

"FROM."

"I said from."

"You entered with from."

"I remembered."

"Too much."

"I know."

"You stopped Orin."

"I know."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

"Good."

"Stop saying good."

"No."

He walked away.

I sat for perhaps forty seconds.

Then Teren pointed at me.

"Bow later."

"What?"

"You're in final."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Apparently not."

He left.

The final act compressed around fatigue.

Marek lost a line and simply looked at Serra.

She supplied the necessary question herself.

He answered it.

No joke.

No rescue visible.

Just moved.

Iven entered carrying the wrong letter, discovered it halfway across the
stage, turned it over, and announced that the message had become
private.

The audience laughed.

He delivered the information from memory and never opened it.

Pell repaired a fastening on Orin's sleeve while Orin stood behind the
curtain swearing quietly.

Nessa held the curtain because the fastening repair required two hands
and apparently the curtain had chosen that moment to become a person.

The confidence bucket caught five drops.

I counted.

Then stopped because I had a final Shopkeeper appearance that I had
forgotten existed.

It was only three lines.

Thank God.

I entered.

A character owed money.

I said, "Under whose authority?"

Silence.

I closed my eyes.

Not visibly, I hoped.

The actor said, "Yours?"

I said, "Correct."

Then, because I had finally learned something about shutting up, I moved
to the next line.

The audience laughed after I had already moved.

Better.

The last scene ended.

Applause came.

Not the roaring eastern-town night.

Warm.

Full.

Long enough.

We bowed.

Serra in red.

Marek grinning.

Orin pretending not to.

Iven breathing hard.

Me trying to remember whether Advocate bowed as Advocate or whether I
was Sword or Shopkeeper at this point.

Apparently myself was sufficient.

Bessa stood at the back.

She was smiling at the room.

Not us.

The room.

Seventy-something people who had paid to sit under her mostly repaired
roof.

That was fair.

Backstage, the first thing Teren said to me was:

"Bridge."

"I said from."

"You announced it."

"I was correcting yesterday."

"Yesterday was this morning."

That was upsetting.

"Five copper?"

He reached into the purse.

"Five copper."

"Forty-three lines."

"Thirty-one."

"Three roles."

"Same show."

"Fuck you."

He put five copper in my hand.

"Paid actor."

I looked at the coins.

Five.

Same as before.

Advocate had not increased them.

Neither had accidentally destroying evidence.

Neither had becoming a sword with jurisdiction.

Five copper.

I put them in my inner pocket.

Bessa appeared.

"Tomorrow."

Teren looked at her.

"What?"

"Tomorrow night."

Marek, from across the room:

"Money?"

Bessa shouted, "Yes."

Marek raised both arms.

Teren said, "How many?"

"Twenty-three already."

"For tomorrow?"

"I asked tonight."

"You sold them during the show?"

"I sell tickets when people are buying tickets."

Teren looked at the ceiling.

The confidence bucket sat empty.

"Same terms?"

Bessa said, "Better if we pass eighty."

"Better for whom?"

"Me."

"No."

They began.

I stopped listening.

A local woman came through the backstage door with a covered pot.

"For the players."

Rinna intercepted it.

"What?"

"Soup."

"What kind?"

"Hot."

Rinna accepted this as sufficient.

Another man brought a small jug of cider and said his wife had liked the
King.

Marek took personal credit.

The man said, "She liked the woman in red."

Marek gave the jug to Serra.

Serra drank directly from it.

"Correct," she said.

The roof man came backstage only long enough to retrieve his coat.

I asked, "Tomorrow?"

He looked upward.

"Roof?"

"Yes."

"Probably."

"That is not confidence."

He pointed at the bucket.

"That's confidence."

Then he left.

Lorn was still sick.

I learned this because Rinna returned from the bakery with his answer.

"No."

That meant he would not be Advocate tomorrow.

I stared at her.

She stared back.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

She carried the soup away.

Tomorrow.

Again.

Advocate again.

Sword again.

Shopkeeper again.

Five copper again.

Maybe.

I should have been horrified.

I was tired enough that horror required energy.

Instead I took the pear from my bag.

Still there.

I sat on a trunk.

Pell sat on the floor beside me.

He looked at the red dress across the room.

Serra was still wearing it.

"Done?" I asked.

He thought.

"Tonight."

"Good."

We shared the pear.

Around us, Teren and Bessa argued about eighty tickets, Nessa tried to
get Serra out of the dress before she spilled cider on it, Marek
attempted to claim a second bowl of soup, Iven searched for the blue
poison jar again, and somebody put the wooden fish into the wrong crate.

Tomorrow we would do it again.

Apparently that was the good news.
