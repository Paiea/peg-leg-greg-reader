TEXT READER · CHAPTER 167

THE APPLICANT

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Davin's early turned out to mean before the dog woke up.

The dog belonged to the people with the painted poles. It was asleep
beneath their wagon when Rinna kicked my boot.

"Up."

"I've heard this one."

"Then improve."

Around us, the fair road was already beginning to move. The pottery
wagon had gone. The preserved-fruit people were tying their last cover.
Somebody farther down the camp lane had a fire going and was burning
breakfast badly enough that the smoke tasted bitter.

We ate standing.

Bread.

Onion.

A little dried meat.

Marek held his piece of onion up to the gray morning.

"Still components."

"No one promised transformation," Serra said.

"Cooking exists."

"So does money."

He ate the onion.

The painted-pole people were packing their canvas. Their dog woke,
stretched, and stood on its back legs against the wagon wheel while one
of them tied a bundle overhead.

Marek pointed.

"Show-off."

The owner said, "He has breakfast."

Marek stopped pointing.

Davin had the horses ready before the rest of us had finished
swallowing. I carried water to the rear wagon, put the bucket where
Rinna had shown me yesterday, and checked the cover knot without being
asked.

It was still holding.

I did not tell anyone.

The road east collected wagons by the mile.

At first there were six of us moving in roughly the same direction. Then
twelve. Then enough that passing became an argument. Farmers drove carts
loaded with squash and apples. A wagon of barrels smelled sharply of
vinegar. A woman walked beside three geese tied together by their feet,
which seemed unfair but effective. Men with rolled carpets sat on top of
them like minor kings.

Temporary signs began appearing at forks.

DAST FAIR.

WAGON FIELD.

STOCK EAST.

TRADERS SOUTH.

One sign said ENTERTAINERS and pointed left.

Marek sat straighter.

"Finally."

Davin kept right.

Marek looked at him.

"Entertainers."

"Old sign," Davin said.

The arrow had been crossed out lower down in charcoal.

Beneath it somebody had written WEST FIELD FULL.

Marek sat back.

"Insulting."

"Accurate," Rinna said.

We had not seen Dast yet.

We saw its parking problem.

Fields on both sides of the road had been divided with rope, stakes,
painted boards, and people holding sticks. Wagons turned off in lines.
Some were waved through. Some were stopped. A man in a yellow cap
shouted at a cart carrying pumpkins until the driver shouted back that
he had been told east.

"Not this east!"

"How many easts do you have?"

"Today? Four!"

That did not help me.

The company reached a fork where three fair workers were sorting
traffic.

One pointed at us.

"Trade?"

Teren said, "Players."

The worker pointed left.

Another worker immediately shouted, "Not left!"

The first one shouted, "They moved players?"

"Yesterday!"

"Where?"

"South three!"

The first worker looked at Teren.

"South three."

"Where's that?"

"Past grain. Before stock."

"Which grain?"

The worker had already turned to a cheese wagon.

We continued.

Marek said, "Organized."

Rinna said, "Very."

Davin said nothing.

He found south three.

I would not have.

The field was beyond two grain yards, behind a row of temporary horse
pens, and marked by a board whose THREE had been painted over a TWO.
Half the field was already full.

A woman at the entrance held out her hand.

"Token."

Teren gave her Bellan's card.

She looked at it.

"Not a token."

"We're looking for Bellan."

"So is everyone."

"Players."

"I can see."

She looked at me, then at Marek's crown case, then at the Sword board.

"Two wagons?"

"Yes."

"One night?"

"Depends."

"Then one night."

Rinna said, "Cost?"

"Four copper for wagons. Water at trough. Horses separate."

"How separate?"

"Ask horses."

Rinna waited.

The woman sighed.

"Stable office."

"Can we hold without paying?"

"No."

Teren looked at Rinna.

Rinna looked at the field.

"Four."

She paid.

The woman handed Teren a flat wooden token marked S3-41.

"Keep that visible. No fires after dark. No dumping wash water in the
lane. No animals tied to the rope. If you block the grain wagons, they
will move you."

"Who?"

"Grain wagons."

That seemed sufficient.

Davin drove us to forty-one.

I saw a patch of grass beside the rope and pointed.

"That?"

"No," Davin said.

"Why?"

"Turn."

I looked.

The wagon needed room to turn.

"Right."

He put us where we belonged.

By the time the horses were unhitched, Teren and Rinna were already
leaving.

"Bellan," Teren said.

Rinna pointed at me.

"Stay."

"I was going to."

"Good."

Apparently my first job in Dast was not getting lost.

I watched the wagons.

That lasted eleven minutes.

Then Nessa handed me a bundle of cloth and said, "Hold."

I held it.

Pell disappeared beneath the rear wagon.

Davin went to the stable office.

Serra left without telling me where she was going.

Orin left in a different direction.

Marek stood in the lane wearing his good coat despite the dust.

"Where are you going?"

"To assess the field."

"You're wearing the coat."

"Exactly."

"Don't spend your copper."

He looked offended.

"I have discipline."

"Recent discipline."

"Still counts."

He walked away.

I held cloth.

The fair moved around us.

South three was not an entertainment camp.

It was overflow.

Two musicians occupied a wagon three spaces down. A family with a puppet
booth was across the lane. The painted-pole people with the dog arrived
twenty minutes after us and were assigned near the far rope. The dog
recognized nobody and seemed fine with that.

Beyond the field, the fair itself kept making noise without showing me
much of itself.

Hammers.

Animals.

Bells.

Someone testing a horn.

Someone else shouting numbers.

Wagon wheels.

A burst of cheering from somewhere west.

Then nothing.

Pell emerged.

"What are you doing?"

"Holding."

"Why?"

"Nessa."

He nodded.

That explained everything.

She returned and took the cloth.

"Good."

"What was it?"

"Clean."

"Still?"

She inspected the edge.

"Mostly."

That was apparently success.

Davin came back with stable terms.

Rinna was not there.

Teren was not there.

He told Nessa instead.

"Too much."

Nessa said, "How much?"

He told her quietly.

She made a face.

"Water?"

"Free."

"Oats?"

"No."

"Of course."

Davin looked at me.

"Rear wheel."

"What?"

"Watch."

He lifted one side of the wagon with a short jack and crawled near the
hub.

I watched.

Not the wheel.

The lane.

That took me a moment.

A grain wagon came through five minutes later, wide enough that its
driver swore at the puppet family for leaving a stool too far out.

I moved our stool before he reached us.

The driver did not notice.

That felt like a very small promotion.

Teren returned alone.

"No Bellan?" Nessa asked.

"Found his table."

"Not him?"

"Found his assistant."

"Not Bellan."

"No."

"Where's Rinna?"

"Prices."

That could mean anything.

Teren took the card from his pocket.

"Bellan is in the covered market until noon, then east judging yard,
then apparently nowhere anyone can promise."

"Useful," I said.

"Come."

I looked at the wagon.

"Davin said watch."

Davin, still under it, said, "Go."

Fine.

Teren and I went looking for a man who was apparently everywhere except
where we were.

The fair became visible by pieces.

First produce.

Long rows of apples, squash, beans, onions, dried herbs, honey, grain,
flour, cider, preserves. Then tools. Then cloth. Then animals again.
Every lane had its own traffic. Every crossing had somebody who knew
which carts were allowed through and assumed I did too.

I followed Teren.

At the first covered lane, I stepped across a painted white line.

A woman carrying a crate said, "Out."

I moved.

Teren kept walking.

"What?"

"Loading."

"How do you know?"

"She said out."

"Before that."

"I didn't."

Good.

We passed the musicians from the road.

They had a real pitch now beneath a striped awning. One played a fiddle.
The other played a small drum with one hand and collected with the
other. They were good.

Not interestingly good.

Just good.

People stopped.

A woman dropped copper without being asked.

Marek was standing ten feet away watching them.

He saw us.

"Research."

Teren said, "Move."

Marek moved.

A puppet booth farther on had children packed three rows deep. The
puppeteer was doing two voices at once while another person changed
figures behind the cloth.

I stopped.

Teren did not.

I caught up.

"You've never seen puppets?" he asked.

"Not those."

"What about them?"

"The hands."

"Later."

That was fair.

The covered market was not one building.

It was a long roofed trading hall with open sides, stone columns, raised
loading edges, and permanent stalls along the center. Harvest fair had
filled every gap.

Cheese.

Smoked meat.

Baskets.

Seed.

Tools.

A knife seller.

A woman selling painted tiles.

At the south end, a square of floor had been left open between columns.

A comic pair were using it.

No scenery.

One bench.

Two people.

A crowd on three sides.

They were arguing over a dead goose that neither of them wanted to admit
owning.

The shorter performer said, "It followed you."

The taller one said, "So did my wife. I deny both."

The crowd broke.

Teren stopped.

So did I.

The pair were better at the space than I would have been.

They knew exactly how far they could turn before one side lost the face.
They kept the bench angled. When a handcart needed through behind them,
the taller performer moved half a step without looking and the cart
passed.

Nobody applauded the movement.

Nobody noticed.

That was probably why it worked.

A woman with a ledger stood near one of the columns watching both the
act and the lane.

Teren approached her.

"Bellan?"

"Not me."

"Where?"

"Judging yard."

"I was told noon."

"It's noon."

I looked up.

There was no useful clock in the roof.

Apparently noon had happened while we watched a dead goose.

Teren showed her the card.

She read it.

"Merra house."

"Yes."

"You're late."

"We didn't have a booking."

"Then you're not late."

"Helpful."

She handed the card back.

"Bellan's at east judging. If he isn't, try the entertainment board."

"Where?"

"North arch."

"Which is?"

She pointed.

"Thank you."

"Don't thank me yet."

We found Bellan beside six pigs.

Not judging them.

Arguing about a singer.

He was a narrow man in a brown coat with fair dust on the hem and a
strip of blue cloth tied around one arm. His hair had gone mostly gray.
He held three folded schedules and one half-eaten pear.

A woman in a green dress was saying, "She has the west platform at
fourth bell."

Bellan said, "She has west platform if the grain speeches end."

"They won't."

"Then she doesn't."

"You promised."

"I scheduled."

"Same thing."

"Not at a fair."

The woman left angry.

Bellan took a bite of pear.

Teren waited.

Bellan looked at him.

"Who?"

Teren gave him the card.

Bellan read it while chewing.

"Right."

That was all.

No recognition.

No delight.

No immediate money.

"You're the Carrow company?"

"East Market."

"How many?"

"Depends on piece."

"How many mouths?"

Rinna would have liked that question.

"Twelve traveling."

Bellan looked at the wagons in the distance as if he could see them
through three buildings.

"Horses?"

"Yes."

"Room?"

"Work first."

"Good."

He unfolded one schedule.

"Covered market is full."

Teren said nothing.

"West platform is full."

Nothing.

"South yard is livestock until tomorrow afternoon."

"Then?"

"Mess."

"East?"

"Local dancing."

"Evening?"

"Paid singers."

"Tomorrow?"

"Depends what breaks."

Teren looked at the schedule.

"What do you have?"

Bellan looked at him for the first time like that was the correct
question.

"North arcade. First bell after noon. Twenty minutes."

"Guarantee?"

"No."

"Collection?"

"Allowed."

"Space fee?"

"No."

"Food?"

"No."

"Lodging?"

"No."

Teren's face did not change.

Bellan continued.

"If you draw, I can give you a second slot at fourth bell near the cider
yard."

"Can?"

"If the puppet people don't extend."

"Guarantee?"

"No."

"Anything guaranteed?"

Bellan took another bite of pear.

"Roof if it rains sideways."

Teren looked toward the covered market.

"North arcade is covered?"

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

"Depends on wind."

I liked him less than Vessa.

Teren said, "Why would I stay?"

Bellan shrugged.

"Because tomorrow has twice today's crowd."

"That's not pay."

"No."

"Merra's man said fair work."

"Merra's man had dinner."

That was good enough that Teren almost smiled.

Almost.

Bellan looked past us.

"Can you stop people?"

Teren said, "Sometimes."

"What do you do?"

"Mixed pieces."

"Music?"

"No."

"Animals?"

"No."

"Fire?"

"No."

"Good. No fire."

He looked at me.

"What does he do?"

Teren said, "Sword."

Bellan looked at my missing leg, then my crutches.

"Actual?"

"No."

"Good."

Apparently this was the second most important safety question.

Bellan folded the schedule.

"North arcade. Tomorrow. First bell after noon. Twenty minutes.
Collection. If it works, find me."

"And if it doesn't?"

"Don't."

Teren held out his hand.

Bellan looked at it.

"For what?"

"Slot."

"You have it."

"Token?"

"No."

"Schedule?"

"I wrote it."

"Where?"

Bellan tapped the paper.

Teren lowered his hand.

"Fine."

Bellan turned toward someone shouting his name.

"Wait."

Teren stopped.

Bellan pointed at me.

"Can he get up three steps?"

I said, "Probably."

Teren said, "Where?"

"North arcade."

"Ramp?"

"No."

"Then no."

Bellan looked at me again.

"Three low steps."

Teren said, "No."

I said, "I can do three steps."

Teren looked at me.

"I can."

"With board?"

"No."

"Then no."

Bellan scratched something on the schedule.

"South end of arcade. Loading slope."

Teren nodded.

"That works."

It was the first accommodation of the negotiation and it happened
because there was a loading slope.

Not because anyone cared about me.

I liked that.

We walked back toward south three.

Teren said, "Don't promise stairs."

"I didn't promise."

"You said probably."

"That's not promise."

"It is to people with schedules."

"Fine."

We found Rinna halfway back carrying a small sack of oats and looking
offended by it.

"How bad?" Teren asked.

"Bad."

"Work?"

"Tomorrow. North arcade. Twenty. Collection."

"Guarantee?"

"No."

"No food?"

"No."

"No lodging?"

"No."

Rinna stared at him.

"That's Kest with more expensive oats."

"More people."

"More people don't eat oats."

"Some pay."

"Some don't."

"Yes."

She shifted the sack.

"Second slot?"

"If first draws."

"Where?"

"Cider yard."

"Better?"

"Probably."

Rinna looked at me.

I said, "I didn't say it."

"Good."

We returned to the wagons.

Marek was gone.

Nessa was angry about dust.

Davin had finished the wheel.

Pell had acquired a paper cone of something fried and refused to say
where.

Serra was back and had a strip of red thread tied around one finger for
no reason I knew.

Orin was still absent.

The dog act was setting painted poles in the far corner of south three.

Their owner saw us.

"Got work?"

Teren said, "Tomorrow."

"Where?"

"North arcade."

The man winced.

Marek appeared behind him.

"Why?"

"Wind."

Teren looked at Bellan's schedule in his hand.

"Mostly covered."

The dog owner laughed.

"Mostly."

Marek looked delighted.

"We have a room."

"No," Teren said.

"We have an arcade."

"Mostly."

"Roof."

"Sometimes."

"Professional."

Rinna dropped the oats beside Davin.

"We are paying to stay tonight."

That quieted Marek.

"How much?"

"Enough."

Teren said, "One night."

Rinna looked at him.

"If tomorrow pays."

"If tomorrow pays."

There it was again.

Not a booking.

Not really.

A place to try.

The fair had not hired us.

It had made room for us to ask strangers for money under a roof that
apparently depended on wind.

I sat on the wagon step.

Across the field, the puppet family was hanging painted cloth. Beyond
them, the dog stood on its back legs while a child clapped.

Somewhere in the fair, musicians were already earning.

The comic pair had a crowd.

The singers had an evening guarantee.

We had tomorrow after noon.

Twenty minutes.

I reached into my coat and touched the wrapped brass clasp.

Dast had a courier row. I had seen the sign near the north arch.

I could write Lyssa tomorrow.

Maybe.

First we had to earn tomorrow.

Teren pointed at me.

"North arcade in the morning."

"Why?"

"Look at the slope."

"I thought you already did."

"I looked at Bellan."

"Different."

"Very."

I nodded.

Apparently I had applied for work and received an inspection.

That was close enough.

Behind us, somebody shouted that a wagon was in the wrong lane.

Three people shouted different answers.

Dast continued without waiting for us.
