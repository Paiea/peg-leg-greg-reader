TEXT READER · CHAPTER 173

THE LAMPLIGHTER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The next town had a sign that lied less than most actors.

**HALVER. COACHES. BEDS. HORSES. HOT FOOD.**

Below that, in smaller paint:

**PLAYERS ASK FIRST.**

Marek read it twice.

"I feel specifically attacked."

Teren said, "Good."

We came down into Halver before noon.

It sat where three roads met around a shallow river, with a stone bridge
on the west side and a newer timber bridge to the south. Most of the
town seemed built for people who intended to leave it. Inns. Stables.
Wheelwrights. Two smithies. A long coach yard with six bays and a roofed
gallery. Shops selling food that could survive pockets.

A green-glass road marker stood at the first fork. The lettering
brightened as our wagon shadow passed over it.

WEST BRIDGE: CARROW ROAD.

I stopped looking long enough that Pell walked into me.

"Move."

"Carrow."

"I can read."

"Can you?"

"Better than you."

He went around me.

Carrow road.

Not Carrow.

But the road that went there.

That was new.

Davin took the wagons toward the coach yard. Teren and Rinna went inside
the largest inn. Nessa disappeared into a lamp seller. Serra said she
needed soap and left before anyone asked why. Orin was simply gone.

Marek looked at the hot-food sign.

"I am going to become a customer."

"You have money?"

"Some."

"Personal expenditure?"

"Do not ruin this."

He left.

I stayed with Davin.

"Rear," he said.

I went to the rear wagon.

Nothing happened.

A coach came in.

I moved a bucket.

A stable boy shouted at another stable boy.

Davin crawled under the axle.

I watched the lane.

This was apparently my life now.

Five minutes later Teren came back.

"Show."

"Here?"

"Maybe."

"That's not show."

"Six o'clock. Coach gallery. Inn wants twenty-five minutes before supper
traffic."

"Pay?"

"Fifteen copper company. Two pitchers small beer. No food."

"That's bad."

Rinna came out behind him.

"It's acceptable."

"Then good."

"Don't help."

Teren pointed at the gallery.

"Shopkeeper. Widow. Short King if room holds."

"Sword?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Coaches."

That was fair.

Nessa returned carrying lamp oil and a paper twist of wicks.

She looked at the gallery.

Then at the light.

Then at Teren.

"Blue?"

Teren said, "Maybe."

I said, "I can hang it."

Nessa looked at me.

Pell appeared from nowhere.

"He can."

"Quiet."

"He did it three times."

"One time."

"Three attempts."

Nessa handed me the oil.

"Carry."

I carried.

The coach gallery was long and narrow, built over the stable offices on
one side of the yard. The open rail faced the coaches below. Behind it
ran a wall with three doors, one staircase, and hooks for travelers'
coats. Benches could be turned toward either the yard or the wall.

The innkeeper, a woman named Fera, wanted us against the far wall.

Teren wanted the stair end.

Nessa wanted neither.

"That lamp," she said.

There was a wall lamp halfway down the gallery.

Fera said, "What about it?"

"Can we use it?"

"You can light it."

"Can we hang glass on it?"

"What glass?"

Nessa showed her the smoky square.

Fera held out her hand.

Nessa did not give it to her.

"Prepared?"

"Yes."

"Heat?"

"No."

"Smoke?"

"No."

"Stain?"

"No."

Fera looked at Rinna.

"If it stains my wall, two copper."

"It won't."

"If it does."

"One."

"Two."

Rinna said, "Fine."

Fera nodded.

That was the magical safety inspection.

I had expected more.

Nessa pointed at the lamp.

"Hang it."

I did.

Wrong.

Not badly.

Just enough that she clicked her tongue.

"What?"

"Gallery rail."

I stepped back.

From where the audience would sit, the pale edge of the glass caught
daylight from the open side.

I moved it.

"Too far."

I moved it back.

"Stop touching it like it insulted you."

"It did."

Pell leaned over the rail.

"Still see the bottom."

I adjusted the hook.

The edge disappeared behind the iron lamp frame.

Nessa walked three benches down.

"Fine."

I smiled.

"Don't."

"I didn't."

"You did."

Fera said, "What does it do?"

Nessa said, "Blue."

Fera waited.

"That's all?"

"That's enough."

I looked at the wall.

The lamp was not lit.

The glass did nothing.

A coach rolled into the yard below us. Six passengers got out. One
argued immediately about a trunk.

Fera watched them.

"You're not blocking that stair."

Teren said, "We won't."

"You are."

We were.

She made us move two benches and the prop basket.

Ten minutes later three passengers came up the stair exactly where the
basket had been.

Local people knew where the future happened.

I was getting used to that.

By afternoon the gallery smelled of lamp oil, horse, beer, and wet wool.

A light rain had started. Not enough to stop coaches. Enough to make
everyone who arrived want shelter.

That helped.

Teren changed the bill.

"King first."

Marek said, "Correct."

"Short."

"Incorrect."

"Widow. Shopkeeper."

"Why King first?"

"People are arriving."

"I can hold them."

"No. You can be loud while they sit down."

Marek considered that.

"Also correct."

The blue lamp was for Widow.

I was not in Widow.

That meant I had a job.

Nessa showed me the ordinary lamp beside it.

"At the turn, Pell shutters this."

Pell touched the shutter.

"Then Rinna cues blue."

Rinna was below arguing with Fera about where the collection bowl could
go.

I said, "What do I do?"

"Nothing."

"I hung it."

"Yes."

"So I'm done."

"Until it slips."

"Does it slip?"

"If you hang it badly."

I looked at the hook.

Nessa walked away.

I looked at the hook harder.

The failed throw blank was in my pocket.

I had taken it out twice on the road that morning.

Not to do anything.

Just to look at the marks.

They were faint and irritating. A shallow line split near the middle,
curved toward the bent end, then stopped. I could not tell whether the
split mattered or whether it was where Varo had decided the piece was
already ruined.

I had tried comparing it to Hessa's diagrams in memory.

That had been useless.

Hessa drew mana.

Varo marked metal.

Nessa hid glass.

Pell used a tube.

All of them could make something happen outside the body.

Only one of those things was the problem Hessa and I had actually been
testing.

I put the blank away.

Marek was practicing nothing.

He stood at the far end of the gallery wearing half his King coat while
eating a meat pie.

"Preparation," he said.

"Food."

"Both."

"Growth."

"Fuck you."

He had bought the pie himself.

I was proud of him.

I did not tell him.

By six, the gallery had thirty people in it.

Then forty.

Some were travelers waiting for coaches. Some were staying at the inn.
Some were locals who had heard there were players. A group of carters
stood along the open rail because they did not want to pay for drinks.

Nobody had paid to see us.

Fera had.

That changed the room.

Teren stood near the stair and watched people settle.

A coach horn sounded outside.

He waited.

The coach came in.

Passengers climbed the stairs.

He waited.

Fera looked at him.

He held up one finger.

A serving girl crossed with six bowls.

Then Teren nodded to Marek.

Marek walked into the middle of the gallery and said, "I have inherited
a kingdom."

A carter said, "Sorry."

Marek looked at him.

"So am I."

The room turned.

Not all at once.

Enough.

The King piece was short.

Marek was good.

That still annoyed me sometimes.

He could take a room that was not interested and make his annoyance seem
like their fault.

Iven joined him.

They did the debt section.

A coachman came up the stairs during it.

Marek moved half a step without looking.

The coachman passed behind him.

No collision.

No pause.

No lesson.

Just work.

The piece ended.

Some people clapped.

A child near the rail asked whether Marek was really a king.

Marek said, "Temporarily."

That got more laughter than the ending.

Widow came next.

Serra entered in dark cloth.

Orin sat on a bench with his head down.

The first part of the piece was ordinary light.

Warm wall lamps.

Rain outside.

People shifting.

A cup dropped downstairs.

Then Serra said, "You left the room warm."

Pell closed the shutter on the ordinary lamp.

Not dark.

Less warm.

Rinna touched the cue mark.

I did not see mana.

I was not looking for it.

The smoky glass changed.

Barely.

The lamp behind it cooled from yellow toward a thin blue-white.

Serra's face changed with it.

Not shape.

Not color exactly.

The shadows under her eyes deepened. The dark cloth stopped looking
brown and became almost black.

Orin lifted his head.

Behind him, Pell pulled a gray cloth away from the wall.

That was all.

A gray cloth.

It revealed pale stone.

The audience went quiet.

I knew the wall was real.

I knew the blue was a prepared glass.

I knew Pell had hidden the pale stone with cloth before the show.

I knew Serra had turned her body so the colder lamp took one side of her
face.

I knew Orin had waited until the room changed before looking up.

The room knew none of that separately.

It knew the room had become colder.

It had not.

That was the strange part.

The rain was real.

The pale stone was real.

Serra's face was real.

The shadow was real.

The lamp was really bluer.

Nothing in front of them needed to be false.

The cold was.

A woman pulled her shawl tighter.

I stared at her.

Nessa hit my arm.

"Hook."

The glass had shifted.

Almost nothing.

One corner had slipped forward.

I reached up and steadied the frame.

Not the prepared line.

The frame.

The edge disappeared again.

The woman kept watching Serra.

Nobody watched me.

That mattered more than I wanted it to.

Widow ended with Serra turning away from Orin.

Pell opened the ordinary lamp shutter.

Rinna released the cue.

Warm yellow returned.

People moved again.

Someone breathed loudly.

A man near the stair said, "Fuck."

Fera smiled.

Not at us.

At the room.

Then she went downstairs because someone was shouting about a horse.

Teren pointed at me.

Shopkeeper.

I had about twelve seconds.

I had been watching the lamp.

I had not put on my coat.

"Fuck."

Pell threw it at me.

I caught one sleeve.

The other folded under.

I went out wearing it wrong.

Iven saw.

His mouth twitched.

I said, "You owe me money."

He said, "You appear to owe your tailor."

The room laughed.

I looked down.

One side of my coat was tucked under my arm.

"Separate account."

More laughter.

Good.

Then I forgot the next line.

Not all of it.

The first three words.

Iven waited.

I could hear the rain.

I could hear somebody downstairs dragging something heavy.

I said, "You have delayed payment."

Wrong line.

Right information.

Iven answered.

We continued.

The scene did not die.

I stopped trying to find the missing words.

The Shopkeeper wanted money.

Iven did not have it.

That was true enough.

Halfway through, a man at the rail said, "Take his boots."

I said, "I have standards."

The man looked at my peg.

I saw him decide whether to say something.

He did not.

Iven said, "Do you?"

I said, "No."

That got him.

He laughed.

Actually laughed.

Then had to turn it into a cough.

The audience saw that too.

We finished.

Teren cut the last planned exchange because another coach horn sounded.

We were off before the passengers came up.

Twenty-three minutes.

Close enough.

Fera paid Rinna fifteen copper.

No argument.

She also sent up the two pitchers.

Marek had already acquired a cup.

Rinna slapped his hand.

"Company."

"I am company."

"Later."

Fera looked at the blue lamp.

"Do that every time?"

Nessa said, "No."

"Why not?"

"Wrong room."

Fera looked around her own gallery.

"What makes this right?"

Nessa pointed at the open rail.

"Rain."

Fera looked at the rain.

Then at the pale stone wall.

Then at Serra.

"Huh."

She went downstairs.

I followed Nessa to the lamp.

"Was the rain important?"

"Yes."

"You didn't make the rain."

"No."

"The stone?"

"Wall."

"Serra's face?"

"Serra."

"The cloth?"

"Pell."

"The blue?"

"Glass."

"And people felt cold."

Nessa looked at me.

"Did you?"

"No."

She waited.

"Maybe."

"Then stop talking."

She took the glass down.

I said, "Nothing was fake."

Pell, carrying the gray cloth, said, "I'm fake."

Nessa said, "You're unpaid if you tear that."

He folded it.

I stood there.

Nothing was fake.

That was not exactly true.

The room had lied.

No.

We had.

No.

We had shown them things that were true and let them make the wrong
thing out of it.

Rain.

Stone.

Blue light.

A widow saying the room had been warm.

A woman tightening her shawl because she already knew rain meant cold.

The effect had not needed to create cold.

It had needed the room to remember cold.

"Greg."

Teren.

I turned.

"Bowls."

"What?"

"Take them down."

There were empty cups and two bowls on the bench.

I took them down.

That was probably healthier.

The kitchen had a flat iron plate with a small red mark worked into one
corner. A cook set a pot on it, touched the mark, and went back to
cutting onions.

The pot began steaming.

I watched for two seconds.

The cook said, "Move."

I moved.

Outside, the rain had thickened.

Davin had put canvas over the wagon loads.

The long box was under it with everything else.

Dry.

Upright.

Uninteresting.

Good.

Rinna found me near the stable.

"Two copper."

She handed me two.

"Show?"

"Rellin."

"Oh."

"Your share."

I looked at the coins.

"Now?"

"Unless you want me to keep them."

"No."

"Then now."

"How much did Marek get?"

"Ask Marek."

"Serra?"

"Greg."

"Fine."

She left.

I put the two copper away.

The failed throw blank clicked against them.

Wrong pocket again.

I moved it.

Marek came out carrying a bowl.

"Hot food."

"Did you buy that?"

"No."

"Steal it?"

"Relationship."

"With who?"

"The cook."

"What's her name?"

He looked at the bowl.

"Soup."

"Strong relationship."

He ate.

We slept in the coach yard for three copper company, wagons and horses
included, no rooms.

The rain stopped sometime after midnight.

In the morning the town smelled washed and metallic.

Davin had us moving early.

Fera sold Rinna bread.

Nessa bought another wick.

Serra returned from somewhere with soap that smelled like pine.

Orin was already at the wagon when I woke.

I did not ask.

At the west fork, the green road marker was dull in daylight.

The letters still said:

CARROW ROAD.

Teren stood beside it.

"One town if we go straight," he said.

Rinna asked, "Work?"

"Maybe."

She looked at him.

He smiled.

She did not.

"Two days?" I asked.

Davin said, "If road."

Everyone looked at him.

He looked back.

"Road."

Marek groaned.

I looked west.

Two days if road.

Maybe three.

Maybe work.

Maybe not.

Lyssa was somewhere at the other end of that direction.

Hessa too.

I touched the failed throw blank through my pocket.

I knew what I wanted to ask Hessa now.

Not how to make a room cold.

We had not made the room cold.

I wanted to know how little had to be real before the rest of it became
true to somebody watching.

That was probably not the right question.

Hessa would frown.

Good.

I had time to make it worse.
