TEXT READER · CHAPTER 184

THE RUNNER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Lyssa asked me for a favor before I had finished chewing.

That should have warned me.

"You're going east?"

"Eventually."

"Good."

She put a parcel on the table.

It was wrapped in gray cloth and tied twice with blue thread.

I looked at it.

"No."

Lyssa stopped.

"What?"

"I am not moving it."

"I want you to carry it."

"Oh."

She stared at me.

"Your life has become strange."

"Recently."

The parcel was not heavy. It was about the size of two folded shirts and
soft enough that I could wedge it under my arm.

"Where?"

"Marra's."

"I know Marra's."

"Do you?"

"Green door."

"Good."

"Not Sen's."

"Very good."

"I made that mistake once."

"You nearly made that mistake once."

"That still counts as learning."

Lyssa tore bread in half.

"Give it to Marra. Tell her the left side is marked. Do not untie it."

"Why would I untie it?"

"I know you."

"That is not an answer."

"It has worked so far."

I took the parcel.

"Anything else?"

"Yes. If Jessa is there, tell her I said the narrow one, not the wide
one."

"The narrow what?"

"She'll know."

"I don't."

"You don't need to."

This was apparently what trust looked like.

I finished breakfast with the parcel against my leg.

My right palm was still tender. The scrap cloth I had wrapped around the
crutch grip after calling the show had shifted overnight and bunched
under my hand. I retied it.

Lyssa watched.

"You should replace that."

"It works."

"It is dirty."

"Those are different questions."

"You've been spending too much time with Hessa."

I smiled before I meant to.

Lyssa noticed.

"Still pleased?"

"Yes."

"You moved paper."

"Yes."

"A very small amount."

"Yes."

"Under glass."

"Yes."

"While supervised."

"Yes."

"Good."

"Why are you doing this?"

"Because you're smiling at bread."

I stopped smiling.

Mostly.

Lyssa picked up her bag.

"I'll be west until midday. Maybe longer."

"People?"

"People."

"Blue thread?"

"Not today."

"What today?"

She kissed me.

"Work."

Then she left.

I had the parcel, my crutches, three copper on the table, a theatre call
before midday, and no reason to go near the Guild.

It seemed manageable.

That was my second warning.

Marra's was not on the way to East Market Hall.

It was only a few streets wrong.

A few streets on crutches was still a few streets.

I went north first, cut across the cloth lane, and found the green door
open.

Marra was inside with one sleeve pinned around a wooden arm and a mouth
full of pins.

I held up the parcel.

"Lyssa."

Marra pointed at a table.

I put it there.

"The left side is marked."

Marra removed two pins from her mouth.

"I know."

"Good."

"Did she tell you that?"

"Yes."

"Then why did you tell me?"

"Because she told me to."

Marra looked at me for a moment.

"Useful."

"Thank you."

"Not you."

She cut the blue thread.

I watched the knot fall loose.

Inside was dark cloth.

Not the same dark cloth from Lyssa's table, unless all dark cloth looked
like dark cloth to me, which seemed possible.

Marra unfolded one edge.

A row of tiny chalk marks ran along it.

"Jessa here?"

"No."

"I have a message."

"Then she's not here."

"I understand the concept."

Marra put two pins back in her mouth.

"She'll be at Sen's."

I stared at her.

"Red door."

Marra nodded.

"Of course."

"Tell her narrow, not wide."

"Yes."

I had delivered one parcel and somehow acquired the rest of the errand.

Sen's red door was two lanes back.

I had almost reached it when someone called my name.

I kept going.

"Greg."

I stopped.

Nessa was standing outside a shop with three coils of cord over one
shoulder and a small paper packet in her hand.

"Hall."

"I'm going."

"Now."

"I have an errand."

"So do I."

She handed me the paper packet.

I looked at it.

"No."

"What?"

"Nothing."

The packet was full of something hard and small.

"What's this?"

"Hooks."

"Where?"

"Hall."

"You're going to the hall."

"No."

"Where are you going?"

Nessa looked past me toward the next street.

"Somewhere else."

Then she left.

I now had a message for Jessa and a packet of hooks for the theatre.

Neither was heavy.

Together they were irritating.

I went to Sen's.

The red door was closed.

I knocked.

A man opened it.

"Jessa?"

"No."

"Is she here?"

"No."

"Do you know where she is?"

"Yes."

I waited.

He waited.

"Where?"

"Marra's."

I looked back toward the lane I had just crossed.

"No."

He shrugged.

"She was going there."

"When?"

"Before."

"How long before?"

He thought.

"Some."

I hated Carrow.

I went back to Marra's.

Jessa was there.

She was standing exactly where I had put Lyssa's parcel.

Marra was not.

I pointed at her.

"Narrow, not wide."

Jessa nodded.

"Good."

"Do you know what that means?"

"Yes."

"Excellent."

She looked at the packet under my arm.

"What is that?"

"Hooks."

"For what?"

"I don't know."

She smiled.

"You are useful today."

"Apparently."

I left before she could give me anything.

By the time I reached East Market Hall, my palm hurt and I was late
enough that Rinna was standing in the doorway.

She looked at me.

I looked at her.

"I have hooks."

"You're late."

"I also had cloth."

"Do you still have cloth?"

"No."

"Then I don't care."

I handed her the packet.

She opened it.

"These aren't ours."

I stared.

"Nessa gave them to me."

"These are costume hooks."

"Yes?"

"She needed curtain hooks."

"They are hooks."

Rinna looked at me.

I understood how Pell felt around Hessa.

"Where is Nessa?"

"Somewhere else."

"Helpful."

"I asked."

"Did you ask what kind of hooks?"

"No."

Rinna handed the packet back.

"Take those to costumes."

"Where are the curtain hooks?"

"With Nessa, apparently."

I carried the wrong hooks twelve paces into the building and gave them
to a woman sewing a brown coat.

She looked inside.

"Good."

"Are they?"

"Yes."

That improved nothing.

Teren was onstage.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"Third Man."

"My goat?"

"Your goat."

Finally.

"Then after, take this."

He handed me a folded note.

I did not take it.

"Where?"

"South market. Lamp oil seller beside the cooper."

"Why?"

"Tell him six small tins tomorrow, not four. Rinna will pay on
delivery."

"Why me?"

"You're going that way."

"I'm not."

"You are now."

I took the note.

"I have a show."

"Yes."

"When am I supposed to go?"

"After."

"After the show?"

"After your piece."

"There's more show after my piece."

"Correct."

"Do I come back?"

"If you want."

This was not a schedule.

It was weather.

I put the note inside my coat.

The Petition ran that afternoon.

I got my goat back.

I stood behind the side cloth, listened to Jori call the first entrance,
and waited for the line before mine.

The curtain moved smoothly.

I noticed again.

Then I stopped noticing.

Sellen said, "My neighbor has complained to half the street and all the
watch."

That was not my cue.

I waited.

Veya answered.

Sellen crossed.

Then came the line.

"My neighbor says the animal has damaged his property."

Jori pointed.

I went.

"My neighbor's goat has eaten all my patience and half my fence."

Sellen turned.

"My father sent me because the goat is innocent."

The audience laughed.

I did not add anything.

Sellen continued.

I continued.

The scene worked.

It was almost disappointing.

Afterward, Teren caught me at the side gap.

"Good."

"That's all?"

"What do you want?"

"I don't know."

"Then good."

He shoved the red doorframe toward me.

"Move."

I moved it.

The repaired foot held.

The curtain worked.

The fake petition went back into its box.

Nobody asked me about magic.

For almost an hour, I forgot I had any.

Then Pell passed carrying a painted bucket.

"Magician."

"Props."

He kept walking.

That was the entire conversation.

I liked it better.

I left for the south market after my piece.

The lamp seller beside the cooper was farther south than Teren had
implied.

Everything was farther when somebody else described it.

I had made it halfway when the cloth around my right crutch grip twisted
again.

I stopped beside a cobbler's stall and fixed it.

The cobbler watched.

"That's terrible."

"It was free."

"Still terrible."

He had a basket of leather scraps under the bench.

I looked at it.

He looked at me.

"How much for a strip?"

"What size?"

I showed him the grip.

He took my crutch before I could object, turned it once, then pulled a
narrow brown strip from the basket.

"This."

"How much?"

"One copper."

"That's too much for scrap."

"It's good scrap."

"It's in a basket on the ground."

"Then use your cloth."

I looked at the cloth.

The cloth looked back.

It was not going to improve.

"Half."

"No."

"Half and I don't tell people you charge full copper for rubbish."

"One and I wrap it."

"Half and you don't."

"One."

I paid one copper.

He wrapped it.

That changed the argument.

The leather went around the grip tight and smooth, with the seam away
from my palm. He tied it under itself and cut the end.

I put weight on the crutch.

Better.

Annoyingly better.

"Good scrap," he said.

"I dislike you."

"Walk on it."

"I don't walk on that end."

He laughed.

I left.

The lamp seller was beside the cooper.

Teren had been right about that part.

I gave him the note.

He read it.

"Six?"

"Yes."

"Tomorrow?"

"Yes."

"Not four?"

"Apparently this is important."

"It is if he wants six."

"Do you have six?"

"Tomorrow."

"Good."

He folded the note.

"Tell Teren I want the old tins back."

"Why?"

"Because they're mine."

"That seems fair."

"Tell him."

I had become a message with crutches.

On the way back, I passed the Guild street.

I did not need to go there.

I kept going.

Then a boy in a gray Guild vest came out of the side entrance and
called, "Greg?"

I stopped.

This was becoming a problem.

He held a folded paper.

"For you."

"From Hessa?"

He looked at the front.

"Yes."

I took it.

"Do I need to go in?"

"No."

"Good."

I opened it on the street.

DAY AFTER TOMORROW. AFTER MIDDAY. BRING THE QUESTION. NO TESTING BEFORE.

Under that, in smaller writing:

THIS INCLUDES PAPER.

I read it twice.

The boy was still there.

"Does she usually add that?"

"I don't know."

"Good answer."

He went back inside.

Two workers were carrying a long shallow crate through the side
entrance. One of them complained that it had been marked for east
storage. The other said something about south.

I kept walking.

Hessa had scheduled direction.

Not tomorrow.

The day after.

I folded the note and put it with Teren's lamp message, then remembered
I had already delivered Teren's lamp message.

My coat was becoming an office.

I reached the hall during the last piece.

Nobody had missed me.

That was useful information.

Rinna was counting coins.

"Six tins tomorrow," I said. "He wants the old tins back."

"Good."

"I bought leather."

"I can see."

"One copper."

"Why are you telling me?"

"I don't know."

She looked at my hand.

"Better?"

"Yes."

"Then stop complaining."

"I wasn't."

"You were preparing."

She was right.

I helped clean.

A costume sleeve had torn under the arm. The woman with the brown coat
was already fixing it using one of the hooks Nessa had given me.

So the hooks had not been wrong.

They had merely been wrong for Rinna.

That felt familiar.

Jori rolled his cue page.

Sellen stole the last baker roll.

Hara accused him.

He denied it while chewing.

The hall had another evening show.

I did not.

Rinna told me I was done.

"Paid?"

She gave me one copper.

It replaced the one I had spent on leather.

That felt less like earning money than moving it through myself.

"Tomorrow?"

"Morning setup. Evening maybe."

"Role?"

"Maybe."

"Don't."

She smiled.

I left.

Lyssa was not home.

I was beginning to suspect she had a job.

I put Hessa's note on the table beside my folded question.

FORWARD?

MY FORWARD OR BOARD FORWARD?

The note looked like an answer to a different question.

DAY AFTER TOMORROW.

I had tomorrow first.

There was still one copper from the previous days on the table, plus the
copper I had just earned. I had spent one on the grip, so the pile had
not changed much.

I touched the new leather.

Worth it.

Then I realized I had forgotten to tell Jessa anything except narrow,
not wide.

That was exactly what Lyssa had told me to say.

So perhaps I had succeeded.

Lyssa came home after dark carrying a basket.

"Did you take it?"

"Yes."

"To Marra?"

"Yes."

"Did you tell her left side?"

"Yes."

"Jessa?"

"Eventually."

Lyssa stopped.

"What happened?"

"She wasn't at Marra's."

"She was supposed to be."

"She went to Sen's."

"Why?"

"I don't know. Then she went back to Marra's."

Lyssa closed her eyes.

"I found her."

"Did you tell her?"

"Narrow, not wide."

"Good."

"That is all I know."

"That is all you needed to know."

I pointed at her.

"Exactly."

She put the basket down.

Inside were onions, a small wrapped piece of cheese, and two things that
might have been turnips.

"What's that?"

"Food."

"I know what food is."

"Do you?"

"I had beans yesterday."

"That is evidence."

I took the basket to the table.

"Hessa sent a note."

"When?"

"I passed the Guild."

"You weren't going to the Guild."

"I didn't."

"Then how did you get a note?"

"They found me."

Lyssa looked at the paper.

"Day after tomorrow."

"Yes."

"Direction?"

"Probably."

She read my question.

"MY FORWARD OR BOARD FORWARD?"

"Yes."

"That's ugly."

"Thank you."

"What does it mean?"

"I don't know yet."

"Good question."

I looked at her.

She started cutting an onion.

"You sound like Hessa."

"Hostile."

"Accurate."

I sat.

My palm still hurt, but the crutch grip no longer rubbed the sore place
when I leaned it against the table.

Lyssa noticed the leather.

"New?"

"One copper."

"Good."

"The man was a thief."

"Is it better?"

"Yes."

"Then maybe not."

"He charged me for scrap."

"He wrapped it."

I hated that everyone understood this except me.

We ate late.

The turnips were turnips.

Neither of us had expected the other to have food ready, so the meal was
bread, onion, cheese, boiled turnips, and the last of yesterday's beans.

It was fine.

Lyssa's day had involved three people changing what they wanted after
cloth had already been cut.

Mine had involved carrying her parcel to the correct green door, walking
to the red door anyway, carrying Nessa's hooks, performing a goat
complaint, buying leather, carrying Teren's message south, receiving
Hessa's message while not visiting Hessa, and returning to a theatre
that had continued perfectly well without me.

"Whose was worse?" I asked.

"Mine."

"You didn't hear mine."

"I lived mine."

"Unfair."

"Yes."

I ate cheese.

Afterward I put tomorrow's theatre time on a scrap beside Hessa's note.

MORNING HALL. EVENING MAYBE.

Under that:

DAY AFTER TOMORROW. HESSA. QUESTION.

I looked at the two pieces of paper.

Neither moved.

I did not ask them to.

Tomorrow I had theatre.

The day after, I had magic.

Tonight I had dishes.

I did those first.
