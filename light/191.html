LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 191

THE WITNESS

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Lyssa was gone before I woke properly.

There was bread on the table, half an apple beside it, and a strip of
paper under the cup.

Not Hessa's paper.

LYSSA: DON'T FORGET THEATRE BECAUSE PAPER HAPPENED.

I stared at it.

Rinna had said almost exactly the same thing.

This was either evidence of a conspiracy or evidence that I had become
predictable.

I ate the apple first because it seemed less judgmental.

Hessa's actual sheet remained under the cup.

AWAY ONLY.

NEAR / FAR.

No date.

Nothing I was allowed to do about it.

I left it there.

On the way to East Market Hall, a carter shouted at his boy to move a
sack away from the wheel.

The word caught.

I hated that.

The boy dragged the sack with both hands.

I kept walking.

East Market Hall had both front doors open and three different people
trying to occupy the same patch of floor.

A rolled hanging blocked half the entry. Two benches stood outside.
Someone had left a basket of costume shoes directly where nobody could
avoid it.

I stepped over the basket and looked at the chalk board.

KING: MAREK / HARA / SELLEN BORROWED HOUSE: VEYA / HARA / EVIN / PELL
THE LETTER BOX: VEYA / SELLEN / GREG / NESSA REAR: JORI / PELL FRONT:
RINNA

I read my name.

Then I read it again.

GREG: WITNESS.

No if.

No maybe.

No missing person.

No little mark beside it that meant somebody else had failed to arrive.

Rinna came past with a roll of tickets in one hand.

"You're on time."

"You threatened me."

"I scheduled you."

"Same thing."

She pointed at the board.

"I saw."

"Good."

"What is The Letter Box?"

"Piece you're in."

"Useful."

"Pages are on the table."

She kept moving.

That was it.

I stood there for another second.

Nessa put a folded green cloth into my hands.

"Right table."

I took it.

Theatre had apparently decided I had experienced enough emotion.

The Letter Box was a short domestic comedy about a locked wooden box
that belonged to nobody until everybody wanted it.

Veya played Mara, the woman whose dead aunt had left the box in her
room.

Sellen played Corren, her brother, who claimed the aunt had promised him
everything worth stealing.

Nessa played the landlady, who claimed anything left in the room after
rent was late belonged to the house.

I was a municipal witness.

My job was to stand there, record who said what, and repeatedly refuse
to decide anything.

I liked him immediately.

There were eighteen lines.

Not forty-three.

Not eight.

Eighteen.

Enough that I had to learn them. Not enough that I needed to reconsider
every decision that had brought me to Carrow.

The important object was not the box.

That was the first problem.

The important object was a little brown purse on the table beside it.

The purse contained the key.

The audience was not supposed to know that until late.

I knew because the page said so.

This became a problem almost immediately.

Teren arrived while I was reading.

He had bread in his mouth and two pins stuck through the cuff of his
shirt.

"You're Witness."

"I gathered."

"Know it?"

"I've seen part."

"Then see the rest."

He pulled one pin from his cuff and used it to point.

"Entrance after Nessa says, 'Then let the town hear it.' Left side. You
have ledger. You don't touch box."

"Today?"

He looked at me.

"Yes."

"Good."

"Keep doing that."

Then he walked away.

I looked after him.

That might have been praise.

I decided not to ruin it by checking.

Rehearsal started with a missing ledger.

The ledger was eventually found under the folded cloth I had moved to
the right table.

This was somehow not my fault.

The first run lasted less than a minute before Teren stopped us.

"Greg."

I had entered correctly.

I had my ledger.

I had said the first two lines correctly.

I looked at him.

"What?"

"Stop looking at the purse."

I looked at the purse.

Then I realized I had looked at the purse.

"I'm not."

Veya laughed.

Teren pointed at me.

"You just did it again."

"Because you said purse."

"Before that."

"I was looking at the table."

"At the purse."

"The purse is on the table."

Sellen said, "He's got you there."

Teren ignored him.

"Your Witness doesn't know the key is in it."

"I know."

"That's the problem."

"I am aware of what my character doesn't know."

"Your face isn't."

I looked at Veya.

She was standing close enough to the purse that her sleeve nearly
touched it.

She had not looked at it once.

I knew it was there.

Because I knew it mattered, I kept seeing how carefully she did not see
it.

This was deeply irritating.

Teren reset us.

"Again."

We went again.

Nessa entered as the landlady and demanded the box.

Sellen accused her of theft.

Veya accused Sellen of being cheap enough to steal from the dead.

Nessa said, "Then let the town hear it."

I entered with the ledger.

"I was told there was a dispute."

"There is," Veya said.

"There isn't," Sellen said.

Nessa pointed at both of them.

"There."

I opened the ledger.

"Good. A dispute."

Small laugh from Pell, who was not the audience and should have been
working.

I continued.

"Names."

They all spoke at once.

I looked down to write.

Then my eyes flicked to the purse.

Teren clapped once.

"Greg."

"Damn it."

"Again."

On the third run, I solved it by staring determinedly at the box.

Teren stopped us.

"Now the box is guilty."

"It is the subject of the dispute."

"You are making it look like it contains a body."

"What do you want me to look at?"

"The people."

This seemed unreasonable.

I tried the people.

Veya was much harder to accidentally reveal.

Sellen was impossible to make look unimportant, but that was his
problem.

We got through the scene.

At the reveal, Veya finally picked up the purse, opened it, and removed
the key.

The action felt larger because she had spent the previous part of the
scene treating the purse like cloth.

I noticed.

Then Teren said, "Greg, line."

I had missed it.

Of course.

"I object to the opening."

Sellen looked at me.

"You object now?"

"Municipally."

Pell laughed again.

Teren rubbed his face.

"That's not the line."

"I know."

"Then use the line."

"I had it."

"Find it."

The actual line was, "I have not witnessed consent to open the box."

Less funny.

Probably better.

We reset.

The hall kept working around us.

A shoe buckle snapped during the next run. Nessa left the scene, fixed
it while still wearing her landlady shawl, and came back before we
reached her next entrance.

Someone spilled a little red paint near the rear wall.

Pell stepped in it.

He spent the next ten minutes leaving half a red footprint everywhere
until Jori noticed and made him clean both the floor and his shoe.

Hara changed stockings because the first brown pair had a run at the
ankle.

Then she changed back because the second pair were apparently the wrong
brown.

I asked what wrong brown meant.

She told me to become older.

I did not ask again.

By midday I had most of the Witness in my mouth.

Not good.

Present.

There was a difference.

I ate bread outside with Pell and EvIn.

Pell had food today.

This disappointed me.

EvIn was reading the Porter pages from The Borrowed House while eating a
cold potato.

"You're Porter?" I asked.

"No."

"Who is?"

He pointed with the potato.

A young man I had seen carrying benches was practicing my old lines
beside the wall.

I watched him.

"I was told this house," he said.

"By whom?" EvIn called.

"A man."

"What man?"

"The one who told me this house."

Pell looked at me.

"Better."

"He hasn't performed it yet."

"Still."

The young man had less trouble with the chest because he had two good
legs and no need to arrange the final four steps around a crutch.

I resented this unfair advantage.

Rinna came outside.

"Greg."

"I am eating."

"After."

"Then why say my name now?"

"So you know after exists."

She went back in.

Pell nodded.

"Good system."

"I hate all systems."

"Paper."

"Don't."

He took another bite of bread.

That was the entire conversation.

After food, Rinna had me move two benches away from the front wall.

Away.

I noticed the word.

Then I moved the benches with my hands because I was not an idiot.

Mostly.

The afternoon rehearsal got better.

I stopped looking at the purse.

Then I started avoiding looking at the purse so aggressively that I
turned my body away from the table every time I crossed.

Veya stopped me.

"Now you hate the purse."

"I don't look at it."

"You move around it."

"I am not touching it."

"Exactly."

She took my place.

"Watch."

She entered.

She crossed the room.

She spoke to Sellen.

She put one hand on the table beside the purse.

She shifted the ledger.

She took the chair.

She never looked at the purse.

She also never avoided it.

It existed.

That was all.

She gave me my place back.

I tried again.

Better.

Not good.

Better.

Teren said, "Fine."

I took it.

Then I ruined something else.

During Sellen's long complaint about the dead aunt promising him the
box, I turned toward him too fully and blocked Veya from the house.

"Greg."

I moved.

"Other way."

I moved too far.

"You're not furniture."

"I have been furniture here."

"Not today."

"Today?"

Teren pointed at the floor mark.

I found it.

The next run worked.

No one applauded.

A cart outside lost a board with a crash loud enough that everyone in
the hall stopped.

Jori went to the front door.

"Not ours."

We continued.

By late afternoon, the floor had been swept twice and was dirty again.

The hall smelled of warm bodies, old paint, lamp oil, damp wool from
coats, and the onion someone backstage had cut without permission.

Rinna opened the house.

People came in.

The man who had asked about the Porter came back.

I saw him take a seat near the left aisle.

He saw me at the side curtain.

His expression changed.

I could not tell whether this was good.

I was in the Witness coat.

Gray-brown, plain, narrow collar.

The ledger was real enough to be heavy.

My shoes were mine.

Nessa had looked at my crutches, looked at the entrance, and moved my
waiting position half a pace closer to the wing without comment.

The Borrowed House played first.

I watched the new Porter from behind the side hanging.

He entered with the chest.

"I was told this house."

The audience laughed earlier than they had for me.

I disliked him.

"By whom?"

"A man."

"What man?"

"The one who told me this house."

Bigger laugh.

I hated him for approximately four seconds.

Then he missed the next line.

I liked him again.

The piece continued.

He recovered.

The audience man near the aisle laughed.

Good.

Fine.

Whatever.

Then came The Letter Box.

I stood left.

Ledger.

Crutches placed where I could reach them after exit.

The purse sat on the table.

I did not look at it.

I knew exactly where it was.

That was worse.

Nessa entered.

Sellen entered.

Veya entered.

The argument built.

The audience laughed when Sellen claimed the dead aunt had always loved
him best.

Veya said, "She called you a damp boot."

"Affectionately."

Bigger laugh.

Nessa stepped between them.

"Then let the town hear it."

My cue.

I entered.

No stumble.

No wrong side.

"I was told there was a dispute."

"There is," Veya said.

"There isn't," Sellen said.

Nessa pointed.

"There."

I opened the ledger.

"Good. A dispute."

The audience laughed.

Not huge.

Enough.

"Names."

They spoke over each other.

I looked at them.

Not the purse.

Sellen tried to hand me the box.

I stepped back.

"I have not witnessed transfer."

"I'm transferring it."

"I have witnessed an attempt."

"That's the same thing."

"It is written differently."

That got a laugh I had not expected.

I did not wait for it.

Teren had trained that out of me by violence.

Metaphorical violence.

Mostly.

The scene moved.

Veya crossed near the table.

Her hand passed within an inch of the purse.

Nothing.

It was just a purse.

Sellen accused Nessa of hiding the key.

Nessa accused him of swallowing it.

I wrote this down because the Witness wrote everything down.

Then Sellen said, "Ask her where she was when the aunt died."

Veya went still.

That was the turn.

The audience quieted.

I knew the purse was about to matter.

My eyes wanted it.

I kept them on Veya.

She looked at Sellen.

Then at Nessa.

Then down at the table as though the table had only just occurred to
her.

Her fingers touched the purse.

The audience followed.

There.

Now it mattered.

She opened it.

The key came out.

A small sound moved through the room.

Not a gasp.

Recognition.

Sellen said, "You had it."

Veya said, "I had her purse."

"With the key."

"I didn't know."

I had my line.

"I have not witnessed consent to open the box."

Sellen turned on me.

"You have witnessed the key."

"Yes."

"Then open it."

"No."

"Why?"

"I have no authority."

The audience laughed.

Sellen stared at me.

"What do you have?"

I looked at the ledger.

"Excellent notes."

That was scripted.

I wanted everyone to know.

The laugh was mine anyway.

The box opened later.

It contained three old letters, a broken comb, and a receipt proving the
aunt had already sold the valuable thing everyone thought was inside.

Veya got the final line.

"She left us the argument."

She was better than all of us.

The audience went quiet for the line, then laughed after.

I stood there and let her have it.

This was harder than it sounded.

We exited.

Sellen handed me my crutch.

"Fine."

"That word is destroying me."

"You want bad?"

"No."

"Then fine."

I had no answer.

Backstage immediately became work.

Jori needed the narrow cord.

Pell had put it on the correct shelf, which made me suspicious.

A costume basket had to move.

Someone had lost a glove.

The red paint footprint returned because apparently Pell had not cleaned
the edge of his sole.

Nessa saw it.

Pell said, "Impossible."

She pointed.

He cleaned it again.

The last short piece had no Greg.

I watched half of it until Rinna gave me a stack of used tickets to
count.

I counted wrong.

"Again," she said.

"I can move paper."

"Not that paper."

I counted again.

Thirty-seven.

She checked.

Thirty-seven.

"See?"

"You needed two tries."

"Repeatable."

She pushed the stack away from me.

I looked at her.

She looked at me.

"Don't."

"I didn't."

"You did."

I hated everyone.

Cleanup took longer than the last piece.

The audience left dirt, two apple cores, one ribbon, and a wooden button
under a bench.

Nobody claimed the button.

Nessa claimed the ribbon for repairs.

Pell claimed one apple core was not his.

Nobody had accused him.

Rinna paid me one copper.

"Witness," she said.

"And front."

"One copper."

"And tickets."

"One copper."

"And I moved benches."

"Do you want less?"

"No."

"Good."

She turned to the board.

"Tomorrow."

I looked.

My name was not written yet.

"What tomorrow?"

"Work."

"Role?"

"Maybe."

"That's how this started."

"What?"

"Nothing."

She erased part of the board.

I put the copper away.

Outside, Carrow had gone blue with evening.

Shops were closing in pieces. One shutter down, another still open. A
woman swept flour from a doorway. Two boys argued over a hoop in the
road. Somewhere farther off, somebody was singing badly and confidently.

I went home.

Lyssa was not there.

I was first.

This felt unusual enough that I stood in the room for a moment as if I
had entered the wrong house.

Then I put bread on the table, set my copper beside the others, and took
off the Witness coat.

No.

My coat.

The Witness coat belonged to the hall.

This was how it happened.

I sat.

Hessa's sheet waited under the cup.

AWAY ONLY.

NEAR / FAR.

No date.

I did not touch it.

Lyssa came in after dark carrying a narrow bundle wrapped in pale cloth.

I looked at the bundle.

She looked at me looking.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"You looked."

"This is becoming oppressive."

She put the bundle against the wall.

"How was theatre?"

"I was a municipal witness."

"Did you witness anything?"

"A purse."

She waited.

"I wasn't supposed to."

"Then why did you?"

"I knew it mattered."

"Did it?"

"Later."

"So you were right."

"Incorrectly."

Lyssa took off her shoes.

Her ankle looked almost normal.

I did not look long enough to get caught.

Probably.

"What did the witness do?" she asked.

"Told everyone I had no authority."

"Good casting."

"I've already had this joke."

"Then stop earning it."

I told her about the purse.

About Teren telling me to stop looking at it.

About Veya standing beside it without making it exist until she needed
it.

Lyssa listened while unwrapping the pale bundle.

Inside was dark cloth.

Not yellow.

Not green.

Not mine.

I did not ask.

"Did you get it right?" she asked.

"In the show."

"Then good."

"Fine."

"What?"

"Teren said fine."

"You said good."

"I am improving the review."

She smiled.

I showed her the copper.

"One."

"For witnessing?"

"And front. And tickets. And benches."

"Underpaid."

"Rinna asked if I wanted less."

"Did you?"

"No."

"Then expertly negotiated."

We ate bread and the last of the cheese.

Later, Lyssa moved the dark cloth to the chair.

I noticed that it went farther from me.

I noticed that I noticed.

Then I noticed that I was an idiot.

Hessa's paper remained under the cup.

Twenty-six draws.

Twenty-two shapings.

Nineteen external attempts.

Away was probably easier.

The purse had been important all afternoon.

The best thing I had done with it was pretend I did not know.

I did not write that down either.
