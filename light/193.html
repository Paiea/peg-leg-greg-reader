LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 193

THE DINER

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Lyssa was still home when I woke.

This felt suspicious.

She was sitting at the table with one knee up, eating the last piece of
bread and reading something that did not belong to me. The pale wrapping
from yesterday had disappeared. So had the dark cloth. In their place
were two onions.

I looked at them.

Lyssa looked at me.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"You bought one onion."

"Yes."

"Now there are two."

"That seems like growth."

"I bought the other one."

"Then we're both contributing."

She tore the bread in half and gave me the smaller piece.

"Your contribution was worse."

I ate it anyway.

Her ankle did not have a wrap on it. I noticed because I had eyes. She
noticed me noticing because apparently she had developed a professional
interest in my eyes.

"It's fine."

"I didn't ask."

"You looked."

"Everyone keeps accusing me of looking."

"Stop being visible."

"That's not how looking works."

She folded the paper she had been reading.

"What are you doing today?"

"Theatre. Probably."

"When?"

I stopped.

That was a useful question.

"Teren said tomorrow."

"That's not a time."

"He also said probably."

"That's worse."

I considered going to the hall immediately.

Lyssa saw that too.

"No."

"What?"

"Don't go there at breakfast because somebody might need you at supper."

"I wasn't."

"You were."

"I was thinking."

"Poorly."

She stood, put the folded paper into the pocket of her skirt, and took
one onion.

"That's mine."

"You bought the other one."

"I bought this one."

"Prove it."

She left with my onion.

I sat at the table with the remaining onion and Hessa's note.

AWAY ONLY.

NEAR / FAR.

Still no date.

I did not read it twice.

This was becoming a skill.

I had no reason to be at the hall before midday.

I knew this because I went there to check.

Rinna was at the front table with her hair tied back and a cup beside
one hand. She looked up when I entered.

"No."

"I haven't asked anything."

"Good."

"What time?"

"After third bell."

I looked toward the board.

It was blank except for old chalk smears.

"Role?"

"After third bell."

"That isn't a role."

"It's also not now."

I stood there.

Rinna drank from her cup.

"Are you going to make me assign you something so you'll leave?"

"No."

"Good."

I left.

This was humiliating.

I had several hours.

Carrow was already busy. The lower market had opened fully, and the
morning traffic had settled into the part of the day where everybody
seemed to know where they were going except me.

I went to the cobbler first.

Not because anything was wrong.

That was new.

The leather grip on my right crutch had darkened where my palm sat. The
seam still stayed where it belonged. It had not twisted once. My hand
was nearly healed.

The cobbler glanced at it when I came in.

"Still there."

"Yes."

"Good."

"I came to tell you."

"No, you didn't."

"I could have."

He went back to a boot sole.

I looked around.

There was nothing I needed.

"How much for soap?"

He looked up.

"Wrong shop."

"I know."

"Do you?"

I left.

The soap seller was three stalls down.

I bought a small hard square for one copper after arguing with myself
longer than I argued with the seller.

It was soap.

I had paid for magic garbage for the same price.

This seemed unfair to soap.

Then I bought a meat bun.

That cost less thought.

I carried both to the lower market and sat on the same low wall where I
had once discovered I could tolerate peace for about fifteen minutes.

Today I had food.

This improved peace.

A dog slept under a cart with one eye open. A woman repaired the bottom
of a basket by threading fresh reeds through the old broken edge. Two
fish sellers argued about whether one of them had moved a bucket.
Neither mentioned magic.

A boy chased a hoop past me.

An old man sat at the far end of the wall and removed one shoe.

I ate the bun.

Nobody asked me where Pell was.

Nobody handed me a message.

Nobody told me to move a bench.

I finished eating and stayed.

The dog changed sides.

The basket got a new bottom.

The fish argument ended without agreement.

The old man put his shoe back on.

I stayed long enough that I stopped measuring it.

That was probably because I was tired.

Eventually a bell rang.

Not third bell.

I knew this because I was not completely useless.

I got up and went nowhere important.

There was a public pump two streets over. I washed my hands, then used
the new soap because I had bought it and wanted evidence that the
purchase had not been stupid.

The water ran gray from my fingers.

Paint.

Dust.

Lamp soot.

Something brown that I decided was food.

The soap smelled faintly of pine and strongly of soap.

I washed my face too.

A woman waiting behind me said, "You missed a spot."

"Where?"

She pointed at my ear.

I washed my ear.

"Other one."

I stared at her.

She smiled.

I washed the other one.

By the time third bell was close enough to be arguable, I went to the
hall.

Rinna looked up.

"Better."

"I was exactly on time."

"You're early."

"Close enough."

"Board."

There was writing now.

KING: MAREK / HARA / SELLEN PETITION: VEYA / SELLEN / GREG / EVIN WIDOW:
VEYA / HARA / NESSA SHORT: HARA / EVIN FRONT: RINNA REAR: JORI / PELL
GREG: THIRD MAN / SET

I read it.

Third Man.

Again.

No emergency.

No explanation.

Just work I already knew.

"Goat?" I asked.

Sellen was behind me.

"Don't."

"I asked one word."

"You asked the wrong one."

"Is it the same piece?"

"Today?"

I looked at him.

He looked at me.

"Yes," he said.

"Good."

"You're getting worse."

Nessa handed me a rolled brown hanging.

"Back wall."

"Today?"

"Yes."

Sellen laughed.

I carried the hanging.

The afternoon setup was boring in the best possible way.

The Petition used the same table but a different chair because the old
chair had developed a loose front rung. Pell had tied it once. Davin had
untied it and written BAD on the seat in chalk.

Someone had still moved it back toward the stage.

Nessa caught it before it got there.

"Who?"

Nobody answered.

Pell said, "Could be anyone."

"It has your cord on it."

"Many people use cord."

"Your knot."

Pell looked offended.

"My knots are not signatures."

Davin walked past.

"They are."

Pell carried the chair away.

I helped hang the brown wall, moved the petition stack to the correct
shelf, then moved it again because Veya wanted more room on the table.

The fake loaf was not in tonight's bill.

I checked anyway.

It was in the kitchen box.

Correctly.

This bothered me.

At rehearsal, The Petition took twelve minutes.

I knew the entrance.

I knew the line.

I knew Sellen was not a goat.

This was useful.

Teren watched from a bench.

"Again from Greg."

I entered.

"My neighbor's goat has eaten all my patience and half my fence."

Sellen came in.

"Your fence was mostly hope."

A laugh existed only in our memory because there was no audience.

I said the next line.

We continued.

Teren stopped us once because EvIn was too far upstage.

He stopped me once because I had set my crutch where Veya needed to
cross.

"Sorry."

"Move it."

I moved it.

That was the entire correction.

No one explained what it meant for my future.

I appreciated this.

Then Teren left.

He had somewhere else to be.

I did not know where.

Nobody told me.

Sellen sat on the edge of the stage and took a small wrapped packet from
his pocket.

"What is that?"

"Food."

"What kind?"

"Mine."

"I wasn't asking to have it."

"You looked."

I hated everyone.

He opened the packet.

Dried fish.

I changed my mind.

"You can keep it."

"I was going to."

We ate before house.

Not together exactly.

Near each other.

I had another piece of bread from a stall outside. Sellen had dried
fish. Pell had acquired an egg.

"Cooked?" I asked.

He cracked it against the edge of a box.

It was cooked.

I felt disappointed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You expected raw."

"No."

"You did."

"You're becoming predictable."

He peeled the egg.

Hara came through in a clean blue overdress with one sleeve pinned but
not yet sewn.

"Nessa wants you."

Pell stood.

"Why?"

"Didn't ask."

He went.

Sellen watched him leave.

"He's good with the shutters."

"I know."

"That's why Nessa wants him."

"I know."

Sellen ate another piece of fish.

"You sound upset."

"I'm not."

"You looked upset."

I considered leaving the theatre forever.

Instead, I asked, "Do you live near here?"

Sellen glanced at me.

"Why?"

"No reason."

"Then no."

"That doesn't answer."

"It answered the question you should've asked."

"What question?"

"Can you come bother me after work?"

"I wasn't going to."

"Then we're done."

I ate bread.

After a moment, he said, "South side."

That was all.

I did not ask where.

It felt like winning something I had not entered.

House was better than yesterday.

Not full.

Better.

Rinna had opened one more row.

People came in carrying the day's heat with them. The hall warmed
quickly. Someone had tracked dry straw onto the floor. A little girl
wore a paper crown that kept slipping over one eye.

I had no front work.

This felt luxurious.

I stood backstage and did almost nothing for four whole minutes.

Then Jori handed me a stool.

"King."

Of course.

The King went cleanly.

The audience laughed more than yesterday.

Marek did not seem relieved.

Hara did not seem surprised.

Sellen missed one word, replaced it with another, and the world did not
end.

I watched from the wing until Nessa told me to stop standing where she
needed to pass.

The Petition was next.

I put on the Third Man coat.

It smelled like somebody else.

Probably because it belonged to everybody.

The petition stack had the real wax seal on top and nonsense beneath.
The chair was the good chair. My crutch was where Veya would not hit it.

I waited.

The scene began.

Veya wanted the road repaired.

EvIn wanted the town to admit the road belonged to somebody else.

Sellen wanted everybody to stop saying his goat had caused anything.

Then my cue came.

I entered.

"My neighbor's goat has eaten all my patience and half my fence."

A decent laugh.

Not huge.

I did not ask it to become one.

Sellen entered.

"Your fence was mostly hope."

Bigger laugh.

I looked at him.

As Third Man.

Annoyed.

Correct.

The scene moved.

There was a point later where I had to hand Veya the petition stack.

I handed it to her upside down.

She looked at it.

I looked at it.

The seal was now at the bottom.

For half a second, my brain tried to fix reality.

Veya did it for me.

She turned the stack over and said, "At least the signatures know which
way the road goes."

The audience laughed.

I said, "More than the council does."

That was not the line.

It was also not terrible.

Sellen made a small sound through his nose.

Veya continued.

The scene survived.

Backstage, Sellen said, "That was fine."

"Which part?"

"The wrong papers."

"I gave them upside down."

"I noticed."

"Veya fixed it."

"Yes."

"I added a line."

"Yes."

"It worked."

Sellen looked at me.

"For everyone."

I stopped.

He had done that on purpose.

"You've been waiting months to say that."

"Days."

"It feels longer."

"That's because you talk."

Nessa came through carrying a basket.

"Move."

We moved.

Widow's Room used the painted doorway.

I had seen it enough times that the false depth barely interested me
from backstage anymore. It was cloth, frame, paint, floor seam, crate.
From the house, people still looked into a room that was not there.

I did not need to learn anything about it today.

I carried a costume basket instead.

The short piece lost a spoon.

This became a problem for approximately six minutes.

The spoon was required for Hara to accuse EvIn of stealing it.

Without the spoon, the accusation became philosophically difficult.

Everyone searched.

I checked the prop shelf.

Pell checked under the stage.

Nessa checked the kitchen box.

EvIn checked his own pocket twice.

Hara said, "Use the knife."

"The knife changes it," EvIn said.

"Into what?"

"Murder."

"Fair."

Eventually Jori found the spoon inside the cup from The Letter Box.

Nobody knew why.

The Letter Box was not even playing tonight.

"Yesterday was yesterday," I said.

Nessa looked at me.

"Don't become Teren."

"I don't know where he is."

"Good."

The short piece happened with the spoon.

After the show, Rinna paid me one copper.

"Third Man. Set."

"I was also stool."

"One copper."

"Petition recovery."

"Veya recovered it."

"I assisted."

"By causing it?"

"Essential."

"One copper."

I took it.

There was no immediate rush to go home.

This was partly because Pell said, "Food?"

It was not a question for me specifically.

It became a group.

Rinna did not come.

Nessa said no before anyone finished asking.

Hara had somewhere to be and left with her blue overdress half unlaced
at the back.

EvIn went with her for the first block, then turned east.

Jori disappeared.

Teren was still absent.

That left me, Pell, Sellen, and eventually Marek, who joined because he
had heard the word food from across the hall.

We went to a stall near the south edge of the market where a woman sold
bowls of beans with chopped greens and small pieces of pork if you paid
extra.

I paid extra.

This was reckless wealth.

The bowl cost enough that I noticed and not enough that I changed my
mind.

We sat on low stools around a table that leaned toward Pell.

"It likes me," he said.

"It's broken," Sellen said.

"Same thing."

Marek ate quietly.

For a while nobody talked about theatre.

This was strange.

Pell complained about a landlord who had raised the price of a room
because the roof had been repaired.

Sellen said that was the point of repairing the roof.

Pell said the old hole had been free.

Marek asked if anyone knew a cheap cart going north in three days.

"Why?" I asked.

He looked at me.

"Going north."

I waited.

Nothing else came.

Apparently this was allowed.

Sellen owed Pell a copper.

I learned this when Sellen put a copper on the table.

Pell immediately handed it to Marek.

Marek put it in his pocket.

"What just happened?" I asked.

"Debt," Sellen said.

"For what?"

"No."

That was the entire explanation.

I ate my beans.

They were good.

Better because I had bought them after work instead of receiving them
because someone else had negotiated food into a job.

Not much better.

Enough.

A group at the next table started arguing about whether the cider was
watered.

The stall woman told them it was, and if they wanted stronger they could
pay for stronger.

The argument ended.

This seemed like excellent governance.

Pell told a story about falling through a chicken-house roof when he was
fourteen.

I did not believe half of it.

Sellen believed less.

Marek believed none.

The story improved under these conditions.

By the time we left, the market lamps were lit.

Sellen went south.

Now I knew that meant something.

Pell went east after remembering he had promised to return a latch.

Marek went north, perhaps because he was practicing.

I went home.

Lyssa was not there.

That was normal.

There was a folded paper under the cup.

Not Hessa's old paper.

A new one.

I opened it.

THREE DAYS.

AFTER MIDDAY.

AWAY ONLY.

NEAR / FAR.

SAME RESTRICTIONS.

I read it once.

Then I put it beneath the old note.

Three days.

Good.

I had time to do absolutely nothing about it.

Lyssa came home while I was taking off my coat.

She carried no mysterious cloth.

This was almost disappointing.

"How was theatre?"

"Fine."

She stopped.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You said it wrong."

"I was Third Man."

"Again?"

"Yes."

"Goat?"

"Everyone knows too much."

She put a small wrapped bundle of greens on the table.

I told her about the upside-down petitions.

She laughed before I got to Veya's line.

"That's not the funny part."

"It is from here."

"I recovered."

"Veya recovered."

"I assisted."

"By causing it?"

I stared at her.

"Do you speak to Rinna?"

"No."

"Suspicious."

I told her I had eaten after work.

"Without me?"

"You stole my onion."

"I bought that onion."

"You cannot prove that."

She unwrapped the greens.

I showed her the new Hessa note.

"Three days," she said.

"Yes."

"Good."

"Apparently."

"What happens then?"

"Near and far."

"Both away?"

"Yes."

"How far?"

"I don't know."

"How near?"

"I don't know."

She nodded.

"You know a lot."

"I moved paper."

"Three days ago."

"Recently."

She pushed the greens toward me.

"Cut those."

I cut them.

No theory emerged.

The soap sat in my pocket until I remembered it and put it by the basin.

Lyssa picked it up.

"You bought soap."

"Yes."

"For us?"

I had not thought that far ahead.

"Obviously."

She smelled it.

"Good."

That one I accepted.

We ate the greens with what was left of the onions.

Tomorrow the hall would probably want me again.

In three days Hessa wanted paper farther away and paper nearer.

Sellen lived somewhere south.

Marek needed a cheap cart north.

Pell had once fallen through a chicken-house roof, unless he had not.

I had bought soap.

It was not a dramatic day.

I liked it anyway.
