LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 194

THE HAND

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

The soap was already wet.

I stood at the basin holding it.

Lyssa was still asleep.

This meant either she had used it before bed or somebody had broken into
our room during the night to wash.

I chose Lyssa.

I washed my face, my hands, and both ears without supervision.

Then I put the soap back exactly where it had been.

This was adulthood.

There was bread on the table. Not much. I cut it in half and left one
piece under a cloth for Lyssa. Beside it I put a note.

THEATRE.

That seemed sufficient.

I considered adding PROBABLY.

I did not.

Hessa's two papers stayed under the cup.

THREE DAYS had become two without either of us doing anything.

I liked that kind of progress.

The East Market Hall was already making noise when I arrived. Hammering
came from inside in an irregular pattern that suggested either repair or
murder.

It was repair.

Davin was on one knee near the left edge of the stage with a short pry
bar under a floorboard. Jori held a lamp low. Nessa stood above them
with both arms full of folded costumes.

"Don't step there," Davin said.

I had not stepped there.

"Good."

A chalk cross marked one board.

"What happened?"

"Splinter."

"To who?"

"Board."

I looked at him.

Davin looked at me.

"Also EvIn."

EvIn was sitting on a bench pulling a long pale splinter from the heel
of his shoe.

"Not me," he said.

"Good."

"Through the sole."

"Less good."

Nessa pushed the folded costumes into my arms.

"Rail."

"Which?"

"Costume."

"There are two."

"Left."

"Today?"

She stared at me.

"Yes."

I carried them away.

The board had already been written.

KING: MAREK / HARA / SELLEN PETITION: VEYA / SELLEN / GREG / EVIN WIDOW:
VEYA / HARA / NESSA SHORT: HARA / EVIN GREG: THIRD MAN / SET

Yesterday.

Almost exactly.

I looked at Rinna.

"Did you forget to erase it?"

"No."

"So I'm Third Man."

"For now."

I did not like those words.

"How long is now?"

"Until it isn't."

This was not an answer, but it was accurate enough that Hessa might have
approved.

I went back to the costume rail.

One of the wooden pegs had pulled halfway out of the wall. Nessa had
apparently decided the solution was to put fewer clothes on it and more
clothes on me.

"Hold that."

Davin had appeared beside me with a hammer.

"What?"

"The rail."

I held it.

He pulled the peg out, looked into the hole, made a dissatisfied sound,
and put the peg in his mouth.

"Tool."

"Which?"

He pointed with the hammer toward an open box.

There were seven things in it.

"That narrows it."

"Thin chisel."

I found the thin chisel.

Wrong one.

He took a narrower one.

"That's thinner."

"Yes."

"You could have said narrowest."

"I said thin."

"This is why civilization fails."

He scraped old wood from the hole.

I held the rail.

Nessa took two costumes off my shoulder without thanking me.

Then she put one back.

"Why?"

"Wrong rail."

"Whose fault is that?"

"Yours now."

I held the rail harder.

The first change came before Davin finished.

Marek walked in carrying a folded scrap of paper and said, "I have a
cart."

Rinna looked up.

"When?"

"Last bell before dark."

"Today?"

"Today to arrange it. Leaves in two days."

"How long?"

"An hour. Maybe."

Rinna looked at the board.

I looked at the board.

Marek looked at me.

"No," I said.

Nobody had asked.

Rinna erased MAREK from The King.

"Who?"

Hara was pinning the sleeve of a brown coat.

"Not me."

"You are already in it."

"I know."

Sellen said, "I can take the King."

Rinna erased something else.

"You are already in it."

"I know."

"I hate all of you."

Teren came through the side door at that moment, carrying a rolled page
and a small bag of nails.

"Good."

Rinna pointed at the board.

"Marek has to leave."

Teren looked at Marek.

"Cart?"

Marek nodded.

Teren looked at Sellen.

"King."

Sellen nodded.

"Hara?"

"Still Hara."

"Fine."

"What about his part?" I asked.

Everyone looked at me.

"Marek's part."

Teren said, "Sellen."

"Sellen was already Sellen."

"Not in The King."

I had forgotten that names were not roles.

This was getting complicated.

Rinna erased half the board and rewrote it.

KING: SELLEN / HARA / EVIN PETITION: VEYA / GREG / EVIN WIDOW: VEYA /
HARA / NESSA SHORT: HARA / EVIN GREG: THIRD MAN / SET

Sellen stared.

"Who am I in Petition?"

"Not in it," Teren said.

"Why?"

"Because you're King."

"Those happen at different times."

"Your coat doesn't."

Nessa said, "And I need the brown coat open."

Sellen looked at the coat.

The coat did not apologize.

Teren went to the stage.

"King first."

I kept holding the costume rail.

Davin tapped the new peg into place.

"Done."

I let go.

The rail stayed on the wall.

"Good."

"Yes."

"Do I get paid for this?"

"No."

"Then less good."

Davin moved back toward the floorboard.

Rehearsal became a sequence of people asking which version of today
existed.

Sellen knew the King well enough to take Marek's part with the page in
his hand for the first run. EvIn took Sellen's smaller place and
immediately complained that the hat smelled like Marek.

"It smelled like that before," Hara said.

"Then Marek smells like hat."

"Line," Teren said.

They continued.

I moved the petition table.

Then Rinna told me not to because The Petition might move later.

"Where?"

"Later."

"That's a time."

"Good."

I left it.

Marek went out to arrange his cart.

I caught him at the door.

"What are you taking north?"

He looked at me.

"Myself."

"That seems difficult to ship."

"I'm going with it."

"Why?"

"See my sister."

There it was.

A whole answer.

I was not prepared.

"Oh."

"She's having a baby."

"You're helping?"

"No."

"Then why are you going?"

Marek gave me the same look Lyssa gave me when I asked questions that
had already been answered.

"See my sister."

"Right."

He left.

I stood there for a moment.

Rinna called, "Greg."

I turned.

"Table."

Of course.

The second change came because Hara had to leave before evening.

This information arrived from a girl I did not know, who came through
the front doors and said, "Hara?"

Hara went outside.

She came back three minutes later.

"I have to go after Widow."

Rinna said, "Why?"

"Work."

"This is work."

"Other work."

"Better?"

"Different."

Rinna looked offended on behalf of employment.

Teren looked at the board.

Hara said, "I can do King and Widow. Not Short."

"Fine."

EvIn said, "I can do Short alone."

"No," three people said.

He looked hurt.

The Short disappeared from the board.

This created space.

Then it created a different problem.

Rinna had sold the evening as four pieces to at least six people.

"Add Letter Box," she said.

I looked at her.

"No."

"You like Witness."

"I do."

"Good."

"I didn't say yes."

"You are Witness."

The chalk changed again.

KING: SELLEN / HARA / EVIN PETITION: VEYA / GREG / EVIN WIDOW: VEYA /
HARA / NESSA LETTER BOX: VEYA / SELLEN / GREG / NESSA GREG: THIRD MAN /
WITNESS / SET

I read my line twice.

"You made it worse."

Rinna said, "I made it four."

"I have two coats."

Nessa said, "One coat."

"Third Man and Witness wear the same coat?"

"No. Witness wears gray."

"That's two coats."

"Third Man doesn't need the brown one today."

"He did yesterday."

"Today."

I closed my eyes.

"Good."

Nessa patted my shoulder once.

I think it was mockery.

We rehearsed The Petition first because everyone in it was present.

That was where I made the wrong mistake.

"My neighbor's goat has eaten all my patience and half my fence."

EvIn entered.

Not Sellen.

I knew that.

We continued.

Veya took the petition.

I said, "And if the council wishes to call that a road, perhaps the
council should walk it barefoot."

Teren said, "No."

I stopped.

"What?"

"Not that cut."

I looked at Veya.

She looked at me.

"What cut?"

"Road cut," Teren said.

"We're not on the road."

"I know where we are."

"Then why are you using it?"

"Because I know it."

EvIn said, "That's a strong defense."

"Thank you."

"It wasn't."

Teren held out his hand.

"Home cut. From the seal."

I knew the home cut too.

That was the problem.

I started again.

Veya held the petition stack.

I said, "The seal is real. That is more than I can say for the road."

"Yes," Teren said.

"Today?"

"Yes."

I pointed at him.

"See? Useful question."

"Keep going."

We kept going.

That was all.

Nobody gathered around to discuss the danger of knowing multiple
versions.

At midday, the floorboard was fixed.

Davin had cut the damaged edge back, set a narrow replacement strip, and
hammered it flush. The new wood showed pale against the old stage.

EvIn tested it with his shoe.

Davin said, "Don't."

EvIn stopped.

"Why fix it if I can't step on it?"

"Glue."

"Ah."

He stepped around.

Pell arrived late carrying a latch.

"Returned it."

"To who?" I asked.

"The person."

"Which person?"

"The latch person."

"That explains everything."

"It did to them."

He saw the board.

"Greg's two people."

"I'm always one person."

Pell looked unconvinced.

Marek returned with dust on his trousers and said the cart was arranged.

"Cheap?" Sellen asked.

"Cheap enough."

"North?"

"Still."

Rinna told him he was no longer King.

Marek looked at the board.

"Good."

I was offended.

"You don't care?"

"I have to pack."

"That's not the point."

"What is the point?"

I did not know.

He took a piece of bread from the table and left again to find Rinna's
string.

Apparently packing had already begun.

The third change was smaller.

The Widow's Room lost its pale shawl.

It had not disappeared.

Nessa had it in her hands.

It had torn under the arm.

"Use the dark one," Hara said.

"No."

"Why?"

"Wrong."

"How wrong?"

Nessa held the dark shawl against Hara.

We all looked.

Hara looked like a widow who had murdered her husband personally.

"Fair," Hara said.

Nessa repaired the pale shawl.

Nobody changed the board.

This felt like mercy.

By house, I smelled less like pine soap and more like old wool.

So the soap had lasted approximately half a day.

Still worth one copper.

The audience was normal.

I had forgotten normal could be noticeable.

Not full. Not thin. Not loud. Not strangely quiet.

People came in, sat down, talked until the lamps lowered, then mostly
stopped.

A baby cried once and was taken outside.

A man sneezed during The King.

Nobody built a chapter around it.

The King went first.

Sellen was good.

Not Marek-good in Marek's exact way. Sellen made the King's irritation
sharper and less tired. Hara adjusted without making it visible. EvIn
remembered the hat and most of the lines.

One audience member laughed where nobody usually did.

Sellen did not marry him.

I approved professionally.

Then The Petition.

I wore the right coat.

This mattered because I had nearly put on the gray Witness coat until
Nessa caught my sleeve.

"Later."

"I knew that."

"You had it on."

"Partly."

"Later."

I changed.

Onstage, the home cut stayed in my head.

"My neighbor's goat has eaten all my patience and half my fence."

EvIn entered.

"Your fence was mostly hope."

He did not say it like Sellen.

He said it like EvIn.

The audience laughed anyway.

I had a brief irrational objection.

Then Veya spoke and I had work.

The petition stack stayed upright this time.

I used the correct line about the seal.

Nobody applauded my accuracy.

The scene ended.

Backstage, I changed coats.

The gray Witness coat caught on the top of my crutch.

I pulled.

It caught harder.

Nessa slapped my hand away.

"Stop."

"I can get it."

"You're making it worse."

She lifted the cloth off the handle in one motion.

I put it on.

"Thank you."

"Go."

The Letter Box was already starting.

I had enough time.

That was all I needed.

Nessa's line came.

"Then let the town hear it."

I entered from the left with the ledger.

"I was told there was a dispute."

"There is," Veya said.

"There isn't," Sellen said.

"There," Nessa said.

I opened the ledger.

"Good. A dispute."

A laugh.

Normal.

I continued.

The purse sat on the table.

It was a purse.

I did not have to fight myself about it.

When the key came out later, the audience made the same small sound
audiences often made.

I gave the consent line.

Sellen argued.

I had no authority.

I had excellent notes.

The laugh was good.

Not enormous.

Good.

Veya got the last line.

"She left us the argument."

The audience laughed, then applauded.

I went offstage.

That was the whole performance.

I had done two roles.

Neither had changed my life.

I liked that too.

Hara left almost immediately after Widow's Room, still carrying the
repaired pale shawl over one arm.

"That's theatre property," Nessa called.

"I'm changing outside."

"Why?"

"Because I'm late."

"For what?"

Hara was already through the door.

Nessa looked at me.

"I don't know."

"I didn't ask."

"You looked."

This disease was everywhere.

Cleanup took longer than the show felt.

The floor collected scraps of thread, one crushed nut shell, two ticket
halves, and a small smear of something sticky near the third bench.

Pell touched it.

"Why?"

I asked.

He looked at his finger.

"Jam."

"How do you know?"

He licked it.

I walked away.

Davin checked the repaired floorboard.

The pale strip held.

Rinna counted coins at the front table.

There were more than yesterday.

I knew because the pile looked less insulting.

She paid me one copper.

"Third Man. Witness. Set."

"Two roles."

"One copper."

"Two coats."

"One copper."

"Rail."

"Davin fixed the rail."

"I held civilization together."

"Do you want less?"

"No."

"Good."

She pushed the copper toward me.

I took it.

On the board, tomorrow had already begun.

Not fully.

Just names and question marks.

I saw GREG once.

Then Rinna erased it.

I did not ask.

This required effort.

She noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You look ill."

"I'm growing."

"Do it somewhere else."

I went home.

Lyssa was not there.

The bread I had left was gone.

My note was still on the table.

THEATRE.

Under it, in Lyssa's writing:

OBVIOUSLY.

I kept the paper.

I did not know why.

Hessa's note stayed under the cup.

Two days.

No new instructions.

No reason to touch it.

I washed my hands with the soap.

The water ran gray again.

The theatre had defeated cleanliness in less than one shift.

Lyssa came home after dark carrying a narrow wooden hoop and a bundle of
pale thread.

I looked at both.

She looked at me.

I looked away.

"Better," she said.

"How was work?"

"Changed."

"Bad?"

"No."

"Then?"

"Changed."

This was apparently an acceptable answer when other people used it.

I told her Marek was going north to see his sister because she was
having a baby.

"That's nice."

"He said he's not helping."

"With the baby?"

"Yes."

"Good."

"I asked why he was going if he wasn't helping."

Lyssa slowly put the wooden hoop on the table.

"What?"

"He said to see his sister."

"Yes."

"I understood eventually."

"Did Marek survive this conversation?"

"Barely."

She took off her outer layer and hung it.

I told her the board changed three times.

"How many people were you?"

"Two."

"That's too many Gregs."

"I said that."

"No, you didn't."

"I said I'm always one person."

"Different."

She was right, which was irritating.

I told her I had nearly worn the wrong coat.

"Did you?"

"Partly."

"Then you did."

"Nessa stopped me."

"Useful Nessa."

"Everyone is very useful when I'm wrong."

Lyssa smiled.

"Convenient."

We ate bread, cheese, and the last of the greens.

I did not mention the one copper until she asked whether I had been
paid.

"One."

"For two Gregs?"

"Exactly."

"Underpaid."

"Rinna offered less."

"Expertly negotiated again."

I pointed at her.

"That joke belongs to me."

"No."

After we ate, she took the wooden hoop and pale thread to the far side
of the room and began doing something with both.

I did not ask.

This also required effort.

Tomorrow the chalk board might say my name.

It might not.

Marek had a cart north in two days. Hara had other work nobody had
explained to me. Davin's floorboard needed another night before EvIn was
allowed to test it by standing on it. The pale shawl had survived.

Hessa wanted me after midday in two days.

Near.

Far.

Away only.

Nothing about today had moved because I wanted it to.

A surprising amount had changed anyway.
