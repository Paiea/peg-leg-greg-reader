TEXT READER · CHAPTER 198

THE GUARD

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Lyssa was gone before I woke.

This was not unusual.

The unusual part was that she had left me the larger piece of bread.

I stared at it for a while.

There were several possible explanations. Generosity was one. Accident
was another. She might also have decided the bread was bad.

I smelled it.

It smelled like bread.

The soap sat beside the basin where I had left it. The edge had already
gone soft from use. I washed my face and hands, then checked my wrist by
moving it once.

Once was enough.

This was disappointing. I had built a whole system around checking
things repeatedly.

The wrist felt nearly ordinary. There was a little stiffness when I bent
it all the way back, but no complaint when I closed my hand. My shoulder
had stopped being interesting too.

Nothing new waited under the cup.

I did not lift it.

I ate the suspiciously large bread and went to work.

Carrow was already warm.

A cart loaded with baskets had stopped halfway across a narrow turn
because a wheel had found a hole everyone else had apparently agreed not
to see. Three people were helping. Four more were explaining how they
were helping incorrectly.

I went around.

At the hall, Rinna was standing just inside the front doors with a small
slate in one hand and a piece of chalk in the other.

She looked at me.

"Guild when?"

"Good morning."

"Guild when?"

"I don't know."

"Tomorrow?"

"I don't know."

"Day after?"

"I don't know."

She lowered the slate.

"This is useless."

"I agree."

"That does not improve it."

"I could lie."

"No."

"Then we have reached the limit of my contribution."

She looked down at the slate again.

There were names on it. Some had little marks beside them. Hara's name
had been written twice, once near the top and once near the bottom.
Marek's had a line through it.

Mine was near the edge.

Rinna tapped it with the chalk.

"Until the Guild sends for you, third bell."

I looked at her.

"Every day?"

"If I need you earlier, I'll send."

"Where?"

"Where you live."

"That seems invasive."

"Then don't live there."

I considered this.

"And if the Guild sends?"

"Tell me before midday if you can."

"What if they send after midday?"

"Then tell me after midday."

"This system has weaknesses."

"So do you. Third bell."

I looked at the slate again.

"Does this change my pay?"

"No."

"Then I object to the administrative burden."

Rinna wrote something beside my name.

"What did you write?"

"Complains."

"That is not scheduling."

"It helps me."

She pointed past me.

"Set."

That was apparently the end of negotiations.

I had been scheduled.

It felt suspiciously similar to what had already been happening, except
now Rinna had chalk.

The hall smelled of old cloth, sanding dust, and something sharp from
the paint shelf. Pell was on the floor beside a low bench with both
hands underneath it.

I stopped.

"Are you trapped?"

"No."

"Do you need help?"

"No."

"Why are you under the bench?"

"Looking."

"For what?"

He slid farther underneath.

"The thing."

I waited.

Pell's hand came out holding a small square nut.

"Found it."

"Excellent."

He slid back out.

"What was it for?"

He looked at the bench.

We both looked at the bench.

"Probably that."

Davin came through carrying a short iron wrench.

"Move."

Pell moved.

Davin took the nut from him, crouched, and fitted it onto the end of a
bolt that ran through the bench brace.

Pell pointed.

"That."

Davin tightened it.

The bench stopped wobbling.

I put one hand on the back and pushed.

Davin looked up at me.

"What?"

"Checking."

"I checked."

"Good."

I removed my hand.

My reputation was spreading.

The board had been written for the afternoon.

KING: SELLEN / HARA / EVIN

PETITION: VEYA / SELLEN / EVIN

WIDOW: VEYA / NESSA / HARA

SHORT: HARA / EVIN

GREG: SET

I read it.

Then I read the blank space after my name.

"Set."

Rinna had followed me in.

"I see that."

"Good."

"Third bell for set?"

"Today you're early."

"I arrived when I usually arrive."

"That is why I fixed it."

I was still deciding whether to be offended when Teren came through the
side passage carrying two folded pages.

He stopped at the board.

"Where's Davin?"

"Bench," Rinna said.

"Where's Jori?"

"Rear."

"Where's Greg?"

I raised a hand.

Teren looked directly at me.

"Good. Guard."

Rinna erased SET.

"Wait."

She wrote:

GREG: GUARD / SET

I pointed.

"You just made a system."

"It survived longer than I expected."

"What guard?"

"The King," Teren said.

"Today?"

He stared at me.

I stared back.

"Today," he said.

"Which version?"

"Local."

"Entrance?"

"Right."

"How many lines?"

"Six."

"Prop?"

"No."

"Costume?"

"Nessa."

Teren handed me one of the pages.

"See? Useful."

Rinna said, "Third bell."

"This is before third bell."

"I sent for you."

"You did not."

"You were here."

"That is not sending."

"Then leave and come back."

I took the page.

"I accept the exception."

The Guard had six lines.

This was too many for a man whose main responsibility appeared to be
standing beside a door.

The first two were announcements. The third refused entry to a
petitioner. The fourth admitted the same petitioner after Sellen ordered
me to. The fifth objected to a man carrying a chicken into the royal
chamber.

There was no chicken.

I checked.

"Why is there no chicken?"

Teren was pinning a page to the side board.

"There used to be."

"What happened to it?"

"Road cut."

"So why is the line still there?"

"It isn't."

I held up the page.

"It is."

He took the page from me, looked at it, and crossed out the fifth line.

"Five."

"This is why I ask which version."

"Yes."

I felt extremely vindicated.

The last line was, "He says it is a gift."

I looked at Teren.

"What is?"

"The basket."

"What basket?"

"The petitioner brings a basket."

"With no chicken."

"Correct."

"What is in it?"

"Nothing."

"Then why do I say it's a gift?"

"Because he says it's a gift."

"This government is deteriorating."

"Learn the lines."

Nessa found me beside the costume rail ten minutes later and held up a
dark coat.

"No."

I had not said anything.

"That one makes you look like a clerk."

"I have been a clerk."

"Not today."

She pushed it aside and pulled out a shorter brown coat with a stiff
collar.

I put it on.

The right sleeve was fine. The left caught slightly where I moved the
crutch.

Nessa watched me cross once.

"Again."

I crossed.

She folded the cuff under by less than the width of a finger and pinned
it inside.

"Again."

I crossed.

"Good."

"Can I breathe?"

"You're not Sellen."

"I've seen what you do to kings."

"Kings complain."

"So do guards."

"Less."

She walked away.

I looked down at the coat.

It was apparently finished.

Sellen came through in Marek's coat with both cuffs folded again. One of
Nessa's inside ties showed for half a second before he tucked it back.

"You have my government now," he said.

"I guard the door."

"Badly?"

"We haven't rehearsed."

"Then probably."

He kept walking.

I followed him into the stage space.

The repaired painted door from yesterday stood in its frame near the
right side. Davin had done something else to it after the hinge pin. It
now opened without the little sideways lift Pell had been using for
days.

I opened it.

It swung cleanly.

I closed it.

It shut harder than I expected.

The crack echoed through the empty hall.

Everyone looked at me.

Davin, from halfway up a ladder, said, "Don't."

"Understood."

I opened it again.

This time I let it close under my hand.

Quiet.

Davin went back to the hanging.

No theory was offered.

The door was simply a door with new behavior, and I had already lost one
argument with it.

Rehearsal took eleven minutes.

My first entrance was before Sellen's second petition.

I stood too close to the door.

Teren said, "Half a step."

I moved half a step.

"Other way."

I moved the other way.

"Good."

The position left room for Hara to pass behind me without catching my
coat. My crutch sat close enough to my body that it did not become a
second guard.

My first line was an announcement.

"Petitioner from East Lane."

Sellen, sitting in the king's chair, looked at me.

"And?"

I checked the page.

"That's the line."

"I know."

"Then why did you say and?"

"Because the king wants more."

"The petitioner from East Lane has a drainage complaint."

Sellen sighed.

"Everyone has a drainage complaint."

That was not on the page either.

Teren said, "Keep going."

I looked toward the imagined petitioner.

"You may enter."

Teren stopped us.

"No. You refuse him."

"I just announced him."

"Yes."

"Why announce someone I won't let in?"

Sellen said, "Government."

I looked at him.

"Fine."

We started again.

I announced the petitioner.

Sellen asked what he wanted.

I told him.

The petitioner tried to enter.

I held up one hand.

"Not until the king calls you."

Sellen said, "I just called him."

"You asked what he wanted."

"I am the king."

"That doesn't make your sentences clearer."

Teren lowered his page.

"Greg."

"Script."

"Please."

I let the petitioner in.

The scene continued.

On the basket entrance, EvIn came toward the door carrying an empty
covered basket.

I said, "You can't bring that in."

EvIn stopped.

"Why?"

"Because it is a basket."

"Is that forbidden?"

I looked at Teren.

Teren said, "Line."

I looked back at EvIn.

"He says it is a gift."

Sellen said, "For whom?"

EvIn said, "You."

Sellen leaned back in the chair.

"Then the guard was right. Take it away."

That got a laugh from Hara, who was sitting on the edge of the stage
eating an apple.

Teren made us do the basket section once more.

Then he left.

No one told me where he was going.

This continued to be allowed.

By midday, Rinna's slate had changed twice.

Hara's second mark disappeared because she had somewhere else to be
after the Widow. EvIn's name moved on the short piece. Pell got added to
rear for the last change. My name stayed where it was.

I found this comforting for almost three minutes.

Then Rinna walked past and said, "Tomorrow, third bell."

"Already?"

"That's what schedules do."

"What am I tomorrow?"

"I don't know."

"So we've scheduled ignorance."

"We've scheduled you."

She kept walking.

I stood there with a folded piece of brown cloth in my hands.

Nessa called from behind the costume rail.

"That stays."

I looked at the cloth.

"I wasn't moving it."

"You were thinking."

"This workplace has become oppressive."

"Put it on the shelf."

"Which shelf?"

She pointed.

I put it there.

At food, Sellen had remembered to bring something.

This was good because I had not brought enough bread to support another
adult.

He had a small paper packet of beans and onions. Pell had half a pear.
Hara had nothing because she said she had eaten before coming.

Nobody believed her.

"I did."

Pell looked at her.

"When?"

"Before."

"Before what?"

"Here."

"That's not a time."

Hara pointed at me.

"He's spreading."

"I have standards," I said.

"You have questions."

"Standards require questions."

Sellen ate a bean.

"Marek doesn't ask this many questions."

"Marek is north."

"Peacefully."

"His sister is having a baby."

Hara looked at me.

"You know why he went?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"I asked."

Everyone stared at me for a moment.

I looked around.

"What?"

Pell said, "Nothing."

"I hate all of you."

"No, you don't," Hara said.

This was an inappropriate amount of confidence.

The afternoon house was normal.

There were enough people that Rinna opened the same rows she usually
opened when nobody needed to have a discussion about rows. A man near
the front had a cough he tried to hold until the quietest possible
moments. Two women in the middle shared a paper twist of roasted seeds.
A boy near the aisle spent the first piece staring at the lamps instead
of the actors.

Nothing about them required strategy.

They had come to see a show.

We gave them one.

The King went first.

I waited at the right entrance in the stiff brown coat.

From there I could see Sellen in profile. Marek's coat still did not
belong to him. Nessa had made it stop advertising that fact.

Hara got the first real laugh.

Sellen got the second.

EvIn got a smaller one by looking at the king as though the king had
personally invented taxes that morning.

My entrance came.

I crossed to the repaired door, stopped where Teren had put me, and
announced the East Lane petitioner.

The audience barely noticed me.

This was appropriate.

The petitioner tried to come through.

I held up my hand.

"Not until the king calls you."

Sellen looked at me.

"I just called him."

I turned slightly.

"You asked what he wanted."

A laugh came from somewhere near the middle.

Not large.

Unexpected.

I heard it and kept going.

Sellen stared at me with the particular exhausted contempt of his king.

"I am the king."

"Then perhaps call more clearly."

That was not my line.

For one small, terrible fraction of a second, I knew it.

Sellen knew it too.

His mouth moved at one corner.

Then he said, "Enter before my guard begins governing."

The audience laughed.

The petitioner came through.

I stepped aside.

The scene continued.

Backstage, when I came off, Hara was waiting to enter from the other
side.

She looked at me.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"That was not the line."

"No."

"Did it work?"

"Yes."

"Then don't become unbearable."

She went on.

This seemed unfair.

The basket section came later.

EvIn approached with the empty covered basket.

"You can't bring that in," I said.

"Why?"

"Because it is a basket."

"Is that forbidden?"

"He says it is a gift."

Sellen looked at EvIn.

"For whom?"

"You."

"Then the guard was right. Take it away."

This time the laugh was larger.

Not because of me.

I stood beside the door and let the king have it.

The strongest moment in the piece belonged to Hara anyway. She entered
late with a folded tax order, listened to Sellen complain for three
lines, then quietly turned the page upside down before handing it to
him.

The house saw it before he did.

The laugh began before Sellen even looked down.

He waited.

Looked.

Then said, "This kingdom has been printed incorrectly."

That was the biggest laugh of the afternoon.

I was standing six feet away.

I contributed by not doing anything.

After The King, Nessa took my guard coat before I had both arms out.

"Tomorrow?"

I asked.

"Maybe."

"Rinna has scheduled me."

"Condolences."

She hung the coat.

The Widow ran cleanly.

The writing box stayed exactly where it belonged.

I did not touch it.

I was not even tempted.

This was mostly because I was carrying a lamp at the time.

The ordinary hall problem arrived during the change for the short piece.

Someone had eaten one of the prop apples.

It was not Pell.

This caused immediate confusion because everyone looked at Pell.

He was offended.

"I didn't."

Nessa held up the remaining apple.

"There were two."

"I know."

"Where is the other one?"

"I don't know."

Hara came through fastening a cuff.

"I ate it."

Everyone turned.

She stopped.

"What?"

"Prop," Nessa said.

Hara looked at the apple in Nessa's hand.

Then at the core in her own hand.

"That was a real apple."

"They both are."

"Why?"

"So people don't bite painted wood."

Hara considered this.

"Reasonable."

"The short needs two."

"Use a pear."

Pell looked down at his half pear.

"No."

Nessa took it from him.

"Hey."

"You weren't using all of it."

"I was."

"Not anymore."

The short went on with one apple and half a pear.

From the house, this apparently remained theatre.

Afterward Pell demanded compensation.

Rinna gave him nothing.

I supported him in principle.

This also earned nothing.

Cleanup took longer than the last piece.

The repaired door had to come out of its frame because tomorrow's first
bill did not use it. Davin took the hinge side. Pell took the other.

I reached for the lower brace.

Davin said, "No."

I stopped.

"Why?"

"Three people."

There were already three people.

"Right."

I carried two folded cloths instead.

My wrist did not complain.

This was becoming boring again.

Good.

Rinna paid me one copper.

"Guard and set," I said.

"Yes."

"Also schedule compliance."

"You arrived before the schedule started."

"Advance compliance."

"No."

"Improvised governance."

"No."

"Prop preservation."

"You did not eat an apple."

"Exactly."

Rinna held out her hand.

I closed mine around the copper.

"Tomorrow, third bell," she said.

"What if I get a Guild note?"

"Tell me."

"What if I don't?"

"Third bell."

"What if you need me earlier?"

"I'll send."

"What if Lyssa is not home?"

"Then I'll send someone to the hall and complain that you're not there."

I thought about this.

"Strong system."

"Go home."

I went home by way of a food stall.

One copper of theatre pay had an alarming tendency to become food if I
looked at stalls too long.

I bought bread.

I considered cheese.

I did not buy cheese.

Then I went back and bought the smallest piece.

This was how poverty happened.

Lyssa was not home when I arrived.

The room smelled faintly of soap, old wood, and the onions we had not
yet finished. I put the bread on the table, the cheese beside it, and
checked under the cup.

There was a new folded paper.

I stared at it.

Then I unfolded it.

TWO DAYS.

AFTER MIDDAY.

REPEAT.

NEAR / FAR.

AWAY ONLY.

SAME RESTRICTIONS.

I read it once.

Then again.

The second reading was administrative.

I put it under the cup.

Twenty-seven draws.

Twenty-three shapings.

Twenty-three external attempts.

Still.

Two days.

Apparently I had schedules now.

Lyssa came home while I was cutting the cheese badly.

She put a small cloth parcel on the table, looked at the knife in my
hand, and said, "What happened to it?"

"It's cheese."

"It was."

"I bought it."

"That doesn't answer me."

I moved one of the crooked pieces toward her.

She took it.

"Good."

"There is that word again."

She ate the cheese.

I pointed at the cup.

"Hessa."

Lyssa looked at it.

"When?"

"Two days. After midday."

"And theatre?"

"Third bell."

She looked at me.

"Every day?"

"Apparently."

"You already did that."

"No. Before, I went there because they might need me."

"And now?"

"Now Rinna has declared that they might need me."

Lyssa ate another piece of cheese.

"So you'll stop going at breakfast."

"No promises."

She nodded as if this confirmed something.

"What did you do today?"

"I guarded a king."

"From what?"

"Petitioners. Baskets. Clear sentences."

"Did you succeed?"

"The kingdom survived."

"Good."

I told her about the missing prop apple.

She guessed Pell.

I told her Hara.

She laughed.

I told her about the half pear.

"Did Pell get paid for it?"

"No."

"Tragic."

"He thinks so."

We ate bread and cheese.

Lyssa opened her cloth parcel after we were nearly finished. It
contained three small wooden buttons and a length of pale binding.

I looked at it.

She looked at me.

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"You were going to."

"I was going to ask whether it was the narrow thing."

"It is another narrow thing."

I stared.

"This city has too many."

She put the binding away.

The Hessa note remained under the cup.

Two days.

Near and far again.

Away only.

Same restrictions.

Tomorrow, third bell.

The day after that, third bell unless the Guild took me after midday, in
which case I had to tell Rinna before midday if possible, unless Rinna
sent for me earlier, in which case apparently the system changed.

I had acquired employment complicated enough to require remembering when
I was employed.

Lyssa reached across the table and took the last crooked piece of
cheese.

"That was mine."

"You didn't know."

"I absolutely knew."

"Too late."

I looked at the empty place where the cheese had been.

Then at the cup.

Then at the bread.

Nothing moved.

Nothing needed to.

Tomorrow I was a man expected after third bell.

In two days I was a man expected to sit in a marked chair and ask paper
to do almost nothing.

This was, somehow, a schedule.
