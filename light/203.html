TEXT READER · CHAPTER 203

THE WORKHAND

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Four copper was enough money to make me early.

Not theatre early.

I had learned.

I was only awake too early.

Lyssa was sitting at the table with a small piece of pale cloth across
one knee and three buttons beside her. I had no idea whether they
belonged together. I had stopped asking questions that created more
cloth.

"You have second bell," she said.

"I know."

"You've said that twice."

"I'm establishing it."

"No."

I ate bread.

There was nothing under the cup.

I did not check until Lyssa left.

Then I checked.

Nothing.

This was still mail.

By the time I reached the hall, I had successfully spent none of the
four copper I did not have.

Rinna was already outside.

"You're early."

"Second bell."

"It hasn't rung."

I looked toward the street as if the bell might be hiding behind a
building.

"Then I'm very dependable."

"No."

Behind her, the handcart was loaded badly.

This was not criticism.

It was simply loaded in a way that made the little blue bundle sit on
top of a lamp tin while one costume basket leaned against the side and
the good stool had been tied upright like a passenger.

Pell stood beside it holding the rope.

"I did that."

"I know."

"How?"

"The stool looks afraid."

Jori came out carrying another lamp.

"Move."

We moved.

The second bell rang while we were tying the last cloth over the cart.

Rinna counted heads.

Marek, Sellen, Hara, Veya, Evin, Nessa, Pell, Jori, Davin, Teren, me.

She counted again.

"Where's the gate?"

Teren said, "Not coming."

I looked at him.

"You decided."

"Yes."

"When?"

"Last night."

"Without me."

"Especially."

"How do I complain about a gate without the gate?"

Teren looked at me.

"The same way you complain about a wife without the wife."

Pell laughed hard enough to lose the end of the rope.

I pointed at Teren.

"That was almost instruction."

"Pull."

We pulled.

The cart went west.

Carrow changed slowly as we walked.

The market streets narrowed first, then opened again where the houses
grew wider and the shop fronts became less crowded. There were still
carts, children, laundry, dogs, people shouting from windows. The
difference was that some of the doors had polished knockers and more of
the upper windows had glass.

The Havel house did not look like a palace.

It looked like somebody had paid to keep repairing it before anything
broke.

Two broad windows faced the street. A narrow covered passage ran beside
the main door. We were sent through that.

Of course we were.

A woman in a dark apron met Rinna before the cart was fully inside.

"You're late."

Rinna looked toward the street.

"We were told second bell."

"Guests came early."

"That doesn't move the bell."

The woman stared at her.

Rinna smiled just enough to keep the money alive.

"I'm Sera. House steward."

"Rinna."

"I know."

Good.

They were going to get along terribly.

The passage opened into a rectangular courtyard.

The awning covered most of it, pale cloth stretched from the house wall
to three wooden poles along the far side. Tables ran along the left,
right, and back edges, leaving an open rectangle in the middle.

A smaller rectangle than promised.

Much smaller.

Guests had not arrived yet, but the tables had.

So had chairs.

Very expensive chairs.

I recognized one.

I pointed.

"Enemy."

Davin caught my wrist before I touched it.

"I wasn't."

"You were pointing too close."

The household wall lamps were already lit even though there was still
daylight above the awning. Four servants moved between courtyard and
kitchen passage carrying cups, folded cloths, bowls, and things that
smelled better than anything I had eaten that week.

Roast onions.

Meat.

Bread.

Something sweet with spice.

Lyssa had told me to eat after.

This suddenly felt unreasonable.

Sera walked Rinna around the playing space.

"Here."

Rinna looked at the floor.

"That is not what you marked."

"We added the small table."

"There are three small tables."

"Family."

"Family can watch from farther away."

Sera looked offended.

Rinna looked employed.

They compromised by moving exactly one chair.

Not a table.

The chair belonged to the house, so Sera moved it herself.

I respected the legal distinction.

The company spread into the only place left.

Backstage became a service passage beside the kitchen, screened from the
courtyard by a hanging woven panel. It was wide enough for two people if
neither person had elbows.

I had elbows.

So did everyone else.

Nessa looked once and reduced us further.

"One basket."

"We brought two," Pell said.

"Now we have one here."

The second basket went back to the cart.

Davin brought the stool in.

Teren looked at the open playing space, then at the stool.

"No throne."

Marek said, "I'm the king."

"You can stand."

"Kings sit."

"Not tonight."

Marek looked at Rinna.

Rinna was still negotiating with Sera.

He looked at the stool.

Then at the expensive Havel chairs.

"No."

"Good," Davin said.

The stool went behind the screen for something else.

Our four lamps were set along the open side, two low, two higher on
simple stands.

Jori lit none yet.

The wall lamps were enough until dusk.

"No smoke," Rinna called.

Jori held up empty hands.

"I know."

Pell said, "We could make very little smoke."

"No."

"I wasn't suggesting."

"You were becoming a suggestion."

I carried the little blue bundle into the service passage and set it
against the wall.

A kitchen worker came through carrying a pot large enough to wash a
child in.

"Move."

I moved.

She passed.

I put the bundle back.

Another kitchen worker came through with a tray.

"Move."

I moved again.

"Where am I supposed to put this?"

Nessa pointed without looking.

There was a narrow shelf above a stack of folded serving cloths.

"That's theirs."

"The shelf isn't."

I put the bundle on it.

Sera appeared at the passage entrance.

"Keep this clear."

"I have received that information."

She looked at the crutches.

Then at me.

"Can you?"

"Mostly."

She left.

Pell said, "Mostly."

"Accurate."

Teren had settled the bill.

The King first.

Broken Gate second.

No gate.

"How broken is it without a gate?" I asked.

"Very," Hara said.

"Good."

We walked the courtyard once.

Not rehearsed.

Walked.

The right entrance at the hall did not exist.

The kitchen passage was now one side. The street passage was the other.
The audience was close enough that if I extended my crutch badly I could
collect a guest.

Teren moved three entrances.

Mine became kitchen side.

"Neighbor comes from there."

"Through the kitchen?"

"Tonight."

"Where does my house live?"

"Outside."

"Which outside?"

Teren pointed.

"That one."

There were four walls.

I accepted one.

Nessa put Marek in the King coat and immediately pulled him back.

"No."

"What?"

"Collar."

"It is my collar."

"It is your sweat."

She changed the inner cloth at the neck.

Guests would be closer than the hall audience. Apparently this made
Marek's neck a public concern.

Sellen watched.

"I never had that problem."

"You had other problems," Nessa said.

He accepted this.

The guests began arriving before we were ready.

Not into seats.

Into dinner.

They entered talking, sat talking, continued talking while food arrived.
Cups knocked against tables. A man near the back laughed at something
that had nothing to do with us.

A child sat on the edge of a step until a woman pulled him into a chair.

Nobody looked at the lamps.

Nobody looked at the screen.

Nobody cared that a king was changing clothes ten feet from their soup.

I liked them.

They were honest.

Rinna came through the passage.

"Earlier."

Teren said, "How much?"

"Now."

"We have ten minutes."

"We have four."

"Why?"

"Fish."

I looked toward the courtyard.

"Fish did this?"

"The fish finished early."

Sera appeared behind Rinna.

"Master Havel would like to begin before the meat."

Teren looked at Rinna.

Rinna looked at the courtyard.

"Five."

Sera said, "Four."

Rinna said, "Five."

Sera left.

"Was that negotiation?" I asked.

"Coat."

Nessa shoved my green Neighbor coat into my chest.

"I don't need this until second."

"Put it where you can find it."

"That seems sensible."

"Don't become frightened."

The King started in five minutes.

Barely.

Marek stood instead of sitting.

The ledger had come.

The stool had not.

Hara and Evin were ready.

Sellen had a smaller court role that did not require Marek's coat, which
improved everyone.

I stayed behind the screen with Jori and the props.

The first lines competed with plates.

Not badly.

Just physically.

A spoon fell.

Someone asked for salt.

A servant crossed the far edge with a covered dish.

Marek waited half a breath before entering because the dish was hot and
the servant had priority.

Then he stepped into the open space.

"Bring me the first complaint."

Three people at the left table were still talking.

Hara entered.

By her second line, two had stopped.

The third was eating.

This seemed fair.

The King worked.

Not like the hall.

The laughs came from different places.

One table reacted quickly. Another seemed to need permission from an
older woman at the end before anyone laughed. A boy near the step
laughed at Evin dropping a rolled paper even though Evin had not meant
to drop it.

Evin looked at the paper.

Then at the boy.

"Evidence."

The boy laughed harder.

Marek said, "Pick up my government."

That got the adults.

Backstage, Pell whispered, "He said evidence."

"Don't."

"Hessa."

"Don't."

The strongest moment came later from Hara.

A guest at the right table said, very clearly, "He's right," while Marek
was refusing a petition.

Hara stopped.

She turned her head toward the voice.

Not all the way.

Just enough.

"Thank you."

The courtyard laughed.

Marek looked at the guest.

"You are not helping."

Bigger laugh.

The guest raised his cup.

Hara returned to Marek.

"One citizen has understood me."

"Then take him with you."

The guest's wife said, "Please."

That was the biggest laugh of the first piece.

Hara waited.

Marek waited.

Then Hara said, "No."

The whole courtyard went.

Behind the screen, Sellen pressed his fist to his mouth.

Teren looked pleased for approximately half a second, then pointed at
the ledger because it was in the wrong place.

I moved it.

The King ended.

We had almost no transition.

Sweets were apparently advancing.

This became obvious when two kitchen workers arrived in the passage
carrying trays.

"Move."

I moved.

Pell moved.

Jori moved a lamp stand.

Nessa did not move because Nessa was pinning Hara's sleeve.

The kitchen workers passed through us like weather.

I reached for my green coat.

It was not there.

I stared at the hook.

Nessa said, "Shelf."

I looked up.

The little blue bundle was there.

The coat was beside it.

I had put it there myself after she told me to put it where I could find
it.

This was insulting.

I put it on.

Broken Gate had no gate.

It had one narrow painted post because Teren had apparently compromised
with himself after saying no gate.

Davin held it upright long enough to decide where it went, then tied it
to a simple base.

From my side it was a post.

From the audience side it was also a post.

Nobody had promised more.

"That's the gate?" I asked.

Teren said, "Tonight."

I was beginning to hate that word.

The second piece started while plates were being cleared.

Veya entered.

Evin followed.

The post survived.

My entrance came from the kitchen side.

I almost went the other way.

Not onstage.

Before.

I turned toward the street passage from habit, took one step, saw Pell's
face, and stopped.

He pointed behind me.

"Tonight."

I pointed at him.

"You're enjoying this."

"Yes."

I reversed.

No one in the courtyard saw.

I entered from the kitchen side.

"Whose cart is blocking my lane?"

The first response was scattered.

A few laughs.

Someone was still moving a plate.

Evin answered.

I continued.

By the time I reached the wife line, the right table had settled.

"My wife says move it."

"Tell your wife I am considering it."

"I did. She said consider faster."

The older woman at the end of the left table laughed first.

Then the rest.

Good wife.

She traveled.

I left through the correct side.

Behind the screen, a kitchen worker handed me a bowl.

I took it.

She stared.

I stared.

"This isn't mine."

"No."

"Why did you give it to me?"

"You were there."

I handed it back.

She carried it away.

Pell whispered, "Professional."

"Shut up."

The later gate collapse was easier because there was almost no gate to
collapse.

Davin tipped the post.

That was the entire disaster.

I returned.

"I leave for ten minutes."

Hara said, "It was twelve."

"Then this is my fault."

The laugh came quickly.

Not huge.

Enough.

The piece continued.

The sweets arrived before we finished.

Not on the tables.

At the passage.

Rows of small pastries dusted with sugar and something brown.

I could smell them.

I had never hated theatre more.

Veya finished the last exchange.

Evin took the blame.

I blamed drainage.

The audience laughed.

We bowed.

Not deeply.

There was nowhere to go.

Then we got out of the way because the pastries were more important.

This was correct.

Packing was faster than setup because everyone wanted food.

The Havel furniture remained untouched.

I would like that recorded.

Not one chair.

Not one table.

The expensive sample chair had survived our entire relationship.

Jori cooled the lamps.

Davin untied the post.

Pell carried the costume basket to the cart.

I put the little blue bundle in the kitchen passage.

"Move."

I picked it up immediately.

The same kitchen worker from earlier looked at me.

"Learning."

"Slowly."

She pointed to the shelf.

I put it there until the passage cleared.

No one congratulated me.

Rinna disappeared with Sera.

They were gone long enough for Pell to become convinced we had been
cheated.

"We haven't been paid."

"The job ended thirty seconds ago."

"That's when payment happens."

"Sometimes people count."

"What?"

"Money."

He looked troubled.

Marek came past carrying the King coat over one arm.

"You worry too early."

"I worry efficiently."

Sellen said, "He saves time later."

Rinna returned with a small purse.

Pell stopped worrying so efficiently.

She paid people one by one.

When she reached me, four copper dropped into my palm.

I counted once.

Four.

Real.

Earned.

Still copper.

Still four.

"Whole job," Rinna said.

"I did the whole job."

"You did."

"I also protected several chairs."

"You didn't touch them."

"Exactly."

"Four."

"Kitchen passage compliance improved over time."

"Four."

"I was given a bowl."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

Rinna stared at me.

"Four."

I put the coins away.

Four was enough.

Then we ate.

Not at the guest tables.

Nobody had promised that.

The kitchen set out food for us in the back passage and near the cart
once the household had moved on to sweets and louder conversation.

There was bread.

A thick stew with meat scraps and onions.

Cooked greens.

Two plates of broken pastries that were apparently too ugly for the
courtyard.

They looked excellent.

Pell reached for two.

Nessa slapped his wrist.

"One."

"There are enough."

"One first."

"This is oppression."

"Eat."

We ate.

Marek had two bowls of stew.

Sellen accused him of storing food for the next northern journey.

Marek said, "My sister fed me."

"Then why are you eating?"

"I'm here now."

Hara took a pastry.

Then another after everyone had one.

Pell saw.

"Law."

Hara bit it.

"No."

Veya sat on the cart edge with her shoes loosened and argued with Teren
about which table had laughed at the gate.

"There was no gate," I said.

"There was a post," Teren said.

"That's not a gate."

"It was enough."

I looked at him.

He was already eating.

I decided not to ask anything that might become education.

Jori said the wall lamps had made ours unnecessary until halfway through
King.

Pell said that meant we carried four for nothing.

Jori said, "We used them."

"Later."

"That's still use."

Davin drank water and said nothing because Davin was a better person
than us.

Rinna came out with one last wrapped pastry.

She looked at the group.

Everyone looked at her.

"No."

She handed it to me.

I took it.

Pell looked betrayed.

"Why?"

Rinna said, "He asked yesterday if they fed us."

"That doesn't explain anything."

"Take it home."

I looked at the wrapped pastry.

"For Lyssa?"

Rinna was already walking away.

I did not know whether this meant yes.

I decided yes was safer than eating it.

The walk home was slower.

The cart was lighter.

We were heavier.

The west streets had gone dark except for lamps over doors and light
from upper windows. Carrow smelled less like dinner as we moved east and
more like wet stone, old smoke, drains, horses, and people closing
shops.

Nobody followed us.

Nobody watched from a doorway.

Nobody delivered a message.

We were tired because we had worked.

At the hall we unloaded what needed unloading.

The costume basket went inside.

The lamps went back.

The post leaned against the wall.

The little blue bundle returned to the same place it had started.

I looked at it.

Pell said, "What?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

Everyone had learned the word.

Rinna said, "Third bell tomorrow."

I stared at her.

"Tomorrow?"

"Yes."

"I worked today."

"That is usually how tomorrow works."

"Guild might send."

"Then tell me."

The schedule survived private employment.

I went home with four copper and one pastry.

Lyssa was awake.

Barely.

She sat on the bed with one foot tucked under her and a piece of thread
between her fingers.

I held out the wrapped pastry.

"I brought you something."

She looked at it.

"Did you steal it?"

"No."

"Good."

"There it is again."

She took the pastry.

"What is it?"

"I don't know."

She unwrapped it.

It was a small folded sweet with nuts inside.

Lyssa bit it.

Then looked at me.

"You ate?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"Stew. Bread. Greens. Pastry."

She narrowed her eyes.

"How much pastry?"

"One."

"Good."

"Stop."

She ate another bite.

I put the four copper on the table.

Not all my money.

Just today's pay.

Lyssa looked at it.

"Four."

"Current evidence supports that."

She pointed the pastry at me.

I stopped.

"Four," I said.

"Better."

"The chairs survived."

"I'm proud of them."

"I didn't touch any."

"Growth."

"Don't."

She asked about the job.

I told her the courtyard had been smaller than promised, the fish had
finished early, the kitchen passage hated me, Marek had been a standing
king, the gate had become one post, Hara had recruited a citizen, and I
had briefly been given a bowl for being present.

Lyssa listened until I got to the bowl.

"Why?"

"I don't know."

"Useful."

"That's what I said."

"No, you didn't."

"I thought it."

There was still nothing under the cup.

I did not check again.

Tomorrow was third bell unless Rinna sent earlier.

Hessa still had an intermediate distance somewhere in her head.

The Havel house had its chairs.

I had four copper.

Lyssa had half a pastry.

I had eaten.

For a private engagement, everything important had survived.
