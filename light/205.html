TEXT READER · CHAPTER 205

THE PORTER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The soup was better the second day.

This was suspicious.

I ate bread dipped into what remained of the broth while Lyssa stood by
the basin tying her hair back. There was less meat than yesterday
because we had eaten it. There were more onions because onions
understood survival.

The new wick burned cleanly in the lamp even though daylight made the
lamp unnecessary.

I had lit it anyway.

Lyssa looked at it.

"We have oil."

"I know."

"It's morning."

"I know."

She waited.

"I wanted to see if the wick worked."

"It did last night."

"That was one observation."

She pointed her spoon at me.

I blew out the lamp.

"Hessa has damaged this household."

"Hessa isn't here."

"That's how extensive the damage is."

My wrist was boring.

I moved it while reaching for the bread and did not think about it until
after. That seemed like the proper definition of boring. My shoulders
were less tired. My good leg still knew about Havel House, but only in
the way legs remembered work when stairs appeared.

The note remained under the cup.

TWO DAYS had become tomorrow without anyone rewriting it.

I did not lift it.

I knew what it said.

Lyssa picked up her bundle.

"Paper tomorrow?"

"After midday."

"Theatre?"

"Third bell today."

"Tomorrow?"

"I have to tell Rinna."

"So you don't know."

"I know one half."

"That's not a schedule."

"You're becoming Rinna."

Lyssa opened the door.

"Go to work."

"It isn't third bell."

"Then eventually go to work."

She left.

I had enough time to clean the bowl, not enough desire to sweep, and no
household purchase requiring intervention.

I considered visiting the hall early.

Then I remembered Rinna had created the third-bell system specifically
to prevent me from appearing whenever anxiety became employment.

I stayed home.

This was becoming a skill.

By the time I left, Carrow had warmed into the ordinary part of the day.

A man was repairing a basket on a doorstep. A woman carried three live
chickens by their feet and told one of them to stop complaining. It did
not. Two boys rolled a hoop down the lane until it struck a barrel, at
which point they blamed the barrel.

I understood their legal position.

The hall door was open when third bell rang.

I entered on the last strike.

Rinna was changing the board.

"Guild tomorrow," I said.

She kept writing.

"After midday."

She erased WIDOW.

I waited.

"Did you hear me?"

"Yes."

"Good."

"Tomorrow, first bell."

I looked at her.

"That's earlier."

"Yes."

"Because Guild?"

"Yes."

"This system has become hostile."

Rinna wrote GREG beneath the morning line.

"First bell. Set. Then leave before midday."

"What happens after Guild?"

"If you're useful, come back."

"How will I know?"

"You'll be here."

That did not answer anything.

It also probably did.

I looked at today's board.

KING: MAREK / HARA / EVIN

BORROWED HOUSE: VEYA / EVIN / PELL / GREG

SHORT: SELLEN / MAREK

WIDOW had been erased.

Under it, in smaller writing, Rinna had added:

IF HARA.

I pointed.

"What does that mean?"

"It means if Hara."

"That's not a sentence."

"It is today."

Hara had outside work.

No one knew when she would finish.

If she arrived in time, Widow would go last.

If not, it would not.

This was apparently enough information to operate a theatre.

My line said:

GREG: PORTER / SET

I knew Porter.

Not perfectly.

Enough.

The Porter in The Borrowed House brought a chest to the wrong house and
then spent several lines refusing to accept that the address had become
his problem.

I had played him before.

I had liked him.

He had procedural integrity.

Rinna handed me a stack of tickets.

"Front."

"Porter."

"Later."

"Set."

"Also later."

"One copper?"

She looked at me.

"I was confirming."

"No."

I went to the front.

Davin had the bent lamp cap on his work table.

It was round again.

Mostly.

I stopped.

"Fixed?"

"Yes."

I reached.

"No."

My hand stopped in the air.

"I was going to look."

"You are looking."

"I was going to look closer."

"No."

He picked up the lamp tin himself, turned the cap once, and set it on
the normal shelf.

"Fixed."

"Thank you for the demonstration."

Davin returned to his tools.

Pell came past carrying the Borrowed House chest.

The chest had been repaired recently too.

I did not touch it.

Davin looked at me anyway.

"I didn't."

"Good."

"This reputation is oppressive."

Pell shifted the chest.

"Help."

"I thought I wasn't allowed to touch repaired things."

"You're allowed to carry them."

"That seems inconsistent."

"Other side."

I took the lighter end because Pell had already angled it that way.

We moved the chest to the wing.

The Borrowed House set was familiar enough that most of the work
happened without anyone explaining it.

Painted wall.

Chair.

Small table.

Chest.

A cloth loaf that was not currently involved in anything and therefore
made me nervous.

I left it alone.

Teren arrived, looked at the board, looked at me, and said, "Porter."

"Yes."

"Entrance?"

"Left."

"Today?"

He looked annoyed.

I smiled.

"Left."

"Chest?"

"With Pell until wing. Then me for the last steps."

"Lines?"

"Six."

"Five."

I frowned.

"Which one died?"

"The address line."

"Why?"

"Too long."

"That line explains why I'm there."

"No one cares why you're there."

"I care."

"Five."

He walked away.

This was rehearsal.

I found my page.

Five lines.

The crossed-out address line had once been useful to me. I mourned
privately.

The first early audience member arrived while the board still said King
first.

He was a narrow man with a red scarf who asked what opened.

"The King."

"Good."

He bought a seat.

Then Evin came in through the rear door and told Rinna he had to leave
before the second bell after the show because his cousin had a cart
waiting.

Rinna looked at the board.

Teren looked at the board.

Sellen looked at Evin.

"Why?"

"Cousin."

"That isn't why."

"It's enough."

Apparently everyone had learned Rinna's grammar.

The bill changed.

Borrowed House moved first because Evin needed to leave.

King moved second.

Short stayed third.

Widow remained IF HARA.

I looked toward the door.

The man with the red scarf had already gone inside.

I found him in the second row.

"Correction."

He looked up.

"Borrowed House opens."

He shrugged.

"Fine."

"I previously said King."

"I remember."

"The board changed."

"Will there still be a king?"

"Yes."

"Fine."

He returned to eating roasted seeds.

I stood there another second.

He looked up again.

"Anything else?"

"No."

This was the correct amount of concern.

At the rear, Nessa was holding my Porter coat.

It was brown.

Everything was brown eventually.

I put it on.

She pulled the collar flat.

"Other side."

"What?"

"Fasten other side."

I had started the wrong hook.

I changed it.

"How did I wear this before?"

"Badly."

"That seems unsupported."

Nessa walked away.

The house filled to ordinary.

Not full.

Enough.

Warm bodies made the room warmer than the street. Someone had brought
onions wrapped in paper. Someone else had brought a baby who was
currently asleep and therefore the most professional person in the
building.

I worked front until Rinna took the tickets from me.

"Go."

Borrowed House first.

I got to the wing.

Pell had the chest.

"Ready?"

"Yes."

"Don't drop it."

"I wasn't planning to."

"Good plan."

Veya and Evin were already onstage arguing about the house.

The premise remained stupid.

Evin had borrowed a house from someone who had apparently not intended
the phrase literally enough. Veya believed the furniture inside proved
ownership. Pell, playing the neighbor, believed all disputes should
happen outside his door.

My cue came.

Pell and I moved the chest to the edge.

Then I took the visible part of the weight for the last few steps.

Both hands on the chest.

Crutches staged where I could reach them after.

Good foot.

Step.

Set.

No heroics.

No wrist complaint.

I delivered the first line.

"Delivery for this house."

Nothing.

Evin said, "Which house?"

"This one."

Small laugh.

He pointed at the painted wall.

"This isn't my house."

"I was told this house."

"By whom?"

The old address line would have answered this.

It was dead.

I used the current line.

"By the man who paid me."

That got a better laugh.

Veya said, "Then take it back."

"I was paid to bring it."

"Then you've brought it."

"Yes."

"Good."

I stood.

The Porter had no further solution.

That was the joke.

The audience understood before the characters did.

My next line got almost nothing.

Then Pell opened the chest from behind and found it empty.

His face did more work than the line.

The house laughed.

I kept being a Porter.

Eventually I left.

Backstage, I reached for my crutches.

Nessa was holding one.

"You left it too far."

"I staged it."

"You staged it for someone with two legs."

I took it.

"That seems discriminatory."

"Move."

King had to set.

No lesson.

No discussion.

I changed out of the Porter coat while Pell and Jori shifted the chest.

Marek was already in his King coat.

Hara was not there.

Rinna looked once toward the rear door and then stopped looking.

King went on without caring.

I carried the small table off and put the King stool on.

Correct stool.

I checked.

Not experimentally.

With my eyes.

The King ran cleanly.

Marek's first complaint got a laugh.

Evin, now playing a petitioner before leaving for his cousin's cart,
rushed one line badly enough that Marek stared at him.

"Are you fleeing?"

"Eventually."

That was not the line.

The audience laughed.

Marek said, "Then state your problem before it escapes with you."

Evin slowed down.

The scene continued.

Not the strongest moment.

That came from Sellen in the short piece.

Hara still had not arrived.

Rinna erased IF HARA.

Widow disappeared.

No announcement.

No apology.

The audience had not been promised it by name.

They got Short instead.

Sellen and Marek were doing the hat piece again, but one of the hats had
lost its ribbon.

Nessa had removed the loose ribbon before the show rather than let it
fall off during.

Sellen came on, saw Marek's hat, looked at his own bare hat, and said,
"Yours has rank."

Marek said, "Yours has survived."

Sellen took off his hat, inspected it, and put it back on backward.

The audience laughed.

He continued the scene with it backward.

Marek did not mention it.

That made it worse.

By the time Sellen finally discovered the problem through a polished
serving plate used as a mirror, half the audience had been waiting for
him to notice.

He stared at himself.

Then at Marek.

"You knew."

Marek said, "I am the king."

Biggest laugh.

Sellen bowed with the backward hat.

"Then perhaps call more clearly."

I made a noise backstage.

Marek looked toward the wing for half a second.

Sellen nearly broke.

They finished.

Afterward Sellen came off and pointed at me.

"Yours."

"No."

"You said it."

"Yesterday."

"Still yours."

"You stole my line."

"I improved its position."

"Current evidence..."

He raised a hand.

"Don't."

Good.

Hara arrived while the audience was leaving.

She came through the rear door carrying a narrow paper-wrapped parcel
and wearing a dark outer coat I had not seen before.

Rinna looked at her.

"Done?"

"No."

"Then why are you here?"

"Because I thought I could make Widow."

"You couldn't."

"I see that."

Hara looked at the erased board.

"Good."

That was the entire crisis.

She put the parcel under her own things.

Pell immediately looked at it.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"You were going to."

"I was going to look."

Hara stared.

Pell left.

I did not ask what was in it.

This was spreading.

Evin had already gone.

No one said goodbye because his cousin apparently had a cart and that
was enough.

Cleanup began.

The repaired lamp tin worked.

Jori filled it only partway because it did not need more.

Davin watched the cap once and then stopped caring.

The Borrowed House chest went back to storage.

I carried one end.

The cloth loaf remained untouched.

This felt like an achievement I could not bill.

At the front, the red-scarf man passed me on his way out.

"King was good."

"Borrowed House opened."

"I noticed."

"I corrected you."

"You did."

"Good."

He left.

I was beginning to understand why Rinna did not explain herself to
customers.

Hara unwrapped her parcel enough to reveal two small wooden handles.

I still did not know what they were for.

She handed one to Nessa.

Nessa said, "Wrong size."

Hara said something rude under her breath and wrapped them again.

Their lives continued without my understanding.

I swept.

Pell and Jori argued over a rope coil.

Pell said Jori had coiled it backward.

Jori asked which end of a circle was forward.

Pell said that was not the point.

I stayed out of it because tomorrow I had a professional appointment
involving directions and did not need recreational ones.

Rinna paid me one copper.

I took it.

No negotiation.

She looked at me.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You look ill."

"I've matured."

"No."

I put the copper away.

"Tomorrow. First bell."

"Set."

"Yes."

"Then I leave before midday."

"Yes."

"Guild after midday."

"I know."

"And after?"

Rinna was counting something on her fingers.

"Come back if you can still stand."

"That's not a time."

"It's not a promise."

Fair.

I went home.

The room smelled like yesterday's soup because tonight's dinner was
yesterday's soup with more water and the second onion.

Lyssa was not home yet.

The lamp lit easily.

I did not need to trim the wick three times.

I put Hessa's note on top of the cup instead of under it.

Tomorrow.

Not because the note had changed.

Because the day had.

Lyssa came in after dark with nothing in her hands.

This was unusual enough that I looked.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You usually have cloth."

"I put it down elsewhere."

"Can you do that?"

"Apparently."

She took off her outer layer and sat.

I gave her soup.

She tasted it.

"More water."

"Yes."

"Less meat."

"Time."

"Still good."

We ate.

I told her Hara had arrived too late for her own play and seemed
pleased.

Lyssa said, "Maybe she didn't want to do it."

"Then why come?"

"Work."

"That explains nothing."

"It explains most things."

I told her Evin left for a cousin's cart.

Sellen stole my line.

Marek helped.

The Porter remained committed to delivery.

Rinna had scheduled me first bell.

Lyssa stopped eating.

"Tomorrow?"

"Yes."

"I thought Guild was after midday."

"It is."

"So theatre first."

"Yes."

"Then Guild."

"Yes."

"And maybe theatre again."

"Apparently."

She looked at me.

"So everyone knows your schedule."

"I told you I knew it."

"What are you doing at Guild?"

"Intermediate."

"Away."

"Yes."

"Same apparatus."

"Yes."

"Same restrictions."

I narrowed my eyes.

"You've been reading my mail."

"You read it out loud."

"That is a security flaw."

Lyssa ate another spoonful.

"Are you excited?"

"A little."

"Nervous?"

"A little."

"Good."

"Why is that good?"

"Because if you said no to either, you'd be lying."

I considered arguing.

I did not.

The note sat on the cup.

I did not touch it again.

Twenty-eight draws.

Twenty-four shaping attempts.

Twenty-seven external attempts.

Those numbers had not changed today.

Today I had carried a chest, worn a brown coat, given one customer
outdated information, watched a play disappear because Hara had another
life, earned one copper, and come home for watered soup.

Tomorrow I had theatre at first bell.

After midday, paper.

For once, both jobs had agreed on the order.
