LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 206

THE SETTER

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

First bell was offensive.

It was not early in any absolute sense. Bakers had been awake. Carters
had been awake. Somewhere, people with livestock had probably been awake
long enough to develop opinions about livestock.

It was early for theatre.

I arrived while Pell was opening the rear shutters.

He looked at me.

"You live here now?"

"Rinna told me first bell."

"That doesn't answer the question."

Rinna was already inside.

She had three benches pulled crooked across the floor, two folded cloths
on the front table, and the expression of someone who had arranged work
specifically so no one could claim not to see it.

"You're here," she said.

"I was scheduled."

"Good."

"That's almost praise."

"Bench."

I took the near end of the first one.

Pell took the other.

We moved it two rows forward because the afternoon bill needed a wider
rear path. Then we moved another one back because apparently the first
movement had revealed a different problem.

"Why didn't we leave the first one?" I asked.

Rinna pointed.

There was a floor mark under it.

"What is that?"

"Where the Widow table goes."

"Widow didn't play yesterday."

"Today isn't yesterday."

This institution had a phrase for everything.

By the time Nessa arrived, we had shifted six benches, moved the
Borrowed House chest to the rear wall, and brought down a narrow painted
panel I did not remember seeing in at least a week.

Nessa looked at it.

"No."

Pell stopped carrying.

"What?"

"Wrong panel."

"It was beside the right panel."

"That doesn't make it right."

We put it back.

The right panel was behind it.

I had been at work less than an hour and had already moved furniture
twice for reasons that stopped existing after I moved it.

This seemed healthy preparation for Guild work.

Davin came in carrying a small wooden brace.

He fitted it under the edge of the King platform, pressed once, then
stood.

I watched.

He looked at me.

"I didn't move."

"You were thinking."

"I am allowed."

"Not about that."

He walked away.

My wrist was boring.

My shoulder was boring.

My hands were dirty.

Hessa would ask about all three.

Around midmorning, Rinna gave me bread because I had brought none.

I looked at it.

"Is this pay?"

"No."

"Then why?"

"Because you're leaving."

"People who leave don't need food?"

"Eat."

I ate.

Teren arrived, changed two names on the afternoon board, and asked why
the narrow painted panel was out.

"It isn't," Pell said.

Teren looked at the panel we had returned to storage.

"Good."

I finished the bread.

No one rehearsed me.

I had no role.

I moved a stool, two lamp tins, a basket of folded coats, and one bundle
Nessa refused to identify because I had asked while she was carrying
pins in her mouth.

Before midday, Rinna looked toward the light at the front door.

"Go."

"I'm going."

"Then go faster."

I picked up my crutches.

Pell said, "Paper."

"Eventually."

"Middle paper."

"Intermediate."

He stared at me.

"Hessa."

"Right."

I left before he could improve the experiment.

The Guild was busier than the theatre and quieter about it.

A woman at the front table had three small brass plates laid in a row
and was arguing that the middle one had not been scratched when she
brought it. The clerk had apparently not accused her of scratching it.

"I am only saying it wasn't."

"I know."

"It was polished."

"I know."

"Look."

"I am looking."

I passed them.

A boy carried a wooden case down the side corridor with both arms and
nearly walked into a man carrying a longer wooden case. They negotiated
this by stopping and looking offended.

The gray-coated woman was at a side table.

She saw me.

"Paper Man."

"Intermediate Paper Man."

She frowned.

"That sounds worse."

"It is more precise."

"That is worse."

I kept walking.

Hessa had the smaller room ready.

The same long pale board sat on the broad table.

The same chair was aligned to the chalk mark on the floor.

The glass shield waited beside matched folded fins.

The near and far positions were still marked on the board.

There was a third mark between them.

Not exactly halfway by my eye.

I pointed.

"Intermediate."

"Yes."

"Why isn't it halfway?"

"Halfway between what?"

"The other two."

"The marks."

"Those are positions, not a scale."

I looked at the board.

"Then how did you choose that?"

Hessa tapped one of the shallow board divisions.

"It is a fixed position between the previous two. We can reproduce it."

"That is a disappointing answer."

"It is the useful one."

She looked at my sleeves.

"Work?"

"Theatre."

"Since first bell?"

"Yes."

"What did you lift?"

"Benches. One end. Lamp tins. Stool. Coats. Wrong panel. Right panel."

"Pain?"

"No."

"Wrist?"

"Boring."

"Show me."

I rotated it.

She watched the full movement, then had me do it again more slowly.

"Shoulder?"

"Boring."

"That is not a measurement."

"It is becoming one."

She pressed lightly along the shoulder and upper arm, then had me lift
and lower the arm.

Nothing meaningful hurt.

My good leg was tired from standing.

My hands had the ordinary grit of set work in the creases.

Hessa wrote something.

"Eat?"

"Rinna forced bread on me."

"Water?"

I hesitated.

Hessa looked up.

"I drank this morning."

"How much?"

"Some."

She gave me water.

I drank it.

"This experiment has a lot of water."

"Your body has a lot of water."

"Currently."

She waited.

I drank more.

Then the room became familiar.

Crutches against the wall.

Good foot flat.

Chair on its mark.

Hands resting where she wanted them.

No bracing against the table.

Board fixed.

Target and control guides matched.

Glass shield.

Same paper stock.

Same folds.

Same requested direction.

Away from me.

Only the position had changed.

Intermediate.

Hessa pointed to the near mark, then the new one, then the far mark.

"We are not testing near."

"Yes."

"We are not testing far."

"Yes."

"We are not testing whether you can produce a larger movement."

"Yes."

"We are not testing a different object."

"I know."

"What are we testing?"

"Whether the paper at the intermediate position responds to the same
request."

"Good."

"That sounded like praise."

"It wasn't."

Everyone had coordinated.

We began with the draw.

The gathering came more easily than it had months ago, which did not
mean easily.

I settled.

Breathed.

Found the familiar inward pull without reaching after it.

There was the small heat behind my sternum, the sense of something
collecting where I had learned not to grab at it, and the familiar
unevenness near the wrist edge.

Hessa watched my hands and shoulders more than my face.

"Hold."

I held.

"Release."

I did.

She wrote.

Twenty-nine successful supervised minimal draws.

Nothing new.

That was apparently success.

The shaping attempt came next.

I gathered again only when told.

This time I tried to give the gathered thing the rough deliberate form
we had been working with.

It wanted to crowd toward the same wrist-side edge.

My shoulder tried to become involved.

I noticed.

Lowered it.

The shape softened.

I nearly chased it.

"Don't."

"I wasn't."

"You were about to."

"That is not evidence."

"It is when I can see you."

I let the shape remain ugly.

It held for two breaths.

Then I released.

Twenty-five supervised deliberate shaping attempts.

Also nothing new.

My reward was paper.

Hessa placed the first matched pair at the intermediate mark.

Target in its guide.

Control beside it.

Both under the glass.

I could see the cut edges clearly enough, but not as easily as at near.

Far had made the little fins feel almost absurdly remote.

This was not remote.

It was simply less close.

I disliked that this was now a meaningful category in my life.

"Same request," Hessa said.

"Away."

"Same effort."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"No."

"Good."

I looked at the target.

Not the control.

Not the glass.

Not Hessa.

I gathered only as much as I had been taught to gather.

The wrist-side pressure appeared.

I did not correct it.

I shaped.

Rough.

Small.

Deliberate enough.

Then I made the request.

Away.

Nothing happened immediately.

I held.

The lower cut edge shifted.

Not much.

It slid away from me by less than the width of the smallest part of my
fingernail, then stopped with the top edge turned slightly.

I released.

Hessa did not speak.

She leaned close to the board.

Then she moved around the table and looked from the other side.

She checked the control.

She checked the glass.

"Target displaced away."

I smiled.

"Small."

I stopped smiling less.

"Control unchanged."

"Intermediate."

"Attempt twenty-eight."

"Intermediate."

"Yes."

I wanted her to say more.

She did not.

She wrote.

The first response had been clean.

That was all it was allowed to be.

We rested.

I drank water.

Hessa had another sheet beside her notes with writing that had nothing
to do with me. She read part of it while I sat.

Someone knocked once.

A clerk opened the door far enough to ask about a prepared frame.

"Not mine," Hessa said.

"Whose?"

"Ask Soren."

"He's out."

"Then it remains not mine."

The door closed.

I liked the Guild more when it had other problems.

After the rest, Hessa replaced both fins.

Fresh matched pair.

Same intermediate position.

Same guides.

Same glass.

She checked my shoulder again.

"Fine."

"Wrist?"

"Fine."

"That is different from boring."

"Boring."

"Better."

Attempt twenty-nine.

I gathered.

The draw itself was not counted separately. We were inside the external
sequence now, working from the supervised state Hessa had already
approved.

The shape came rougher.

I backed off before it collapsed.

Hessa said nothing.

I requested away.

The paper remained exactly where it was.

I waited.

Nothing.

A tiny change in the shadow made my heart jump.

Then I realized I had shifted my head.

The paper had not moved.

I released.

"No observed response," Hessa said.

"I saw that."

"Good."

"Still not praise?"

"No."

She checked the control.

Unchanged.

Target unchanged.

Attempt twenty-nine was a failure.

I was annoyed.

Not surprised.

Annoyed was easier.

"Again?"

"Rest."

"I feel fine."

"That is why we rest before you don't."

I sat back.

My forearm had started to feel used.

Not pain.

The same kind of low complaint that came after holding something awkward
too long.

My shoulder was still quiet.

Hessa made me open and close my hand.

Then rotate the wrist.

"Anything?"

"Used."

"Where?"

I showed her.

She pressed once.

"Pain?"

"No."

"Good."

That one might have been praise.

I did not risk it.

We waited longer.

Outside, something metal hit the floor.

Someone swore.

Another voice said, "Don't pick that up."

The Guild continued being the Guild.

For attempt thirty, Hessa used another matched pair.

She checked the intermediate mark before placing them.

"Same."

"Same."

"No more because the last one failed."

"I know."

"No less because the first one worked."

"I know."

"Same request."

"Away."

I settled.

This time the shape formed cleaner at first and then tried to tighten.

I almost followed it.

Stopped.

Let it be smaller.

Hessa watched my shoulder.

I requested away.

The fin stayed still.

One breath.

Then the top edge turned.

It was not a slide like the first.

The bottom barely shifted at all.

The whole folded piece rotated a tiny amount so the far corner ended
farther from me than before.

I released immediately.

Hessa moved.

I did not.

She checked the target from both sides.

Then the control.

"Movement."

"Away?"

"Rotation with net displacement away."

"That is a very Hessa answer."

"It is an accurate answer."

"Control?"

"Unchanged."

I smiled again.

She let me.

For several seconds.

Then she wrote.

Attempt thirty.

Intermediate.

Response.

I looked at the far mark.

Hessa saw me.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"You looked."

"I am allowed to look."

"Not at that."

"Davin said the same thing this morning."

"About paper?"

"Wood."

"Good."

"I don't like that you two agree."

She put her pencil down.

"How does the forearm feel?"

"Used."

"Wrist?"

I rotated it.

The last part of the motion felt tighter than it had when we started.

Not hurt.

Tired.

"Shoulder?"

When I lifted it, there was a faint pull near the front.

Hessa nodded.

"Done."

"One more would make the pattern nicer."

"That is not a reason."

"It would if it worked."

"And if it did not?"

"Then the pattern would be worse."

"Exactly."

I considered this.

"You're protecting the pattern from me."

"I'm protecting the evidence from fatigue."

"And me?"

"Also you."

That was less funny.

I sat back.

Hessa wrote for a while.

I watched the three marks on the board.

Near.

Intermediate.

Far.

The middle one did not look important.

None of them did.

They were chalk and shallow grooves on a pale board.

At near, paper had responded repeatedly.

At far, it had not responded cleanly enough to establish anything.

Now, at the place between them, it had moved twice out of three
requests.

Microscopically.

Under glass.

In a room where I was not allowed to touch it.

This was somehow the most magic I had.

Hessa turned the sheet so I could read.

29TH SUCCESSFUL SUPERVISED MINIMAL DRAW.

25TH SUPERVISED DELIBERATE SHAPING ATTEMPT.

INTERMEDIATE POSITION.

ATTEMPT 28: AWAY RESPONSE OBSERVED. CONTROL UNCHANGED.

ATTEMPT 29: NO OBSERVED RESPONSE. CONTROL UNCHANGED.

ATTEMPT 30: ROTATIONAL RESPONSE WITH NET DISPLACEMENT AWAY. CONTROL
UNCHANGED.

Below that:

AWAY RESPONSE REPEATED AT INTERMEDIATE POSITION IN CURRENT APPARATUS.

INTERMEDIATE RESPONSE ESTABLISHED UNDER CURRENT CONDITIONS.

DISTANCE DEPENDENCE REMAINS SUPPORTED.

FAR RESPONSE REMAINS UNESTABLISHED.

NO RANGE ESTABLISHED.

NOT GENERALIZED.

I read it twice.

"So it reaches farther than near."

"It responded at the intermediate position."

"That is what I said."

"No."

"It is physically farther."

"Yes."

"And it responded."

"Yes."

"So."

"So we have evidence of response at that position."

I pointed at the sentence.

"You wrote established."

"Under current conditions."

"Which are the conditions."

"Current ones."

I looked at her.

She looked at me.

"Hessa."

"Greg."

I sat back.

"I can move paper somewhat less close to me."

"Under controlled conditions."

"Twice."

"Today."

"Out of three."

"Yes."

"This is exhausting."

"Good."

"That one was definitely praise."

"It wasn't."

She took a fresh sheet.

I watched her write two words.

INTERMEDIATE / FAR.

Then:

AWAY ONLY.

SAME APPARATUS.

I leaned forward.

"Next?"

"Possible next comparison."

"Possible?"

"I want today's record clean before deciding whether we repeat
intermediate alone or compare it with far."

"You just wrote compare."

"I wrote the question."

"That seems like deciding."

"It isn't."

I had begun to suspect precision was mostly a weapon.

"No date?"

"No date."

"Restrictions?"

She looked at me.

"Same."

"All of them?"

"All of them."

No independent draw.

No independent shaping.

No external testing.

No paper.

No coins.

No thread.

No larger objects.

No practice.

No loopholes I had not thought of yet.

I nodded.

Hessa looked at my forearm.

"No lifting for the rest of today."

"I have theatre."

"You had theatre."

"I might have theatre."

"No lifting."

"Can I stand?"

"Yes."

"Rinna said come back if I can still stand."

"Then your theatre manager has created a poor medical standard."

"She's not my manager."

"What is she?"

I thought about it.

"Rinna."

Hessa accepted this.

I left the Guild with thirty external attempts behind me and absolutely
no permission to move anything interesting.

The gray-coated woman was gone.

The brass-plate argument had ended.

One of the three plates remained on the clerk's table with a scrap of
paper beside it.

I did not know whether it had been scratched before.

This bothered me less than it would have earlier.

I went back to the hall.

Not because I intended to work.

Because Rinna had said come back if I could still stand, and technically
I could.

The afternoon show had started.

I stood at the rear door.

Rinna looked at me.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"Can you carry?"

"Hessa says no lifting."

"Then no."

"I can do tickets."

"Done."

"I can count."

"Already counted."

"I can stand."

"I can see that."

"Decoratively."

Rinna considered me.

"No pay."

"Then I'm going home."

"Good."

Pell passed behind her carrying a stool.

He saw me.

"Middle paper?"

"Twice."

He stopped.

"How many?"

"Three."

"Good."

"Don't."

He kept moving.

I went home.

Lyssa was not there.

The soup was finally gone.

This was probably for the best.

I washed my hands, drank water because Hessa had infected that part of
my life too, and sat with my forearm resting across my lap.

It was tired.

Not hurt.

My wrist had gone from boring to mildly opinionated and would probably
return to boring if I stopped asking it questions.

I did.

Lyssa came home near dark with a small loaf under one arm.

"You're early."

"Technically."

She set the bread down.

"Guild?"

"Yes."

"Intermediate?"

"Yes."

"Did it move?"

"Twice."

She looked at me.

"How many tries?"

"Three."

I narrowed my eyes.

"What?"

"You asked the correct question."

"I've met you."

"One did nothing."

"And two moved."

"At the intermediate position, under the current apparatus, away from
me, with controls unchanged."

Lyssa was quiet.

"I wasn't going to correct you."

"You were thinking it."

"I was thinking you sound like Hessa."

"That's worse."

She cut the bread.

I told her the first one had slipped.

The second had done nothing.

The third had turned.

I showed the amount with my fingers and then made the gap smaller
because I had exaggerated.

Lyssa looked at the gap.

"That much?"

"Less."

She made it smaller.

"Probably."

She looked at me.

"Magic."

"Apparently."

"What next?"

"Maybe intermediate and far."

"Maybe?"

"Hessa wrote it and then claimed writing it wasn't deciding."

"That sounds like Hessa."

"No date."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"You have work."

"So does she."

"Exactly."

We ate bread with the last onion fried in the bottom of the pot and a
little salt.

No meat.

No pastry.

No private courtyard.

The lamp burned steadily.

Twenty-nine successful supervised minimal draws.

Twenty-five supervised deliberate shaping attempts.

Thirty supervised external-effect attempts.

The paper had moved at the intermediate position twice.

Far still had not established anything.

I had no range.

I had no new permission.

I had no spell.

I had spent the morning moving benches and the afternoon moving paper
almost nowhere.

It had been a productive day.

Annoyingly, both jobs could prove it.
