LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 207

THE SERVANT

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

My wrist was boring again.

This was disappointing only because I had spent enough time discussing
it that I felt it should have developed a personality.

I rotated it once over breakfast.

Lyssa saw.

"Done?"

"Done."

"Good."

There was nothing under the cup.

I did not check twice.

There was also no soup, no meat, and only the end of yesterday's loaf.

Ordinary life had recovered from Havel House.

Lyssa left before me with a folded strip of dark cloth over one shoulder
and no explanation. I did not ask. I had learned that asking about cloth
produced more cloth.

I waited for third bell.

This remained harder than magic.

At the hall, Nessa had six coats laid across the rear bench and was
pinning small scraps of paper to the collars.

I stopped.

"Names?"

"Pieces."

"Why?"

"Because I'm leaving."

That was apparently enough information for everyone except me.

"Where?"

"Work."

"This is work."

"Other work."

"Better work?"

Nessa looked at me.

"Paid work."

"How much?"

"No."

I respected the answer because it was the same one I would have given
Pell.

Rinna had already changed the board.

KING: MAREK / HARA / EVIN

EMPTY TABLE: VEYA / SELLEN / GREG

SHORT: EVIN / PELL

GREG: SERVANT / SET / COATS

I pointed.

"Servant."

Teren looked over from the front.

"Four lines."

"Important?"

"No."

"Excellent."

The Servant in The Empty Table carried in a tray, announced that the
missing guest had sent word, was blamed for the guest's absence, denied
responsibility, and left.

I had seen the piece twice.

Apparently that qualified me.

Nessa handed me a gray coat.

"Not yours."

"I know."

"Good. Sellen, second piece."

She handed me a brown coat.

"Also not yours."

"Veya after Empty Table."

"Why does Veya need a coat after the piece she's in?"

"Because she leaves."

"Right."

Nessa gave me a third coat.

I looked at it.

"Hara?"

"No."

"Marek?"

"No."

"Evin?"

"No."

"Then I have exhausted people."

"Pell."

I stared at the coat.

"Why does Pell have a coat?"

"He plays a magistrate."

Pell, from behind the flat, said, "Authority."

Nessa said, "Temporary."

She made me hang the coats on three separate pegs.

Then she moved two of them.

"Why?"

"Because you put them in the wrong order."

"I used the order you handed them to me."

"That was not the performance order."

"You knew I was going to do that."

"Yes."

This was Nessa's final act before abandoning us.

Her other job involved three fitted coats for some household
entertainment on the north side. That was all I learned. She left with a
roll of pins, a wrapped bundle, and a bag that looked heavier than
anything involving thread should look.

No one wished her luck.

Sellen said, "If they pay you before us, don't come back."

Nessa said, "I wasn't planning to."

Then she was gone.

The hall continued.

This was mildly alarming.

I had assumed certain people were structural.

Apparently the structure was accustomed to being missing pieces.

My Servant rehearsal lasted long enough for Teren to tell me not to limp
with the tray.

"I don't limp."

"You compensate."

"I am missing a leg."

"I know."

"Then this seems connected."

"Use the table edge before the entrance. Set the tray. Take the crutch.
Enter."

I tried it.

It was easier.

I disliked that.

"Again," Teren said.

The second time I picked up the wrong tray.

There were two.

One had cups.

One had a folded letter.

Teren pointed.

"Letter."

"I knew that."

"You were carrying cups."

"I was testing you."

"No."

We finished.

Before the house opened, I ate half an onion with bread and regretted
both separately.

Pell had beans.

Marek had acquired a pear from somewhere and refused to explain it.

Hara arrived with dust on one sleeve and immediately asked why Nessa's
pegs had paper tied to them.

"Because we're children," Sellen said.

"Accurate."

The house was ordinary.

Twenty-something people.

Two bench complaints.

One man wanted to know whether The King was the one with the tax
collector.

"No," I said.

"Which is that?"

"I don't know."

He accepted this.

The King played first.

I worked rear.

Marek was good.

Hara was good.

Evin missed the edge of the platform with his heel and recovered without
anyone caring.

No one needed me.

Then Empty Table.

I put on the Servant coat.

It was tan.

I had expected gray.

Apparently the gray coat belonged to Sellen later.

This would have mattered if I had been responsible for remembering it.

I was not.

I carried the letter tray to the table edge, set it, shifted my crutch,
picked it up again, and entered.

"The guest has sent word."

Veya turned.

"From where?"

"From elsewhere."

That got a small laugh.

It was supposed to.

Sellen said, "Elsewhere is not an address."

"I was not given the address."

Nothing.

Fine.

Later Veya asked whether I had delayed the guest.

"I carry messages."

"Poorly."

"I carry them completely."

That got a better laugh.

I left.

Four lines.

No revelation.

Backstage, I handed Veya the brown coat.

She looked at it.

"Why?"

"Nessa said after Empty Table."

"Not me."

I looked at the paper tied to the collar.

PELL.

I had read the wrong peg.

Veya handed it back.

"Authority."

"Temporary."

Pell appeared.

"Mine?"

"Unfortunately."

The short belonged to Evin and Pell.

Pell's magistrate coat was too broad in the shoulder and made him look
like someone had promoted a sack.

This improved the piece.

Evin's character kept asking permission to do things he had already
done.

Pell kept granting it afterward.

The strongest moment came when Evin moved Pell's chair before asking.

"May I move your chair?"

Pell looked down at the empty space where the chair had been.

"No."

Evin nodded.

"Good."

Pell remained standing for the rest of the scene because neither of them
could get the chair back without breaking the rhythm.

The audience laughed every time he looked toward it.

I was holding Sellen's gray coat.

It turned out Sellen did not need it at all because the piece had
changed.

I hung it back.

This was my entire contribution.

Afterward, Rinna paid me one copper.

I took it.

She waited.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"I negotiated yesterday."

"You didn't."

"Recently."

"Go sweep."

I swept.

Nessa had not returned.

No one knew whether she would.

Hara left before cleanup finished.

Marek and Sellen argued about the pear.

Pell found the chair.

The hall emptied.

It should have ended there.

Most days did.

I was carrying a sack of costume scraps toward the rear storage when
someone knocked on the front door.

Not the polite knock people used before a show.

Three hits.

Hard enough that Rinna stopped counting coins.

Pell looked toward the door.

"Closed."

The knock came again.

Rinna put the coins away.

"Stay."

This was addressed to everyone and therefore obeyed by almost no one.

I stayed because I had one crutch under my arm and a sack of cloth in
both hands.

Rinna opened the door halfway.

Two men stood outside.

I could see one clearly.

Heavy coat despite the warmth. Thick neck. Hair cut close. The other was
narrower and stood a step back where the street light did not reach him
as well.

The first man said something too low for me to hear.

Rinna answered normally.

"No."

He said something else.

Rinna's shoulders changed.

Not much.

Enough.

I put the sack down.

Marek had stopped arguing about the pear.

Sellen was no longer smiling.

Pell whispered, "Who?"

"No idea."

The man at the door pushed it wider.

Rinna pushed back.

Not enough to stop him.

He stepped inside.

The second man remained outside.

"Private conversation," the first man said.

Rinna said, "Then have it privately somewhere else."

His eyes moved past her.

Hall.

Benches.

Rear door.

People.

Me.

He was not impressed.

That was sensible.

"We were told this place has been doing better."

Rinna said nothing.

"Havel work."

That landed differently.

Not because Havel mattered.

Because he knew.

The man smiled.

"Private courtyards. Better houses. More coin."

Rinna's voice stayed flat.

"What do you want?"

"Small share."

"No."

"Then next time a cart wheel breaks, or a lamp goes missing, or someone
decides your door should stop opening, you can pay a larger share."

Pell made a tiny sound.

The man looked at him.

Pell stopped.

This was not theatre.

It was also not new.

Not to me.

Different city.

Different clothes.

Different words.

Same arithmetic.

A person did not need to be stronger than everyone in a room if he could
make the room imagine the people who were not there.

The man had brought one visible friend.

He wanted us to supply the rest.

Rinna said, "Leave."

He took another step.

Marek moved.

I said, "Don't."

Everyone looked at me.

Including the man.

This was inconvenient.

I had not meant to say it aloud.

The man looked down at my crutches.

Then up.

"You talking to me?"

"No."

This was true.

I had been talking to Marek.

The man smiled.

"Good."

He turned back toward Rinna.

Behind him, through the open door, the second man shifted his weight.

Street-side.

Watching both directions.

Not relaxed.

Not backup waiting to enter.

A lookout.

Two men.

Maybe more elsewhere.

Maybe not.

The first man wanted us to believe more.

That was useful.

I looked toward the rear.

The narrow painted panel from yesterday was still half out of storage.

Beside it hung the old dark backing cloth.

Pell followed my eyes.

I looked at the lamp.

Then at him.

He frowned.

I touched two fingers to my own chest, then pointed behind the panel.

Pell's frown deepened.

I pointed again.

He moved.

Slowly.

The man did not notice.

Rinna did.

She did not look at Pell.

Good.

I had no plan.

This was familiar too.

Plans were usually what people called the thing they discovered
afterward.

I picked up Sellen's gray coat.

"Put that down," Rinna said.

"Yes."

I did not.

The man looked at me again.

"Problem?"

"Probably."

"Greg," Rinna said.

I looked at her.

She knew my name.

The man now knew my name.

That was unfortunate.

I said, "He should leave."

The man laughed once.

"Why?"

I looked at the door.

At the lookout.

At the man's boots.

Clean except for pale grit at the inside heel.

Not Carrow mud.

Stone dust.

His coat had been brushed recently but not well. There was a darker line
near the left pocket where something heavy had rubbed.

Knife or tool.

His right hand was bare.

Left glove.

Not fashion.

Maybe injured.

Maybe hiding marks.

I did not know.

The important part was that I did not need to know all of it.

I needed him to think I knew more than I should.

"You came through Mason's Cut," I said.

His smile changed.

Tiny.

Good.

I had guessed from the pale stone grit.

Mason's Cut had pale dust. I had walked through it twice.

He said, "So?"

I looked at his left hand.

"Then you should have stayed there."

This meant nothing.

His eyes narrowed.

Better.

Rinna did not ask.

Excellent.

Behind the painted panel, Pell moved.

The backing cloth lifted.

Not much.

A person-shaped darkening appeared against the pale wall beyond it.

Too tall to be Pell because the panel cut him at the wrong place.

Unless he held something.

He did.

A hat.

Marek's King hat, probably.

The shadow acquired a head.

The man saw it.

His eyes flicked once.

There.

Then back to me.

I did not look.

Theatre had taught me that part.

Not magic.

Not even good theatre.

Just do not look at the thing you want someone else to notice.

From behind the panel came one quiet knock.

Then another.

Pell had found wood.

The man glanced again.

I said, "You brought two."

He looked at me.

The lookout outside stopped moving.

I had counted them.

That was all.

I continued before either could decide this was obvious.

"One at the door. One inside. No cart. No third man on the east corner."

The inside man's face changed.

There might have been a third man on the west corner.

I had no idea.

I had said east.

Specificity was dangerous.

Specificity was also useful.

"Who are you?" he asked.

There it was.

Not who do you think you are.

Who are you.

Different question.

I put Sellen's gray coat on.

It did not fit.

Too narrow through the shoulders.

Good.

People noticed wrong clothes when they knew what the clothes should
mean.

He did not know.

I did.

That was enough.

"Tonight?"

I looked around the hall.

"Servant."

Marek made a noise that might have killed us.

I kept going.

"Yesterday I was at the Guild."

The man's eyes sharpened.

True.

"Before that, Havel."

True.

"Before that, here."

True.

I shifted my crutch.

"You're asking the wrong question."

Also true.

He stared at me.

Behind the panel, the shadow moved.

Pell had apparently understood enough to make it turn away, as though
whoever stood there had become bored with us.

That was much better than looming.

The man saw that too.

"Guild what?"

I smiled.

Not because I was brave.

Because Greg the Servant had smiled twice tonight and my face knew how.

"Paper."

He stared.

I let him.

This was stupid.

It was also impossible to evaluate.

He had expected a frightened troupe.

Instead he had a one-legged actor in the wrong gray coat telling him
accurate things about his route, counting his men, mentioning the Guild,
and refusing to explain why a tall shadow behind a panel did not care
enough to join the conversation.

There was no lie large enough to catch.

Only small truths arranged badly.

The man said, "You're funny."

"Sometimes."

"You think this changes anything?"

"No."

True.

It did not change who he was.

It changed this minute.

That was all I needed.

I looked at his left glove again.

"You should go before your hand gets worse."

I knew nothing about his hand.

He looked at it.

That was the first real mistake he made.

Marek saw.

Rinna saw.

I definitely saw.

The glove mattered.

Maybe not the way I implied.

Did not matter.

The man stepped back.

One step.

Not retreat.

Recalculation.

"I'll come back."

Rinna said, "Don't."

He looked at her.

Then at me.

"Greg."

I hated that.

"Yes."

He smiled again.

Less.

Then he left.

The lookout went with him.

Rinna shut the door.

Locked it.

No one spoke.

Behind the panel, Pell said, "Can I come out?"

Rinna closed her eyes.

"Yes."

Pell emerged holding the King hat on the end of a broom.

Marek stared.

"My hat."

"It was authority."

"Temporary," Hara would have said if she were here.

Sellen sat down.

"What the fuck was that?"

I took off the gray coat.

"Extortion."

"I know that part."

Rinna looked at me.

"How did you know Mason's Cut?"

"Boots."

"What about his hand?"

"I didn't."

Silence.

Pell lowered the broom.

"You didn't?"

"No."

"He looked."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

Marek said, "You told him it was worse."

"I told him he should leave before it got worse."

"That means the same thing."

"No."

Rinna looked at me for a long time.

"It meant the same thing to him."

That was harder to answer.

I sat.

My good leg had started shaking.

This annoyed me because it was late.

Sellen noticed.

He did not say anything.

Good.

Pell put the King hat on the bench.

"You said Guild."

"I was at the Guild yesterday."

"You made it sound like Guild."

"I said I was at the Guild."

"You said paper."

"I did paper."

Pell stared.

"That's horrible."

"Yes."

Marek said, "The shadow?"

I looked at Pell.

"Pell did that."

Pell straightened.

"I did."

"With my hat."

"Authority."

Marek took the hat away.

Rinna went to the front window and looked through the narrow edge of the
shutter.

"They're gone."

No one relaxed.

That seemed appropriate.

She turned.

"We close together."

No argument.

"We do not walk alone tonight."

No argument.

"Pell, stop looking proud."

Pell stopped looking proud.

Mostly.

Rinna looked at me again.

"That wasn't nothing."

"I know."

"He may come back."

"I know."

"You don't do that again without me knowing what you're doing."

"I didn't know what I was doing."

"That is worse."

"Probably."

She rubbed her forehead.

"Tomorrow, third bell."

Sellen laughed.

It came out wrong.

Rinna looked at him.

"What?"

"Nothing. Just thought maybe we'd cancel theatre because of the
criminals."

Rinna stared.

"No."

"Good."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

We left together.

Marek and Sellen went one direction.

Pell walked with me.

Rinna walked behind us until the next crossing, then turned toward her
own street.

No one followed.

At least no one I saw.

I did not turn the walk into a hunt for shadows.

That was how you supplied your own extra men.

Pell stayed quiet for almost a whole block.

Then:

"Servant?"

"What?"

"You told him you were a servant."

"I was."

"That was your answer."

"Tonight."

"He asked who you were."

"Yes."

"And you said servant."

"Yes."

Pell thought about it.

"That was the scariest part."

"It shouldn't have been."

"It was."

At my street he stopped.

"You good?"

"No."

"Same."

He left.

Lyssa was home.

She had two pieces of cloth on the table and one expression on her face
when I came in.

"What happened?"

I put my crutches against the wall.

"Why does everyone ask that before hello?"

"Greg."

I told her.

Not well.

The knock.

The man.

Havel.

Money.

Threat.

Pell behind the panel.

King hat on broom.

Mason's Cut.

Guild.

Paper.

The glove.

Lyssa did not interrupt until I got to the glove.

"You didn't know?"

"No."

"You guessed?"

"I implied."

"You guessed."

"I didn't actually say his hand was injured."

"Greg."

"I said he should leave before it got worse."

She stared at me.

I sat.

The shaking had stopped.

That was better.

Or worse.

Lyssa came around the table and stood in front of me.

"Did he touch you?"

"No."

"Rinna?"

"No."

"Anyone?"

"No."

"Did he have a weapon?"

"Probably."

"Did you see one?"

"No."

I looked at her.

"That's Hessa's fault."

"What?"

"That question."

Lyssa did not smile.

Fair.

I told her Rinna had us leave together.

I told her the men were gone when we left.

I told her tomorrow was still third bell.

That finally got a reaction.

"Of course it is."

"Apparently criminals do not alter repertory."

"Good."

"Good?"

"No. I don't know."

She sat across from me.

For a while neither of us spoke.

Then she said, "You scared him."

"I confused him."

"Same thing?"

"No."

She looked at me.

I thought about Hessa.

"No. Definitely not."

The room was quiet.

The lamp burned.

Nothing moved under the cup.

No note.

No paper.

No magic.

I had not moved anything with mana.

Pell had moved a broom.

I had moved a coat.

The man had moved himself.

That last one felt different.

I did not like how much.

Lyssa reached across the table and took my hand.

"Eat."

"We don't have much."

"I know."

We had bread.

A little salt.

Half an onion.

Enough.

Tomorrow I would go back to the hall.

The man might come back.

He might not.

He might have more people.

He might have none.

I knew almost nothing about him.

That was the part Hessa would have approved of.

The rest, probably not.

I had told the truth.

Mostly.

He had done the lying for me.
