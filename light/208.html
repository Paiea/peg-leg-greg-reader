LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 208

THE DOORMAN

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

I woke because someone hit wood outside.

I was upright before I knew where I was.

Lyssa made a sound beside me that was less awake than annoyed.

The noise came again.

Wood. Wheels. A man swearing.

I waited.

A cart rolled past the window.

The wheels kept going.

Nothing knocked on our door.

I lay back down.

Lyssa said, "Cart?"

"Cart."

"Good."

She was asleep again before I was.

This seemed unfair.

By morning I had converted the night into one bad wake-up and several
hours of perfectly ordinary sleep. My good leg felt fine. My forearm had
stopped complaining. My wrist remained so boring that I rotated it once
and immediately felt embarrassed.

There was nothing under the cup.

No note.

Lyssa watched me put the cup back.

"Are you going?"

"Third bell."

"That wasn't the question."

"Yes."

She tore the last piece from the loaf and gave me the smaller half.

I looked at it.

"You're concerned for me."

"I'm hungry."

"That is less flattering."

"You can buy bread."

"With money."

"Yes."

I ate.

Lyssa had work near Marra's again. We left together because our routes
overlapped for three streets, not because either of us said we should.

At the second crossing she stopped.

"Men come back, you leave."

"That's not how doors work."

"Greg."

"I know."

She looked at me.

I corrected myself.

"If they come back and leaving is possible, I leave."

"Better."

"What if Rinna says stay?"

"Then Rinna can explain herself to me."

"That seems worse for her than the men."

"It might be."

She went left.

I went right.

Carrow was offensively normal.

A woman was arguing over radishes. Two boys were rolling a hoop downhill
where carts also wanted to exist. A dog had found something dead enough
to be valuable and was defending it from another dog.

Nobody knew I had frightened myself with a cart.

Good.

At the hall, the front door was locked.

I knocked.

Nothing.

I knocked again.

A voice inside said, "Who?"

"Pell."

The door opened.

Pell looked at me.

"You're not Pell."

"I wanted to see if you checked."

He started closing the door.

I put my crutch in the gap.

"Greg."

"Too late."

"Open."

He opened.

Inside, the hall looked mostly like itself.

Mostly.

Davin had the front door hinge exposed.

Jori was under the handcart.

Marek was standing at the rear bench with his King hat in both hands.

Pell pointed at him.

"He slept with it."

"I did not."

"You brought it home."

"I protected it."

"From crime."

"From Pell."

Rinna was at the front table.

She looked up.

"Third bell."

"Exactly."

"Door stays latched until house."

I nodded.

"Rear too."

"Fine."

"No one talks about Havel money outside the company."

"I didn't before."

"No one talks about Guild."

I considered this.

"I barely did before."

Rinna looked at me.

"Greg."

"Fine."

"No one leaves alone after close for the next few days if we can help
it."

Pell raised a hand.

"What if I want to?"

"No."

"What if I'm very fast?"

"No."

"What if I'm carrying a broom?"

Marek said, "No hat."

Rinna closed her eyes.

"Especially no hat."

Pell looked at me.

"We've lost our best man."

"It was a broom."

"It was tall."

"You put a hat on it."

"It worked."

"That is not the same as being good."

"It can be both."

Marek took the King hat away from him despite Pell not currently holding
it.

Davin kicked the front door lightly.

It did not move.

"Fine," he said.

"What was wrong with it?" I asked.

"Nothing."

"So you fixed nothing."

"I checked it."

"That's Guild work."

Davin looked at me.

"No."

"Good."

Jori came in from the rear.

"Cart's fine."

Pell looked disappointed.

Rinna noticed.

"Why are you disappointed?"

"I'm not."

"You are."

"I thought if they threatened the wheel, maybe they'd done something to
the wheel."

"That would be bad."

"Yes."

"You look disappointed."

Pell examined the floor.

Rinna looked at me.

"You."

I waited.

"You bought us time."

"Probably."

"You also gave him your name."

"I didn't."

"You let me do it."

"I was busy."

"Doing what?"

I looked at the painted panel.

It was back in storage.

"Making poor decisions quickly."

"Yes."

She tapped the table once.

"If he comes back, you don't start another performance without telling
me."

"I don't know how I would tell you first."

"Try."

"That sounds like planning."

"Try that too."

This was unreasonable, but I accepted it.

Before house, Rinna made us check the things that could actually cost
money.

Not everything.

That was important.

Pell wanted to check the roof.

"For what?" Rinna asked.

"Access."

"To what?"

"The roof."

"No."

He wanted to check the cellar next.

We did not have a cellar.

This did not stop him immediately.

Davin checked the lamp shelf because lamps had been named in the threat.
All four tins were where they belonged, including the one whose cap he
had repaired after Havel. He opened it, closed it, and put it back.

"Still fixed?" I asked.

"Yes."

"Could have changed overnight."

"It didn't."

"Good."

Jori checked the rear latch because doors had been named. The wood
around it was old and scarred in ways that predated all of us. He found
one screw working loose.

Pell became excited.

"Sabotage."

Jori held up the screw.

"Age."

"That's what sabotage wants you to think."

Jori tightened it.

Rinna sent Pell to move benches.

The benches had not been threatened.

This made them safer.

Marek watched the street through the front shutter once, then
deliberately stopped. Sellen arrived, heard half the story from Pell,
heard the other half from Rinna, and somehow ended with less information
than when he entered.

"So Greg is Guild now?"

"No," everyone said.

Sellen looked at me.

"Congratulations."

"Thank you."

Rinna pointed at him.

"No."

"Withdrawn."

Veya came in carrying a small paper packet of roasted seeds. She
listened while hanging her outer wrap.

"Did they say who sent them?"

"No," Rinna said.

"Did they name anyone?"

"No."

"Did they say how much?"

"'Small share.'"

Veya snorted.

"Small isn't a number."

I looked at her.

"That's what Hessa would say."

"Who?"

"Never mind."

She ate a seed.

That was the entire council.

Nobody drew a map.

Nobody assigned weapons.

Nobody discovered a criminal empire hidden under the floor.

Teren arrived, complained that the front bench had moved three inches,
and made us move it back.

For several minutes, this was the largest problem in the building.

I was grateful.

The board gave me no role.

GREG: HOUSE / DOOR / SET.

I pointed.

"Door."

Rinna said, "Yes."

"Because of yesterday?"

"Because you have two hands and people pay at the door."

"I have two hands every day."

"Then you've been qualified longer than I thought."

I took my place at the front.

For the first hour, nobody tried to extort us.

This was excellent business.

A bread seller came by with a basket and asked if we wanted yesterday's
heels cheap. Rinna bought several.

A boy delivered a message for Teren.

A woman wanted to know whether tonight had Widow.

It did.

A man selling lamp wicks tried to convince Jori that every wick in the
hall was dangerously old.

Jori showed him three new ones.

The man said those were also dangerously old.

Normal threats returned.

I preferred them.

Then a woman carrying a basket of green onions stopped at the door.

She had been at the hall before. I remembered her because she always
smelled like onions even when the basket contained something else.

"Who's your Guild man?" she asked.

I thought I had misheard.

"Our what?"

"Guild man."

Rinna looked up from inside.

"What Guild man?"

The woman leaned around me.

"The one you had at the door last night."

There was a silence.

Pell, somewhere behind the flat, made a sound.

Rinna said, "We did not have a Guild man."

The woman looked at me.

Then at my crutches.

Then back at Rinna.

"That's not what I heard."

"What did you hear?"

"That some collectors came asking and you had Guild standing inside."

"No."

"Gray coat."

I looked down.

My coat today was brown.

This felt safer.

The woman said, "One leg."

Pell coughed.

Rinna looked at me.

I said, "I was wearing Sellen's coat."

The woman frowned.

"Why?"

"It was available."

"You're Guild?"

"No."

"You work there?"

"No."

"I was there."

Rinna said, "Stop helping."

I stopped.

The woman adjusted the onion basket.

"So you don't have Guild protection."

"No," Rinna said.

The woman looked disappointed.

This was worse than Pell and the cart wheel.

"Shame," she said. "Sounded useful."

Then she left.

I watched her cross the street.

She spoke to nobody.

At least not before turning the corner.

Pell appeared beside me.

"Guild man."

"No."

"Gray coat."

"No."

"One leg."

"Unfortunately yes."

"That's a good description."

Rinna pointed toward the rear.

Pell disappeared.

I stayed at the door.

I had wanted one man to misunderstand me for perhaps thirty seconds.

I had not considered that he might explain his misunderstanding to
someone else.

Or that someone else would improve it.

There had been no Guild man at the door.

I had not been at the door.

I had been inside.

The coat had been gray.

That part survived.

The Guild had moved closer.

That part had grown.

Protection had appeared from nowhere.

This was bad.

It was also, for the moment, useful.

That was worse.

Rinna came to stand beside me.

"Don't correct everyone."

"I just did."

"Badly."

"I told the truth."

She looked at me.

I stopped.

"Right."

A broad man came up the street.

I noticed him before he noticed the hall.

Heavy shoulders.

Dark coat.

No visible basket.

He looked toward the door.

I shifted my crutch.

Rinna followed my eyes.

The man came closer.

His hands were empty.

No glove.

No pale grit that I could see.

He stopped directly in front of me.

"How much?"

I stared at him.

"For what?"

"The show."

"One copper."

He looked annoyed.

"For both of us?"

A woman was behind him.

I had not noticed her because I had been watching his shoulders.

"Yes."

He paid.

The woman said, "Is Widow tonight?"

"Yes."

"Good."

They went in.

Rinna said nothing.

I said, "He was broad."

"Terrifying."

"Dark coat."

"Criminal."

"Empty hands."

"Clearly a mastermind."

"I corrected."

"You charged him."

"Correctly."

"Good."

She went back inside.

I hated that she was right.

The house filled normally.

Not full.

Not thin.

Enough.

The onion woman did not come.

The broad man did.

So did his wife.

He was not dangerous unless Widow was bad.

The bill was King, Widow, then a short with Sellen and Veya.

I stayed at the door through the first lines of King, then latched it
and went rear.

The change was immediate.

Street became wall.

Door became door.

Marek became King.

People laughed.

My body stopped listening for knocks because Pell needed the low stool
and Jori had put it behind the wrong flat.

Work was useful that way.

It occupied the parts of you that otherwise invented people on corners.

King played cleanly.

Marek had his hat.

He checked it before going on.

Pell noticed.

"Trust issues."

Marek said, "Correct."

Widow came next.

Veya had the chipped cup.

Nessa had returned from her outside job sometime before house and was
already angry at a sleeve.

I had not seen her arrive.

"How much?" I whispered.

"No."

Good.

Hara and Veya played Widow.

The broad man laughed early and loudly.

His wife did not.

This seemed to embarrass him, so he laughed less.

Halfway through, Veya reached for the chipped cup and found it empty.

It was supposed to be empty.

She looked inside anyway.

Hara said, "Still poor?"

Veya turned the cup upside down.

"Improving."

A few people laughed.

Hara reached under the table, produced the small wooden spoon, and held
it up.

"Rich."

Veya took it with both hands.

The broad man's wife laughed first.

Then everyone else.

It was not a large moment.

It was the best one.

Backstage, Pell whispered, "Guild spoon."

I hit him lightly with the folded cloth I was holding.

"No."

He smiled.

Between pieces I found the gray coat from last night hanging where it
belonged.

I stopped in front of it.

Sellen came up behind me.

"Mine."

"I know."

"You wore it badly."

"I was under pressure."

"It pulled across the shoulders."

"Criminals didn't mention it."

"They noticed."

"You don't know that."

"I would have."

He took the coat down, shook it once, and put it on for a later change
that Teren had already decided not to use.

Then he took it off again.

"Why did you put it on?" he asked.

"I needed him to not know what it meant."

Sellen frowned.

"What did it mean?"

"Nothing."

"Then why that coat?"

"It was there."

He stared at me for a moment.

"That's irritating."

"Yes."

"Did you at least look mysterious?"

"No."

"Shame."

He rehung the coat.

That conversation should have made the thing smaller.

Instead it made me remember how little of last night had required
anything special.

A coat that meant nothing.

A broom.

A hat.

Boot dust.

A true place name.

A man with a glove.

Almost everything had already existed before I touched it.

I went back to moving the stool.

The short played after.

Sellen missed a line.

Veya waited.

He found it.

Nobody died.

Nobody knocked.

The broad man left with his wife and thanked me at the door.

I watched his boots.

Mud.

Normal Carrow mud.

He noticed.

"Something wrong?"

"No."

I looked up.

"Sorry."

He glanced down at his boots.

"Stepped in something?"

"Probably."

He left.

I closed the door.

That was how easy it was.

Look at someone's boots long enough and they supplied a reason.

I did not like knowing that.

Cleanup took longer because everyone checked things that did not need
checking.

Jori checked the rear latch twice.

Davin checked the cart wheel again.

Pell checked behind the painted panel.

"For what?" I asked.

"Crime."

"Is it there?"

"No."

"Good."

Marek took the King hat home again.

Rinna saw.

"Really?"

"Yes."

"Fine."

She paid me one copper.

I took it without negotiating.

She noticed that too.

"Scared?"

"Growing."

"Good."

"I don't think that's what you meant."

"No."

Nessa was sorting the coats.

I asked whether she had heard anything.

"About what?"

"Last night."

"Yes."

"From who?"

"Someone who heard you were Guild."

I closed my eyes.

"How?"

"I didn't ask."

"Did you correct them?"

"No."

"Why?"

"I had three coats to fit."

This was deeply reasonable.

"What did they think I did?"

Nessa tied a bundle.

"Didn't ask."

"You're very bad at rumors."

"I'm excellent at coats."

She left.

Rinna made us leave in groups again.

Not a formation.

Not escorts.

Just no one alone if it was easy to avoid.

I walked with Jori and Pell for three streets.

Pell wanted to discuss the shadow.

I did not.

He wanted to discuss whether the shadow needed a better hat.

Marek was not present, so he was brave.

I still did not.

At the crossing, Jori went south.

Pell went east.

I had two streets alone.

Nothing happened.

A cat knocked over something behind me.

I turned.

Cat.

I continued.

At home, Lyssa was trimming thread ends from a pale cuff.

She looked up.

"They come back?"

"No."

"Good."

"People think I'm Guild."

Her scissors stopped.

"What?"

"Not everyone."

"How many?"

"At least two."

"That's too many."

"One was selling onions."

"That's worse."

"I don't know why."

"Onion sellers talk."

This seemed possible.

I told her about the woman at the hall.

Gray coat.

One leg.

Guild man at the door.

Protection.

Lyssa listened.

Then she asked, "Are you Guild?"

"No."

"Good."

"I am allowed inside."

"That is not the same."

"I know."

"Do they?"

"No."

There it was.

We ate bread with salt and the last raw onion.

The lamp burned steadily.

Still no note under the cup.

I told her Rinna had latched the doors before house.

Davin checked hinges.

Jori checked cart.

Nobody left alone after close.

No men returned.

The show happened.

Veya got rich with a spoon.

Lyssa nodded at the important parts.

"Tomorrow?"

"Third bell."

"Of course."

I sat back.

The rumor bothered me more than I wanted it to.

Not because it was false.

Most rumors were.

Because parts of it were true in exactly the wrong places.

Gray coat.

One leg.

Guild.

Men left.

Those pieces fit together better after they had left me.

I had controlled my words.

I had not controlled the story.

Outside, someone knocked on someone else's door.

I waited.

Once.

Twice.

Then a woman shouted that she was coming.

I ate the rest of my bread.
