LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 210

THE TENANT

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

There was still nothing under the cup.

I lifted it.

Nothing.

I put it down.

Lyssa was scraping the last beans from yesterday into a smaller bowl.

"You look disappointed."

"I'm maintaining continuity."

"With what?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

She divided the beans badly, which meant my portion was larger.

I corrected this by eating before she noticed.

She noticed.

"Thief."

"Evidence?"

"The missing beans."

"Could be natural loss."

"Eat."

I did.

My wrist was boring. My forearm was boring. My shoulder had stopped
having opinions about paper.

The room smelled faintly of onion and old lamp oil. The new wick burned
evenly even though there was enough daylight that we did not need it.

Lyssa pinched it out.

"We paid for that."

"Exactly. We should admire it."

"We can admire it tonight."

She had another bundle today. Dark cloth, pale thread, one wooden toggle
sitting loose on top until she wrapped it.

I did not ask.

This had become a skill.

"Third bell?" she asked.

"Third bell."

"Anything else?"

"No."

"Good."

That was becoming a dangerous word.

She left first.

I waited.

Nothing happened.

Then I went to work.

The handcart was missing a wheel.

Not missing in the sense that somebody had stolen it.

Missing in the sense that the wheel was lying beside the cart while Jori
held the axle up with both hands and Davin crouched in the dirt behind
the hall.

Pell stood nearby.

He had no useful job.

This was obvious from posture alone.

I stopped in the rear doorway.

"What happened?"

"Pin's gone," Davin said.

I looked at the wheel.

"That sounds repairable."

"It is."

"Good."

Davin looked up.

"Did you think that was the important part?"

"Usually."

Jori shifted his grip.

"Can we put this down?"

"No," Davin said.

"I've been holding it."

"I know."

"That was information."

"Keep holding it."

I moved closer.

The cart wheel had a thick wooden hub. The end of the axle passed
through it, with a hole near the end where a small retaining pin
normally kept the wheel from walking itself off.

The hole was empty.

No broken wood.

No snapped pin.

Just empty.

"Broke?" I asked.

Davin pointed.

"If it broke, part would still be in there or the hole would be
damaged."

"Unless it broke very conveniently."

"Wood doesn't care about convenience."

"People do."

"Yes."

He ran a thumb around the axle end.

"Pulled."

Jori said, "Thank you."

Davin looked at him.

"For confirming the thing I've been saying since I picked it up."

"Can I put it down?"

"Now."

They lowered the axle onto a block.

I looked toward the rear lane.

The gate was shut.

Rinna stood just inside it.

"They said wheel," she said.

"Yes."

Nobody needed more.

Pell said, "I checked the roof."

Rinna looked at him.

"And?"

"No cart pin."

"Useful."

"I also checked the front."

"For cart pins?"

"For men."

"Any?"

"Three."

We all looked at him.

"Normal men."

"How did you determine that?" I asked.

"They ignored me."

"Strong evidence."

Rinna pointed at the hall.

"Inside."

Pell went.

I stayed.

"When did we last know it was there?"

Jori wiped his hands on his trousers.

"I checked the cart after those men came. Wheel, other wheel, shafts,
bed."

"Yesterday?" I asked.

"Again yesterday."

Rinna nodded.

"So sometime after close."

"Or this morning," Jori said. "Rear lane was empty when I opened. Cart
was where we left it. I moved it half a turn before I saw the wheel
shift."

"Half a turn?"

"The wheel."

"Not the cart."

"I know what a wheel is."

"Good."

Davin stood.

"Nobody rode it. Nobody loaded it. Pin's gone. That's what we know."

"Bren?" I asked.

Rinna said, "No."

"I was asking if we're still calling him that."

"No."

"That's not what I asked."

"It is the answer you need."

Davin found a short piece of hard wood in his box and began shaving it
down.

I watched.

He looked at me.

"No."

"I'm not touching it."

"You were thinking."

"That has not been prohibited."

"Yet."

He went back to the replacement pin.

Rinna said, "The cart-yard story is real."

That pulled my attention away from Davin's knife.

"Real how?"

"I asked."

"Who?"

"No."

"Of course."

"The yard got the same approach. Small share. Wheel trouble. More
later."

"Same man?"

"Don't know."

"Bren?"

"Don't know."

"Glove?"

"Greg."

"That's a reasonable identifying detail."

"It would be if anyone I asked saw him."

"So no."

"No."

That was better.

Not good.

Better.

The method existed outside our hall.

Someone had asked another small business for money and named the same
kind of cheap damage.

Now our wheel pin was gone.

It still did not tell us who pulled it.

It did tell us that whoever had pulled it had chosen something we had
already been told to fear.

That was enough to make the rear lane feel narrower.

Rinna said, "We're opening."

"Good."

"You sound surprised."

"I wasn't."

"You looked at the cart."

"The cart is currently missing a wheel."

"It has a wheel."

"Not functionally."

Davin held up the new pin.

"It will."

Rinna pointed inside.

"Board."

I went.

The board said:

BORROWED HOUSE: VEYA / SELLEN / GREG

KING: MAREK / HARA / EVIN

SHORT: PELL / NESSA

GREG: TENANT / SET

I read it.

"Tenant?"

Teren was moving the narrow wall panel.

"Six lines."

"Do I own anything?"

"No."

"Accurate."

Borrowed House had enough versions that I had stopped pretending there
was one correct arrangement.

Today Veya was the owner.

Sellen was her brother.

I was the tenant who had been living in a room long enough to believe
the broken shutter belonged to someone else.

This was not difficult acting.

Teren handed me a key.

"Door."

"Which door?"

"The one we don't have."

"Excellent."

"You give it to Veya after she asks why you didn't lock it."

"What am I locking?"

"Your room."

"With a door we don't have."

"Yes."

"Do I have the room?"

"No."

"Very efficient property."

Teren left.

I put the key on the correct prop shelf.

Then moved it when Nessa told me that shelf was for King.

No lesson.

The cart came inside twenty minutes later.

Both wheels attached.

Davin had fitted the replacement pin and then fitted a second small
wedge because he did not like the first one.

Jori rolled the cart once across the rear floor.

Davin crouched and watched the wheel.

"Again."

Jori rolled it back.

"Good."

I said, "Can I touch it now?"

"No."

"At what point does this become personal?"

"Earlier."

Rinna had the cart pushed farther inside than usual.

It blocked part of the rear path.

This meant the large painted panel had to come through front.

This meant Pell and I carried it around the benches.

This meant a woman arriving early found two men carrying a wall toward
her.

She stepped aside.

"Renovating?"

"No," I said.

Pell said, "Constantly."

We set it.

Rinna made us move it.

Then Teren made us move it back half the distance.

Davin needed the rear floor clear long enough to test the cart again, so
the Widow table also went somewhere it did not belong. Nessa came
through carrying two folded shawls, stopped, and looked at it.

"No."

"Temporary," I said.

"Still no."

"Where?"

She pointed to a space beside the costume pegs.

I moved it.

Pell came through with a basket.

"No."

"Why?"

"That's where the basket goes."

"It has never gone there."

"It does today."

I looked at Nessa.

She had already left.

I put the table against the wall.

Teren came back.

"Why is Widow there?"

"Pell."

Pell said, "Greg."

"Shared responsibility."

Teren pointed to the other side.

We moved it again.

For several minutes, the extortionists lost to a painted wall and a
table nobody could agree where to put.

Davin rolled the cart forward once more. The chalk line stayed straight.

Jori leaned over the opposite wheel.

"Other pin's good."

Pell said, "Unless that's what they want us to think."

Rinna looked at him.

"Who?"

"Them."

"Who are them?"

Pell opened his mouth.

Nothing useful came out.

"Good," Rinna said. "Keep it that way."

That quieted him for almost a minute.

A delivery boy knocked at the front with a bundle of lamp wicks for
Jori. Greg the Guild man was apparently not required for this
transaction. Jori paid, counted the bundle, complained that two were
shorter than last time, and made the boy stand there while he compared
them.

The boy cared about none of our problems.

I liked him immediately.

House opened.

I worked front until the first piece.

I counted nine.

Then heard something scrape in the rear.

I looked.

Nothing visible.

I counted again.

Ten.

Rinna said, "Again."

"Why?"

"You said nine."

"I revised."

"Again."

I counted.

Ten.

A woman had come in while I was listening to the rear.

"Ten."

"Good."

"Was this a test?"

"No."

"Then why did I fail?"

"You were counting."

"Apparently not."

She took the tickets.

I went backstage.

The missing pin sat on the corner of Rinna's table.

Not the original pin.

There was no original pin.

Davin's replacement was in the cart.

The empty space was what mattered.

I disliked that.

Breaking the cart would have cost someone effort.

Making us inspect it every morning was cheaper.

They did not need the wheel to come off.

They needed Rinna to imagine it coming off while loaded with lamps.

I did not say this.

Rinna was already checking the rear latch.

She did not need philosophy.

Borrowed House began.

My Tenant coat was brown and too warm.

The key was in my pocket.

I entered after Veya called for me.

"How long have you lived here?" she asked.

"Long enough to know the shutter was broken before I arrived."

"You arrived last month."

"Then it has been broken at least a month."

Small laugh.

Sellen stepped into the room that did not exist.

"You said the roof leaked."

"It does."

"It has not rained."

"I am planning ahead."

Better laugh.

Veya held out her hand.

"Why didn't you lock the room?"

I reached for the key.

Wrong pocket.

I had put it in the inner pocket because the outer one had a hole.

That had been sensible before I was onstage.

Now I had to find it.

Veya watched.

Sellen watched.

The audience watched.

I found the key.

Veya said, "Were you hiding it?"

"From the door."

A laugh.

Not huge.

Enough.

I gave it to her.

She held it up.

"There is no lock."

"Then I have been extremely careful for nothing."

That was the written line.

It got the normal laugh.

My next line got nothing.

The final two happened.

I left.

Backstage, Pell said, "Door feared you."

"Correct."

"No lock."

"That's why."

King followed.

The cart did not matter.

The rear latch did not matter.

Bren might have mattered somewhere in Carrow, but Marek had the hat and
Hara had a petition and Evin had managed to sit in the King's chair
before Marek entered.

Marek stopped.

Evin looked up.

"You're early."

"This is my chair."

"I was keeping it."

"For whom?"

"You."

Marek held out his hand.

Evin stood.

"You're welcome."

Marek sat.

The audience laughed.

Later, Hara handed Marek the petition upside down.

He turned it.

She turned her head to match.

He turned it again.

She matched again.

Marek finally held it sideways.

Hara stared.

"Now neither of us can read it."

"Good."

"Why?"

"I was losing."

That got the biggest laugh of the afternoon.

The audience had no idea a cart pin was missing.

That was their right.

Between pieces I went rear for water.

The cart sat against the wall with the chalk mark still unbroken.

I checked it without touching.

Rinna saw me.

"Don't."

"I didn't."

"You were about to."

"I was observing."

"Observe the stool."

The stool for the short was still on the wrong side because I had left
it there after King.

I moved it.

That was less satisfying than checking the cart and more useful.

The short belonged to Pell and Nessa.

Pell was trying to return a borrowed coat.

Nessa kept identifying things wrong with it.

"That button isn't mine."

"It was attached when I got it."

"Then it was wrong earlier."

"The sleeve is shorter."

"Your arm may have grown."

"One?"

"Possibly."

Pell looked at his arms.

The audience laughed.

Nothing threatened anyone.

Nothing implied anything.

A coat was a coat.

This was comforting.

Afterward, the hall returned to itself.

The painted wall went back.

The Tenant key returned to the shelf.

I checked the pocket before hanging the coat and found a seed shell.

I did not investigate.

Rinna paid me one copper.

I took it.

No one mentioned danger pay.

Good.

The cart was stored fully inside.

Jori put a chalk mark across the replacement pin and axle.

I watched.

"What's that?"

"If the pin moves, line breaks."

"That's clever."

"It's a line."

"Still."

Davin said, "Don't touch it."

"I am developing a reputation."

"You already have one."

"Unfortunately."

The word landed differently than Rinna's joke had days ago.

Nobody followed it.

Rinna latched the rear.

"Tomorrow third bell."

"Still?"

She looked at me.

"Do you want them to close us?"

"No."

"Then third bell."

Pell said, "What if they take another pin?"

Rinna said, "Then Davin makes another pin."

Davin looked offended.

"I have other work."

"Then they owe you."

"Who?"

"Exactly."

That ended it.

We left in groups again.

Not military groups.

Not assigned pairs.

People simply waited an extra minute when their roads overlapped.

I walked with Nessa for one street.

She had another bundle.

"Three coats?" I asked.

"Two."

"Business declining."

"Different house."

"How much?"

"No."

She turned north.

I went east.

Nothing happened.

At home, Lyssa had one onion cut in half and a heel of bread on the
table.

She looked at me.

"They come back?"

"No."

"What happened?"

"Someone pulled the cart pin."

Her knife stopped.

"Did you see them?"

"No."

"Anyone?"

"No."

"Davin says it was pulled, not broken."

"Who?"

"Unknown."

She resumed cutting.

"The men said wheel."

"Yes."

"So maybe."

"Yes."

"What else?"

"The other cart yard really did get the same demand."

"From him?"

"Unknown."

"Bren?"

"Still maybe."

She nodded.

That was all.

No dramatic realization.

No demand that I stop going.

No plan to flee Carrow.

She put the onion in a pan with a little fat.

"Rinna opening tomorrow?"

"Third bell."

"Good."

I looked at her.

"You use that word too much."

"What word?"

"Good."

"Theatre is open."

"Yes."

"You are not hurt."

"Yes."

"The cart is fixed."

"Yes."

"Then good."

That was difficult to argue with.

I checked the cup.

Nothing.

Still no Hessa note.

The lamp burned steadily while onion softened in the pan.

Someone had taken a piece of wood from a cart.

Davin had put another piece back.

Rinna had opened the doors.

We had performed.

The threat had cost time.

Attention.

A little fear.

It had made Jori check two wheels instead of one and Rinna ask questions
she would rather spend on tickets. It had put chalk on a cart pin and
changed where the cart slept.

That was the arithmetic I recognized.

They did not have to smash the wheel.

If they could make us spend ten minutes every day proving the wheel
still existed, they had already taken something.

Not enough to close the hall.

Not enough to pay them.

Not enough to stop Lyssa cutting an onion or Nessa fitting coats or Pell
arguing with furniture.

But something.

It had not yet cost the day.

That felt temporary.

It also felt true.
