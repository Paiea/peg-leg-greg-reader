TEXT READER · CHAPTER 214

THE BROTHER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

My forearm had become boring again, which was exactly what Hessa had wanted after one piece of paper moved at the far mark. Nothing waited under the cup. Lyssa split the last heel of bread with me, confirmed I still had third bell, and asked about Bren.

"Not scheduled."

"Good."

At the theatre, Jori's chalk line across the replacement cart pin was still straight. Pell had already checked the other wheel. Rinna put me down as the Brother in The Empty Table: six lines, the correct entrance, and a blue scarf I was supposed to leave with because the character thought it belonged to him. Teren corrected my door side once. Nessa handed me the coat. The hall went back to being a hall.

The knock came while I was moving the Empty Table table.

Not hard.

Three quick taps.

Rinna looked at the door.

So did everyone else.

That was new.

Before Bren, a knock had meant somebody wanted the door.

Now a knock had acquired options.

Rinna went herself.

She opened it only far enough to see out.

A boy stood there.

Maybe fourteen.

Maybe younger.

Thin arms. Dusty knees. A cord around one wrist. He held a folded piece
of rough cloth in one hand.

He looked past Rinna.

"Greg?"

Nobody moved.

The boy looked around again.

"One leg?"

Pell said, "That's him."

Rinna turned her head.

Pell looked offended.

"What? It is."

The boy held out the bundle.

"Man paid me to bring this."

Rinna did not take it.

"What man?"

"Said Bren."

The room got quieter.

Not silent.

Jori's knife still scraped once against wick.

Then stopped.

Rinna said, "You know him?"

The boy shook his head.

"Where did he find you?"

"South market."

"What did he look like?"

The boy shrugged.

"Man."

"Large?"

"Large enough."

"Coat?"

"Had one."

"Glove?"

The boy looked annoyed now.

"I didn't count his clothes."

Fair.

Rinna pointed at the bundle.

"What is it?"

"He didn't tell me."

"Did you open it?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"He paid me not to."

This was the first thing about the boy that made complete sense.

"How much?" Pell asked.

Rinna looked at him.

"What?"

Pell said, "Information."

The boy said, "One copper."

Pell looked impressed.

I was too.

The boy extended the bundle again.

"For Greg."

Rinna said, "No."

The boy stared at her.

"I have to give it."

"You can put it down."

"That's giving it."

"Fine."

He crouched and set it on the floor outside the door.

Then he remembered something.

"Bren says the share is still small."

Nobody answered.

The boy waited.

Rinna said, "Anything else?"

"No."

"Did he tell you to bring an answer?"

"No."

"Good."

She closed the door.

The boy's footsteps went away.

Pell moved first.

"One copper."

"The boy earned it," Rinna said.

Pell sighed.

Rinna waited a few breaths before opening the door again.

The bundle remained on the ground.

The boy was gone.

No one else stood immediately outside.

Rinna picked it up.

She did not hand it to me.

This seemed correct.

She put it on the front table and unfolded the cloth.

The iron pin inside was dirty.

Small.

Ordinary.

I knew what it was before Davin came over.

So did Jori.

Pell said, "That's ours."

"Probably," I said.

"He kept it."

"Apparently."

"That's rude."

Davin picked up the pin.

He turned it once.

There was a flattened mark along one side.

Jori leaned over.

"Nick."

Davin nodded.

I asked, "Certain?"

Davin looked at me.

"I made it."

"The first one?"

"Yes."

He set it beside the edge of the table.

Jori pointed to the nick.

"From the old axle edge. Before I cleaned it."

"So ours," Rinna said.

Davin nodded.

"Ours."

That changed the shape of things without making it larger than it was. A boy had carried a message in Bren's name, and whoever sent him had possessed our missing pin. It still was not a surname, an organization, or proof Bren had pulled it himself.

The returned iron was cheap. The proof that someone had touched the cart was not.

Rinna folded the cloth over the pin.

"What do we send back?" I asked.

"Nothing."

I looked at her.

"Nothing?"

"Nothing."

"He addressed it to me."

"I noticed."

"That feels like it deserves something."

"No."

Pell said, "We could send his copper back."

"We don't have his copper," Rinna said.

"The boy does."

"Then especially no."

She carried the bundle to the small shelf near her papers.

The threat note was still there.

BREN?

LEFT GLOVE.

CART YARD.

PAID ONCE. MORE.

Rinna added one line beneath it.

PIN RETURNED.

MESSAGE IN BREN'S NAME.

She did not write BREN PULLED PIN.

Good.

I said, "Vale told me to tell him if I got the other half of the name."

Rinna looked at me.

"Did you?"

"No."

"Then don't."

"Bren returned a pin."

"Vale asked for a name."

"Yes."

"Good."

I disliked when everyone became Hessa.

Rinna turned away.

Then turned back.

"You made yourself part of this."

"I was already standing there."

"You made him think you were more than standing there."

I had no useful answer.

She was right.

The bluff had bought us time.

It had also apparently changed who Bren thought he was negotiating with.

I had wanted one man to hesitate.

I had not intended to receive mail.

Rinna pointed toward the stage.

"Table."

Right.

Theatre.

The Empty Table table was still halfway between the wall and where it
belonged.

I moved it.

Not alone.

Davin took the heavier end because Hessa had said no lifting yesterday
and because apparently I was now surrounded by people who listened when
told things.

We set it.

Teren checked the spacing.

"Too far left."

Of course.

We moved it six inches.

Crime could not protect me from furniture.

By the time the doors opened, the returned pin was off the front table.

The replacement pin remained in the cart.

The chalk remained straight.

The audience knew none of this.

They came in wanting seats.

One woman wanted to know whether Widow was playing.

A man with a red nose wanted the aisle.

Two boys argued over who had paid last time.

An older woman brought her own cushion and treated our bench as a
hostile surface she had prepared to defeat.

I worked front until Rinna sent me backstage.

My brown Brother coat waited on the hook.

The blue scarf waited on Veya's chair.

The pin waited somewhere near Rinna's papers.

I tried not to think about any of them.

The Empty Table began. I used the correct entrance and reached the part I already knew until I looked toward Veya's chair. The blue scarf had folded into almost the same shape as the cloth around the returned pin.

My line disappeared.

Veya covered the pause before the audience could own it. The next line came back. Near the end I remembered the scarf, claimed it was mine, and Veya answered, "Because you've stolen it before." The audience laughed. Backstage, Nessa took the scarf from me.

"You missed."

"One line."

"I know."

The threat had managed to enter the play without ever entering the building again. After the show we scattered by ordinary routes. I went west with Sellen until his turn and stopped checking every corner. That was how you supplied your own extra men.

At home, Lyssa was later than me.

I had time to cut the remaining onion before she arrived.

This was dangerous because I was not good at cutting onions evenly.

She came in carrying an empty cloth bag and looked at the pieces.

"What happened?"

"To the onion?"

"To you."

Apparently my face remained a security failure.

"Bren sent the cart pin back."

She stopped.

"The missing one?"

"Davin says yes."

"Did Bren bring it?"

"No."

"Who did?"

"Boy. Runner, I think."

"He knows Bren?"

"Says a man called Bren paid him one copper in the south market."

"What did he say?"

"'The share is still small.'"

Lyssa put the bag down.

"To Rinna?"

"To me."

Her eyes came back to my face.

"Why you?"

"I don't know."

"You know some possibilities."

"Yes."

"Those aren't the same."

Everyone had become Hessa.

I put the onion in the pot.

"Rinna sent nothing back."

"Good."

"I asked."

"Of course you did."

"I thought maybe silence looked weak."

"Does it?"

"I don't know."

That was the problem.

I knew what I had said to Bren the first time.

I knew what Pell had put behind the panel.

I knew what Bren had seen.

I did not know what he had built from it.

Now he had sent something specifically to Greg.

Not the Guild man.

Not the gray coat.

Not Rinna.

Greg.

Maybe that meant he had learned enough to doubt the rest.

Maybe it meant he believed it more.

Maybe he wanted me to wonder.

Lyssa added beans to the onion.

"Vale?"

"No."

"You didn't tell him?"

"He asked for the other half of Bren's name."

"And?"

"I have a pin."

"Not a name."

"Exactly."

She nodded.

No Hessa note waited under the cup.

I did not check twice.

The counts remained thirty, twenty-six, thirty-four.

One far response remained one far response.

The theatre had both wheels.

The door still opened.

The lamps were still ours.

Bren had spent one copper to return a piece of iron worth less than the
message attached to it.

I had made him uncertain.

Now he was making sure I knew he was still there.
