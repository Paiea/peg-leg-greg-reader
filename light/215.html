LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 215

THE BAILIFF

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Nothing happened in the morning.

This was becoming suspicious.

No runner.

No cart pin.

No note under the cup.

No one knocked on the door and asked whether I had reconsidered paying
criminals.

Lyssa had already left by the time I finished washing because a woman
near the west market had decided two repaired cuffs were not the same
length.

"They were the same length yesterday," Lyssa had said.

"Are they the same length today?"

"Yes."

"Then this seems easy."

She had looked at me for a long moment.

"You're very good with customers you don't have."

Then she left with the cuffs.

My body was ordinary.

Hessa had not summoned me.

Vale had not summoned me.

Bren had not summoned me.

For a few hours, nobody wanted anything.

I almost enjoyed it.

At third bell, the replacement cart pin remained exactly where Jori had
left it.

The chalk line was straight.

I looked once.

Pell came up beside me.

"You know we can both look."

"I know."

"You're making it feel competitive."

"Then lose."

He checked the other wheel.

I did not.

Growth.

The original pin was no longer on Rinna's shelf in plain sight. I knew
she had put it away somewhere with the little cloth bundle. I did not
ask where.

If Bren wanted us to think about it, making him work harder seemed
polite.

The hall smelled like dust, old paint, lamp oil, and something Hara was
eating out of a paper twist.

Nessa had two costume boxes open.

Jori had the rear door propped while Davin carried in a narrow painted
post.

Marek was wearing the King hat.

Pell looked at it.

Marek looked at Pell.

"No."

"I didn't move."

"You leaned."

"That isn't illegal."

"It is now."

Rinna was at the front table with a woman I had not seen before.

The woman wore a dark green overshirt over work clothes and had a narrow
leather purse hanging crosswise under one arm. Not rich. Not poor enough
to pretend money did not matter. She had ink on two fingers and a little
flour on one sleeve.

Rinna saw me.

"Greg."

I went over.

"This is Sera Venn."

Not Havel Sera.

Different Sera.

Carrow had apparently begun recycling names to save money.

The woman nodded.

"Venn is fine."

"Greg."

"I gathered."

She looked at my leg.

At least she did not say Guild.

Rinna said, "Venn keeps accounts for the Copper Kettle."

I knew the place.

Not well.

A tavern and eating room north of the market, wide front, covered side
yard, cheap stew, respectable beer according to people who drank more
beer than I did.

Venn said, "Owner's daughter is marrying a cooper."

"Congratulations to the cooper," I said.

Venn's mouth moved slightly.

"Maybe."

Rinna ignored me.

"They want entertainment after supper. Two pieces. Short. Covered yard
if weather holds, main room if not."

"When?" I asked.

"Tomorrow," Rinna said.

That got my attention.

Venn added, "Second bell after midday. Guests eat first. You play
after."

"How many?"

"Forty. Maybe fifty if cousins become real."

"How much room?" Rinna asked.

Venn described it with her hands.

Longer than our stage.

Narrower.

One support post in the middle of the covered yard.

Three tables that could move.

Two that could not.

No raised stage.

One side against a wall.

One side open to the lane.

Rinna asked about lamps.

Venn said they had six wall lamps and two hanging.

"Oil?"

"Ours."

"Benches?"

"Ours."

"Food?"

"Company eats after first piece."

That improved Pell's posture from across the room.

Rinna asked, "Payment?"

Venn looked at me.

Then at Rinna.

Rinna did not move.

Venn leaned closer and told her quietly.

I could not hear.

That was new.

Not secret enough to require another room.

Just not spoken across the hall.

Rinna asked two questions about when and who paid.

Venn answered.

Then Rinna said, "We'll do it."

I looked at her.

She looked back.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Say it."

"Are we taking the cart?"

Venn's eyes moved between us.

Rinna said, "We haven't decided."

Venn said, "You need it?"

"Maybe."

"We have stools."

"Not the same thing."

"Nothing is."

Venn gave Rinna a small folded paper with the tavern name, tomorrow's
time, and the owner's mark.

Then she left because she had actual work.

No ominous pause.

No strange glance toward the cart.

No secret Bren tattoo.

She just left.

Pell came over before the door finished closing.

"Food?"

"After first piece," I said.

"I heard."

"Then why ask?"

"I wanted confirmation."

Rinna unfolded the paper again.

Teren joined us.

So did Nessa.

Marek remained farther back because he was already dressed and had
discovered responsibility.

Rinna said, "Two pieces. Tomorrow. Copper Kettle."

Nessa asked, "Which?"

"King and Widow."

"Not Empty Table?"

"No."

Pell looked wounded.

"Short?"

"No."

"Why are we going?"

"Money."

That answered most theatre questions.

Teren asked about space.

Rinna repeated Venn's description.

Nessa asked about changing.

Small back room near kitchen.

Davin asked what had to travel.

That was when the conversation changed.

Not much.

Just enough.

Before Bren, the answer would have been a list.

Now Rinna looked toward the cart.

"Minimum."

Davin nodded.

"King platform?"

"No."

Marek said, "Good."

Pell said, "Coward."

"The floor exists," Rinna said. "Use it."

Nessa said, "Two costume boxes."

"One."

"I need two."

"One."

"Then someone wears their own trousers."

Pell looked down at his trousers.

"They're theatrical."

"No," Nessa said.

Jori asked, "Painted wall?"

Teren shook his head.

"Too much."

"Door?"

"No."

"Table?"

"The Kettle has tables."

Veya had come in without me noticing.

"Do their tables know the lines?"

"They'll learn."

Hara arrived behind her.

"Are we traveling?"

"Tomorrow," Pell said. "Food."

Rinna said, "Work."

"Food at work."

"Yes."

Hara accepted this.

I looked at the cart.

One costume box.

A prop basket.

Maybe lamps if the tavern's lighting proved bad, but Venn said they had
lamps.

No platform.

No painted wall.

No door.

They could probably hand-carry everything if enough people went.

Rinna was already counting.

"Davin?"

"Carry."

"Jori?"

"Carry."

"Nessa?"

"Costumes."

"Teren?"

"Front and space."

She kept going.

No guards.

No weapons.

No one proposed hiring six large men with matching sticks.

Just fewer things.

I said, "No cart?"

Rinna looked at Davin.

Davin said, "Don't need it."

Jori nodded.

"Not for that."

Rinna said, "Then no cart."

That solved one problem by leaving it inside.

"What about pay?" Pell asked.

Rinna looked at him.

"You'll know your share."

"When?"

"When I pay you."

"Can I know the company amount?"

"No."

"Could I guess?"

"You can do anything quietly."

Pell thought.

Marek said, "Don't."

"I haven't guessed yet."

"You're leaning."

Apparently leaning remained illegal.

Rinna tapped Venn's paper.

"We go together. We leave together. Nobody tells the street what they're
paying."

Hara opened her paper twist.

"Can we tell the street they're feeding us?"

"No."

"That's cruel."

"It's stew."

"You don't know."

"I know the Kettle."

That settled it.

Nobody said Bren.

Nobody needed to.

The cart stayed home.

The payment stayed between Rinna and the client.

The company would move together.

All of those choices could have been ordinary.

Yesterday made them less ordinary.

Rinna turned to today's board.

"Now stop doing tomorrow."

That was fair.

Today's board read:

DEBTOR'S UNCLE: EVIN / VEYA / SELLEN / GREG

KING: MAREK / HARA / EVIN

SHORT: NESSA / PELL

GREG: BAILIFF / SET

I stared at Bailiff.

"Am I collecting money?"

"No," Rinna said.

"Good."

"Why?"

"No reason."

Teren said, "You bring the notice."

"What notice?"

"The debt notice."

I looked at him.

He looked at me.

Neither of us said anything.

"That's unfortunate," I said.

"Five lines."

"More unfortunate."

The Bailiff entered halfway through Debtor's Uncle with a folded notice,
asked which man was responsible for the debt, was pointed toward three
different people, became increasingly irritated, and finally left the
paper with Veya because she was the only person who would take it.

I had seen Pell do it once.

Pell had played the Bailiff like a man personally offended by
handwriting.

Teren gave me a plain dark vest.

"No coat."

"Authority is shrinking."

"Good."

The notice was a folded sheet with meaningless marks.

Real paper.

No mana.

I held it normally.

This was not difficult.

We walked the entrance twice because today's stool sat where I wanted my
crutch.

The first time I clipped it.

The second time I moved around it.

Teren moved the stool two inches anyway.

"That was my solution."

"It was a bad solution."

"It worked."

"It made noise."

"Temporary applause."

"No."

The house came in normally.

No one asked for Guild.

No boy came back.

A Vale cart passed the open front while I was taking one copper from an
older man.

I recognized the painted side mark.

It continued down the street.

Nobody inside it noticed me.

Antonius existed without participating.

This felt healthy.

Debtor's Uncle started.

I waited in the wing with the notice.

Evin complained about the debt.

Sellen denied owing it.

Veya said the house owed everyone.

That got a laugh.

My cue came.

I entered without hitting the stool.

Professional.

"I have a notice."

Evin pointed at Sellen.

"For him."

Sellen pointed at Evin.

"For him."

I looked between them.

"Which of you is poorer?"

Veya said, "Yes."

Good laugh.

That was written.

I opened the notice.

There was nothing useful on it.

Also written.

"Someone owes twelve copper."

Evin said, "Then it cannot be me."

"Why?"

"I owe more."

That landed.

Sellen looked offended.

"Braggart."

I turned to him.

"Your name?"

He gave the wrong name.

I looked at the paper.

"That is unfortunate."

"Why?"

"I can't read this one either."

Small laugh.

The next part was simple.

I was supposed to hand the notice to Veya.

Instead, I handed it to Evin.

No Bren.

No threat.

No traumatic folded cloth.

I simply gave it to the wrong person.

Evin looked down at it.

Then at me.

Veya held out her hand.

I took it back from Evin and gave it to her.

"Administrative correction."

The audience laughed more than the line deserved.

Veya said, "Do I owe it now?"

"Apparently."

"Efficient."

We moved on.

Backstage, Teren said, "Veya."

"I found her."

"Eventually."

"Administrative process."

He walked away.

Normal failure.

Beautiful.

King went next.

The strongest moment belonged to a leaking cup.

Not a magical cup.

Not a prepared effect.

A bad cup.

Hara poured water into it before going on.

Nobody noticed the thin crack near the bottom.

Marek sat in the King chair.

Evin entered with the cup.

Set it beside him.

Water began creeping across the table.

Slowly.

Marek kept talking.

Hara saw it first.

She was supposed to accuse Evin of stealing a ribbon.

Instead, while Marek spoke, she moved the cup three inches toward him.

The water followed.

Marek noticed.

He moved it back.

Still speaking.

Hara moved it toward him again.

The audience started laughing before Evin understood why.

Evin looked at the cup.

Then at Marek.

"Your Majesty leaks."

Marek looked down at himself.

"I do not."

Hara pointed.

"Your table disagrees."

Marek lifted the cup.

Water dripped onto his sleeve.

He stared at it.

Then handed it to Evin.

"Arrest the cup."

Evin held it away from his body.

"On what charge?"

"Treason."

That broke the room.

Hara had to turn away.

Marek kept his face straight.

Mostly.

The cup continued leaking onto the floor until Davin reached from the
wing and took it during a crossing.

No one died.

The King survived.

The table required a cloth.

Short followed with Nessa and Pell.

Pell had spent the entire interval asking whether the cup would be
traveling tomorrow.

Nessa told him no.

He asked if the child from yesterday would be traveling tomorrow.

No.

King hat?

Marek said no from across the room.

Pell had become very easy to manage.

At close, Rinna paid me one copper.

Normal local day.

I put it away.

No danger pay.

No private-job money yet.

Tomorrow's work did not pay today.

This also felt healthy.

Then tomorrow invaded again.

Nessa packed the costume box.

One.

She was unhappy about it.

"Veya's second dress stays."

Veya said, "Good."

"You say that now."

"I hate that dress."

"You look good in it."

"I hate it attractively."

Nessa accepted this as possible.

Davin and Jori put together the prop basket.

No cart.

Teren wrote the two-piece order.

KING.

WIDOW.

Rinna checked the client paper again.

"Meet here before midday," she said. "We walk from here."

Pell asked, "How long?"

"Long enough."

"Food?"

"Yes."

"Good."

"Do not tell anyone what they're paying."

"I don't know what they're paying."

"Excellent."

The cart remained inside.

Jori checked the replacement pin anyway.

Chalk straight.

He caught me watching.

"Still there."

"I know."

"You want to check?"

"No."

He looked disappointed.

Apparently my restraint was ruining community.

We left in the usual pieces.

Nothing happened.

No runner.

No lookout I could prove.

No man in a heavy coat waiting at the corner.

No one asked where we were playing tomorrow.

I passed a produce stall and bought two small carrots because they were
cheap and because Lyssa had recently demonstrated that food could
contain colors other than brown.

At home, she had not returned yet.

I cut the carrots more evenly than yesterday's onion.

This was progress nobody would record.

Lyssa came in after dark enough that I had started the beans.

She put her bag down harder than usual.

"Same length?" I asked.

"Yes."

"Did she agree?"

"Eventually."

"What changed?"

"I measured them in front of her."

"Were they the same?"

"Yes."

"So the measuring changed nothing."

"It changed her."

That sounded familiar.

She washed her hands.

"Your day?"

"New job tomorrow."

She looked over.

"Outside?"

"Copper Kettle. Wedding. Two pieces."

"Late?"

"Probably not very. We go after midday. Food."

"Cart?"

"No."

The question came immediately.

Before Bren, I was not sure it would have.

"Don't need it," I said. "One costume box. Prop basket. Tavern has
tables and lamps."

"Pay?"

"Rinna knows."

"You don't?"

"No."

"Good."

I looked at her.

"Why is that good?"

"Because you talk."

"Only when necessary."

She looked at me.

"Sometimes while asleep."

"Unverified."

She tore one carrot piece from the board.

"Bren?"

"Nothing."

"Pin?"

"Still with Rinna. Replacement still in cart."

"Vale?"

"Passed one of his carts."

"Did it rescue you?"

"No."

"Bad cart."

"Hessa?"

"Nothing."

She ate the carrot.

"Then wedding."

"Wedding."

It was good work.

That was the problem.

Not because work was dangerous.

Because good work was visible.

Havel had been visible.

The cart yard's good runs had been visible.

Tomorrow we would leave the hall carrying costumes and props and come
back after feeding fifty people laughter for money.

The easiest answer would have been not to go.

Rinna had not even seriously considered it.

Neither had I.

We needed the work.

More than that, the company wanted it.

Bren had asked for a share of something we had not earned yet.

Tomorrow we were going to earn it anyway.
