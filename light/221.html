TEXT READER · CHAPTER 221

THE PARTICIPANT

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The note was under the cup.

Lyssa found it while stealing the last heel of bread.

"Your physician has learned table manners."

"She isn't my physician."

"Your terrifying Guild woman, then."

"Better."

The note said:

AFTER MIDDAY. SAME ROOM. EAT FIRST.

Under that:

NO PRACTICE.

"Romantic," Lyssa said.

"She knows what I like."

"Restrictions?"

"Food."

"Cruel."

She was already dressed for customer work, carrying two wrapped bundles I had learned not to ask about unless I wanted the complete political history of cuffs.

Before leaving, she kissed my cheek and said, "Eat again before you go."

"Hessa wrote that."

"Then two women agree. Be afraid."

I was.

Mostly not about the experiment.

The theatre had no new message, no brown coat, and no Bren. Rinna had the Olin paper put away and treated the silence like information instead of relief.

Jori put me at the edge of the stage holding a painted wall while he replaced two leather ties.

"That knot is ugly," I said.

"You're holding canvas."

"I can still see."

"Then look somewhere else."

"It remains ugly when I do that."

For the next hour I was a clamp with opinions.

At midday Rinna gave me cheese and pointed toward Hessa's note when I tried to refuse.

"I regret sharing information," I said.

"Learn from Olin."

I ate the cheese and went to the Guild.

Hessa had preserved the room exactly enough to make me uncomfortable: same table, same glass, same paper fin, same control, same far marks on the floor.

"Nobody needed the room?" I asked.

"They did."

"And?"

"They used another room."

I considered the institutional power required to make that sentence happen.

"I want to become you."

"Sit down."

She checked position, symptoms, food, water, and the ordinary impossible presence of my phantom foot. Everything was quiet.

"Question?" she asked.

"Can far repeat."

"Under what conditions?"

"These. Away only. Same position."

"Good."

No intermediate test. No new distance. No stronger attempt. Hessa was asking one question and refused every opportunity I offered to make it more interesting.

The supervised minimal draw was clean. Thirty-one.

The first shaping attempt did not count because my shoulder braced before I noticed.

"Do you want a larger number or a usable record?" Hessa asked.

"Both."

"Choose."

"Usable record."

The second was clean.

Twenty-seven.

Then the glass went between me and the far paper.

Attempt thirty-five produced nothing.

Target still. Control still.

I rested until Hessa was satisfied that my forearm had stopped pretending work was an emergency.

Attempt thirty-six began with less hope.

This was probably useful.

I drew, shaped, held away.

For several seconds, nothing.

Then the target fin turned.

Not much. A small rotation with a little net movement away from me.

"Return," Hessa said.

I did.

The control had not moved.

She checked the glass, the marks, the paper, then wrote long enough to become offensive.

"Observed?" I asked.

"Yes."

"Away?"

"Net away. With rotation."

"What does it establish?"

"Nothing yet that the next attempt cannot make less simple."

There she was.

Far response observed again. Different session. Same apparatus.

She still would not let the sentence grow teeth.

Attempt thirty-seven produced nothing.

Target still. Control still.

My shoulder started to rise near the end. I noticed and lowered it.

Hessa noticed me noticing.

That did not make the paper move either.

"One of three today," I said.

"Yes."

"Two far responses total."

"Observed in separate sessions under the current apparatus."

"Far has repeated."

She considered that.

"A far response has now been observed in more than one session under the current apparatus."

"That is what I said with fewer words."

"No. You removed response, observed, session, and apparatus."

"They were implied."

"That is where bad records breed."

I looked at the paper.

Something had happened twice now at the far mark.

That did not make it a range. It did not make it reliable. It did not make it useful.

Those distinctions were becoming less annoying because I had started understanding why they existed.

Hessa's summary remained brutally unromantic:

FAR RESPONSE OBSERVED ACROSS MORE THAN ONE SESSION UNDER CURRENT APPARATUS.

FAR RELIABILITY NOT ESTABLISHED.

INTERMEDIATE REMAINS MORE RELIABLE ON CURRENT EVIDENCE.

NO RANGE ESTABLISHED.

NOT GENERALIZED.

I pointed at it.

"You could have written 'Greg is magnificent.'"

"Unsupported."

"Cruel."

"Also irrelevant."

The session ended at thirty-one supervised minimal draws, twenty-seven supervised shaping attempts, and thirty-seven supervised external-effect attempts.

The restrictions did not change.

No independent draw. No practice. No testing objects because I was curious.

"One tiny celebration?" I asked.

"You may eat."

"Again?"

"Frequently, if possible."

I returned to the theatre with the rest of the afternoon.

Rinna asked, "Useful magic?"

"One piece of paper moved a little."

"Can you do it again?"

"Sometimes. Under glass. In one room. With Hessa watching. At a marked distance. After being fed."

Rinna nodded.

"So no."

"I dislike how quickly everyone understands this."

"Can you carry pages?"

"Yes."

"Useful."

She handed me a stack.

I carried pages.

At home Lyssa had already occupied most of the table with cloth.

"Well?" she asked.

"Paper moved."

"Good."

"Once today."

"Less good?"

"Still good. Just specifically good."

"Did you hurt yourself?"

"No."

"Did she change the rules?"

"No."

"Then specifically good."

I sat across from her.

"It happened at far again."

"Far from what?"

I opened my mouth.

Closed it.

"The paper."

"How far?"

"A marked place in Hessa's room."

Lyssa looked at me.

I pointed at her.

"Exactly. This is why she won't call it a range."

"I didn't ask about a range."

"You accidentally became scientific."

"Terrible."

The brown-coat man had not produced another message. The theatre still stood. My arm felt normal.

Two far responses existed now.

Not reliability. Not a weapon. Not a solution.

More than once.

For tonight, that was enough.
