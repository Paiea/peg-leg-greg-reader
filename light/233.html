LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 233

THE REPLICATE

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

The note was under the door.

Not pinned to it.

Not under the cup.

Not balanced against the bread.

Under the door.

Hessa had apparently discovered mail.

AFTER MIDDAY.

SAME ROOM.

EAT.

LIGHT MORNING.

I read the last line twice.

Lyssa was already gone.

Her side of the bed had cooled. The table held bread, half an onion, my increasingly criminal shirt cuff, and nothing else that belonged to a customer.

No cloth.

No pins.

No chalk.

Yesterday the whole table had belonged to supper.

Today it belonged mostly to my bad sewing.

I touched the cuff.

Two loose threads looked back at me.

"Light morning," I told them.

They did not improve.

The room had no answer for what I was supposed to do with several hours in which nobody wanted labor from me.

Jori had said tomorrow maybe.

Maybe was not a call.

Vale had not sent anyone.

Hessa had specifically told me not to arrive tired, which made volunteering for office work feel like an attempt to lose an argument before it started.

I ate bread.

Then more bread because Hessa would ask.

I considered the onion.

The onion had done nothing wrong.

I left it.

Outside, Carrow had already decided to be busy without me.

A woman on the lower landing was washing a rug with enough water to create a private river down the steps. Two men carried a narrow cabinet through the street sideways and argued about whether sideways counted as progress. A boy walked behind them holding one drawer.

The drawer appeared to be winning.

I went nowhere important.

This was harder than it sounded.

I bought a small meat pie near the west market for later, then spent ten minutes explaining to myself that purchasing food before I was hungry was planning rather than moral collapse.

Antonius Vale did not appear from an alley to disagree.

Good.

I walked slowly enough that my shoulders stayed ordinary.

My right hand felt normal.

Wrist normal.

Forearm normal.

Right leg normal.

Residual limb comfortable.

Phantom foot absent until I checked for it, then present because apparently observation remained intrusive.

I sat on a low wall near a courtyard and read one of the old theatre pages I had found in my coat yesterday.

Five lines.

I still remembered four.

The fifth came back only after I looked.

This proved nothing.

A cart passed.

A dog barked at it.

The dog received no result.

By the time the sun had moved far enough to make after midday a defensible claim, I ate the meat pie and drank water from a public pump.

Then I went to the Guild.

The same room was waiting.

Actually the same.

Same narrow table.

Same board.

Same glass shield.

Same paper-fin target.

Same paper-fin control.

Same chair.

Same intermediate mark.

Same far mark.

Same little scratches in the floor around the chair legs.

Nothing had been improved.

This was becoming reassuring.

Hessa stood beside the board with three pages arranged in a line.

She looked at me.

"Work?"

"No."

That stopped her.

"No theatre?"

"Jori said maybe."

"Vale?"

"No."

"Carrying?"

"A meat pie."

She looked at my hands.

"Anything else?"

"My dignity."

"Sit."

There she was.

I put my crutches where they had gone before and sat.

The chair legs met the floor marks without adjustment.

My right foot went to its mark.

Hips square.

Forearm where she wanted it.

Shoulder down.

Hessa checked anyway.

"Food?"

"Bread. Meat pie."

"Water?"

"Yes."

"Sleep?"

"Normal."

"Right hand?"

"Normal."

"Thumb?"

"Normal."

"Wrist?"

"Normal."

"Forearm?"

"Normal."

"Shoulder?"

"Normal."

"Right leg?"

"Normal."

"Residual limb?"

"Comfortable."

"Phantom?"

"Quiet."

She wrote all of it.

I looked at the target.

"You chose."

"I did."

"One of the several questions."

"Yes."

"Do I get to know which one?"

She turned the top page around.

Four words had been written before I arrived.

INTERMEDIATE

FAR

INTERMEDIATE

FAR

I read them.

Then read them again.

"That's backward."

"From last time."

"Which makes it backward."

"Last time began far."

"I remember. Far, intermediate, far, intermediate."

"Yes."

"Today intermediate, far, intermediate, far."

"Yes."

I waited for more.

Hessa waited for me to stop expecting more.

I lost.

"Why?"

"Last session the apparently easier condition came second and fourth."

"Intermediate."

"Yes."

"Both moved."

"Observed response occurred on both intermediate attempts."

"Hostile sentence."

"Accurate sentence."

"And far did not behave."

"One far attempt produced no observed response with the control unchanged. One produced target and control movement and was contaminated for target-specific interpretation."

"I remember the little bastard."

"The control did its job."

"It moved."

"That was useful."

"To you."

"To the record."

Same thing, apparently.

I tapped the page.

"So now intermediate gets fresh Greg."

"Now intermediate comes first and third."

"And far gets second and fourth."

"Yes."

"Same distances?"

"Same marks."

"Same paper?"

"Same target and control."

"Same direction?"

"Away only."

"Same glass?"

"Yes."

"Same room?"

She looked around.

"I was checking."

"No new object?"

"No."

"No toward?"

"No."

"No farther far?"

"No."

"No secret third distance?"

"No."

"Then the question is whether the difference survives changing order."

"Approximately."

I leaned back.

"That is almost interesting."

"Do not improve it."

"I wasn't."

"You were about to."

Fair.

She tapped the order page.

"It was written before you arrived. Four external attempts if the apparatus and your body remain usable. No additional attempt because a result is inconvenient. No changing order because one condition responds."

"And if the control moves?"

"We classify the attempt from what happened."

"Repeat?"

"Not automatically."

Still hated that phrase.

Hessa moved the order page away from me.

"First the minimal draw."

"Of course."

"Restrictions have not changed."

"I know."

"Good."

That was not permission to become casual.

Thirty-two successful supervised minimal draws sat behind me.

The next one still required Hessa to watch.

I settled.

Breathed.

Let the room become small.

She gave the instruction.

I drew the smallest amount I could manage.

Clean.

Contained.

No thumb spread.

No wrist catch.

No shoulder movement.

Nothing strange in the residual limb or phantom foot.

"Return."

I returned.

Hessa watched the end of it.

No lingering sensation.

"Count."

Thirty-three successful supervised minimal draws.

She wrote.

I looked at the paper fins.

They remained unimpressed.

The shaping attempt came next.

No new form.

No stronger hold.

No clever variation because boredom had become dangerous.

I drew under supervision and shaped exactly what she asked for.

The sensation held cleanly.

My right shoulder stayed down without needing correction this time.

That felt like progress for almost half a second.

Then I remembered that the goal was not to feel impressed by my own shoulder.

Hessa watched my forearm.

"Return."

I returned.

She checked hand, wrist, forearm, shoulder.

"Any change?"

"No."

"Count."

Twenty-nine supervised deliberate shaping attempts.

Then the glass shield came between me and the paper.

The room changed.

Not physically.

Socially.

Hessa stopped being a woman who could be annoyed by my sentences and became a woman waiting for one small object to either move or not move while another object sat nearby for the sole purpose of ruining our confidence.

Attempt forty-two.

Intermediate.

Away.

The target sat at the intermediate mark.

The control sat where it always sat.

Hessa checked both against the board marks.

She checked the glass.

She checked the door.

Somebody crossed the corridor outside.

We waited.

The footsteps faded.

Hessa looked at me.

"Begin when ready."

I settled.

Intermediate first.

That mattered more to me than it should have.

The last two intermediate attempts had both produced clean observed movement.

I knew that.

Hessa knew I knew that.

The paper did not know anything.

Probably.

I drew.

Shaped.

Held away.

Nothing happened.

For long enough that I felt my mind begin trying to help.

Not my body.

My mind.

A little more.

A little harder.

Maybe the last session had been stronger.

Maybe I had been fresher after theatre than I was after a useless morning.

Maybe the angle was wrong.

Maybe.

The target moved.

Small.

Away.

Not much.

Enough that the edge crossed its mark.

The control stayed still.

I did not breathe until Hessa said, "Return."

I returned.

She stepped to the board.

Measured.

Checked the control.

Checked the glass.

Then checked again.

"Observed target response. Small net displacement away. Control unchanged."

I smiled.

Hessa looked at me.

I stopped.

"That was intermediate."

"Yes."

"First attempt."

"Yes."

"Fresh Greg."

She wrote.

"Do not name a condition after yourself."

"Too late."

She finished the line.

"Any body change?"

"No."

"Hand?"

"Normal."

"Wrist?"

"Normal."

"Forearm?"

"Warm."

"Different from ordinary effort?"

I considered.

"No."

"Shoulder?"

"Normal."

"Residual limb?"

"Fine."

"Phantom?"

"Quiet."

She wrote.

I looked at the order page.

Far next.

For one stupid second, the clean intermediate response made the next attempt feel like a test of a thing we already knew.

That was exactly why Hessa had written the order before I arrived.

Attempt forty-three.

Far.

Away.

Same target.

Same control.

Same glass.

Same body position except the target went to the far mark.

Hessa reset everything herself.

I watched.

"You trust me with nothing."

"Correct."

"Efficient relationship."

"Begin when ready."

I settled again.

Far had cleanly responded before.

More than once.

Not last session in a way we could use.

But before.

The paper knew none of that either.

I drew.

Shaped.

Held away.

The target did not move.

The control did not move.

I kept the effort where it belonged.

No leaning.

No shoulder rise.

No tiny attempt to become more persuasive because the paper was ignoring me.

Nothing.

Hessa called the stop.

I returned.

She checked.

"No observed response. Control unchanged."

"Far."

"Yes."

"Second."

"Yes."

"Pattern."

"No."

"Shape."

"No."

"Suggestion."

She looked at me.

I surrendered.

"Recorded event."

"Yes."

She wrote.

We stopped for water.

Not because I was hurt.

Because the plan included a break after two external attempts.

Hessa made me stand.

I used the crutches and walked the short length of the room twice.

My right leg had stiffened a little from sitting.

Normal.

It loosened.

My shoulders felt worked, but less than after theatre.

Hessa checked the residual limb before I sat again.

Comfortable.

No skin issue.

No pain.

No weakness.

No tingling.

No reason to stop.

I sat.

The third word on the order page waited.

INTERMEDIATE.

I liked it now.

That was the problem.

"You expect this one to move," Hessa said.

I looked at her.

"Are you reading my mind now?"

"No."

"Disappointing."

"Do you?"

I looked at the target.

"Yes."

She wrote something.

"That feels unfair."

"It is part of the session."

"What did you write?"

"That you said yes."

"Devastating record."

She reset the target at intermediate.

Attempt forty-four.

Away.

Same apparatus.

Same marks.

Same everything we could reasonably keep the same.

Except it was later.

Except I had seen one intermediate response.

Except I wanted another.

Hessa looked at me.

"Begin when ready."

I settled.

Draw.

Shape.

Away.

Nothing.

I waited.

Nothing.

The urge came quickly this time.

Increase effort.

Not much.

Just enough to make the expected thing happen.

I noticed the urge before I acted on it.

That was useful and irritating.

I kept the effort where it had been.

The target stayed still.

The control stayed still.

I could feel my attention narrowing around the paper as if dislike had mechanical force.

It did not.

Hessa called the stop.

I returned.

Nothing had moved.

She checked anyway.

Target on mark.

Control on mark.

Glass unchanged.

"No observed response. Control unchanged."

I stared at the paper.

"Intermediate."

"Yes."

"It didn't."

"No observed response."

"I saw."

"Good."

That was almost offensive.

I rubbed my thumb once.

Hessa's eyes dropped to it.

"Why?"

"Annoyance."

"Physical."

"No. Mental."

She held out her hand.

I gave her mine.

Thumb normal.

Grip normal.

Wrist normal.

Forearm warm.

Shoulder normal.

"Any discomfort?"

"No."

She let go.

I looked at the target again.

"So intermediate is not the good one."

Hessa sat back.

"It has produced more clean observed responses than far under this apparatus."

"That is not what I said."

"Correct."

"It failed."

"Yes."

"After working first."

"Yes."

"Which is annoying."

"Not evidentiary."

"It is evidence of my character."

"Unfortunately."

I looked at her.

There.

Definitely humor.

She refused to admit it.

One attempt remained.

Far.

Fourth.

The condition that had just failed was the one I had expected to work.

That made far feel less doomed.

Which was also probably useless.

Hessa reset the board.

We waited for a wagon outside to pass.

The room settled.

Attempt forty-five.

Far.

Away.

I began when ready.

Draw.

Shape.

Hold.

Nothing.

The paper remained a paper fin at a distance.

The control remained a second paper fin whose entire career consisted of not helping me feel special.

I maintained the effort.

No extra push.

No shoulder rise.

No change in posture.

Nothing moved.

Hessa called the stop.

I returned.

She checked target.

Control.

Marks.

Glass.

Then me.

"No observed response. Control unchanged."

"Far."

"Yes."

"Again."

"Yes."

That one hurt less than attempt forty-four.

I did not like what that said about expectation.

Hessa wrote the final line.

Forty-two: intermediate, small observed response away, control unchanged.

Forty-three: far, no observed response, control unchanged.

Forty-four: intermediate, no observed response, control unchanged.

Forty-five: far, no observed response, control unchanged.

Four clean attempts.

No control movement.

No apparatus disturbance.

No body change large enough to stop.

I read the list upside down from my chair.

"That is uglier."

"Good."

"You people need a new word."

"No."

"Last session intermediate was two for two."

"Yes."

"Today one for two."

"Yes."

"Far last time was one clean no-response and one contaminated attempt."

"Yes."

"Today two clean no-responses."

"Yes."

"So the difference is still there."

Hessa did not answer immediately.

That meant I had made the sentence too large.

I tried again.

"Intermediate has produced more clean observed responses than far under this setup."

"Yes."

"Across more than one session now."

"Yes."

"Intermediate also failed cleanly today."

"Yes."

"So intermediate response is not reliable."

"Correct."

"Far still has the earlier clean responses from other sessions."

"Correct."

"Today does not erase them."

"No."

"Today also does not strengthen far."

"Correct."

"No range."

"No range."

"Not generalized."

"Not generalized."

I leaned back.

The chair complained.

"Did reversing the order matter?"

"We do not know."

"Excellent."

"We now have one session beginning far and one beginning intermediate under the same four-condition structure."

"With different results."

"Yes."

"Which means?"

"It means we have those results."

I closed my eyes.

"One day I am going to make you say something satisfying."

"That would be poor supervision."

I opened them.

"Restrictions?"

"Unchanged."

"Independent draw?"

"No."

"Independent shaping?"

"No."

"Paper?"

"No."

"Coins?"

"No."

"Thread?"

"No."

"Larger objects?"

"No."

"External testing?"

"No."

"Practice?"

"No."

"Barrier?"

"No."

"Theatre?"

"No."

"Loopholes?"

"No."

"Good."

Hessa stacked the pages.

"That word finally useful?"

"No."

She checked my hand once more.

Then wrist.

Forearm.

Shoulder.

Right leg.

Residual limb.

No concerning change.

Thirty-three successful supervised minimal draws.

Twenty-nine supervised deliberate shaping attempts.

Forty-five supervised external-effect attempts.

The numbers had grown.

My permissions had not.

This was becoming the most reliable result in magic.

At the door I stopped.

"Do you still have several questions?"

"Yes."

"More or fewer?"

"Different."

That was somehow worse.

"Next?"

"Not decided."

"Again?"

"Yes."

I looked back at the board.

Intermediate had moved once and failed once.

Far had failed twice.

The control had done absolutely nothing all afternoon and therefore had an excellent day.

I left.

Outside, the sky had clouded without rain.

Carrow was entering the part of afternoon where everybody wanted to finish one thing before dark and therefore blocked each other in the street.

I bought nothing.

This was partly discipline and mostly because I had already bought a meat pie.

At home, Lyssa was not there.

The table had acquired cloth again.

Not much.

A narrow folded piece of brown lay near the wall with three pins stuck through one corner. My chair remained free.

Selective occupation.

I approved.

I put Hessa's note beside the basin and cut bread.

My cuff caught on the knife handle.

I looked at the two loose threads.

They had become three.

Evidence accumulated.

I did not repair it.

That was genuine restraint.

Lyssa came in after dark carrying a small bundle of turnips and no garment.

She saw me.

Then the bread.

Then the note.

"Paper?"

"Once."

She put the turnips down.

"Out of how many?"

"Four external attempts."

She took off her outer layer.

"Same distance?"

"Two intermediate. Two far."

"Which moved?"

"One intermediate."

"The other intermediate?"

"No."

"Far?"

"No."

She nodded.

That was it.

I waited.

She started cutting a turnip.

"You have no larger conclusion?"

"I am hungry."

"Hessa would hate your methods."

"Hessa told you to eat."

I looked at the note by the basin.

"She has spread again."

Lyssa pushed the cut turnip toward me.

"Rules?"

"Same."

"Good."

There was that word.

I ate.

The turnip needed salt.

We had salt.

I added too much.

Lyssa ate one bite, looked at me, and moved the bowl farther from my hand.

No one recorded the result.
