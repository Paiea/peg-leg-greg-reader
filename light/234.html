LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 234

THE CONDITION

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

Rinna had said no.

Vale had said maybe nothing.

Hessa had said nothing at all.

This produced a morning in which I was technically free and emotionally suspicious.

Lyssa was already gone when I woke. The brown cloth remained near the wall with the same fold and the same three pins. My damaged shirt remained on the shelf with the same three loose threads because I had finally discovered the repair method called leaving it alone.

I ate bread and one of the remaining onions.

The onions were winning the long war.

No note waited under the door.

I checked once.

Then I stopped checking because doing it twice would have become a habit and I already had enough of those.

Outside, Carrow had the clear cold light that made every wet stain on the stone look permanent. A cooper was rolling two empty barrels toward the lower lane. A woman had three chickens tied together by the legs and was losing an argument with all of them.

I went for breakfast that was not an onion.

This was my only plan.

I found a stall selling hot grain with chopped apple and bought a bowl. The woman gave me a wooden spoon with a crack down the handle.

I looked at it.

She said, "It holds."

"Strong standard."

"It holds."

I ate with it.

It held.

When I came home, a folded piece of paper was under the door.

Apparently timing remained a form of mockery.

I put the bowl down on the table and picked up the note.

Hessa's writing.

AFTER MIDDAY.

SAME ROOM.

EAT.

FOUR INTERMEDIATE.

I read the last line again.

Then I turned the paper over.

Nothing.

I looked toward the brown cloth.

"At least you are consistent."

The cloth declined comment.

Four intermediate.

No far.

No new mark.

No new direction written.

If Hessa had decided to invent a secret fifth distance, she had hidden it efficiently.

I did not try to design the session for her.

This was growth and also self-defense.

I spent the rest of the morning doing ordinary things badly enough to remain a person.

I washed the bowl from breakfast.

I swept half the room before discovering the broom was moving dust into the same corner the draft preferred.

I moved it somewhere else.

The draft won again.

I stopped.

I ate more bread before leaving because Hessa had written EAT in capital letters and I had no interest in sitting through the questions if I arrived hungry.

The walk to the Guild stayed ordinary.

Right hand normal.

Wrist normal.

Forearm normal.

Shoulders normal.

Right leg normal.

Residual limb comfortable.

The left crutch tip gripped the dry stone without comment.

My body had nothing new to contribute.

Good.

The same room was waiting.

Hessa had changed one thing.

The order page.

Everything else was where it had been.

Same chair.

Same marks in the floor.

Same board.

Same glass shield.

Same paper-fin target.

Same paper-fin control.

Same intermediate mark.

Same far mark, even though nothing sat there.

I looked at the empty far mark.

"Abandoned."

"No."

"Unused."

"Today."

"Better."

Hessa pointed at the chair.

I put the crutches in their usual place and sat.

My right foot found its mark.

Hips square.

Forearm where she wanted it.

Shoulder down.

She checked every part anyway.

"Food?"

"Grain. Apple. Bread."

"Water?"

"Yes."

"Work?"

"No."

"Theatre?"

"Rinna said no yesterday."

"Vale?"

"No."

"Carrying?"

"Breakfast."

She looked at my hands.

"Anything else?"

"An onion."

"Today?"

"No."

"Why did you say it?"

"I wanted credit for surviving it."

She wrote nothing.

Cruel.

"Hand?"

"Normal."

"Thumb?"

"Normal."

"Wrist?"

"Normal."

"Forearm?"

"Normal."

"Shoulder?"

"Normal."

"Right leg?"

"Normal."

"Residual limb?"

"Comfortable."

"Phantom?"

"Quiet."

She wrote the baseline.

I looked at the page beside her.

INTERMEDIATE.

INTERMEDIATE.

INTERMEDIATE.

INTERMEDIATE.

"That looks lazy."

"It is controlled."

"Same thing four times."

"Yes."

"No far?"

"No."

"No alternate order?"

"No."

"No farther far?"

"No."

"No new direction?"

"No."

"No larger target?"

"No."

"No different paper?"

"No."

"No exciting mistake?"

Hessa looked at me.

"Not planned."

"Good. I was worried."

She moved the page so I could read it properly.

"Last session established two useful things."

"Intermediate moved once."

"And did not move once."

"Far did not move twice."

"Yes."

"So intermediate won."

"No."

"I knew you'd say that."

"Then stop saying it."

I leaned back.

"The current apparatus has produced more clean observed responses at intermediate than far."

"Yes."

"Intermediate response has occurred across more than one session."

"Yes."

"Intermediate also failed cleanly."

"Yes."

"Therefore it is not reliable."

"Yes."

I pointed at the four identical lines.

"So now you want to know whether it behaves when we stop changing distance."

"Approximately."

"That is almost satisfying."

"Do not improve it."

There she was.

Hessa tapped the page.

"Today does not compare distances."

"Because far isn't tested."

"Correct."

"It does not establish range."

"Correct."

"It does not establish that intermediate always works."

"Clearly."

"It does not establish why it works when it works."

"No."

"It asks whether clean response continues to occur when we repeat the same intermediate condition in one session."

"Yes."

I considered the paper fins.

"Four?"

"Predeclared."

"Break?"

"After two external attempts."

"Extra attempt if all four fail?"

"No."

"Extra attempt if all four work?"

"No."

"Repeat one because I dislike it?"

"No."

"Good supervision remains hostile."

"Minimal draw first."

Of course.

Thirty-three successful supervised minimal draws sat behind me.

The number sounded large until Hessa gave the instruction and the actual task became small again.

I settled.

Breathed.

Drew the smallest amount I could manage.

Clean.

Contained.

No thumb spread.

No wrist catch.

No shoulder movement.

No change in the residual limb.

No phantom response.

"Return."

I returned.

Hessa watched the end of it.

"Any lingering sensation?"

"No."

"Count."

Thirty-four successful supervised minimal draws.

She wrote it.

Then shaping.

Same deliberate form as before.

No new shape.

No stronger hold.

No longer duration merely because the count had increased.

I drew under supervision and shaped exactly what she asked.

The sensation held where it should.

My shoulder stayed down.

My wrist remained quiet.

"Return."

I returned.

Hessa checked my hand, wrist, forearm, shoulder.

"Change?"

"No."

"Count."

Thirty supervised deliberate shaping attempts.

Then the glass shield came between me and the target.

Hessa moved the paper fin to INTERMEDIATE.

The control remained in its established place.

The far mark stayed empty.

Attempt forty-six.

INTERMEDIATE.

AWAY.

Hessa checked target and control against their marks.

She checked the glass.

She checked the door.

The room was quiet.

"Begin when ready."

I settled.

This should have felt easier than far.

That thought arrived without permission.

I noticed it.

I did not tell the paper.

Draw.

Shape.

Away.

Nothing.

I held the effort where it belonged.

Nothing.

The target remained on its mark.

The control remained on its mark.

My mind started offering suggestions.

More effort.

Different angle.

Longer.

Every suggestion was a way to stop doing the test we had actually planned.

I let them pass.

Hessa called the stop.

I returned.

She checked the target.

Then the control.

"No observed response. Control unchanged."

"Intermediate."

"Yes."

"Again."

"Later."

She wrote.

I looked at the page.

One identical word had already produced a different feeling than I had expected.

Annoying.

Hessa checked my hand.

"Normal."

"I didn't ask yet."

"It is."

"Forearm?"

"Warm. Ordinary effort."

"Wrist?"

"Normal."

"Shoulder?"

"Normal."

"Residual limb?"

"Comfortable."

"Phantom?"

"Quiet."

Attempt forty-seven.

Same target position.

Same control.

Same glass.

Same body geometry.

Same direction.

Same stupid paper.

Hessa reset the target even though it had not moved.

She checked the mark anyway.

I watched.

"Do you expect it to move?" she asked.

That was new.

I looked at her.

"You did not ask that last time."

"I am asking now."

"Is that another condition?"

"No. It is a note."

I looked at the target.

"Less than before."

"Why?"

"The first one didn't."

She wrote.

I disliked being preserved.

"Begin when ready."

I settled.

Draw.

Shape.

Away.

For several seconds, nothing.

Then the target shifted.

Small.

Enough to be obvious because I had spent too much of my life staring at paper.

It moved away from its mark and turned slightly at the end.

The control did not move.

I returned when Hessa told me.

She crossed the room.

Measured.

Checked target.

Checked control.

Checked glass.

Then checked all three again.

"Observed target response. Small net displacement away. Minor rotation. Control unchanged."

I smiled.

Hessa looked at me.

"I am allowed to have a face."

"Yes."

"Good."

She wrote.

Attempt forty-six, no response.

Attempt forty-seven, response.

Same intermediate condition.

That was already enough to make the room feel less interested in my preferences.

We took the planned break.

I stood with both crutches and walked the room twice.

Right leg stiff from sitting.

Normal.

It loosened.

Forearm warm.

Normal.

Shoulders worked, not painful.

Residual limb comfortable.

No weakness.

No tingling.

No reason to stop.

Hessa made me drink water.

I did.

"Any change from before the external attempts?"

"Forearm warmer."

"Expected?"

"Feels like ordinary effort."

She wrote it.

I sat again.

The order page still had two identical words left.

INTERMEDIATE.

INTERMEDIATE.

"Do you expect the third one to move?" Hessa asked.

I looked at her.

"You've made this worse."

"Answer."

"Yes."

"More or less than attempt forty-seven?"

"More."

"Because forty-seven moved."

"Yes."

She wrote.

I pointed at the page.

"That is going to become embarrassing later."

"It is already data."

"Hostile profession."

Attempt forty-eight.

Target at intermediate.

Control unchanged.

Glass unchanged.

Door shut.

Same chair marks.

Same body position.

AWAY.

I began.

Draw.

Shape.

Hold.

Nothing.

I waited.

The urge to help arrived faster because I expected a response.

A little more would probably do it.

That was the thought.

Not knowledge.

Not evidence.

Not permission.

I kept the effort where it had been.

The target remained still.

The control remained still.

Hessa called the stop.

I returned.

She checked.

"No observed response. Control unchanged."

I laughed once.

Not because it was funny.

Because the paper had excellent timing.

Hessa looked at me.

"You expected response."

"Yes."

"And?"

"It did not care."

She wrote that too.

"Please don't write 'did not care.'"

"I didn't."

"Good."

She checked my hand and wrist.

No change.

Forearm warm.

Shoulder normal.

Right leg normal.

Residual limb comfortable.

One external attempt remained.

Attempt forty-nine.

Same intermediate mark.

Same target.

Same control.

Same glass.

Same AWAY direction.

Hessa reset everything.

Then she looked at me.

"Expectation?"

I considered lying for variety.

"Less."

"Why?"

"Because forty-eight didn't move."

She wrote.

"I am very predictable."

"Not the target."

That landed.

I looked at the paper.

"Rude."

"Begin when ready."

I settled.

The room became small.

Not exciting.

Not mystical.

Chair.

Glass.

Paper.

My own hand.

The sensation I could produce under supervision and still did not understand well enough to trust.

Draw.

Shape.

Away.

Nothing.

I held.

Nothing.

My forearm warmed another degree.

The target stayed where it was.

The control stayed where it was.

Hessa called the stop.

I returned.

She checked both fins.

No movement.

"No observed response. Control unchanged."

I nodded.

That one did not sting much.

Maybe expectation mattered to disappointment even when it did not matter to paper.

Hessa finished the line.

Forty-six: intermediate, no observed response, control unchanged.

Forty-seven: intermediate, small observed response away, minor rotation, control unchanged.

Forty-eight: intermediate, no observed response, control unchanged.

Forty-nine: intermediate, no observed response, control unchanged.

One clean response.

Three clean non-responses.

No control movement.

No apparatus disturbance.

Same condition repeated four times.

I read the list.

"Ugly."

"Useful."

"You need another word."

"No."

I leaned back.

"Intermediate can still respond."

"Yes."

"It did today."

"Yes."

"Under the same apparatus."

"Yes."

"Without far between attempts."

"Yes."

"So far wasn't causing the intermediate response."

Hessa did not answer.

I closed my eyes.

"Too large."

"Yes."

I tried again.

"Today shows an intermediate response can occur in a block where far is not tested between attempts."

"Yes."

"Better."

"Accurate."

"Intermediate also failed three times in the same block."

"Yes."

"So unreliable remains."

"Yes."

"More unreliable?"

"No."

I opened my eyes.

"Because we do not have a defined rate."

"Among other reasons."

"Does this weaken the distance difference?"

"Not by itself."

"Because we did not test far today."

"Correct."

"Does it strengthen intermediate existence?"

Hessa considered.

That meant the wording mattered.

"It adds another clean observed intermediate response under the current apparatus."

"Across another session."

"Yes."

"So the response has repeated across sessions and within a same-condition block."

"Observed response has occurred across sessions. Today contains one response within a repeated intermediate block."

I stared at her.

"You built that sentence to hurt me."

"I built it to be true."

Fair.

I looked at attempts forty-six through forty-nine.

One movement.

Three nothing.

"Could fatigue explain the three failures?"

"No."

"Could expectation explain them?"

"No."

"Could anything?"

"Many things could."

"Useful."

"We did not test those things."

There it was.

Hessa stacked the pages.

I looked at her hand.

"Next question?"

"Not chosen."

"Different again?"

"Yes."

"More different?"

She did not answer.

I accepted defeat.

"Restrictions?"

"Unchanged."

"Independent draw?"

"No."

"Independent shaping?"

"No."

"Paper?"

"No."

"Coins?"

"No."

"Thread?"

"No."

"Larger objects?"

"No."

"External testing?"

"No."

"Practice?"

"No."

"Barrier?"

"No."

"Theatre?"

"No."

"Loopholes?"

"No."

I nodded.

"Thirty-four, thirty, forty-nine."

"Yes."

The counts had grown again.

Nothing else had.

This no longer surprised me.

Hessa checked my body one final time.

Right hand normal.

Thumb normal.

Wrist normal.

Forearm warm from effort.

Shoulder normal.

Right leg a little stiff from sitting.

Residual limb comfortable.

Phantom quiet.

No concerning change.

At the door, I looked back at the empty far mark.

"Do we still think distance matters?"

Hessa did not look at the board.

"Under the current apparatus, the accumulated record still supports a difference associated with distance."

"Associated."

"Yes."

"Not range."

"No."

"Not reliable intermediate."

"No."

"Not generalized."

"No."

"Good."

Hessa looked at me.

I smiled.

"Still hate the word."

"Leave."

I left.

Carrow had reached late afternoon while we were arguing with one piece of paper.

The streets were busy enough that I had to wait at two crossings. A cart loaded with clay jars blocked half the lower lane. Someone above me shook a rug out a window and created weather from dust.

I bought a small piece of cheese and ate it walking slowly home.

No debt calculation.

No reward calculation.

Cheese.

At the apartment, Lyssa was still out.

The brown cloth remained.

Three pins.

Same fold.

I looked at it.

"One of us is reliable."

I regretted the sentence immediately.

I ate the rest of the cheese.

By dark, Lyssa had not returned.

That happened.

I put beans in the pot, cut one onion, and used less salt than before.

The room became warmer.

Someone downstairs laughed hard enough to cough.

A cart rattled past.

No note came under the door.

When Lyssa finally returned, she looked tired in the ordinary way, not injured. She had no bundle.

She looked at the pot.

"Beans?"

"Improved beans."

She tasted them.

"Beans."

Cruel but fair.

We ate.

She did not ask about Hessa.

I did not volunteer.

Halfway through the bowl she noticed the folded note beside the basin.

"Paper?"

"One."

She looked at me.

"Out of four?"

"Yes."

"Same distance?"

"All intermediate."

She nodded and kept eating.

That was the entire analysis.

After supper she moved the empty bowls aside and put both elbows on the table.

I did the same.

The brown cloth stayed by the wall.

Three pins.

Still nothing I understood.

I looked at Lyssa.

She looked back.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

For once, the most reliable thing in the room was not the paper.
