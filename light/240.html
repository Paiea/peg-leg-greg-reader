LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 240

THE BEARER

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

The pages were still on the table when I woke.

That was useful because I had put them there for exactly that reason.

The brown cloth was also still near the wall.

Three pins.

Same fold.

My damaged shirt remained on the shelf with three loose threads.

Lyssa was gone.

No beans.

Apparently tomorrow had kept them.

I did not investigate.

I read The Missing Key again while eating bread.

Eight lines.

Three entrances.

Messenger.

The second entrance still came sooner than my body wanted it to. The first was simple. Deliver a note. Leave.

The second came after barely half a page.

The third had enough room before it that I could forget I existed.

Dangerous structure.

I read the second cue twice, folded the pages, and left before I could turn eight lines into a military campaign.

The morning was cool and dry. Carrow had already filled the lower streets with carts, baskets, shouting, and one man carrying a chair on his head because civilization was flexible.

My neck was fine.

Shoulders normal.

Right hand normal.

Residual limb comfortable.

Left crutch tip boring.

Good.

The theatre side door opened cleanly when I pushed it.

I stopped.

Closed it.

Opened it again.

No catch.

Jori, somewhere behind the stage, said, "Yes."

I looked into the room.

"Was that for me?"

"The latch."

"Fixed?"

Davin appeared in the rear passage with a small file in one hand.

"Mostly me."

Jori said, "Mostly the file."

Davin looked at the file.

"Then pay it."

I went inside.

The narrow door frame from yesterday now stood onstage with an actual door hanging in it. The door had been painted a tired brown that looked respectable from ten feet away and diseased from three.

Nessa was at the property table with three folded notes, a brass-colored key, and the short dark-green coat.

She pointed at the coat.

"On."

"Good morning."

"Coat."

"Warmer."

She looked at me until I put my pages down.

The coat fit across my shoulders better than the black Magistrate coat had. Short hem. Clear of the crutches. Deep inside pocket on the right.

I put one hand into it.

Nessa slapped the outside of the pocket.

"Not yours."

"I was checking."

"You found it."

She handed me the first folded note.

"One."

Then the second.

"Two."

Then the third.

"Three."

They were not identical.

The first was folded once.

The second twice.

The third had one corner tucked under.

I looked at her.

"Useful."

"Yes."

"Did you do that for me?"

"No. I did it for the play."

Fair.

I put them into the inside pocket in order.

Marek came through the stage door wearing a pale vest and carrying the brass-colored key.

Nessa held out her hand.

He stopped.

"I just got here."

"Key."

"I need it."

"Later."

"It is called The Missing Key."

"And this one is not missing yet."

He gave it to her.

Healthy system.

Hara sat at the edge of the stage tying the repaired shoe tighter. The front no longer opened when she flexed it.

"Comfortable?" I asked.

"No."

"Still attached?"

"Unfortunately."

Pell was reading near the first row.

Teren came in from the house aisle with his own pages.

"Places."

No speech.

No encouragement.

The profession remained efficient.

The Missing Key was worse than I had understood at home.

This was praise.

Hara played the Mistress of the house.

Marek played her younger brother, who had borrowed the key to a locked writing cabinet and then denied borrowing anything.

Pell played a family clerk who kept an inventory of objects nobody else respected.

My Messenger worked for someone who was not in the play and had apparently spent the entire day carrying increasingly unhelpful notes to the same address.

The missing key mattered because the Mistress needed a paper from the cabinet before an aunt arrived.

The aunt never appeared.

The paper might not have mattered.

Nobody knew.

The cabinet was represented by a narrow painted box on the side table.

The first note said the absent master had taken the key with him.

The second said he had sent it back yesterday.

The third said it was under the blue vase.

There was no blue vase onstage.

There had been.

Marek's character had broken it before the play began.

Excellent construction.

We started with entrances.

I waited outside the brown door frame with the first note in my pocket.

Hara and Marek argued about the cabinet.

Pell read from the inventory.

Hara said, "Then where is the key?"

I entered.

Teren said, "No."

I stopped.

"I have a message."

"You have a door."

I looked at the door.

"Yes."

"Use it."

"I did."

"You entered like the wall had failed."

I looked back at the frame.

The door was still swinging.

Apparently I had pushed harder than necessary.

Again.

Hara: "Then where is the key?"

I opened the door.

Too slowly.

Teren said, "No."

I looked at him.

"Strong range."

"You're deciding the entrance matters."

"I am entering."

"Correct."

That was not helpful.

Hara said, "He means stop presenting the entrance."

Teren looked at her.

She shrugged.

"Did I improve it?"

"Unfortunately."

Again.

This time I opened the door and came through it because I needed to be on the other side.

Better.

I crossed toward Hara.

The easiest route took me in front of Pell.

Jori said, "Back."

I backed up.

He pointed at the space behind Pell's chair.

"Through there."

The lane was wide enough if I kept the right crutch close.

I tried it.

Clear.

No chair.

No coat hem.

No foot.

"Keep," Teren said.

Nessa put a chalk nick near the door side of the route.

Not a speech.

A place.

Again.

I entered, used the lane, stopped beside Hara, and held out the first note.

"Message for this house."

Hara took it.

I waited.

She opened it.

Read.

Looked at Marek.

I waited more.

Teren said, "Why are you still there?"

"I want to know what it says."

"Greg does."

"Messenger also might."

"Messenger has work."

I looked at the door.

"Exit."

"Yes."

I left.

That was the correction.

Not mysterious.

Deliver.

Leave.

The second entrance was worse because the scene had barely recovered from the first note.

Marek insisted the absent master had the key.

Pell found a receipt showing the key had been sent.

Hara asked who had brought the receipt.

Pell said, "A messenger."

I entered.

Teren said, "No."

I stopped in the doorway.

"That is literally me."

"That is not your cue."

I looked at the page.

My cue came two lines later.

Marek had to say, "Then find the fool who carried it."

I closed the door.

From the stage Hara said, "Convenient."

"Hostile workplace."

Again.

This time I waited through "A messenger."

Waited through Hara asking which messenger.

Waited through Marek saying he did not care.

Then:

"Then find the fool who carried it."

I opened the door.

"Again?"

Small laugh from Hara.

Not audience.

Just Hara.

Teren let it stand.

I handed the second note to Pell.

Then I stayed to see him open it.

Teren pointed at the door.

Right.

I left.

Third entrance.

By then the brother had admitted he might have touched the key.

The Clerk had established that touching and borrowing were different entries in the household accounts.

Hara was considering murder.

My third note came after a longer exchange.

I almost relaxed too much.

Nessa caught me reading over Marek's shoulder from offstage.

"Ready."

"I am."

"You're not."

She tapped my pocket.

I had put the third note in the wrong inside fold after the second pass.

I checked.

First note gone.

Second note gone.

Third note had slid flat under my pages.

I fixed it.

"Thank you."

"Pocket."

There were relationships built entirely from nouns.

We ran it again.

Then again.

The door stopped being an event.

The notes stayed in order.

I left after delivering them.

My second entrance waited for the actual cue instead of the word messenger.

By the fourth pass, Teren stopped stopping me.

This felt suspicious.

At midday, Rinna came in with bread, two apples, and no new information.

I knew that because nobody asked.

She set the food down.

Marek reached for an apple.

Rinna slapped his hand with a folded handbill.

"Paid performers first."

"I am a performer."

"Not paid yet."

"Technical distinction."

Hara took the better apple.

Of course.

I sat on the stage edge to rest my right leg.

Ordinary stiffness.

Shoulders worked.

Right palm warm from the crutches.

Nothing concerning.

Pell sat one seat below me.

"Messenger."

"Clerk."

He nodded.

We had communicated fully.

Rinna looked at Teren.

"Keep it?"

"Maybe."

I looked at the pages.

"Performance?"

Teren looked at me.

"Eat."

Clear.

We ate.

Nobody said brown coat.

Nobody said lamp glass.

Nobody said front door or back door except Davin, who came through the repaired rear passage and told Jori the back door now shut properly.

Jori went to test it.

It did.

They moved on to the next problem.

By early afternoon, Teren had decided.

"The Missing Key stays."

Marek raised both hands.

"Justice."

Teren ignored him.

Rinna looked at me.

"Messenger."

"I remain."

"Today."

"Yes."

Nessa took the dark-green coat off me, brushed chalk from one sleeve, and handed it back.

Apparently that had been a reset.

The first piece was one Greg was not in.

Good.

I spent it backstage with the coat on a peg and the three message props on Nessa's table.

Marek was in the first piece too.

He came off after one scene sweating under the pale vest.

"Audience good."

"How many?"

"Audience."

"Useful."

"They laughed."

"At you?"

"Near me."

More honest than expected.

Hara came through behind him.

"Not at him."

There it was.

When The Missing Key came up, the set changed quickly.

Brown door frame.

Two chairs.

Small table.

Painted cabinet.

No blue vase.

The missing vase was represented by its absence, which was economical.

Nessa placed the three notes in my coat pocket herself.

"One. Two. Three."

"I know."

"I know you know."

This was somehow worse.

The house quieted.

Hara and Marek began.

From offstage, I could hear the play find its shape.

Marek was better with an audience.

Not because he became smarter.

Because his denial grew more confident every time the room doubted him.

Pell entered with the inventory book.

Hara asked where the key was.

My first cue.

I opened the door.

Not important.

Just a door.

Cross behind Pell.

Stop.

"Message for this house."

Hara took the note.

I left.

The audience barely noticed me.

Good.

Behind the flat, Nessa held up two fingers.

Second note.

I put my hand into the pocket and found the double fold.

The scene moved faster than rehearsal.

That was the first problem.

Pell said, "A messenger."

My body prepared.

Not cue.

Hara asked which messenger.

Marek said he did not care.

"Then find the fool who carried it."

I opened the door.

"Again?"

The audience laughed.

More than Hara had.

Not a lot.

Enough to make the next beat feel longer.

I crossed.

Handed Pell the note.

Then made my mistake.

I stayed.

One beat.

Two.

I wanted to hear the laugh settle.

I wanted to see Pell open the note.

I had spent the whole morning being told not to do exactly this.

Teren was not visible.

He did not need to be.

Hara looked at me.

Not Greg.

Messenger.

She held out one hand.

"Unless you've brought the key too?"

Written line.

My exit was supposed to happen before it.

Because I was still there, the question landed directly on me.

I said my next written line too early.

"No key."

That line belonged to the third entrance.

Pell looked at me.

Marek looked at me.

I knew immediately.

The audience did not.

Hara did.

She looked at my empty hand.

Then at Pell's note.

Then said, "Wonderful. We have acquired a messenger who spoils tomorrow."

The room laughed.

Not a giant explosion.

A good one.

She turned away from me.

That was my exit.

I took it.

Backstage, my face was hot.

Nessa held out one hand.

I stared at her.

"Third note."

Right.

I took it from my pocket and gave it to her.

"No."

She put it back into my hand.

"Keep it."

"I just used the line."

"Not the note."

Correct.

The scene continued.

I had created a problem.

Hara had continued.

Important distinction.

I stood by the door and listened.

Pell read the second note.

The absent master claimed he had sent the key yesterday.

Marek's brother said that proved nothing.

Hara asked what exactly would prove something.

The audience laughed before the next line because they had learned him.

Good.

My third cue approached.

I had already spoken "No key."

The third entrance still existed.

The note still existed.

The remaining lines still existed.

I did not invent a replacement.

Marek admitted that there had once been a blue vase.

Hara became very still.

Pell checked the inventory.

"Blue vase. Hall table."

Hara said, "Where is it?"

Marek said, "Define is."

Big laugh.

Mine came after.

I opened the door.

Third entrance.

The audience recognized me.

A smaller laugh started before I spoke.

I did not wait for it.

"Last message."

Hara looked at me.

"Does it contain the key?"

That was supposed to set up the line I had already used.

No key.

I could not say it again without making the mistake visible.

For half a second, I had nothing.

Then Pell took the note from my hand.

Not a rescue flourish.

He simply took it because the Clerk had taken the previous one.

"Perhaps we should read it."

His line was not written there.

It worked.

Hara turned to him.

The scene moved.

I left.

Pell opened the third note and read the actual message.

"The key is under the blue vase."

Silence.

Hara looked toward the empty table.

Then at Marek.

Marek looked at the empty table too.

"Define under."

The audience broke.

Hara did not hurry.

She walked to him.

Slow.

Held out one hand.

Marek reached into his vest and produced nothing.

Other pocket.

Nothing.

Then he looked toward Nessa's property table offstage as though the missing vase might be hiding there.

Hara said, "Which piece?"

That was written.

It owned the room.

Marek's answer disappeared under the laugh.

He waited.

Good.

Then tried again.

The scene finished with Pell discovering the cabinet had never been locked.

The brass-colored key had belonged to the hall cupboard.

Hara opened the painted cabinet with one finger.

Looked inside.

Looked at Marek.

Closed it.

Curtain.

The audience gave us the kind of applause a short stupid play deserved.

Warm.

Not immortal.

Enough.

Backstage, I took off the green coat.

Nessa checked the pocket before taking it.

All three notes accounted for.

"Good," she said.

I looked at her.

"Don't."

She almost smiled.

Teren found me while the others were resetting.

"You stayed."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"The laugh."

"Yours?"

"Partly."

"Then leave with it."

I nodded.

That was specific enough to hurt.

"I also used the third line."

"Yes."

"Pell continued."

"Yes."

"Hara too."

"Yes."

I waited.

Teren looked at me.

"Again tomorrow if it stays paired."

Not praise.

Not punishment.

A work condition.

"Same role?"

"Unless I change it."

"Useful."

"Don't improve it."

There he was.

Rinna paid me one copper.

I put it away.

No Vale calculation.

No threat calculation.

Money.

The back latch closed cleanly when people began leaving.

I noticed because yesterday it had not.

That was all.

Hara sat on the stage edge retightening the repaired shoe.

I stopped beside her.

"Thank you."

"For what?"

"Continuing."

She looked at me.

Then at Teren across the room.

"Did he make you say that?"

"No."

"Then you're getting strange."

"Already happened."

She nodded.

"Fair."

Marek appeared with the brass-colored key.

Nessa appeared behind him.

He put it down without being asked.

Growth.

Possibly fear.

I left before anyone found more work.

At home, Lyssa was already there.

The brown cloth was still near the wall.

Three pins.

Same fold.

My damaged shirt still had three loose threads.

Lyssa had a bowl in front of her and something wrapped in paper near her elbow.

Not beans.

I looked at it.

She looked at me looking.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"You were going to."

"Maybe."

She ate.

I put The Missing Key pages on the table.

"Show?"

"Yes."

"Good?"

I took bread.

"Hara was."

Lyssa nodded as if this confirmed an existing law.

"I was useful."

"Less good."

"Relationship damaged."

She ate another spoonful.

I sat across from her.

No report about lamp glass.

No analysis of doors.

No magic.

No Vale.

After a minute she pointed at the pages.

"Tomorrow?"

"Maybe."

"Then don't lose them."

I looked at the title.

The Missing Key.

I looked back at her.

She had already gone back to eating.

I kept the pages where I could see them.
