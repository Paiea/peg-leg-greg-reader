LIGHT EDITION · CHAPTER 244

THE UNCLE

Text-first edition · no chapter illustrations

The theatre wanted me before I wanted breakfast.

This was poor scheduling.

The boy knocked while I was cutting bread and said, "Rinna."

He had learned enough not to wait for me to ask who.

"What did I do?"

"Nothing."

"Then why is she sending you?"

He looked at the piece of paper in his hand.

"Teren wants you before midday."

"Role?"

He read.

"Uncle."

I looked at him.

"Whose?"

He checked the paper again.

"It just says Uncle."

"Strong family."

He held the page out.

I took it.

The note said:

IF FREE.
BEFORE MIDDAY.
READ FIRST.
UNCLE.

No play title.

No line count.

No warning about clothes, paint, flowers, keys, magistrates, landlords, or other professions I had acquired without training.

The boy was already turning away.

"Wait."

He stopped.

"Performance?"

He shrugged.

Of course.

"Rehearsal?"

Another shrug.

"Are you paid by the answer?"

"No."

"Excellent system."

He left.

Lyssa had gone earlier. The brown cloth remained folded near the wall with its three pins. My damaged shirt still had three loose threads. The better one still had blue paint on the sleeve.

I wore the blue one.

The market onions from yesterday sat in the basket.

Three good.

No dying administrator among them.

The right crutch grip disappeared under my hand when I stood.

Fine.

I ate bread, drank water, and read the theatre note one more time in case UNCLE had become more specific.

It had not.

Outside, Carrow had already started without me.

A baker's assistant carried a flour sack against one shoulder. Two women stood beneath an upstairs window arguing with somebody they could not see. A dog slept in a doorway until the shopkeeper opened it, then moved exactly far enough to remain inconvenient.

I went west.

The theatre front doors were open.

No audience.

No handbills.

No one waiting with a threat report.

Inside, the stage held a table, four chairs, a narrow sideboard, and a covered serving dish large enough to make me suspicious immediately.

That was probably the problem.

Nessa stood at the property table with three wooden plates and a cloth napkin over one shoulder.

Venn was onstage carrying a pot.

Marek sat in one of the chairs eating something from his hand.

Teren saw me from the aisle.

"Good."

I hated that word everywhere now.

"Uncle?"

He held out pages.

"Read."

The play was called The Guest Pot.

That was not better.

I found UNCLE in the margin.

Eleven lines.

One entrance.

One exit.

Mostly seated.

Promising.

Hara played the woman of the house. Marek was her younger brother. Venn played the household cook.

The plot was simple enough to become dangerous.

Hara's character had promised her visiting uncle a large supper.

There was no supper.

The cook had been told the wrong day.

The brother had spent the food money on something described only as "necessary business," which meant the play did not respect him enough to specify.

The covered serving dish onstage was empty.

Everyone except my character knew this.

I looked at the dish.

Teren said, "Stop."

I looked at him.

"I haven't started."

"Exactly."

He pointed at the dish.

"You read ahead."

"That is generally encouraged."

"Not with your face."

Hara, sitting at the stage edge, said, "He does have one."

"Temporary," I said.

Teren handed me the pages.

"Again. From the entrance."

"We have not done the first one."

"Then this will be educational."

I went to the side door.

Nessa caught my sleeve.

"Chair."

She pointed to the near side of the table.

The chair had been angled slightly away from the table, leaving a clearer lane for my crutches and enough space to sit without moving the furniture during the scene.

Jori was near the wing with a short board and chalk.

I looked at him.

He looked at the chair.

"Fine."

That was the entire design review.

I went out.

Teren called, "Begin."

Hara and Marek were already arguing.

"He's early," Marek said.

"He is on time."

"That is early for family."

"Only yours."

Venn came in carrying the pot.

Hara looked at him.

"Well?"

Venn looked into the pot.

Then at her.

"No."

Marek said, "That is not a useful answer."

Venn put the pot on the sideboard.

"It is the most useful answer I have."

Small laugh from Hara.

Not audience.

Still deserved.

Then my knock.

Hara called, "Come in."

I entered.

I had eleven lines.

I also had a covered serving dish sitting three feet from my future chair and the knowledge that it contained nothing.

I looked at it.

Teren said, "Stop."

I closed my eyes.

"I need eyes less."

"You need the uncle to have your eyes."

"He can have different ones?"

"He hasn't read the play."

I looked at the dish again.

"Enviable."

Teren stepped onto the stage.

"You are coming for supper."

"Yes."

"You expect supper."

"Yes."

"Then why are you checking whether the supper is a lie before you sit?"

Because I knew.

That was the problem.

"I am protecting myself."

"The uncle isn't."

"Poor man."

"Again."

We reset.

This time I entered and looked at Hara first.

Then Marek.

Then the chair.

The serving dish remained at the edge of my vision where it belonged.

Hara crossed to greet me.

"Uncle. You made excellent time."

"My stomach kept the appointment."

That got a small laugh from Marek, who had heard the line before.

Hara said, "Sit. Supper is nearly ready."

I sat.

The chair angle worked.

My crutches settled to the side without touching the table.

I looked at Hara.

Not the dish.

Teren said nothing.

Progress through deprivation.

The scene continued.

Marek's brother tried to explain why no bread had arrived.

Venn's cook came in and out with increasingly useless objects.

A spoon.

An empty bowl.

A folded cloth.

At one point he returned with a cabbage leaf.

My line was, "Is that the first course?"

Venn looked at the leaf.

"No. It is evidence."

I laughed.

Teren stopped us.

"Greg."

"That is funny."

"Your character is hungry."

"He can still appreciate craft."

"Not before his line."

I looked at Venn.

He was not smiling.

Professional betrayal.

Again.

Venn entered with the cabbage leaf.

I looked at it.

Waited.

"Is that the first course?"

"No. It is evidence."

This time I let Hara react first.

She took the leaf from him.

"Evidence of what?"

"That there was once a cabbage."

Better.

Marek's character said he could explain.

Hara said, "You have been explaining since breakfast. It has not produced lunch."

That was the strongest line so far.

Mine were fine.

I began to understand the job.

Hungry.

Expectant.

Increasingly suspicious, but only after the room gave me reasons.

Not suspicious because I had pages.

Teren ran the covered-dish section.

Hara finally told Venn to bring supper.

Venn went to the sideboard.

He lifted the covered serving dish with both hands.

Brought it to the table.

Set it in front of me.

The lid stayed on.

My line was, "That smells subtle."

Marek laughed again.

Teren ignored him.

Hara said, "Family recipe."

Then the next written beat belonged to Venn.

He was supposed to stop Hara before she lifted the lid and admit there was nothing inside.

Instead, during the first run, his sleeve caught the edge of the napkin.

The napkin pulled.

One wooden plate slid off the table.

It hit the stage floor.

Did not break because it was wood.

Still loud.

Everyone looked down.

Teren said nothing.

Venn picked up the plate.

Put it back.

Then said his line.

"There is one problem."

Hara looked at the plate.

"Only one?"

That was not written.

Marek laughed.

I did too.

Teren let the scene continue.

Venn said, "The dish is empty."

Hara looked at him.

Then at Marek.

Marek's character immediately stood.

"I should check the street."

Hara caught his sleeve.

"No."

Audience or no audience, that was the shape of the play.

The brother tried to leave.

The house would not let him.

The uncle remained hungry.

The cook remained honest enough to make everyone else uncomfortable.

At the end, Hara's character lifted the empty dish lid anyway.

She looked inside.

Then at me.

"My apologies."

My last line was, "I have had lighter suppers."

Small.

Good.

Marek's character offered to fetch food.

Hara said, "With what money?"

He sat again.

End.

Teren looked at us.

"Again."

No speech.

We went again.

The second run went cleaner.

I did not look at the dish early.

Venn did not catch the napkin.

Marek remembered that he was trying to conceal the missing money rather than advertise guilt through posture.

Hara improved without announcing it.

On her first line after my entrance, she stopped moving before she said my stomach had kept the appointment.

The stillness gave the line more room.

Teren did not comment.

He did not need to.

After the run, he changed two crosses.

One was mine.

He moved my entrance line half a step wider around the table so my right crutch did not come close to Marek's chair when Marek stood too early.

Jori shifted the chair by less than the width of his hand.

Then he chalked the rear leg.

No accommodation speech.

The scene simply had geometry.

Rinna appeared during the third run.

She stood at the back with her ledger.

I saw her.

The uncle did not.

I kept looking at Hara.

Teren noticed.

"Good."

There it was again.

After the scene, Rinna said, "Paired."

I looked at her.

"Today?"

"Yes."

"Performance?"

"After the short piece."

"Pay?"

She looked at me.

"Yes."

"Healthy theatre."

Marek said, "He asks now."

"I have learned the dangerous connection between labor and food."

Rinna left.

The performance changed the room.

Not dramatically.

Chairs became audience chairs.

The stage floor got swept.

Nessa checked plates.

Venn checked the pot.

Hara disappeared into costume and came back wearing a brown house dress with pale cuffs.

Marek acquired a vest that made him look more trustworthy than the play allowed.

My Uncle got a dark coat with a short hem and no important pockets.

Nessa said, "Sit."

I sat.

She adjusted one sleeve.

"Fine."

"Strong character work."

"Coat."

"Right."

The first short piece went on without me.

I stayed backstage with Venn.

He had flour on one sleeve that did not belong to any prop I had seen.

I pointed at it.

"Real?"

He looked down.

"Breakfast."

"Excellent continuity."

He wiped at it.

Made it worse.

I respected him more.

From the stage, Hara delivered a line in the short piece and the audience laughed.

Venn listened.

Not with panic.

Just enough to know where the play was.

I asked, quietly, "How long have you been here?"

He looked at me.

"Theatre?"

"Yes."

"Before you."

"That is not a length."

"It is enough."

I considered arguing.

The short piece onstage reached a laugh.

Venn looked back toward the sound.

Conversation concluded.

The short piece ended.

Applause.

Movement.

Reset.

Then The Guest Pot.

I waited by the side door.

The audience settled.

Hara and Marek began.

The first laugh came on "That is early for family."

Bigger than rehearsal.

Venn's first "No" got another.

Good room.

My knock.

"Come in."

I entered.

Hara.

Marek.

Chair.

Not dish.

"Uncle. You made excellent time."

"My stomach kept the appointment."

Laugh.

Small.

Mine.

I sat.

The chair worked.

The crutches stayed clear.

I listened.

That was the job.

For three minutes I did it correctly.

Marek lied about bread.

Venn brought the spoon.

The bowl.

The cabbage leaf.

"Is that the first course?"

"No. It is evidence."

I waited for Hara.

She took the leaf.

"Evidence of what?"

"That there was once a cabbage."

Good laugh.

Hara: "You have been explaining since breakfast. It has not produced lunch."

Bigger.

Hers.

Correct.

Then the covered dish.

I knew the beat.

The audience did not.

Venn brought it.

Set it down.

I smelled nothing.

My line:

"That smells subtle."

A laugh began.

And I looked at the lid.

Not long.

Not dramatically.

Just early.

The audience saw it.

I knew because the laugh shifted.

A few people laughed again before Hara spoke.

They were ahead.

Only a little.

Enough.

Hara said, "Family recipe."

The second laugh was smaller than rehearsal had promised.

My stomach dropped.

Not Greg's stomach.

The uncle's remained hungry.

I kept the scene.

Venn stepped in.

"There is one problem."

Hara looked at him.

This time the napkin stayed where it belonged.

"The dish is empty."

The audience laughed.

Still.

Less surprise than it should have had.

Marek stood too quickly.

"I should check the street."

Hara caught his sleeve.

"No."

That got them back.

Good.

The scene moved.

Hara questioned him.

He folded.

Venn stood beside the sideboard with the expression of a man who had reported a fire and declined to apologize for heat.

The audience liked him.

Near the end, Hara lifted the lid.

Empty.

She looked inside longer than she had in rehearsal.

Then looked at me.

"My apologies."

My last line.

"I have had lighter suppers."

Laugh.

Fine.

Then Marek offered to fetch food.

Hara asked, "With what money?"

He sat.

The audience hit hard.

That was hers.

The play ended.

Backstage, I came off knowing exactly what I had done.

Teren waited until the applause had started before speaking.

"You looked."

"Yes."

"Before the cook."

"Yes."

He nodded.

That was almost worse than a lecture.

"I knew it was empty."

"The uncle didn't."

"I know."

"Good."

I stared at him.

He walked away.

Of course.

I had looked at a prop too early in a comedy.

That was enough failure for one profession.

After curtain, work resumed.

Nessa stacked plates.

Venn carried the empty dish back to the property table.

Marek tried to steal the cabbage leaf.

Nessa said his name.

He put it down.

Hara came off with the strongest applause still following her.

I said, "You recovered the lid."

She looked at me.

"It was still empty."

"Audience knew."

"Some did."

"Because me."

"Some."

She removed one cuff pin from her costume.

"Then don't look tomorrow."

I stopped.

"Tomorrow?"

Hara shrugged.

"I don't schedule you."

Right.

Rinna appeared with coins.

One copper for me.

Normal performance day.

I took it.

"Tomorrow?"

"Not paired yet."

There it was.

Maybe.

Not a promise.

"Uncle?"

"Maybe."

"Same piece?"

"Maybe."

"Healthy certainty."

Rinna had already moved to Venn.

She paid him too.

Good.

The company existed in multiple directions.

I helped Jori move my chair back after the audience cleared.

The chalk mark on the rear leg came up with the cloth.

The floor kept a faint white line.

Jori rubbed it once with his boot.

Mostly gone.

The covered dish returned to the sideboard.

Empty.

Still not a mystery.

Outside, evening had cooled the street.

My right palm was ordinary.

Shoulders used but fine.

Right leg mildly tired from standing between scenes.

Residual limb comfortable.

Left tip normal.

Both grips normal.

No new problem.

I bought a small piece of cheese on the way home.

I spent ordinary money on cheese.

The debt remained elsewhere.

At the apartment, Lyssa was already there.

No customer.

No new dark cloth.

The brown cloth remained folded with three pins.

My damaged shirt remained at three loose threads.

She saw the coat pages before the cheese.

"Theatre?"

"Uncle."

She looked at me.

"Whose?"

"Exactly."

I put the cheese on the table.

She cut bread.

I told her the play was about an empty supper.

She looked at the cheese.

"Did you eat?"

"Yes."

"Good."

I waited.

"That is the wrong response."

"You ate."

"I performed hunger."

"Hard."

"Thank you."

She ate cheese.

After a while I said, "I looked at the empty dish too early."

Lyssa looked at me.

"You knew."

"Yes."

"Then don't."

I stared.

"Teren made me work much harder for that answer."

"Smart."

"Hostile."

She ate cheese.

I laughed.

The brown cloth remained where it was.

The blue streak remained on my better shirt.

The onions remained onions.

No Hessa note arrived.

No Vale work appeared.

No brown coat came to the door.

Tomorrow The Guest Pot might exist for me.

Or not.

Rinna had said maybe.

I had become old enough in Carrow to know what maybe was worth.

I ate the cheese before it became evidence.
