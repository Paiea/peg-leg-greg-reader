TEXT READER · CHAPTER 251

THE UNDERSTUDY

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

I learned thirty-eight of the Uncle's forty-three lines before sleep.

This was either excellent or terrible depending on which five remained.

Lyssa woke me by moving around the room quietly enough that I noticed only after she had already dressed.

The pages were beside my hand.

I had apparently gone to sleep with one finger holding my place.

Useful technique if I wanted to learn lines through skin.

I sat up.

My neck objected.

"Morning," Lyssa said.

"Debatable."

She had the darker spool in her work bag now. The reddish-brown sample was still gone. Brown cloth, three pins. Damaged shirt, three threads.

I checked my body because that remained a thing bodies required.

Right shoulder stiff from sleeping badly, not injured. Hands fine. Right leg fine. Residual limb fine. No headache. No mana-related anything.

Five lines.

I picked up the pages.

Lyssa looked at me.

"Food first."

"I know five lines badly."

"Food."

"Possibly six."

She put bread in front of me.

I ate while reading.

This produced neither good eating nor good reading.

The first missing line was in scene one and began with, "A table is an agreement."

I knew the rest after that.

The second was an answer to Hara.

The third was not actually missing. I had combined it with the next line because Pell always paused between them and apparently my memory had stored his pause as punctuation.

That left three.

Lyssa took the page out of my hand.

"Hey."

"Say them."

"What?"

"Without paper."

I stared at her.

She waited.

Fine.

I started at the beginning.

Not performing.

Just words.

The first seven came easily.

Eight arrived late.

Nine through fourteen held.

At fifteen I stopped.

Lyssa looked at the page.

"You have mistaken silence for agreement."

"That's Hara's line."

"Then answer it."

I did.

She continued reading the other parts when I needed cues.

Badly.

Not because she could not read.

Because she read every character as Lyssa.

The young wife sounded like Lyssa.

The brother sounded like Lyssa.

The servant sounded like Lyssa with less patience.

"You are ruining the company," I said.

"Line."

I gave her the line.

We reached scene two.

One of the missing three returned when she gave me the cue.

The next did not.

She read it.

I repeated it.

"Again," she said.

I repeated it again.

"From before."

"You have become Teren."

"No."

Correct.

We went from two lines earlier.

This time it stayed.

The last missing line was near the end.

I knew the joke before it.

I knew Hara's answer after it.

The middle remained blank.

Lyssa read it.

"That's stupid."

She looked at me.

"The line."

"Then remember it."

Cruel.

I said it twice.

Then once without looking.

Forty-three lines.

Probably.

Lyssa gave the pages back.

"Food."

I looked down.

I had eaten the bread.

"Done."

She checked the plate as though I might have hidden it.

"Good."

"I am developing a reaction to that word."

"Good."

She left for the north side.

I left for the theatre.

The walk gave me twenty minutes to discover that knowing forty-three lines at a table did not mean I knew forty-three lines while moving uphill on crutches.

I lost three.

Recovered two.

Lost another.

Recovered the first.

By the theatre door I had stopped counting because the number was becoming personal.

Nessa was inside.

Of course she was.

The stage was half lit. Davin was carrying a narrow bench. Jori had one knee on the floor beside the dinner table, tightening something beneath it.

Marek was not there yet.

This improved the morning briefly.

Nessa looked at me.

"Pell's still sick."

There it was.

"Fever?"

"Lower. His sister says he's eating."

"Good."

I heard myself.

Nessa did too.

Neither of us commented.

"Teren wants the whole piece after warm-up."

"Pages?"

"Available."

Not forbidden.

Available.

Different problem.

I put my coat away and went to the stage.

Jori stood.

"Mark's still yours."

I looked down.

The chalk mark from yesterday remained six inches wider than Pell's old one.

A little scuffed.

Still visible.

"For now," I said.

Jori had already gone back under the table.

That was apparently too much philosophy for furniture.

Marek arrived carrying nothing.

I distrusted this immediately.

"Where's the hat?" I asked.

"Nessa has it."

"Growth."

"I learned."

Nessa called from the wing, "You lost it after cleanup and I found it on the prop shelf."

Marek looked at me.

"Different kind of learning."

Teren arrived before I could answer.

He had coffee or something pretending to be coffee in a cup that did not belong onstage.

"Words?" he asked.

"Mostly."

"How mostly?"

"Forty-three in a room. Fewer while walking."

"Good."

I closed my eyes.

He said, "Start with scene two."

Of course.

Not scene one, where I was strongest.

Scene two.

We set.

Hara arrived while Nessa was checking the cups. She took one look at me in the Uncle's chair and said, "Still alive."

"Pell or me?"

"Both."

Useful.

The other actor joined us.

Marek took the Steward's tray.

Teren stood in the aisle.

"No pages unless you need them."

"That is a dangerous amount of freedom."

"Start."

We started.

Scene two began with Hara trying to leave.

Yesterday I had spent most of the scene trying not to forget Pell's words.

Today the first line arrived before I reached for it.

Then the second.

Then Hara answered and something changed.

Not much.

I heard her.

Actually heard her.

Yesterday her lines had been landmarks between mine.

Today they were accusations.

The Uncle had invited everyone to dinner and then spent the evening insisting that attendance meant agreement.

That was funny because he did not understand why anyone kept trying to leave.

I had known that yesterday.

Knowing and playing were apparently separate industries.

Hara said, "You cannot seat a person into consent."

I answered, "I can seat you into supper. The rest is your own bad judgment."

The line got no laugh because there was no audience.

But Hara's mouth changed.

She was no longer carrying me.

She answered harder.

I leaned back.

Not like Pell.

Pell leaned forward when the Uncle felt challenged. I had copied that yesterday because I had seen it a hundred times.

I leaned back because this Uncle did not think he was challenged yet.

He thought everyone else was being difficult in his house.

Hara stopped after the next exchange.

Teren did not.

We continued.

Marek entered.

Correct door.

Tray flat.

Red cup.

I took it without looking at him.

He waited.

Hara put hers back.

He left with everything.

Good Steward.

Still annoying.

The missing line came halfway through the scene.

I felt the blank open.

Yesterday I had looked empty and Hara had built me a bridge.

Today I knew where the pages were.

Side table.

Three steps.

I also knew the scene.

Hara wanted to leave.

The Uncle wanted her to stay because leaving would mean the dinner had failed and he had not yet admitted failure was possible.

The exact words remained gone.

I said, "Sit down. You can hate me after dessert."

Not the line.

Hara stared at me for half a beat.

Then answered with her actual next line.

"I have managed both at once so far."

The scene continued.

Teren let it.

That worried me more than stopping would have.

We finished scene two.

"Again," he said.

I looked at him.

"From where?"

"The missed line."

I went to the pages.

Found it.

Read it.

"Oh."

The actual line was better.

Annoying profession.

"Again," Teren said.

We took it from two lines earlier.

This time I had the words.

Hara answered.

The scene moved.

Teren said, "Keep the first one tonight if you lose it again."

I stared at him.

"My wrong line?"

"Your replacement line."

"It was worse."

"Yes."

"Then why keep it?"

"Because stopping is worse."

Specific.

Not permission to rewrite the play.

I nodded.

He took a drink.

"Again from the top."

We ran the whole piece.

I lost no lines in scene one.

In scene two I used the correct line.

In scene three I discovered a new problem.

I was early.

Not on an entrance.

On everything.

Yesterday I had waited for words because I needed them to arrive.

Today I knew them, so I started stepping on the ends of other people's lines.

Teren stopped us the third time.

"Greg."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"I'm early."

"Why?"

"Because I know what comes next."

"Then stop proving it."

Hara looked at the ceiling.

I pointed at her.

"You enjoyed that."

"Very much."

Teren said, "Again."

We went again.

I waited.

Not theatrically.

Just long enough for the other person to finish having their line.

Remarkable technology.

The scene improved.

Then I overcorrected and left a hole large enough for Marek to look toward the wing.

"Not that long," Teren said.

"Useful range."

"Find it."

We did.

By midday, I could get through the play without pages.

Not perfectly.

Without pages.

That distinction mattered.

Teren called break.

I sat on the edge of the stage with my crutches beside me and drank water.

Marek sat two feet away.

"You sound different."

I looked at him.

"From yesterday?"

"From Pell."

There it was.

I waited for the insult.

He ate something from a paper twist.

"Pell sounds angrier."

"The Uncle is angry."

"Later."

I thought about it.

Pell's Uncle entered irritated.

Mine entered certain.

Neither choice was necessarily better.

One belonged to Pell.

The other had happened because I had stopped copying him.

Marek held out the paper twist.

Nuts.

I took two.

"Thanks."

"Mine is better."

"Your Steward?"

"Yes."

"Than mine?"

"Yes."

I chewed.

"Explain."

"I don't talk as much."

I nearly choked.

Hara walked past us.

"He's right."

"This company has become unsafe."

Marek looked delighted.

Then Nessa called him because one of the trays had a loose binding.

He went.

I watched him go.

He knew the route now.

He knew the cups.

He knew to wait for Hara's return.

He had learned my role by watching me, then learned it again by doing it badly enough for Nessa to correct him.

That was how most things worked here.

I had simply been on the other side of it yesterday.

Rinna arrived during the afternoon run.

She listened from the back for half a scene, then left to speak with someone at the side door.

I recognized the lamp-glass seller when she came farther inside.

Not because she had become important.

Because I had bought lamp glass from her and Carrow was becoming full of people I could place.

She and Rinna spoke quietly near the door.

I heard none of it.

Good.

I was currently supposed to be ruining a dinner.

We finished the run.

Rinna was alone again by the time I came offstage.

She had a small wrapped parcel in one hand.

"Glass?" I asked.

"No."

That was the entire answer.

I did not ask again.

This required less effort than it once would have.

Maybe I was becoming old.

No.

Different problem.

Before doors opened, Teren gave me one note.

"Don't make him cleverer than he is."

I frowned.

"I'm not."

"You are starting to enjoy being right."

"The Uncle is right about some things."

"Yes."

"Then?"

"He thinks that means he's right about the room."

I considered that.

Hara was nearby adjusting a sleeve.

I looked at her.

"Are you going to enjoy this too?"

"Already am."

Fine.

Teren pointed toward the stage.

"He wants the dinner to work. Let him want that."

That I understood.

No life lesson.

Dinner.

I could do dinner.

The audience came in.

Not full.

Not thin.

Enough people to make the room become performance instead of rehearsal.

The Second Toast was second on the bill tonight.

I waited backstage with the Uncle's hat on my head.

Nessa had adjusted it again because yesterday's folded cloth had shifted.

"Better?" she asked.

I moved my head once.

"Yes."

"Don't give it to Marek."

"I have learned."

Marek said from behind me, "That's mine."

"No," Nessa and I said together.

He looked offended.

Then we were called.

I took the chair.

Crutches against the stool.

Right foot on Jori's mark.

Pages on the side table.

I had asked Nessa to leave them there.

Not because I expected to need them.

Because removing them to prove I did not need them would have been stupid.

Progress.

The lights shifted.

We began.

The opening line came.

Then the next.

I did not think about Pell.

That was new.

The first scene moved around the table.

The Uncle corrected the seating.

Hara ignored him.

He corrected it again.

Someone in the audience laughed before the chair gag arrived.

Small laugh.

Mine.

I felt it.

Dangerous.

I did not chase it.

The scene kept moving.

Marek entered through the correct door.

Tray flat.

Blue cup.

I took it.

He left with everything.

No one applauded his tray.

Good theatre.

Scene two.

Hara tried to leave.

I told her she could not seat a person into consent.

No.

She told me that.

For one horrifying instant the lines crossed in my head.

Then she said hers.

I answered mine.

Clean.

The audience laughed at the next exchange.

The missing line approached.

I knew it was coming.

That almost made it worse.

Hara gave me the cue.

The actual line arrived.

I said it.

No rescue.

No replacement.

We moved on.

That should not have felt as good as it did.

It did.

Scene three.

I waited for the other actor to finish.

Not too long.

Just enough.

Teren had somehow made pauses measurable without numbers.

The final toast approached.

I stood on the wider mark.

Hara's timing shifted around my cross.

Not to save me.

Because this was now the timing we had rehearsed.

The Uncle raised his cup.

For the first time since Pell had gotten sick, I was not trying to get through Pell's role.

I was playing the fucking scene.

The line landed.

Hara answered.

The other actor cut in.

The dinner collapsed exactly as written.

The audience laughed.

We finished.

Applause.

Ordinary again.

Maybe a little stronger.

I could not tell from inside it.

That was probably healthy.

Backstage, Nessa took the hat.

I gave it to her immediately.

"Growth," I said.

"Minimum standard."

Marek put down the tray.

"I was better."

"You were quieter."

"Exactly."

Hara came through.

I looked at her.

She said, "You didn't need me."

There were several answers available.

Most of them annoying.

"Not tonight."

She nodded once.

Then added, "You were early in three."

There it was.

"Once."

"Twice."

"One was enthusiasm."

"Two."

She left.

Teren found me after the next piece.

I was moving a chair with Jori.

He waited until we set it down.

"Better."

One word.

I wanted more.

This was embarrassing.

"How?"

He looked at me.

Apparently I had requested a note and would now suffer the consequences.

"Yesterday you knew where the play went. Today you were in it."

I waited.

"And?"

"And you were early twice."

Hara had infected him.

"Anything else?"

"Don't ask for praise until I invent some."

He walked away.

Jori picked up the chair again.

I helped him.

That should have annoyed me more.

It did not.

Rinna paid after cleanup.

Same arrangement as yesterday.

Marek received the Steward share.

I received the Uncle's share for the piece plus the rest of my work.

Another slightly better day of money.

Two days did not become a salary.

Pell's fever did not become my opportunity simply because I benefited from covering him.

Still.

The coins were real.

I put them away.

Rinna said, "Pell's sister thinks tomorrow or the day after."

"Good."

"You can stop saying it like you're surprised."

"Everyone has been using it against me."

"No one is using good against you."

"That's what makes it effective."

She had already moved on to Marek's pay.

Outside, the night was colder than yesterday.

I took the wider route home.

My head was tired in a way Hessa's experiments did not produce.

Not pain.

Crowded.

Forty-three lines.

Three scenes.

A wider chalk mark.

Hara changing timing around my cross.

Marek learning the Steward.

Two days of better pay.

Pell getting better somewhere else.

The needle case did not occur to me until I was almost home.

That seemed worth noticing.

Then I noticed that I had noticed it, which ruined the achievement.

At home, Lyssa was there.

The darker spool was not.

Neither was the reddish-brown sample.

The brown cloth remained folded near the wall with three pins.

My damaged shirt remained on the shelf with three loose threads.

I took out the pages.

Lyssa looked at them.

"Need?"

"No."

"Then why have them?"

I considered this.

"Insurance."

She nodded.

I put them on the table.

Then I took out my pay and put it with the rest.

More than yesterday morning.

Still not enough to become careless.

Enough that the difference existed.

Lyssa was heating something in the pan.

"Pell?" she asked.

"Better. Probably back tomorrow or the day after."

"You?"

I sat.

"Better too."

She looked over.

I waited for it.

"Good," she said.

I pointed at her.

"Hostile household."

She smiled and went back to the pan.
