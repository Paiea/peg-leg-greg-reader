TEXT READER · CHAPTER 318

THE EMPTY PEG

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The problem with the return trip was that somebody else had not returned.

Not us.

We were exactly where we were supposed to be.

This was apparently insufficient.

Daro had the cart loaded before sunrise.

Return freight was less impressive than outbound freight.

Three sacks of something that smelled medicinal.

Two narrow crates.

A bundle of bent metal hoops tied together with rope.

One covered basket that occasionally clicked.

I did not ask about the clicking basket.

This required effort.

Sevren noticed.

"Sick?"

"Growing."

"Looks painful."

"Deeply."

Bell Ford's bridge hummed under the first cart before dawn.

We crossed west after the road worker cleared Daro.

The bridge note was higher with the lighter load.

I noticed.

Did not investigate.

By full morning Bell Ford was behind us.

The road rolled through orchards and low stone fields.

My body had accumulated the trip.

Not damage.

Accumulation.

Right thigh tighter.

Back aware of the bench.

Palms normal because cart sitting was not crutch walking.

Residual limb warm under the trouser leg but comfortable.

The folded seat cloth did its job.

I changed position before discomfort became a debate.

Old Greg had been very good at ignoring his body until it became strategically inconvenient.

Current Greg had fewer spare parts and better habits.

Progress.

Daro stopped twice before midday.

Water for the striders.

Wheel check.

Harness check.

People check only in the broad sense that none of us had fallen off.

At the second stop, Sevren walked fifty yards down the road and back.

I joined him.

Not because he needed company.

Because my right hip wanted movement.

"Bell Ford worth it?" he asked.

"You already asked."

"Checking if bridge got worse overnight."

"Bridge remains acceptable."

"High praise."

"Town stew also acceptable."

"Careful. You'll become a travel writer."

"I have standards."

We turned back.

The clicking basket clicked.

I looked at it.

Sevren said, "No."

"I said nothing."

"Your face."

"My face has been persecuted this entire trip."

Daro climbed back onto the front board.

"Bees."

I stopped.

"What?"

"Basket."

"Those are very large clicks."

"Not bees inside. Bee clasps."

That explained almost nothing.

I accepted it.

Growth continued.

We reached the unnamed relay house from the outbound trip near midday.

No overnight this time.

Daro changed a wheel pin.

Sevren exchanged the dark blue Bell Ford token for a pale wooden marker.

I ate included bread and cheese.

Confirmed.

Nobody was surprised anymore.

Then we kept west.

Three Wells appeared in late afternoon.

The three stone wells caught the lowering sun.

The relay house roof looked bluer than I remembered.

Daro pulled into the yard.

"Night here."

Expected.

My body approved.

Sevren did not answer.

He was looking at the relay wall.

A row of wooden pegs ran beneath a shallow roof.

Most held something.

Tokens.

Small leather message sleeves.

Colored cords.

One peg near the center was empty.

Sevren walked to it.

Touched the little number plate below.

Then looked toward the east road.

"What?" I asked.

He did not answer immediately.

A relay clerk came out carrying a slate.

Older woman.

Short hair.

Ink stain on one cuff.

She looked at the empty peg.

"Not in."

Sevren said, "I see."

"Expected fourth bell."

I looked at the sky.

Fourth bell had passed.

Not by much.

"Who?" Sevren asked.

"Pell road. Naro run."

Names.

Not my names.

The clerk checked her slate.

"No delay mark from east post."

Sevren rubbed his jaw.

"How long?"

"Your cutoff is sixth."

"I know."

"Then why ask?"

"Hoping rule changed."

"It did not."

The clerk went back inside.

I looked at Sevren.

"Your transfer?"

"Maybe."

"Maybe?"

"If Naro has west packet, I take it to Carrow. If not, empty peg stays empty."

"And if he arrives after sixth?"

"Next west runner."

"You wait until sixth."

"Yes."

"Then leave."

"Yes."

Simple.

Except Sevren kept looking at the east road.

I recognized that too.

Rules could be simple while waiting remained annoying.

We had nearly two bells.

Daro took the striders to the stable side.

I put my bag in the relay sleeping room.

Lower platform available.

Good.

Then I came back out.

Sevren sat on a low wall facing east.

I sat beside him.

"You do this often?"

"Wait?"

"For missing runners."

"Enough."

"Usually late?"

"Sometimes."

"Usually problem?"

"Usually mud. Wheel. Animal. Clerk who thinks five minutes is not late because clocks are oppressive."

"Reasonable clerk."

"No."

We waited.

Carts entered.

People drew water.

A stable boy chased one road strider around a post while the animal moved exactly fast enough to remain annoying without becoming difficult.

A woman sold boiled roots from a pot.

I had no money.

The roots smelled excellent.

This was educational.

Sevren had money.

He bought two.

Handed me one.

I looked at it.

"Loan?"

"Friendship."

"More dangerous."

"Eat."

I ate.

Hot.

Pepper.

Good.

"I owe you?"

"No."

"Recorded."

"You are unbearable."

"Also recorded."

Fifth bell rang.

No rider.

The relay clerk came out once.

Looked east.

Looked at Sevren.

Said nothing.

Went back inside.

I asked, "If you leave at sixth and he arrives one minute later?"

"He misses me."

"You don't get blamed?"

"Not if I mark out correctly."

"And if you wait?"

"Daro leaves tomorrow after first bell. Carrow board expects my west token. If I delay without authorization, that becomes my delay."

There it was.

Not courage.

Not loyalty.

Work boundaries.

The late rider's problem did not automatically become Sevren's problem simply because Sevren could imagine helping.

I had opinions about this because I had spent one life collecting other people's problems until they became a career.

"You want to wait," I said.

"I am waiting."

"Past sixth."

Sevren looked at me.

"Maybe."

Interesting.

"Why?"

"Naro's usually clean."

"Meaning?"

"If he's late without mark, something unusual happened."

"That argues for concern."

"Yes."

"Not necessarily for you waiting."

He gave me a long look.

"You become wise on one bit road trip?"

"I spent the bit. Wisdom was included."

He snorted.

We waited.

A cart came from the east.

Not Naro.

Two women.

Empty produce racks.

Sevren watched them pass.

Fifth bell and half.

The clerk came out with the slate.

"Still nothing."

"I see."

"If no mark by sixth, you mark empty and go."

"I know."

"Saying it for your friend."

She looked at me.

I lifted both hands as much as crutches allowed.

"Passenger."

"Good."

She went inside.

Sevren said, "She likes rules."

"I like her."

"Of course."

Near sixth bell, a road boy came running from the east entrance.

Not a rider.

Maybe twelve.

He carried a narrow red strip tied around one wrist.

The relay clerk saw him and moved fast.

Not panic.

Purpose.

The boy handed her a small folded board.

She read it.

Then looked at Sevren.

"Axle at East Cut. Naro safe. Cart not. Packet held there. Replacement goes morning."

Sevren exhaled.

One breath.

That was all.

"No west packet tonight?"

"No. Mark empty."

He stood.

Went to the peg.

Took a little chalk stick from the box beneath it.

Drew one diagonal line across the number plate.

Then wrote something on the clerk's slate.

Signed.

Done.

No rescue expedition.

No sprint east.

No demand to borrow Daro's cart.

No Greg fieldcraft.

No mystery.

Naro was safe.

Axle broken.

Replacement morning.

System adjusted.

Sevren sat again.

"You wanted to wait," I said.

"Yeah."

"Would you have?"

He thought.

"Five minutes."

"Unauthorized?"

"Probably."

"Rebel."

"Dangerous life."

Sixth bell rang.

The empty peg stayed empty.

That was the title of the evening, apparently.

We ate relay dinner.

Included.

Beans again.

Carrow and the east road were engaged in a conspiracy involving beans.

Daro joined us.

Sevren told him the transfer would not come.

Daro nodded.

"Then lighter morning."

That was his entire emotional response.

I liked road people.

The sleeping room filled slowly.

A pair of traders argued over snoring rights.

Someone had hung damp socks too close to the stove.

The relay clerk came through once and removed them without discussion.

Authority.

I lay on the lower platform.

Right thigh tired.

Back tired.

Residual limb fine.

Hands fine.

Tomorrow Carrow if wheels remained round.

I thought about the empty peg.

First life would have made me curious about East Cut.

What broke the axle?

Why no delay mark?

Was the post damaged?

Bandits?

Creature?

Weather?

Sabotage?

That brain had survived by assuming every irregularity might become dangerous.

Useful habit.

Expensive habit.

Here, a road boy had arrived with a red strip and a board.

Naro safe.

Cart not.

Replacement morning.

Enough.

I did not need the rest.

Sevren's bunk was above mine.

"You awake?" he asked.

"No."

"Good."

Silence.

Then:

"Glad Naro's fine."

"Yeah."

More silence.

I smiled in the dark.

The road system had lost one axle and one evening transfer.

It had not become a quest.

Tomorrow we would go home.
