TEXT READER · CHAPTER 329

THE WAY OUT

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The scream came from downstairs.

The oval pointed toward the stairs.

I trusted the scream.

I did not trust the oval.

That seemed like the correct order.

I grabbed both crutches and moved.

Urgency on two crutches was not running.

It was committing harder to every placement.

Right crutch.

Left crutch.

Right foot.

Down.

Again.

The first stair was always the worst because my body wanted speed and the geometry wanted honesty.

Lyssa was behind me.

"Who was that?"

"Don't know."

"Tavi?"

"Don't know."

She hated the second answer more.

So did I.

The oval hung from its cord in my left hand because putting it back in the bag would have cost seconds and dropping it would have been stupid.

It pointed down the stairwell.

Not perfectly.

Roughly.

Enough to annoy me.

At the first landing, the front half of the building smelled wrong.

Not smoke.

Hot metal.

Wet plaster.

Something sharp underneath.

I stopped.

Lyssa nearly hit me.

"What?"

"Smell."

Then another voice yelled from below.

Cala.

"Back! Back from the wall!"

Good.

Cala was alive.

That removed one terrible possibility.

Not the others.

I moved again.

The oval clicked.

I had crossed the landing frame.

Normal.

It rotated toward the frame behind me.

Also normal.

Then, halfway down the next flight, it turned in my hand.

Away from the frame.

Toward the rear of the building.

No threshold crossed.

I almost stopped.

Did not.

Scream first.

Artifact later.

At the ground floor, Tavi stood near the kitchen doorway holding both hands over his ears.

Alive.

Unhurt.

Cala had one arm across his chest keeping him back.

The scream had come from the woman next door.

Neris.

She was in the shared front hall barefoot, staring at a section of wall beside the building entrance.

The plaster there had split open.

Not a crack.

Opened.

A wedge-shaped chunk had fallen outward onto the floor, exposing blackened lath and a narrow metal pipe behind it.

The pipe glowed dull orange at one joint.

No flame.

Yet.

Neris pointed at it.

"It hissed."

The pipe hissed again.

Everyone moved back.

Good instincts.

A bead of pale light formed along the joint.

Then ran upward instead of down.

Bad sign.

I knew this category.

Not the local installation.

Heat line.

Service line.

Something carrying more than ordinary hot water.

Old Greg had lived in enough buildings to know you did not touch glowing wall pipes with cloth and optimism.

"Rear out," I said.

Cala looked at me.

"Why?"

Because the oval was pointing rear.

Bad answer.

Because the front wall contained a glowing service line beside the main entrance.

Good answer.

"Front entrance is beside the line. Rear is farther from it."

Cala nodded immediately.

"Tavi. Shoes."

"No time," I said.

"Floor's stone."

"Still no time."

Tavi already had one shoe in his hand somehow.

Children could manifest the wrong object under pressure with impressive consistency.

Lyssa grabbed a folded blanket from the bench.

Cala said, "Leave it."

Lyssa left it.

Also good.

Neris looked toward her own door.

"My papers."

"No," Cala said.

Neris took one step anyway.

The pipe joint popped.

Not exploded.

Popped.

The pale bead of light vanished.

A line of heat shimmer spread across the exposed metal.

Neris stopped wanting papers.

We moved rear.

The building's back route was worse than the front on a normal day.

Narrow kitchen passage.

Small storage turn.

Rear service door into the courtyard.

I had crossed all of it hundreds of times.

The oval hung in front of me.

It pointed toward the rear service door.

That was the same direction I already wanted.

Which made it useless as evidence and useful as comfort.

Annoying combination.

Cala went first with Tavi.

Neris behind her.

Lyssa waited for me.

"Go," I said.

"No."

"I'm slower."

"Yes."

"That was not permission to agree."

"Move."

We moved.

The storage turn was tight enough that I had to shorten both crutches and angle one forward.

Not literally shorten them.

There was no miracle hinge.

I choked my grip higher and used less swing.

My right shoulder complained from yesterday's theatre work.

Today it lost the vote.

Behind us, something struck the front hall wall.

Heavy.

Maybe more plaster.

Maybe the pipe support.

No flame sound.

No scream.

Keep moving.

Cala opened the rear service door.

The oval clicked before I crossed it.

Everyone else had already gone through.

I stopped for half a heartbeat.

There.

Again.

Update before my crossing.

Lyssa saw me stop.

"Greg."

"Moving."

I crossed.

Click again.

The oval rotated back toward the rear service door from the courtyard.

Normal after crossing.

We were out.

All of us.

Neris.

Cala.

Tavi.

Lyssa.

Me.

No one burned.

No one crushed.

No one heroically carried furniture.

Excellent evacuation.

The courtyard was already filling with people from the other units.

Someone from upstairs had heard Cala yelling and come down the rear stair.

A man from the next building shouted, "Heat line?"

Cala answered, "Wall joint. Front hall."

He ran toward the street instead of the building.

Good man.

Probably fetching someone who knew more.

The oval pointed at the rear service door.

I did not need it anymore.

Then the front windows flashed white.

Not fire.

A hard white pulse behind the glass.

Everyone ducked anyway.

A second later, the front door slammed open by itself.

Steam rolled out low along the floor.

Not ordinary steam.

Too bright.

Tiny pale sparks moved inside it and vanished when they touched stone.

Charge steam.

Maybe.

Local name unknown.

Whatever it was, I was suddenly even happier to be in the courtyard.

Tavi said, "Cool."

Cala said, "No."

"But it is."

"No."

Strong household doctrine.

A municipal response cart arrived faster than expected.

Not instantly.

Fast enough that someone nearby had probably known exactly which bell chain to pull.

Two workers in gray coats came through the courtyard gate carrying long red-handled tools and a folded black mat.

A third went to the street side.

No one asked me to help.

Perfect.

The lead worker said, "Anyone inside?"

Cala counted us aloud.

Not names.

Numbers.

Then pointed at Neris.

"Her unit shares the front wall. Everyone from ours is out. Upper rear came through courtyard. Front upstairs unknown."

Useful.

The worker shouted toward the street.

Another voice answered, "Clearing now."

The black mat went across the rear threshold.

One worker pressed a red-handled tool against the courtyard-side wall.

The handle lit amber.

He said, "Line still carrying."

The other worker swore.

Technical language transcended professions.

They did not enter through the rear.

Instead they moved around to the front.

Interesting.

I looked at the oval.

It still pointed toward the rear service door.

Which was now the door we had just used to escape.

Fine.

Consistent.

But before crossing, it had pointed there too.

That mattered more.

Maybe.

Neris was shaking.

Not dramatically.

Hands mostly.

Cala took her elbow.

"Sit."

"My papers."

"Still no."

"They're from my mother."

Cala's expression changed.

Not enough to let her go back in.

"We'll see after."

Neris nodded.

Sat on an overturned crate.

Tavi stood beside her with one bare foot and one shoe.

He looked at the shoe.

Then at his foot.

Then put the shoe down without putting it on.

I did not ask.

Some mysteries belonged to children.

Lyssa looked at the oval.

"It pointed back here before we crossed."

"Yes."

"That seems less like last door."

"Yes."

"More like retreat."

I hated that she had used the word first.

"Maybe."

"You remember something?"

"A phrase. Not enough."

"Good."

Apparently everyone in my life had become Hessa.

The municipal workers came back around after several minutes.

The lead one had soot on one sleeve.

"Line's isolated."

Cala asked, "Fire?"

"No sustained flame. Joint failed inside wall. Pressure cooked the plaster and vented through the hall."

"Cause?"

"Not yet."

Good answer.

"Can we go in?"

"Rear only. One at a time. Don't touch front wall. Don't open front units until we clear them."

Neris stood.

"My papers."

The worker pointed at her door.

"Front wall unit?"

"Yes."

"Not yet."

She sat again.

Cala asked, "Our kitchen?"

"Rear side is clear enough to enter and collect essentials. No staying inside until inspection."

There it was.

We were temporarily out of our own apartment.

Not homeless.

Not disaster.

Enough to become annoying quickly.

Cala looked at me.

"Can you get shoes?"

I looked at Tavi.

One bare foot.

"Apparently necessary."

The municipal worker said, "One person. Rear route only."

Cala started to volunteer.

I said, "I'll do it."

Everyone looked at me.

I regretted speaking.

"Why you?" Cala asked.

Because the oval had just led rear before we crossed.

Again bad answer.

"I know exactly where his shoes are likely not to be."

Tavi said, "One is upstairs."

"See? Strong intelligence."

Cala frowned.

"You were just moving fast on stairs."

"I'm fine."

Lyssa said, "He is."

Traitor.

The worker looked at my crutches.

"Rear stair dry?"

Cala said, "Yes."

"Then one trip. Essentials only. If you smell hot metal stronger, turn around. If the rear wall warms, turn around. If you see pale light in plaster, turn around."

"Good list."

"Not a challenge."

"Understood."

I went back in.

This time the oval mattered.

Not because I trusted it over the municipal worker.

Because retreat had become an actual operational concern.

I crossed the rear service door.

Click.

Oval pointed back to courtyard.

Good.

Kitchen passage.

Storage turn.

No new qualifying threshold.

Rear stair door.

Click.

Now the oval pointed back to the stair door.

There was the limitation.

The courtyard was no longer the remembered retreat.

One-step only.

I knew that.

I still disliked it.

Up the stairs.

My pace slower now.

Urgency without adrenaline pretending gravity had resigned.

The air smelled normal on the rear side.

At the upstairs doorway, click.

Oval updated.

Courtyard gone.

Stair door gone.

Only upstairs doorway remained.

This object was not safety.

It was breadcrumbs if you agreed to eat every old crumb when you dropped a new one.

Useful.

Dangerous if misunderstood.

I found Tavi's second shoe under the table.

Of course.

Then grabbed Cala's small document tin because essentials meant essentials and because she would pretend not to want it until later.

Lyssa's dark work stayed.

My holdfast stayed.

The basket of repair junk stayed.

I looked at the holdfast.

Two-copper object.

Unknown.

Mine.

Not essential during a service-line evacuation.

Good.

Then the oval turned.

Not toward the upstairs doorway.

Toward the window.

I stopped.

No scream this time.

No white flash.

No obvious danger.

Just the oval rotating slowly toward the window.

I did not follow it.

The worker had said rear route only.

Window was not route.

Maybe it was pointing at open air.

Maybe toward the courtyard beyond the wall.

Maybe toward something else entirely.

I wrote nothing.

Not now.

I took the shoe and document tin.

Crossed the upstairs doorway.

Click.

The oval pointed back to it.

Normal.

Down the stairs.

Rear stair door.

Click.

Updated.

Kitchen passage.

Rear service door.

Click.

Updated.

Courtyard.

Done.

The municipal worker checked what I carried.

"That's it?"

"Shoe and documents."

He looked at Tavi.

"Good priorities."

Tavi took the shoe.

"I knew where it was."

"You said upstairs."

"It was."

Technically correct.

The inspection took most of the afternoon.

The front hall wall was opened wider.

The failed joint belonged to an old building heat-service run embedded through the front partition. Exact cause stayed unknown. The workers said they needed a linewright before restoring service.

No sustained fire.

No structural evacuation beyond the building for now.

We could sleep inside if the rear route remained clear and the front hall stayed closed off.

Neris eventually got her papers after a worker entered with her.

She cried harder when she came back out holding them than she had during the failure.

Nobody commented.

Good neighbors.

By evening, temporary black boards covered the opened wall section.

A red chalk line marked where nobody was supposed to stand.

Tavi stepped over it once.

Cala moved him back without words.

He stayed back.

Progress.

The oval hung upstairs again.

Not beside the doorway this time.

I moved its cord to a hook where I could see both the upstairs doorway and the window.

Lyssa sat on the bed edge.

"So?"

"So what?"

"It pointed toward the way out before we crossed it."

"Yes."

"Then later it pointed at the window."

I looked at her.

"You saw?"

"No. Your face did."

Unfair.

I took out the narrow scrap.

Under the Ch328 note I wrote:

HOUSE LINE FAILURE

OVAL TURNED TO REAR ROUTE BEFORE MY CROSSING

REAR = ACTUAL SAFER EXIT GIVEN FRONT-WALL FAILURE

Then I stopped.

The sentence was too strong.

I crossed out `SAFER`.

Wrote:

REAR = EXIT WE USED / FRONT BESIDE FAILED LINE

Better.

Then:

INSIDE RETURN TRIP: NORMAL LAST-THRESHOLD OVERWRITE

UPSTAIRS OVAL TURNED TOWARD WINDOW WITHOUT CROSSING / NO EVENT OBSERVED

Lyssa read it.

"You think it knows escape routes."

"I think I want it to."

"Different."

"Very."

The first-life phrase sat below everything:

REMEMBERS RETREAT ?

I stared at it.

Retreat could mean last threshold.

Retreat could mean current viable exit.

Retreat could mean whichever direction had been used as retreat before.

Retreat could be a terrible translation of a technical word I remembered emotionally instead of correctly.

Old Greg had known how to use dozens of things he could no longer name cleanly.

That was embarrassing.

Also realistic.

I added:

DOES NOT = SAFE PATH

Then underlined it.

Because even today, the object had pointed toward the rear route before we crossed it.

That had been useful.

Later it pointed at a window I was not supposed to use.

Also useful.

Not because the window was right.

Because it stopped me from turning a helpful artifact into an authority.

The oval rotated on its cord.

Slowly.

Toward the upstairs doorway.

Normal again.

I smiled.

Lyssa said, "What?"

"Nothing."

"You have artifact face."

"I don't have an artifact face."

"You do."

"What does it look like?"

"Like you bought trouble and are happy it came with instructions."

I looked at the oval.

Then at the red chalk line visible through the stair opening below.

"Instructions would be generous."

"Warnings, then."

That was closer.

For one copper, I had bought a thing that sometimes remembered the last way back and sometimes, maybe, suggested a way out.

The difference between those two functions was large enough to get somebody killed.

So I wrote one final line:

USEFUL ENOUGH TO CARRY

NOT TRUSTED ENOUGH TO OBEY

That felt right.

For now.
