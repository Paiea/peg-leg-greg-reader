TEXT READER · CHAPTER 333

THE FAR POINT

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Hessa sent for me three days after Mara told me not to open the holdfast.

This was probably unrelated.

I still distrusted timing.

The message came folded around a piece of chalk.

Not wrapped.

Not tied.

Just paper folded twice with a short white stick inside.

Tavi shook it.

"Treasure?"

"No."

"How do you know?"

"It sounds educational."

He handed it over immediately.

Good instincts.

The note said:

GREG

MIDDAY

SAME ROOM

BRING BOTH CRUTCHES

NO OTHER EQUIPMENT

HESSA

I read the last line twice.

No other equipment.

The oval stayed home.

The holdfast stayed home.

The narrow scrap stayed in my bag because that was arguably stationery.

Then I took it out too.

Hessa had written no other equipment.

I had learned something about exact language.

Lyssa watched me remove the scrap.

"Scared?"

"Respectful."

"Same face."

"Hostile household."

I left with two crutches, clothes, and a deep sense that I was underprepared for sitting in a room.

The room was the same.

Table.

Chair.

Marks.

Nothing theatrical.

Hessa was already there.

She looked at my hands.

Then at my bagless shoulders.

"Good."

"I almost brought notes."

"Why didn't you?"

"You said no other equipment."

"Notes are not equipment."

I stared.

She stared back.

"You wanted me uncertain."

"No. I wanted you to decide whether language mattered."

"That is worse."

"Sit."

There she was.

I sat.

Both crutches leaned within reach.

Hessa moved the table away.

Not far.

Enough to clear the floor.

Then she placed one chalk mark near my right foot.

One at middle distance.

One farther than she had ever asked me to work.

Not dramatically far.

Maybe two arm spans beyond the middle mark.

Still inside the room.

Still visible.

Still far enough that I disliked it immediately.

"What are we doing?"

"Three positions."

"Near, middle, far."

"No."

"You enjoy that word."

"Near, middle, and farther."

I looked at the last mark.

"Important distinction."

"Yes."

Far had become a category in my notes.

Hessa apparently refused my categories.

Reasonable.

"Same object?"

"No object."

"External effect?"

"Eventually."

"Draw first?"

"Yes."

Good.

Something familiar.

I placed both hands on my thighs.

Hessa said, "Minimal draw."

I did.

One.

Small.

Supervised.

Clean enough.

Not exciting.

Hessa watched my shoulders more than my hands.

"Again."

Second minimal draw.

Also fine.

"Stop."

I stopped.

"What changed?"

"Nothing."

"Your right shoulder rose on the second."

I frowned.

"Crutch fatigue?"

"Maybe."

"From walking here?"

"Maybe."

"Helpful."

"You answer maybe when you don't know."

"I learned from specialists."

No reaction.

Cruel.

She had me shift the right crutch farther from the chair.

Then the left.

Not use them.

Just change where they sat in peripheral view.

"Minimal draw."

Third.

Clean.

"Again."

Fourth.

A tiny hitch at the end.

Not enough to lose it.

Enough that I felt the shape change.

Hessa said, "What happened?"

"Late correction."

"Why?"

"I felt it thin."

"Did it?"

I thought.

"Maybe not."

"Then why correct?"

"Habit."

There it was.

Not magic problem.

Operator problem.

Old Greg knew this.

That was the irritating part.

You could understand a principle for forty years and still build the wrong correction into a new body.

"Again," Hessa said.

Fifth minimal draw.

No correction.

It thinned.

Then stabilized.

Hessa nodded once.

"Good."

That was almost praise.

I hated how much I liked it.

Then deliberate shaping.

No external effect yet.

Near position.

Small rotation.

Not object rotation.

Internal shaping target aligned to marked right-hand region.

The same kind of work as before.

Attempt one of the day.

Clean.

Second.

Slight drift.

Third.

Clean enough.

Hessa moved me to middle.

Not physically.

Attention.

The marked region moved outward.

First middle attempt failed early.

No external effect.

Just shaping losing edge before completion.

Second held longer.

Third gave the faint edge lift feeling I had known before.

Hessa stopped me.

"What did you feel?"

"Possible lift."

"Possible?"

"I know."

"Good."

I looked at her.

"You like possible now?"

"I like accurate."

Fair.

Then she pointed to the farther chalk mark.

"Same shaping."

I looked at it.

"Without external effect?"

"Yes."

"Just position specification."

"Yes."

That was new enough.

I closed my eyes.

"Open."

I opened them.

"Why?"

"Because you will not always be allowed to remove the room."

Annoying.

True.

I set the target.

Not a physical thing.

Not a point I could touch.

A farther specified region.

Same operation.

Different distance.

First attempt collapsed immediately.

Not painful.

Not dangerous.

Just absent.

Second reached farther but smeared.

Third held a fraction of a breath and then vanished.

Hessa said, "Stop."

I exhaled.

"That's it?"

"For shaping."

"That sounded ominous."

She walked to the farther mark.

Then placed a small white bead on the floor.

Not a sleep bead.

Not game hardware.

Plain ceramic.

No visible mark.

"External effect?"

"Yes."

There it was.

My first supervised external-effect attempt in a while.

I sat straighter.

Hessa said, "Do not improve because you are excited."

"That feels targeted."

"It is."

The near aggregate had four clean in ten.

Middle one in six plus one possible edge lift.

Far zero in one.

Old totals in my head.

Thirty-six supervised minimal draws.

Thirty-two deliberate shaping.

Seventy-eight external effects.

Hessa had not asked me to track those aloud.

Good.

I knew anyway.

She pointed at the near chalk mark first.

"Move the bead one grain toward you."

The bead sat at far mark.

"That's farther."

"The bead is farther. The requested effect is near to itself."

I paused.

"You are separating my target distance from effect distance."

"Yes."

That mattered.

A lot.

My earlier categories had mixed too many things together.

How far away the target was.

How large the intended effect was.

Where in my own shaping process I felt control.

Different axes.

I hated useful lessons.

"One grain," Hessa said.

I began.

First external attempt of the day.

The bead did not move.

Second.

Nothing.

Third.

I thought it shifted.

Then realized I had changed my head angle.

"No," I said before Hessa could.

"Good."

Fourth.

A tiny scrape.

Maybe.

No.

I looked.

The bead sat against a chalk fleck that had not been touching it before.

Hessa crouched.

Did not touch.

"Again."

Fifth.

Nothing.

She marked the chalk beside the bead with her fingernail.

"That fourth was movement."

My chest tightened.

Not because it was large.

Because it was far.

Target far.

Effect tiny.

Different category.

"Clean?" I asked.

"No."

"Why?"

"You overcorrected after contact."

Of course.

"So external effect occurred but not clean."

"Yes."

Good.

Better than fake success.

She moved the bead back.

"Again."

Sixth.

Nothing.

Seventh.

Tiny movement.

No scrape this time.

One grain.

Maybe less.

Hessa watched.

Then said, "Clean."

I stared at the bead.

There it was.

Far target.

Tiny effect.

Clean.

Not power.

Control reaching farther than before.

My heart wanted to make it important.

Old Greg wanted to classify it.

New Greg wanted to do it again immediately.

Hessa said, "Stop."

Cruel woman.

"Why?"

"Because now you want the result."

"That seems normal."

"It is."

"Then?"

"And therefore useful to stop."

I leaned back.

"You train joy out of people."

"No. I train greed out of repetitions."

That was annoyingly good.

I looked at the bead.

"One clean far-target tiny effect."

"Yes."

"Can I write that down?"

"Later."

"You enjoy power."

"You are nineteen."

"Physically."

She looked at me.

I shut up.

Then she did something worse.

She moved the bead from the farther mark to the middle mark.

"Same one-grain effect."

"After stop?"

"New condition."

"That's cheating."

"Yes."

First middle-target attempt.

Clean.

Second.

No movement.

Third.

Clean enough.

Hessa said nothing.

Then near target.

First clean.

Second overstrong.

The bead moved three grains.

I grimaced.

"Too much."

"Yes."

Third near.

Clean.

She stopped the session.

I waited.

She did not summarize.

Of course.

"What are you testing?" I asked.

"You."

"Specifically."

"Whether your distance problem is one problem."

I looked at the three chalk positions.

"It isn't."

"Probably not."

Good word.

"Far shaping still weak."

"Yes."

"But far target with tiny external movement can succeed."

"Yes."

"So the target-position specification can reach farther than sustained shaping quality suggests."

"Maybe."

"And the effect magnitude matters separately."

"Yes."

"And my correction habit gets worse when I feel thinning."

"Yes."

"And you could have told me this in one sentence."

"No."

"You absolutely could."

"You would have believed the sentence instead of the result."

I hated her.

Professionally.

"Next time?"

"Not selected."

There it was.

No schedule.

No automatic progression.

No promise.

I stood carefully.

My right shoulder was tired from the walk and the sitting posture more than the magic.

Hessa noticed.

"Crutches."

I took both.

"Any restriction change?"

"No."

"Outside practice?"

"No."

"Independent tiny far-target bead movement?"

"No."

"Worth asking."

"No."

There she was.

I left.

The street felt louder after Hessa's room.

Not brighter.

Not magical.

Just less bounded.

I wanted my notes.

Badly.

That was probably why she had let me leave without them.

At home I went upstairs before anyone could ask how it went.

The oval hung beside the doorway.

The holdfast sat wrapped on the high shelf.

Neither had anything to do with the session.

Good.

I took out the narrow scrap.

Then realized I had left it home.

Of course.

So I used a fresh scrap.

I wrote:

HESSA

MINIMAL DRAWS: 5 TODAY

DELIBERATE SHAPING: NEAR 3 / MIDDLE 3 / FARTHER 3

EXTERNAL: FAR TARGET 7 / MIDDLE 3 / NEAR 3

Then I stopped.

That was already too compressed.

I added:

FAR TARGET != FAR EFFECT

ONE-GRAIN MOVE AT FAR TARGET CLEAN ON 7TH TODAY

EARLIER FAR SHAPING STILL WEAK

DISTANCE MAY BE MULTIPLE PROBLEMS

Then:

LATE CORRECTION HABIT WHEN FEELING THIN

DO NOT CHASE CLEAN RESULT

That last one was Hessa's lesson.

I resented giving her credit.

I counted totals.

Minimal draws had been thirty-six.

Five more made forty-one.

Deliberate shaping had been thirty-two.

Nine more made forty-one.

External effects had been seventy-eight.

Thirteen more made ninety-one.

I stared at that.

Forty-one.

Forty-one.

Ninety-one.

Round enough to tempt meaning.

Meaningless.

Good.

Near aggregate needed updating too.

But not cleanly from today's sequence because the old categories had mixed target position and effect type.

That was the whole problem.

I crossed out the heading I had started:

NEAR / MIDDLE / FAR

Then wrote:

OLD DISTANCE COUNTS MIXED AXES

KEEP FOR HISTORY / DO NOT PRETEND COMPARABLE

That felt better.

Not satisfying.

Better.

Lyssa came upstairs.

"How'd it go?"

"I moved a bead."

"Congratulations."

"Far away."

"Still congratulations."

"One grain."

"Tiny congratulations."

I looked at her.

"You are impossible."

"Did Hessa say good?"

"Once. Maybe twice."

"Huge day."

She was not wrong.

I folded the fresh scrap and put it with the old notes.

Two copper remained in the purse.

The holdfast still needed a future controlled test.

The oval still clicked at doors and sometimes did stranger things.

My own magic had just done something cleaner farther away than I expected.

None of those facts solved the others.

That mattered.

The world was getting more magical without becoming simpler.

I liked that better.
