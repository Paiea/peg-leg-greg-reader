TEXT READER · CHAPTER 339

THE SECOND VOICE

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The next thing I bought was nothing.

This was financially responsible and emotionally unsatisfying.

The rest stone stayed in its box.

The holdfast stayed on its shelf.

The oval stayed in my bag because apparently I had decided one badly understood object should accompany me through ordinary life.

One copper stayed in my purse.

For two whole mornings.

I was becoming wealthy through inactivity.

Then Rinna sent a child to find me.

Not Tavi.

A theatre child.

This distinction mattered because theatre children arrived with messages and Tavi arrived with objects he had improved into danger.

The girl stood in the front hall holding a folded scrap.

"Rinna says if you're free before second bell, come by."

"Why?"

"She didn't say."

"Excellent management."

The girl shrugged.

"She said bring your crutches."

I looked down.

"I was planning to."

She smiled.

Good.

Somebody appreciated me.

I went upstairs for my shoulder bag.

Lyssa was working near the window.

"Theatre?"

"Apparently."

"Paid?"

"Apparently not enough information."

"Don't volunteer for six hours."

"I never volunteer for six hours."

She looked at me.

I corrected myself.

"I rarely intend to volunteer for six hours."

Better.

At the theatre, the front doors were open.

Not for audience.

Air.

The place smelled like dust, cloth, old wood, lamp oil, and yesterday's rain dragged in on shoes.

House left was empty.

My seat from the performance was just a chair again.

I liked that.

Things should be allowed to stop being important.

Rinna was standing near the stage with Nessa and Jori.

A long wooden case sat open on the floor.

Inside were four narrow brass tubes, each perhaps the length of my forearm.

Not weapons.

Probably.

One had a flared mouth.

One had a square collar.

Two were plain except for tiny silver bands near the ends.

Marek was onstage saying, "I maintain it improved the scene."

Hara said, "You entered from the wrong door."

"With conviction."

"Wrongly."

"Conviction survives error."

"Unfortunately."

Rinna saw me.

"Greg."

"I was summoned."

"You were invited."

"The child said bring my crutches."

"Did you?"

I looked down again.

Rinna did not smile.

Nessa did.

Jori said, "We need your walk."

That was more concerning.

"You can't have it."

"Borrow."

"Also suspicious."

Rinna pointed at the stage.

"We're changing the second act entrance."

I waited.

"And?"

"And the new sound pickup catches Marek's boots too hard."

Marek called, "My boots are expressive."

Hara said, "Your boots are loud."

"Same thing."

Rinna continued as if nobody had spoken.

"It also catches Jori's left brace when he crosses behind the wall."

I looked at Jori.

He tapped the leather-and-metal support strapped around his left wrist.

I had seen it before.

Never cared.

Apparently it clicked.

"And you want my crutches because..."

Nessa said, "Because if we fix the pickup for boots and a wrist brace, then discover tonight it hates crutch tips, Rinna will kill us."

Rinna said, "I will not kill anyone."

Nessa looked at me.

"See? Terrifying."

There it was.

Not a job.

Not magic training.

Not artifact appraisal.

They wanted my noise.

I could provide that.

"What pickup?"

Jori pointed toward house left.

Mounted beneath the front edge of the stage was a shallow wooden box with a brass face.

I had noticed similar boxes during the performance.

Wall-mounted sound devices.

Ordinary stage magic.

Useful enough that I had not thought about them while the play worked.

The best technology disappeared.

This one had apparently stopped disappearing.

Jori said, "Old second voice."

"That's a name?"

"Nickname."

"For what?"

"Sound return."

Rinna said, "It carries quiet stage speech forward without making the room sound shouted at."

That was a much better description.

I looked at the brass box.

"And now it likes boots."

"New pickup throat," Jori said.

He indicated the open wooden case.

"Original cracked last week. Replacement is close, not same."

"Registered?"

Rinna gave me a look.

I had become annoying in new directions.

Jori answered anyway.

"The main housing is registered theatre equipment. Throat is replaceable service part. This one's from North Lantern stock."

"Known compatible?"

"Known compatible with this housing family."

"Not this exact box."

"Correct."

I liked him.

"What changed?"

Jori said, "Speech carries. Foot contact carries too much."

Nessa added, "Chair legs. Prop table. One dropped spoon became the voice of God."

"I would pay to see that."

"You did not. We were rehearsing."

Marek called, "It was moving."

Hara said, "It was a spoon."

"Art resists categories."

Rinna closed her eyes briefly.

I understood why she had invited me.

Not because I was uniquely qualified.

Because I was available and came with a noise source they did not regularly have onstage.

Social accumulation could be humiliatingly practical.

Jori said, "Walk the new entrance. Normal pace."

"Where?"

He showed me.

Stage right behind the false wall, through the narrow opening, six steps across visible stage, stop at the painted table.

I looked at the route.

"That turn is tight."

Nessa said, "For you?"

"For anyone with two crutches and dignity."

"We can remove dignity."

"Already did theatre."

She nodded.

Fair.

The route was possible.

I set my crutches.

Walked.

Normal.

Tip.

Step.

Tip.

Step.

The brass box beneath the stage gave a faint hum.

Then my right crutch struck the board at the edge of the false wall.

The room answered.

THOCK.

Not loud exactly.

Large.

The sound seemed to come from the stage itself.

Marek applauded.

"Greg has arrived."

"Shut up."

Rinna said, "Again."

I did it again.

This time no wall strike.

Normal crutch contact still carried.

Not every placement.

Interesting.

One tip landed near the front quarter of the stage and produced a soft amplified knock.

Another three feet back did not.

I stopped.

"There."

Jori said, "I heard it."

He stepped onto the stage and tapped his heel along the same line.

Nothing.

Tapped again.

Nothing.

Nessa dragged a chair leg lightly.

The box hummed.

No carry.

Marek stamped.

Rinna said, "Don't."

He had already stamped.

The sound carried.

"Useful," he said.

"Obvious," Hara said.

Jori crouched near the front boards.

"Again, Greg. Same route."

I did.

One placement carried.

Different exact spot.

Same general strip.

Jori marked two chalk ticks.

Then asked Hara to speak from center stage.

She said, conversationally, "Marek is an idiot."

The room carried it beautifully.

Marek put a hand to his chest.

"Technology has betrayed me."

Rinna said, "Again."

Hara repeated the sentence.

Same.

Speech clean.

Jori moved the replacement throat's silver collar one notch.

Hara spoke again.

Softer.

Still clear.

Marek walked.

Boots quieter.

I crossed.

My crutches were quieter too.

"Done," I said.

Jori said, "No."

Of course.

He moved the collar another notch.

Hara spoke.

Her last word thinned strangely.

Not volume.

Shape.

The final consonant seemed to vanish.

Jori moved it back.

"There."

Rinna said, "So one notch from original."

"For this room today."

Good answer.

Rinna nodded.

No universal setting.

No solved forever.

Humidity, room load, throat condition, whatever else.

Jori asked me to cross twice more.

I did.

The pickup still caught one harder crutch placement near the front strip, but not ordinary walking.

"Acceptable?" Nessa asked.

Rinna looked at me.

"Can you walk the entrance without striking hard there?"

"Yes."

"Would someone unfamiliar with the stage?"

I considered.

"Probably not reliably."

She looked at Nessa.

"Then mark the strip backstage. Don't route visitors across it during live pickup."

Nessa nodded.

That was it.

No one redesigned the theatre around me.

No one declared the device unsafe.

They found the interaction and changed operating procedure.

I had become a test condition.

I was weirdly proud.

Marek said, "Can we keep Greg for the second act?"

Rinna said, "No."

"He has presence."

"He has errands."

I said, "Do I?"

Rinna looked at me.

"You should."

Fair.

Jori removed the throat again.

That surprised me.

"Thought you set it."

"I did."

"Then why remove it?"

"Because I want to check the seat before tonight."

He turned the brass tube in his hands.

The square collar had a faint dark line along one edge.

Not crack.

Maybe grime.

Maybe contact polish.

He held it beside the original cracked throat from the case.

The two were nearly identical.

Nearly.

The replacement's square collar was a fraction deeper.

I would not have noticed if they were not side by side.

"Close," I said.

"Close makes work," Jori replied.

That sentence belonged on every workshop wall in civilization.

He fitted the old cracked piece beside the new one.

"Original sat deeper?"

I asked.

"Shallower."

"Then new one is deeper."

"Yes."

"Wouldn't that seat farther?"

"If the housing were unworn."

There.

He pointed to the brass box under the stage.

"Seat's been dressed twice. New throat's nominally deeper, old housing is nominally less of a housing than it used to be."

I smiled.

"Technical term?"

"Extremely."

Nessa said, "Can I put that on the repair card?"

"No."

Rinna had moved to the edge of the stage.

"How long?"

Jori said, "Quarter hour."

"You said that before Greg came."

"Now I mean it."

"Dangerous."

She turned to me.

"Thank you."

"That's all?"

"Were you hoping for applause?"

"Money."

"No."

"Worth asking."

"You were here less than an hour."

"My rates are predatory."

"Your rate was invitation."

Also fair.

Then she said, "There may be paid house work next week. Two evenings. Not confirmed. If it happens, Nessa will ask you."

I paused.

There.

Not payment now.

Not promise.

Not conveniently thirty copper.

Just my name existing inside somebody's future staffing thought.

"What kind?"

"House route and late reset."

"Rate?"

"If it happens, you'll get the rate with the offer."

I nodded.

Healthy institution.

Marek called, "I can offer half a bit to insult Hara through the second voice."

Hara said, "You can do that for free."

"Market collapse."

I left before they recruited me into art.

Outside, Carrow had become bright.

Not warm.

Bright.

Clouds had broken enough that every wet roof edge threw light back at the street.

I stood under the theatre awning for a moment and adjusted my shoulder bag.

One copper.

No new money.

No artifact purchase.

No advancement.

Except that Rinna might think of me when paid house work appeared.

Jessa had thought of me when retired parts appeared.

Antonius had thought of me when a boundary check needed another pair of eyes.

Senna had drawn me and sold the drawing.

People were beginning to carry versions of me around when I wasn't present.

That was reputation, I supposed.

Terrible concept.

You could not supervise it.

I took the longer route home.

Not for philosophy.

Tanner's Cut was still partially closed.

The municipal board had been changed.

CHANNEL WORK

OLD DRAIN TRACE

EAST WALK OPEN

CARTS SINGLE FILE

The red stakes had moved.

The old diagonal drain was covered again except for two lifted sections farther south.

The green-coated specialist was not there.

Different workers.

Different day.

The service crawler sat on its spool cart, folded into itself like a dead brass centipede.

Definitely not alive.

Probably.

I crossed using the east walk.

The oval clicked at the ordinary street frame where two buildings nearly met overhead.

Normal.

No strange click.

Good.

At home, the rest stone was still in its box.

Good.

The holdfast was still on the high shelf.

Good.

Tavi's spoon carrier had gained a string.

Bad.

"What does the string do?" I asked.

"Pulls it."

He demonstrated.

The wooden spoon moved across the floor.

I stared.

"You made it functional."

"Yes."

"This is how civilization begins to fail."

He pulled it again.

One of the metal loops fell off.

Civilization recovered.

Lyssa asked, "Paid?"

"No."

"Six hours?"

"Less than one."

She looked impressed.

"You're learning."

"There may be paid work next week."

"May?"

"Two evenings. House route and reset. Not confirmed."

"Rate?"

"Not offered yet."

She nodded.

No fantasy arithmetic.

Good household.

I told her about the second voice.

Not every technical detail.

Enough.

Replacement throat.

Speech clean.

Boots too loud.

Crutches useful as an inconvenient test.

She laughed at that.

"You got invited because you're noisy."

"Specialized expertise."

"Should charge more."

"Rinna disagreed."

I went upstairs later.

The narrow scrap sat where I had left it.

Auction notes.

Mara rates.

Oval observations.

Hessa counts.

I considered adding something about the theatre pickup.

Replacement compatible by family but not exact.

Housing wear changed fit.

One-notch adjustment balanced speech against impact noise.

Useful.

But not mine.

Jori had the repair card.

The theatre had the equipment.

I had been a crutch-shaped variable.

I did not need to collect every fact I touched.

That was another kind of growth.

I put the scrap away.

Then noticed the rest-stone box.

One copper object.

Known fault.

No rated use.

I wanted to open it.

Not activate.

Just look.

I didn't.

That surprised me more than the theatre.

Maybe access was changing me in the useful direction.

When everything had been scarce, every open door felt temporary.

You rushed through before it closed.

Bought the thing.

Asked the question.

Took the work.

Used the chance.

Now there were enough doors that I could leave some unopened.

I hated that thought immediately.

Too wise.

I went downstairs before I wrote it down.
