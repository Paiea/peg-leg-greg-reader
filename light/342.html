TEXT READER · CHAPTER 342

THE HOUSE LINE

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The theatre finally offered me money.

This happened while I was eating one of Beren's onions.

Not raw.

I had standards.

Cala had put them into a pan with something salty and enough fat to make agriculture worthwhile.

Nessa knocked once, entered after Cala answered, and held out a folded paper.

"Rinna said if I let you negotiate in person, we'd lose daylight."

"That is insulting."

"She knows you."

Fair.

I took the paper.

Two evenings.

House route and late reset.

Second bell through clear.

Two copper each evening.

Payment after clear.

No meal.

No standing call.

There it was.

Actual work.

Actual rate.

Four copper total if I did both nights.

Not thirty-six.

Not destiny.

Still useful.

"Tonight?"

"Tonight and three nights from now."

"Why three?"

"Because that's when the show is."

Good system.

"What does house route mean?"

"Front door to seats. Keep aisles clear. Help late seating. Watch the left side after first bell because the flower frame makes that route stupid."

"And reset?"

"After house clear, chairs, aisle markers, loose programs, anything audience moved that shouldn't stay moved. Jori handles stage. We handle house."

"How late?"

"Depends how slowly rich people leave."

"Dangerous employment condition."

"You want it?"

I read the paper again.

Two copper tonight.

One copper currently in my purse.

Three after the shift if nobody invented deductions.

Five after the second evening if both happened as offered and I did not spend anything in between.

I did not calculate what five copper could buy.

Not immediately.

That was growth.

Then I calculated it.

Mara housing observation.

Public counter options.

Reserve afterward.

Fine.

Partial growth.

"Yes."

Nessa held out her hand.

"Paper."

"I thought I kept it."

"It's the house copy. I need your mark."

"I have a mark now?"

"You can write your name."

Less exciting.

I signed.

She tore the bottom strip along a scored line and gave that to me.

GREG / HOUSE ROUTE + LATE RESET / TONIGHT + THIRD NIGHT / 2 C EACH / PAY AFTER CLEAR

Useful.

No ambiguity.

Cala read it over my shoulder.

"Good."

Lyssa asked, "How many hours?"

Nessa said, "Usually four. Maybe five."

Lyssa looked at me.

"See?"

"I didn't volunteer."

"You're improving."

Nessa left.

I finished the onions.

Work started before second bell.

The theatre looked different from the front doors when the doors mattered.

I had entered through them as audience.

I had carried things through side routes.

I had watched people become a crowd after they were already seated.

I had not watched the crowd form.

That was a different organism.

Nessa gave me a narrow gray sash.

"House."

I tied it around my upper arm.

"Do I get authority?"

"No."

"Then what does sash do?"

"Makes people ask you questions."

"Terrible artifact."

"Not magical."

"Worse."

Front table sat just inside the doors.

Two workers handled names, paid seats, comp marks, and program stacks.

I knew neither well.

One was an older man with a narrow mustache and a voice designed to say no politely.

The other was a young woman who could count tickets while answering three questions and never looked impressed by anyone.

Good people.

My route started after them.

Front doors.

Short entry aisle.

Split.

House left or house right.

My responsibility mostly left.

Third-row-back aisle seat still existed.

Not mine tonight.

Work had displaced sentiment immediately.

Healthy.

Nessa walked me through the route.

"Before first bell, normal seating. After first bell, no center crossing during scene one. Left late entry holds at back wall until cue."

"What cue?"

"Blue lamp under rail goes dark."

I looked.

Small blue lamp tucked beneath side rail.

"And then?"

"Move whoever is waiting down the left aisle during the scene change. Quiet. If they argue, don't argue back."

"What do I do?"

"Get me."

Excellent procedure.

"Flower frame?"

"Act two. It stages through left rear. Nobody stands in that pocket after second act starts."

She pointed at a shallow rectangular area near the rear wall marked with two chalk corners.

"That's the frame path."

"Audience can see it?"

"Not when they're where they belong."

Theatre summarized.

"What about crutches?"

"Your crutches?"

"No, the crowd's."

She looked at me.

I smiled.

"Mine. Is there anywhere they catch?"

"You've walked it."

"Not with thirty people moving around me."

That made her stop.

Good.

We walked it again.

Different question.

At the left split, a decorative rope post narrowed the turn.

Fine alone.

Stupid if somebody stopped there.

Nessa moved the rope post six inches.

At row seven, one chair sat slightly proud of the others.

My right crutch could pass.

A wider body with a coat probably could too.

Two people passing opposite would not.

Nessa pushed it in.

Small changes.

No speech.

House geometry.

"Anything else?" she asked.

I looked.

"Back wall hold needs a clear edge. If three late people stand there, fourth blocks the door."

"They usually stack two."

"Usually isn't tonight."

She looked at me.

"Why?"

I shrugged.

"No reason."

Then someone outside shouted about the north road.

We both turned.

Not theatre voice.

Street voice.

A man at the front table was explaining something to the mustached worker.

"North side carts are coming through East Cut now. Gate board says secondary road closed."

There.

Long mile residue.

Not my mystery.

Still part of the city.

Nessa asked, "That affect us?"

The young ticket woman said, "If people planned North Gate, they'll be late."

Mustache said, "Or not come."

Both plausible.

Rinna appeared from somewhere behind civilization.

"What happened?"

They told her.

She did not look at me.

Good.

I was not North Road authority because I had ridden it once.

Rinna said, "Keep first bell on time. Hold late seats by normal cue. If more than six waiting, open right rear too after scene one."

Nessa said, "Right rear is clear?"

"Check it."

They moved.

I remained where assigned.

Also growth.

I did not volunteer my road story.

Doors opened properly a little later.

People arrived in patterns.

Pairs first.

Older couples.

A family with three children and one child who appeared to be operating independently of the family.

Two young men who had clearly decided clothing could substitute for manners.

A woman with a cane.

A broad man carrying a folded coat over one arm despite wearing another coat.

Why.

People asked questions because of the gray sash.

Nessa had been truthful.

"House left?"

"This way."

"Row five?"

"Past the rail, second opening."

"Is there a privy?"

"Front hall right."

"Can I take wine in?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because I enjoy denying joy."

He stared.

I pointed at the sign.

"Also that."

He laughed and finished the cup outside.

Good outcome.

The woman with the cane had row eight, center-left.

She looked at the aisle.

Then at the chair.

Then at me.

"Does that row have another exit?"

"Right end."

"Closer?"

"From your seat, no."

She nodded.

No request.

No problem.

I did not rearrange the world because she owned a cane.

She walked in and sat.

Simple.

First bell approached.

House filled.

Not full.

Healthy crowd.

Then the north-side people arrived.

Not six.

Eleven in the first group.

Then four more.

Then a family of five arguing about whose fault the route change was.

The entry hall thickened.

Front table slowed.

Not chaos.

Compression.

People behind could not tell why people ahead had stopped.

Which made them step sideways.

Sideways made the door narrower.

The door becoming narrower made people push forward.

Crowds were stupid geometry with opinions.

First bell rang.

Rinna did not delay it.

Good.

Mustache kept checking names.

Ticket woman kept counting.

I started directing already-cleared late arrivals away from the front table.

"House left wait against wall. Keep center open."

A man said, "We're row three."

"You're still wall."

"We paid for row three."

"And row three will continue existing after scene one."

He did not like me.

Fine.

Nessa appeared beside me.

"How many cleared?"

"Eight left. Four right. More at table."

She looked once.

"Open right rear after cue. I'll take right. You keep left."

Good.

No heroism.

Split load.

The front doors remained open for late intake.

First scene began.

We could hear the second voice carrying Hara's quieter lines into the room.

From the back wall, it worked.

No giant spoon noises.

Jori had won his argument with brass.

Blue lamp under left rail stayed lit.

We waited.

Waiting people became restless in different ways.

Some whispered.

Some stared at me as if I personally controlled theater time.

One child sat on the floor.

Excellent child.

The row-three man asked, "How long?"

"Until the blue lamp goes dark."

"How long is that?"

"Less time if you don't make me invent an answer."

His wife laughed.

He stopped asking.

Then another group arrived.

Five.

The back hold was now too deep.

Exactly the geometry we'd checked.

Fourth layer blocked the front-door line.

I moved the outermost waiting group into a shallow side pocket near the program table.

Not the flower-frame pocket.

Different side.

Kept walkway between.

The young ticket woman saw what I was doing and shifted the empty program basket off the floor without being asked.

Distributed competence.

The center opened again.

Blue lamp went dark.

"Left group," I said. "Follow me. Quiet."

I did not take all of them.

That would be stupid.

First six.

Enough to clear pressure.

I moved down the left aisle.

Crutch tips controlled.

Not silent.

Quiet enough.

The stage was in transition.

Canvas wall turning.

Two actors crossing behind light.

Audience attention forward.

I delivered people to rows.

Row-three man tried to turn the wrong direction.

His wife pulled his sleeve before I did anything.

Marriage as route control.

I returned along wall side.

Second group.

Another six.

Then two.

Nessa handled right rear.

Within minutes the back wall had become wall again instead of human storage.

The front table still had late arrivals, but normal numbers now.

Problem compressed.

Not solved forever.

Just handled.

I liked work with edges.

Act one continued.

For most of it I stood near left rear and did almost nothing.

This was correct.

Aisle work was successful when no one needed aisle work.

My shoulders began to complain anyway.

Standing on crutches for long periods was not walking.

Walking distributed work differently.

Standing made every minute collect in the same places.

I shifted.

Sat briefly on the back ledge during a long scene when Nessa covered the door.

No one praised endurance.

Good workplace.

At interval, everything became movement again.

People stood.

People who had spent an entire act knowing where the exits were suddenly forgot.

Wine remained banned inside.

This caused democracy to fail again.

The woman with the cane came out along left aisle.

No issue.

She stopped near me.

"You work here now?"

"Tonight."

"You were in that portrait."

I stared.

"What?"

"Street studies. Senna."

There it was.

Reputation through art.

Terrible.

"Apparently."

"You looked annoyed in it."

"Accurate artist."

She smiled and kept walking.

That was all.

No destiny.

No patron.

Just somebody had seen my face somewhere I wasn't.

I hated Chapter Whatever of my life.

Second act created the flower-frame problem.

Not because the frame failed.

Because audiences loved standing exactly where work needed to happen.

Two men lingered in the marked rear-left pocket talking through the act-start bell.

I said, "Need this space clear."

One moved immediately.

The other said, "We're not in the aisle."

"Correct. You're in the frame path."

He looked around.

"What frame?"

Perfect timing.

Behind him, Nessa and another worker appeared carrying the tall flower frame sideways.

The man moved.

Evidence-based management.

Nessa did not break stride.

"Thanks," she said.

"Demonstration helped."

Frame vanished backstage.

Act two started.

House settled.

I heard Marek enter.

On time, as far as I could tell.

Growth everywhere.

Then a messenger came to the front door from North Gate.

Not for me.

For the theatre generally.

The mustached worker read the board-copy notice and brought it to Rinna during the next safe interval.

I saw only the header as it passed.

NORTH SECONDARY CLOSED UNTIL MORNING REVIEW

Fine.

No urge to chase it.

A whole road could remain wrong without Greg attendance.

Excellent.

The rest of the show was mostly work.

Not story.

Not magic.

Work.

Late couple after second act start.

Dropped scarf under row six.

Child needed privy during quiet scene.

One chair shifted into aisle because a large man leaned on it while standing.

A program crumpled into something wet for reasons I refused to investigate.

At bows, audience became a crowd in reverse.

This was almost worse.

Everyone knew the exit.

Everyone wanted it simultaneously.

Nessa opened both rear paths.

I kept left traffic moving and stopped people from clustering just outside the door to discuss the thing they had literally just seen.

"Keep moving past the threshold."

"We're waiting for someone."

"Wait six steps farther."

"Why?"

"Because everyone behind you also owns a body."

They moved.

Useful principle.

The woman with the cane left without needing me.

Row-three man left complaining about Marek's character.

His wife appeared delighted.

The independently operating child had acquired a program from somewhere despite his family already holding four.

Normal theatre magic.

House clear took longer than performance ending.

Not much.

Enough.

Then reset.

Gray sash off.

Programs collected.

Two chairs returned to line.

One rope post moved back to marked position.

A dropped glove to lost-property box.

Crushed paper.

Cup outside door.

Not inside.

Civilization held.

Nessa and I worked house left while the ticket woman counted something at front table.

Mustache locked one cash box.

Rinna walked the rows once.

"Clear?"

Nessa said, "Left clear."

I said, "Back clear."

Right-side worker called clear.

Rinna nodded.

No ceremony.

The ticket woman handed Nessa a small folded pay slip and two copper.

Nessa handed both to me.

"Count."

I did.

Two.

"Sign."

I signed the slip.

There.

Work converted to money.

No mystery.

I now had three copper.

It felt good.

Not miraculous.

Good.

Rinna passed behind us.

"Third night still good?"

"Yes."

"Same call."

"Unless North Road eats the city."

She looked at me.

"Did it eat anything?"

"Mostly distance."

"Then it can wait until morning."

Correct.

I walked home tired.

Not destroyed.

Shoulders heavy.

Hands fine.

Right leg fine.

Left knee fine.

The oval clicked at the theatre door.

Normal.

At the building door.

Normal.

At the upstairs doorway.

Normal.

I put three copper on the table upstairs.

One old.

Two new.

Not all my money in the world anymore.

Still all my liquid money.

Different feeling.

I looked at the holdfast shelf.

Mara's simple controlled observation cost two copper if nothing special.

I could now pay it and keep one.

That was new.

I did not immediately decide to.

Also new.

The rest stone sat boxed below it.

One copper purchase.

Known fault.

Still unopened.

Oval in my bag.

Long mile note on scrap.

Everything wanted attention if I let it.

I sat instead.

Lyssa came up a little later.

"Paid?"

I showed her the copper.

"Two?"

"Two."

"How bad?"

"People are badly designed."

"Theatre?"

"People."

She nodded as if this confirmed prior research.

"North road?"

"Closed until morning review."

"You going back?"

"No."

She looked at me.

Not surprised.

Just checking.

That mattered too.

"Third night?"

"Still on."

"Then sleep."

"That's authoritarian."

"Yes."

I slept.

Before sleep, though, I had one thought I almost wrote down.

The long mile had reminded me the world could stretch a road without asking.

The theatre had reminded me twenty people arriving late could turn a doorway into a problem without any magic at all.

Geometry was apparently hostile in multiple disciplines.

I did not write that either.

Too broad.

Also I was tired.
