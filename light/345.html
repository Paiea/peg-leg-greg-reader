TEXT READER · CHAPTER 345

THE BENCH

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

I paid Mara two copper because I had finally reached the dangerous financial condition known as having enough money to ask a question properly.

Not that morning.

That would have looked impulsive.

I waited until after breakfast.

Maturity.

The holdfast came down from the shelf wrapped exactly as it had gone up.

Ring sideways.

Base on sailcloth.

Packing slats still protecting the distorted housing.

I checked the wrapping before touching anything else.

Nothing had moved.

Probably.

I had not marked the dust.

I was not becoming that person.

Yet.

Lyssa watched from the doorway.

"Mara?"

"Yes."

"Two copper?"

"If simple."

"And if not simple?"

"I stop being a customer."

"Good."

Cala looked at the wrapped object.

"You're carrying that through the house?"

"Inactive."

"That wasn't my question."

"Yes."

"Don't hit the wall."

Reasonable magical safety procedure.

I put it in the shoulder bag only long enough to discover it did not fit comfortably.

The base caught the opening.

The ring made the shape worse.

I removed it.

Wrapped bundle under one arm while using two crutches was also stupid.

I stared at the object.

The object remained economically inconvenient.

Lyssa said, "Basket."

"I know."

"You looked like you didn't."

We had a shallow household market basket with two handles and enough room to keep the holdfast flat.

I lined it with folded cloth.

Set the wrapped object inside.

Then tied the handles together loosely with ordinary cord so the bundle could not bounce out.

This created a new problem.

Carrying basket plus two crutches.

I looked at Lyssa.

She looked at me.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"Your face did."

"My face is private."

"Buy a clasp."

Cruel.

In the end I hooked the basket handle over the right crutch grip below my hand and hated it immediately.

It swung.

No.

I tried a short cord from basket handles to shoulder bag strap.

Better.

Basket rode against my right hip.

Still awkward.

Manageable for a short walk.

Artifacts changed logistics before activation.

Excellent.

I left with five copper in my head and three in my purse because two had already become conceptually dead.

That was not accounting.

That was self-defense.

Mara was at South Registry Row.

Third table from the corner today.

Different stool.

Same expression.

She looked at me.

Then the basket.

"You packed it better."

"Free advice had consequences."

"Rare."

I set the basket down carefully.

"Simple controlled observation. Two copper if simple."

"That's what I quoted."

"Still true?"

"Depends what you brought."

"Same thing."

"Things change."

Fair after last night.

I unwrapped only enough for her to identify the holdfast.

She looked at the opposing-hook mark.

Rear screw.

Distorted seam.

Then at me.

"No opening?"

"Correct."

"No load certification?"

"Correct."

"No repair?"

"Correct."

"Behavior only?"

"If simple."

"Two copper."

I paid.

She counted once.

Three remained.

Not painful.

Noticeable.

Different.

Mara wrote a narrow work strip.

HOLDFAST / CLOSED HOUSING / CONTROLLED BENCH OBSERVATION / NO LOAD CERT / NO OPENING

Then she added:

OWNER PRESENT

"Why that?"

"So nobody thinks I took custody."

Good.

"Do I sign?"

"At end if you agree with observation record."

Also good.

She stood.

"Bring it."

I looked at basket.

Then crutches.

Mara watched me calculate.

"I'll carry basket."

"You sure?"

"I'm charging you."

Excellent economic model.

She took the basket.

We went through a narrow side door behind the row.

Not into some secret artifact vault.

A work court.

Open roof slats.

Stone floor.

Three benches.

Two occupied.

At one, a man was sorting ceramic disks into felt-lined trays.

At another, a woman had a brass tube clamped between wood jaws while she measured something with a black thread.

Neither looked at me long.

Professional paradise.

Mara took the empty bench.

It had a thick wood top scarred by use and a pale stone inset roughly the size of a dinner plate.

Beside it sat a low iron frame with four adjustable feet.

"Observation bed?" I asked.

"Bench plate."

Of course.

"What does it do?"

"Less than you want."

"Promising."

She set my basket on a side stool.

"It gives me a known surface and tells me if gross force leaves the plate boundary."

"How?"

"Edge line changes."

The pale stone had a thin gray line around its rim.

"Magnitude?"

"No."

"Direction?"

"Sometimes enough to see which edge reacts first. Not measurement."

"Mana?"

"It doesn't tell me source."

Good.

A bounded tool.

I loved it already.

Mara added, "If your piece does something outside what I consider simple, I stop."

"And keep two copper?"

"Yes."

"Cruel."

"Bench time happened."

Fair.

She unwrapped the holdfast herself while I watched.

Ring sideways.

Base flat.

No activation.

The thing looked exactly as disappointing as before.

Old dark metal.

Circular base.

Ring.

Housing distortion.

A piece of equipment with the aesthetic confidence of a door fitting.

Mara placed it at center of pale plate.

"First observation."

Nothing happened.

"Strong start."

"Closed housing at rest. No visible response on plate."

She wrote that.

Then she put four little wooden blocks around the base without touching it.

"Reference only."

"So if it moves."

"Yes."

She waited.

Nothing.

Good.

Then she brought out a long wooden rod with a forked end.

No magic I could see.

"I am going to rotate the ring from outside hand reach."

My attention sharpened.

The seller had turned the ring during demonstration.

I had not since buying it.

"How far?"

"Small."

"Define."

"Until first response or one eighth turn. Whichever comes first."

Good.

"And if it resists?"

"Stop."

"Clicks?"

"Stop and observe."

"Moves the base?"

"Stop."

"Anything else?"

"If I dislike it."

Perfect measurement.

She fitted fork over ring.

Slow pressure.

The ring moved.

Barely.

A dry internal scrape.

Then a click.

Mara stopped.

Immediately.

No drama.

The gray edge line on the bench plate darkened at the near-right edge.

Not all around.

A short section.

I leaned forward.

"Force outside plate?"

"Gross response crossed boundary at that edge."

"Direction?"

"Don't make it cleaner than it is."

Good.

She waited.

The darkened line faded.

The holdfast sat still.

Ring now perhaps a finger-width from original sideways position.

"Any movement?" I asked.

"Base didn't visibly shift against blocks."

She checked each reference block.

No obvious contact change.

"Again?"

"Not yet."

She put the wooden rod down.

Then did something I did not expect.

She placed a plain iron washer on the pale plate.

Not touching holdfast.

About a handspan away.

"Why washer?"

"Known loose object."

"Magical?"

"No."

She waited.

Nothing.

Moved washer to other side.

Nothing.

Closer.

Nothing.

"So not attracting metal."

Mara looked at me.

"So this washer did not visibly move at these positions while the holdfast sat in this state."

I smiled.

"I knew I liked you."

"That's unfortunate."

She moved washer away.

Next came a short length of rope.

Thick.

Old.

She laid one end across the plate, again not touching holdfast.

Nothing.

Then a little wooden peg.

Nothing.

A flat iron strip.

Nothing.

"You're checking material response."

"Crude environmental response."

"Because broad family includes anchoring, rigging, restraint, load control."

"Because those are broad guesses and I don't want one object nearby to become a theory."

Better.

She removed everything.

"Now I return ring."

"To original?"

"As close as observed."

Forked rod.

Slow pressure backward.

The ring moved.

Dry scrape.

No click.

Then stopped at sideways.

Mara removed rod.

Bench line did not darken.

Interesting.

"One-way response?"

"One observed response on one movement."

Right.

I shut up.

Mara wrote.

Then she looked at the holdfast for a while.

"That's simple observation."

I stared.

"That's it?"

"You paid for simple."

"I hate honest businesses."

"Good."

I looked at the work strip.

"What did we learn?"

"Read it at end."

"What do you think?"

"Different service."

"Cruel."

"Correct."

I looked at the holdfast again.

It had responded to a tiny ring turn.

Not visibly moved.

Bench plate registered something leaving boundary at one edge.

No nearby loose washer, rope, peg, or strip moved while it sat afterward.

Returning ring did not reproduce visible plate response.

That was not much.

It was more than yesterday.

"Could the plate response be from internal movement?"

"Yes."

"Could it be from force into bench?"

"Yes."

"Could it be lateral?"

"Possibly."

"Could it be magical?"

"Probably, given object history and seller demonstration, but plate doesn't identify source."

"Could the housing distortion be causing asymmetry?"

"Possible."

"Could the ring position matter?"

"Obviously."

"Could it be dangerous?"

"Everything can be dangerous if you use enough confidence."

"Specific."

"I have not established safe load, safe surface, intended fixture, intended attachment, rated direction, service condition, or release behavior."

There.

A beautiful list of ignorance.

"So yes."

"So don't use it."

"You are destroying my hobbies."

"You need cheaper hobbies."

I thought about theatre.

"Apparently not."

Mara gave me the strip.

It read:

CLOSED HOUSING / VISUAL ONLY BEFORE MOVEMENT

RING MOVED LESS THAN 1/8 TURN TO FIRST INTERNAL CLICK

BENCH EDGE RESPONSE: NEAR-RIGHT SECTION DARKENED / FADED AFTER STOP

NO VISIBLE BASE SHIFT AGAINST REFERENCE BLOCKS

NO VISIBLE MOVEMENT OF LOOSE WASHER / ROPE END / WOOD PEG / IRON STRIP IN OBSERVED POSITIONS AFTER RESPONSE

RING RETURNED APPROX ORIGINAL SIDE POSITION / NO SECOND EDGE RESPONSE OBSERVED

NO LOAD APPLIED

NO HOUSING OPENED

NO FUNCTION / SAFE USE / CONDITION CERTIFIED

I read it twice.

"Accurate?" Mara asked.

"Yes."

"Sign owner line."

I signed.

She signed provider line.

Then stamped it.

Not registration.

Just her work mark.

I folded the strip.

"Question."

"One."

"Free?"

"If I decide."

"What would be the next useful observation if I ever spend more?"

She looked at the holdfast.

Not at me.

"Fixture context."

"Meaning?"

"Something called a holdfast that produces boundary response when ring moves is telling you less on a loose bench than it would mounted the way its family expected."

"Do you know how?"

"No."

"Can you determine how?"

"Maybe. Not for two copper."

There it was.

The next price tier.

"How much?"

"I didn't quote."

"Why?"

"Because I'd need to decide whether I can do it safely and whether I need someone else."

Good.

No invented price.

"Would opening housing help first?"

"Maybe. Might also make the object worse."

"Because distorted seam."

"Because old equipment dislikes curiosity."

I respected that professionally.

She rewrapped the holdfast.

Same orientation.

Ring sideways.

Packing slats.

Basket.

"Anything else?"

I almost asked about the rest stone.

I had it at home.

Retired industrial component.

Known release delay outside yard limit.

This was a registry-row work court.

People here had benches.

Procedures.

Maybe Mara knew counterload systems.

Maybe she didn't.

I had three copper left.

I stopped.

One question had been purchased.

I did not need to turn the visit into every artifact I owned.

"No."

Mara looked faintly surprised.

That was satisfying.

"No?"

"Growth."

"Expensive?"

"Two copper."

She handed me the basket.

This time I knew how to rig it to my shoulder strap without experimenting in public.

Also growth.

Outside, South Registry Row was busy.

A woman argued over a maker mark at one table.

A courier carried three sealed boxes with a red route tag.

A man in blue work sleeves had an object shaped like a fork with seven tines and absolutely no business existing.

I wanted to know everything.

I went home.

This was becoming alarming.

At home, Cala saw the basket.

"Still whole?"

"Yes."

"Wall?"

"Also whole."

"Good."

Lyssa asked, "Worth two?"

That was harder.

I put the holdfast back on the high shelf before answering.

Same place.

Ring sideways.

Wrapped.

Out of Tavi reach.

Then I showed her the observation strip.

She read slowly.

"So what does it do?"

"Unknown."

She looked at me.

"You paid two copper."

"Correct."

"And now you know what it does?"

"No."

"You got robbed."

"I know it produces some kind of boundary response after a small ring movement without visibly moving itself. I know returning the ring didn't reproduce the same response. I know several loose materials nearby didn't visibly react. I know Mara thinks fixture context is more useful than more loose-bench guessing."

Lyssa stared.

"You paid two copper to know less."

"Exactly."

"That's stupid."

"It's excellent."

She handed the strip back.

"Why?"

I thought about the privacy clasp.

Registration said privacy.

Current behavior had included silence.

The long mile looked like a road and behaved like an argument about geometry.

The oval looked like a piece of junk and had become a retreat problem I still did not understand.

Knowing what not to claim was becoming expensive.

"Because yesterday I could make up ten explanations," I said. "Today six of them are worse."

Lyssa considered that.

"Still sounds like you paid two copper to lose ideas."

"Bad ideas are expensive if you keep them."

She made a face.

"That sounded wise."

"Damn it."

Tavi came in.

"What did you buy?"

"Less certainty."

"Can I have it?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Very expensive."

He accepted this more easily than Lyssa.

Children understood markets.

I went upstairs later with three copper.

Three.

Not five.

That mattered.

The holdfast had not become useful.

It had become narrower.

That also mattered.

I took out the narrow scrap.

Wrote:

HOLDFAST BENCH / 2 C

<1/8 RING TURN -> INTERNAL CLICK

BENCH EDGE RESPONSE NEAR-RIGHT

NO VISIBLE BASE SHIFT

RETURN TO SIDE POSITION -> NO SECOND RESPONSE

NO LOAD / NO OPEN / NO FUNCTION

NEXT USEFUL QUESTION MAY REQUIRE FIXTURE CONTEXT

Then:

3 C CASH / 26 C VALE

I looked at the rest-stone box.

Did not touch it.

I looked at the holdfast.

Did not touch it either.

The oval was near my bag.

Three strange objects.

Three copper.

Twenty-six debt.

One answer that had mostly removed answers.

Good.

That was what information was supposed to do before it became knowledge.
