TEXT READER · CHAPTER 360

THE WRONG TAG

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

North Secondary looked healthier from a distance.

This was suspicious.

The last time I had been there, the route had spent most of its energy proving that roads could have administrative trauma.

Now the red closure boards were gone from the outer approach.

Yellow route markers remained.

White cord marked one narrow work lane beside the public path.

Three new iron housings sat along the left retaining wall with fresh paint around their bolts.

Nothing was smoking.

Excellent start.

I arrived before second bell.

Saret was already there.

Of course he was.

People who carried iron tags for a living developed punctuality or disappeared into paperwork.

"You ate?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Good."

"Good morning to you too."

"Morning."

That was probably his version.

He handed me a narrow slate board with six lines written in chalk.

Not numbers.

Locations.

NORTH ENTRY / WALL THREE

CUT TWO / LOW POST

OLD DRAIN TURN / INNER FACE

MARKER NINE / EAST BASE

BEND FOUR / RAIL SIDE

UPPER RETURN / GATE FOOT

"Six?"

"Six replacements."

"That's all?"

"If they match."

Dangerous sentence.

Saret had a folded paper list in one hand and three spare chalk sticks in coat pocket.

"You read physical," he said. "I read list."

"I remember."

"Say whole tag. Don't summarize."

"Marker, location, stamped line, part present."

"Yes."

"No diagnosis."

"Yes."

"No repair."

"Yes."

"No helpful corrections."

He looked at me.

"Especially that."

I liked work where competence involved restraint.

A gray-coated route supervisor met us at the entry rope.

Woman maybe fifty.

Wide face.

Braided silver hair.

Carried a long black key on cord around neck.

"Saret."

"Morning."

"Second reader?"

"Greg."

She looked at my crutches.

Then work lane.

"Ground route. Two short stair sets. Dry today. You need alternate?"

"No."

"If that changes, say it before you're tired."

Good.

"I will."

"I'm Pell. Route supervisor. You do not cross red stitch lines on stone."

She pointed.

Thin red thread had been laid into shallow grooves across several side cuts.

Not rope.

Actual thread.

Bright enough to see.

"Physical boundary?" I asked.

"Work boundary."

"Any effect?"

"No."

Excellent.

Not every red line had to be magical.

Pell unlocked side gate.

"Four copper after sign-off if completed. If one location cannot be read safely, mark unread and continue. Do not create a clean list by leaning into bad access."

I looked at Saret.

"I like her."

Pell said, "Don't."

Also excellent.

We started.

The route felt different from public side.

Not mysterious.

Maintained.

That mattered.

Public path was pale stone bordered by low walls.

Inside work lane I could see backs of route housings, cable grooves, ceramic sleeves under guard plates, little maintenance marks nobody on public side would notice.

The magic remained mostly invisible.

Infrastructure had the decency to look like infrastructure.

First location.

NORTH ENTRY / WALL THREE.

Fresh iron bracket at knee height.

Tag hung from short chain under lip.

I crouched carefully with both crutches planted wide.

Read.

"North entry. Wall three. Tag N-E-three. Replacement line A-seven. Physical housing present. Two mounting bolts visible."

Saret checked paper.

"Match."

He marked line.

That was it.

No revelation.

Second location.

CUT TWO / LOW POST.

Low post meant low.

Very low.

I sat on nearby stone edge rather than pretending nineteen-year-old pride improved geometry.

"Cut two. Low post. Tag C-two-L. Replacement line B-four. Post present. Face plate present."

"Match."

Third.

OLD DRAIN TURN / INNER FACE.

Narrower lane.

Wet wall.

One ceramic drain rib humming softly behind cover.

I did not care why.

Growth continued.

Tag was tucked under inner face near ankle height.

"Old Drain Turn. Inner face. Tag O-D-T-nine. Replacement line C-eleven. Housing present. Guard present."

Saret looked at list longer.

"Again."

I read again.

"O-D-T-nine. C-eleven."

He checked.

"Match."

"You hesitated."

"My writing is bad."

"Honest."

"Keep moving."

We did.

At first stair set, route bent upward beside retaining wall.

Seven shallow steps.

Wide.

Dry.

Saret went ahead without helping.

Good.

I took them at own pace.

Right foot.

Crutches.

Shift.

No bridge moving beneath me.

Better.

Fourth location.

MARKER NINE / EAST BASE.

This one had a new stone marker taller than my knee, iron collar at base.

Fresh pale seal compound around edge.

Tag hung from east side.

I read.

"Marker nine. East base. Tag M-nine-E. Replacement line D-six. Marker present. Collar present."

Saret stopped.

Not hesitated.

Stopped.

"Again."

I looked at him.

"M-nine-E. D-six."

He unfolded list farther.

Ran finger down column.

"List says D-eight."

There it was.

A mismatch.

Nothing exploded.

No music.

No conspiracy appeared from alley.

Just D-six on metal and D-eight on paper.

"Physical says D-six," I repeated.

"Yes."

"What now?"

"We mark mismatch."

He drew square around line.

Pell had told us to call her if mismatch affected route status.

Did this?

Not our decision.

Saret raised two fingers toward next visible worker.

Not shout.

Worker relayed signal up lane.

Pell arrived maybe three minutes later.

"Which?"

Saret showed list.

I pointed without touching.

"Marker nine east base. Physical tag M-nine-E. Replacement line D-six. List says D-eight."

Pell crouched.

Read herself.

"D-six."

"Yes."

She looked at seal compound.

Then at mounting collar.

Did not open anything.

"Was this one replaced yesterday?"

Saret said, "List says yes."

"By line D-eight."

"Yes."

Pell stood.

"Could be wrong tag. Could be wrong list. Could be wrong unit."

Three possibilities.

No conclusion.

She took black key from neck and unlocked small side box mounted on wall twenty feet away.

Inside were folded route sheets.

She pulled one.

Not maintenance record.

Installation handoff maybe.

Read.

Her mouth tightened.

"Stop read here."

Saret asked, "Whole route?"

"No. This marker only. Continue other locations. Do not count marker nine as cleared."

"Public route?"

"Public route stays current restriction until I know what was installed."

There.

Our mismatch mattered.

But not because Greg solved it.

Because accurate reading prevented a false clean sign-off.

Pell waved over two workers.

"Verify D-six versus D-eight. No opening until install sheet and physical lot agree."

One worker said, "Could just be tag left from old."

Pell said, "Could. Verify."

No one rewarded speculation.

Civilization survived.

Saret marked:

MISMATCH / HOLD CLEARANCE

Then looked at me.

"Next."

I liked him too.

Procedurally.

Fifth location.

BEND FOUR / RAIL SIDE.

We walked farther uphill.

The route here opened over shallow drainage channel.

Not canal.

Stone cut with pale water moving beneath slotted covers.

Rail-side housing was new brass-faced rectangle with three narrow blue windows.

One window flickered.

I did not ask.

Actually I did.

"Is flicker normal?"

Saret said, "I don't know."

Good.

"Does it affect tag read?"

"No."

Better.

I read tag.

"Bend four. Rail side. Tag B-four-R. Replacement line F-two. Housing present. Guard rail clear."

"Match."

Sixth location.

UPPER RETURN / GATE FOOT.

Second stair set.

Only four steps but steeper.

Pell's warning returned.

Say something before tired.

I wasn't tired enough to need alternate.

But shoulders had started noticing morning.

I told Saret.

"Slower on stairs. Fine otherwise."

"Fine."

No drama.

He simply slowed.

Upper return had old gate frame with one new dark-gray foot plate.

Tag was clean.

"Upper return. Gate foot. Tag U-R-G. Replacement line G-five. Foot plate present. Three anchor points visible."

Saret checked.

"Match."

He folded paper.

"Done."

It was not even midday.

There was something deeply satisfying about a job ending when advertised.

We returned to marker nine.

Pell and two workers were still there.

One held small tray containing two iron tags.

Old and new maybe.

I did not approach until Pell waved.

"Your read stands," she said.

"Good."

"Install sheet says D-eight. Physical lot stamp on collar says D-eight. Tag says D-six."

Saret asked, "Wrong tag?"

"Looks like."

Looks like.

Still bounded.

Pell continued, "We're replacing tag and re-running independent read after. You two are done."

I looked at collar.

"So unit is probably correct?"

"Probably. Not your sign-off."

"Right."

Good correction.

Pell looked at me.

"You were hired to read. You read."

This city kept finding ways to praise me that sounded like instructions.

I liked those best.

At office, clerk had two columns.

Saret signed one.

I signed one.

Not repair certification.

Work completion only.

Pell signed supervisor line with note:

6 LOCATIONS VISITED

5 MATCH

1 TAG/LIST MISMATCH HELD FROM CLEARANCE

FOLLOW-UP REQUIRED

Then clerk counted four copper into my hand.

Actual money.

Heavy enough to exist.

I counted once.

Four.

Three in purse already.

Seven total.

No bits.

There.

Yesterday's crossed-out plus sign became real only now.

I liked that too.

Saret got paid separately.

I did not ask how much.

Another growth miracle.

Outside office, he said, "You free next week?"

I looked at him.

"For what?"

"Nothing yet."

"Then no."

He frowned.

"You don't know."

"Exactly."

He almost smiled.

Almost.

"Fine. If something exists, I'll ask."

"Good system."

We separated.

The rest of morning belonged to me.

This was the part I had purchased with five copper I never earned.

No.

Bad sentence.

I had accepted less total guaranteed money for this.

Different.

Still real.

I could go home.

Could find Senna.

Could check whether Hanging Road reopened.

Did not.

Could go near market.

Dangerous.

Did not.

Could retrieve Three Reeds and invent a reason to use it.

Absolutely not.

I bought lunch instead.

One bit.

A bowl of lentils with smoked onion and flatbread.

Ordinary spending.

No crisis.

No calculation beyond wanting lunch and having money.

This was maybe economic progression too.

I sat outside under awning and ate while workers passed.

A pair of route hands carried a replacement marker in sling.

Not ours.

Probably.

I did not follow them.

After lunch I walked home slowly.

Lyssa was not there.

Charcoal coat also not there.

Cala said, "She went out."

I looked at her.

Cala looked at me.

"Don't."

"I didn't ask."

"Your face."

Everyone had become tyrant about face.

Tavi was on floor building spoon carrier again.

It had acquired third wheel.

"Where's Lyssa?" he asked.

I looked at Cala.

"Out."

Tavi nodded.

Reasonable child.

I went upstairs.

Opened notes.

Wrote:

NORTH SECONDARY / TAG WALK COMPLETE

6 LOCATIONS

5 MATCH

1 MISMATCH

MARKER 9 EAST BASE

PHYSICAL TAG D-6 / LIST D-8

HELD FROM CLEARANCE

SUPERVISOR FOLLOW-UP

Then:

PAID 4 C

CASH NOW 7 C

26 C VALE

I paused.

Seven.

Not twelve.

Not the nine-plus-three I would have after South Quay if three days already passed.

Those days had not passed.

Comparison was fake.

Today I had seven and an afternoon.

That was current reality.

I added:

READING CORRECTLY CAN BE ENOUGH.

Then stared.

Too neat again.

I crossed it out.

Wrote instead:

DON'T FIX A MISMATCH BY MAKING THE PAPER FEEL BETTER.

Better.

Downstairs door opened.

Lyssa came in.

I heard Cala say nothing.

Then Tavi shouted, "YOU BOUGHT IT."

I closed eyes.

There it was.

Not my decision.

Not my ledger.

Still interesting.

I went downstairs.

Lyssa stood by door wearing charcoal coat.

High collar folded down.

Dark horn buttons.

Deep pockets.

It fit even better after alteration.

She looked at me.

I remembered instruction.

No talking.

No faces.

We were not in shop anymore.

Still.

I said nothing.

She waited.

I kept face neutral.

This became painful.

"Well?" she said.

"You told me not to make faces."

"In the shop."

"I'm being respectful beyond contract."

"Greg."

"It looks good."

She tried not to smile.

Failed slightly.

"Nine?" I asked.

Then immediately regretted it.

Her eyebrow rose.

"You said my money isn't your ledger."

"Correct. Withdrawn."

"Eight and six bits."

I blinked.

"You negotiated."

"I waited."

"Different weapon."

"He had someone else try it. Sleeves were wrong. I came back. He offered eight and six bits if I took it with sleeve adjustment only."

"And you did."

"Yes."

"Happy?"

She thought.

Actually thought.

"Yes."

Good.

That was enough.

Cala touched sleeve.

"Good wool."

Lyssa said, "I know."

Tavi put both hands into one pocket.

"This pocket is huge."

"Get out of my coat."

"I'm checking it."

"You don't live in there."

"I could put spoon carrier in it."

"No."

"Only little one."

"No."

His speech continued while she removed him from pocket.

I watched.

Seven copper in my purse.

Lyssa had bought something she wanted without asking permission from Greg's invisible economic council.

North Secondary still had one held tag mismatch and one route supervisor who owned problem.

South Quay had another counter.

Carrow continued.

Nothing required me at center.

This was becoming suspiciously comfortable.

Later, before dinner, I added one more line to notes:

AFTERNOON USED WELL.

Then crossed it out too.

I had not used afternoon.

It had simply happened.

That was allowed.
