TEXT READER · CHAPTER 365

THE PARTNER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Lyssa asked me to carry cloth.

This was suspicious.

Not because cloth was dangerous.

Usually.

Because Lyssa could carry cloth herself.

"How much?" I asked.

She stood beside table with two folded bundles tied in gray cord.

"Those."

"That is not a weight."

"They're cloth, Greg."

"Armor can be cloth."

"They are not armor."

"Blankets can kill."

"Only if I use one correctly."

Cala kept peeling turnips.

Tavi looked interested.

I pointed at him.

"Witness."

"To murder?"

"Potentially."

Lyssa pushed one bundle toward me.

"Marra wants these back before midday. I have the other one and the sample book."

There.

Actual reason.

Three hands worth of objects.

She had two.

I had none, technically, because crutches occupied both.

But shoulder bag existed.

And straps.

"You want my bag?"

"I want your body."

Tavi made a noise.

Lyssa closed eyes.

I said, "Excellent phrasing."

"For carrying cloth."

"Too late."

Cala said, "Go."

Lyssa looked at her.

"He started it."

"You finished it. Go."

We went.

The bundle fit under shoulder-bag strap against my right side if tied separately with a short cord.

Not elegant.

Stable enough.

Lyssa checked knot twice.

"If it slides, tell me."

"I have survived cargo before."

"That sentence is why I said tell me."

Fair.

We took East Cloth route toward Marra's green door.

No blue hour.

No flying fish.

No bridge becoming architecture experiment.

Carrow was almost disappointingly normal.

Morning carts.

Wet stone.

A woman shouting prices for pears.

Two apprentices arguing over whether a red bolt was rust or brick.

Lyssa slowed at one shop window.

Not coat shop.

Thread.

Rows of narrow spools behind glass.

She looked maybe two seconds.

Then kept walking.

I said nothing.

This was apparently relationship growth.

At next corner she said, "You can ask."

"About thread?"

"You were visibly not asking."

"I was respecting boundaries."

"Loudly."

"Difficult skill."

She smiled.

"Marra might have larger work."

That changed route without changing street.

"Larger how?"

"Don't know."

"You know something."

"I know she sent a note yesterday asking if I could stay after delivery."

"That is something."

"Yes."

"You didn't mention it."

"I am mentioning it now."

I waited.

She waited.

This was trap.

Not malicious.

Relationship trap.

"Do you want me there?" I asked.

"I asked you to carry cloth."

"That is not answer."

"I know."

We walked another half block.

Then she said, "Maybe."

Good.

Messy answer.

"For what?"

"I don't know yet. If she offers something, I might want another person hearing terms. Not deciding. Hearing."

There.

Role arrived before title.

"You want witness."

"Maybe."

"Not adviser?"

She gave me look.

"Especially not adviser unless I ask."

"Cruel."

"You survived."

"Barely."

"And if I don't want you in room, you leave."

"Yes."

She glanced over.

Maybe checking whether yes had hidden argument.

It didn't.

"Okay," she said.

Marra's green door was open.

Inside smelled like wool steam, chalk, lamp oil, and hot iron.

Three worktables occupied front room.

One woman I recognized from prior visit sat pinning dark sleeve to form.

Jessa was near back shelves with mouth full of thread.

She saw us.

Pulled thread free.

"Late."

Lyssa looked at wall clock.

"We're early."

"Then I was emotionally prepared too soon."

Jessa's speech remained hers.

Good.

Marra came from back room carrying ledger and two cloth cards.

"Lyssa."

"Marra."

"Greg."

"Furniture."

Marra looked at bundle tied to me.

"Useful furniture."

"Rare."

Lyssa untied it before conversation could become species.

We set both bundles on table.

Marra opened first.

Gray-blue work cloth.

Checked edges.

Second bundle, heavier brown weave.

She rubbed thumb across seam sample Lyssa had pinned to top.

"Good."

Lyssa said, "The brown pulls if you turn seam too tight."

"I saw."

"Then why did you mark same allowance?"

Marra looked at her.

"Because I wanted to see if you would follow mark or cloth."

Lyssa went still.

Not angry exactly.

I stayed furniture.

"That's stupid," Lyssa said.

Jessa, from shelf, said, "It is a little stupid."

Marra ignored her.

"Why?"

"Because if you want to know whether I can judge cloth, ask me to judge cloth. Don't put bad instruction on paid work and wait to see whether I catch it."

Marra set sample down.

"You changed it."

"Yes."

"Correctly."

"That's not point."

There was history here I did not own.

Maybe apprenticeship culture.

Maybe Marra's style.

Maybe Lyssa hated tests disguised as work.

I knew which side I liked.

Did not matter.

Not my argument.

Marra said, "Fair."

Lyssa blinked.

So did I, internally.

Marra continued, "I should have told you it was judgment sample."

Lyssa's shoulders lowered half inch.

"Yes."

"Next time I will."

Jessa said, "Write date down."

Marra looked at her.

"Shelf."

"I'm shelving."

She was not.

She started.

Marra turned ledger around.

"Sit."

Lyssa sat.

I remained standing until she looked at me.

"You too, if you're staying."

I looked at Lyssa.

She nodded.

I sat.

Partner.

Not business partner.

Not yet anything contractual.

Just person invited to stay.

Marra opened ledger to marked page.

"I have a six-week house order."

Lyssa did not move.

Marra pointed.

"South Hall kitchens replacing service aprons, sleeve guards, carry wraps, and heat-cloths. Not uniforms. Work goods. They want first delivery in ten days, then weekly lots."

"How much?" Lyssa asked.

"Enough that I cannot do all finishing here without dropping regular work."

"How much for me?"

Good question.

Marra slid one cloth card forward.

Not money yet.

Scope.

"First lot: twelve aprons, twelve sleeve pairs, eight carry wraps. Cut here. You take finishing home. Return in four days. If accepted, second lot larger."

Lyssa read card.

"Heat-cloths?"

"Not first lot."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"I don't know your edge treatment for them."

"Good."

This time not test.

Just answer.

Lyssa read again.

"Materials supplied?"

"Cloth, thread, fasteners supplied. You supply needles, shears, pressing setup."

"Pattern?"

"Cut pieces here. Marked."

"Corrections?"

"My error, I pay rework. Your seam error, you rework. Material defect, bring back, don't substitute."

"Rejected finish?"

"Specific reason written."

"How much?"

Marra finally turned second card.

FIRST LOT

6 C

ON ACCEPTED RETURN

Lyssa went quiet.

Six copper.

Not enormous.

Not trivial.

For four days home finishing around other life.

Potential second lot larger, unknown.

I kept face neutral.

This was her money.

Her work.

Her decision.

Marra looked at me.

"You can breathe."

"I am."

"Very carefully."

Lyssa said, "He does that when trying not to have opinion."

"I have many opinions."

"Keep them."

"Heroically."

Marra pushed card closer.

"You can take until tomorrow morning."

Lyssa asked, "Why me?"

Marra leaned back.

Not short answer apparently.

"Because you finish clean. Because you notice cloth before mark. Because when you are late, you tell me before I have to find you. Because you have enough pride to redo bad work and enough sense to complain about it first. Because Jessa is already full and because I am not paying shop rate for work I don't need done in shop."

Jessa called from shelf, "Also she is less annoying than Fera."

Marra said, "That too."

Lyssa looked at card.

"Six."

"Six."

"For first lot."

"Accepted return."

"And second?"

"If first passes, likely eight to ten depending quantity. I won't promise before Hall confirms weekly count."

Good.

No imaginary future income.

Lyssa tapped card.

"Can I do first here instead?"

Marra's eyebrows rose.

"Why?"

"I want to see pace before I bring twelve aprons into Cala's house and discover I hate everyone."

I looked at her.

She looked at me.

"Everyone includes you."

"I assumed."

Marra considered.

"Bench space costs me."

"How much?"

"One copper off lot if you use rear bench all four days."

Lyssa made face.

"No."

"Then home."

"What about one day?"

"Two bits."

There it was.

Bits.

My continuity headache returned from dead.

Not Greg's money.

Lyssa's.

Safe.

I nearly laughed.

No one asked why.

Lyssa said, "One day rear bench. I finish first four aprons and sleeve pairs here, learn pace, take rest home. Two bits off six copper?"

"Two bits bench fee, paid today. Six copper still on accepted return."

Important distinction.

Lyssa frowned.

"Why not deduct?"

"Separate books."

"Of course."

Marra shrugged.

"I didn't invent books."

"Someone did. I dislike them."

I said, "Strong position."

Lyssa looked at me.

"Furniture."

"Silent furniture."

Marra smiled.

Lyssa pulled small purse from coat pocket.

Charcoal coat.

Deep pocket.

Useful already.

She counted two bits.

Her bits.

Not mine.

Paid Marra.

Marra wrote bench use on card.

"Today?"

"Today."

"You brought shears?"

Lyssa held up sample book.

"No."

Marra looked at her.

Lyssa sighed.

"I thought we were talking."

"We are. Work can start after midday if you go get them."

"I can."

She looked at me.

There it was again.

Not request yet.

I waited.

"Can you bring my shears and needle roll back?"

"Yes."

"They're in upper room, left drawer of work chest. Needle roll tied yellow. Not blue."

"Anything else?"

"Small pressing cloth. Hanging behind stove."

"Anything else?"

She thought.

"Food."

"For you?"

"Unless you plan to eat it on route."

"I hadn't until accusation."

Marra said, "Bring enough. She gets mean when hungry."

Lyssa looked at her.

"You offered me work."

"I contain contradictions."

I stood.

"I can return before midday."

Lyssa caught my sleeve lightly before I moved.

"Wait."

I waited.

She looked at Marra.

"Can he hear one more thing?"

Marra said, "Your choice."

Lyssa looked back at me.

"Stay."

I sat again.

Marra turned ledger page.

"House order has delivery penalty. Mine, not yours, unless we write otherwise."

Lyssa's face sharpened.

"Why tell me?"

"Because if this becomes regular, I may offer you weekly committed lots. Those would have timing terms. First lot does not."

"What timing terms?"

"Not written yet."

"Then don't tell me maybe terms."

Marra paused.

"Fair."

Again.

Lyssa said, "First lot only. Six copper accepted return. Four days. Materials supplied. My seam errors I rework. Your cut/mark errors you own. Defects come back. No delivery penalty passed to me. One-day rear bench separately paid two bits today. Correct?"

Marra nodded.

"Correct."

Lyssa looked at me.

"What did you hear?"

Ah.

Not advice.

Witness.

I repeated it.

Exactly.

Marra corrected one phrase.

"My cut or written-mark error. Not every mark error. If Hall changes spec after cut, that is Hall change. I still handle it, but different category."

Lyssa said, "Fine. Add that."

Marra wrote note.

Then all three looked at it.

Good.

Not contract exactly.

Work card.

Clear enough.

Lyssa said, "Okay. First lot."

Marra held out hand.

Lyssa shook it.

I did not.

Not my deal.

That distinction felt better than expected.

I left for house.

On route I realized I had become errand boy.

Partner had lasted seventeen minutes.

Roles were flexible.

At home Cala was still at table.

Tavi gone outside with explicit permission, apparently.

"She took it?" Cala asked.

"First lot. Six copper if accepted. Four days. She's using rear bench today for two bits."

Cala nodded once.

Not shocked.

"Good."

"She wants shears, yellow needle roll, pressing cloth, food."

"She said yellow?"

"Yes."

"Good. Blue has coarse needles."

Useful.

I went upstairs.

Left drawer.

Two rolls.

Yellow.

Blue.

I took yellow.

Shears in leather sleeve.

Pressing cloth behind stove downstairs.

Cala wrapped bread, cheese, apple.

"For both?"

"She said food."

"Take enough for both."

"I wasn't invited to work."

"You will be there at midday."

This was household logistics, not prophecy.

She was right.

I carried everything back in shoulder bag.

Marra's rear workroom was hotter now.

Two pressing irons sat near small stove.

Lyssa had first apron pieces laid out.

Jessa occupied opposite bench with dark green sleeve guards.

I handed tools.

Lyssa checked yellow roll.

"You listened."

"Occasionally."

"Food?"

I held bag.

"Cala assumed I also eat."

"Smart woman."

Marra called from front, "Don't put food on work bench."

Lyssa called back, "I know."

Jessa said, "She says it because Fera did."

"I don't care why she says it."

"There was mustard."

Lyssa stopped.

"On cloth?"

"White cuff."

"Why was Fera eating mustard near white cuff?"

Jessa spread hands.

"Now you understand why you're less annoying."

Different register.

Longer gossip.

Good.

I could leave.

I probably should.

Lyssa threaded needle.

Then said, without looking up, "You can stay for lunch."

"Furniture?"

"Lunch furniture."

I stayed.

Not all afternoon.

That mattered too.

I watched first seam because she asked me to check whether cloth bundle at table edge blocked walkway for my crutches.

It did.

We moved it.

Then I sat by wall and read an old public broadsheet somebody had left on chair.

No investigation.

Mostly shipping notices and argument about canal toll.

Lyssa worked.

Her speech changed when hands busy.

Less dry.

More muttering.

"No, that's stupid. Why would you put notch there? Fine. Fine, I see why. Still ugly. Jessa, is this quarter turn or half?"

"Half on brown. Quarter on gray."

"Card says quarter."

"Old card."

Lyssa stopped.

"Marra."

Marra came.

They corrected card.

No secret test this time.

Good.

At lunch we sat on back step.

Lyssa ate apple first.

"You think I should've taken it?"

There.

Permission.

"Yes."

"Why?"

"First lot is bounded. Materials supplied. Six copper is meaningful. You paid two bits to learn pace before bringing rest home. You kept penalty off first lot. And you can still hate it after four days."

She chewed.

"Too many reasons."

"You asked."

"I know."

"Also Marra clearly wants you because you're good."

She looked away.

Not embarrassed exactly.

Something adjacent.

"She wants cheap finishing."

"Also true."

She looked back.

"See, that's why I ask you."

"Because I ruin compliment?"

"Because you don't make me choose between compliment and math."

That was better than compliment.

I ate bread.

"Six isn't shop rate," I said.

"No."

"But you aren't paying materials or shop overhead except today bench."

"No."

"How many hours?"

"Don't know yet. That's why today."

"Then today matters."

"Yes."

She leaned back against wall.

"If it takes me four full days, six is bad."

"Probably."

"If I can do it around other work in maybe two long days plus pieces, better."

"Yes."

"If second lot is ten and bigger, depends how much bigger."

"Yes."

She frowned.

"You're annoying when agreeing."

"I can disagree randomly."

"Don't."

We ate.

After lunch I left.

No reason to sit there proving support.

That would become surveillance with good intentions.

Lyssa did not ask me to stay.

I did not make her.

At door she said, "Can you tell Cala I might be late?"

"How late?"

"Before dinner. Probably."

"That is not late."

"For starting dinner work it is."

"I'll tell her."

"Thanks."

Then she went back to bench.

I went home.

Cala heard report.

"Good," she said.

"You say good a lot."

"When things are good."

"Disturbing consistency."

She handed me turnips.

"Cut."

Apparently my role changed again.

I cut turnips badly enough that Cala took knife after third.

"Too thick."

"They become thinner when chewed."

"Move."

I moved.

Tavi returned with mud on one knee and no explanation worth believing.

Normal afternoon.

Lyssa came home before dinner.

Tired.

Not miserable.

Important difference.

She dropped into chair.

"Four aprons. Four sleeve pairs."

"Accepted?" Cala asked.

"Marra checked first two of each. Clean. Rest stay until lot return."

"Pace?" I asked.

Lyssa rubbed fingers.

"Better after second. If home setup doesn't fight me, maybe two and half days total work. Spread over four."

"Six still good?"

She thought.

"Good enough to finish first lot."

Not same as yes forever.

Good.

She looked at me.

"You told Cala terms?"

"Only six, four days, supplied materials, two-bit bench today."

"Penalty?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Not household-relevant unless future lot."

She nodded.

Then paused.

"Thank you."

"For carrying cloth?"

"For not making it yours."

That landed harder than six copper.

I covered with stupidity.

"I considered stealing contract."

"You'd sew badly."

"I could learn."

"No."

"Hostile workplace."

She smiled.

Later upstairs I opened notebook.

My own ledger first.

CASH: 8 C

VALE: 23 C

Nothing changed.

Then I almost wrote Lyssa's six-copper job.

Stopped.

Her money.

Her work.

Not my ledger.

I wrote only:

LYSSA / MARRA FIRST LOT

ASK BEFORE ADVICE

IF ASKED TO WITNESS TERMS, REPEAT TERMS

DO NOT BECOME OWNER OF DECISION

Then I stared at last line.

Partner was dangerous word.

People used it to mean business.

Romance.

Work.

Crime.

Cards.

Two people carrying opposite ends of table.

Today it meant simpler thing.

She wanted me beside her while she decided something that belonged to her.

So I stood beside her.

Then I left when she didn't need me.

Partner.

For that.

Enough.
