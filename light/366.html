TEXT READER · CHAPTER 366

THE DANCER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

I was invited dancing because somebody hated me.

That was my first theory.

My second was worse.

"You want to go?" Lyssa asked.

She stood by table with hair half tied and charcoal coat open.

No cloth bundle.

No work card.

No emergency.

Suspicious.

"Where?"

"Lantern Court."

I waited.

"For?"

"Music."

"Why?"

She stared at me.

Cala, from stove, said, "Because people go listen to music."

"At home music cannot trample me."

Tavi looked up from floor.

"You can't dance."

There.

Household support.

"I can dance."

"With crutches?"

"I had legs before you were born."

"You have legs now."

"One and a half."

Cala said, "Greg."

"One and some paperwork."

Lyssa laughed.

Tavi looked at my left knee like maybe paperwork lived there.

"Can you?"

Good question.

Could I dance now?

First life, yes.

Not professionally.

Not well enough to impress anyone who mattered.

But enough taverns, festivals, weddings, victory feasts, terrible diplomatic dinners, and one memorable siege-ceasefire where three kingdoms pretended nobody had poisoned a duke.

This body?

Different problem.

"Probably badly," I said.

Lyssa said, "Good."

"That was fast."

"I don't want good dancing. I want to go out."

There.

No productivity.

No contract.

No object.

No money plan.

Just out.

My brain immediately searched for reasons not to.

Crowd.

Uneven paving.

Standing too long.

Crutches around feet.

Unknown dance style.

Possibility of being nineteen in public.

Last one severe.

Lyssa watched all of that happen on my face.

"You can say no."

That made saying no harder.

Not manipulation.

Freedom.

Annoying.

"When?"

"After dinner."

"How long?"

"Greg."

"Reasonable question."

"Until we want to leave."

Terrible answer.

Excellent answer.

"Fine."

Tavi said, "I want to see."

"No," Cala said.

"Why?"

"Because you're staying here."

"Why?"

"Because I said so."

Tavi looked at me.

I looked away.

Not my jurisdiction.

After dinner Lyssa changed nothing except hair.

That surprised me.

I expected some ritual.

Different dress.

Different shoes.

Something.

She wore charcoal coat over ordinary clothes, hair tied back with dark ribbon, boots she already owned.

I wore what I had.

Brown shoulder bag stayed home.

Oval stayed home with it.

That felt strange.

I almost went back for bag.

Then didn't.

No artifact.

No notebook.

No wrapped charcoal.

Just me.

Disgusting.

Lantern Court sat north of market district where three streets widened around old fountain basin that no longer held water.

Tonight it held musicians.

Not literally in basin.

Mostly.

A low platform had been laid across one half.

Five players.

Two hand drums.

Long-necked string instrument.

Short brass horn.

And something like wooden ribs hung from frame, struck with padded sticks.

Each rib glowed faintly when hit.

Not bright.

Amber pulse along grain.

Sound carried farther than it should have.

I noticed.

Then did not investigate.

Growth remained exhausting.

Lanterns hung on cords across court.

Actual lanterns.

No flying fish.

People stood around edges with cups, bread, skewers, folded paper trays.

Center paving mostly clear.

Maybe forty people.

Not formal.

Not performance exactly.

Music for being there.

Lyssa stopped at edge.

"Still want to leave?"

"I never said I wanted to leave."

"Your shoulders did."

I loosened them.

"Traitors."

She smiled.

First song already moving.

Not one I knew.

Pattern simple enough.

Four-beat phrase with third dragged slightly late.

People in center danced mostly in pairs or loose groups.

Steps lateral.

Turn.

Back.

Cross.

Some hand contact.

Some not.

My first-life body supplied six wrong dances immediately.

None useful.

Lyssa said, "Drink?"

"What?"

"You look like you're preparing military campaign."

"Terrain matters."

"It's a courtyard."

"Historically dangerous terrain."

She bought herself something warm from stall.

I did not ask price.

She did not offer me one.

Good.

I stood near low wall where I could lean hip if needed.

We listened through first song.

Then second.

Lyssa moved with music without dancing.

Small shift in shoulders.

Heel tapping.

I watched center.

Not because afraid.

Because information gathering was noble and not cowardice.

An older man danced with woman using cane.

Not same as crutches.

Useful anyway.

She kept cane mostly planted during turns, pivoted around right foot, let partner move more.

Another pair barely moved feet at all.

Just weight shift and hands.

Nobody cared.

That was problem.

If nobody cared, I lost excuse.

Lyssa finished drink.

"Come on."

"No."

She laughed.

"You said yes."

"To going out."

"Correct."

She did not pull me.

She walked into edge of dance space alone.

Then turned around.

Not challenging.

Just there.

I hated this.

I went.

First problem was crutch tips.

Not slipping.

Paving dry.

But people stepped backward without looking.

I kept to outer ring.

Lyssa stood facing me.

"What do I do?"

"You said you could dance."

"I lied under pressure."

"There was no pressure."

"Exactly. More dangerous."

She showed step.

Left.

Together.

Right.

Together.

Small turn on fourth phrase.

I looked down.

"Don't."

"I need feet."

"You know where yours is."

"Optimistic."

"Greg."

I looked up.

We tried.

Left for her meant right for me.

Easy with right foot.

Together meant bring crutches, then body.

Other direction meant left knee and crutch geometry.

No foot to place.

Could shift pelvis over prosthetic absence only in ways this body did not permit because there was no prosthesis.

Permanent BKA knee preserved.

No fake limb.

So standard step broke.

I stopped.

Lyssa stopped.

No pity.

Good.

"Different," she said.

"Profound."

"Can you turn?"

"Yes."

"Show me."

I planted right crutch slightly forward, left crutch wider, right foot pivot.

Quarter turn.

Reset.

Another.

Not fast.

Stable.

Lyssa watched.

"Okay."

She changed her own step.

Not teacher anymore.

Testing shared geometry.

She moved around me on dragged third beat while I shifted weight and pivoted smaller.

Then returned.

"Again."

Again.

This worked.

Barely dance.

Definitely movement.

Third try, I caught rhythm.

Right foot step.

Crutches together.

Weight shift.

Small pivot.

Lyssa crossed past left side instead of expecting me to travel there.

Return.

My shoulders stopped trying to solve.

Music did something irritating.

Body followed.

Not old body.

This one.

That distinction arrived without permission.

I missed beat.

Lyssa said, "There."

"What?"

"You were doing it."

"Then you spoke."

"Sorry."

"Sabotage."

She laughed and nearly stepped on right crutch.

I moved it.

"Danger."

"Your stick attacked me."

"Crutch."

"Aggressive crutch."

We continued.

One song.

Then I stopped.

Not because pain.

Hands tired faster than expected.

Forearms warm.

Right calf doing more than fair share.

"Wall," I said.

Lyssa nodded.

No `are you okay` parade.

We went back to edge.

I leaned.

She stood beside me breathing slightly harder.

"That was dancing," she said.

"Legally?"

"Barely."

"I'll appeal."

A woman near us laughed.

Not at crutches.

At sentence.

She was maybe thirty, broad face, red scarf, holding paper tray of fried roots.

"He'll lose," she told Lyssa.

"I know."

I said, "Corrupt court."

Woman ate root.

"All dance courts corrupt."

Then she walked away.

No introduction.

Perfect stranger interaction.

Third song faster.

We watched.

A group of four took center, spinning in linked pattern.

No chance.

Not yet.

Maybe never.

Fine.

Lyssa said, "You don't have to do every one."

"I know."

"You looked offended."

"By their knees."

"Their knees?"

"Showing off."

She laughed again.

Fourth song slower.

Different pattern.

People paired closer.

Less traveling.

Lyssa looked at me.

I said, "Maybe."

"That's almost enthusiasm."

"Don't ruin it."

We went back.

This time I chose movement first.

No attempt to copy standard lateral step.

Right foot marked beat.

Crutches carried rhythm as much as body.

Tap.

Plant.

Shift.

Pivot.

Lyssa adapted.

Her hands stayed mostly free until she held my forearm once during turn.

Not support.

Contact.

Important difference.

I felt it anyway.

Music changed phrase.

She moved closer.

I moved crutch out before she arrived.

Competence.

Tiny.

Useful.

Then somebody behind me backed into left crutch.

Hard enough to knock tip sideways.

My body reacted before thought.

Right crutch planted.

Right foot loaded.

Left knee lifted clear.

I caught balance.

The man spun around.

"Shit. Sorry. Sorry, I didn't see."

He was young, maybe my age, flushed from dancing.

Hands up.

"I'm good."

"You sure?"

"Yes."

He looked at crutch.

"I kicked it."

"It survived."

"I can get someone."

"For crutch feelings?"

He blinked.

Lyssa said, "He's fine."

"Right. Sorry."

He backed away more carefully.

No fight.

No humiliation.

No public lesson.

Just collision.

My heart had jumped harder than incident deserved.

Body remembered falling better than pride admitted.

Lyssa saw that.

Didn't say it.

She asked, "Wall?"

I considered.

Then shook head.

"Finish song."

Her expression changed.

Small.

Not praise.

Okay.

We finished.

More careful.

Not frozen.

That mattered.

At end I went wall voluntarily.

Hands definitely tired now.

I rubbed palms.

No blister.

No skin issue.

Lyssa leaned beside me.

"Want to leave?"

I looked around.

Lanterns.

Amber ribs pulsing with each struck note.

People talking over music.

Fried-root smell.

A child asleep across someone's shoulders near fountain edge.

Two women arguing whether horn player had skipped verse.

The man who hit my crutch now dancing six feet farther away from everyone.

I smiled.

"Not yet."

Lyssa said nothing.

Good.

We stayed two more songs without dancing.

Then one where she danced with a woman she apparently knew from cloth district.

I had never met her.

Lyssa introduced us only after.

"Greg, Pera. Pera, Greg."

Pera had curls tied in yellow cloth and spoke as if sentences needed extra rooms.

"Oh, you're Greg. I thought you were taller, which is stupid because Lyssa never said tall, she said you use crutches and somehow my brain decided that meant tall, maybe because crutches are long, which makes no sense because then I'd think everyone with broom was tall."

I liked her immediately.

"I am devastated."

"See, that. She said you do that."

Lyssa said, "I did not say that."

"You did. Not those words. You made face."

"What face?"

Pera made an expression that looked like someone smelling competent cheese.

Lyssa stared.

I said, "Accurate."

"Don't encourage her."

Pera pointed at my crutches.

"You danced?"

"Allegedly."

"Good. My uncle dances with one leg and a cane but he cheats because he was already terrible before losing it, so nobody can tell what changed."

Lyssa made choking sound.

I laughed.

Actual laugh.

Pera continued, "He says same thing. Anyway I need water. Bye."

She left.

I looked at Lyssa.

"You have been hiding her."

"For safety."

"Mine or hers?"

"Everyone's."

Good.

Dialogue variance alive.

Last dance we did was not really a dance.

Musicians slowed into closing piece.

People mostly swayed.

Lyssa stood beside me rather than opposite.

I kept right crutch planted, left angled, right foot tapping.

She leaned shoulder lightly into mine.

No turn.

No step pattern.

Just beat.

I could do beat.

First life supplied memories anyway.

A hall with polished floor.

A woman whose name I had forgotten before she died.

A winter festival in capital that no longer existed in same form.

Boots.

Two feet.

No crutches.

Older hands.

Older scars.

Then Lantern Court came back.

Nineteen.

One right foot.

Left knee.

Two crutches.

Lyssa's shoulder.

Amber ribs.

Not replacement.

Nothing replaced anything.

That was childish arithmetic.

This was another thing.

Song ended.

We walked home slowly.

Not because I couldn't faster.

Because neither of us did.

Halfway home Lyssa said, "You liked it."

"Parts."

"You liked it."

"I liked Pera."

"Dangerous."

"I liked fried-root smell."

"You didn't buy any."

"I can appreciate without acquisition."

"Write that down."

"Never."

She bumped my shoulder lightly.

"You danced."

I considered denying.

Didn't.

"Yeah."

At house Cala was awake.

Tavi was not.

"Good?" she asked.

Lyssa said, "He danced."

Cala looked at me.

"Did he?"

"Barely."

"Traitor."

Cala smiled.

"Good."

Of course.

Upstairs I sat on bed and checked hands.

Red pressure marks.

Normal.

Right calf tired.

Shoulders tired.

No injury.

No fall.

No magic.

No money earned.

No debt reduced.

No artifact learned.

No thread resolved.

I had gone somewhere because Lyssa wanted to go.

Then stayed because I wanted to stay.

I almost opened notebook.

Didn't.

Nothing needed recording.

That felt wrong enough to be useful.

I lay back.

Dancer.

Bad one.

Still counted.
