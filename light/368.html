TEXT READER · CHAPTER 368

THE BIDDER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

I learned about the auction because Sella said, "You like bad objects."

This was slander.

Accurate slander remained slander.

We met on South Cut by accident.

Actual accident.

No bridge rising.

No ropes snapping.

No one needed evacuation.

Sella stood beside a tea cart with one hand around clay cup and the other holding folded notice.

She saw me.

Looked at notice.

Looked back at me.

"You like bad objects."

"I like misunderstood objects."

"Same disease with better coat."

"What's notice?"

She handed it over.

CITY RECOVERY SALE

NORTH WEIGH YARD

SECOND BELL TOMORROW

UNCLAIMED / RETIRED / DAMAGED SERVICE GOODS

INSPECTION FROM FIRST BELL

NO POWERED TESTING ON SALE FLOOR

SOLD AS MARKED

BUYER REMOVES SAME DAY

Below that were lot families.

Hand tools.

Office fixtures.

Lamp housings.

Counterweights.

Route markers.

Kitchen service goods.

Two lines marked SPECIAL HANDLING.

No individual lots.

My pulse did something embarrassing.

Sella drank tea.

"There."

"What?"

"Face."

"I have face."

"Object face."

Apparently everyone had taxonomy for me now.

"Why aren't you going?"

"Because I work for living."

"Cruel."

"Also most recovery sale is junk."

"Most."

"Greg."

I folded notice.

"What does sold as marked mean here?"

"Whatever condition card says is what yard represents. They don't promise hidden function, remaining service life, compatibility, or repair cost. If card says cracked housing, you bought cracked housing. If card says retired, you bought retired."

"Inspection handling?"

"Usually yes unless red cord. No activation. No disassembly."

"Deposit?"

"No bidder deposit for public lots under silver threshold. Pay immediately if you win."

"Cash only?"

"Copper, silver, registered yard credit."

I had copper.

Ten.

Ten suddenly became less like room and more like loaded weapon.

Sella took notice back, then stopped.

"You want it?"

"Yes."

She gave it to me.

"Don't buy counterweight because it looks lonely."

"I have standards."

"You bought retired rest stone."

"It had history."

"Disease."

She walked away.

I stood there reading notice again.

No individual lots.

No guarantee anything interesting existed.

Could go and buy nothing.

Healthy people did that.

I knew several.

Probably.

At home Cala read notice once.

"Same-day removal."

Of course she found storage line first.

"Yes."

"Chest is not bigger because you have ten copper."

"I know."

Lyssa was stitching sleeve guard at table.

She didn't look up.

"Do you?"

"Hostile household."

"Storage household."

Tavi asked, "Can I come?"

Three adults said no.

He looked betrayed by democracy.

I set notice beside plate.

"I'm only inspecting."

Lyssa looked up now.

"That's worse sentence than 'I'm only looking.'"

"Inspection is more disciplined."

"You bought lantern after looking."

"Shade unit."

"See?"

Cala said, "If you buy something large, it doesn't come upstairs until we know where it goes."

Fair.

"Agreed."

"If it leaks?"

"I don't buy leaking."

"If seller says it doesn't leak?"

"I still inspect for leaking."

"If it starts leaking later?"

"Why is everything leaking?"

"Because you buy old magic."

Also fair.

I slept badly.

Not from worry.

Worse.

Anticipation.

First bell next morning I was at North Weigh Yard.

Healthy.

Normal.

North Weigh sat behind freight scales where stone lanes widened around roofed sheds.

Recovery sale occupied Shed Four.

A board at entrance repeated rules.

A clerk gave each bidder flat wooden paddle with painted number after name entry.

Mine was thirty-two.

No fee.

No bidder deposit.

"Payment due at lot close," clerk said. "Removal by sunset. Red-cord goods handled only by yard staff. Questions to floor clerks. Condition cards control."

"Can I withdraw bid before close?"

She looked at me like I had asked whether gravity negotiable.

"No."

Good rule to know.

Inside smelled like dust, iron, wet rope, old wood, lamp oil, and a little ozone.

There were maybe sixty lots.

Most were boring.

Beautifully boring.

Bent hooks.

Cracked wash basins.

Three bundles of mismatched shelf brackets.

A brass office bell with no striker.

Two hand lamps missing shutters.

A box of route tabs whose glow had faded to weak green.

A kitchen warming plate with one corner warped.

I walked everything once before touching anything.

First-life habit.

Auction rooms punished love at first sight.

Especially when love had condition card.

Lot twelve interested me.

RETIRED WALL INDEXER

MANUAL ROTATION FREE

MARKING CORE REMOVED

FRAME TRUE AT INTAKE

MOUNT PLATE INCLUDED

Six copper opening estimate written smaller below.

Without marking core it was furniture with ambition.

Passed.

Lot seventeen was red-cord.

A squat iron cylinder in wood cradle.

SERVICE PRESSURE RESERVOIR

RETIRED AFTER SEAL DRIFT

RESIDUAL CHARGE CLEARED AT INTAKE

DO NOT OPEN ON SALE FLOOR

Opening estimate four copper.

Absolutely not.

I loved it.

Absolutely not.

Growth.

Lot twenty-three was a set of three counterweight shoes.

Sella's voice entered brain.

Don't buy counterweight because it looks lonely.

I moved on out of spite.

Then lot thirty-one stopped me.

Small.

Good start.

Wooden case about length of forearm, shallow, with brass corners and two leather loops on back.

Inside, under lifted lid, sat six narrow ceramic slats arranged like ribs.

Not instrument.

Each slat had a blackened metal tongue at one end and faded number scratched near other.

Condition card:

LOT 31

RETIRED DRAFT-FINDER ARRAY

FORMER USE: WEST ARCHIVE STACKS

SIX OF EIGHT VANES PRESENT

TWO MISSING

CASE HINGE REPAIRED

PASSIVE RESPONSE OBSERVED AT INTAKE

CALIBRATION UNKNOWN

MOUNT STRAPS HARDENED

NO POWERED TESTING

No price estimate.

Interesting.

Draft finder.

Passive response.

Archive stacks.

Missing two of eight.

Not magic treasure.

Probably old environmental tool.

I looked for floor clerk.

Woman in orange sash approached.

"Question?"

"What does passive response observed mean on this card?"

"Intake clerk saw vane movement in airflow without activation source. That's all."

"Mana component?"

"Unknown. Former registry lists draft-finder array. Yard did not open tongues or test calibration."

"Why retired?"

She checked secondary tag hanging under card.

"Archive replacement cycle. Unit marked incomplete at removal. No fault code transferred."

"Can I lift case?"

"Yes. Don't remove vanes."

I lifted.

Maybe four pounds.

Fine.

Back loops could hang from wall or strap to frame.

I tilted slightly.

Ceramic slats barely moved.

No obvious loose rattle.

I set it down.

"Any compatible replacement vanes in sale?"

"Not listed as compatible."

Good answer.

"Thank you."

She moved on.

I stared.

What did I need draft finder for?

Nothing.

House had windows.

Theatre had smoke strips and professionals.

Travel maybe.

Bad answer.

Artifact desire liked to invent future selves.

I walked away.

Lot thirty-eight was better in obvious way.

A compact folding brazier with intact heat plate.

Condition card said serviceable manually, heat rune removed, ordinary charcoal use only.

Opening estimate two copper.

Useful.

Boring.

Could actually use.

We already had stove.

Passed.

Lot forty-four made me stop longer.

PAIR OF ROUTE ECHO PINS

RETIRED MUNICIPAL SURVEY

ONE RETURNS CLEAN TONE AT INTAKE

ONE RETURNS INTERMITTENT TONE

RANGE / PAIRING / CALIBRATION UNKNOWN

NO FIELD TEST

Two iron pegs with blue glass heads.

Potentially useful.

Potentially broken.

Potentially exactly kind of object that consumed three weeks and then taught me I owned two pegs.

I asked price expectation.

Floor clerk said no estimate.

I walked away.

That hurt.

Good.

At second bell auction started.

Not theatrical.

A yard caller stood on crate with clerk beside him.

Lots moved fast.

One through ten mostly sold for bits and low copper.

I did not bid.

Lot twelve wall indexer opened at four copper despite six estimate.

Two office furnishers fought to seven.

Visible salvage value.

Reasonable.

Lot seventeen pressure reservoir opened three.

A repair shop bidder took it at five.

Also reasonable.

They could service seals.

I could stare at seals.

Lot twenty-three counterweight shoes sold two copper to freight man.

I felt nothing.

Mostly.

Lot thirty-one arrived.

"Retired draft-finder array," caller said. "Six of eight vanes. Passive response observed. Calibration unknown. West Archive former service. Opening one copper."

One copper.

My paddle moved before brain approved.

"One, thirty-two."

Another paddle lifted.

"Two, fourteen."

Bidder fourteen was older woman with ink-stained fingers.

Maybe archivist.

Maybe collector.

Maybe murderer.

Irrelevant.

Two copper.

At two, object was still interesting.

At three?

I had ten.

Three left seven.

Vale twenty-three unchanged.

Could pay debt instead.

Could keep reserve.

Could buy actual food/tools later.

Draft finder did not solve current problem.

Paddle fourteen stayed up.

Caller looked around.

"Two copper. Any three?"

I raised.

"Three, thirty-two."

Woman fourteen looked at me.

Not hostile.

Assessing.

Raised.

"Four, fourteen."

Four.

This was where auctions became confession.

What did I believe object worth to me?

Not hidden market value.

Not theoretical magic.

To me.

Four copper was forty percent of liquid cash.

For incomplete unknown-calibration passive environmental instrument.

No.

I lowered paddle.

Caller waited.

"Four copper. Once. Twice. Sold fourteen."

Pain.

Clean pain.

Good pain.

Woman fourteen won draft finder.

I still had ten copper.

This was success.

I hated success.

Lot thirty-eight brazier sold three.

I did not bid.

Lot forty-four.

Echo pins.

Caller read card.

"Opening one copper."

No paddles.

Interesting.

"One copper?"

Still none.

My brain started inventing.

One clean tone.

One intermittent.

Municipal survey.

Could maybe establish direction or paired route response.

Could maybe be useless without missing receiver.

Card said pair.

Former pair, maybe.

Unknown pairing/calibration.

One copper was cheap because obvious condition bad and use uncertain.

This was exactly where information gap could exist.

Also exactly where garbage lived.

I raised paddle.

"One, thirty-two."

Caller looked.

"Two?"

A man near rear lifted paddle.

"Two, forty-one."

Of course.

He wore surveyer's gray vest.

Actual likely user.

Bad sign for bargain.

Good sign for object relevance.

I looked at pins.

Two copper.

Could justify.

Maybe.

I raised three.

"Three, thirty-two."

Survey man raised four immediately.

No hesitation.

There.

He knew something or had compatible equipment.

Either way market signal.

Four.

Would I go five?

Half cash.

For one intermittent retired pin pair with unknown range/calibration.

No.

Paddle down.

"Four, forty-one. Once. Twice. Sold."

Second loss.

Strangely easier.

Because bidder made sense.

Market wasn't asleep waiting for me.

Good.

Auction continued.

I almost left.

Then lot fifty-two appeared.

Not artifact.

At least not obviously.

A narrow leather carrying harness with two adjustable side loops and broad back strap.

Condition card:

PORTER'S SIDE HARNESS

USED

STITCHING SOUND

ONE BUCKLE REPLACED

NO LOAD RATING REPRESENTED

Opening one copper.

I stared.

This was mundane.

Potentially useful.

Side loops could carry long object against torso while leaving hands free.

Crutches occupied hands.

Shoulder bag already did some of this badly.

Could carry cloth.

Could carry narrow cases.

Could maybe carry Three Reeds if padded, though artifact storage rules screamed.

Could carry groceries.

Could carry nothing and become expensive belt.

One copper.

No one bid.

I raised paddle.

"One, thirty-two."

Silence.

"Any two?"

A porter near front inspected it from seat.

Did not bid.

That mattered.

Maybe because used.

Maybe because no rating.

Maybe because he had better.

Caller said, "One once. One twice. Sold thirty-two."

I had bought harness.

Not magic.

I felt betrayed.

Payment clerk took one copper.

Cash nine.

She gave receipt and lot release tag.

I carried harness to inspection bench before leaving.

Same orange-sash clerk watched while I checked stitching more closely.

No hidden cuts.

Replacement buckle mismatched brass.

Leather dry but not cracked through.

"Can I put it on empty?"

"Yes."

I did.

Awkward with crutches.

Clerk held one strap only after I asked.

Harness sat diagonally across back/side.

One loop landed too low near left hip.

Adjustable.

I shortened.

Better.

Right side could hold narrow bundle without interfering with right crutch if object stayed behind elbow line.

Left side more complicated because residual limb and crutch path.

Could still use one-sided.

No load rating.

Important.

Not for heavy objects until someone competent checked it.

I removed.

"Do you know maker?"

Clerk looked at worn stamp.

"Looks like Heddel porter goods. Old mark. Could be copy."

"Any shop still services?"

"Heddel shop closed years ago. Leatherworker can assess stitching. Buckle standard enough."

Good.

No magical secret.

Just tool.

I left with harness over shoulder and nine copper.

Outside, bidder fourteen was loading draft-finder case into small handcart.

I tried not to look.

Failed.

She noticed.

"You wanted it."

"At three."

She smiled.

"At four too."

"Apparently not."

"Good."

I looked at her.

She tapped case.

"I'm buying six vanes, not array."

There.

Information.

"Replacement stock?"

"Maybe. Same ceramic profile as old archive humidity shutters. If tongues are wrong, I bought six pretty strips."

"You knew profile before bidding?"

"I could see it."

Fair.

"West Archive?"

"My father serviced stacks twenty years."

More information.

Market rational.

She looked at harness.

"You bought strap."

"Apparently."

"Useful."

"Less exciting."

"Useful often is."

She pushed cart away.

No name.

No subplot.

Just person who beat me because she knew more.

Excellent.

Survey man with echo pins passed next.

He didn't stop.

One pin in hand already.

He flicked glass head with fingernail.

It gave faint clear tone.

Other answered from box at his hip.

Not the second sale pin.

Compatible equipment.

Of course.

Four copper made even more sense.

I laughed.

He glanced back.

Kept walking.

At home Cala saw harness.

Looked behind me.

"That's all?"

"You sound disappointed."

"I'm checking for cylinder."

"There was cylinder."

Her face changed.

"Greg."

"I did not buy cylinder."

"Good."

Lyssa was at table again.

She held out hand.

"Give me harness."

"Why?"

"Because I sew."

Good reason.

I handed it over.

She bent leather.

Checked stitching.

Pulled replacement buckle.

"Dry."

"Not cracked through."

"Yet."

"Clerk said leatherworker assessment before load."

She nodded.

"One copper?"

"One."

"That's fine."

I felt absurdly pleased.

"I lost two auctions first."

"You didn't lose."

"They bought objects I wanted."

"Did you bid more than you wanted to pay?"

"No."

"Then you didn't lose."

I hated when people used my own logic against me.

Tavi came in.

"What is it?"

"Harness."

"Magic?"

"No."

He lost interest instantly.

Children were honest markets.

Later I took harness to leatherworker Cala knew two streets west.

Not a named recurring character.

He charged no inspection fee because it took less than minute and I did not ask him to repair.

"Stitching's fine for light carry," he said. "Leather wants oil. Don't trust that replacement buckle over maybe ten, twelve pounds until pin changed. Side loops weren't for heavy freight anyway. Documents, tools, narrow parcels."

"Can you replace pin?"

"Yes. Not today."

"Cost?"

"Depends pin. Come back if you care."

I cared later.

Not today.

At home I oiled leather with Cala's permission and cloth she gave me.

No artifact test.

No hidden glow.

No ancient rune appearing under dirt.

Leather became slightly darker.

That was all.

I tried harness empty again.

Then with rolled blanket.

Light.

Bulky enough to test geometry.

Right-side carry worked better than shoulder-bag strap improvisation.

Weight sat lower and closer to torso.

Crutch path clear if loop shortened.

Left-side carry interfered with left crutch during longer swing.

So one-sided.

Useful constraint.

I wrote:

CASH: 9 C

VALE: 23 C

RECOVERY SALE

DRAFT FINDER: STOPPED AT 3 / SOLD 4

ECHO PINS: STOPPED AT 3 / SOLD 4

PORTER HARNESS: 1 C

LIGHT CARRY ONLY UNTIL BUCKLE PIN CHECKED

RIGHT SIDE BETTER

Then I stared at first two lines.

Stopped at three.

Twice.

That felt better than buying either.

Not because objects were bad.

Draft finder might be excellent for woman who knew its vanes.

Echo pins were clearly worth more to man with compatible equipment.

Value was not inside object alone.

It lived between object and owner.

Dangerous sentence.

Sounded like philosophy.

I crossed it out before writing it.

No need.

I had nine copper, one useful harness, and no mysterious pressure cylinder leaking in bedroom.

Bidder.

Apparently restraint counted too.
