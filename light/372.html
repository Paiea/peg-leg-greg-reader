TEXT READER · CHAPTER 372

THE GUIDE

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Naro arrived with a jar full of bad decisions.

The jar clicked.

I looked at it.

He held it farther from body.

Good instinct.

"What is that?"

"I don't know."

"Excellent start."

"Mevi says don't shake it."

"Better."

"I shook it before she said."

There it was.

I was sitting at kitchen table oiling porter harness because leather had started feeling dry at one edge.

No load.

No experiment.

Maintenance.

Lyssa occupied window with second-lot apron pieces arranged in stacks that looked organized to her and hostile to everyone else.

Cala was cutting onions.

Tavi was supposed to be shelling peas.

He had shelled six and arranged them by size.

Productivity had become taxonomy.

Naro stood in doorway holding jar wrapped in dishcloth.

Behind him Mevi looked tired enough to have raised him personally from infancy despite being thirteen.

"He found it under west drain," she said.

"I didn't find it under drain."

"You put hand under drain and pulled it out."

"That's finding near drain."

"Under."

"Near."

I held hand up.

"Jar."

Naro set it carefully on table.

Cala moved onions away.

Lyssa moved cloth farther away without looking up.

Household competence.

The jar was ordinary thick glass.

Palm high.

Wire clasp.

Cloudy lid seal.

Inside were seven things that looked like black seeds.

Maybe eight.

They moved when I wasn't looking directly at them.

No.

That sentence was stupid.

I looked directly.

Seven.

One rolled slowly against glass.

Clicked.

Then stopped.

"Did you put them in jar?"

Naro shook head.

"Found like that."

"Where exactly?"

"West drain."

Mevi looked at him.

"Under west drain."

"Fine. Under."

"Which west drain?"

"Behind dye lane."

That narrowed nothing.

Carrow had many dye lanes because apparently clothing required infrastructure.

Mevi said, "The one by old red wall where water goes into covered channel before Narrow Bell."

I knew it.

Not far from yesterday's wall walk route.

Different problem.

Good.

"Why bring it here?"

Naro said, "Because you have weird things."

Cala made tiny sound that might have been laugh.

I looked at her.

No support.

"I do not operate municipal lost-and-found for weird things."

"You knew moth lamp."

"Shade unit."

"Dark lamp."

We had lost that war.

Mevi said, "I told him take it to drain keeper. He said Greg first because if dangerous you'd know."

"That is not true."

Naro looked genuinely surprised.

Important educational opportunity.

"I know some dangerous things," I said. "I do not know every dangerous thing. Nobody does."

"Hessa?"

"Especially not Hessa."

Somewhere, wall cracked.

Lyssa said, "She heard that."

"Probably."

I looked at jar again.

Black seed things.

Dry despite having apparently lived beneath drain.

No glow.

No obvious mana response I could feel without drawing.

I was not drawing.

No reason.

First-life memory offered several families.

Drain mites egg cases.

Silt pearls.

Drowned cinder beetle pupae.

Certain root pods that crawled toward moisture.

None useful without local context.

Good.

I said, "We're taking it to drain keeper."

Naro brightened.

"Can I come?"

"You are the witness."

"What's witness?"

Yesterday followed me home.

"You found it. You show exact place."

"Guide."

"You are guide."

He liked that better.

Mevi said, "I am coming because his exact place changes when he talks."

Also good.

I stood.

Harness stayed table.

No need.

Shoulder bag stayed mostly normal.

Oval was in it.

I considered removing oval because weird jar plus unexplained retreat object created unnecessary temptation.

Then decided no.

Oval was regular carry now.

Not using it was different from pretending I never carried it.

"Cala, jar stays closed?"

She looked at me like question insulted house.

"Yes."

"Good."

Naro reached.

I reached first.

"I'll carry."

"I carried here."

"And shook it."

"Before Mevi said."

"History remains."

I wrapped jar tighter in dishcloth and put it in shallow market basket Cala handed me.

Not bag.

If glass cracked, I wanted problem visible and away from papers.

Lyssa said, "If it hatches in house while you're gone, I'm charging Naro rent."

Naro looked at jar.

"Can they hatch?"

"She doesn't know."

Lyssa said, "Correct."

"Then why say?"

"Because now you'll stop shaking it forever."

Mevi nodded slowly.

"Useful."

Lyssa had found pedagogy.

We left.

Naro immediately walked ahead.

Mevi pulled him back by shirt.

"Guide means show, not vanish."

"I know way."

"Greg doesn't."

"He lives here."

"He doesn't live in drain."

I let them continue.

Street was warm after morning rain.

Gutters still carried thin water.

Yesterday's Pell mural remained at Fish Row when we crossed far end.

People had added more chalk.

I did not stop.

Good.

Different day.

At old red wall, Naro pointed toward iron grate at curb.

"There."

Mevi said, "No."

"Yes."

"You found it farther down."

"Same drain."

"Exact place."

Naro sighed like burdened surveyor.

We continued thirty paces.

Covered channel ran beneath lane.

Stone lip widened where maintenance hatch sat between wall and narrow public wash trough.

One rusted iron bar crossed opening.

Mud collected below.

Naro pointed.

"There."

Mevi said nothing.

Confirmation.

I crouched as much as left leg/crutches allowed.

Did not put hand under anything.

The drain mouth had fresh black smears along inner stone.

Not tar.

Maybe plant residue.

Three tiny black shapes clung above waterline.

Same size as jar contents.

One moved.

Definitely moved.

"Do not touch," I said.

Naro said, "I know now."

Growth.

A woman washing bucket at trough looked over.

"Those again?"

There.

Local knowledge.

I turned.

"You know them?"

"No."

Almost.

"But again?"

"Saw black little things yesterday. Drain crew scraped them."

"Which crew?"

She pointed downhill.

"Blue hooks."

Useful.

Carrow municipal uniforms continued being color plus object.

"Where do blue hooks stage?"

"Lower sluice by bell bridge."

"Thank you."

Naro whispered, "I knew that."

Mevi said, "No you didn't."

"I knew bridge."

"Everyone knows bridge."

We walked downhill.

Jar clicked once in basket.

I stopped.

Naro stopped too.

"Did you shake?" he asked.

"No."

"Then they shake themselves."

Possible.

Not conclusion.

I resumed.

Lower sluice sat where covered neighborhood drains opened into wider stone run before riverward channel.

Two workers in blue canvas vests had long iron hooks, exactly as advertised.

One stood knee-deep on dry side shelf scraping root mat from grate.

Other sat on wall eating something wrapped in leaf.

I approached seated one.

"You drain keeper?"

She swallowed.

"No."

Of course.

"Drain crew?"

"Yes."

"We found these under west drain near old red wall. Someone there said your crew scraped similar yesterday."

I set basket on wall, unwrapped enough glass to show contents without opening.

Her expression changed immediately.

Good.

Recognition.

"Don't open that."

"Wasn't planning to."

"Where exactly?"

Naro stepped forward.

Guide time.

"Under maintenance mouth by red wall after wash trough, not first grate, second opening where one iron bar is rusty and mud sits under left side."

Mevi looked at him.

He looked pleased.

Accurate when role mattered.

Worker stood.

"How many?"

"Seven," I said.

Naro said, "Eight before."

I looked at him.

"What?"

"I think eight."

Mevi closed eyes.

"You said seven."

"I said maybe."

"You did not."

I looked at jar.

Seven visible.

Could one be behind another.

Could count have been wrong.

Could one have changed.

No useful drama yet.

"Current visible seven," I said. "Original count uncertain."

Worker nodded.

Professional woman after my own heart.

"Did he open jar?"

"No," Mevi said.

"Shake it?"

Naro looked at ground.

"Once."

"How hard?"

"Not hard."

Mevi said, "Hard enough to hear."

Worker looked at Naro.

"Any dust come off?"

"No."

"Any smell?"

"Drain."

"From jar."

"I didn't smell jar."

"Good."

She called to partner.

"Sella!"

I knew that name.

The woman on dry shelf turned.

Of course.

Hanging Road Sella.

Operational lead who had once told people exactly where to put feet while road tried to become architecture.

She climbed out using side steps, saw me, then jar.

"Greg."

"Sella."

"Why do you have sluice seeds?"

There.

Name.

"Naro found them."

Sella looked at Naro.

"Where?"

He repeated exact location, slightly faster.

She listened.

"Jar already there?"

"Yes."

"Closed?"

"Yes."

"You open?"

"No."

"Shake?"

He hesitated.

"Once."

"Don't."

"I know now."

Sella accepted that.

She took jar only after putting on thick gray work gloves.

Not magical-looking.

Just gloves.

Held it toward light.

"Seven."

Other worker said, "He thinks eight before."

Sella looked at Naro.

"Did you count eight or think eight?"

Naro frowned.

Long pause.

"I saw... a lot. Then counted after Mevi yelled. Seven. Maybe I thought eight because one moved."

Good.

Sella nodded.

"Seven established."

No missing monster.

Thank you.

"What are sluice seeds?" Mevi asked.

Sella turned jar.

"Not seeds."

Naturally.

"Then why call them seeds?" Naro asked.

"Because they look like seeds."

"That's bad naming."

Sella looked at me.

"He's yours?"

"No."

Mevi said, "He's nobody's right now."

Naro objected.

"Mama's."

"Mama is at work."

"Still hers."

Sella handed jar to partner, who placed it inside lidded metal bucket.

"They're crawler cases," Sella said. "Small channel cleaners. City releases them in upper runs when silt mats get bad. They follow wet mineral line and eat soft buildup."

That sounded useful.

"Why in jar?"

"Because these were pulled."

"Why?"

"Wrong run maybe. Or spent. Or somebody collected them."

"Dangerous?"

"Not like that."

I waited.

Sella noticed.

"Don't eat them. Don't crush them in hand. Don't put live cases in household drain. They can chew soft lime and old seal paste if trapped where they shouldn't be."

Naro looked fascinated.

Mevi looked horrified.

"He was going to keep them."

"I was not."

"You said maybe."

"Maybe isn't going to."

Sella said, "Don't keep them."

"Fine."

Good.

I looked toward upper channel.

"You scraped similar yesterday?"

Other worker nodded.

"Nine cases. Different mouth."

Sella's face changed slightly.

Not alarm.

Work.

"Which mouth?"

"Red wall first grate."

Naro said, "See, same drain."

Mevi said, "Not exact place."

Sella ignored them.

"Any release scheduled upper west?"

Worker shook head.

"Not this week."

There.

Actual question.

Sella looked at jar again.

"These markings are old batch."

I saw no markings.

Of course.

"Where?"

She pointed through glass.

One black case had tiny pale notch near tip.

Then another.

"Cut mark?"

"Kiln stamp. Old north sluice batch."

"How old?"

"Could be months."

"Could they survive?"

"Cases can. Active crawler depends."

"You said live cases can chew seal paste."

"If active."

Good distinction.

"Are these active?"

"One moved."

Naro said, "They all move."

Sella looked at him.

"You saw all seven?"

"No."

"Then one moved."

Naro considered this injustice.

I liked Sella.

She turned to partner.

"Take bucket to lower office. Mark found west upper, second mouth and first yesterday. Ask Dren if old north batch was recovered after spring flush."

Partner nodded.

No conspiracy.

No city panic.

Administrative memory.

Exactly right.

Sella looked at us.

"Show me mouth."

Naro brightened again.

"I'm guide."

"Good. Guide."

He almost ran.

Mevi caught shirt.

Sella said, "Walking guide."

Naro walked.

We went back uphill with Sella carrying hook and a small chalk board clipped to belt.

I could have left.

Jar delivered.

Problem transferred to correct specialist.

But Naro and Mevi were still involved, and Sella had asked show mouth.

I stayed.

Not because expert.

Because adult-shaped enough for current route.

At red wall, Sella crouched beside maintenance opening.

No gloves needed because she did not touch cases.

Three black shapes still clung above waterline.

She counted.

"Three."

Then scraped tiny pale residue from stone with hook tip.

Smelled it.

I did not copy.

"Old mineral feed," she said.

"Meaning?"

"This run used to receive kiln rinse before reroute. Crawlers follow mineral line."

"So old batch could have stayed dormant somewhere and followed wet line after rain?"

Sella looked at me.

"Could."

Not yes.

Good.

"Or someone moved them."

"Could."

"Or release record wrong."

"Could."

"You hate conclusions too."

"Conclusions make paperwork."

Different motive than Hessa.

Same result.

She marked board:

WEST UPPER / OLD RED

3 VISIBLE LIVE-RESPONSE CASES

OLD NORTH MARK POSSIBLE

CHECK SPRING RECOVERY / KILN-RINSE LEGACY LINE

Then she looked at Naro.

"Where was jar?"

He pointed under lip.

"There. Behind mud ridge."

"Show without hand."

He used stick.

Good.

Sella scraped mud gently with hook.

Glass ring appeared.

Not another jar.

A broken jar base.

Same thick glass maybe.

Could be unrelated.

Sella exposed only enough to see.

"Do not touch," she said.

Naro put hands behind back dramatically.

Mevi said, "You don't have to perform."

"I'm showing."

Sella studied glass.

"Could be container broke and somebody put survivors in second jar."

"Who?" Naro asked.

"Don't know."

"Why?"

"Don't know."

"When?"

"Don't know."

"Are you drain keeper?"

"No."

I laughed.

Sella looked at me.

"Helpful."

"Sorry."

Not very.

She used hook to lift broken glass into small scrap pan from belt pouch.

Then chalked edge of mouth with blue X.

"Crew will screen this section before late water release."

"Should people avoid drain?" Mevi asked.

"People should always avoid putting hands under drains."

She looked at Naro.

"New rule."

Naro said, "What if coin?"

"Get adult."

"What if adult can't reach?"

"Get hook."

"What if no hook?"

"Lose coin."

He looked appalled.

"Depends how much."

Sella almost smiled.

"No."

"Copper?"

"No."

"Silver?"

"Find drain crew."

"So depends."

Sella stared at him.

Mevi covered face.

I said, "He got you."

"No," Sella said.

"Technically."

"Greg."

Apparently my name had become disciplinary tool across city.

We stepped away while she checked first grate.

Naro watched closely.

Not touching.

Good.

Mevi sat on wash-trough edge.

"Mama is going to kill him."

"Probably not."

"She said if he put hand in drain again she'd nail his sleeves to table."

"Probably also not."

"She might."

Naro said, "Loose nails."

Mevi pointed at him.

"See?"

I did.

Sella returned.

"Two more at first grate."

Five visible across two mouths today.

Plus seven jar.

Nine scraped yesterday.

Could be old batch surfacing after rain.

Could be local line residue.

Could be human movement.

Sella did not decide.

Neither did I.

"Anything we need do?" I asked.

"You? No."

Wonderful words.

"Children? Stay out of drains."

"Already policy," Mevi said.

"Enforcement issue," I said.

Naro glared.

Sella unclipped small square blue card from belt pouch and handed it to Mevi.

"If you see more above grate, tell blue-hook crew. Don't collect. Card shows lower office mark."

Mevi took it.

Not me.

Good.

"Thank you."

Naro said, "Can I have card?"

"No," Mevi and Sella said together.

Naro looked at me.

"No."

Betrayal complete.

Sella left to mark next channel.

We walked home.

Naro was quieter for nearly one block.

Then:

"Crawler cases are kind of animals?"

"Ask Sella next time."

"You don't know?"

"No."

"But you know magic."

"Some."

"And monsters."

"Some."

"And weird things."

"Some."

He frowned.

"You know less than I thought."

There.

Progress.

"Excellent."

"That's not excellent."

"It is for you."

Mevi laughed.

Naro did not.

At our door he stopped.

"I did good showing place."

"Yes."

No joke.

He had.

"And telling shake?"

"Yes."

"Even though bad?"

"Especially because bad."

He nodded.

That mattered more.

Mevi said, "I'll tell Mama he told truth."

Naro looked relieved.

Then suspicious.

"Tell her after I explain."

"No."

"Mevi."

"No."

They went two doors east arguing about order of confession.

I went inside.

Lyssa had moved from three finished wrap loops to eight.

Stack complete.

"Done?" I asked.

"With rework. Not returned."

Important.

"How was jar?"

"Sluice crawler cases."

She stopped.

"In house?"

"Closed jar."

"Greg."

"For less than ten minutes."

"Greg."

"Cala was here."

Cala said, "Do not involve me."

Abandoned.

I explained.

Old batch marks.

Drain crew had seen others yesterday.

Five more visible today.

Possible old kiln-rinse mineral line.

Possible dormant old batch.

Possible moved cases.

No conclusion.

Blue-hook crew screening section before late water release.

Lyssa listened.

Then said, "So Naro put hand under drain."

"Yes."

She returned to folding wraps.

"That's the important part."

Household risk assessment.

Tavi entered.

"What jar?"

No.

"Nothing."

"You said jar."

"I did not."

Lyssa said, "He did."

Traitor.

Tavi looked at me.

"What jar?"

"Drain creatures."

His eyes widened.

"Where?"

"Gone."

"Can we get some?"

"No."

"Why?"

"They eat drains."

"Cool."

"Wrong lesson."

"Can they eat spoon?"

Everything returned to spoon eventually.

"I don't know."

"We could test."

"No."

"Science."

"Still no."

He left disappointed in institutional research.

I sat at table.

Porter harness still lay where I had left it.

Oil had soaked into dry edge.

I wiped excess.

No heroic use.

No artifact.

Just maintenance after morning got interrupted by jar.

I thought about Naro saying I knew less than he thought.

Good.

People had started bringing me strange things.

That was dangerous in different way than strange things themselves.

Reputation could become claim before skill did.

I did know more than Naro about some things.

Enough to keep jar closed.

Enough to ask exact place.

Enough to recognize when local specialist mattered more than old-life category memory.

Not enough to identify sluice cases by sight.

That was fine.

Guide did not always mean person in front.

Sometimes it meant knowing which door to walk someone toward.

I looked at notebook upstairs in my mind.

No need to write crawler theory.

Not mine.

But one line maybe.

Later I did.

Not about crawlers.

I wrote:

IF PEOPLE BRING UNKNOWN OBJECTS: CLOSED / PLACE / WHO KNOWS LOCALLY

Then stared.

Too much like system.

Useful system, though.

I added:

DO NOT BECOME WEIRD-THING OFFICE

Better.

Nine copper.

Twenty-three Vale.

Magic unchanged.

No new object.

No secret jar.

No missing eighth crawler waiting in my bag.

Thank gods.

Guide.

Mostly toward someone else.
