TEXT READER · CHAPTER 374

THE HOST

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The first problem with inviting people over was that we already lived there.

This meant house looked like house.

Tavi's shoes under chair.

Lyssa's second-lot cloth occupying half kitchen table because upstairs floor had declared independence.

My crutches against wall.

Cala's onions hanging by stove.

One temporary board upstairs covering wardrobe hole.

Normal.

Apparently unacceptable once guests existed.

"Move that," Lyssa said.

I looked at my crutches.

"I need those."

"Not those."

"Helpful."

She pointed at stack of paper beside my elbow.

"Your notes."

"Those are organized."

"They are three piles."

"Exactly."

"What are piles?"

I looked.

"Known. Unknown. Things I am pretending I'll return to."

"Third pile upstairs."

"Floor problem."

"Greg."

I moved papers.

We were having dinner.

With people.

Not accidental people who wandered through doorway carrying jars or rope.

Invited people.

This had been Lyssa's idea three days ago before wardrobe attacked.

Then wardrobe attacked.

Then she said dinner should still happen because if we canceled every time house became inconvenient, Cala would never let anyone inside again.

Cala said this was slander.

Then moved two cracked bowls out of sight.

Guests were Senna and Merren.

An odd pair only if you assumed people arrived in matched sets.

Senna had been nearby for South Cloth committee sketch work.

Merren had finished lower counter shift early and apparently knew Senna through a cousin's stall awning that Senna once painted badly on purpose.

I had questions.

Nobody answered them.

Lyssa invited both because she wanted "people who aren't work people."

Merren was absolutely work person.

Lyssa said not ours.

Technicality.

I had contributed bread.

Bought earlier with household food money Cala handed me, not Greg ledger.

Important because nine copper remained nine copper and I refused to accidentally become accountant for dinner.

I also contributed seating geometry.

Wardrobe repair had displaced one upstairs chair downstairs.

We now had five chairs and one stool.

Six people if Tavi counted.

He did.

Aggressively.

"I'm not stool," he said.

"Nobody said you were."

"You looked at me then stool."

"I was counting."

"Count yourself stool."

Fair.

I took stool.

Left leg appreciated less than philosophy did.

We adjusted with folded cushion.

Host sacrifice.

Senna arrived first carrying nothing.

I respected this immediately.

"I was told not to bring food," she said.

"Correct."

"So I didn't."

"Excellent guest."

She walked in, saw cloth stacks, temporary relocated work table, Tavi's shoes, my stool, and said, "This is better."

Lyssa narrowed eyes.

"Better than what?"

"Clean houses. They look frightened."

Then Senna stared at west wall for long enough to become suspicious.

"No."

She looked at me.

"I didn't say anything."

"You're composing."

"Wall needs something."

"Wall has wall."

"That's why."

I pointed kitchenward.

"Sit."

She did not.

She wandered.

Senna's speech got shorter when looking, longer when explaining why everyone else had bad eyes.

Useful distinction.

Merren arrived with a jar of pickled radish.

Naro had damaged jar reputation.

I looked at it.

Merren noticed.

"Food."

"Thank you."

"Normal food."

"Better."

"Should I be worried?"

"No."

Lyssa from kitchen said, "Yes."

Merren took shoes off by door without asking.

Tavi inspected radish jar.

"Does it move?"

"If shaken."

"Don't," I said.

Merren looked between us.

"I missed something."

"Drain creatures," Tavi said.

"Dinner," Cala said.

Strong redirect.

We sat.

Cala had made rice, fried greens, fish with ginger, and thin broth.

Not feast.

Good dinner.

Lyssa put her second-lot cloth into two baskets and slid them under side table before food came out.

She kept work card on shelf.

I noticed because Ch373 had trained me to fear furniture.

No one needed know that.

Senna sat opposite me.

Merren beside Cala.

Lyssa beside Tavi because Tavi beside Senna would result in interrogation.

This lasted forty seconds.

"Why did you draw Greg?" Tavi asked.

Senna ate fish.

"He sat still."

"He doesn't."

"He did once."

"Why?"

"I paid him nothing."

"He said you wanted one copper."

I looked at Tavi.

"Why do you know that?"

"You said."

Probably.

Household privacy remained theoretical.

Senna said, "I wanted one copper for portrait. He didn't buy it. Then I sold studies."

"Did Greg get money?"

"No."

Tavi looked at me, scandalized.

"Your face made money and you didn't get any?"

"That's how art works."

Senna pointed chopsticks at me.

"No."

"Sometimes."

"Also no."

Merren said, "At counter if your face makes money, usually somebody filled wrong form."

Cala laughed.

Good.

Merren had not been invited to perform procedure, but procedure escaped anyway.

Lyssa said, "No work talk for first bowl."

"That was face talk," Merren said.

"You said counter."

"Context."

"Work context."

Merren considered.

"Fine."

She ate.

Senna said, "South Cloth committee hates my second layout."

Lyssa looked at her.

"That's work."

"Art."

"Paid?"

"Yes."

"Work."

Senna sighed.

"Your rules are oppressive."

"First bowl."

We made it six more minutes.

Then Tavi asked Merren whether carts could lie.

Nobody understood.

He explained.

"If counter says cart has six things but cart has seven, cart knows?"

Merren set spoon down.

"No."

"Then who lies?"

"Usually paper."

"Paper can't know."

"Correct."

"Then person."

"Sometimes person is wrong. Sometimes mark copied wrong. Sometimes one item added after count. Sometimes one removed. Sometimes two people use different lot boundary. Sometimes receiving count is wrong. Sometimes dispatch count is wrong. Sometimes nobody lied and numbers still disagree."

Tavi stared.

Merren had overexplained him into silence.

I loved it.

"Again," I said.

Merren looked at me.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Greg remained not only person allowed dry response.

That thought arrived with dangerous familiarity.

I killed it before it became manuscript note in my head.

Normal people could be funny.

Revolutionary.

Lyssa pointed at my bowl.

"Eat."

I ate.

Second bowl released work embargo.

Immediately everyone refused to talk work.

Of course.

Senna asked Cala how long she had lived in house.

"Twenty-two years."

"Same furniture?"

Cala looked toward ceiling.

"Some less successfully than others."

Wardrobe had reputation now.

Senna asked about wall repairs, old window leak, previous tenant who painted kitchen shelf blue then apparently changed mind halfway.

Cala told story.

Longer than usual.

Not because Cala became talkative.

Because house was hers.

Previous tenant was her aunt's friend Neli, who believed blue paint kept flies away because somebody in River Quarter told her. She painted one shelf, ran out of blue, used green, then decided flies were colorblind.

"Were they?" Tavi asked.

"They remained flies."

"That's not answer."

"It's all I have."

Senna got up mid-story to inspect shelf.

There was blue under chipped green.

She looked delighted.

"Don't paint it," Cala said.

"I wasn't."

"You looked."

Apparently face-reading contagious.

Merren asked why I had one chair upstairs if floor bad.

"Chair is on sound side."

"You checked?"

"Carpenter did."

"Good."

"No work talk."

"This is house safety."

Lyssa said, "You're worse than me."

"I count things."

"We know."

Dinner moved.

Not plot.

People.

Senna eventually asked Lyssa about coat.

Charcoal coat hung by door now because wardrobe exile.

"You bought it," she said.

Lyssa touched collar from chair.

"Yes."

"Nine?"

"Nine."

I looked at her.

Not because price new.

Because exact purchase had happened off my page somehow.

No.

Life happened when I wasn't present.

Healthy.

"Worth it?" Senna asked.

Lyssa thought.

"Ask after winter."

"You like it."

"I like looking at it."

"That's worth something."

"Not nine copper by itself."

Senna shrugged.

"Depends face."

"Coat doesn't have face."

"Everything has face."

Merren said, "Carts don't."

Tavi immediately said, "They could."

We lost ten minutes to whether carts had faces.

I voted yes if front lamps counted as eyes.

Lyssa said that was stupid.

Senna said wheels were ears.

Tavi said wheels obviously feet.

Merren tried to establish definitions.

Cala left table to get more broth.

When she returned, debate had reached axles.

She stood holding pot.

"I regret guests."

Good dinner.

After food, Tavi wanted game.

I did not know game.

Apparently everyone else did.

"Three Claims," Lyssa said.

"No."

"Why?"

"I don't know it."

"That's why."

Suspicious.

Rules simple.

Each person says three claims about self.

Two true, one false.

Others guess false.

Terrible game.

People could simply lie.

That was mechanism.

I hated it.

Tavi went first.

"I fell in river. I ate six eggs once. I saw ghost."

"Ghost false," I said.

"No."

"River false," Cala said.

"Yes."

I looked at him.

"You saw ghost?"

"Maybe."

"Then claim not established."

Everyone booed me.

Actual booing.

Hostile house.

"Game accepts maybe," Lyssa said.

"Then game has no standards."

"Your turn."

No.

Senna went.

"I broke my left wrist falling off roof. I once painted magistrate's dog blue. I hate pears."

Merren said, "Dog."

Cala said, "Pears."

Lyssa said, "Roof."

I looked at Senna's left wrist.

No obvious deformity.

Meaning nothing.

"Pears."

Senna smiled.

"Dog."

Tavi shouted.

"You didn't paint dog?"

"I painted magistrate's goat blue."

"That's cheating," I said.

"No."

"Near-truth."

"Still false."

Game was corrupt.

Merren went.

She took too long.

"I have never slept outside city wall. I can whistle through two fingers. I was engaged for eleven days."

Silence.

Interesting.

Tavi said, "Whistle."

Merren whistled so loudly Cala nearly dropped cup.

Tavi screamed with joy.

Senna said, "Engaged."

Merren shook head.

Lyssa said, "Outside wall."

"False. I slept outside twice."

I looked at her.

"Eleven days?"

She looked back.

"Eleven."

"Why?"

"Because on day twelve I wasn't."

Dry.

Earned.

No explanation.

Good.

Tavi demanded story.

Merren said no.

He demanded again.

Still no.

Boundary survived.

Cala's turn:

"I have punched a priest. I have never been farther than Bell Ford. I can make six kinds of dumpling."

I knew dumplings likely true.

Bell Ford uncertain.

Priest extremely plausible.

"Bell Ford false," I said.

Everyone else chose priest.

Cala looked at me.

"Why would you think I punched priest?"

"You asked question like answer was obvious."

She stared.

"Priest false."

Damn.

"Bell Ford true?"

"Never farther."

That landed differently.

Not sad.

Just fact.

Twenty-two years in house.

Never farther than Bell Ford.

First life I had crossed kingdoms until maps became suggestions.

Second life I had already traveled more than Cala in some directions and less in every way that mattered.

I did not say.

Lyssa's turn.

She looked at me before speaking.

Bad.

"I once cut my own hair with kitchen knife. I hate dancing. I have slept on roof."

"Dancing false," I said immediately.

She smiled.

"Yes."

Too easy.

"Kitchen knife?" Senna asked.

"Fourteen."

"Why?"

"Because my mother said no."

"How bad?"

Lyssa held hand near ear.

"Here."

Senna winced.

"Roof?"

"Summer. Too hot inside."

I pictured it.

Lyssa fourteen with terrible hair.

Then roof.

Different ages maybe.

Not mine.

Still liked images.

My turn.

Everyone looked at me.

I had problem.

First-life truths were unavailable.

Not because impossible to say.

Because saying them as game would be insane.

I had killed things larger than houses.

I had crossed frozen sea on foot.

I had slept inside dead titan rib cage during storm.

Two truths and one lie would destroy dinner.

Second-life truths were narrower.

Good.

"I hate olives. I have never broken a crutch. I can sew a button."

Lyssa laughed before anyone guessed.

"Crutch."

"Why?"

"You broke one before I met you."

"How do you know?"

"Cala."

I looked at Cala.

"Household privacy."

"The whole street heard."

Fair.

Tavi said, "Olives false."

"No. I hate olives."

Senna said, "Button false."

"I can sew button."

Lyssa looked offended.

"Show me later."

"No."

"Then unverified."

Hessa infected everyone too.

Game continued.

Then Senna changed it.

"One claim. Something nobody here knows."

Merren said, "That's not game."

"Better."

"How guess?"

"You don't."

"Then what is mechanism?"

"Knowing person."

Merren looked physically pained.

I understood.

Lyssa said, "Fine."

Senna first.

"I can't draw horses from memory."

I stared.

"You've drawn people moving."

"People are easier. Horses have too many wrong places."

Tavi said, "Four legs."

"Exactly."

Merren said, "I sing when I'm alone."

No one laughed.

She looked relieved.

Cala said, "I wanted to leave Carrow once."

Quiet.

Not dramatic.

Senna asked, "Why didn't you?"

Cala shook head.

"One claim."

Boundary.

Accepted.

Lyssa said, "I keep first bad thing I make when learning something."

I looked toward cloth baskets.

"You still have first apron?"

"Not this contract. First things."

"Where?"

"One claim."

Fine.

Tavi said, "I know where."

Lyssa looked at him.

"No you don't."

"Maybe."

"You don't."

He smiled.

My turn again.

Something nobody here knew.

Dangerous category.

Could choose trivial.

I had once eaten whole lemon peel.

Probably.

Could choose current-life private.

I sometimes woke reaching for left foot.

Too intimate for game.

I sometimes forgot age when tired because first life sat behind eyes like second calendar.

Absolutely not.

I looked around table.

Senna waiting without impatience.

Merren trying not to look like she was counting pause.

Cala quiet.

Lyssa watching me more carefully than game deserved.

Tavi bouncing knee.

I said, "I like being invited places more than I like going."

Lyssa smiled first.

Not teasing.

"That makes sense."

Senna said, "That's terrible."

"Why?"

"Because then host has to invite you and also hope you don't come."

"Efficient."

Merren said, "No."

Tavi said, "I like going."

"We know."

"Nobody asked mine."

"Go."

He thought hard.

"Sometimes I pretend asleep so Cala carries me."

Cala stared.

Tavi realized error.

Too late.

"You what?"

"Game over."

He ran.

Cala stood.

"Tavi."

"Host protection," he shouted from stairs.

"There is no host protection."

"Greg!"

I looked at Cala.

"Apparently there is."

She pointed spoon at me.

"You carry him next time."

"Impossible."

"Then no protection."

Fair.

Dessert was sliced pears.

Senna ate three.

Confirmed.

Merren helped Cala wash bowls despite being told guest.

Lyssa and Senna looked at coat again.

Tavi returned once legal danger passed.

I sat on stool with cushion shifted wrong and did nothing useful.

This felt suspiciously good.

Then lights went strange.

Not out.

Strange.

Oil lamp flame remained normal.

But west side of room brightened in thin pale bands across wall.

Senna stopped talking.

Merren turned.

Cala looked toward window.

Outside, evening had darkened.

Bands moved slowly upward.

Like reflected water.

Except no water high enough.

Tavi said, "Pretty."

Correct.

Lyssa said, "Greg."

Less correct tone.

I stood.

No mana.

No artifact yet.

At window, pale bands came from street side, sweeping across upper walls of houses.

People outside were looking up.

No shouting.

No bells.

No red boards.

Not emergency yet.

Then I saw source.

Across lane, old glass sign above closed spice shop had begun throwing light.

Not generating.

Catching something.

A faint blue-white shimmer moved along clouds, and sign glass split it into narrow moving bars.

Senna came beside me.

"Sky ribbon."

"You know it?"

"Seen once."

"Dangerous?"

"I was eight. Everything dangerous."

Useful.

Merren called from table, "If it's sky ribbon, west lanes sometimes cover mirror signs."

I looked at her.

"Why?"

"Glare. Horses. Cart drivers."

There.

Not cosmic event.

Traffic problem.

Carrow.

Outside someone shouted, "Cover glass!"

Then another voice farther down.

"West signs!"

People moved.

Cala already had old sheet.

I looked at Three Reeds chest location upstairs.

No.

Then paused.

Known artifact.

Known real capability: directional reduction of stray light beyond faced direction while preserving main lit region, at least in controlled lamp and ordinary oil-lamp contexts. Rear plate warms. Safe duration unknown.

This was not lamp.

Different source.

Not rated.

Do not use as experiment.

But actual need existed outside.

Real need does not establish safe use.

Yesterday's lesson.

Damn.

Mundane sheet existed.

We used sheet.

Cala and I went outside.

Senna followed because of course.

Merren followed because everyone did.

Lyssa stayed with Tavi and house.

Across lane spice-shop sign sat above reach.

Owner absent.

Neighbor had ladder.

People were already covering reflective signs with cloth.

Not ours.

We helped hold ladder.

I did not climb.

Cala climbed two rungs, neighbor higher, sheet over glass.

Bands vanished from our wall.

Down lane another sign flashed harder.

A horse shied.

Handler controlled it.

No injury.

Merren pointed.

"That one."

We moved.

For twenty minutes dinner became neighborhood sign-covering.

Not heroic.

Useful.

Senna knew which painted glass would split light worst by looking at angle and backing.

Merren knew two shop owners and where spare awning cloth was stored.

Cala knew whose ladder could be borrowed.

I held bases, carried folded cloth on right shoulder, and kept Tavi from joining because he escaped house once.

Lyssa retrieved him by collar.

"Host protection," he told her.

"No."

Good.

Sky ribbon itself strengthened briefly.

Pale blue-white arc visible between roofs.

Beautiful.

People stopped for maybe thirty seconds.

Even workers covering signs.

Then somebody said, "Still have three west windows."

Work resumed.

I loved city slightly for that.

By time ribbon faded, lane looked ridiculous.

Sheets tied over signs.

Awning scraps over upper windows.

One shop used trousers.

Nobody admitted whose.

We returned home.

Food cold.

Dessert mostly gone because Tavi.

"I saved two pears," he said.

There were six people.

"Generous."

We reheated broth.

Senna looked energized.

"I want paint that."

"Sky?"

"No. Trousers."

Of course.

Merren said, "Please don't paint identifying trousers."

"Why?"

"Because someone will know."

"That's point."

"That's exactly problem."

Cala sat down and laughed harder than line deserved.

Maybe tired.

Maybe dinner.

Maybe trousers.

Mood mattered.

Lyssa leaned against my shoulder briefly while standing behind stool.

"Good dinner," she said.

"We left halfway."

"Still."

"House almost killed contract yesterday. Sky attacked signs today."

"Tomorrow nothing."

"Don't say that."

She knocked wood.

Senna heard.

"Tomorrow I'm drawing Greg's wall."

"No."

"See? Something."

Merren left first because early shift.

Senna left later because she forgot time.

At door she said, "Invite me again."

There it was.

I liked being invited places more than going.

Apparently hosting had reverse problem.

People could ask to return.

"Maybe."

"That's yes."

"No."

"Lyssa?"

Lyssa said, "Yes."

Authority compromised.

Senna left satisfied.

After door closed, house became house again.

Bowls.

Cloth baskets.

Shoes.

Temporary table.

My crutches.

No guests.

Still felt different.

Not cleaner.

More used.

I opened notebook later.

Nine copper.

Twenty-three Vale.

Magic unchanged.

No artifact use.

I almost wrote sky ribbon.

Didn't.

Merren or city records knew sky ribbon better.

I almost wrote sign-covering procedure.

Didn't.

Not everything needed become mine.

Instead:

DINNER: SENNA / MERREN

Then after moment:

AGAIN: PROBABLY

I stared.

Host.

Apparently.
