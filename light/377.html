TEXT READER · CHAPTER 377

THE NEIGHBOR

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The floor stopped being theoretical at breakfast.

A board squealed upstairs.

Not squeaked.

Squealed.

Long, offended sound like pig discovering taxes.

Everyone at table stopped.

Tavi looked ceiling.

"Ghost."

"Carpenter," Cala said.

"Carpenter ghost."

"Eat."

Upstairs, somebody hit something twice.

Then carpenter shouted, "Cala?"

Cala closed eyes.

Not fear.

The expression of woman whose morning had just developed invoice.

I stood.

"I'll go."

"Why?"

"Tall."

"That has never stopped you from making problem worse."

Fair.

We both went.

The upstairs room still looked like house interrupted mid-thought.

Wardrobe against wrong wall.

Temporary boards over repaired section.

Bed shifted.

Chest with artifacts locked and farther from work path than before.

Carpenter Brenna knelt beside exposed strip where one temporary board had come up.

Not Brenna from any grand plot.

Brenna the carpenter.

Carrow contained names without destiny.

She pointed into floor cavity.

"Found wet."

Cala crouched.

I stayed standing because crouching with one leg and crutches was possible but not contribution.

Dark stain ran along side of joist.

Not flood.

Damp.

Fresh enough wood darker.

"Roof?" Cala asked.

"No."

Brenna tapped pipe running through cavity.

Thin ceramic line, wrapped at joint with old gray compound.

"Wash line. Slow seep at collar. Floor damage wasn't only age."

There.

New information.

Not magical.

Still expensive-shaped.

"Active?" I asked.

"Small. See bead?"

Water gathered at underside joint.

One drop.

Wait.

Another.

"How long?"

"Can't know. Enough staining that not this morning."

Cala said nothing.

Brenna continued.

"Need line shut before I close floor. Plumber can reseal collar if ceramic isn't cracked. If cracked, section replacement."

"Today?"

"If you want floor closed today, yes."

Cala looked toward stairs.

Household arithmetic moved behind face.

I knew that face now.

Not my ledger.

Still my house.

Difference mattered.

"Who do you use?" Cala asked.

"I can send for Deren. He's two streets over on South Bell job. Might come midday."

"Price?"

"Not mine to quote."

Good.

Brenna had learned boundaries before meeting me.

"Can floor stay open?" I asked.

"Yes, if nobody walks this strip. Temporary cover can go back, but I won't finish over active seep."

"Damage worse if wait?"

"Slow seep. Could be same for days, could worsen. Shut wash line when not needed until looked at."

Cala nodded.

"Send for him."

Brenna sent apprentice.

Breakfast became late.

Lyssa arrived while I was carrying cups downstairs.

She had charcoal coat over arm, hair pinned badly, and expression of person who had been awake longer than day deserved.

"Why is carpenter apprentice running?"

"Plumbing."

"Of course."

She put coat down.

"My thing went through."

I stopped.

"Which thing?"

"All of it."

That narrowed nothing.

She sat.

Cala handed tea without asking.

Lyssa drank half before speaking.

Good sign she needed it.

"Second lot accepted. Twelve wraps, sixteen aprons, sixteen sleeve pairs. One apron tie had twist. I fixed it there. Everything else accepted. Nine copper."

There.

Nine.

Her money.

Not mine.

"And loops?"

"Accepted too. One copper rework paid separate."

Ten copper total today.

I did not say total.

I was learning.

"Good," I said.

She stared.

"That's it?"

"Do you want math?"

"No."

"Compliment?"

"Maybe."

"You're good at this."

She drank tea.

"Better."

Cala said, "Eat."

Lyssa took bread.

Tavi said, "Greg spent six copper on half bridge."

Traitor.

Lyssa pointed bread at me.

"I know."

"It doesn't work."

"We don't know that."

"Same thing."

"Not same thing."

"If I have half bread and don't know if bread works, it's bad bread."

"Bread has lower functional uncertainty."

Cala said, "Stop teaching him that sentence."

I had not.

Which was worse.

Lyssa laughed into tea.

Then upstairs board squealed again.

She looked ceiling.

"What's plumbing?"

We explained.

Her face shifted from amusement to practical concern.

"Can we use wash basin?"

"Cala is shutting line between uses until plumber comes."

"Bath?"

"Different line," Cala said.

"Good."

Priorities.

Lyssa finished bread, then pulled folded paper from coat pocket.

"Marra offered third lot."

I looked at paper, not reached.

"Want me involved?"

She hesitated.

"Later. Not now. I want sleep before deciding anything."

"Good."

"Don't say good like Hessa."

"I don't."

"You do sometimes."

I hated accumulation when used against me.

She went upstairs, saw open floor, came back down, and chose sofa.

Reasonable.

By midday plumber arrived.

Deren was narrow man with enormous mustache and habit of explaining every step to Cala even when she had not asked.

Not condescending exactly.

Compulsive narration.

"See, ceramic line itself is old kiln work, good kiln work actually, they don't fire these collars this thick anymore because everyone wants lighter runs and then everyone complains when lighter runs chatter, which is not same as leaking, mind you, chatter is support problem usually, unless pressure pulses, but this isn't pressure line, so here what we've got is collar bedding giving up. Maybe seal paste. Could be hair crack under wrap. Won't know until dry."

Cala listened.

I loved him immediately from safe distance.

Different speech register.

Man could drown city in explanation.

He shut line at lower valve.

Opened joint wrap carefully.

Gray compound flaked.

No magic flash.

No artifact.

Wet ceramic.

Deren dried it.

Waited.

Inspected with little brass mirror.

"No crack I can see. That's good. Could still have one where collar seats, but I don't think so. Bedding's gone brittle. Old seal paste gets like biscuit. Not good biscuit. Biscuit left in cupboard behind flour where nobody remembers it until winter."

Brenna said, "Price."

"Yes, yes. Reseat and seal, two copper including compound. If collar breaks while I remove it because hidden crack, replacement section adds three. I stop before replacement and ask."

Cala nodded.

"Two."

I stayed silent.

House money.

Not mine.

Deren looked at me.

"You live here?"

"Yes."

"Then you should know lower shutoff sticks quarter turn before close. Don't force past resistance because old stem. Back eighth, then forward."

Useful.

I wrote that down on household scrap, not artifact notebook.

Deren noticed crutches.

"You need clear floor path while this open."

"Yes."

"Brenna, your cover has lip."

Brenna looked.

"Temporary."

"Temporary still catches."

She looked at my crutch tips.

Then at cover.

"Fair."

She beveled edge with plane.

Thirty seconds.

No committee.

No special speech.

Just neighbor-scale competence.

I liked Carrow.

Annoying realization.

Deren resealed collar.

Two copper from Cala.

No hidden crack.

He left line dry for one bell, then reopened slowly.

No bead.

Wait.

Still no bead.

Brenna refused to close floor immediately anyway.

"I want it under pressure until late afternoon."

Deren approved.

"If dry then, floor closes. If damp, send boy."

Cala asked whether two-copper repair had any guarantee.

Deren launched explanation involving workmanship, old ceramic, new seal compound, freeze risk that Carrow apparently did not have often enough to matter, and one customer who used pipe as shelf support.

Eventually answer: if his new seal fails within thirty days under ordinary household use, labor redo no charge; material fault outside joint separate.

Cala made him write it.

I definitely liked her.

Afternoon had no assignment.

No work.

No market.

No training.

Five copper remained five.

Step Keeper stayed locked.

This was apparently possible.

I sat outside because upstairs smelled of wet wood and seal compound.

Jessa passed with oil ledger under arm.

"Bell Ford."

I looked at her.

"What?"

"Sella said you went."

"News travels."

"You bought bridge junk."

"News travels too far."

She sat on low wall without invitation.

"What was Ford like?"

Question surprised me.

Not what bought.

Not road.

Place.

"Smaller. More trees. Blue tile on public roofs. Bridge has bells."

"Bells?"

"Lots. Wind and water."

"Annoying?"

"Actually no."

"Huh."

She looked down street.

"Never been."

"Really?"

"Why would I?"

"Because road continues."

"That's a terrible reason."

"Worked for me."

"You had free ride."

"Also true."

Jessa picked lint from sleeve.

"Olin went once. Hated smoked river eel. Talked about it for six years like town personally cooked wrong."

"Did he try other food?"

"No."

"Then his sample is weak."

"Tell him."

"Absolutely not."

She smiled.

Then said, "You going again?"

I had not thought beyond object.

"Maybe."

Maybe again.

Expensive word.

But travel version.

"Why?"

"I don't know."

Jessa nodded like that was better answer than expected.

"If you do, bring eel so I can tell Olin he's wrong."

"You can go."

"I work."

"So do I. Sometimes."

"Your employment model is unsettling."

Fair.

She left.

Naro arrived ten minutes later.

Of course.

No jar.

Improvement.

Mevi with him.

Naro held stick.

Ordinary stick.

I checked anyway.

"We came to see Bell Ford rock."

Tavi had advertised.

"It's Tavi's."

"He said it has stripe."

"It does."

"Why?"

"Rock."

"That's not why."

"Minerals. Probably."

"Which?"

"Don't know."

Naro sighed.

My reputation continued correcting downward.

Healthy.

Tavi came out with rock.

Children examined it like state treasure.

Mevi sat on step.

"Mom says thank you again for drain thing."

"Sella did actual work."

"Mom thanked them too."

Good.

"Any more cases?"

"Don't know. Crew put screen. We weren't supposed go look."

"Did you?"

Naro said, "Near."

Mevi said, "He went near."

"Not to mouth."

"You went to red wall."

"Wall is not mouth."

I held up hand.

"I don't need exact legal defense."

Naro looked disappointed.

"Did you touch anything?"

"No."

"Good."

"I learned."

"Better."

He considered whether compliment acceptable.

Then Tavi announced Bell Ford had sun gnats that ate flower shine.

I had told him once.

Now ecology propagated.

Naro immediately asked whether flower becomes invisible.

"No," I said.

"Then what's shine?"

"Ask Rell."

"Who's Rell?"

"Woman in Bell Ford?"

"She lives there?"

I paused.

I did not know.

"Woman I met on trip."

"Then how ask?"

"You don't."

"Bad system."

Mevi laughed.

The four kids went inside after Cala approved.

House acquired noise.

I stayed step.

Neighbor.

That was what day felt like.

Not owner.

Not appraiser.

Not worker.

Not teacher.

Person who knew shutoff now.

Person whose crutch path got beveled because plumber noticed.

Person people asked what Bell Ford looked like.

Person children visited because another child had rock.

First life had neighborhoods too.

I had mostly passed through them.

Even when lived somewhere, rank and work and travel made place temporary in my head.

There were exceptions.

People.

Always people.

I remembered one old inn where owner kept my preferred cup for three years even though I appeared maybe twice each winter.

Blue glaze.

Cracked handle.

Terrible cup.

Mine because she said so.

Then inn burned during border raid.

Owner survived.

Cup did not.

I had been more upset about cup than reasonable.

Objects become evidence that place expected you back.

Dangerous thought around current chest.

I did not write it.

Late afternoon Brenna checked pipe.

Dry.

She pressed cloth to joint.

Dry.

Floor closed.

Actual repaired boards went down.

Not all. One section needed finish tomorrow because replacement plank had to settle after cut.

But path restored.

Wardrobe still wrong wall until tomorrow.

Cala asked total carpenter cost.

Brenna reminded her original quote plus extra plank only, plumbing separate. No surprise labor for waiting because she had other work downstairs trim while plumber worked.

I did not need numbers.

Not my ledger.

Again.

At dinner Lyssa woke from sofa looking less dead.

Marra's third-lot paper remained folded.

She did not open it.

Good.

Cala made soup.

Naro and Mevi had gone home.

Tavi put Bell Ford rock beside bowl.

"No," Cala said.

He moved it to windowsill.

Lyssa looked at me.

"Did you test bridge half?"

"No."

"Are you sick?"

"Resting it."

"It needs rest?"

"I need rest from myself."

She nodded.

"That I believe."

After dinner she finally unfolded Marra paper.

I did not lean.

Growth.

She read silently.

Longer than expected.

Then handed it to me.

Permission.

Third lot was not simply bigger.

Different.

South Hall wanted repeat service goods plus first **heat-cloth** trial.

Eight aprons.

Eight sleeve pairs.

Six wraps.

Four heat-cloths using supplied treated weave.

Twelve copper accepted return.

Six days.

Heat-cloth seams had separate stitch spacing and edge-fold requirements.

One sample piece supplied.

If heat-cloth sample failed, service-goods portion could still be accepted separately at listed subrate.

Marra had finally separated failure categories.

Good contract evolution.

Lyssa said, "I don't know treated weave."

"Then question is whether you want learn it for this price and structure."

"I know."

I shut up.

She tapped paper.

"I think I do."

"Okay."

"But I want one day before I say yes."

"Okay."

"Stop being agreeable."

"No."

Better.

She smiled.

"There."

Cala said from stove, "Tomorrow is still a day."

Lyssa folded paper.

"Tomorrow."

No acceptance yet.

No new income.

No rush.

House had dry pipe.

Floor mostly closed.

Lyssa had ten copper earned from completed second lot plus rework, though exact private total remained hers.

I had five.

Chest had one more questionable object.

Tavi had free rock.

Maybe he won economy.

Before bed I checked lower shutoff.

Not to operate.

To know location.

Quarter-turn resistance point visible by old scratch.

Back eighth, then forward.

Deren's words.

Useful knowledge that did not belong in artifact book.

I wrote on small card and tucked beside household maintenance papers:

UPSTAIRS WASH LINE
LOWER SHUTOFF STICKS BEFORE CLOSED
DO NOT FORCE
BACK 1/8 THEN FORWARD
DEREN RESEALED COLLAR
30-DAY LABOR REDO IF NEW SEAL FAILS UNDER ORDINARY USE

Cala read it.

"Good."

Not Hessa good.

House good.

Different.

I went upstairs.

Repaired path held.

No squeal.

Wardrobe remained wrong.

Chest locked.

I did not open it.

Outside, somebody argued about onions.

Cart rolled over uneven stone.

A child called another child liar with tremendous conviction.

Carrow.

Home, apparently.

Neighbor.

That one had happened slowly.
