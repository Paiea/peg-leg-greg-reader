TEXT READER · CHAPTER 379

THE BROKER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Vale had a chair for me.

That was how I knew something was wrong.

Not wrong dangerous.

Wrong expensive.

His office usually had chairs because offices did, but the one across from desk had been moved half a pace left, leaving clean space for crutches against wall.

Prepared.

I distrusted preparation when directed at me.

Vale looked up.

"Sit."

"How much does chair cost?"

"Nothing yet."

Worse.

I sat.

Vale pushed folded paper across desk.

Did not say debt.

Also worse.

"I have a client with a problem," he said.

"Congratulations."

"You know someone."

"I know several people now. It's getting out of hand."

He ignored.

Paper stayed folded.

"Client bought a retired mill component from North Secondary disposal. Not magical component, before you become interested."

I became less interested.

"Counterframe guide assembly. Iron, bronze shoes, two stone seats. Heavy. Awkward. Purchased for rebuild project."

"And?"

"One stone seat has old yard mark K-fourteen family."

Interest returned.

Not K-14-6 necessarily.

Family.

My thumb-sized counterload rest stone sat boxed at home.

"What exact mark?"

"K-fourteen-nine."

There.

Different item.

Same family maybe.

Could mean nothing beyond yard series.

Vale watched face.

Annoying man.

"Client wants what?"

"Information. Specifically someone who can tell him whether retaining the stone seat creates disposal, registration, or resale complication before he cuts assembly apart."

"I cannot."

"I know."

Good.

"Then why chair?"

"Because you know Mara."

I leaned back.

Ah.

Not appraiser.

Not expert.

Connection.

"You know Mara too."

"Professionally. She does not owe me casual interruption."

"Neither does she owe me."

"Correct."

Vale folded hands.

"But she has answered bounded questions for you before. Client will pay for a proper inspection if she accepts. I want introduction, not free opinion."

That was cleaner.

"Why not send client to her?"

"I did."

"And?"

"Her board is full through next six days."

"Then wait six days."

"Client intends cutting frame tomorrow because shop space costs money."

There.

Time pressure.

Not emergency.

Money pressure.

"Could store assembly?"

"At cost."

"How much?"

"Client's problem."

Right.

I looked folded paper.

"What are you offering me?"

"One copper for introduction if Mara agrees to hear request. Two additional copper if she accepts a paid inspection within client's two-day window."

Three potential.

Not for expertise.

For access.

Brokerage.

I disliked word before thinking it.

"No payment tied to her answer?"

"No."

"No payment tied to client buying, keeping, cutting, selling?"

"No."

"No percentage?"

"No."

"Good."

Vale almost smiled.

"You have learned something."

"Mostly suspicion."

"Useful curriculum."

I unfolded paper.

Client: Orsen Pell.

Workshop: East Mill Court.

Assembly: retired counterframe guide, disposal lot number, purchase date, declared mundane frame hardware with two stone seats retained in assembly.

One seat mark transcribed `K-14-9`.

Question requested:

DOES MARKED STONE SEAT REQUIRE SPECIAL HANDLING / REGISTRATION / INTACT RETENTION BEFORE DISASSEMBLY?

No claim it worked.

No claim magical.

No request valuation.

Good.

"Why me instead of paying Mara rush rate?"

"Because she has not offered rush rate."

"Good answer."

"I occasionally have them."

"Do I owe debt service if this works?"

"No. Cash task. Separate."

Important.

"Why?"

Vale looked irritated.

"Because not every useful thing you do for me must become debt accounting."

I stared.

"Who are you and what happened to Vale?"

"Take task or leave."

There he was.

"I'll ask Mara whether she wants request. I will not tell her client needs answer, only that client has time window."

"Fine."

"I will not pressure."

"Fine."

"If she says no, one copper still if she heard request?"

"If you actually deliver request, yes."

"If she says come back six days?"

"One copper."

"If she refers someone else?"

Vale paused.

"One copper for introduction to Mara. If referral leads to accepted inspection within two days, second two copper applies."

"Write that."

He did.

I liked Cala's influence on me.

Or hated.

Same result.

I left with paper.

Seven copper remained seven.

Potential ten.

More interesting: K-14-9 existed.

I did not go home for K-14-6.

Did not need it.

Also carrying own related object into someone else's inspection request would muddy everything.

I wrote mentally:

DO NOT MAKE YOUR COLLECTION THE CENTER OF OTHER PEOPLE'S PROBLEMS.

Good rule.

Possibly insulting rule.

Mara was at rear bench when I arrived.

Not closed-housing bench.

Different room.

Open windows, two apprentices sorting tags.

She saw me.

"No."

"I haven't asked."

"Your face asked."

Lyssa was right.

My face needed regulation.

"Paid request. Not mine."

That changed her attention.

"Who?"

"Orsen Pell, East Mill Court. Retired counterframe guide assembly. One stone seat marked K-14-9. He wants to know whether special handling, registration, or intact retention matters before disassembly. Two-day window because shop space. Vale is intermediary."

Mara held hand.

Paper.

I gave it.

She read.

"Did you see assembly?"

"No."

"Good."

Interesting standard.

"Did Vale?"

"Paper doesn't say."

"Did client remove stone?"

"Paper says retained in assembly."

She read again.

"K-fourteen is yard family, not artifact class."

There.

Bounded information.

My K-14-6 note already treated family broadly, but useful reinforcement.

"Nine could be ordinary seat. Six could be ordinary seat. Mark alone doesn't establish active behavior."

"Understood."

"You understand when supervised."

Hessa had competition.

Mara looked toward apprentice.

"Siv, tomorrow second bell?"

Apprentice Siv checked slate.

"You have South Lock fittings."

"Third bell?"

"Merren inspection at third."

"Fourth?"

"Lunch."

Mara stared.

Siv did not move.

"You told me to write lunch."

"I did."

"So lunch."

Mara sighed.

Different voice.

Apprentice protected specialist from herself.

Useful.

"Fifth bell," Mara said.

Siv checked.

"Open half bell before East Gate housing."

Mara looked at request.

"Half bell enough for handling classification if assembly accessible and papers present. Not valuation. Not function certification. Two copper inspection fee plus travel one copper. Three total. Client accepts before I go."

I nodded.

"Want me carry answer?"

"Yes. Exact."

She wrote:

TOMORROW FIFTH BELL
HALF-BELL HANDLING / PAPER INSPECTION ONLY
3 C TOTAL INCLUDING TRAVEL
ASSEMBLY ACCESSIBLE, STONE NOT REMOVED
PURCHASE / DISPOSAL PAPERS PRESENT
NO VALUATION
NO FUNCTION CERTIFICATION

Then below:

IF ACCEPTED, SEND CONFIRMATION BY END OF TODAY.

"Anything else?"

"Don't tell him K-fourteen means anything useful."

"You just told me it means yard family."

"That's all."

"I won't."

She handed paper.

"And Greg."

"Yes?"

"Do not bring yours."

I stared.

"I wasn't going to."

"Good."

Damn it.

I left.

Vale's office would have been easy.

But task was introduction and accepted inspection within two-day window.

Client needed accept.

I could route through Vale.

Or go East Mill Court.

Paper had address.

Which was intended?

I returned Vale first.

He was not surprised.

"Three copper inspection," I said. "Tomorrow fifth bell, half bell, handling and papers only. Assembly accessible, stone stays in, purchase and disposal papers present. No valuation, no function certification. Confirmation by end today."

Vale nodded.

"Good."

"Client needs receive."

"I'll send runner."

"Then my task waits."

"Yes."

"One copper earned?"

"Yes."

He paid one.

Eight.

Not ten yet.

Correct.

"You can leave," Vale said.

"I want to know if accepts."

"Curiosity or money?"

"Both."

"At least honest."

I sat in prepared chair.

Vale worked.

I discovered watching someone do debt ledgers was worse than owing debt.

Maybe.

No.

Owing still worse.

Ten minutes.

Twenty.

Runner returned breathing hard.

"Accepted."

Vale looked up.

"Terms?"

Runner handed note.

ORSEN PELL ACCEPTS 3 C
ASSEMBLY REMAINS INTACT / ACCESSIBLE
PAPERS READY
TOMORROW FIFTH BELL

Vale handed me two copper.

Ten.

Simple.

Three copper earned because I knew who to ask and did not distort request.

That was unsettling.

"Do I attend inspection?" I asked.

"No."

"Good."

Vale looked at me.

"Unless Mara asks."

"She won't."

"Probably not."

I folded my copy of terms.

"Does client know I got paid?"

"My fee arrangement with you is my expense. His three copper goes Mara directly."

"Does he pay you?"

"Yes."

"How much?"

"Not your problem."

Fair.

I stood.

Vale said, "There may be more of this."

I stopped.

"What is this?"

"Knowing who actually knows things."

Dangerous compliment.

"I know very few people."

"More than before. More importantly, you increasingly know limits of what they know."

That landed harder.

"Don't turn me into referral desk."

"I don't intend to. Referral desks are cheaper."

"Comforting."

"I intend to call when mismatch costs more than your fee."

There.

Vale.

Not kindness.

Economics.

Better.

"Case by case," I said.

"Obviously."

I left with ten copper.

Not rich.

But back to pre-Step-Keeper cash after two days.

Purchase had not vanished.

I still owned object.

Money recovered through network.

That felt different from earning by lifting, tallying, teaching, watching lamp, ushering.

Information routing.

First life had plenty.

At higher rank, people constantly asked who could solve what.

I had treated that as command structure.

Sometimes it was.

Sometimes I had been doing this and not noticed because nobody paid three copper equivalent for introductions when I was S-class.

They paid in favors.

Worse currency.

Harder ledger.

I stopped at bread stall.

Did not buy because bit continuity remained metaphysical curse.

No.

Because I wasn't hungry.

Important distinction.

At home, Lyssa had sample heat-cloth half assembled.

Treated weave looked ordinary until light hit angle, then fibers had faint bronze dullness.

Not glow.

Not evidence magic.

Just material.

I stayed two steps away.

She noticed.

"Why are you hovering?"

"I'm not touching."

"You can sit."

"Closer?"

"Yes."

Progress.

I sat opposite.

"How does it handle?"

"Stiffer across fold. Worse on needle turn. Edge wants crawl if I pull same as normal weave."

Work knowledge.

"So different tension?"

"Maybe. Don't help."

"Was asking."

"Your asking has elbows."

I did not know what that meant.

Probably accurate.

She sewed three stitches.

Stopped.

Pulled back one.

"Marra said treated side must face marked side of fold."

"Which is treated side?"

She pointed tiny black thread woven into selvage.

"This edge mark tells orientation."

"Does she know why?"

"She said South Hall kitchen knows. I need make it correctly, not become kitchen."

Excellent.

I said nothing.

Lyssa looked suspicious.

"You agree too hard."

"I'm learning restraint."

"No you're not."

Fair.

Cala came in with market basket.

"You got paid?"

News again.

"How?"

"Vale's runner passed me. Said you were sitting in office waiting for money."

Carrow surveillance network was mostly people walking streets.

"Three copper."

"For?"

"Introducing Vale's client request to Mara. She accepted paid inspection."

Cala set basket down.

"You charged friend for knowing friend?"

There.

Social discomfort.

"Vale paid me. Mara gets her own three copper from client. I did not take from Mara."

"Did Mara know you were paid?"

I paused.

No.

Not stated.

Cala saw pause.

"Hm."

That hm cost more than copper.

"Should she?"

"I don't know."

Annoying honest answer.

"You think yes."

"I think if somebody brought me work because another person paid them, I'd want know if it changed what they said."

"It didn't."

"Then telling is easy."

Damn.

Lyssa did not look up from sewing.

"She's right."

"You weren't invited."

"I'm at table."

"Hostile table."

She smiled.

I considered.

Vale paid one for delivering request, two if inspection accepted.

That created incentive to make Mara accept.

I had deliberately not pressured.

But Mara did not know incentive existed.

That mattered.

Brokerage without disclosure could become manipulation even if this time clean.

First life favors again.

Hidden incentives everywhere.

I hated discovering ethics after getting paid.

"I'll tell her," I said.

Cala unpacked onions.

"Good."

Not Hessa.

Not house good either.

Maybe Cala good.

Too many goods.

I went back.

Mara saw me and looked immediately annoyed.

"What now?"

"Disclosure."

That got eyebrow.

"Vale paid me one copper to deliver request, plus two if you accepted inspection within two days. I did not tell you before asking. I should have."

Mara stared.

Long enough.

"Did Vale condition payment on my conclusion?"

"No."

"On fee?"

"No."

"On accepting within two days."

"Yes."

"Did you pressure my schedule?"

"I gave window and said client had shop-space reason. You chose fifth bell."

"Did you tell Vale anything beyond my written terms?"

"No."

She considered.

"Then inspection stands."

Relief.

"Future?"

"Tell me before I answer if your pay depends on yes."

"Yes."

"If you're paid flat to carry request, I don't care. If bonus depends on my acceptance, I care."

Specific.

Good.

"Understood."

"And don't become broker who thinks every friendship is inventory."

There.

"I was worried about that."

"Then keep worrying enough."

Mara returned work.

I did not apologize six times.

Growth.

Outside, I updated note:

REFERRAL RULE
DISCLOSE IF MY PAY CHANGES BASED ON SPECIALIST SAYING YES
FLAT DELIVERY FEE LESS IMPORTANT, BUT DO NOT HIDE IF RELEVANT
NO PAY TIED TO RESULT / VALUATION / PURCHASE
FRIENDSHIP IS NOT INVENTORY

Last line Mara's.

I hated it because good.

At third bell I had theatre training.

Not chapter center.

Still happened.

Nessa spent half bell on capacity slate, emergency exits, patron disputes, cash handoff, lost-property procedure, and exact difference between house lead and door cover authority.

No incident.

No drama.

I passed enough to be cleared for ordinary doors/gallery cover, but not cash box close and not emergency house lead.

Paid one copper.

Eleven.

Nessa added my name to call list.

There.

Durable.

"Don't make me regret it," she said.

"Specific failure category?"

"All."

Maro, ankle still wrapped, said, "She's supportive."

Nessa ignored.

I left with eleven copper.

One more than before Step Keeper.

Interesting.

Not because artifact produced value.

Because city did.

Relationships did.

Skills did.

Access did.

That distinction mattered if I wanted collection without becoming idiot who expected every object to pay for itself immediately.

At home, Step Keeper remained locked.

I opened chest only to put papers in proper folder.

Did not touch housing.

There it was.

Six-copper question.

Beside other questions.

Chest getting crowded.

Soon storage needed answer.

Not tonight.

I counted copper once.

Eleven.

Vale twenty-three.

Then wrote one final line:

ACCESS HAS VALUE.

Crossed it out.

Too broad.

Wrote:

KNOWING WHO TO ASK CAN BE WORK.

Better.

Then below:

DON'T SELL THE PERSON.
SELL THE ROUTING.

I stared.

Still sounded like broker.

Because apparently I had been one.

Broker.

Case by case.
