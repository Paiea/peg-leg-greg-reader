TEXT READER · CHAPTER 301

THE PATIENT

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Morning hurt more.

Of course it did.

Night had been generous enough to let me believe shoulder was only annoyed.

Morning clarified.

Shoulder was furious.

Right palm stiff.

Forearm sore.

Left side fine.

Right foot fine.

Good.

Important inventory.

I sat on station bench and lifted arm slowly.

Forward.

Fine until shoulder height.

Sideways.

Worse.

Back.

Absolutely not.

Vessa watched from doorway.

"Still funny?"

"Less."

"Good."

"Cruel."

"Useful."

She came over.

Pressed around shoulder.

Not gentle.

"Sharp?"

"There."

"Here?"

"Less."

"Here?"

"I hate you."

"That's not a location."

Sori from table said, "Upper outside shoulder. He means upper outside shoulder."

"Traitor."

"Recorder."

Dask was already awake.

Of course.

He had my damaged crutch across knees.

I stared.

"Why do you have that?"

"Inspection."

"Permission?"

He looked at crutch.

Then me.

"Implied by bite marks."

"No."

"Fair."

He handed it back immediately.

Growth.

I inspected myself.

Four tooth grooves along upper shaft.

One deeper than rest.

No crack I could see.

Metal cuff fine.

Tip seated.

Hand grip tight.

Kel crouched beside me.

"Roll it."

I rolled shaft across bench.

No wobble.

He thumbed deepest groove.

"Surface bite."

"You certified crutches now?"

"No. I know wood."

"Better."

Vessa said, "No fighting with it today."

"Planning none."

"No hard leverage either."

"Less fun."

"Greg."

"Yes."

There.

Patient.

Apparently that was today.

Not patient as calm person.

Patient as injured idiot forced to accept information.

I hated role already.

Relief cart had arrived after dark.

Two med hands and one station replacement.

Pellin survived night without worsening bleed.

Good.

His leg looked awful anyway.

One med hand, **Jessa Ril**, had broad wrists, gray hair tied tight, and tone of someone who considered everyone between her and bandages an administrative problem.

She checked Pellin first.

Then Noll.

Then Vara.

Then me because Vessa told her.

I objected.

"I'm not station staff."

Jessa said, "Pain doesn't respect payroll. Sit."

Fine.

She rotated shoulder more carefully than Vessa.

Still hurt.

"Bruised soft tissue, maybe strain. No obvious break. No obvious joint displacement. You get numbness?"

"No."

"Weak grip?"

I squeezed fingers.

Right weaker because sore.

"Pain-limited."

"Any neck pain?"

"No."

"Good. Keep load down today. Cold wrap now, warm later if swelling stays low. If pain climbs instead of settles, stop being clever."

"I can try."

She looked at Vessa.

"Can he?"

Vessa said, "Intermittently."

Everyone loved me.

Jessa wrapped shoulder with cool clay cloth.

Felt wonderful.

I hated that too.

Pellin was going south in medical cart to Harrow Fen infirmary.

There.

Harrow Fen again.

He looked annoyed by direction.

"I was supposed to finish north line."

Jessa said, "You're supposed to keep leg."

"Both can happen."

"Not today."

I liked her.

Noll stayed at station with replacement keeper **Beren Holt**.

Vara stayed half day to finish incident handoff, then would ride south with supply wagon if shoulder worsened.

No deaths.

Good ending for yesterday.

Not dramatic enough for songs.

Better.

Breakfast quiet at first.

Not awkward.

Just tired.

Then Dask said, "I reviewed the fight sequence."

Sori put spoon down.

"No."

"I wasn't asking Greg to repeat anything."

"Still no."

"I only have one question."

"You never have one question."

"One primary question."

Kel stood.

"I'm checking mule."

Coward.

Maro drank tea and looked at wall.

Vessa kept eating.

Dask pointed at my crutch.

"When first hound entered inside Kel's pole, you changed right crutch from support to thrust before shifting balance."

"Yes."

"Why didn't you fall?"

Good question.

Actual question.

I thought.

"Because left crutch and right foot already formed stable triangle. Right crutch wasn't carrying much at that moment."

He blinked.

"You knew exact load?"

"No. Felt it."

"First-life habit?"

"Partly. Current-body habit too."

There.

Important.

Not everything useful belonged dead old man.

This body had taught me things first one never needed.

Crutch angles.

One-foot pivots.

How much weight a shoulder could take before hand placement got stupid.

How ground lied differently when you only got one foot to vote.

S-class memory helped.

Nineteen-year-old Greg had done work too.

I liked that.

Dask nodded slowly.

"So not transferable."

"Not directly."

"Good."

Sori looked suspicious.

"Why good?"

"Because I was afraid he had secret equation."

"You would marry equation."

"Depends equation."

Maro finally laughed.

There.

Normal.

Vessa finished bowl.

"Route."

Everyone reset.

Beren had current north line report.

Three Posts to **Lark Spur** open.

Upper return-bed road wet but usable on foot and pack mule.

No wagon until Lark Spur.

Signal frames between stations operating manually because of false tones.

Important.

Not closed.

Manual.

Workers physically confirm before moving anything consequential.

Good system response.

Beren said, "You may hear tones. Ignore unless local hand confirms."

Dask looked wounded.

"Ignore?"

"Record if you want. Don't obey them."

Better wording.

Vessa asked, "Distance?"

"Six local miles to Lark Spur. Two raised runs, one bog bridge, then return-bed ridge."

"Shelter?"

"Halfway stone shed. Lark has station bunks if you arrive before night clerk sells them to freight crew."

"Sells?"

"Assigns. But he acts like landlord."

Sori wrote name.

"Who?"

"Elden Marr."

"Good."

I asked, "Anything likely to bite us?"

Beren looked at me.

"Usually no."

Fuck this region.

We left after first bell.

Pellin left south at same time.

He lifted hand from cart.

"Bronze."

I lifted left hand.

"Leg."

He looked down at bandaged leg.

"Fair."

Jessa slapped cart side.

They rolled west-south toward Harrow Fen road.

For one moment I wanted to go with them.

Not because injured.

Market awnings.

Food.

Artifacts.

Civilization.

Then north levee waited.

I wanted that too.

Good problem.

We walked.

Slowly.

Meaning I walked slowly.

Everyone else had to suffer pace or spread.

Vessa chose pace.

I objected after quarter mile.

"You can go normal. I can trail with Kel."

"No."

"I'm not fragile."

"No."

"That's not argument."

"Correct."

Sori said, "Patient."

I hated title more.

Shoulder hated crutch load after fifteen minutes.

So we adjusted.

Not heroic.

Practical.

Harness strap changed to take shoulder bag weight off right side.

Kel carried record case for first run.

I used right crutch shorter plants.

Vessa cut pace by maybe ten percent.

Maro took lead.

Dask was told not to quantify slowdown out loud.

He agreed with visible pain.

Road north left reeds gradually.

Upper return beds were wide shallow basins cut into marsh shelf, mostly dry now.

Cracked pale clay under water stains.

Low stone ridges separated each bed.

Every hundred paces, black iron stakes stood with small glass cups mounted near top.

Some cups empty.

Some held dull silver liquid.

I looked.

Dask looked too.

Neither asked.

Maro noticed.

"Old return samplers."

Dask inhaled.

Maro held up finger.

"No. That's enough."

Beautiful.

Second raised run crossed on timber matting.

My right hand hurt more than shoulder there.

Grip pressure.

I said, "Ten breaths."

Vessa stopped.

Nobody commented.

That mattered more after yesterday.

Because they had seen what I could do when pushed.

And nobody treated rest as contradiction.

Good.

Competence did not require pretending body hadn't sent bill.

We continued.

At third mile, manual signal crew stood beside low mast.

Two people.

One woman on lever.

One man with sighting glass.

As we approached, mast sounded one low tone.

Nobody moved.

They waited.

Woman looked toward next ridge.

Man checked sighting glass.

Then raised green paddle manually.

Only then did woman move lever.

Signal arm shifted.

There.

Infrastructure learning from uncertainty.

Dask whispered, "Redundancy."

"Yes."

"Good redundancy."

"Yes."

He looked almost emotional.

Sori said, "Don't cry."

"I don't cry at redundancy."

"You would."

"Maybe once."

Manual crew had news.

False tones continued intermittently west and south.

No unexplained physical gate movements confirmed north of Three Posts.

Arm drift happened at two stations, one west-facing and one north-facing.

Maintenance from Harrow Fen moving circuit.

Again.

Separate observations.

No theory yet.

Good.

We ate at stone shed halfway.

Bread.

Cold meat.

Pickled something purple.

I disliked it.

Dask loved it.

Suspicious.

Maro inspected my shoulder wrap.

"You swelling?"

"Little."

"Pain worse?"

"Same."

"Hand?"

"Sore."

"Good."

"Why is everyone saying good to pain?"

"Because worse is bad."

Fair.

He handed me flask.

I drank.

Sori sat beside me.

Quiet for maybe minute.

Then:

"You scared me yesterday."

There.

Not accusation exactly.

I looked at her.

"Hounds?"

"No. You."

Fairer.

I waited.

She rubbed thumb along cup rim.

"Not because you were violent. We were all violent. Vessa hit one hard enough I heard ribs."

Vessa from other side said, "I can hear you."

"Good."

Sori continued.

"You changed."

"Yes."

"Like you stopped spending words."

That was accurate.

"Probably."

"And people became pieces."

There.

Harder.

"Variables," I said.

"That's not better."

"No."

I looked at hands.

"That's why I don't live there."

She nodded.

"I figured after."

"You were still people."

"I know."

"Important distinction."

"Still unsettling."

"Also fair."

She looked toward Vessa.

"When she said down, you looked annoyed."

"I was."

"At her?"

"At everything getting slow again."

There.

Truth.

Locked mode made world simple.

Normal world had nuance.

People.

Feelings.

Pain.

Purple pickles.

Harder in different way.

Sori said, "That sounds awful."

"Sometimes useful."

"Not same thing."

"No."

She nodded.

Conversation done.

Not fixed.

Better.

We left shed.

Bog bridge came near fourth mile.

Not bridge exactly.

Series of floating timber rectangles chained between stone anchors.

Wonderful.

My favorite.

Vessa looked at me.

"Can you?"

I tested first panel with crutch.

Moved two inches.

Shoulder said no enthusiasm.

"Yes. Slow."

Kel went ahead without being asked.

Good.

He positioned one panel at a time with boot before I transferred.

No pride issue.

Yesterday I fought hounds.

Today Kel stabilized floating wood for me.

Both true.

Halfway across, a small silver fish jumped onto panel.

Flopped.

I stopped.

Dask behind said, "Don't kick it."

"Why would I kick it?"

"Reflex."

"Whose?"

"Unknown."

Sori said, "Dask wants fish incident."

I nudged fish with crutch tip edge toward water.

It flipped itself off.

Victory.

No ledger entry.

Progress.

On far side, ground rose.

Marsh thinned.

First real trees in hours.

Thin white-barked trunks with dark leaves.

Maro called them **glass birch**.

Dask said they were not actually glass.

We all said, "We know."

He looked betrayed.

Lark Spur appeared before evening.

More than station.

Less than town.

Road junction on dry ridge.

Stable.

Two inns pretending to be sheds.

Repair yard.

Signal tower.

Small row of market tables under patched awnings.

There.

Market.

Greg lived.

Vessa saw my face.

"One hour after station check."

I stopped.

"Really?"

"If bunks secured and route board clean."

"I love you."

"Don't."

Sori laughed.

Station clerk **Elden Marr** did act like landlord.

Thin man with silver spectacles and vest too nice for ridge dust.

"Survey priority gets four bunks."

Vessa said, "We're six."

"Then two floor."

"Assignment covers six beds."

"It covers six sleeping places."

Sori stepped forward.

Oh no.

Twenty minutes later we had six bunks.

I don't know what she did.

I did not ask.

Some wars belonged experts.

Route board north showed next day open to **Greyhook Rise**, then upper return beds beyond.

No closure.

Manual signal confirmation still active.

Good.

Vessa gave us hour.

"No buying anything that affects load without asking."

She looked at me.

"Still targeted."

"Yes."

I walked market with shoulder wrapped and one crutch carrying slightly less weight.

Tiny market.

Food.

Boot repair.

Two baskets of old brass fittings.

A woman selling weather charms that looked like tied wire.

One table of retired station hardware.

There.

Of course.

I stopped.

Seller was boy maybe sixteen with scar across chin and expression of ancient disappointment.

"Don't touch black tags."

"Wasn't."

"Everyone says that after."

On table:

Old signal cups.

Cracked relay plates.

Ceramic caps.

One palm-sized brass **line quietener** with three notches and faded black-slash retirement mark.

Label handwritten:

`DAMPENS FALSE / ECHO TONE ON SHORT COPPER RUNS. RETIRED. 8c.`

Eight.

Interesting.

Very interesting.

Visible utility.

Current regional false-tone problem.

Price reflected it.

Rational.

I asked, "Why retired?"

Boy shrugged.

"Makes true weak tones quieter too. Stations stopped using after missed flood warning twelve years ago."

There.

Downside.

Real.

"Documented?"

He produced stamped old maintenance card.

Lark district.

Short copper runs only.

Not universal.

Not useless.

Potentially dangerous if used stupidly.

Eight copper.

I had twenty-three.

Could buy.

Did not.

Not yet.

I asked if he'd hold until morning.

"One bit."

There.

Bits unspecified.

Continuity trap.

I smiled.

"No."

"Then maybe here tomorrow."

Fair.

I walked away.

Patient.

Not because injured.

Because waiting could also be competence.

Wait for shoulder.

Wait for evidence.

Wait before buying tool just because current problem made it sexy.

Wait before deciding crew now saw me differently forever.

Wait before opening reserve again.

I could do that.

Probably.

At food stall I bought nothing because dinner covered.

Growth continued.

Back at bunkhouse, Dask was asleep already.

Early.

He looked dead.

Sori whispered, "No grinding yet."

Kel said, "Give him time."

Maro had taken top bunk.

Vessa was writing route notes.

I lay down carefully.

Shoulder complained.

Hand complained.

Good.

Not good.

Stable.

Today no fight.

No magic.

No crisis.

We moved six miles north anyway.

Sometimes adventure was surviving enough yesterday to still have road today.

Patient.

I could hate it tomorrow.
