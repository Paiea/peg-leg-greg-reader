TEXT READER · CHAPTER 398

THE REPAIRMAN

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Rusk looked at my crutch and said, "Dog?"

"Reed hound."

"Dog with wet opinions."

Fair.

Greyhook repair yard occupied half a terrace below freight anchors.

Three open-sided sheds.

Two saw benches.

Iron brazier.

Rows of staff shafts, wagon poles, chain hooks, replacement slats, and enough clamps to restrain a small god.

Wind moved through everything.

Not gusts.

Constant pressure.

Every loose strap had somewhere to slap.

Every hanging tool had been tied twice.

Rusk was maybe sixty.

Maybe forty-five with weather.

Hard to tell here.

Broad shoulders.

White beard cut short.

Two missing fingertips on left hand.

He took my right crutch only after I handed it over.

Good start.

Rolled shaft.

Pressed thumb around tooth grooves.

Held it near ear and flexed gently.

Then looked at me.

"You hit dog with this too?"

"Yes."

"After it bit?"

"Before and after."

"Mm."

That sound contained judgment and professional interest.

He set crutch on padded bench.

"Wood's sound. Deepest tooth got through finish, not through working depth. Hand grip fine. Upper cuff fine. Tip fine."

I relaxed more than expected.

Rusk noticed.

"You like these."

"I need these."

"Different."

"Both."

He nodded.

"Also different."

There.

Repair people.

He proposed shallow resin fill in grooves, wrap reinforcement around deepest mark, re-seal shaft.

Not structural splice.

Not replacement.

"Cost?"

"Assignment?"

Vessa from behind me said, "Personal equipment damaged during field incident under assignment. Reasonable repair covered if standard."

Rusk looked at her.

"Reasonable is ugly word."

"Use cheaper one."

"Covered."

Excellent.

No copper.

Economy liked paperwork sometimes.

"How long?" I asked.

"Hour if wind behaves. Two if it doesn't."

I looked outside.

Wind stole a rag off peg despite being tied.

Rusk watched it snap at cord.

"Two."

Fair.

He pointed at my shoulder.

"You favoring right?"

"Little."

"Then sit somewhere. Don't practice being heroic in my yard."

Everyone had same advice now.

I was becoming predictable.

I sat on low crate beside shed wall.

Left crutch across knees.

Right shoulder dull ache.

Better than yesterday.

Still there.

Greyhook weather hold had turned whole ridge into temporary city of people waiting professionally.

Freight crews played cards under chain awning.

Two mule handlers argued about salt blocks.

Wind tower operators walked terrace lines checking cloth strips, vanes, and weighted cords.

One woman spent ten minutes retying same canvas cover because wind wanted job too.

There.

New ecology.

Work shaped by air instead of water.

Dask arrived carrying my wrapped line quietener like sacred relic.

I frowned.

"Why do you have that?"

He stopped.

"Sori gave it to me."

Sori from doorway said, "I gave him the bag because you left it on bunk. I did not give him permission to unwrap anything."

Dask held package farther from body.

"It remains wrapped."

"Good."

He handed it over.

I took with left hand.

Shoulder complained less.

"Bench?" he asked.

"Maybe."

"Here would be ideal."

Rusk from bench said, "For what?"

Dask began.

"Retired short-run line quietener, passive brass plate, likely split leaf internal but older type, intended to suppress echo or false tone on short copper signaling runs while unfortunately reducing weak true tones as well."

Rusk kept working.

"So bad bell helper."

Dask looked pained.

"That is incomplete."

"Still understood."

I liked Rusk.

Greyhook had its own test bench for retired signal hardware.

Of course.

Wind towers used short copper runs from weighted strip frames to local click boxes.

Not magic maybe.

Not only magic.

Mostly mechanics and line resonance.

Rusk said bench belonged yard foreman, not him.

Foreman **Tala Venn** was currently up tower three swearing at west vane.

"If she comes down not mad, ask."

"If mad?"

"Ask tomorrow."

Good system.

Weather hold board changed at first evening bell.

Aven Ro crossed terrace shouting before sign even got chalk.

"North Reach still held! West strip disagrees! Tower one green, tower two green, tower three red twice in quarter bell! No one gets creative!"

A freight man shouted, "What if we're already creative?"

"Then be creative indoors!"

She kept walking.

Excellent management.

Vessa checked route board.

"Morning earliest."

No frustration in voice.

Just fact.

I had learned this crew traveled by fact more than desire.

Useful.

Rusk opened resin tin.

Smelled like pine and hot stone.

Filled tooth marks with narrow bone spatula.

Wrapped deepest section in thin dark fiber tape soaked with resin.

Not pretty.

Strong-looking.

"Will wrap change flex?" Dask asked.

Rusk: "Tiny."

"How tiny?"

"Tiny enough I'm not measuring for you."

Dask inhaled through nose.

Sori sat beside me.

"He's growing."

"Painfully."

"Like fungus."

Dask looked over.

"I can hear you."

"Good."

Crew normal.

That helped.

No one had asked me to demonstrate hound technique.

No one wanted locked Greg back out for entertainment.

Good people.

Maybe.

Tala Venn came down tower three near second bell.

Small woman.

Maybe fifty.

Hair braided under leather cap.

One eye watering from wind.

She was absolutely mad.

She kicked tower stair bottom.

"West vane is lying."

Rusk said, "Vane can't lie."

"Then whoever set lower weight is lying through vane."

She saw us.

"Survey?"

Vessa nodded.

"Held north."

"I know. Everyone knows because Aven has lungs."

Aven somewhere above shouted something unintelligible.

Tala pointed upward.

"See."

I waited.

Dask did not.

"We have retired short-run line quietener and would like bench-only comparison if yard permits."

Tala stared.

Then at package.

Then at me.

"Yours?"

"Yes."

"Live line?"

"No."

"Good, because I would hit you with it."

Reasonable.

She took maintenance card.

Read.

"Old West Locks pattern."

"Likely," Dask said.

"Not likely. Stamp says West Locks."

Dask leaned.

There was tiny district mark under grease stain.

He looked betrayed by own eyesight.

Tala enjoyed this.

"Bench three. Short copper. Mechanical tone source. No field light. No active relay. We can see if plate still does basic bad-bell-helper job."

Rusk smiled without looking up.

Dask whispered, "It is not called that."

I said, "It is now."

Bench three sat inside second shed.

Simple enough.

Two upright pegs.

Copper line between.

Small striker box on one end.

Listening plate on other.

A second branch with adjustable slack and old echo loop.

Tala explained.

Normal strike made one clean click at plate.

Echo loop could create weaker delayed second click.

Quietener inserted between two short leads.

No magic.

No live signaling.

No stakes beyond curiosity.

Exactly right.

I unwrapped brass plate.

First time as owner.

Heavy for size.

Three notches.

Black retirement slash faint.

Spring leaf inside slit visible when tilted.

Dask wanted to touch.

Did not ask.

I looked at him.

He sighed.

"May I?"

"After first run."

"Fine."

Growth under oppression.

Tala ran baseline.

CLACK.

Then weaker tick.

Clear enough.

She adjusted slack.

Again.

CLACK.

Tick.

"Echo deliberately exaggerated," she said. "Real line messier."

Greg understood.

We inserted quietener.

No drama.

No glow.

No hum.

Tala struck.

Clack.

Nothing obvious after.

Dask leaned toward plate.

"Again."

Tala: "You say please to my bench."

He blinked.

"Please."

Again.

Clack.

Tiny delayed sound maybe.

Much less.

There.

It worked.

At least on toy problem designed to show exact use.

Useful.

Known.

Then Tala reduced main strike strength.

"Now downside."

Baseline weak strike without quietener.

Click.

Echo softer.

With quietener:

Tick.

Barely.

Dask frowned.

I did too.

Tala nodded.

"There. It doesn't know truth. It knows shape and strength."

Good line.

Not magic wisdom.

Engineering.

That was why thing was dangerous.

It could not distinguish false from important.

Neither could lots of people.

I liked it more.

Which was bad.

"One more," I said.

Tala looked.

"Why?"

"Different echo delay. Same main strength."

She nodded.

Good question apparently.

Adjusted branch.

Longer delay.

Baseline gave clean main + later weak tick.

Quietener damped later tick less than short-delay echo.

Interesting.

Dask started speaking fast.

"Frequency or phase sensitivity maybe, or mechanical settling time, possibly leaf preload relation, if we vary line tension and delay independently we'd need grid, perhaps twelve runs minimum, preferably twenty-four."

Tala held up hand.

"No."

He stopped.

"Why?"

"Because my bench is for work. You got answer. Plate functions. Downside functions. Go away."

I wrapped quietener.

"Thank you."

"You bought a problem."

"Yes."

"Good. Most interesting tools are."

There.

Collector logic.

Not power.

Problem with shape.

Back outside, Rusk had crutch hanging under roof beam to cure.

"One hour."

Shoulder had stiffened while sitting.

I stood carefully.

Left crutch only plus wall hand.

Bad idea for travel.

Fine for three steps.

Sori offered arm.

I took it.

No speech.

Small recalibration.

Greyhook evening market was mostly workers selling worker things.

Wind goggles.

Leather tie loops.

Weighted cloak hems.

Hook gloves with reinforced palms.

Dried meat.

Tiny bottles of eye oil.

One table of bent signal vanes sold as wall decoration to people with questionable taste.

I wanted one.

Did not buy.

Fifteen copper.

Vale twenty-three.

Money not crisis.

Still money.

I bought nothing.

Look at me.

Growth.

Kel did buy a weighted scarf.

Sori mocked him until wind hit her face on upper terrace.

Then she asked where seller was.

Kel refused to tell for almost five minutes.

Justice.

At third bell, Greyhook performed wind check.

All three towers visible from yard.

Huge slatted arms angled into wind.

Weighted cloth strips hung from side booms.

Tower one green flag.

Tower two green.

Tower three red.

Then green.

Aven watched through brass tube.

"Need half bell agreement."

People groaned.

Not us.

We had bunks.

I had repaired crutch soon.

Weather could be weather.

Rusk returned right crutch after cure.

Resin dark around tooth grooves.

Thin reinforcement wrap maybe four inches long.

He handed it to me.

"Test here."

I did.

Plant.

Weight.

Forward.

Back.

Small twist.

No creak.

No give.

Grip same.

Flex maybe different by amount Dask would ruin evening trying to name.

"Feels good."

Rusk: "Feels sound?"

I corrected.

"Feels sound."

"Good. Don't hit dogs for two days."

"Planning not to."

"Plans attract dogs."

Maybe true.

Vessa appeared with route update.

"Window possibly dawn. Aven wants everyone ready first bell. If towers disagree, we wait. If they agree half bell, we cross immediately."

"What is cross?"

"Greyhook crown to North Reach shelf. About one and three-quarter miles exposed. Anchor chain every thirty paces. Two wind shelters. One stair descent. No wagons. Mule line separate lower shelf."

I looked shoulder.

She saw.

"Can you do it if window opens?"

Good question.

Not can you force it.

Can you do it.

"Probably. Need right shoulder reassess morning. Chain requirement?"

"No hand-on-chain if green window. Chain is emergency only."

Better.

"Then probably yes."

"If probably becomes no, say no."

"Yes."

There.

Normal Greg.

No performance.

Night bunks were in long stone room built into ridge.

Wind thumped roof anyway.

Dask wanted to write bench results into personal notes.

I let him copy maintenance card observations but kept ownership notes myself.

New artifact entry mentally:

Line quietener.

Works on short copper bench.

Reduces short-delay echo strongly.

Reduces weak true strike too.

Longer-delay echo damped less in one bench condition.

No claim beyond bench.

No live use.

No magic interaction known.

Useful.

Dangerous.

Mine.

That last word felt good.

Collection growing.

Not because I needed to fill slots.

Because objects had stories and constraints and ways they could change decisions.

Holdfast waited Carrow.

Retreat oval waited Merecross.

Rest stone.

Shade unit.

Step Keeper.

Now this.

Most of them I barely understood.

Excellent.

Sori lay on bunk above me.

"You smiling at brass?"

"No."

"You're smiling."

"Shoulder pain."

"Looks romantic."

"Go to sleep."

"Customer."

"Don't start."

She laughed.

Wind hit wall.

Stone room shuddered tiny amount.

Nobody cared.

I did.

New place.

New rules.

Tomorrow maybe we crossed exposed crown where wind itself decided whether road existed.

Tonight I had one repaired crutch, one sore shoulder, one dangerous little brass plate, fifteen copper, twenty-three copper of Vale, and no reason to become more than that.

Repairman.

Not because I fixed crutch.

Rusk did.

Not because I fixed quietener.

Didn't need fixing.

Because day had been about keeping useful things useful.

Body.

Equipment.

Judgment.

Even restraint.

Turns out maintenance was adventure too.

Annoying.
