TEXT READER · CHAPTER 362

THE CROSSER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Tower three went green before breakfast.

Nobody trusted it.

Reasonable.

Aven Ro stood under the route board with a cup in one hand and her coat snapping around her knees.

"One green. Two green. Three green. Clock starts now. Nobody celebrates."

Dask looked offended.

"I wasn't going to celebrate."

"Good. Keep doing that."

Greyhook morning had apparently begun angry.

I liked consistency.

My shoulder liked nothing.

Better than yesterday.

Not good.

I tested it beside the bunkhouse while the tower crews watched their weighted strips.

Forward lift.

Fine until high.

Side lift.

Still complained.

Grip.

Sore, functional.

Right crutch.

Plant.

Weight.

Twist.

The dark resin marks and narrow reinforcement wrap held.

No creak.

No give.

Good.

Vessa watched from three paces away.

"Probably?"

"Still probably."

"Any worse?"

"No."

"Any numbness?"

"No."

"Then probably can go."

There.

Not heroic.

Medical science.

Probably.

Aven shouted from board.

"Quarter bell!"

The three strips stayed green.

Wind moved through Greyhook hard enough to make every hanging chain talk.

Not loud exactly.

Constant.

Iron against iron.

Cloth snapping.

Wood knocking.

People raising voices because the ridge stole half of every sentence.

Kel came out wearing the weighted scarf he'd bought yesterday.

Sori pointed.

"Still stupid."

A gust hit her sideways.

She caught the wall.

"Where was the seller?"

Kel smiled.

"Market closed."

"I hate you."

"No you don't."

"Currently flexible."

Dask had tied his pencils to his coat.

Individually.

Three little cords.

I stared.

He noticed.

"Wind."

"I know what wind is."

"Then why are you looking?"

"I'm learning what fear looks like in a pencil."

Maro laughed into his sleeve.

Vessa said, "Load check."

We did.

My bag sat close against body.

Quietener wrapped inside with maintenance card and two short copper leads.

Tavi's striped rock farther down.

Knife.

Water.

Food.

Nothing loose.

No stupid decorative vane.

Regret.

Aven shouted again.

"Half bell! Window open!"

Everything changed quickly.

Not panic.

Work.

A gate on the north stair opened.

Two freight crews moved first toward the lower mule shelf.

Our cases had already been transferred to that line before dawn.

We carried only personal load and one compact record case Sori refused to surrender.

Vessa checked each of us.

"Crown to shelter one. Shelter one to stair. Stair to shelter two. Shelter two to north gate. Nobody races the window. If red call comes, nearest shelter. If chain bell rings, clip if you have clip, hold if you don't. Greg?"

"No clip."

"Emergency chain with both hands if stopped."

I looked at crutches.

"That would be exciting."

"Avoid excitement."

"Plan already established."

Aven handed us six green cloth tags.

"Tie visible. North gate counts tags, not faces. If you lose one, tell them before they decide the wind ate you."

I tied mine to bag strap.

Sori tied Dask's because his knot was apparently insulting.

"It was secure," he said.

"It was a measurement knot."

"That's not a thing."

"It looked like one."

We climbed to crown.

Then Greyhook ended.

Not literally.

Emotionally.

The ridge ahead had no interest in people.

North Reach ran like a narrow back of red-black stone between two enormous drops.

West side fell into pale broken country full of dry gullies and low cloud shadow.

East side dropped sharper into a green-gray basin I couldn't see the bottom of.

The maintained path was maybe eight feet wide at first.

Sometimes twelve.

Sometimes five.

Anchor posts stood every thirty paces or so, iron sunk into stone, heavy chain sagging between them at knee to waist height depending ground.

Not a railing.

Too low and too far from path in places.

Emergency infrastructure.

Something to reach if walking stopped being walking.

Wind shelters looked tiny from crown.

Stone boxes with one side open away from prevailing gust.

One maybe half mile out.

Second beyond stair.

North gate invisible.

I had crossed worse.

First life.

Different body.

Different world.

That fact used to annoy me.

Now it was just accounting.

Vessa said, "Maro first. Kel second. Greg center. Sori. Dask. Me rear. Ten paces spacing until shelter one."

Nobody argued.

We crossed.

First twenty paces were easy.

Of course.

The wind wanted confidence before collecting tax.

Crutch tips clicked against stone.

Right.

Left.

Foot.

Not actually right-left-foot.

My body knew sequence better than language did.

Two points ahead.

One behind.

Weight centered low.

Do not let upper body chase gust.

Let shoulders move a little.

Keep pelvis boring.

Boring pelvis survived weather.

That was probably not a proverb.

Could be.

At forty paces first real gust hit from west.

Not push.

Impact.

My coat flattened against side.

Bag tugged.

Right crutch unloaded for half heartbeat.

I let it.

Didn't snatch for ground.

Left crutch and right foot held triangle.

Then right tip came down again.

There.

Current body.

Current competence.

Nobody said anything.

Good.

Maro raised fist ahead.

Stop.

We stopped.

Not because danger.

Because a freight line below was moving.

Far under west lip, three mules walked narrow shelf with linked cases between handlers.

Wind carried no hoof sound up.

Made them look fake.

Tiny animals moving without noise.

Dask behind me said something.

Wind stole all of it.

I turned.

"What?"

He shouted.

Still nothing.

Sori yelled, "HE SAID THE WEST VANE MOVED!"

I looked back toward Greyhook.

One tower arm shifted.

Green strips stayed green.

Fine.

Dask shouted again.

Sori shouted, "HE KNOWS!"

This system was excellent.

We moved.

Path narrowed.

Six feet.

Then five.

Chain closer on west side.

I hated chain being west side.

If gust pushed east, chain was behind direction of travel.

Probably intentional for prevailing wind.

Today wind mostly west.

Fine.

Mostly was a dangerous word.

Shelter one came after enough walking that shoulder had started warming rather than hurting.

Good sign.

We entered one at a time.

Inside, silence hit almost as hard as wind outside.

Not true silence.

Stone hummed.

But conversation returned.

Kel said, "I can't hear myself think out there."

Dask said, "I can."

Sori: "Tragic."

Vessa checked time strip nailed inside shelter.

Simple weighted ribbon protected by glass.

"Window still green. Five minutes water."

I drank.

Shoulder assessment.

Warm.

Not worse.

Palm okay.

Right crutch wrap still sound.

Maro leaned against back wall.

"Next section worse."

"Encouraging," I said.

"You want lie?"

"Depends quality."

"Next section has pastries."

"Terrible."

"I don't practice."

Outside, chain bell rang once.

Everyone looked.

Vessa was already at opening.

Second ring.

Not alarm pattern.

Aven had explained.

Two isolated rings meant gust front passing Greyhook crown.

Three fast meant stop and shelter.

Continuous meant clip or hold.

We waited.

No third.

Vessa watched dust farther west.

A brown sheet moved across lower gullies.

Not toward us exactly.

Parallel.

"Two minutes," she said.

Nobody complained.

There.

Weather wasn't plot.

Weather was schedule.

We left.

Next section had no pastries.

Maro had lied.

Path angled down along east face.

Wind changed because ridge changed it.

Instead of steady west pressure, gusts curled upward.

That was worse.

Crutch tips got light without warning.

Not sliding.

Unweighting.

I shortened stride.

Crutch.

Crutch.

Foot.

Pause when needed.

Kel ahead turned once.

I gave thumbs-up.

He kept going.

No babysitting.

Good.

At first stair, Vessa changed order.

"Greg behind Kel. Sori behind Greg. Maro takes bottom."

Stone stair cut twenty-three steps down across exposed face.

Chain at outer edge.

Inner wall shoulder-high first half, knee-high second.

Wonderful.

I looked.

Old Greg brain offered six ways to descend quickly.

Current shoulder rejected four.

Current body rejected another.

One remained.

Fine.

"Backward?" Kel asked.

"Sideways."

I turned toward wall.

Right foot down.

Left crutch lower step.

Right crutch same level as foot until weight transferred.

Then down.

Slow.

Stable.

Wind shoved coat against wall.

Useful for once.

Step nine.

Ten.

Eleven.

Shoulder complained.

Not dangerous.

Twelve.

Thirteen.

A gust hit upward.

Right crutch tip skipped half inch.

Stopped against stair lip.

My body froze exactly long enough.

No more.

Sori behind said nothing.

Good.

I reset.

Continued.

At bottom Maro waited.

"Fine?"

"Yes."

"Actually?"

I rolled shoulder once.

"Six breaths."

Vessa heard from above.

"Take ten."

"Abuse of authority."

"Nine now."

I shut up.

Ten breaths.

There.

Patient survived becoming crosser.

We moved.

Second shelter was smaller.

Also occupied.

Two Roads workers sat inside with a coil of bright orange rope between them and a broken wind-strip frame on floor.

One woman had nose packed with cloth from a bleed.

The other man was eating something that smelled aggressively of onion.

"Room," woman said.

We squeezed.

Eight people in stone box built for maybe six.

Dask immediately looked at broken frame.

Then looked at owner.

Progress.

"May I?"

Woman followed his eyes.

"No."

"Okay."

More progress.

She pointed at my repaired crutch.

"Dog?"

Regional gossip again.

"Yes."

"Three Posts?"

"Unfortunately."

Onion man said, "Heard you killed six."

"Killed none."

"Heard eight."

"Still none."

"That's less interesting."

"For hounds, better."

Woman snorted and immediately regretted it because nose.

"Fuck."

Sori handed her clean cloth.

"Thanks."

Vessa asked, "North section?"

Woman became work again.

"Crosswind stronger after marker forty. Not red. Tower call still green. One loose chain between fifty-two and fifty-three, flagged blue. Don't use it. North gate knows."

"Any debris?"

"Small grit. Nothing with ambition."

Good phrase.

I kept it.

A bell rang outside.

One.

Pause.

One.

Not pattern.

Dask counted anyway.

Of course.

Road woman said, "Greyhook crown settling. You're fine."

Vessa waited another minute.

Then we went.

North section widened briefly.

That was trap emotionally.

Because wider path meant stronger wind.

Nothing broke it.

Sound disappeared almost completely.

Not quiet.

Pressure.

Air roaring so evenly it became absence.

I could see Kel's coat snap and hear almost none of it.

Sori's mouth moved once.

No sound.

She stopped trying.

Good.

We communicated by hands.

Maro fist.

Stop.

Palm low.

Crouch slightly.

Point west.

Gust.

We waited.

Dust and tiny stone grains skittered across path.

One hit cheek.

Then another.

Nothing with ambition.

Accurate.

Maro dropped hand.

Move.

At marker forty-seven, wind shifted east for three breaths.

That was interesting.

Not magical necessarily.

Terrain.

Air.

Ridge.

Dask's head turned hard.

I saw question happen.

He didn't stop.

Also progress.

Marker fifty-two had blue cloth tied around chain.

Loose section sagged lower than others.

We stayed inner path.

No touch.

Marker fifty-four.

Fifty-five.

Then the wind stopped.

Completely.

Everyone stopped too.

Not because order.

Because wrong.

My coat fell against body.

Kel's weighted scarf hung dead.

Dust dropped.

For maybe two breaths North Reach became still.

Not sheltered.

Still.

I felt skin tighten.

Dask looked at Vessa.

Maro looked at sky.

Vessa raised fist.

Hold.

No bells.

No tower signal visible this far.

Three breaths.

Four.

Then wind returned from west with a slap.

We leaned.

Normal.

Vessa pointed forward.

Move.

Good.

No investigation in middle of exposed ridge.

That was how people died while being curious.

North gate appeared after marker sixty.

Stone wall across widened shelf.

Low building.

Two wind masts.

Three people waving us in.

We crossed final stretch.

My shoulder hurt now.

Not injured-new.

Used.

Palm sore.

Breathing harder.

Still controlled.

North gate worker counted green tags before faces.

"Six!"

Vessa answered, "Six!"

Gate shut behind us.

Not dramatic.

Wood and iron.

Then I was inside lee wall and suddenly heard everyone again.

Dask said, "At marker fifty-five the wind velocity approached zero for approximately four breaths."

Sori said, "Hello to you too."

"It did."

"We survived. Say hello first."

He looked at me.

"Hello."

"Wrong person."

I sat on first available bench.

"Acceptable effort."

Vessa checked me.

"Shoulder?"

"Used. Not worse in a new way."

"Hand?"

"Sore."

"Crutch?"

I planted right one twice.

Solid.

"Rusk wins."

Kel pulled off weighted scarf.

Sori held hand out.

He stared.

"What?"

"I want to try it."

"You called it stupid."

"I contain growth."

He gave it to her.

North gate worker came over.

Older man, broad face, missing half an eyebrow.

"Survey?"

Vessa showed papers.

He read.

"Merecross. Long way."

"Getting longer."

"That's roads."

He pointed north.

"Your cases come mule shelf in maybe an hour. North Reach Station is down slope, not up. Don't follow the tower road unless you want weather people yelling at you."

"Route beyond?"

"Station will brief."

Dask said, "Was the still-air interval at marker fifty-five expected?"

Worker's face changed a little.

Not fear.

Recognition.

"You got one?"

"Approximately four breaths."

"Huh."

"What is it?"

"Usually nothing."

I hated usually.

Pella had taught me that.

Worker scratched missing eyebrow side.

"Reach gets dead pockets. Wind folds wrong around stone. Most are one breath, two. Four's longer. Tower crews record them."

"Magical?" Dask asked.

"Weather."

"That doesn't answer."

"It answers enough for breakfast."

I liked him.

Vessa said, "Name?"

"Holl Renn."

Dask turned.

Holl looked at him.

"No relation that I know."

"I hadn't asked yet."

"You turned around."

Dask closed mouth.

Excellent.

We waited for cases.

North side of Reach was not what I'd expected.

I hadn't expected anything specific.

Still.

Greyhook's dry red ridge fell away into a high basin full of dark grass and white stone ribs.

Not marsh.

Not forest.

Open rolling country.

Wind moved across grass in huge visible sheets.

Far north, something silver cut horizontally across basin.

River maybe.

Road definitely.

And beyond that, low blue hills layered into distance.

More world.

There it was again.

That feeling.

Carrow existed behind me.

Merecross behind that.

Bell Ford.

Alder Basin.

West Locks.

Three Posts.

Lark Spur.

Greyhook.

Names accumulating.

Not replacing home.

Adding to map inside me.

I had wanted a door that didn't lead home before dinner.

Apparently the world had taken that personally.

The mule cases arrived forty-eight minutes later.

Dask knew because of course he did.

No damage.

One seal dusty.

Sori checked custody.

Kel checked straps.

Vessa got station directions.

We descended.

North Reach Station sat half mile below gate behind a long white stone windbreak.

Bigger than Three Posts.

Smaller than Lark Spur.

Two bunkhouses.

Stable.

Signal mast.

Survey office.

Public cookhouse.

And something I had not seen in days.

A proper bath sign.

I stopped.

Maro nearly walked into me.

"What?"

I pointed.

He followed finger.

"Bath?"

"Bath."

"You've seen bath."

"Not recently enough."

Sori came beside us.

"Assignment covers?"

Vessa, ahead, didn't turn.

"Basic wash only. Hot soak personal."

There.

Economy returned.

I looked at sign.

**BASIC WASH - ASSIGNMENT / ROAD CREW**

**HOT BASIN - 1C**

One copper.

I had fifteen.

Vale had twenty-three of my future.

My shoulder hurt.

My hand hurt.

I had crossed a wind ridge on crutches.

This was not existential.

This was value.

I said, "I'm buying the bath."

Sori looked at me.

"Immediately?"

"Before anyone invents work."

Vessa finally turned.

"Briefing first."

"Cruel."

"Then bath."

Acceptable.

Inside survey office, station lead laid map on table.

Her name was **Ira Fen**.

Thin woman with silver hair cut to chin and ink on three fingers.

She spoke like every sentence had already been edited once before leaving her mouth.

"You crossed during a clean window?"

Vessa said, "Mostly. Four-breath dead pocket near fifty-five."

Ira wrote that without reaction.

"Common enough. Long enough to log."

Dask visibly relaxed because someone had finally used acceptable wording.

Ira continued.

"Your north assignment changes here. Merecross requested observational continuity through North Reach, then discretion for extension if regional conditions justify. They do."

There.

Something moved.

Not wind.

Story.

Vessa leaned over map.

"What conditions?"

Ira tapped three places.

"False tones at West Locks. Three Posts arm drift. Greyhook dead pockets longer than seasonal average. North basin has a different problem."

Dask inhaled.

Ira looked at him.

"Don't connect them yet."

He exhaled.

Good woman.

She tapped silver line I'd seen from gate.

"This is the **Lume River**. North road crosses at **White Span**. For four mornings, the river has reflected sunrise from the wrong direction."

Silence.

Ira added, "Only for several minutes. Physical sun remains east. Reflection appears west-facing on calm water. No heat change. No known navigation effect. Local ferrymen hate it."

I looked at map.

New problem.

Different grammar.

Good.

"How far?" Vessa asked.

"White Span twelve miles by road. Halfway settlement at **Kettle Vale**. You can make Kettle Vale tomorrow if you leave first bell. White Span day after."

There.

Not home.

Farther.

Ira said, "You have two choices. Remain here one day for North Reach observations, or continue under extension and take the basin route."

Vessa looked at crew.

Not just me.

Good.

Kel shrugged first.

"Road's open."

Maro said, "I haven't seen Lume in years."

Sori looked at Vessa.

"Custody okay if station stamps extension."

Dask said nothing.

That was suspicious.

Then: "I would like to observe the river."

Of course.

Vessa looked at me.

Shoulder sore.

Hand sore.

Fifteen copper.

One bath waiting.

Home farther south than it had ever been in this life.

"Greg?"

I looked at map.

Kettle Vale.

White Span.

Lume River.

Wrong sunrise.

Then north beyond it.

"Bath first," I said.

Vessa's mouth twitched.

"Then?"

"Then north."

There.

Crosser.

Apparently one ridge wasn't enough.
