TEXT READER · CHAPTER 404

THE COMPARER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Hedda Morn woke us with a spoon against a pot.

Not bell.

Spoon.

Three hard strikes from farmhouse kitchen.

Dask sat upright in hammock and hit head on beam.

"Fuck."

Hedda shouted through wall.

"Breakfast!"

Sori opened one eye.

"Efficient system."

I had slept in bed.

Rank had privileges.

Or disability.

Same result sometimes.

My shoulder was stiff when I first moved it.

Not sharp.

Not new.

I rolled it slowly.

Forward.

Side.

Better than Greyhook.

Hand closed around crutch grip without complaint until last bit of squeeze.

Also better.

Right crutch wrap still dark where Rusk's resin had filled tooth marks.

Still ugly.

Still mine.

I stood.

No drama.

Breakfast was oats with pear.

Of course.

Pear bread.

Pear preserve.

And something Hedda called orchard tea that tasted like hot bark remembering fruit.

Kel drank twice.

"Good."

Hedda looked at him.

"No."

He drank third time anyway.

Maro asked road condition.

"Dry," Hedda said. "East ditch soft after marker six. Don't step in it."

Dask said, "Why marker six specifically?"

"Because that's where ditch is soft."

"I mean what caused it?"

"Water."

"From where?"

Hedda pointed spoon toward ceiling.

"Sky."

Dask looked at me.

I ate pear.

No help.

We left first bell.

Low Fen Orchard disappeared behind black-barked trees in twenty minutes.

Bellweather road stayed close to Lume now.

Closer than yesterday.

Sometimes river only thirty yards away beyond willow scrub.

Sometimes road climbed white shelf and water dropped below us, broad and brown-green with silver where morning touched it.

No wrong sun.

Physical sun east.

Reflection where geometry expected.

Good.

Or boring.

Boring was data.

Dask looked at river every opening.

Sori finally said, "If you turn your head any farther, it comes off."

"I am observing."

"You're walking into Maro."

He corrected course.

Maro said, "Observed."

I liked route crew.

At second mile, old sight posts became frequent.

Not every hundred yards.

Maybe every half mile where road and river aligned.

Stone uprights.

Iron frames.

Some empty.

Some held blackened oval plates.

One had been repurposed to hang laundry.

Dask stopped.

Vessa did not.

He caught up.

Growth.

Maro explained while walking.

"Bellweather used mirrors before bank lamps got cheap. Fog sits low. Boatmen couldn't always see far shore. Sight plates caught dawn or lamp and gave line."

"Directional silvering?" Dask asked.

"Mirror."

"Treatment could have directional response."

"Mirror."

"Those statements aren't mutually exclusive."

"Still mirror."

Sori said, "I'm putting that on your grave."

Maro considered.

"Fine."

At marker six, ditch was soft.

We didn't step in it.

Revolutionary.

Bellweather announced itself by sound.

Hammers.

Bells.

Boat horns.

People yelling numbers.

Then smell.

Tar.

Coal smoke.

Wet wood.

Fish.

Something sweet frying.

Then town.

Bellweather Crossing was bigger than Westbank and Kettle Vale combined.

Not Carrow-big.

Different.

Long instead of dense.

Town stretched along both banks with bridge at center and ferry docks above and below.

Main bridge was iron-and-stone, three broad arches, newer than White Span.

Upstream stood older crossing remains.

Two massive stone piers with no deck between them.

Each pier carried a tall frame of silvered plates arranged like vertical fan.

There.

River mirrors.

Not one.

Dozens.

Most blackened.

Some bright.

One missing.

Dask made noise.

Not word.

Noise.

Sori said, "Control yourself."

"I am."

"Worse."

A road clerk met us at west checkpoint.

Woman named **Pera Lonn**.

Short.

Round glasses.

Blue ink stripe down left sleeve.

She read Ira's extension paper twice.

"White Span wrong dawn."

Vessa said, "Observed six mornings total, two outside-witness mornings."

"And you brought Renn."

Dask brightened.

Pera pointed at me.

"Him."

I sighed.

"Why?"

"Dog story."

"Zero dead."

"Heard fifteen."

"Inflation."

Pera looked at crutches.

"You fought fifteen dogs on those?"

"I did not fight fifteen dogs."

"How many?"

I thought about actual moving hounds, threats, contacts, numbers in reeds.

"Enough."

Vessa said, "We're here for mirrors."

Pera nodded.

Work replaced gossip.

"Good. Because if you're here for sunrise, you missed it."

"We know."

"Better. I hate visitors who think dawn waits for paperwork."

She stamped extension.

"Bellweather archive keeps mirror logs. Old north pier still has six serviceable plates. South pier nine. We do not use them for navigation anymore. Maintenance checks twice yearly because heritage office pays us to pretend rust has feelings."

Dask inhaled.

Pera held up finger.

"No touching without keeper."

He exhaled.

"Understood."

"Archive first. Keeper after lunch."

Good.

No repeated dawn.

Comparison by records and infrastructure.

Bellweather archive occupied second floor above ferry office.

Stairs.

Of course.

Twenty-eight.

I counted because body did.

Sori noticed because she notices everything annoying.

"Need stop?"

"No."

At top shoulder warm.

Fine.

Archive smelled like dust, river damp, and old leather.

Pera handed us to **Olden Marr**, archivist.

He was maybe seventy, narrow as ruler, white hair tied with red string.

He wore fingerless gloves despite warmth.

First thing he said:

"Do not call them magic mirrors."

Dask looked personally attacked.

"I haven't."

"You have face."

I liked Bellweather.

Olden pulled four ledgers.

"Dawn sights. Forty-two years surviving. Earlier burned in ferry-office fire."

"Any wrong-direction reflections?" Vessa asked.

Olden looked over glasses.

"If there were, I assume that's why you're here."

"Yes."

"Then no."

Simple.

Dask said, "None?"

"None recorded."

"That isn't same as none occurring."

Olden's expression softened slightly.

"Correct. You may stay."

There.

Dask passed exam.

We split ledgers.

Not read every page.

Thank gods.

Olden had indexes.

Season.

Visibility.

Mirror faults.

Fog.

Glare.

False lamp.

Sun alignment.

Boat incidents.

We searched terms around reflection anomalies.

There were many normal problems.

Morning glare blinding eastbound pilot.

Fog doubling lamps.

Ice on plates creating split highlights.

Bird shit.

More bird shit.

A note from twenty-three years ago:

**SOUTH ARRAY SHOWED TWO DAWNS. CLEANED GULL RESIDUE. RESOLVED.**

Dask stared.

"Technically two dawns."

Olden said, "Technically gull shit."

Sori laughed loud enough to get shushed by another clerk.

Useful absence accumulated.

Forty-two years indexed.

No persistent west-facing sunrise on river.

No multi-minute wrong-direction surface reflection matching White Span description.

No record of bridge shadow failing to extinguish a false river reflection.

No recurring calm-water-only west band.

That did not prove Bellweather couldn't do it tomorrow.

But it mattered.

White Span looked more local.

Or newer.

Or unnoticed elsewhere.

Or dependent on geometry Bellweather lacked.

Many options.

Good.

Olden brought a map.

Lume bent differently here.

At White Span river ran east-southeast.

Bellweather crossing sat on long north-south bend before river turned east again.

Old mirror arrays faced southeast and southwest from old piers.

"Why two directions?" I asked.

Olden tapped map.

"Morning fog. One array caught dawn. Other caught bank lamps. Later both caught lamps depending season."

"So not symmetrical use."

"No."

Dask said, "Were plates differently treated?"

"Yes."

Maro looked betrayed.

Olden continued.

"Morning plates had blue-black backing. Lamp plates red-brown. Silver wash same family. Different backing reduced glare."

Dask looked at Maro.

Maro said, "Mirror."

Fine.

We went to lunch.

Ferry office fed assignment crew lentil soup and fried river cakes.

I bought nothing.

Thirteen copper survived noon.

Pera joined us halfway through.

"Keeper available. North pier only. South access closed because someone dropped wrench through ladder yesterday and decided gravity was management's problem."

Kel said, "Was it?"

"Eventually."

North pier access was ground-level walkway from bank to old stone base.

No climbing.

Good.

Keeper **Jori Pell** met us there.

Maro looked at him.

Jori looked at Maro.

"No."

Maro blinked.

"I didn't ask."

"Everyone asks."

Dask said, "Are you related?"

Both men said, "No."

Excellent.

Jori was younger, maybe twenty-seven, thick black hair and one gold tooth.

He carried maintenance satchel full of cloths, oil, gauges, and a little square viewing hood.

Six serviceable north plates remained on old fan array.

Each oval roughly forearm long.

Silver face slightly smoked.

Back dark blue-black.

Frames could rotate limited arc and tilt.

Stops prevented full turn.

Not mystical.

Engineering.

Old engineering was still engineering.

Jori demonstrated without changing stored calibration.

He opened one viewing hood around lower plate.

"Cuts side glare. Look from mark."

Brass foot mark embedded in stone.

I looked.

Plate reflected bright patch of southern sky and thin line of river.

Move head two inches.

Reflection changed sharply.

Directional by geometry and hood.

Not magic necessarily.

Dask looked after permission.

Sori after him.

Maro.

Kel.

Vessa.

Then Jori said, "White Span people describe light staying under bridge shadow?"

"Yes," Vessa said.

"Mirrors can't tell you about that."

Dask said, "Not directly."

"Not indirectly either unless you invent story."

I liked him too.

Jori showed backing.

Blue-black layer had fine mineral flecks.

"Glare cut. Helps dawn edge show in fog."

"Magical treatment?" Dask asked.

Jori shrugged.

"Silver wash takes charge during making. Backing takes some. Once made, plate mostly plate. You want guild answer, ask mirror guild. You want ferry answer, it reflects things."

Maro smiled.

Vindicated.

I asked, "Any plate ever show source not actually in front of it?"

Jori scratched chin.

"Cracked ones can double. Bent ones shift. Backing peel makes ghosts. Fog makes idiots."

"Persistent wrong direction?"

"Not if plate healthy."

"River surface?"

"River does what river wants."

Not answer.

Accurate.

Dask asked to inspect maintenance records.

Jori pointed back toward archive.

"Olden has them."

Dask visibly suffered.

We had just left archive.

Vessa said, "Later if needed."

There.

Restraint.

Then something useful happened.

Not anomaly.

Money.

Pera found us at pier.

"Regional office sent pay clarification. Extension days count as field days."

Sori immediately said, "Starting day four."

Pera nodded.

"Yes. Your original route language. Two copper per completed field day after third. Merecross already paid departure six. North extension didn't void terms."

I stopped looking at mirror.

There.

Economy.

"How many completed paid days do you have logged?" Sori asked.

Pera checked slip.

"Three additional since threshold, through yesterday. Today pays on completion."

I did arithmetic.

Six copper accrued.

Not paid yet.

Today would make eight accrued after workday complete.

Sori looked at me.

"Don't spend it before paid."

"I wasn't."

"Your face."

Fuck everyone knowing my face.

Pera said, "Bellweather can settle regional extension pay tomorrow morning if Vessa signs tonight's completion. Or carry ledger onward."

Vessa said, "Settle here."

Good.

Tomorrow potentially eight copper.

Thirteen to twenty-one.

Vale still twenty-three.

Interesting.

Not rich.

But closer to something.

Artifact markets got different when purse crossed twenty.

I did not say that.

Sori would hear anyway.

She narrowed eyes.

I looked innocent.

Poorly.

We spent late afternoon comparing Bellweather's present river surface to White Span notes.

Not formal anomaly watch.

Just ordinary observation.

Wind light southwest.

Water moderate ripple.

Real sun west now because afternoon.

Reflections normal.

WS-F/9 plate stayed in bag.

No reason.

Quietener stayed in bag.

Even less reason.

Good tools did not demand use.

That was maybe most important thing about owning them.

At fifth bell, Vessa closed workday.

Pera signed local completion.

Sori copied pay note.

Dask asked one final question about backing mineral composition.

Olden sent him to mirror guild three streets uphill.

He looked at Vessa.

"Tomorrow," she said.

"Before departure?"

"Maybe."

He accepted maybe like man swallowing bone.

Bellweather lodging was above old ferry tavern.

Six beds.

No hammocks.

Luxury.

Dinner not included.

Assignment rate covered room, not food.

There.

Economy again.

Tavern board:

**STEW + BREAD - 2 BITS**

**FRIED RIVER CAKES - 3 BITS**

**MEAT PIE - 5 BITS**

Bits.

Still no universal conversion.

I had bits.

Unspecified amount.

Annoying even internally.

I bought stew and bread.

Paid in bits.

Copper remained thirteen.

No need to invent conversion because cashier knew and I didn't care enough.

Freedom.

Sori sat across from me.

"You're pleased."

"Food."

"Pay."

"Also pay."

"Eight tomorrow if signed."

"Apparently."

"Twenty-one."

"Stop."

"Vale twenty-three."

"I know."

"Almost symmetry."

"Debt is not collectible set."

"You collect worse things."

She had point.

Kel joined with meat pie.

"What's next after Bellweather?"

Vessa sat down at end table with Pera's route note.

"Regional office gave two options."

Of course.

She unfolded paper.

"One: turn south along old ferry road toward Merecross network. Not Carrow directly. Two: continue east to **Sable Quay**, where Lume widens into trade basin."

Dask appeared from nowhere.

"Sable Quay has mirror guild."

Vessa stared.

"How do you know?"

"Olden."

"Of course."

Maro sat.

"How far?"

"Eleven miles. Road good. One night likely at **Rill House** or camp depending pace."

Sori asked, "Operational reason?"

Vessa read.

"Bellweather comparison suggests White Span behavior not historically common here. Regional office wants records delivered to Sable Quay survey annex because Quay handles lower-Lume traffic and has broader sight-system archive. Extension authorized if crew willing."

There.

Again.

A legitimate door.

Not quest.

Paperwork.

Best kind.

Kel said, "East."

Maro nodded.

Dask was already spiritually in mirror guild.

Sori looked at me.

"Don't say market."

I hadn't.

Now I was thinking it.

Sable Quay.

Trade basin.

Eleven miles farther from home.

Twenty-one copper tomorrow if pay settled.

Dangerous combination.

Vessa said, "Greg?"

I ate last bread.

Shoulder low ache.

Hand almost normal.

Crutch sound.

Bag slightly heavier than when I left Carrow.

Map much heavier.

"East," I said.

Sori sighed.

"He means market."

"I mean east."

"Same face."

Fuck my face.

Outside, old Bellweather mirrors caught evening lamps one by one.

Not wrong light.

Just light.

That mattered.

White Span had shown us something strange.

Bellweather showed us what forty-two years of ordinary strange looked like when people bothered to write it down.

Glare.

Fog.

Cracks.

Ice.

Bird shit.

Normal failures.

Normal explanations.

Comparison did not solve the wrong sunrise.

It did something better.

It made the mystery smaller without making it less interesting.

Local.

Maybe.

New.

Maybe.

Geometry-dependent.

Maybe.

At least not obviously everywhere.

That was enough.

Tomorrow I would collect eight copper.

Then we would go east.

Apparently that was what my life did now.

Compare one place to another until map became too large to pretend I was only visiting.
