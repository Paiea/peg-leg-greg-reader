TEXT READER · CHAPTER 349

THE TALLYMAN

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Sea work started before sunrise because apparently oceans also hated reasonable hours.

I reached East Hook in dark blue light with Maro carrying breakfast and Sori carrying opinions.

Dask was asleep.

Coward.

Venn Marine Recovery already looked awake.

Lanterns along quay.

Recovery barge loaded.

Divers checking lines.

Riggers testing winch brake.

Nali stood on wet planks with one foot planted on broad split sole of her marine limb and other on crate.

Clipboard under arm.

Coffee in hand.

She looked at me.

"Early."

"You said before first bell."

"Most people hear 'before' and negotiate emotionally."

"I can complain while punctual."

"Good."

Tavren came from office.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"Today you tally."

Four copper possibility.

There.

He pointed to long board hanging beside launch ramp.

Columns.

**ITEM**

**LIFT POINT**

**EST. WET WEIGHT**

**ACTUAL CRANE SCALE**

**CONDITION CLASS**

**CUSTODY**

**YARD DESTINATION**

"You keep shore-side load tally and condition intake," Tavren said. "Nali owns condition calls. Jerr owns rigging. You do not tell divers how to dive. You do not tell crane how to crane."

"Understood."

"If counts disagree, stop handoff before object leaves marked lane."

"Yes."

"If temple mark appears, nobody lifts unless observer present."

"Yes."

"Personal effects separate."

"Yes."

"If you get clever, do it quietly enough that nobody dies."

"Reasonable."

He walked away.

Nali said, "That was basically affection."

"I'm learning dialect."

Merry Wake lay outside east breakwater.

I never saw whole wreck.

Only marker buoy and recovery lines disappearing into gray water.

That was more interesting somehow.

Ship had sunk eight years ago after hull breach in winter storm.

No heroic legend.

No famous treasure.

Courier ship.

Cargo.

Mail.

People.

Insurance fight afterward lasted longer than vessel had sailed.

Now salvage rights finally sold.

Venn had contract for specified hardware and cargo-room safes.

Families still held claim windows on identified personal effects.

Temple cargo excluded pending observer.

Nothing clean.

Good.

First lift came up as steering quadrant segment.

Heavy bronze arc crusted green.

Divers had tagged **MW-SH-1**.

Jerr's crane crew landed it inside yellow intake lane.

Scale called eighty-six wet.

I wrote eighty-six.

Not eighty-five because it looked like eighty-five.

Growth.

Nali checked ownership stamp under growth.

Courier company mark.

Condition class three.

Mechanical salvage.

Yard rack B.

Simple.

Second lift was loose fittings basket.

Third was one safe.

Safe mattered.

Small iron box, maybe knee high, two lifting eyes, one bent.

Chain came tight.

Jerr swore.

"Hold."

Crane stopped.

Diver signal line twitched twice.

Runner shouted from rail.

"Bottom snag."

Jerr adjusted angle.

I watched.

Not my craft.

After three minutes safe rose another foot.

Then something came with it.

Canvas.

Dark bundle snagged around lower corner.

Nali saw before me.

"Stop."

Crane stopped.

Bundle broke surface.

Cloth wrapped around long narrow shape.

Not cargo packing.

Too careful.

Jerr said, "Personal?"

Nali did not answer.

"Lane C," she said. "No unwrap."

Safe and bundle landed together.

Ownership problem immediately.

Safe belonged salvage contract if cargo-room manifest matched.

Bundle might not.

I wrote both as one lift but two intake objects.

Nali glanced.

"Good."

Little things.

Paid little things.

A yard clerk named **Hessa Brin** came over from custody table.

Not my Hessa.

Probably.

Did not ask.

She wore blue cord around sleeve meaning family-claim custody.

"Bundle goes sealed rack."

Nali nodded.

A man outside yellow rope shouted.

"That's mine."

There.

Human problem arrived before breakfast finished.

Man maybe forty.

Weathered coat.

Red eyes.

Not drunk.

Or not only drunk.

He pushed to rope.

Worker blocked him.

"Back."

"That is my brother's."

Nali looked at him.

"Name?"

"Toren Vale."

Of course Vale.

Sori, farther back, physically turned away.

I almost laughed.

Wrong moment.

Toren pointed at bundle.

"That wrap. My mother stitched those blue corners. Eren carried charts in it."

Hessa Brin said, "Claim registry?"

"Filed six years ago."

"Number?"

"I don't carry dead brother's paperwork in pocket."

"Then we verify before release."

"You have it right there."

"And if you're wrong?"

He slapped chest.

"I'm not."

Could be true.

Could be grief making certainty.

Could be both.

Nali said, "Nothing leaves wet intake until cataloged."

Toren looked at her.

"I've waited eight years."

There it was.

Procedure versus somebody's life again.

Different shape.

Not emergency.

Still sharp.

"I know," Nali said.

"No, you don't."

Her face did not change.

"No. I know policy."

That answer was honest.

Also bad.

Toren's eyes went to bundle.

"You tear that open and dump his things on table, I swear to every god in this harbor..."

Hessa Brin stiffened.

"Nobody said dump."

"That's what yards do. Inventory. Photograph. Assign lot."

"Family effects don't become auction lot."

"Unless you decide it isn't his."

There.

He wanted one thing badly.

Not value.

Not money.

He wanted brother's bag back intact.

Custody process wanted identity established before release.

Both reasonable.

Nali looked at me.

Not because I was authority.

Because I was holding tally.

"Record dispute. Do not move bundle until custody review."

I wrote exactly that.

Toren saw.

"You're writing?"

"Yes."

"Write it's his."

"I can't."

"Of course."

Tessa echo.

Everybody knows how to explain why they can't help.

I hated that line now.

Useful line.

Hated it.

I looked at bundle.

Blue corners visible.

Canvas water-dark.

One brass strap buckle under growth.

Maybe ownership mark without opening.

"Can we examine exterior only?" I asked Hessa.

She looked at Nali.

Nali looked at custody line.

"No opening," Hessa said. "No cutting. No contents exposure. Exterior handling permitted with witness if crane lane clears."

Better question.

Not solution.

We moved bundle separately onto slatted table.

Toren stayed behind rope.

Hessa present.

Nali present.

I did not touch until told.

Exterior had blue cross-stitch corners.

Four.

One mostly rotted.

Buckle greened over.

Leather strap stamped faintly.

I wet cloth and cleaned only stamp face.

Letters:

**E. VA...**

Could be Vale.

Could be Varn.

Could be half a dozen names.

Toren saw from rope.

"Eren Vale."

Hessa said, "Maybe."

He flinched like she'd hit him.

I understood why.

Maybe was accurate.

Still cruel word after eight years.

I looked at stitch pattern.

"Claim file might include description?"

Hessa nodded slowly.

"If family filed identifying details."

"Can runner pull file while object stays sealed?"

"Yes."

"Do that."

Toren snapped, "I told you."

"I know."

"Then why file?"

"Because if it matches your claim, nobody has to open it to prove enough."

He stopped.

There.

Not perfect.

Possible.

Hessa sent runner.

We resumed recovery.

Work did not pause for grief.

That felt wrong.

Also necessary.

Second safe came up clean.

Then steering linkage.

Then two sealed brass cylinders from cargo locker.

Portable charge screen showed no active response.

Temple observer had not arrived yet.

No temple seal.

Standard cargo.

Condition three.

Rack C.

My board filled.

Wet weight.

Dry estimate blank until later.

Custody marks.

Dispositions.

Then diver bell rang hard.

Three fast.

Every worker changed.

No shouting first.

Just movement.

Jerr locked crane.

Tender crew hauled signal.

Nali was already toward ladder.

I did not know code.

Maro did not either.

Sori went still.

A diver surfaced wrong.

Too fast.

One arm over line.

Helmet half flooded.

Tender grabbed harness.

Second diver came up behind helping.

They got first onto platform.

Worker yelled, "Leg cut!"

Blood in water.

Not much visible.

Enough.

Tavren appeared from nowhere.

"Clinic. Now."

No Greg.

No hero.

Qualified people moved.

I backed lane clear.

That was useful.

Then I saw problem on my board.

Current lift line still attached below.

Crane load status uncertain.

If emergency crew forgot, somebody might move equipment with live line.

I wrote **HOLD - DIVER INCIDENT / LINE STATUS UNKNOWN** in giant letters and clipped red tag to crane tally hook.

Jerr saw.

Nodded.

"Good."

Tiny.

Useful.

Diver's name was **Pella Ro**.

Cut from torn hull edge through suit at calf.

Not amputation.

Not yet anything dramatic.

Clinic cart took her.

Nali returned five minutes later with wet hands.

"Stable enough. Deep cut. They think tendon spared."

She flexed her own left knee absent-mindedly.

Not symbolism.

Worker knowledge.

"Recovery pauses until line inspected," she said.

Jerr agreed.

Tavren looked at sky.

Money disappearing by minute.

Nobody argued.

Procedure clean because danger clear.

Human cost still there.

Pella would lose work days at minimum.

Maybe more.

Dock injury pool covered some.

Nali said it without speech.

Another reason limbwrights existed.

Another reason funds existed.

Not because harbor romantic.

Because harbor hurt people often enough to build institutions around damage.

Toren Vale was still waiting.

Runner returned during pause with claim file.

Hessa read.

"Blue cross-stitch corners, four. Brass buckle stamped E. VALE. One leather chart wrap, narrow. Mother listed as Sela Vale."

Toren stopped breathing.

Hessa looked at bundle.

Then at file.

"Exterior match sufficient for provisional family identity."

"Provisional?"

"We still need signature and release witness."

"But no opening?"

"No opening required for identification if you accept sealed contents as-is."

He nodded too fast.

"Yes."

Hessa said, "If later there's someone else's property inside, you inherit reporting obligation."

"Yes."

"If there is hazardous material, you report."

"Yes."

"If seal breaks before release stamp..."

"I won't touch it."

His voice cracked.

There.

He did not thank us.

Good.

He shouldn't have to.

He had waited eight years for people doing their jobs.

Hessa moved bundle to family rack under seal.

Toren walked beside rope like it might disappear again.

I watched too long.

Nali tapped board.

"Tallyman."

"Right."

Work resumed after line inspection.

No hidden monster.

No ancient curse.

Just ship broken under water and humans trying to extract pieces without adding bodies.

By midday I understood why recovery paid better than simple records.

Every object crossed three systems at once.

Weight.

Condition.

Ownership.

Sometimes hazard.

Sometimes memory.

Sometimes all of them.

A corroded hinge was hinge until stamp made it evidence.

A sealed bag was canvas until man behind rope recognized mother's stitching.

A bronze cylinder was scrap until temple seal said absolutely not.

That one arrived after lunch.

Temple observer still twenty minutes away.

Diver sent up slate first:

**MARK FOUND / LOWER LOCKER / TEMPLE SUN-THREE**

Tavren immediately canceled lift.

Client representative objected.

First time I met him.

**Sel Marr**, salvage-rights broker.

Clean boots.

Bad sign in recovery yard.

"Contract says cargo-room recovery."

Tavren said, "Temple exclusion says mark stays submerged pending observer."

"It's already rigged."

"Then it can stay rigged."

"We're paying diver time."

"Yes."

"Every minute costs."

"Yes."

"You could bring it to surface and leave sealed."

"No."

Sel looked at Nali.

"Your view?"

"No."

Then at me.

Why me?

Because new face sometimes looked flexible.

"You did records on this contract?"

"Today."

"Does bringing sealed cargo up alter custody?"

I knew enough now.

Not enough to answer broad.

"Contract says stays underwater until observer. Surface violates literal condition."

"Literal."

There was disdain in word.

"Yes."

"Do you think it matters?"

Human question disguised as technical.

"I don't know why temple required it."

"Exactly."

He smiled like I'd helped.

"So maybe it doesn't matter."

"Or maybe that's why it does."

Smile gone.

Good.

Sel said, "You people love uncertainty when somebody else pays for it."

Toren echo in different clothes.

Everybody had line.

Tavren said, "Observer is twelve minutes out now."

"Twenty became twelve."

"Time works."

Sel swore and walked away.

No grand conflict.

Just money versus custody rule whose reason wasn't visible.

Correct procedure won this one.

Maybe rightly.

Still cost somebody.

Observer arrived eleven minutes later.

Temple woman named **Iri San**.

She checked mark underwater through diver slate and surface lens.

Then authorized lift into separate blue-rope lane.

Reason for underwater rule turned out boring and sensible.

Certain old temple shipping seals used water-pressure fracture paint. If broken cargo was lifted dry before observer saw pattern wet, evidence could vanish.

There.

Unknown reason had actual reason.

Sel looked annoyed.

Not embarrassed.

People rarely rewarded universe for proving them wrong.

Object came up.

Ceramic chest with sun-three seal, one corner cracked.

Fracture paint feathered around crack underwater-blue.

Iri photographed/sketched before drying.

Then custody transfer.

I wrote what I was told.

Not story of gods.

Condition.

Custody.

Weight.

By late afternoon my right hand cramped from writing.

Shoulder held.

Crutches were annoying on wet planks because every tally move meant board, pencil, crutch, body.

More hands.

Again.

Nali moved through same yard with clipboard in one hand and pry bar in other.

Her marine limb clicked softly on wet boards.

Not magic.

Not effortless.

Twice she stopped to retighten socket strap.

Once she sat and rubbed stump through canvas because day was long.

Still had two hands.

I wanted one more than morning.

Badly.

Tavren reviewed tally at sunset.

He checked weights against crane sheet.

One mismatch.

Mine said forty-two.

Crane sheet forty-seven.

I traced item.

Loose fitting basket had drained before my second notation.

Wet scale initial forty-seven.

I had copied later rack weight forty-two into wrong column.

Mistake.

There.

Not hypercompetent perfection.

I corrected with strike-through, initial, note.

Tavren watched.

"Why?"

"Wrong column. Rack weight after drain."

"Could've made crane sheet wrong."

"Could have. It wasn't."

He nodded.

Next.

No shame ceremony.

Good workplace.

Nali checked condition entries.

"Useful."

Tavren checked red hold tag after diver incident.

"Who put that?"

"Me."

"Good."

Then family-dispute note.

"You solved?"

"No. Claim file matched exterior enough for custody to release sealed later."

"Better answer."

He counted coins.

Four copper.

Assigned load tally and useful records.

No evening inventory.

Exactly contract.

Twenty-three to twenty-seven.

There.

Four copper closer to silver I could not yet count toward because conversion remained unstated.

Still closer.

Maybe.

Definitely more money.

Tavren said, "Tomorrow same wreck. Weather good. Pella out. Crew short. You do same unless Nali changes."

"Okay."

"Third day maybe quay collapse if divers finish."

"Okay."

He looked at my crutches.

"Masta fitting you?"

"Not yet."

"Saving?"

"Trying."

"Don't rush deposit."

Family trait.

"She said same."

"She usually right when expensive."

"Good to know."

He left.

Nali sat on crate loosening side stay on her limb.

"First full day?"

"Yes."

"Feet hurt?"

"One foot. Hands more."

She laughed.

"Work leg won't fix work."

"I've been lied to."

"Masta definitely didn't lie."

"No."

She rubbed scar line through socket edge.

"You still want one?"

I looked at board.

Wet yard.

Two crutches leaning against crate.

My hands sore from moving everything around them all day.

"More."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"Means you saw bad parts too."

There.

That mattered.

She strapped limb back.

Stood.

"Tomorrow bring gloves. Yard issue pair sucks."

"I don't own recovery gloves."

"Then use yard pair and complain. Harbor tradition."

I could do that.

At Anchor's End that night, Dask had three mirror pamphlets and no new mirror.

Sori looked at four copper.

"Twenty-seven."

"Yes."

"Still not enough."

"Yes."

"You look happy."

"Annoying, isn't it?"

Maro asked, "Good day?"

I thought about Pella bleeding into sea.

Toren waiting beside rope.

Temple chest staying underwater while money burned.

One wrong number on my tally.

Four copper.

Nali rubbing stump at end of shift.

"Useful day," I said.

That was more accurate.

Tomorrow before first bell again.

Merry Wake still under water.

My leg still missing.

Money still not enough.

Fine.

All three were things I could work with.
