TEXT READER · CHAPTER 401

THE RETAINER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Duskport beer did not improve with sleep.

That was first finding.

Second finding: head mostly fine.

Stomach annoyed.

Mouth tasted like burned rope had filed complaint overnight.

Useful.

I drank water.

A lot.

Sori was already downstairs at Anchor's End with tea and face of person who had won by suffering less.

"Morning."

"No."

"You look fine."

"I am fine. Beer is not."

Maro sat across from her eating eggs.

"I slept well."

"Traitor."

Dask arrived last carrying mirror pamphlet and looking exactly normal.

"I think bitterness may be preservative-adjacent rather than flavor-forward."

Sori pointed spoon at him.

"You are banned from describing breakfast."

Good.

I ate bread.

Kept it.

Then East Hook.

Final trial day.

That mattered more than beer.

Weather had changed.

Rain gone.

Wind up.

Not dangerous yet.

Enough to wrinkle harbor water and slap loose canvas against frames.

Tavren stood at yard gate with slate.

"Merry Wake delayed."

Nali looked past him toward sea.

"Outside chop too ugly?"

"Divers can work. Crane barge can't hold clean enough for safe heavy lift until turn."

He looked at me.

"Quay collapse."

There.

Different job.

Good.

East Hook lower service quay had lost twelve feet of retaining face overnight.

Not whole quay.

Section.

Old timber crib behind stone had rotted, one tide seam opened, then yesterday's rain and overnight wash took enough fill that front edge slumped.

No one injured.

Two handcarts lost.

One storage cage tilted into water.

And three businesses immediately disagreed over what counted as emergency recovery versus private property preservation.

Excellent morning.

Tavren pointed at map.

"We stabilize retrieval lane only. Harbor Works owns quay. We recover objects if authorized. We do not repair wall."

Nali added, "No one steps beyond red chalk. Ground under intact stone may not be intact."

Jerr had riggers there already.

Pella still absent.

Different diver today, **Oss Renn**, no relation to Dask unless universe hated me.

I did not ask.

Growth.

My assignment:

shore tally.

condition intake.

load sequence board.

Not crane command.

Not structural decision.

Tavren said, "If Works changes boundary, you rewrite board before line moves."

"Yes."

"If owner screams, you still rewrite board."

"Likely yes."

"Good."

We went.

Collapse looked worse up close.

Stone face had folded inward like broken teeth.

Water sucked through gaps.

Mud under surface moved brown with each surge.

A green-painted storage cage leaned half-submerged against remaining wall.

One handcart wheel stuck above water.

Second cart gone.

Three owners stood behind rope arguing before crew arrived.

**Lena Vos**, rope chandlery.

**Perrin Holt**, lamp-oil warehouse.

**Serr Arlo**, courier storage.

Lena wanted cage first because it held imported dry rope.

Perrin wanted submerged oil lockbox before water compromised seals.

Serr wanted courier dispatch chest because it contained route documents and customer deposits.

Harbor Works inspector **Toma Kess** wanted none moved until void edge was sounded.

Everybody had reason.

No obvious villain.

Again.

Toma put iron sounding bar against stone three feet behind red chalk.

Hollow.

Moved back another foot.

Solid.

Red line shifted.

I erased board.

Rewrote staging.

Lena said, "Every minute rope sits wet costs me."

Toma said, "Every minute worker stands on hollow quay costs leg."

That shut conversation for almost three seconds.

Then Perrin said, "My oil box is already underwater."

Serr said, "My chest has names and deposits."

Tavren looked at water.

"Recovery order follows access and hazard, not who shouts loudest."

Lena opened mouth.

"Shout if you want. Doesn't change line."

She closed it.

I liked him.

First problem was green cage.

Visible.

Accessible from water.

Heavy enough that crane needed clean angle.

Rigging team set sling from stable side.

Nali had me prepare condition lane because cage might split under lift.

I set tarps.

Tags.

Two empty baskets.

Crutches annoyed me immediately.

One hand for slate.

One hand unavailable because crutch.

Then switch.

Then brace slate against chest.

Then move.

Slow.

Nali walked past carrying pry bar and tags in both hands.

Wet-deck foot today again.

She noticed eyes.

"Work."

"Working."

"Stare after."

Fair.

Lift began.

Green cage came up crooked.

One lower corner caught broken stone.

Jerr stopped.

No drama.

Line held.

Oss reached from skiff, shifted sling angle.

Second lift.

Cage cleared.

Then bottom seam opened.

Rope coils started dropping.

Not expensive magic.

Just thirty years of gravity waiting.

Jerr yelled, "Catch basket!"

This was not my lane.

But catch basket was beside me.

I could move it.

I hooked one crutch over shoulder, grabbed basket with free hand and dragged, ugly and fast, into tarp lane while Nali caught second side.

Two coils landed in.

Third hit mud.

Fourth stayed cage.

Good enough.

No hero.

Just hands shortage.

Again.

With peg I could have carried basket instead of dragging.

Noted.

Later.

Cage owner Lena crossed arms.

"How much ruined?"

Nali said, "Not my decision. Condition first."

I recorded:

four rope coils visible.

Two basket-caught, wet.

One mud-contact.

One cage-retained.

No assumption about sale loss.

Lena hated that.

"They're wet."

"Yes."

"Write ruined."

"I can't establish ruined."

"You can see them."

"I can see wet."

She looked ready to murder epistemology.

Nali said, "He writes wet. You argue value with insurer."

Lena swore.

Fair.

Then oil lockbox.

Perrin's.

Smaller.

Submerged under collapsed cart frame.

Oss found it.

But before lift, charge screen from skiff gave faint amber on latch.

Perrin said, "It's lamp oil. No charge."

Nali said, "Box disagrees."

"It has anti-spark seal."

There.

Useful.

Not dangerous magic necessarily.

Anti-spark seal could retain tiny charge.

Nali changed handling class to low residual known-use if mark matched.

Mark did.

Lift approved with no opening.

I learned local hazard vocabulary without pretending surprise.

Then Serr's courier chest.

Divers couldn't find it.

That became problem.

Serr had seen it at midnight before collapse.

Said chained to inner post.

Oss found post.

Chain cut.

Chest absent.

Current could have moved it.

Could be under debris.

Could have been removed before collapse.

Could have drifted into basin.

Serr's face changed.

"It was there."

Toma said, "I believe you. Doesn't make it here now."

"There are deposits in it."

"How much?" Sori asked.

She had come after noon with Maro, mostly to see if I still had head.

Serr looked at her.

"Eleven customers. Fourteen copper total. Documents more important."

Not huge by Duskport market.

Huge if customers thought courier stole money.

He wanted full search immediately.

Tavren had contracted stabilization recovery until third bell.

Full basin search would mean extra diver time, likely push Merry Wake turn window, cost crew, maybe no result.

Again.

Human wants.

Correct procedure had no clean answer.

Serr said, "I'll pay."

Tavren said, "Not about only pay."

"Then what?"

"Diver limit. Current. Other booked work."

"So my customers lose deposits because you're busy?"

Tavren did not flinch.

"Maybe."

Serr laughed bitter.

"You all love maybe."

That hit.

Tessa.

Same line shape.

Different person.

I hated it.

Sori saw me notice.

I looked at collapse map.

Not for clever answer.

For actual options.

"Can Harbor Works sweep downstream grates?" I asked Toma.

"Already scheduled after wall secure."

"Today?"

"Likely before dusk."

"Can recovery mark chest description to them?"

"Yes."

"Can East Basin watch post?"

Tavren said, "Harbor watch can circulate missing property notice."

Serr said, "That's not search."

"No," I said.

I did not pretend.

"It's not."

He looked at me.

"Then don't sell it like one."

"I'm not."

Silence.

Better.

Tavren chose:

No extended diver search.

Current too poor near collapsed face.

One additional ten-minute sweep within safe lane only.

Then stop.

Harbor Works grate sweep + watch notice + recovery description.

Serr hated it.

Accepted because what else.

Ten minutes found nothing.

That stayed unresolved.

Good.

Around second bell, wind shifted.

Crane barge outside breakwater signaled Merry Wake window opening early.

Tavren had choice.

Keep all crew on quay collapse through third bell.

Or split and send recovery barge team to wreck now.

Short crew because Pella out.

Jerr said he could take three riggers plus Oss.

Nali said condition intake at yard could handle delayed returns.

Tavren looked at me.

"You can hold quay board with Toma?"

There.

More responsibility.

Not structural authority.

Not crane.

Board.

Records.

Owner sequence.

"Yes."

He looked at Nali.

She nodded.

"He can."

That meant something.

Tavren split crew.

He went Merry Wake with Jerr.

Nali stayed half bell, then returned yard for incoming.

I stayed quay with Toma and two recovery hands.

First time Tavren was not physically there.

No Vessa.

No Tavren.

Toma owned safety boundary.

I owned recovery board/tally.

Easy.

Until wall moved.

Not collapse.

Sound.

Deep wooden crack under stone.

Toma's head snapped.

"Back."

Everybody moved.

One owner didn't.

Perrin stepped toward oil box staging because he thought box was slipping.

I said, "Back."

He said, "It's moving."

"Back."

He took another step.

I did not lock in fully.

Didn't need retirement fund.

Just louder.

"Perrin. Back now."

Something in voice worked.

He stopped.

Toma grabbed his coat and pulled him behind second line.

Three seconds later one intact-looking paving slab dropped six inches into void.

Not dramatic explosion.

Worse.

Proof.

Perrin went white.

His oil box had not moved.

Shadow had.

Toma re-marked boundary another six feet.

Recovery halted.

That killed last cart attempt.

Perrin said nothing.

Then, quietly:

"Thank you."

I said, "Toma heard it."

"You yelled."

"Also true."

No sermon.

Work resumed only after Toma approved alternate lane.

We finished what could finish.

At third bell, Tavren returned wet and tired.

Merry Wake had produced second safe and one steering linkage.

No injuries.

Good.

He checked quay board.

Read every correction.

Serr missing chest notice.

Boundary shifts.

Canceled cart lift.

Perrin incident.

Green cage partial loss.

Then looked at me.

"You change safety line yourself?"

"No. Toma did. I rewrote board after."

"Tell owner ruined rope?"

"No. Wet/mud contact only."

"Tell courier chest stolen?"

"No. Missing after collapse."

"Direct Perrin?"

"Told him back after Toma called back."

Toma said, "He listened second time."

"Eventually," I said.

Tavren nodded.

That was review.

Then pay.

No custody inventory tonight.

Four copper.

Thirty-two to thirty-six.

There.

Trial total had become:

Two half-day.

Four first full.

Five second.

Four third.

Fifteen copper earned at Venn Marine Recovery.

Not fortune.

Real.

Tavren put coin pouch away.

"Trial done."

There.

I waited.

Didn't sell myself.

He hated that probably less.

"You staying Duskport?"

"For now."

"How long?"

"At least long enough to make bad financial decisions about leg."

Nali, beside him, snorted.

Tavren looked at left knee.

"Masta?"

"Yes."

"Travel?"

"Probably."

"Expensive."

"Yes."

"Good."

I blinked.

"Good?"

"Cheap limb that fails under load costs more."

Fair.

Then offer.

Not permanent.

Not guild.

Not charity.

**Open temporary recovery hand, condition/tally class.**

Five-day block.

Minimum three full assigned days if weather/work supports.

Base 3c useful records day.

4c when load tally assigned.

5c with signed evening custody inventory.

Same restrictions.

No diving.

No rigging authority.

No salvage claim rights.

If yard has no work, no pay.

If Greg leaves for fitting appointment, tell them before morning board if possible.

Tavren said, "You're useful."

Vessa phrase.

Different person.

Same high praise.

"I'll take it."

"Starts tomorrow second bell. Merry Wake if sea holds, East Hook inventory if not."

"Second bell?"

"You worked three early days. Sleep."

Beautiful employer.

Nali said, "Also Masta wants you tomorrow before first bell."

I looked at her.

"Why?"

"She sent runner. Said if you're serious, she wants proper measurements before swelling patterns change with more yard work."

There.

Not deposit.

Not fitting start.

Measurements.

"Cost?"

"Assessment included if you eventually buy. One bit if you waste her morning."

"That's fair."

Sori appeared from behind stacked cage.

"You're going."

"Apparently."

"Good."

I counted current money mentally.

Thirty-six copper.

Vale twenty-three.

Travel leg one silver plus eighteen.

Still far.

But now there was open work block.

And Masta wanted real measurements.

That changed target from fantasy shopping to project.

Dangerous.

Good.

We walked back toward Anchor's End.

Duskport smelled like salt, tar, and brewery smoke.

Somewhere Black Keel existed.

I did not go.

Growth.

Maro asked, "Beer?"

"No."

Dask said, "Could compare another label."

"No."

Sori said, "Miracle."

I looked down at crutches.

Then at hands.

Both occupied.

Tomorrow morning Masta would measure what could replace some of that occupation.

Not leg.

Tool.

Option.

Then second bell, back to work.

I liked shape of it.

Earn.

Measure.

Work.

Save.

Maybe fit.

Not dramatic.

Serious.

For first time since Duskport, I had something close to plan.

Naturally, that made me suspicious.
