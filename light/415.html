TEXT READER · CHAPTER 410

THE CANDIDATE

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Masta Venn measured everything.

That included things I had not known could be measured.

"Stand straight."

"I am."

"No. You're standing crutch-straight."

"Different?"

"Very."

She tapped left hip.

"You load away from this side before you think about it."

"Reasonable."

"Useful. Also problem."

There.

Morning.

Before first bell.

Hark & Venn smelled like leather and hot glue and whatever oil Masta used on brass fittings.

I had eaten.

She asked.

Apparently that mattered.

I had also not had beer.

She did not ask.

Probably because Duskport could smell its own crimes.

Masta had cleared long bench and brought out strips of cloth, wax pencil, calipers, leather tape, a wooden measuring fork, two temporary support blocks, and one expression that suggested I was furniture with opinions.

"Pants up."

I rolled left leg cloth above knee.

She looked at stump.

"Any soreness from yard?"

"No skin breakdown. Mild pressure after long crutch day."

"Where?"

I pointed.

Lateral upper edge.

She marked it.

"Scar sensitivity?"

"Low."

"End pressure?"

"Depends."

"Good answer."

She pressed gently.

Different places.

Asked each time.

No ceremony.

No sympathy face.

Good.

Circumference at several heights.

Length from knee line.

Knee flexion.

Extension.

Hip line.

Pelvis level.

Right leg length.

Crutch height.

Even shoe sole thickness.

"Why shoe?"

"Because if I build left side to imaginary floor and right boot adds half inch, you walk like roof."

"Fair."

She had me stand between rails.

Right foot centered.

Hands lightly on rails.

Then she placed padded block under left stump and raised it by thin wooden plates.

One.

Two.

Three.

"Tell me when pelvis feels level."

"That's not reliable."

"Exactly. Tell me anyway."

I hated craft people.

"There."

She looked.

"You're wrong."

"Excellent test."

"You think level is low left because that's your normal."

There.

That landed.

She raised one more plate.

"Now?"

"Too high."

"Good."

Dropped half.

"Now?"

I waited.

Body argued.

Then settled.

"Strange."

"That means close."

She checked hips with measuring bar.

"Close."

Interesting.

Current body had spent years building a map around absence.

A fitted leg would not simply add support.

It would force map to change.

Masta wrote numbers.

"You overprotect left side."

"I have no foot there."

"Yes. That's why."

"Insightful."

"Pay me silver and I have more."

I liked her less.

She moved on.

Temporary socket shell next.

Not finished socket.

Open leather-and-canvas cup with straps.

"This is not fitting."

"You keep saying that."

"Because people hear 'measurement' and think they're walking by lunch."

"I'm not people."

"Worse."

She wrapped stump in thin sock, seated temporary cup, tightened straps one at time.

Pressure.

Odd.

Not pain.

Contained.

That was best word.

I had spent years with air below knee.

Now something held shape around it.

Body disliked novelty.

Not exactly fear.

Attention.

Every nerve suddenly had meeting.

"Pain?"

"No."

"Numbness?"

"No."

"Burning?"

"No."

"Pressure?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

I pointed.

She marked shell.

Loosened.

Shifted pad.

Retightened.

"Better?"

"Different."

"Useful word."

Then support post.

Just plain adjustable wooden shaft under socket.

No foot.

No finished hardware.

She locked it to rail fixture so it could not slide.

"Load ten percent."

"How do I know ten?"

"You don't. That's why I watch."

Great.

I shifted.

Right leg immediately tried to keep almost everything.

"More left."

I shifted.

"Too much shoulder."

"Shoulders aren't on leg."

"Your whole body is on leg."

There.

Again.

I relaxed arms.

Shifted pelvis.

Pressure moved down temporary socket.

Not bad.

Not comfortable.

Real.

"Hold."

I held.

Five breaths.

Ten.

Fifteen.

Muscles I used constantly still found new complaint.

Left hip working differently.

Lower back adjusting.

Right calf relaxing slightly.

Hands doing almost nothing.

That was the thing.

Hands.

Both free on rails rather than bearing body.

I lifted right hand completely.

Then left.

Masta immediately said, "Don't perform."

"I wasn't."

"You were about to."

Probably.

I put hands back.

She had seen enough.

"Travel leg, yes."

There.

Simple.

"Not basic peg?"

"You could use one. Wrong tool if you plan roads, wet yards, ships, stairs, carrying, fighting, whatever nonsense face says."

"Face says nothing."

"Face says expensive mistakes."

Rude city.

She pulled stool.

"Your main problems aren't stump length or knee. Those are good. Your problem is habit. You unload left side before danger exists. Pelvis sits a little low left when tired. Right side overworks. Crutches reinforce some of that because they make it functional."

"So leg fixes it."

"No."

Of course.

"Leg gives you chance to train different pattern. If you wear badly, you can build worse pattern faster."

"Good."

"Also socket volume will change during day. Yard swelling. Heat. Salt. Long standing. First month means adjustments."

"How many?"

"Enough."

"Helpful."

"Could be three. Could be nine."

There.

Not product.

Process.

I asked, "Would you still quote one silver eighteen?"

She looked at measurements.

"Yes for travel work build as described. Maybe plus small material difference if you choose reinforced side stay after trial shell. I don't know yet."

"Deposit still twenty?"

"Yes."

Thirty-six copper in purse.

Could do it.

Still no.

Not yet.

"How much do I need liquid before you think I'm not being stupid?"

She smiled slightly.

First time.

"That's not my job."

"You had opinion last time."

"Last time you'd have one copper. Easy opinion."

Fair.

I thought.

Lodging.

Food.

Return route.

Vale.

Uncertain workdays.

Silver balance unknown in copper terms because denomination structure here still not mine to invent.

Twenty-copper deposit would leave sixteen.

Better than one.

Still maybe thin if work stopped.

"Not today," I said.

Masta nodded.

No disappointment.

"Good. Measurements stay six weeks before I remeasure everything. Major body change, injury, skin problem, come sooner."

"No charge?"

"Not if you eventually buy. One bit if you disappear forever and wasted morning."

"Threatening."

"Business."

She handed me small card.

**GREG / LEFT BKA / TRAVEL-WORK CANDIDATE / BASELINE TAKEN**

Below that numbers I did not understand yet.

Real.

Not mine.

Not leg.

Still real.

I put it in shoulder bag.

"Second bell," she said.

"Work."

"Then stop standing here."

Good morning.

East Hook at second bell had no wreck lift.

Wind still wrong outside breakwater.

Instead Tavren pointed inland.

"North Pier warehouse strike."

I looked at him.

"Strike?"

"Not labor strike. Crane strike."

"Ah."

"Hook hit upper beam. Part of loading gallery collapsed. No injuries. Cargo trapped. Harbor inspector cleared one lane."

Different again.

Good.

North Pier warehouse belonged to **Kerrin & Sons Provisioning**.

Three floors.

Stone lower.

Timber upper loading gallery over quay.

One crane boom had swung in gust during morning transfer and smashed through gallery corner.

No one hurt because warning bell sounded before secondary collapse.

Now half upper platform hung at angle.

Sacks, crates, two copper-bound ice cabinets, and one long white case remained inside cleared zone.

Harbor inspector owned structure.

Kerrin owner wanted perishables first.

Insurance assessor wanted nothing moved until damage plates finished.

Dock master wanted quay lane reopened before evening tide because two provisioning ships were due.

Of course.

People.

Tavren said, "We remove what inspector authorizes. Greg, board and tally. Nali condition. Sesh rigging."

New rigger **Sesh Om**.

Short woman, enormous forearms, braided beard ornaments despite having no beard.

Actually hair braids tied under chin because wind.

Looked beard-like.

I did not comment.

Growth.

Inspector **Meren Cole** had chalked green retrieval corridor across lower floor.

Anything outside green stayed.

Owner **Jase Kerrin** hated green.

"Ice cabinets first."

Meren said, "One is outside corridor."

"Contents are worth thirty copper by tonight."

"Floor under it is cracked."

"Can rig from above."

Sesh said, "No."

Jase looked at her.

"You haven't looked."

"Looked. Still no."

Efficient.

Second cabinet was inside green.

We took that first.

Then food crates.

Then long white case.

White case had no obvious ownership mark.

Just three blue dots and brass end caps.

Nali stopped intake.

"Cold?"

I held hand near without touching.

"Slightly."

She screened.

Green.

Then amber at center.

"Not open here."

Jase said, "That's mine."

Nali said, "Mark?"

"Provisioning inventory."

"Paper?"

He called clerk.

Clerk brought manifest.

Long white case listed as **LEASED PRESERVATION ROD / NORTH COAST COLDWORKS / RETURN DUE 17TH**.

Not Jase's property.

Lease equipment.

Interesting.

"Need Coldworks custody notice," I said.

Sori would be proud.

Nali nodded.

Jase did not.

"I need it working."

"Maybe," I said.

He glared.

"You people love maybe?"

Tessa line hit memory.

There.

Different person.

Same frustration.

I did not get defensive.

"Yes," I said. "Because I don't know if it works."

He stared.

Then looked away.

No clever fix.

We tagged leased equipment and sent runner.

Meanwhile second ice cabinet remained outside green.

Jase kept watching it.

Thirty copper by tonight.

Real loss.

Meren kept line.

Real collapse risk.

No one became wrong.

At third bell, upper platform creaked.

Not loud.

Enough.

Meren raised fist.

Everyone stopped.

Then another crack.

Sesh said, "Out."

We moved.

I backed on crutches.

Bad direction for speed.

Current body knew.

Turn.

Right foot.

Crutches.

Move.

Nali was ahead carrying board and pry bar.

Both hands occupied.

Her left work leg planted and pushed clean.

Mine did not exist.

I moved anyway.

Fast enough.

Gallery corner dropped another foot after we cleared.

Dust.

Boards.

One sack burst.

No injury.

Meren closed green corridor entirely.

Jase swore.

Then stood very still.

His second ice cabinet was still inside.

Thirty copper maybe dying behind unsafe floor.

Nobody told him procedure was good news.

Tavren said, "We stop."

Jase said, "Can you recover from quay side?"

Sesh looked.

"Maybe after brace crew. Not now."

There it was again.

Maybe.

Human word.

Not cowardice.

Not answer.

Sometimes only honest bridge between no and yes.

Jase rubbed face.

"Fine."

He did not mean fine.

We packed.

Short day because structure closed.

Tavren still had another yard job after fourth bell, but only two hands needed.

He picked Nali and Sesh.

I expected no pay or maybe partial.

Temporary block said assigned days, not guaranteed full.

Tavren looked at board.

"Records useful. Half day plus stop not your fault. Three copper."

I blinked.

"Full base?"

"You were assigned full day. Harbor closure ended it. Yard absorbs closure."

Good employer again.

"Thanks."

"Don't thank payroll."

Thirty-six to thirty-nine.

There.

Thirty-nine copper.

Still no deposit.

Still target.

But card in bag now.

Baseline.

Candidate.

That word did something.

Not ownership.

Not promise.

Direction.

At Anchor's End, Dask was drawing three mirror diagrams on napkin.

Sori had letter.

Not home.

Harbor court runner.

She looked up when I entered.

"Harven made hearing."

There.

One thread.

"Outcome?"

"Appeal partially granted. Labor levy reduced from six months to two. Work ban stayed, review in ninety days."

Not clean.

Of course.

"He saw her?"

Sori nodded.

"Yes."

That mattered more than ruling for him maybe.

"Mira?"

"Runner note says quarantine accepted before third bell. Birds held observation overnight, contract not automatically void."

Two threads.

Not miracles.

Consequences moved.

"Tessa?"

Sori shook head.

No.

Still nothing.

Good.

Not good.

Honest.

Maro came in behind me with food.

"Work?"

"Three copper. Half day. Warehouse tried to become floor."

"Successful?"

"Partly."

He nodded like that was normal answer now.

I pulled Masta card from bag.

Set it on table.

Sori read.

**TRAVEL-WORK CANDIDATE / BASELINE TAKEN**

Her face changed.

"Oh."

"Yeah."

Dask immediately leaned.

"What are measurements?"

I covered card.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because you'll graph me."

"I wasn't going to."

"You absolutely were."

Maro looked at card.

"Deposit?"

"No."

Sori nodded.

"Good."

"Still good?"

"At thirty-nine? Better. Still not enough for me to stop judging."

"Comforting."

"You have lodging, food, Vale, uncertain work, and silver balance you still can't casually solve."

"I know."

"Good."

She pushed card back.

"But now it's real."

There.

Yes.

Real enough to measure.

Not real enough to own.

That was fine.

Probably.

I put card away.

Tomorrow board might have work.

Might not.

Five-day block had four more days.

Thirty-nine copper.

One silver plus eighteen.

Left side measured.

Body mapped wrong on purpose for years.

New map possible.

I wanted to see what it felt like.

Badly.

Which meant I had to keep doing boring thing.

Earn.

Save.

Listen.

Avoid falling through warehouse.

Reasonable plan.

Suspiciously reasonable.
