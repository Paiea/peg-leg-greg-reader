TEXT READER · CHAPTER 326

THE EARNER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

I did not have peg leg.

This became important because Dask thought I did.

"When do you get it?"

Breakfast.

No greeting.

Sori looked over tea.

"He hasn't paid deposit."

"I know. I mean when."

"When I have money."

Dask frowned.

"You have forty-three copper."

"Yes."

"Deposit is twenty."

"Yes."

"So you have money."

"I also enjoy food."

Maro buttered bread.

"And rooms."

"Occasionally."

Dask considered this as competing technical requirement.

"Right."

Pell Varo passed table.

"Peg?"

Apparently conversation had escaped.

"Future peg."

"Shame."

"Why?"

"Would improve name."

"What name?"

He kept walking.

"Greg."

I stared after him.

Sori smiled into tea.

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"You stared after him."

Fuck face.

East Hook board had no work for me.

That was first time since trial.

Merry Wake heavy lift delayed another half day.

North Pier brace crew still owned warehouse.

South Chain needed divers and ironworkers, not tally hand.

Tavren pointed at board.

"No assignment until maybe third bell."

No work, no pay.

Terms were terms.

"Anything else?"

"Not from me."

There.

Could wait.

Could wander.

Could put twenty copper on Masta's bench and feel productive.

I looked west toward Hark & Venn.

No.

Not because afraid.

Because deposit did not make balance smaller enough.

One silver eighteen remained one silver eighteen minus whatever deposit counted toward final.

Still silver.

Still Duskport costs.

Still Vale.

Still no conversion I trusted enough to pretend.

Tavren said, "Harbor labor board."

"What?"

"If you want day work. East Hook exchange under red clock. Tell them recovery condition/tally, not rigger."

"You recommending me?"

"No."

"Good."

"I'll confirm you worked here if asked."

Different.

Useful.

So I went.

Duskport labor exchange occupied covered square under enormous red-faced tide clock.

Three boards.

**SHIP**

**YARD**

**SPECIAL**

People clustered under each.

Sailors.

Porters.

Clerks.

Cooks.

Carpenters.

Riggers.

Two people with work hooks.

One man missing eye.

Woman with rigid left peg leaning against post while she ate apple.

Nobody looked twice at my crutches.

That remained strange enough to notice.

Clerk asked classification.

"Recovery condition and tally. Temporary Venn hand. Not rigging."

"Writing?"

"Yes."

"Weights?"

"Yes."

"Charge screening?"

"Record only unless supervised."

She looked up.

"Good answer."

Apparently Duskport rewarded not lying about jobs.

She stamped slate.

"Two openings. Grain lighter inventory, two copper half day. Or West Teeth storm stores, three copper if full count finishes before sixth bell."

"What's West Teeth?"

"Sea wall batteries."

"Weapons?"

"Storm hardware."

"What kind?"

"Ask them."

Three copper.

New part of city.

"West Teeth."

She handed token.

"Gate Seven. Ask for Kett."

I went west.

Duskport changed past shipyards.

Fewer taverns.

More stone.

Sea wall rose higher, thick enough for carts.

Every hundred yards sat squat towers with iron shutters facing ocean.

Not military.

Maybe once.

Now storm defense.

Gate Seven guard took token.

"Kett!"

Someone shouted back:

"Busy!"

Guard shouted:

"Labor!"

"Then send him!"

Good interview.

Kett Arlo was short, broad, gray-haired, and missing left hand above wrist.

Work hook attached today.

Not fancy.

Dark steel hook with leather cuff.

He looked at crutches.

Then token.

"Venn?"

"Temporary."

"Can count?"

"Usually."

"Bad answer."

"Yes."

"Better."

He pointed underground.

Stairs.

Of course.

West Teeth stores sat inside sea wall.

Dry chambers behind thick stone.

Rows of equipment:

storm anchors.

chain drums.

folding flood braces.

sealed lamp boxes.

blue-glass tide rods.

And long iron tubes with ceramic collars.

I stopped.

Kett said, "Don't touch those."

"Wasn't."

"Everyone says that while touching."

"What are they?"

"Breakers."

I looked at him.

"Storm breakers?"

"Wave-break charges. Not explosive. Push face off incoming surge if wall team times them right. Old ones can crack collar and push sideways. So don't touch."

Magic infrastructure.

Serious.

Not artifact market.

City equipment.

"Job?"

"Inventory before storm season. Numbers, seal condition, obvious corrosion, rack location. You see damage, mark. You do not test."

"Easy."

Kett stared.

"Leave."

"Sorry."

"Nothing in harbor easy."

Fair.

We worked.

Mostly boring.

Good boring.

Rack A storm anchors.

Rack B brace pins.

Rack C lamp boxes.

Two missing seals explained by maintenance chits.

One chain drum mislabeled east instead of west.

Three tide rods with cloudy glass, already condemned.

I counted.

Kett checked.

I learned West Teeth had twelve storm stations along outer wall.

During severe coastal surge, crews used mechanical barriers first.

Breakers only when wave timing and wall load crossed threshold.

Expensive charge cells.

Dangerous if mistimed.

Duskport had lost twenty-seven feet of old seawall in storm thirty-one years ago.

After that city stopped pretending stone alone was enough.

"How many people work this?"

"Storm day? Hundreds."

"Regular?"

"Forty-two across wall. More maintenance contracts."

He adjusted hook cuff with teeth and right hand.

I watched.

Not because missing hand.

Because technique.

He caught look.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Bullshit."

Duskport hated privacy.

"I'm getting fitted. Maybe."

He looked at left knee.

"Hark and Venn?"

"Masta."

"Good."

"Everyone knows her?"

"Everyone missing something knows someone who knows her."

There.

Harbor ecosystem again.

"How long hook?"

"This one? Twelve years. Hand gone nineteen."

"Work easier?"

"Some."

"Worth it?"

"Some days."

I waited.

He sighed.

"You want speech?"

"No."

"Good. Hook holds rope. Carries buckets. Pulls doors. Doesn't feel. Gets cold. Catches shit. Leather rubs. I take it off when stump swells. Better than no hook for work. Worse than hand. That's all."

"That's enough."

"Should be."

Back to count.

Near fourth bell, we found problem.

Rack H.

Wave-breaker collars.

Inventory sheet said twelve.

Rack held eleven.

No maintenance chit.

No transfer mark.

Kett stopped joking.

"Count again."

I did.

Eleven.

He checked adjacent rack.

Nothing.

"Seal log."

Door seal intact from morning.

Previous night seal intact.

Last full inventory eight days ago: twelve.

Could be paperwork.

Could be moved before seal.

Could be theft.

Could be maintenance without chit.

Could be one dangerous piece of storm hardware somewhere it should not be.

Kett closed room.

"Nobody leaves section."

There.

Human problem became security problem.

Guard captain arrived.

**Lera Morn**.

Tall.

Very formal speech.

"Who discovered discrepancy?"

"He did," Kett said.

She looked at me.

"State your count and actions."

"Sheet twelve. Physical eleven. I recounted once. Kett independently checked adjacent rack. We checked maintenance/transfer chits at station. None present. We checked door seal log. Current and prior night seals recorded intact. We did not open or test remaining collars."

"Did you touch rack contents?"

"Only tag rail and inventory board."

"Did you observe any person handling Rack H before discovery?"

"No."

"Do you have any theory?"

"Several possibilities. No evidence choosing one."

She nodded.

"Thank you."

No praise.

Good.

Then everyone got searched.

Including me.

Bag opened.

Quietener.

WS-F/9.

Tavi rock.

Masta card.

Guard held striped rock.

"What is this?"

"Rock."

"Purpose?"

"Child gave it to me."

She looked at me.

"Sentimental property."

"Yes."

She returned it carefully.

Good guard.

Nothing found.

Kett was furious.

Not at me.

At missing collar.

"Those don't walk."

"People do."

"Helpful."

"Sorry."

Search expanded to maintenance cages.

Then cart records.

I kept out unless asked.

At fifth bell, maintenance runner arrived holding paperwork.

Not collar.

Paperwork.

Transfer order.

Rack H unit moved three days ago to **North Wall Station Two** after cracked ceramic found during visual check.

Order had been filed under *breaker tubes*, not *collars*.

Physical move legitimate.

Rack sheet stale.

No theft.

No missing dangerous hardware.

Kett swore for long time.

Lera Morn did not.

She said:

"Classification mismatch caused security response. Correct both indexes."

That was scarier than swearing.

Kett looked at me.

"You still get paid."

"Good."

"Why wouldn't he?" Lera asked.

"People get weird when day becomes paperwork."

"Then people are wrong."

I liked her too.

Duskport dangerous.

We finished count before sixth bell.

Barely.

Three copper.

Forty-three to forty-six.

Not Venn money.

Mine anyway.

Labor board work.

That mattered.

I could earn outside one employer.

Option.

Again.

At gate Kett said, "You come back tomorrow, maybe work."

"Maybe?"

"Storm stores don't need emotional commitment."

"Good."

"But Venn pays better when they have work."

"Also good."

"You saving for leg?"

"Apparently whole city knows."

He grinned.

"Peg-Leg Greg."

There.

I stared.

He laughed.

Not cruel.

Just delighted with himself.

"That's terrible."

"It's excellent."

"I don't even have peg."

"Future-proof."

"Fuck you."

He laughed harder.

Guard at gate heard.

"Peg-Leg Greg?"

"No."

Kett said, "Yes."

"No."

Guard smiled.

"See you, Peg-Leg."

Fuck.

I left West Teeth.

By time I reached Anchor's End, I had almost forgotten.

Then Pell Varo looked up.

"How was work, Peg-Leg Greg?"

I stopped.

"How."

He pointed at porter drinking near door.

Porter pointed at another porter.

Other porter shrugged.

Duskport information moved faster than plague.

Sori was at table.

Already laughing.

Maro looked confused.

Dask looked delighted.

"It scans," he said.

"What scans?"

"Peg-Leg Greg. Three beats."

"I know how words work."

"Also descriptive."

"Not currently."

Sori wiped eyes.

"That's what makes it better."

"I hate this city."

Pell called from desk:

"Duskport!"

Fucking Duskport.

I sat.

Sori said, "Forty-six."

"Yes."

"Getting closer."

"Yes."

"Peg-Leg Greg."

"I will throw tea at you."

"You need both hands."

Silence.

She realized.

I realized.

Then she laughed so hard table shook.

I did too.

Could not help it.

Because yes.

That was entire fucking problem.

Need both hands.

Peg-Leg Greg.

Stupid.

Good stupid.

I looked at crutches leaning against chair.

Then Masta card in bag.

Travel-work candidate.

Forty-six copper.

Still no peg.

Still not enough.

But nickname had arrived before equipment.

That felt unfair.

Also somehow right.

Later, lying in loft, I thought about what Kett had said.

Better than no hook for work.

Worse than hand.

Masta:

Tool, not miracle.

Nali changing feet.

My own pelvis reading crooked as normal.

The leg would not restore me.

That word was wrong.

Restore implied old shape.

I was not going backward.

First life had two legs.

Second life had one leg and crutches and nineteen years of adaptations built around them.

A work limb would make third arrangement.

New body map.

New options.

Maybe something else too.

Not power.

Not yet.

But I remembered being reset into this body.

How much of early life had been learning what I could not do the old way.

Then learning another way.

Then another.

Eventually limitations became furniture.

Normal.

Useful even, sometimes, because you stop wasting anger on wall and start building door.

Peg would not remove wall.

Might put door somewhere I had forgotten to look.

That thought sat differently.

Not S-class switch.

Not locked mode.

Something smaller.

Forward.

Potential.

I had been thinking about leg as mobility purchase.

More hands.

Standing.

Carrying.

Work.

All true.

But maybe body change would change what I attempted before thinking no.

That mattered.

First life me had known body could usually be trained toward problem.

Second-life me knew that too in theory.

Crutches proved it.

Magic training proved it.

Work proved it.

Still, some quiet part of me had organized world around permanent subtraction.

One leg.

Two crutches.

Plan from there.

A peg did not erase subtraction.

It changed equation.

I smiled in dark.

Peg-Leg Greg.

Terrible.

If they kept saying it, I might have to buy thing just to make them technically correct.

Financial strategy.

Very mature.
