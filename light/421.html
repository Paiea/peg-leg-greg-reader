TEXT READER · CHAPTER 415

THE STUDENT

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

I woke up before bell.

Not because disciplined.

Because somewhere in Duskport was object shaped for body.

That was different.

Masta said after work.

So naturally morning took six years.

Anchor's End breakfast tasted normal.

Too normal.

Sori watched me finish eggs.

"You look calm."

"I am calm."

"You checked time three times."

"Clock keeps changing."

Dask looked at wall clock.

"That's function."

"Thank you."

Maro smiled into tea.

Pell passed behind me.

"Big day, Peg-Leg?"

"Maybe shell. Not leg."

"Technicality."

"Entire process is technicality."

"Good luck."

That one landed differently.

Not joke.

I nodded.

East Hook board had work.

Full day.

Of course.

**MERRY WAKE / LOWER CABIN CORRIDOR / HARDWARE + PERSONAL EFFECTS HOLD**

Tideglass had cleared storm-heart area with exclusion around former mount.

Tavren pointed at me.

"Tally and condition. Four if clean. No custody tonight. You leave after fifth bell."

"Why?"

"Because Masta will complain to me if you show late."

"She'd complain to you?"

"She complains to everyone."

Nali said, "Family trait."

Good.

Work moved.

Merry Wake had become familiar enough to be dangerous that way.

Same barge.

Same cold iron smell.

Same lift bell.

Same brown water.

Different cabin.

I kept reminding myself different.

First load was three brass bunk frames and one warped locker door.

Second was kitchen hardware basket.

Third brought up something nobody wanted.

Mattress springs.

Eight years underwater had improved them aesthetically.

Not functionally.

I recorded:

**FERROUS BED FRAME / HEAVY CORROSION / NO MARKS VISIBLE / DISPOSITION BULK SCRAP**

Then Nali slapped board.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"You're writing future leg with eyes again."

I looked down.

She was right.

My handwriting had become smaller.

"Sorry."

"Don't be sorry. Be here."

Fair.

Next lift mattered more.

Divers found narrow wall cabinet below old passenger berth row.

Not valuable-looking.

Thin cedar box with brass corner plates.

One plate stamped **M. VALE / 3C**.

Sori family name made my head turn automatically.

Nali saw.

"Know it?"

"Name. Not person."

"Then write that much."

There.

We checked manifest.

Passenger list had **Mera Vale**, seamstress, berth 3C.

No declared valuables.

No relation established to Sori.

Common enough surname maybe.

Could be.

Could not.

I did not build family tree because letters matched.

Progress.

Cabinet went personal-effects lane.

I made note to ask Sori later whether name meant anything.

Not now.

Then fourth lift nearly ate entire schedule.

Half-collapsed brass ventilation trunk came up with three loose iron rods threaded through it.

Weight wrong.

Not too heavy.

Uneven.

Rigging shifted twice.

Jerr stopped.

Reset sling.

Stopped again.

No emergency.

Just bad object.

Tavren watched.

"Leave it."

Jerr said, "Could make it."

"I know."

"Weather holding."

"I know."

"Then why leave?"

Tavren pointed at torn side seam.

"Because third adjustment made it worse. We come back with spreader frame."

Jerr stared another second.

Then nodded.

No argument.

That mattered.

Competent people did not always finish thing because technically possible.

I wrote hold reason.

Not failed recovery.

Deferred recovery pending spreader frame.

Different words.

Different truth.

By fifth bell, records clean enough.

Four copper.

Thirty-one to thirty-five.

Tavren handed coins.

"Go."

"I'm going."

"You're still standing here."

Rude.

I went.

Hark & Venn had front shutters half closed.

Shop not closed.

Private fitting time.

Masta stood inside with sleeves rolled and chalk on forearm.

On bench was leg-shaped object.

Sort of.

Not peg.

Not finished leg.

Socket shell in pale layered leather over stiff inner form.

Adjustable straps.

Temporary straight support post.

Plain flat test foot at bottom.

Ugly.

Beautiful.

Mostly ugly.

I stopped.

Masta said, "If you cry I charge extra."

"I wasn't going to cry."

"Good. Pants off."

There.

Romance ended.

She checked stump first.

Skin warm.

No open spot.

No abrasion worse than normal.

Mild puffiness after full yard day.

She measured circumference again.

Compared baseline.

"Up a little."

"Bad?"

"Expected. Better fitting test after work anyway. Shows real volume."

She pressed lateral upper edge.

"Tender?"

"No."

Lower front.

"No."

Posterior scar.

"No."

"Good."

She handed liner.

Not magical.

Soft cloth and thin padded leather sections.

"On."

I did.

Then shell.

First problem:

My stump did not know where to go.

Which sounded stupid.

It had one direction.

Still.

Socket pressure came around entire limb differently from crutch life.

Not pain.

Containment.

Masta tightened front strap.

Then upper.

Then side.

"Stand between rails."

I put crutches forward automatically.

"Rails."

"Crutches are faster."

"Rails."

I hated student voice.

I set crutches against wall.

Hands on rails.

Right foot under me.

Left test foot touching floor.

Nothing happened.

Of course nothing happened.

I was wearing temporary wooden post.

Not destiny.

"Load ten percent."

"Again with numbers I cannot feel."

"Again with me watching."

I shifted.

Right leg kept most weight.

Masta tapped left hip.

"More."

I shifted.

"Shoulders down."

I dropped shoulders.

"Pelvis level."

"Feels level."

"It isn't."

There.

Again.

My body lied.

Not maliciously.

Professionally.

Nineteen years experience lying about what centered meant.

Masta placed two fingers on iliac crest.

"Here."

Shift.

"There."

I moved until she said stop.

Felt crooked.

Apparently correct.

"Hold."

Ten breaths.

Pressure built along outer upper socket.

"Any sharp?"

"No."

"Burning?"

"No."

"Numb?"

"No."

"Pressure?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

I showed.

She chalked shell.

Then:

"Step."

Fuck.

I moved left post forward.

Too far.

Test foot slapped floor.

Right knee locked hard.

Hands crushed rails.

Masta said, "Again. Smaller."

I stepped back.

Almost caught toe.

"Again."

Third try.

Left forward.

Short.

Load.

Right through.

Awful.

No rhythm.

Not walking.

Negotiating with floor.

"Again."

Five passes.

Then ten.

My left hip started working in way it had not worked under crutches.

Right calf got angry because it did not know whether to relax.

Lower back argued.

Socket pressure changed each step.

I was sweating.

This was embarrassing.

First life I could sprint broken ground in armor.

Current life I could move through Duskport faster on crutches than many people walked.

Trial shell made me worse.

Much worse.

Good.

Masta had warned.

Still hated it.

"You're trying to be good at it," she said.

"Seems reasonable."

"No. You're trying to hide bad information from me."

I looked at her.

"I'm not hiding anything."

"You correct before I can see mistake. Stop."

That was irritating because true.

I had started compensating toe catch with shoulder.

Started shortening right step before she called it.

Started using rails harder on left load.

Old habits adapting instantly around new problem.

Which was exactly wrong for fitting.

"Walk badly on purpose?"

"Walk honestly."

Worse.

I did.

She watched.

After four lengths she stopped me.

Marked shell in three places.

Upper lateral too much pressure under fatigue.

Back edge pinching when I extended hip.

Test foot angle slightly too externally rotated because my body turned it to feel safe.

Not hardware only.

Me too.

Masta loosened shell.

Took it off.

Skin pink at outer upper edge.

One narrow pressure line behind knee.

No blister.

No broken skin.

She pressed blanching area.

Color returned.

"Good."

"Looks bad."

"Looks useful."

She heated leather edge.

Worked it over form.

Added thin pad opposite pressure line instead of directly on it.

Adjusted post rotation two degrees inward.

Then shell back on.

Second round.

Better.

Not good.

Better.

That word again.

Different.

Useful word.

I made six lengths.

On seventh, something happened.

Nothing dramatic.

I stopped at middle of rails.

Weight split maybe sixty-forty.

Maybe more right.

Did not matter.

Hands resting on rails.

Not pushing.

Resting.

I lifted right hand.

Masta opened mouth.

"I'm not performing."

"We'll see."

Left hand still rail.

I stood.

One hand free.

Then I lifted left too.

Only half second.

Maybe one.

Masta did not stop me immediately.

Then body started drifting right.

I grabbed rail.

No fall.

No miracle.

But for one second both hands had belonged to me while standing.

Not borrowed from crutches.

Mine.

I looked at them.

Stupid.

Hands looked same.

Masta said, "Don't get sentimental."

"Wasn't."

"You were."

"Maybe slightly."

"Fine. One second. Then back to work."

Rude woman.

We did more.

She shortened session before I wanted.

"Why stop?"

"Because your skin gave enough information."

"I can keep going."

"Not question."

"I feel fine."

"Also not question."

She removed shell again.

Pink upper edge stronger now.

Still no damage.

"Tomorrow no shell."

"What?"

"I adjust tonight. You work normally. Come next evening if skin stays clean."

"So every other day?"

"Maybe. Maybe not. Depends skin. Depends volume. Depends what you do."

"When do I get finished leg?"

She looked at me.

I knew answer before she said it.

"When this stops lying."

She tapped shell.

Then my hip.

"And this stops lying."

Fair.

She showed hardware choices.

Not final.

A slightly broader travel foot than test block.

Wet-grip option like Nali's but less aggressive.

Possible reinforced side stay if repeated lateral load remained.

No articulated ankle yet.

Too expensive and unnecessary for first build.

"Basic travel foot first," she said. "Learn it. Then we can talk other feet later if money and use justify."

"How much later?"

"After you stop trying to buy future body before learning current one."

Everybody in city knew me now.

I dressed.

Shell stayed.

Important.

I did not take it home.

Not fitted enough.

Not safe unsupervised.

Masta hung it on peg behind bench.

Peg.

Of course.

I pointed.

"Funny."

"What?"

"Nothing."

She handed crutches.

My body returned to familiar movement immediately.

Fast.

Efficient.

Easy.

That was almost disappointing.

Crutches felt like home.

Trial shell had felt like argument.

Outside, Sori, Maro, and Dask were waiting across street.

I stopped.

"Why are you here?"

Sori said, "Pell told us."

"Pell knows too much."

"Correct."

Dask looked behind me.

"Where is it?"

"Shop."

"You don't keep it?"

"No."

He looked betrayed by manufacturing process.

Maro asked, "How was it?"

I considered.

"Bad."

Sori smiled.

"Good bad?"

"Useful bad."

"Did you fall?"

"No."

"Did you walk?"

"Technically."

Dask said, "Define technically."

"No."

We walked back.

On crutches.

Faster than shell.

Much faster.

I told them about rails.

About pressure.

About pelvis lying.

About one second.

Sori did not joke immediately.

That was how I knew she understood.

Then:

"Both hands?"

"One second."

"Still counts."

Maybe.

At Anchor's End Pell looked up.

"Well?"

"Not peg yet."

"Terrible marketing."

"I was worse than crutches."

"Even better."

"How?"

"Now nickname has character arc."

I laughed.

Fuck him.

Night was strange.

Body tired in unfamiliar places.

Left hip.

Lower back.

Right calf less than expected.

Skin intact.

I checked twice because Masta told me to, not because anxious.

Mostly.

No mindset switch.

That mattered.

I had stood with both hands free for one second.

That mattered too.

Equation had not changed yet.

Not really.

It had been written on board.

New variable present.

No solution.

Tomorrow I would go back to crutches and work.

Day after, maybe shell again.

Then again.

And again.

That was less romantic than transformation.

Probably why it might work.
