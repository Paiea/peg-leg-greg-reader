TEXT READER · CHAPTER 366

THE TEMP

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

My left hip hurt.

Not injured.

Offended.

Different category.

I woke and moved leg stump under blanket.

No sharp pain.

No skin pull.

No heat.

Just muscles complaining that yesterday I had introduced new management.

Right calf also sore.

Interesting.

Usually right calf worked too much and complained because I made it do everything.

Today it complained differently.

Apparently even partial redistribution had opinions.

I sat up.

Checked skin.

Upper lateral redness mostly gone.

Posterior line pale.

No blister.

No broken skin.

Good.

No shell today.

Masta had said no shell.

So no shell.

Very mature.

I hated it.

Downstairs Pell looked at me.

"Walking?"

"Yes."

He looked at crutches.

"Strong evidence."

"No shell today."

"Ah. Peg-Leg Greg on administrative pause."

"Eat glass."

"Breakfast first."

Sori was already there.

Maro had tea.

Dask had auction pamphlet again.

"Why do you still have that?"

"Reference."

"For what?"

"Prices."

"You own no auction house."

"Correct."

Useful conversation ended.

Sori looked at my face.

"How bad?"

"Hip sore. Skin fine."

"Good."

Then she did not ask more.

Also good.

I ate eggs.

East Hook board had my name.

Final day of block.

**MERRY WAKE / VENT TRUNK / LOWER CABIN / SPREADER FRAME**

Tavren tapped board.

"Same assignment."

Tally and condition.

Four copper if useful.

No custody planned.

One nominal full day remaining.

Last one.

Temporary employment had expiration date.

Comforting.

Nali looked at my gait before saying hello.

"Shell?"

"No-shell day."

"Good."

"You all sound relieved when I follow instruction."

"We know you."

Rude.

Merry Wake was becoming familiar.

Not home.

Worksite.

Different.

I knew where barge boards hung.

Which corner stayed slick.

Where Jerr left spare sling tags.

Which diver liked tea too sweet.

How Nali wanted condition entries ordered.

Time leaving residue.

There.

Today spreader frame came with us.

Ventilation trunk had waited.

Good.

Nobody punished steel for not cooperating yesterday.

Jerr and crew set wider support.

Two sling points instead of one central bite.

Load came up cleaner.

Still ugly.

Warped trunk flexed but seam did not worsen.

I watched HT-LP-4 in bag.

Did not pull it out.

Not my test.

Not needed.

Growth everywhere.

Second lift was three cabin doors bundled separately.

Third was galley copper.

Then lower cabin produced problem.

Not magical.

Human.

Diver brought up sealed canvas roll from under bunk frame.

Long.

Heavy for size.

No obvious charge.

Label plate half gone.

One surviving line:

**R. SOMA / ...WAY OFFICE**

Hessa checked manifest.

Passenger **Rulen Soma**, assistant courier clerk.

Not ship employee.

No declared professional kit.

Sel Marr was not there.

Good.

Different people.

Nali said, "Personal effects lane until identified."

Tavren nodded.

Simple.

Then man on quay shouted.

"That's office property."

Of course.

He wore blue courier sash.

Mid-forties.

Thin.

Sweating despite cold wind.

**Harl Fen**.

Current Duskport branch manager for **Narrowway Courier Office**.

Not Rulen's employer exactly.

Successor office.

Same company line after merger thirty years ago.

Harl had old register copy showing Rulen issued secure dispatch roll before voyage.

No serial.

No dimensions.

No description beyond `sealed dispatch roll / route books / bonds`.

Harl wanted immediate office custody.

Hessa wanted evidence.

Reasonable.

Harl was also terrified.

Not because object worth fortune.

Because office had current audit in three days and historical bond gaps still sat on books as unresolved liability.

"If those are old bond books," he said, "I need them before audit."

Hessa said, "If they are personal effects, you don't."

"They aren't."

"You have evidence they may not be."

"He was courier clerk."

"Yes."

"The roll says Narrowway."

"Partly."

Harl looked at me like maybe I was softer.

Mistake.

"You understand records."

"Some."

"Then write office property pending opening."

"Can't."

"Why not?"

"Because we haven't established that."

He exhaled through teeth.

"You people keep treating uncertainty like virtue."

There.

Again.

Not same dispute.

Same wound.

People needing answer before evidence ready.

I said, "Uncertainty isn't virtue. It's current condition."

He hated that.

Fair.

Tavren cut in.

"Roll stays sealed. Hessa routes it. Work continues."

Harl said, "And my audit?"

Tavren looked at him.

"Still in three days."

Not helpful.

True.

We moved.

By third bell Harl was still there.

Not shouting now.

Just sitting on bollard.

That was worse.

I took water break near him.

He said, "My father ran branch before me."

"Okay."

"His father before him."

"Long office."

"Long mistakes too."

There.

He rubbed face.

"Those old bonds aren't my fault."

"Probably not."

"Audit doesn't care."

"Probably not."

"If roll has route ledger, I can close six lines. Maybe seven."

"Maybe."

He laughed once.

No humor.

"There it is."

"Sorry."

"No. You're not."

I thought.

"I'm sorry it matters before we know."

That got different look.

Not forgiveness.

Less anger.

Good enough.

Hessa eventually got answer without opening roll.

Not full answer.

She found Narrowway company seal pressed into surviving brass cap.

Then old corporate mark book from port archive confirmed cap pattern used on company-dispatch containers, not private employee cases.

That established office ownership class strongly enough for transfer pending controlled opening.

Harl stood.

"So mine."

Hessa said, "Narrowway's."

"Yes."

"Not yours."

"Fine."

She was relentless.

I admired it.

Roll transferred sealed to company custody with witness and audit notation.

Harl got what he needed operationally.

Still did not know contents.

Still did not know whether books helped.

Partial relief.

More honest than miracle.

Rest of day quiet.

Which meant tired.

By fourth bell stump felt normal from crutches.

Hip soreness less.

Right calf still aware.

I checked skin at lunch in privacy.

Still clean.

Good.

At fifth bell Tavren paid four copper.

Thirty-five to thirty-nine.

Then did not dismiss me.

Uh oh.

Nali leaned against table.

Jerr pretended not listening.

Tavren said, "Block's done."

"I know."

"You worked four full Venn days after trial plus one transfer."

"Yes."

"Useful."

"Usually."

"Don't ruin it."

I shut up.

He set slate down.

"I can put you on another block."

There.

Expected.

Still mattered.

"Same terms?"

"Not exactly."

Interesting.

"Three-day flex."

"Meaning?"

"You check board when you want. If I have condition/tally work, same rates. Three base, four load, five custody. No minimum days. No penalty if fitting takes morning or you leave city. Tell me when you know you're unavailable."

"Why no minimum?"

"Because I know what you are now."

"Temporary?"

"Useful temporary."

There.

I smiled despite self.

Tavren continued.

"I'm not hiring guild clerk. You're not becoming recovery specialist because you survived two weeks. You can read work, ask before guessing, and you're less annoying than most desperate people."

"High praise."

"Don't make it emotional."

Nali said, "He already did."

"Traitor."

I looked at slate.

Three-day flex.

No minimum.

Could work.

Could fit.

Could leave.

Could labor board.

Could go auction and become poor idiot.

Could maybe travel farther.

Option.

Again.

"Yes," I said.

Tavren nodded.

Done.

No contract ceremony.

Good.

Then Nali said, "Masta said come by."

I looked at her.

"Today?"

"Skin check only. No shell unless she changes mind."

Good.

I went.

Hark & Venn smelled like warm leather and oil.

Masta looked at me.

"Pants."

"You really should vary greeting."

"No."

Skin check.

Upper redness gone.

Posterior line gone.

No abrasion.

She pressed around stump.

"Sore?"

"Hip. Not skin."

"Expected."

"Right calf too."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"Means you actually loaded left side enough to change right-side pattern."

There.

Evidence.

Small.

Real.

She turned toward bench.

Trial shell sat there.

Adjusted.

I looked.

She caught look.

"No shell today."

"I know."

Everyone had face theory.

"Tomorrow?"

"If skin stays clean."

"Tomorrow after work?"

"After whatever you do. Come before sixth bell."

"How long?"

"Until I stop."

Good.

Then she did something rude.

She handed me shell.

Not to wear.

Just hold.

"Feel weight."

I took it.

Lighter than expected.

Not light.

Support post and test foot gave awkward balance.

Socket itself dense.

"Finished one heavier?"

"A little. Better foot. Side stay maybe. Hardware."

"How much heavier?"

"Enough you'll complain."

"Useful."

"You'll get used to some. Not all."

I turned shell in hands.

Object made for body.

Strange category.

Armor was made for body.

Weapons too.

Boots.

Braces.

But this was closer.

Not replacement.

Interface.

Masta took it back before I became philosophical.

Good woman.

"Tomorrow we walk again," she said.

"Better?"

"Maybe."

"How encouraging."

"I don't care if better. I care if data better."

Right.

Walk honestly.

I left.

Outside, dusk light across street.

I stood on crutches.

Familiar.

Fast.

Efficient.

No shame in that.

Important.

Future leg did not need to defeat crutches to justify itself.

It needed to add options.

I headed toward inn.

Halfway there, someone called my name.

Not Peg-Leg.

Greg.

Sori.

She was standing outside post office with folded paper.

My stomach changed before brain did.

Home.

She held it out.

"Carrow."

There.

Finally.

I took paper.

Cala's handwriting on outside.

Not elegant.

Fast.

I opened.

Three hands inside.

Cala first.

**You went farther than two nights. Obviously. Theatre still standing. Vale asked twice whether you're dead. I told him probably not. Work is work. Don't make us come get you because you decided sea is personality now.**

I laughed.

Then Tavi.

Big crooked letters.

**DID YOU SEE A SEA MONSTER. BRING ROCK IF SEA ROCK DIFFERENT. COME BACK BUT NOT YET IF YOU ARE DOING IMPORTANT THING.**

Chest hurt.

Small.

Good hurt.

Then Lyssa.

Neater.

**I am glad you went. I am also annoyed you went far enough that being glad takes effort. Both can be true. Cala says that is adulthood. Cala is wrong about many things but not this one. Send dates when you know them. Do not turn uncertainty into disappearance.**

There.

That one stayed.

Sori watched face.

"Bad?"

"No."

"Good?"

"Annoying."

"So good."

"Yes."

I reread Tavi line.

Come back but not yet.

Smart kid.

Dangerous.

Home did not demand immediate return.

Worse.

It gave permission with conditions.

Send dates when you know them.

I did not know dates.

But I could answer something.

Tomorrow shell.

Three-day flex work.

Maybe a few more days Duskport.

Then?

Road had widened.

Could go back.

Could go farther.

Could wait until leg usable enough for travel trials.

Could not pretend no choice existed.

There.

Different pressure.

At Anchor's End I wrote reply before dinner.

Not long.

**Alive. Duskport. Working temporary harbor recovery. Paid deposit on work leg. First trial shell happened. Worse than crutches so far, which is apparently correct. Staying at least several more days for fittings and work. After that I don't know yet. I will send another date when I do. Sea monster: one chain ray, which probably counts. Tavi, sea rocks are different.**

I stopped.

Added:

**I miss you.**

There.

Simple.

No need to be clever.

Pell said outgoing courier left first bell tomorrow.

Good.

Dinner.

Sori asked about Venn.

"Three-day flex."

"You taking it?"

"Yes."

"So staying?"

"At least few more days."

Maro nodded.

Dask asked, "Does flex overlap fittings?"

"That's point."

"Efficient."

"Don't make this about efficiency."

"It literally is."

Sori said, "Let him have emotion for six seconds."

Dask looked at me.

"Are you having emotion?"

"Not anymore."

Good friends.

I counted money once.

Thirty-nine copper.

Still huge remaining leg balance.

Still Vale.

Still food.

Still room.

Still enough to breathe.

Tomorrow shell again.

No guarantee better.

No guarantee final leg soon.

Home knew where I was.

Work knew I could leave.

Masta knew I would come back.

That felt strange.

Not trapped.

Expected.

Different.

Temporary did not mean unreal.

Maybe that was whole trick.

You could belong somewhere for a while without promising forever.

Dangerous thought.

I went upstairs on crutches.

Fast.

Familiar.

Tomorrow I would be bad at walking again.

Excellent.
