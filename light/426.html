THE SCOUT

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

No shell.

Masta had been clear.

My skin agreed.

Mostly.

Outer-distal red oval faded overnight but not gone.

Tender maybe one.

No heat.

No open skin.

So recovery day.

Crutches.

Fast.

Familiar.

Annoyingly excellent.

At breakfast Sori said, "You look disappointed."

"I'm on best mobility system I currently own."

"You look disappointed while saying that."

"Humans contain multitudes."

Dask looked up.

"That phrase is structurally vague."

"Eat."

Maro had small paper packet beside bowl.

"What?"

"Salt moss notes."

"You made notes?"

"Herbalist asked what color roots were at upper arch versus inner wall. I didn't know."

"So you're going back?"

"Maybe."

Sori pointed spoon at him.

"Not selling?"

"No."

"Good."

"I might sell information."

We stared.

Maro blinked.

"What?"

"Nothing," I said. "Capitalism found herb boy."

He frowned.

"I don't like that."

Healthy.

East Hook board had my name nowhere.

Flex work.

Could wait.

Could labor exchange.

Could spend day pretending one faded pressure mark required spiritual retreat.

Tavren stood with mug.

"No shell?"

"Recovery day."

"Good."

"Work?"

He pointed.

Board:

**MERRY WAKE / DIVE HOLD**

**NORTH PIER / PRIVATE CREW**

**SOUTH CHAIN / IRONWORK**

Nothing.

"Maybe afternoon intake," he said. "No promise."

"Labor board."

"Probably."

I turned.

"Greg."

I looked back.

Tavren held folded notice.

"This came through harbor office. Might suit you. Might not."

He handed it over.

Cheap gray paper.

Stamped Duskport Outer Works.

**SHORT CONTRACT: ROUTE CONDITION / LOAD RECORD / FIELD OBSERVATION**

Location:

**GREYFIN ROAD / SALT MARSH CAUSEWAY / NORTH REACH DEPOT**

Three days minimum.

Possibly five.

Travel supplied from Duskport.

Return uncertain by one day due tide/weather.

Pay:

**4c per full route day**

**5c if assigned observation ledger**

Meals bunk supplied at depot.

No hazard premium unless marsh closure active.

I read twice.

Outside Duskport.

Not far.

Far enough.

"What's Greyfin Road?"

"North coastal supply route. Marsh causeway about twelve miles out."

"Problem?"

"Outer Works thinks one section settling. Carrier guild says road fine and survey markers wrong."

"Why me?"

"Didn't say you. Notice says condition/tally hand familiar with irregular load records."

"You thought me."

"Maybe."

"Recommendation?"

"No."

Of course.

"Who owns contract?"

"Outer Works. Field lead named Enna Ro."

Pella Ro?

Could be nothing.

Duskport surnames repeated like gulls.

"Departure?"

"Tomorrow first bell if filled."

There.

I looked west.

Hark & Venn.

Recovery day today.

Possible medium-foot trial after.

Tomorrow departure would conflict.

Masta might murder contract.

Or me.

Tavren said, "Read whole notice."

I did.

**Applicants with medical/fitting appointments may join at second relay if pre-cleared. Second relay departs following morning.**

There.

Choice.

Not trap.

"Two departure windows."

"Yes."

"How long second relay behind first?"

"One day."

"Same pay?"

"Starts when you join."

"Good."

Tavren drank.

"You want it."

"Maybe."

"You read money once. Read route three times."

Rude observation.

"Where does it go after North Reach?"

"Road continues to **Merehook** and upland split. Contract doesn't."

Merehook.

Never heard.

Good.

A road beyond Duskport.

Actual road.

Not carriage here and back before dinner.

Something in chest moved.

Not destiny.

Interest.

Training brain, apparently, now applied to geography.

What can I do now that I could not do yesterday?

Not walk twelve miles on trial leg.

Definitely not.

But go somewhere while leg process continued?

Maybe.

First I needed facts.

"Where do I apply?"

"Outer Works annex by North Gate."

"Thanks."

"Not recommendation."

"I know."

"Tell Masta before you vanish."

"I know."

"Tell your people too."

I looked at him.

"Which people?"

"The three who keep appearing around you."

Oh.

Right.

People.

I went North Gate.

Duskport outer works annex looked like government had discovered damp wood.

Long room.

Benches.

Maps.

Mud.

Three clerks arguing over axle widths.

I liked immediately.

Application desk woman asked skills.

"Condition records. Load tally. Recovery intake. Basic field notes. No structural authority. No rigging authority."

"Magic survey?"

"Limited observations only. No certification."

"Can walk road?"

There.

I looked at crutches.

"On crutches, yes, depending distance and pace."

"Causeway team rides supply wagon between sections. Field checks mostly under two hundred paces at a time."

Good.

"Any marsh work?"

"No wading unless emergency evacuation."

Better.

"Medical limitation?"

"Left below-knee amputation. Fitting in progress."

Clerk looked at crutches once.

"Need accommodation?"

"Boarding step if wagon high. Maybe extra minute mounting."

She wrote.

No drama.

"Field lead decides. Wait."

I waited.

Ten minutes.

Woman in dark green coat came out.

Mid-thirties maybe.

Hair in tight braid.

Left cheek scar from nose to ear.

No smile.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

"Enna Ro."

There.

"Any relation to Pella Ro?"

She stopped.

"My sister."

Oh.

"Venn diver?"

"Yes."

"Calf cut."

Her face changed tiny amount.

"You were there."

"Shore tally."

"She said somebody kept red-hold until line status cleared."

"That was me."

"Good."

Not warm.

Useful.

"Sit."

We sat map table.

Greyfin Road ran north from Duskport along coast, then inland across broad salt marsh on raised stone-and-timber causeway.

North Reach Depot sat beyond marsh.

Marks every quarter mile.

Three sections under dispute.

Carriers reported roughness but no visible collapse.

Outer Works survey pegs showed one segment settling **three to five fingers** relative prior season.

Carrier guild claimed markers themselves had shifted because one benchmark post leaned after storm.

"Who's right?"

Enna said, "If I knew, I wouldn't hire observation hand."

Good.

"What matters if causeway is settling?"

"Heavy freight restriction. Possibly close one lane."

"And if markers wrong?"

"Unnecessary restriction costs carriers money and North Reach fuel supply arrives late."

"How much?"

"Enough they're angry."

Human problem.

"Anybody wanting answer badly?"

Enna looked at me.

"Everyone."

There.

Carrier guild wanted road open.

Outer Works wanted not kill anyone.

North Reach depot wanted fuel before weather turn.

Marsh villages wanted supply carts but also did not want causeway failure flooding culverts.

No villain.

Correct answer might still hurt someone.

Excellent.

I hated that I thought excellent.

"What would I do?"

"Ride section wagon. Record axle class, visible wheel response, marker readings as called by survey tech, drainage state, timber sound notes, and any mismatch between road behavior and benchmark claim."

"I don't interpret survey?"

"No."

"Who does?"

"Me and Halen Brusk. Licensed road surveyor."

"Carrier representative?"

"Jorren Pike rides first day."

Of course.

"So someone can argue live."

"He will."

"Good."

She almost smiled.

"Pella said you're annoying."

"Good annoying?"

"She was bleeding. Hard to classify."

Fair.

"Pay notice says four, five observation ledger."

"You get five if I assign master field ledger. I probably will after first section if your handwriting doesn't offend me."

"My handwriting is beautiful."

"Then I'm already suspicious."

I liked her.

"Second relay?"

"You need fitting?"

"Yes. Trial limb."

"How long?"

"Unknown. Shop deciding geometry."

"Can you leave tomorrow?"

"Probably shouldn't."

"Then second relay. I need certainty tonight."

There.

Commit.

Not forever.

Four or five days away from Duskport, maybe six with weather.

Then maybe back.

Maybe road beyond.

Merehook.

I said, "I need talk to my limbwright and companions."

Enna nodded.

"By sixth bell."

"If I join second relay, when does contract end?"

"Three field days minimum after arrival. Could extend two if disputed segment requires repeat loaded pass. You can refuse extension after minimum."

"Return transport guaranteed?"

"To Duskport, yes, first available works wagon. Weather may delay."

"Good."

"You ask better questions than most."

"Experience."

"With roads?"

"With bad jobs."

That got actual smile.

Small.

I left with conditional slot paper.

Hark & Venn next.

Masta read notice.

Then looked at me.

"No."

"No what?"

"Not tomorrow first relay."

"I know."

"Good."

"Second relay?"

She read again.

"Maybe."

Better.

"Skin?"

She checked.

Outer-distal oval nearly gone.

"Tomorrow medium foot trial. Short. Shop/courtyard. If boring, I can send shell with you?"

I froze.

"What?"

"Not finished. Trial use."

"Outside Duskport?"

"Maybe."

There.

That was new.

Masta held finger up.

"Maybe means maybe. If tomorrow shows same pressure problem, no. If clean, I can convert temporary post to sturdier travel test setup and give you strict wear schedule."

"How strict?"

"Very."

"Hours?"

"Probably thirty minutes morning, thirty evening first road day. Crutches primary. No marsh edge. No carrying load while walking unless flat and supervised by common sense, which worries me because yours is selective."

"Rude."

"Also daily skin check. If red mark lasts more than twenty minutes after removal, stop use until clear. If blister, stop. If pain increases, stop."

"Finished hardware?"

"Still no."

"Foot?"

"Medium test foot if tomorrow works."

"Side stay?"

"Still probably no reinforced stay."

"Cost?"

"Still not promising."

Of course.

"Would travel delay final build?"

"A few days."

"Would travel data help?"

"If you follow rules, yes. Different surfaces. Wagon mounting. Real sitting. Real fatigue."

There.

Road could become fitting data.

Not reason to go.

Useful if went.

"Do not choose contract because you think I need field test," Masta said.

"I wasn't."

"Good. Choose contract because you want contract."

That landed.

I did.

Want contract.

Not just money.

Road.

Marsh.

North Reach.

People arguing about whether causeway was safe.

Possibility of continuing farther after.

Merehook.

I wanted door.

Again.

Anchor's End.

Sori read conditional paper.

"Three to five days."

"Minimum three field days. Travel makes more."

"Maybe six total."

"Maybe."

Dask took notice.

"Marsh causeway?"

"Yes."

His eyes lit.

Bad.

"Settlement dispute?"

"Yes."

Worse.

"Survey benchmarks?"

"Yes."

He looked at me.

"I'm coming."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because you just made face."

"What face?"

Sori said, "He made face."

Maro read over Dask shoulder.

"North Reach has salt fen."

Oh no.

"You're both terrible."

Sori said, "I want to go too."

I stared.

"Why?"

"Because we didn't come here to become your waiting room furniture."

Fair.

"Also," she added, "route-contract disputes are interesting."

"There it is."

"And if we stay in Duskport while you leave, we pay rooms separately and then maybe chase you later."

Money logic.

Strong.

"Contract only hires me."

"We can buy seats on second relay if available."

Dask already reading travel note.

"Non-contract passengers allowed to North Reach for two copper each if wagon capacity remains."

Sori looked at him.

"Useful."

"I read entire notice."

"Terrifying."

Maro said quietly, "I'd like to go."

There.

Different voice.

Not excitement exactly.

Want.

"Why?"

He took moment.

"Because road keeps going."

Oh.

Same.

We were all infected.

I sat.

"If you come, I'm not paying fares."

Sori said, "Obviously."

Dask said, "I can pay two copper."

Maro nodded.

"Me too."

Sori looked at me.

"You're not responsible for everybody just because you found notice."

That was aimed deeper.

I knew.

"Fine."

"Fine we're coming?"

"Fine you can try seats."

Dask immediately stood.

"North Gate closes applications sixth bell."

"Sit down."

"Why?"

"Because I haven't accepted mine."

Three faces.

I hated democracy.

Again.

Why hesitate?

Fitting.

Money.

Duskport work.

Carrow farther behind.

Unknown road.

Exactly.

Unknown road.

That was point.

I had spent years making subtraction normal.

Then Carrow made belonging normal.

Now Duskport had made possibility noisy again.

Not because leg fixed me.

Leg not even mine yet.

Because I had started asking what next instead of what safely fits existing life.

Could still say no.

Important.

But did I want no?

No.

I wanted road.

So I went back North Gate before sixth bell.

Enna Ro at desk.

"Decision?"

"Second relay."

"Certain?"

"Yes."

She stamped conditional slot.

**GREG / FIELD OBSERVATION HAND / SECOND RELAY**

"Report North Gate before first bell day after tomorrow. Pack for six days. Could be four. Could be seven if weather ugly."

"Return guaranteed eventually."

"Eventually is government specialty."

"Good."

"Your companions?"

I looked behind.

Sori, Dask, Maro at passenger desk.

Of course already there.

"Apparently."

Enna watched them.

"They useful?"

"Sometimes accidentally."

"Good enough."

Passenger seats confirmed.

Two copper each from them.

Their money.

Not mine.

That mattered enough to notice.

Back outside, road gate stood open.

Beyond Duskport walls, north road ran between low dunes until distance turned gray.

We were not leaving today.

Not tomorrow either.

Day after.

One fitting test between.

Maybe trial shell came.

Maybe not.

Either way contract existed.

Road existed.

Duskport had stopped being destination.

It was node.

That felt right.

Dask said, "Greyfin causeway uses paired settlement posts according to notice. If one benchmark leaned after storm, comparative seasonal readings could produce false settlement if reference geometry wasn't re-established."

Sori said, "We have not left gate."

"I'm preparing."

Maro asked, "Do marshes have biting flies?"

Nobody answered.

He looked worried.

Good.

Adventure.

At Anchor's End Pell heard.

"Leaving?"

"Temporarily."

"You said that about Duskport."

"Still true."

"Peg-Leg Greg becomes Road Greg."

"No."

"Marsh Greg."

"Worse."

"Government Greg."

"Absolutely not."

He grinned.

"Peg-Leg sticks."

Unfortunately.

I packed that night.

Not much.

Quietener.

WS-F/9.

HT-LP-4.

Tavi rock.

Clothes.

Notebook.

Repair kit.

Masta card.

Crutch spare wrap.

No shell.

Maybe tomorrow changed that.

Maybe not.

I looked at purse.

Forty-three copper.

No pay today.

Contract could make twelve minimum if three full route days at base, more if ledger.

Useful.

But money wasn't first thought.

That surprised me.

First thought was road.

Second was fitting.

Third money.

Carrow somewhere after all three.

Not less important.

Just farther.

I wrote short note to Cala/Lyssa/Tavi even though last reply barely left.

**Possible route north from Duskport. Greyfin causeway / North Reach. Three to five field days plus travel. Not leaving until day after tomorrow. I will send again from depot if post exists.**

Then Tavi line:

**Marsh probably has monsters. Dask says statistically this is irresponsible.**

Dask was downstairs.

He had not said that.

Could later.

I slept badly.

Not worry.

Anticipation.

Training brain applied to road was apparently called being awake.

Annoying.

But there.

Duskport still had unfinished leg.

Unfinished work.

Unfinished people.

And now road north.

Good.

Story could hold more than one unfinished thing.
