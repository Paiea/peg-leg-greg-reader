TEXT READER · CHAPTER 346

THE PASSENGER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

I returned a government pencil.

That made leaving feel official.

Outer Works annex had morning ledger window open before first bell.

I brought:

master ledger.

waxed cover.

folding rule.

chalk.

one pencil full length.

one pencil shorter by maybe three fingers.

Bedroll.

Enna checked list.

"You used pencil."

"Extensively."

"Acceptable."

"Generous institution."

"We survive."

She signed return.

Master ledger left my hands.

Strange.

Three days of road condensed into object no longer mine to carry.

Good.

Wasn't mine.

Enna looked at trial leg bundle on harness.

"Going north."

Not question.

"Yes."

"Merehook?"

"Trying."

"Fen Crossing first."

"Apparently."

"Road past Fen climbs. Don't trust distance boards after rain."

"Why?"

"Because one washed out last year and someone put it back facing wrong way."

"Excellent system."

"Public service."

She handed contract close chit.

"If you come back through Duskport, Venn can verify your three days from this."

"Useful."

"Try not to become employee of every road you meet."

"No promises."

"That's what worries me."

No hug.

Good.

Jorren stood outside arguing with transfer clerk.

He saw me.

"Leaving?"

"Yes."

"South?"

"North."

He stared.

"Why?"

"Road."

"That's not answer."

"It is to me."

He shook head.

"Merehook charges dock toll on carts even if cart never touches dock."

"Why?"

"Because Merehook."

Useful cultural note.

Tera came in from clinic lane with blue scarf damp at ends.

"Oil got there," she said.

"Good."

"School too."

"Good."

She looked at me.

"You write too much."

"Probably."

"But you wrote who decided things."

"That mattered."

"It did."

Then she left.

Bera Tann didn't say goodbye.

She yelled from fuel office:

"If Merehook sells you anything by 'coastal measure,' ask which coast."

"Why?"

"Because there are three."

"Of course."

North Reach useful to last minute.

Then Duskport wagon bell rang.

First bell.

Southbound Works wagon rolled from depot yard.

Two empty places.

One could have been mine.

I watched it go.

No drama.

No regret surge.

Just wagon shrinking south.

Return option physically leaving.

Sori came beside me.

"Feel anything?"

"Hungry."

"Avoiding."

"Little."

"What?"

I watched wagon turn behind fuel shed.

Gone.

"Now we have to pay."

She laughed.

Good.

Passenger office opened half bell later.

North-east coach was smaller than Outer Works wagon.

Covered roof.

Side benches.

Six passenger places.

Four of us.

One woman with cage.

One old man with no luggage and suspiciously clean boots.

Clerk said, "Fen Crossing, three copper."

I paid.

Fifty-eight to fifty-five.

Three copper for road.

Sori paid hers.

Dask.

Maro.

No group purse.

Good.

"Merehook connection?" I asked.

Clerk grimaced.

There.

Friction.

"Usually fourth bell Fen coach."

"Usually?"

"North slope wash yesterday. Merehook coach came late. Might come today. Might overnight Fen."

"Fare still two copper?"

"If regular coach."

"If not?"

"Private cart wants what private cart wants."

Excellent.

"Rooms Fen?"

"Two inns. One decent, one cheaper."

"Which is which?"

"Depends what you fear."

Great town already.

We boarded.

Boarding step lower than Works wagon.

Still high enough I used both arms and right leg deliberately.

No stool.

Manageable.

Driver asked, "Need board?"

"No."

Not pride.

Actual no.

Baseline.

Seat.

Trial leg strapped under bench.

Crutches along side rail.

Passenger woman put cage between feet.

Inside cage something hissed.

Dask leaned.

"What is that?"

Woman said, "Marsh ferret."

Cage hissed again.

"Why?"

"Because it bites."

"Why do you have it?"

"Because it bites other things."

Dask nodded as if complete.

Apparently was.

Old clean-boots man slept before coach moved.

Professional.

Second bell.

North Reach behind.

Road rose almost immediately.

Marsh flattened west.

Ridge took over east.

Causeway had been line through water.

This was cut through rock and low scrub.

Salt wind stayed but changed.

Less harbor.

More dry mineral.

White crust on black stone.

Shrubs with silver undersides.

Small blue flowers inside cracks.

Road narrow.

Not bad.

Not good.

Wheel ruts shallow.

Drainage channels carved directly into rock in places.

Every few miles, old stone wind markers shaped like half-open hands pointed prevailing gust.

Magic maybe.

Or engineering with decorative ego.

I wanted to ask.

Didn't yet.

Passenger.

No job.

That felt different.

Nobody wanted notes.

I could look because I wanted.

Dangerous freedom.

Sori watched farms disappear.

Dask counted wind markers.

Maro asked driver about blue flowers.

Driver said, "Salt stars. Don't eat."

Maro said, "Why?"

"Make tongue numb."

Maro wrote that down.

We all had problems.

First stop was Dry Bell Post.

Not town.

Stone shelter, water trough, tea shed, one signal pole.

Coach changed front horse.

Twenty minutes.

Surface packed gravel.

Dry.

Stable.

I considered trial leg.

Then didn't.

Twenty-minute stop would become fourteen by time shell on/off/check.

Bad data.

Also tea.

Passenger choices.

I bought tea with one bit.

Copper unchanged.

Cage woman took ferret out.

Terrible decision.

It wore tiny leather harness.

Dask crouched.

"Can I touch it?"

"No."

"Because bites?"

"Yes."

"Consistent."

Old man woke, drank water, slept again.

I respected him.

Back road.

Higher.

By midday we could see coast north-east.

Not Duskport coast.

Cliffs.

Long pale shelves falling into dark blue water.

Maro sat forward.

"There."

"Salt cliffs?"

"Beginning."

White streaks ran cliff face.

Some natural salt.

Some bird colonies maybe.

Sun flashed on wings far out.

Glass gulls perhaps.

Too far.

Maro smiled without saying anything.

Good.

Dask looked inland instead.

"Observatory would need high stable line."

Sori said, "You can see town from here?"

"No."

"Then maybe wait."

"I am inferring geography."

"Quietly."

Coach rounded ridge.

Road problem appeared.

Not disaster.

Road crew.

Half slope washed onto north lane.

Mud, stones, uprooted brush.

One lane open against cliff side.

Coach stopped behind produce cart.

Three workers shoveled.

Forewoman wore yellow sash.

"One at time! Eastbound waits!"

Driver swore.

"How long?"

"Half bell if everyone stops asking!"

Fair.

We waited.

Then stranger collision.

Produce-cart driver started arguing because his greens would wilt before Fen market.

Coach driver said passengers had connection.

A mule cart from opposite direction had medicine crate.

Yellow-sash forewoman said nobody moved until loose rock above lane was barred down.

Greens man said slope had held twenty minutes.

Forewoman said, "Twenty minutes is not geology."

I almost liked her immediately.

Not my job.

Important.

Greens man pointed at medicine cart.

"Then let them through and us after."

Opposite driver said, "If I come down, coach can't go up until I clear."

"Then clear."

"Road one lane, genius."

Voices rose.

I stayed seated.

Beautiful.

No ledger.

No authority.

Could simply not own problem.

Then loose stone clicked above.

Everyone heard.

Forewoman shouted, "Back from lane!"

One worker moved.

Greens man didn't.

He was by front wheel trying to drag cart tighter edge.

Another stone bounced.

Small.

Then fist-sized rock came down.

Not at man.

At mule.

Mule jerked.

Cart wheel climbed road lip.

Greens man grabbed rein badly.

Cart tilted toward ditch.

I moved because now there was actual thing.

Crutches down.

Out coach.

Not running.

Fast as crutches allowed.

Forewoman already on mule head.

Other worker at rear wheel.

I got downhill side of greens man.

"Let rein go."

"It'll bolt."

"She has head. Let go."

He did.

Good.

Cart shifted again.

Rear worker shouted, "Chock!"

No chock near.

I planted right crutch behind downhill wheel.

Not as chock.

Too weak for full cart.

Just enough to stop first backward quarter roll while worker jammed real stone block under.

Crutch flexed.

Bad idea if held longer.

Block in.

I pulled crutch.

Sound.

No crack.

Forewoman calmed mule.

Done.

Nobody applauded.

Excellent.

Greens man breathed hard.

"Thanks."

"Worker did more."

"Still."

"Sure."

Forewoman looked at crutch.

"You use that as wheel stop again, I'll charge you for stupidity."

"Fair."

"Name?"

"Greg."

"I'm Nessa Vale."

Vale.

I did not react.

Common enough maybe.

Sori heard from coach.

She did react tiny.

Not relation claim.

Later.

Maybe.

Nessa Vale pointed at slope.

"Now everyone stays back until my people finish."

Greens man nodded.

Much less argumentative.

Medicine cart got first passage downhill because destination clinic post south.

Then produce cart uphill.

Then coach.

Not because fairness abstract.

Because vehicle order physically worked.

We lost nearly bell.

Connection worse.

Driver said, "Fen fourth bell coach probably gone if on time."

Dask said, "You said probably late."

"Yes."

"So delay may help."

Driver looked back.

"I dislike your optimism."

"It's probabilistic."

Worse.

I inspected crutch at next straight.

No split.

Ferrule fine.

Still stupid.

Useful for quarter second.

Would not repeat unless needed.

Baseline included tool abuse limits.

Fen Crossing appeared late afternoon.

Not fen.

Maybe once.

Town sat where three roads met around broad stone bridge over narrow green river.

Old reeds in floodplain.

Mills upstream.

Coach yard beside bridge.

Bell tower.

Two inns facing each other like enemies.

One painted blue.

One unpainted.

Clerk had been right.

Fear unclear.

We rolled in after fifth bell.

Merehook coach was still there.

Good.

Also full.

Bad.

Six seats.

All claimed.

Driver taking parcels.

Dask went straight window.

"Any cancellations?"

Clerk said, "No."

"Standing?"

"No."

"Roof?"

"Absolutely no."

Good town.

Sori asked, "Tomorrow?"

"First coach third bell. Four open seats currently. Two copper each."

There.

Simple.

Stay night.

Cost lodging.

Blue inn: two copper shared room bed space, supper included.

Unpainted: one copper floor loft, no supper, blanket extra bit.

Maro looked at blue.

Sori looked at unpainted.

Dask asked about locks.

We were already local.

I chose unpainted.

One copper.

Fifty-five to fifty-four.

Need supper separately.

Could still keep total around expected five maybe not.

Road cost what road cost.

Sori chose blue.

Interesting.

"Why?"

"I want actual bed."

Fair.

Dask chose unpainted because cheaper and near coach yard.

Maro chose blue because soup smelled good.

Party split across street.

Good.

No adventuring-party hive mind.

I bought bowl stew unpainted for one bit.

Copper unchanged.

Trial leg came out after supper.

Stable inn yard.

Dry stone.

No rush.

Twenty minutes.

I wore it.

Not to progress chapter.

Because I wanted both hands to wash shirt at pump.

There.

Actual function.

Crutches leaned within reach.

I stood at waist-high wash board.

Both hands soap/shirt.

No carrying.

No walking except three steps between pump and board.

Leg did quiet work.

Not glamorous.

Perfect.

Dask came out.

"You're doing laundry."

"Yes."

"With leg."

"Don't make it weird."

"It is mechanically relevant."

"Everything is mechanically relevant to you."

"Most things."

He held shirt.

"Can I test something?"

"No."

"You didn't hear."

"Correct."

Twenty minutes.

Upper pink mild.

Cleared.

No pain.

I wrote note.

**Fen Crossing inn yard. 20 min. Standing task at wash board. Minimal walking. Both hands sustained ~11 min. Mild upper pink cleared. No pressure point.**

That one mattered more than six steps maybe.

Not because bigger.

Because ordinary life widened.

After dark I crossed street on crutches to blue inn.

Sori and Maro had better chairs.

Unfair.

Maro said, "Coach tomorrow?"

"Four seats open."

"Good."

Sori said, "Paid?"

"Not yet. Clerk holds until morning unless prepaid."

"Why didn't you prepay?"

"Wanted know everyone still going."

She looked at me.

"I am."

Maro nodded.

Dask from doorway said, "Obviously."

"You weren't invited into dramatic timing," Sori said.

"Door was open."

I looked around.

New town.

One night.

Tomorrow coach.

Maybe Merehook by evening.

Maybe another road problem.

No contract.

No one waiting for us.

Nessa Vale somewhere behind clearing slope.

Possible coincidence.

Glass gull cliffs ahead.

Observatory ahead.

Trial leg working as laundry equipment.

Carrow very far south now.

I should send letter from Fen if post existed.

Tomorrow.

Maybe.

No, not maybe.

Lyssa said do not turn uncertainty into disappearance.

I would send.

Growth annoying.

Back at floor loft I lay beside Dask because cheap decisions had consequences.

He snored lightly.

Of course.

I counted money.

Fifty-four copper.

Spent four today:

three fare.

one floor.

Bits for tea/stew.

Tomorrow two more copper if coach.

Fifty-two.

Road was now costing more than original estimate.

Not catastrophic.

Real.

That felt better.

I had chosen this.

No employer paying bunk.

No guild reimbursing travel.

No mission expense.

My money.

My road.

Passenger.

Different kind of responsibility.

Outside, Fen Crossing bells marked late watch.

Bridge water ran under dark.

Tomorrow east.

If four seats still existed.
