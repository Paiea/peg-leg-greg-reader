THE GO-BETWEEN

Maro came back breathing harder than usual.

Not panicked.

Hill.

Merehook had opinions about vertical travel.

He held folded scrap.

"Table."

Hara turned.

Corren stopped pretending he wasn't watching door.

Sori moved first.

"You copied it?"

Maro nodded.

"And sign date."

Good.

He put paper on counter.

His handwriting looked like plants had learned accounting.

Readable enough.

Header:

**MEREHOOK MARKET FREIGHT CONVERSION / EFFECTIVE LAST MONTH, DAY TWELVE.**

Three regional columns.

Upper coast.

Lower coast.

North Reach dry.

Bera had not been joking about three coasts.

Of course.

Hara pointed.

"There."

North Reach dry class B converted to Merehook handling class C at her crate weight.

Three copper surcharge.

Corren said, "Yes."

Hara looked at me.

Not because I had answer.

Because people did that when answer hurt.

Sori read whole copied table.

Then footer.

"Transition note."

Corren leaned.

Maro had copied that too.

Small writing.

**Consignments contracted before effective date may be assessed under prior handling class upon presentation of dated freight agreement or seller's sealed order. Market office review required.**

Hara stared.

"My order is older."

Corren said, "Invoice date?"

She pulled papers.

Order date.

Two months old.

Cutting completion later.

Shipment invoice three days old.

Corren swore.

Quietly.

"Seller's sealed order?"

"Buyer order has seal."

She slapped packet down.

Blue wax.

Corren did not touch immediately.

"Market office review required."

There.

Still problem.

Office closing.

Maybe closed now.

Sori checked clock.

"Seven bell in maybe twelve minutes."

Hara said, "Then review it."

Corren's jaw tightened.

"I am custody. Not review."

"You work for market."

"Different bond. Different ledger."

"Three copper different?"

"My bond account, yes."

Hara laughed once.

Bad laugh.

"So rule says I may owe old fee, but nobody here can say so before ship leaves."

"Correct."

"Wonderful."

Corren looked miserable.

Also correct.

I asked, "Can custody take disputed amount without recording it as fee?"

Sori looked at me.

Employment shoulders.

I knew.

Corren said, "Bond deposit?"

"I don't know. I'm asking."

Good boundary.

He looked toward back shelf.

"There is provisional security."

Hara said, "What does that mean?"

"Means money held against unresolved market charge."

"Then do that."

"Not automatic."

Of course.

"What does it require?"

"Dispute slip. Custody seal. Review clerk countersign next business opening."

"Can crate release?"

Corren hesitated.

That mattered.

"If seller posts full disputed amount plus one copper security."

Hara's face changed.

"Four copper."

"Three disputed. One security."

"And if I win?"

"Four returned."

"If I lose?"

"Three becomes fee. One returns."

"When?"

"After review."

"Tomorrow?"

"Maybe."

She closed eyes.

Buyer pays twenty-eight on delivery.

Four now.

Money comes back maybe.

Could work.

Could also trap four copper while she needed crew money.

"Why didn't you say that?" she asked.

Corren looked offended.

"Because you asked me to waive fee."

"I asked you to release crate."

"Without fee."

"Because fee may not apply."

"Which is not same as provisional security."

They stared.

Sori said, "You are both annoyingly precise in incompatible directions."

Corren glanced at her.

"Thank you?"

"Not compliment."

Maro leaned toward gull feather stall outside.

Still here physically.

Mentally birds.

Good.

Hara counted pouch.

Seven copper total maybe.

Hard to tell.

She counted again.

"If I post four, I have three left until buyer pays."

Corren said nothing.

"Cutting crew already paid," she said. "Cart hire tomorrow two copper. Food one. If buyer delays payment because crate late, I'm done until next sale."

There.

The loophole wasn't free.

Just different shape of pain.

I asked, "Buyer here?"

"Ship office lower harbor."

"Will they pay on custody release or physical delivery?"

"Physical delivery to ship scale."

"Can they come here?"

Hara frowned.

"Why would they?"

"Maybe because their ship leaves."

She looked at clock.

"Ten minutes."

Corren said, "Market runner can carry notice."

"How long?"

"Lower harbor and back? Too long."

Sori said, "Not back. Just get buyer authorized person moving uphill while paperwork happens."

Corren looked at her.

"Runner fee one bit."

Hara stared.

"Naturally."

One bit mattered less than four copper.

She paid runner.

Corren rang little brass bell.

Boy appeared from somewhere.

Markets created children by bell.

Corren wrote:

**HARA QUILL / SALTSTONE / CRATE 18-Q / DISPUTE / BUYER SHIP DEPARTURE MORNING / REQUEST AUTHORIZED REPRESENTATIVE.**

Runner left.

Seven bell sounded while Corren filled provisional security slip.

Office closed.

Technically.

Custody still open for release window.

Hara put four copper down.

Slowly.

I felt it.

Not mine.

Still.

Four copper was almost my Merehook travel from Fen and bunk.

Money had weight when watched.

Corren counted.

Sealed three into small dispute envelope.

One into separate security line.

"Do not lose this receipt."

"I will eat it."

"Please don't."

"Then stop talking like paper is edible."

Different voices.

Good.

Crate still held until buyer representative arrived or Hara elected release-to-carrier without delivery confirmation.

Hara said, "How long can we wait?"

Corren looked out.

"Custody gate closes half bell after seven."

"And if buyer doesn't make it?"

"You can take crate tonight."

"Then ship scale closed."

"Yes."

"So I lose ship."

"Possibly."

Hara leaned on counter.

Nobody talked.

This was the part procedure couldn't solve.

Clock.

Distance.

Somebody else's schedule.

Sori asked, "Could buyer authorize payment on market release rather than ship scale?"

Hara said, "Contract says delivery to ship scale."

"Then no."

Simple.

No clever wording magic.

Maro said, "Runner fast."

We all looked.

"I saw him downhill."

"How fast?" Hara asked.

Maro shrugged.

"Fast."

Useful but not quantitative.

Ten minutes.

Then fifteen.

Custody workers began closing side shutters.

Corren did not rush them.

Wouldn't matter.

Hara paced.

Sori sat.

I stood on crutches and watched entrance.

Dask finally returned from instrument lane carrying cracked tide index like sacred relic.

"What happened?"

Sori said, "Administrative suspense."

"Good kind?"

"No."

He joined.

Five minutes before gate close, runner came back alone.

Hara's face dropped.

Then runner said, "Buyer clerk coming. Bad knee."

Hara stared at him.

"You couldn't help?"

"She has cane. Told me stop fussing."

Fair.

Corren checked clock.

"Gate can remain open if authorized party has been summoned before close and custody supervisor approves delay."

Hara rounded on him.

"That one you had ready?"

"Because now condition exists."

"I hate this city."

"Common."

Corren sent another bell.

Supervisor arrived.

Older man.

Name Sel Ordan.

No nonsense.

Corren explained in four sentences.

Sel asked, "Security posted?"

"Yes."

"Runner time?"

Corren gave.

"Buyer representative confirmed approaching?"

Runner nodded.

Sel wrote ten-minute extension.

Not twenty.

Not mercy unlimited.

Ten.

"After that gate closes."

Hara said, "She's on bad knee."

Sel said, "Then you may meet her at upper lane and bring authorization. Gate does not need person, needs signed buyer receipt authority."

Hara froze.

Then ran.

Actually ran.

Down market lane.

Corren called, "Crate stays!"

"Obviously!"

She disappeared.

I looked at Sori.

"Do we follow?"

"Why?"

Excellent question.

We didn't.

Not our race.

Six minutes.

Seven.

Eight.

Hara came back walking fast beside woman maybe fifty with gray braid and ironwood cane.

Buyer clerk.

Bad knee.

Angry at stairs.

"Who designed this fucking town?"

Merehook finally had universal truth.

She signed buyer authority accepting market release into bonded cart custody and agreeing ship scale confirmation could occur first thing before loading.

Hara said, "Will ship wait?"

Buyer clerk said, "Captain will wait one bell if cargo physically at lower yard before dawn. After that tide matters more than you."

Cost again.

Not money exactly.

Sleep.

Cart.

Timing.

Hara nodded.

"I can do that."

Corren released crate into bonded night-cart chain.

Cart fee?

Two copper, as Hara expected tomorrow.

Except tonight required night surcharge one bit.

Hara stared at ceiling.

"One bit," she said.

Corren said, "Carrier, not market."

"I know. I'm practicing suffering."

She paid.

Crate moved.

Not solved.

But moving.

Her four copper remained tied until review.

She would still need dawn delivery.

Buyer still could reject at scale if crate issue.

Market rule still maybe bad.

Corren still had bond exposure if paperwork wrong.

Nobody whole.

Hara looked at us.

"Thank you."

I said, "Maro copied table."

Maro had wandered to doorway again.

"Birds."

"Yes."

Sori said, "Greg asked one useful question."

"Two," Dask said.

"Nobody asked you."

"I counted."

Hara laughed.

Better laugh.

"You visitors?"

"Very."

"Then here's advice. Never buy saltstone by upper-shelf face."

"Why?"

"Because cutters polish the pretty side and let backside teach religion."

I wrote that mentally.

Maybe literally later.

Corren closed gate.

He looked exhausted.

Hara left with cart chain.

We remained.

Sori said, "You did it again."

"What?"

"Found employment without getting paid."

"I asked questions."

"Employment shoulders."

"No ledger."

"Progress."

Fair.

We walked uphill toward lodging.

Merehook after market close changed.

Wind louder.

Shops shuttered.

Upper road bowls singing faintly.

One old throat-glass bowl gave higher tone before gust reached lane.

I stopped.

Sori saw.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your artifact face is worse than road face."

"I don't have artifact face."

Dask said, "You do."

Traitor.

At old-instrument lane, seller was closing.

Cliff-line keeper still there.

Six copper.

Black frame.

Oval ceramic.

Could darken after threshold pull.

Not certified.

Interesting.

Could be useful with harness.

Could be useful with crutch-load experiments.

Could be useful with future limb load testing maybe.

Could also be six copper I didn't have to spend.

Fifty copper.

Leg quote still silver plus eighteen.

Road still cost money.

Merehook had two more days of interesting without trying.

I walked past.

Seller did not lower price.

Good.

Dask looked disappointed on artifact's behalf.

"You wanted it."

"Yes."

"Why not buy?"

"Because wanting is not accounting."

Sori said, "Frame that."

"Don't."

Upper ridge wind bowl sang.

Then another farther along.

Wave of warning moving through city before wind itself.

That was magic.

Or engineering.

Probably both.

I liked Merehook.

Dangerous.

At lodging, I checked trial-leg skin from no wear.

Normal.

No reason to put shell on at night just to make progress happen.

Good.

Maro sat by window with legal glass-gull feather.

Held it toward lamp.

Outer vane flashed translucent.

Not glass.

Close enough to understand name.

Dask cleaned cracked tide index.

Sori counted her own money where none of us could see total.

Healthy boundaries.

I wrote note to self:

**Old Tide Eye tomorrow. Lower gallery maybe public. Ask before climbing stupid amount of stairs.**

Then:

**Hara / conversion dispute: technically current table appears to support surcharge, but transition exception may apply to pre-effective sealed order. Provisional security 4c enabled release pending review. Buyer timing still required separate action.**

Not because job.

Because interesting.

Difference maybe thin.

That helped.

Outside, harbor chains clanked below.

Somewhere Hara was moving crate downhill before dawn.

Corren was probably worrying about tomorrow's countersign.

Both had done correct things.

Both still paid for system.

I had not solved it.

Good.

Maybe helping did not require owning ending.

Visitor lesson.

Tomorrow observatory.

If open.

If stairs didn't kill me first.
