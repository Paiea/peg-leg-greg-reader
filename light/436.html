TEXT READER · CHAPTER 338

THE PROSPECTOR

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Staying cost money.

Merehook made that clear before breakfast.

Two copper for floor bunk tonight.

Food bits.

Auction two days away.

Dask lecture tomorrow.

Maro wanted north cliff path if nesting wardens opened it.

Sori wanted to compare one more customs table because apparently rage could become hobby.

I wanted all of it.

Problem.

I had fifty copper.

Also leg costing one silver plus eighteen copper.

Also road.

Also no idea what silver meant in copper because world remained committed to being inconvenient.

I ate cheap oat cake.

"We staying?" Dask asked.

"Are you?"

"Lecture."

"That's tomorrow."

"Yes."

"So you're staying."

"Correct."

Maro said, "Cliff path maybe opens afternoon."

Sori drank tea.

"I paid for actual bed. I intend to use city around it at least another day."

Three answers.

None mine.

Good.

I went market.

Not to buy.

To look for money.

Different.

Merehook morning work board sat under customs arch.

Not jobs exactly.

Short labor.

**TWO HANDS / LOWER SALT STORE / 2C HALF DAY**

No.

**CLIFF STAIR SCRAPE / OWN TOOLS / 3C**

Also no.

**COUNTING HAND / FISH AUCTION / 2C + MEAL**

Maybe.

**UPPER ROPE HOUSE / LINE TAG SORT / PIECE RATE**

Interesting.

I asked board clerk.

"Rope house?"

"Menn Coil. Upper shelf east. They sort retired line tags before Survey surplus."

There.

Auction adjacent.

Not protagonist destiny.

Actual prep labor.

"Pay?"

"Depends lot. Usually two to four copper half day if you can read marks and don't steal."

"High standards."

"Merehook declining."

I took chit.

Menn Coil smelled like hemp, tar, salt, and old rain.

Warehouse open cliff side with rope racks from floor to roof.

Woman at desk had shaved head and three brass rings through one ear.

"Board chit."

I handed.

She looked at crutches.

Then me.

"Tag sorting seated."

Good.

"Can read coast marks?"

"Some. Not Merehook-specific."

"Can ask instead of invent?"

"Yes."

"Four bins. Keep. Scrap. Survey return. Unknown. Wrong bin costs me more than you earn."

"Comforting."

"I'm Pera Menn."

Name new enough.

Good.

She pointed table.

Crates of old line hardware.

Ceramic threshold tags.

Brass eyelets.

Lead seals.

Tiny iron frames.

My cliff-line keeper cousin had family reunion.

Pera caught face.

"Don't shop my scrap while paid."

"I wasn't."

"You were spiritually shopping."

Fair.

Work simple until not.

Most tags had clear marks.

Survey crest meant return.

Cracked ceramic scrap.

Current certified green slash keep.

No mark unknown.

Then old tags.

Numbers without units.

Symbols changed.

I asked.

Pera answered when knew.

When didn't, unknown.

Good system.

Half bell in, I found black ceramic oval with two dark crescents.

Not crack.

Load witness.

Old style.

Mark **8H**.

"Used?"

Pera glanced.

"Triggered twice maybe. Scrap."

"Can reset?"

"Heat. But those old ones drift after third reset."

"How know twice?"

"Crescent layering. First goes brown-black. Reset leaves ghost. Second dark rides ghost."

Useful.

My HT-LP-4 pressure crescent came to mind.

Different witness.

Same family of evidence.

I put scrap.

Pera watched.

"Most people pocket that after asking."

"Most people get paid?"

"Not after."

Good.

Second crate had wind-bowl throat inserts.

Broken.

Clouded.

Some chipped.

One looked intact.

I held to light.

Milky green.

Pera said, "Unknown."

"Looks good."

"Looks is why unknown. Survey rejected lot because response marks missing. Could be road-grade. Could be decorative replacement. Could be old factory second."

"Can test?"

"Not here."

Unknown.

I put unknown.

This was excellent anti-shopping training.

At second bell, warehouse alarm rang.

Not fire.

Three short.

Everyone stopped.

Pera walked cliff opening.

Wind bowls outside had changed tone.

Low to high.

Then high cut out.

"Shelf gust," she said.

Workers started closing outer shutters.

I moved because table nearest opening had loose paper tags.

Grabbed weight bar.

Both hands problem.

Crutches problem.

No trial leg.

I hooked left elbow around one crutch, right hand bar, left hand paper stack.

Ugly.

Worked.

Then gust hit.

Warehouse inhaled.

Loose rope ends lifted.

One hanging coil swung off peg.

Big.

Not deadly unless under.

Worker under.

"Down!"

He ducked.

Coil hit table edge and rolled.

I moved sideways badly.

Crutch tip slipped half hand on hemp dust.

Body corrected before thought.

Right shoulder into rack.

Left crutch recovered.

Coil rolled past.

Pera kicked chock under it.

Done.

Worker swore.

I swore.

Pera looked at me.

"You good?"

"Yes."

Shoulder sting.

No injury.

Crutch fine.

"You yelled first."

"Coil moved."

"Saw."

No medal.

Good.

Wind outside screamed maybe thirty seconds.

Then bowls dropped tone.

Pera reopened one shutter slit.

"Old bowl warned late."

"Which?"

She pointed road corner.

New bronze bowl still vibrating.

Older throat-glass one had gone high first but cut out before gust.

"Glass chipped last week. Should've been replaced."

"Still warned."

"Badly."

"Useful error?"

She looked.

"Observatory got to you."

Apparently phrase local enough.

We reset warehouse.

Worker I'd warned was named Torl.

He said thanks once.

Then made me help count fallen tags.

Correct relationship.

At midday Pera reviewed bins.

Three wrong.

Out of many.

One I had put scrap should have been Survey return because crest hidden under corrosion.

One unknown was keep because current maker mark tiny edge.

One keep was unknown because green slash counterfeit old reseller mark.

"Not bad," she said.

"How much?"

"Three copper."

"Board said two to four."

"You earned three. Four if no wrong. Two if five wrong. One if thief. Zero if stupid enough to argue after thief."

Clear economy.

She paid.

Fifty-three.

There.

Staying tonight now cost less emotionally.

I asked, "These bins going auction?"

"Some. Survey return doesn't. Keep doesn't. Scrap mostly weight sale. Unknown gets mixed inspection lots tomorrow."

"Public can inspect?"

"Auction morning, one bell before."

"Can test?"

"Depends lot. Powered items no. Passive mechanical usually if clerk permits. Charged glass no handling without bond."

Useful.

"Any point coming if I have fifty-three copper and need most of it?"

Pera laughed.

Not mean.

"Then don't bid like collector. Bid like worker. Decide what problem you're buying solution for before bell starts."

That one worth more than three copper.

"Also," she said, "Survey people love lots. They'll bundle one useful thing with six pounds garbage because paperwork hates small objects."

"Excellent."

"Bring bag."

"I'm not saying I'm coming."

"You asked four auction questions."

Fair.

I left.

Sori found me eating fish pie on wall.

"You got job."

"Half day."

"Employment shoulders."

"Temporary shoulders."

"How much?"

"Three."

"Good."

"Stop approving me."

"No."

She had papers.

Of course.

"Market pamphlet is outdated. Harbor customs current. Upper coach board missing transition note. I wrote one letter."

"One?"

"Three drafts. One sent."

"Growth."

"Fuck you."

Nice.

Maro came back with salt on boots.

"Path open."

"See spiders?"

"One."

He sat.

"Big?"

He held fingers maybe palm width.

"That's medium."

"For spider?"

"For fantasy spider."

"It lives on cliff."

"Everything gets points for location."

He had silk strand in folded paper.

"Legal?"

"Warden gave. From fence."

Good.

Dask spent morning university annex reading public lecture notice like it might change.

He arrived with two pages questions.

"You know lecture isn't interrogation."

"Questions are permitted."

"Two pages?"

"Not all."

Worse.

We compared plans.

Dask lecture tomorrow fifth bell.

Auction day after.

Maro wanted another cliff walk maybe.

Sori had no obligation but wanted see harbor customs archive because one clerk told her old tables stored there.

I had fifty-three copper.

Staying tonight two.

Tomorrow another two if staying auction eve.

Food bits.

Then auction temptation.

Then road afterward.

Could earn again.

Maybe.

Not guaranteed.

I looked at trial leg bundle back lodging.

Money wanted become leg.

Auction wanted become options.

Road wanted become more road.

Too many mouths.

I paid tonight bunk.

Two copper.

Fifty-one.

Decision one day at time.

No need prepay future curiosity.

Afternoon stable enough for trial wear.

I used twenty-five minutes in middle-shelf market lanes.

Not stairs.

Cargo ramps.

Medium foot on packed stone.

Crutches light.

Task: buy soap and carry small paper parcel from one stall to lodging.

One hand parcel.

One hand free enough for coin.

Crutches bundled only for final flat stretch.

Fourteen steps carrying parcel.

Stopped because gait got sloppy.

Not because pain.

That distinction felt important.

At lodging shell off.

Upper pink mild.

One faint lower-front line.

Gone at twenty minutes.

Note.

**Merehook middle shelf. 25 min. Packed stone/cargo ramps. Light parcel. 14 unsupported carry steps on final flat. Stopped on gait degradation. No pain. Faint lower-front line cleared within 20.**

No celebration.

Good data.

Evening I went back old-instrument lane.

Cliff-line keeper still six copper.

Seller recognized me.

"Still six."

"I didn't ask."

"Face did."

Apparently city studied faces.

I did not buy.

Delay frame still seven at observatory shop if I wanted climb again.

Did not.

Instead I bought nothing and wrote list.

Not object list.

Problem list.

**Need to measure timing between two events without trusting my count.**

**Need passive load witness where I cannot watch continuously.**

**Need warning before environmental change reaches me.**

**Need mobility that frees hands.**

Last one already had price.

One silver plus eighteen.

Others could wait.

That was maybe what Pera meant.

Don't buy object.

Buy solved problem.

Dask leaned over shoulder.

"You made procurement framework."

"I made list."

"Same."

"No."

"Functionally."

I closed notebook.

"Lecture tomorrow."

"Yes."

"Auction after."

"Yes."

"We might leave before auction."

He paused.

Good.

"Okay."

Actually okay.

Not manipulation.

"Do you want auction?"

"Yes."

"Enough to pay two nights and lose road day?"

He thought.

"I don't know yet."

"Good."

"Stop approving me."

"No."

Stole Sori.

Worked.

Outside, wind bowls started low song.

Not warning.

Just evening wind.

I had fifty-one copper.

Three more than morning after paying tonight? No.

One more than morning.

Work three.

Bed two.

That was road economics.

Not dramatic.

Important.

Auction in two days.

Leg farther away if I got stupid.

Maybe closer if I got useful.

Prospecting wasn't finding treasure.

Mostly deciding which holes not to dig.
