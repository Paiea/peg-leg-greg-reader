TEXT READER · CHAPTER 434

THE DECIDER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Auction cost started before auction.

Two copper for another night.

Food.

Time.

Opportunity to make worse decisions tomorrow.

I sat on lodging step with notebook open.

**Stay for auction:**

Cost at least two copper lodging.

Maybe more.

Maybe buy nothing.

Maybe buy something useful.

Maybe buy garbage because useful object attached to six pounds bureaucracy.

**Leave before auction:**

Save money.

Keep road moving.

Miss unknown opportunities.

Dask lecture fifth bell today regardless.

Maro cliff path today.

Sori archive maybe.

Me.

No obligation.

Annoying.

Decision harder without emergency.

Emergency decides for you.

Freedom makes paperwork.

I closed notebook.

Sori came out with tea.

"You made list."

"No."

"It's open."

"Former list."

"What are you deciding?"

"Whether auction worth staying."

"Do you want to stay?"

"That's not same question."

"I know."

Rude competence.

"Do you?" she asked.

I looked uphill where upper shelf buildings caught morning light.

Observatory somewhere beyond.

Coastal Survey offices farther east.

Auction tomorrow.

"Yes."

"Then what's actual question?"

"Whether wanting justifies cost."

"Better."

"Stop approving me."

"No."

She sat.

"I'm staying tonight. Archive clerk said I can see prior freight tables this afternoon."

"You know you don't work here."

"I know. That's why I can leave whenever annoyed."

Good argument.

Maro had already gone.

Dask was polishing tourist tide index.

For lecture.

I asked why.

"Presentation."

"You're not presenting."

"Still."

Fine.

I decided not to hunt another job.

One work day enough.

Every chapter didn't need employment.

Instead I went lower harbor.

Not because task.

Because after days of roads and shelves I had barely stood near Merehook water.

Cargo ramp down easier than stairs.

Still long.

Crutches warmed shoulders.

Trial leg stayed wrapped at lodging.

Recovery day.

Good.

Lower harbor was louder than Duskport in different way.

Less broad warehouse roar.

More cliff echo.

Hammer strike bounced between stone walls.

Gulls screamed and got doubled.

Wind bowls sang above ramps.

Water slapped pilings under cliff shadow.

Ships sat close because harbor mouth narrow.

Some had tall deep keels.

Some squat coastal tubs.

One black-hulled packet had two mastheads with glass fins that shimmered in crosswind.

Not decorative maybe.

I looked too long.

Dock porter said, "Wind vanes."

"Magic?"

"Little."

"What do they do?"

"Tell captain which layer lies."

Good sentence.

I asked.

He pointed.

"Lower fin reads deck wind. Upper reads mast wind. If different enough, you don't trust harbor bowl alone."

Layered warning.

Another problem solved by disagreement.

Merehook apparently built entire personality around not trusting one instrument.

Respect.

Hara found me near bonded cart yard.

She looked less furious.

Not happy.

Less furious.

"Security came back," she said.

"All four?"

"All four."

There.

"Appeal approved?"

"Transition exception. They stamped it before second bell."

"Good."

"Still paid night cart. Still lost sleep. Still almost missed ship."

"Also true."

"I filed notice complaint."

"Of course."

She narrowed eyes.

"You sound like woman I met yesterday."

Probably Sori.

"Dangerous city."

Hara laughed.

Better laugh.

Then pointed at me.

"You buying Survey junk tomorrow?"

Apparently whole city knew.

"Maybe."

"Don't buy cracked tension ceramic unless seller lets you heat-reset it first."

"Why?"

"Some darken pretty and tell nothing."

"Useful."

"I cut stone. We use line tags on lifts."

There.

Artifact knowledge from work, not lore dispenser.

"Auction worth staying?"

She shrugged.

"If you know why you're there."

Everyone same lesson.

Conspiracy.

"If you don't?"

"Then merchants know why you're there."

Worse.

I wrote that down.

She saw.

"You really do write too much."

"Apparently."

At third bell, harbor alarm changed.

Not emergency.

One long, two short.

Workers started moving carts away from outer quay.

I asked porter.

"Swell set."

"Observatory?"

"And outer bell. West stair spray probably up again."

Old Tide Eye warning route now ordinary city behavior.

Not dramatic.

Integrated.

Good.

I watched crews chain two light carts together before pulling uphill.

Why?

Wind.

Single empty cart easier to flip.

Paired carts heavier and stable.

Merehook answered environment with weird habits accumulated into normal.

That was what I wanted from travel.

Not different wallpaper.

Different grammar.

At midday I found auction posting outside Survey yard.

Public preview tomorrow first bell.

Auction second.

Terms:

cash settlement same day.

No credit under one silver lot.

Charged glass requires handling bond.

Certified Survey staff first-claim period ended today.

Lots posted by number only until morning inspection.

No refunds on `as retired` condition unless clerk records material misdescription.

There.

Real risk.

I copied rules.

Not lots.

Could decide attendance tonight.

Still.

Then human pressure found us anyway.

Near customs ramp, crowd around one wagon.

Not fight.

Argument.

Two fishermen.

One cart owner.

One harbor tally clerk.

Broken axle.

Cart loaded with iced whitefish.

Wheel canted.

Fish going bad if not moved.

Owner wanted unload onto customs floor.

Clerk said floor bonded for sealed import only, local fish not authorized there.

Fisherman said then let them block ramp while transferring.

Clerk said ramp had outbound tide cargo due.

Everyone had legitimate problem.

I stopped ten paces away.

Employment shoulders threatened.

I rolled them back.

Not mine.

Then one fisherman recognized Hara? No.

Recognized my crutches maybe.

"You know rigging?"

Bad question.

"Some."

"Can you tell if we can sling axle enough to roll cart?"

There.

Now bounded.

"I can look. Not certify."

"Fine."

Cart axle split near hub.

Wheel leaned but not detached.

Load maybe too much for improvised carry.

They had rope.

Bad rope.

Frayed.

I said, "Don't lift whole cart on that."

Owner said, "We just need six paces."

"Still whole cart."

Harbor clerk said, "There is dock jack twenty paces down."

Everyone stared.

Fisherman said, "Why didn't you say?"

"You were yelling about floor authorization."

Fair.

They fetched jack.

Problem changed.

Not solved.

Need support once raised.

Harbor carpenter had blocking.

Five minutes later they had wheel relieved enough to unload fish into two handcarts without blocking ramp.

I did nothing except say frayed rope bad.

Excellent.

Clerk looked at me.

"You Survey?"

"No."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"Survey would write form."

Merehook had opinions.

I left before becoming fish employee.

Fourth bell approached.

University annex uphill.

Dask lecture.

I met Sori outside.

She carried copies.

"Archive?"

"Yes."

"Fun?"

"No."

"Then why face?"

"Because old table changes explain three current abbreviations."

"Fun."

"Fuck you."

We entered.

Lecture room small.

Thirty seats.

Maybe eighteen people.

Students.

Two harbor workers.

One old woman knitting.

Dask front row.

Of course.

Speaker **Ilen Marr** arrived late with chalk dust on sleeve.

Mid-forties.

Hair tied poorly.

Talk title exactly what notice promised:

**TIDAL HARMONICS AND CLIFF-CHAMBER LAG IN MEREHOOK OBSERVATION SYSTEMS**

Dask looked spiritually fulfilled.

I prepared for suffering.

Lecture better than expected.

Ilen did not explain tides from first principles.

Blessing.

She focused on why cliff chambers distort timing.

Main idea:

A pressure signal entering narrow sea chamber does not arrive as simple copy of offshore wave.

Geometry stores and releases pressure.

Some frequencies amplify.

Some cancel.

Temperature changes gas pocket compressibility.

Cracks alter effective chamber volume.

So two instruments connected to same sea can disagree without either being broken.

Dask wrote violently.

I wrote one sentence:

**Delay can belong to medium, not source.**

That mattered.

Magic.

Body.

Tools.

If external effect arrived late, maybe not weak attempt alone.

Could be channel.

Could be environment.

Could be me.

Could be all.

Useful caution.

Not breakthrough.

After talk, questions.

Dask had two pages.

He asked one.

Good restraint.

"If chamber geometry changes slowly, how do you distinguish instrument drift from medium drift?"

Ilen smiled.

Actual good question.

"You don't from one instrument."

Of course Merehook answer.

"You need independent reference, repeated conditions, or controlled excitation if system permits. Usually all three badly."

Dask nodded like receiving sacred text.

Someone else asked about rebuilding old chamber.

Ilen said impossible without changing thing measured.

Another asked whether magical damping could stabilize response.

"Yes," Ilen said, "and then you've built different instrument."

Good.

After lecture, Dask cornered her.

Not literally.

Socially.

He showed tourist tide index.

Ilen said, "That's souvenir."

"I know now."

"Good souvenir."

Dask glowed.

She asked where from.

Merehook market.

Then told him maker copied housing from an old South Shelf training dial.

So fake had lineage.

Dask loved it more.

I left him there.

Sori went with me.

"You got something from that."

"Maybe."

"What?"

"Delay can belong to medium, not source."

She waited.

"Magic?"

"Maybe."

"Leg?"

"Maybe."

"People?"

I looked at her.

"Unfortunately."

She laughed.

Evening decision.

Back lodging.

Maro returned from cliff path with no purchases and one sketch of spider web geometry.

Dask returned with lecture notes and no new object.

Miracles.

We ate cheap stew.

I put money on blanket.

Fifty-one copper.

If stay tonight, two copper.

Already tonight? Wait.

Current lodging paid through morning from last chapter.

Need tonight to reach auction tomorrow.

Two copper.

Forty-nine.

Auction budget separate.

Road after.

Leg.

I asked group.

"I'm staying auction."

Sori said, "Okay."

Dask said, "Also."

Maro thought.

"I might leave after morning cliff path if north cart goes."

There.

Not hive.

"Without us?"

"Maybe."

"Where?"

"Salt farms east. One day."

Interesting.

Sori said, "I might stay one more day after auction if customs archive lets me copy old table set."

Also interesting.

Party could fragment.

Good.

I paid two copper for night.

Fifty-one to forty-nine.

Decision made.

Auction tomorrow.

Not because artifact destiny.

Because I wanted to see what became available, understood cost, and chose it anyway.

That felt different from impulse.

I wrote auction cap.

Not total spending cap yet.

Problem caps.

**Timing tool:** max 5c unless complete enough to use immediately.

**Passive load witness:** max 4c unless reset state demonstrated.

**Environmental warning:** max 4c unless portable and independent.

**Unknown lot:** max 2c, only if one identifiable useful component already worth that.

Then one more:

**Do not spend leg money because auction brain says rarity.**

Hard to quantify.

Important.

Dask leaned over.

"You made procurement framework again."

"Auction limits."

"Same family."

"Go away."

"We're sharing room."

Terrible design.

Maro said from floor, "I might go salt farms."

Dask looked up.

"Before observatory auction?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because road."

I stared.

He looked at me.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Sori laughed so hard she had to put cup down.

Fuck all of them.

Outside, evening wind bowls started low song.

Merehook stayed itself.

Tomorrow auction.

After that maybe salt farms.

Maybe Sori archive.

Maybe Dask stays with instruments until city ejects him.

Maybe I leave alone for a day.

That possibility landed differently than expected.

Not bad.

Just real.

Travel together did not mean same road forever.

I had chosen to stay.

For one day.

Decision didn't need become identity.

Just next move.
