Harrow Cape was closed.

Solved one.

Dawn board had red strip nailed across route line.

**CROSSING HELD / NORTH SWELL / RECHECK TOMORROW**

Bellweather coach still two copper.

Old Merrow packet still four.

Dask stood beside board.

"Bellweather."

"I know."

"Second bell."

"I know."

"Old relay room."

"I know."

He looked at me.

"You don't want Bellweather."

"I want boat."

There.

No reason grander.

I had been on roads.

Wanted sea.

Old Merrow meant island town.

Half day if sea kind.

Four copper.

Twice Bellweather.

Also boarding with crutches.

Also trial leg not boat leg.

Also new place.

Dask folded coach notice.

"I'm taking Bellweather."

"Good."

"You taking packet?"

"Yes."

"Good."

No weird face this time.

Growth.

We ate breakfast.

At second bell he left.

Not dramatic.

He had pack, cracked tourist tide index, two pages of old lecture questions he still had not thrown away, and enough confidence to interrogate beacon relay town alone.

"Send note if you buy broken beacon," I said.

"I won't buy broken beacon."

"That's not what Sori said."

"She said don't let you buy one."

"Technicality."

"Accuracy."

Coach bell rang.

He climbed aboard.

"Greg."

"Yeah?"

"Road Response Kit is not auction trash."

"It currently lies two times in ten."

"Uncharacterized artifact."

"Five-copper liar."

"Retired comparative demonstrator."

"Bye, Dask."

"Bye."

Coach left north.

Now one.

Interesting.

I bought Old Merrow ticket.

Four copper.

Thirty-nine to thirty-five.

Ticket clerk looked at crutches.

"Lower boarding gate. Tell deckhand before gangplank."

"Why?"

"Upper plank steeper. Lower has cargo ramp."

Good.

"Trial limb?" she asked, seeing bundle.

"Shop property. Not for wet deck."

"Keep wrapped."

"Plan."

Packet **Narrow Gull** sat lower bay.

Two masts.

One low black hull.

Broad enough for cargo.

Passenger deck aft under partial awning.

Smelled fish, tar, wet rope, lamp oil.

Homey if home hated furniture.

I told deckhand.

He looked at crutches.

"Cargo ramp. Wait after handcart."

No fuss.

Handcart rolled aboard.

Then me.

Ramp wide.

Ribbed wood.

Wet.

Crutch tips held okay if vertical.

I took it slow.

Deckhand did not grab me.

Also good.

At top he said, "Aft bench. Rail left. Don't use loose coil as handhold."

"Wasn't planning."

"Everyone says that."

Boat people.

I liked them already.

Passengers maybe fourteen.

Two traders.

Family with sleeping toddler.

Three young men in matching blue work coats.

Older woman carrying basket covered cloth.

Thin man with red scarf who looked seasick while tied to dock.

Promising.

Cargo included flour sacks, two crates marked lamp glass, barrels, bundled reeds, one locked iron chest with customs seal.

Not mine.

Important reminder.

Captain gave safety speech.

Short.

"North swell. We run lee route around South Tooth. If I say sit, sit. If crew says move, move. Red line on deck is working lane. Stay out. If sick, rail leeward, not windward unless you want it back."

Useful.

Red-scarf man closed eyes.

We left third bell.

Lerrin pulled away.

Beacon field changed from infrastructure to pattern.

White South Head.

Amber inland.

Green north.

From water, spacing made sense differently.

Lines not town.

Directions.

Old life had sea travel.

Different ships.

Different magic.

Same truth.

Land looked simpler from water than living on it.

Water looked simpler from land than being on it.

Narrow Gull cleared bay.

Then moved.

Actually moved.

Coach motion was road translated through wheels.

Boat motion came from everywhere.

Up.

Down.

Roll.

Yaw.

Small constant corrections.

Crutches terrible.

I stayed seated.

Correct.

Trial leg stayed wrapped.

Very correct.

Red-scarf man lasted maybe twelve minutes.

He went leeward rail.

Captain's advice saved him from learning weather personally.

I ate nothing.

Also correct.

First hour quiet.

Then coast broke into black teeth.

South Tooth apparently one giant basalt spine sticking sea.

Narrow Gull went inside it where water dark but flatter.

Crew changed sail.

One of blue-coat men pointed.

"Glassbacks."

I looked.

Nothing.

Then water bulged.

Long translucent ridge under surface.

Not fish exactly.

Maybe fish.

Fantasy classification dangerous.

Second ridge surfaced thirty paces out.

Clear dorsal plate caught light like smoked glass.

Then vanished.

"Dangerous?" I asked.

Blue coat shrugged.

"If netting. Not packet."

"Why?"

"They follow charge lines. Nets catch fins. Boats too loud."

Good enough.

Magical ecology without lecture.

We rounded South Tooth.

Sea got worse.

Not storm.

Swell.

Long heavy rise.

Narrow Gull climbed.

Dropped.

My stomach filed complaint.

Denied.

Everyone sat.

Crew didn't.

Old woman basket screamed.

Not woman.

Basket.

Cloth moved.

Goat.

"Don't start."

Not far.

"Hold lane!"

"Crew only."

Correct.

No Greg.

Good.

Not broken.

Crew fixed.

Done.

Not relevant.

Keep pack closed.

Harder than sounded.

Use me.

After South Tooth, route turned west around low island chain.

Then water.

Artifact.

Three grooves.

One bead trembling center.

He tapped plate once.

"What is that?" I asked deckhand when he passed.

"Wake shear plate."

"Measures?"

"Cross-current disagreement under hull."

"Bow wants one way. Stern wants another. Plate tells captain how rude water is being."

Good explanation.

"Accurate enough to know when stop being clever."

Not for sale.

I wanted one anyway.

Problem list item maybe.

No.

Don't multiply wants by seeing nouns.

"Old Merrow is north of that cut."

"You're going south."

"Temporarily."

"Why?"

"Shear."

"Looks fine."

"Good. You can navigate looks from your seat."

Not because captain charming.

Because boat still captain's.

Not loud.

"Calls cistern crews when wet wind crosses east ridge."

Better.

Black apparently not normal.

Raised when shouldn't be.

Captain ignored.
