TEXT READER · CHAPTER 359

THE STRANGER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Rain made waiting smaller.

Old Merrow disappeared.

Harbor wall.

Chain house.

Terraces.

All gray.

Narrow Gull sat under south wall with anchor line pulling steady.

Vessa breathed easier.

Not normal maybe.

Better.

Deckhand checked twice.

She complained both times.

Good sign.

"I am breathing," she said.

"We're enthusiastic about that," he said.

Goat sneezed.

Also breathing.

Rain hit awning hard enough conversation needed effort.

Captain stayed forward.

Crew watched harbor through glass.

I watched because nothing else.

Black-white signal changed.

Black.

White.

White.

Then black again.

Deckhand swore.

"What?"

"West clutch moved. Didn't hold."

I had no idea how he read that from flags.

Didn't ask yet.

Passenger trader did.

"So chain down?"

"No."

"Then what does white mean?"

"Depends sequence."

"Helpful."

"It is if you work harbor."

Good.

Another twenty minutes.

Rain tongue sounded through rain.

Lower now.

Long note.

Captain came aft.

"We're taking shore tender."

Passengers looked up.

"Chain won't be ready before high-water turn. Harbor can ferry passengers and hand luggage over inner wall landing. Cargo stays aboard until chain drops."

Trader said, "My goods?"

"Stay aboard."

"I have delivery window."

"Your goods have hull. People don't need to sleep in mine."

Fair.

Captain pointed at me and Vessa.

"You two first tender."

Not special Greg.

Mobility and breathing.

Reasonable.

I said, "Lower transfer?"

"Yes. Tender comes starboard lee. Two crew hands. You keep crutches unless you want help."

"Keep."

"Fine."

Vessa said, "I can walk."

Captain said, "Then walk into tender when told and save argument for land."

She muttered something rude.

Alive.

Tender arrived as squat open boat with canvas hood.

Harbor crew in yellow oilskins.

Transfer looked bad.

Not dangerous bad.

Annoying bad.

Packet rose half pace.

Tender rose different half pace.

Gap changed constantly.

Deckhand showed timing.

"Wait for level. Step when I say."

I couldn't step normally.

Right foot to tender bench.

Both crutches handed separately? Better.

I looked.

"Can I sit transfer?"

Deckhand blinked.

Then nodded.

"Better."

Good.

I sat packet edge when tender came level.

One harbor crew steadied shoulder only after I said yes.

Lowered onto tender bench.

Crutches passed.

Pack passed.

No heroics.

No falling.

Vessa came after, much angrier about assistance than me.

Goat basket last.

Goat screamed at sea.

Sea ignored.

Tender crossed maybe sixty paces.

Chain visible under water once rain thinned.

Thick dark links stretched across harbor mouth maybe two feet below surface.

Would absolutely ruin hull.

Good captain.

Inner wall landing low and broad.

We disembarked via wet stone ramp.

Crutches worked if slow.

Old Merrow officially.

No bell.

No sign.

Just wet island.

Harbor clerk took packet tickets and wrote **PASSENGER LANDED / CARGO HELD**.

Trader behind us argued from second tender before feet dry.

Not my problem.

Vessa leaned on wall.

"You staying where?"

"No idea."

"Excellent planning."

"It's going well."

She pointed uphill.

"Merrow House cheap if you don't mind cistern bell. Two copper bed. One copper loft behind bakery if still open."

"Which do you use?"

"My daughter."

"Cheaper."

"Usually."

Goat sneezed again.

"You need help?"

"No."

Then she looked uphill.

Rain.

Steep terrace.

Basket.

Breathing.

"Maybe with goat."

There.

Actual ask.

I could carry goat basket badly with crutches.

Could strap to pack maybe.

No trial leg in rain.

Not worth.

Harbor porter cart stood nearby.

Two wheels.

One copper lower harbor to third terrace according board.

I pointed.

"Porter cart."

Vessa grimaced.

"One copper for hill I have climbed forty years."

"Today you breathed weird on boat."

"Rude."

"Accurate."

She paid one copper herself.

Good.

Porter loaded basket.

Goat objected.

I walked beside cart because same direction maybe.

Old Merrow terraces were water first.

That became clear immediately.

Every roof fed gutter.

Every gutter fed stone channel.

Every channel crossed lane under little grates into wall cisterns.

Rainwater moved everywhere but almost none loose.

Road edges had carved lips.

Steps had center grooves.

Door awnings drained into ceramic jars.

Children in rain capes cleared leaves from gutter mouths with sticks.

Not emergency.

Routine.

Rain tongue sounded again above.

People changed behavior.

Two shopkeepers rolled open side channels.

Cistern crew in blue belts ran uphill carrying hooks.

Vessa noticed me looking.

"East wet."

"Meaning?"

"Rain tongue says wind carrying enough water to fill high cisterns. Lower crews open spill paths before top tanks overflow."

"Every rain?"

"Not every rain. Wrong wind runs off west cliffs."

Specific.

Good.

"How old?"

"Tongue? Older than me. Re-skinned three times."

"Magic?"

"Some. Mostly tension cloth and charged ridge needles. Ask someone who likes explaining."

She did not.

Perfect.

Third terrace split.

Porter turned left.

Vessa pointed right.

"Bakery loft there."

I looked left.

"Your daughter?"

"Two streets."

"You good?"

She stared.

"I have lived here forty-eight years."

"That's not breathing answer."

Pause.

"Better."

"Good."

"You worry like old aunt."

"I had one."

Maybe.

Different life.

She touched my arm.

"Thank you for drops."

"You told me what to do."

"Most men hear old woman shake and become stupid."

"I am stupid in specialized ways."

She smiled.

"Peg-Leg Greg."

"Stop."

Porter cart left.

Vessa left.

No new party.

Good.

I found bakery loft.

One copper.

Floor mat.

Dry roof.

Shared wash room.

Paid.

Thirty-five to thirty-four.

Pack down.

Trial leg checked dry.

Road Response Kit dry.

Everything else damp edges only.

Good.

I ate bread heel and fish paste for bits.

Then rain eased.

Old Merrow came back.

Town climbed maybe five terraces above harbor before ridge farms.

Not large.

Dense.

Stone retaining walls pale gray.

Roofs green tile.

Cistern mouths marked colored ceramic disks.

Blue full?

Red restricted?

Didn't know.

Stranger.

Important.

I went outside on crutches.

No guide.

No Dask asking system questions.

No Sori reading signs faster.

No Maro noticing ecology first.

Just me.

Weird.

First thing I did was misread sign.

**UPPER CATCH / THIRD RING / PUBLIC DRAW AFTER SIXTH**

I thought ring meant terrace.

Local boy said, "Bell ring."

"Ah."

He kept walking.

Humbling efficient.

I found harbor overlook.

Chain still up.

Narrow Gull anchored outside.

Cargo trapped.

Two other small boats waited inside unable leave.

Not just packet delay.

Island exchange pinched both directions.

Harbor board had chalk notices:

**CHAIN HOUSE WEST CLUTCH / SHEAR PIN FAILED**

**REPLACEMENT PIN FROM UPPER MILL / RUNNER SENT**

**PASSENGER TENDER ACTIVE**

**CARGO HOLD UNTIL MECHANICAL CLEARANCE**

There.

Specific failure.

Not mystery.

Shear pin.

Designed failure maybe.

Better than breaking chain machinery.

One fisherman said to another, "Should've had spare in house."

Other said, "Used last spare spring storm."

"And nobody replaced?"

"Mill order delayed."

Human problem.

Maintenance debt.

Not corruption.

First fisherman spat water.

"Now my ice boat sits inside and mainland bait sits outside."

Second said, "Complain to chain committee."

"I am on chain committee."

Excellent.

I kept walking.

No employment shoulders.

Harbor market smaller than Merehook.

Island goods.

Dried kelp cakes.

Rain cloth.

Ceramic water markers.

Rope treated with something silver.

One stall sold cistern floats.

Not artifacts exactly.

Some charged.

One object caught eye.

Small clay disk with blue center.

Seller said, "Leak singer."

"What?"

"Stick against cistern wall. If water passes crack behind it, center whistles."

"How?"

"Porous salt clay around charged reed bead."

"Reusable?"

"Until salt clogs. Then soak fresh water."

"Price?"

"Three copper."

Useful.

Portable environmental warning.

My list.

Three copper under old cap four.

But problem specific to water leaks.

Do I have cistern?

No.

Could detect seep behind wall maybe.

Still.

I did not buy.

Seller did not care.

Growth.

At fifth bell, rain tongue sounded one short note.

People relaxed.

Cistern crews came downhill wet.

One woman carried broken ceramic float and swore continuously.

Different voice variety.

"Third ring's full," she told stall seller. "Fourth nearly. West catch gave us mud because somebody thinks grate cleaning is a philosophical position."

Seller said, "You want float?"

"I want Dera to clean grate. Float won't fix Dera."

Good.

She noticed me staring at leak singer.

"Don't buy that for travel."

I looked.

"Why?"

"Because seller will tell you it hears all leaks. It hears clean-wall seep in quiet room. Put it on ship hull and it'll sing because world is wet."

Seller said, "I did not say ship."

"You thought ship."

"I thought sale."

Honest.

I laughed.

"Thanks."

Woman shrugged.

"I'm Rell Aster. Upper cistern crew."

"Greg."

She looked at crutches.

Then at me.

No nickname.

Good.

"Visitor?"

"Very."

"Then don't drink from red-mark spouts. Those are wash water."

Useful.

"Blue?"

"Public draw. White private. Yellow restricted during low reserve."

"Thank you."

"Also bakery loft woman cheats blankets."

"I noticed blanket thin."

"See."

Then she left.

No quest.

Perfect.

Evening chain dropped.

I heard before saw.

Harbor bell three slow.

People near market looked toward water.

Narrow Gull entered maybe half bell later.

Cargo unloading began immediately in rain-dark.

Trader sprinted toward dock like delivery had personally insulted him.

Vessa's goat probably already home.

I went down because curious.

Chain house door open.

Workers carried broken shear pin out.

Iron cylinder maybe forearm long, snapped clean at groove.

Designed weak point confirmed.

Harbor mechanic held replacement.

New pin bright.

Fisherman chain-committee man argued with him.

"Why no spare?"

Mechanic said, "Because last spare went in spring storm and requisition sat at upper mill waiting batch forge."

"We approved emergency stock."

"You approved purchase. Treasurer delayed because mill surcharge doubled."

"Because you ordered one instead of three."

"Because budget approved one."

There.

Nobody wholly wrong.

Maintenance was money.

Redundancy cost money before failure.

After failure everyone loved redundancy.

Familiar everywhere.

I stayed outside argument.

Good.

Then saw something else.

Broken pin groove had a dark ceramic ring around center.

Witness material maybe.

Load indicator?

Harbor mechanic saw me looking.

"Shear witness."

"Shows overload?"

"Shows whether pin failed at rated shear or cracked early."

"Can tell now?"

"Tomorrow after dry. Wet darkens everything."

Useful.

"If failed rated, replacement and move on?"

"Mostly. If early, inspect alignment before trusting gate."

There.

Artifact integrated into mundane maintenance.

Not treasure.

Evidence.

My kind of thing.

I almost asked more.

Stopped.

Stranger.

Mechanic had work.

I went uphill.

At loft, I considered trial leg.

Floor dry.

Body okay.

But day had packet transfer, crutches, hills.

No need.

Rest.

I unpacked Lot 67 instead only enough to check moisture.

Dry.

No experiment.

Sticky mark still liar.

I wrote notes.

**Old Merrow chain gate: designed shear pin failed. No spare at house because prior spare consumed + replacement delayed by small-batch surcharge/budget. Cargo both directions held. Passenger tender workaround. Evidence ring on pin distinguishes rated shear vs early crack after drying. Redundancy is cheap only after failure.**

Then stopped.

No report requested.

Just mine.

Solo travel strange.

Nobody asked where tomorrow.

No group table.

No shared plan.

I had thirty-four copper.

Old Merrow had upper ridge farms, west rain catch, small packet routes, and apparently an inland quarry road according wall board.

Also next Narrow Gull departure maybe two days.

No obligation.

No companion to default toward.

I lay under cheating blanket.

Thin indeed.

Rain tongue silent.

Cistern water moved inside walls.

Somewhere below, chain gate worked again.

For tonight.

Stranger meant nobody knew what I was useful for.

That was freeing.

Also inconvenient.

Good.
