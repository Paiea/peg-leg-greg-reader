Morning solved the water before it solved the bridge.

**SHORE BOAT / HARBOR VILLAGE / 1C / SECOND RING**

Under it:

**FOOTBRIDGE CLOSED**

Good.

I paid one copper.

Twenty-nine to twenty-eight.

Road by boat.

Ternhaven apparently preferred not choosing categories.

Lio Pell left before me.

Her crab cages went inland on local reed cart because crab route used south mud track I was not stupid enough to attempt.

Orric came by with his aunt.

Not Vessa.

Different aunt.

He had red cap cleaned badly and green vest cleaned worse.

"You leaving?"

"Trying."

"North ferry goes Kelp Isles."

"I heard."

"Dorrin has huge kelp bell."

"What's kelp bell?"

"Huge bell. For kelp."

Excellent information.

His aunt said, "Orric."

"What?"

"Stop advertising islands to strangers."

"He's already stranger."

Technically.

I waved.

No emotional rescue reunion.

Good.

Shore boat was flatter than yesterday's.

Six passengers.

Two sacks mail.

Three baskets smoked fish.

One wooden crate full of ceramic floats.

Crutches aboard first.

Pack second.

Me seated transfer third.

Getting less embarrassing mostly because I stopped assigning embarrassment to correct technique.

That felt useful.

Boat hugged coast.

Salt Mouth looked worse from water.

Two missing bridge spans.

One remaining support tilted downstream.

Workers had ropes across both banks and a red board nailed to each approach.

Nobody crossing.

Good.

A narrow pole skiff moved through inner channel carrying lumber.

Bridge repair starting.

Not my problem.

Also good.

Ternhaven main harbor appeared after maybe half bell.

Not much bigger than East Cove.

Busier by factor ten.

Low stone quay.

Flat-roof sheds.

Long racks running parallel shore.

Green everywhere.

Kelp.

Not pretty decorative kelp.

Industrial kelp.

Wet bundles hung from racks.

Dry sheets stacked under weights.

Workers carried black ropes dripping green water.

Boilers steamed behind sheds.

A sharp mineral smell sat over everything.

Fish plus salt plus hot leaves.

Ternhaven smelled like ocean being cooked into paperwork.

I liked it.

Shore boat docked at low steps.

Bad for me.

Worker pointed side ramp used for handcarts.

Better.

I used that.

Town board stood directly off quay.

**NORTH FERRY / DORRIN / TOMORROW THIRD RING / 4C / TIDE CHECK SECOND**

**NORTH ROAD CART / FERRY HAMLET / TODAY FIFTH RING / 1C**

**SOUTH LIGHT / WALK ONLY**

**OLD SALT ROAD / CLOSED HEAVY CARTS**

There it was.

Dorrin tomorrow.

Four copper.

And one more copper just to get north ferry hamlet unless I wanted walk.

Twenty-eight.

Five more to keep going.

Twenty-three.

Same as Vale.

That symmetry annoyed me.

I did not buy anything yet.

First find town.

Main lane had no stairs.

Thank every god interested in civil engineering.

Boardwalk over damp ground.

Packed shell roads.

Low buildings because wind.

Channels everywhere because tides.

At intersections, short poles held little white ceramic paddles.

Paddles turned not with wind but with current in shallow channels below.

One side painted green.

Other black.

I asked kelp worker.

"Tide paddles?"

"Drain paddles. Green means channels pulling out. Black means sea pushing in."

"Town floods?"

"Town negotiates."

Good.

Ternhaven identity.

Not stop water.

Negotiate.

Kelp sheds used that same rhythm.

Workers moved fresh kelp onto lower racks while drainage green.

When paddles turned black, they lifted lower bundles higher.

Not magic exactly.

Then I saw magic.

Long drying rack had thin blue stones set every armspan.

Each glowed faintly where kelp touched.

"What do those do?"

Worker looked like question obvious.

"Pull charge out."

"Why?"

"Because charged kelp cooks uneven."

"Kelp is charged?"

"Some."

"From what?"

He shrugged.

"Sea. Storm. Glassbacks. Deep lines. Depends kelp."

Useful uncertainty.

"Blue stones tell how much?"

"No. They bleed it. Red ones tell. Blue cheaper."

There.

Artifact infrastructure as ordinary processing.

I did not ask to buy stone.

Progress.

Maybe.

Breakfast market sold kelp cakes.

I bought one with bits.

Bad.

Not inedible.

Just aggressively ocean.

Woman selling them said, "You need hot sauce."

"Why sell without?"

"Tourists learn."

I added sauce.

Better.

A man at next stall had a tiny brass device clamped to table.

Black bead.

Track.

Not same as Lot 67.

Similar enough my brain turned.

Repair stall.

Sign:

**CLOCKS / TIDE COUNTERS / DRYING TIMERS / SMALL MECHANICALS**

I stared.

Then remembered five-copper liar.

Leg money.

I walked over anyway.

Not every approach purchase.

Old woman behind bench wore thick magnifying lens over one eye.

"What broke?"

"Maybe nothing. Maybe friction."

"Those are different prices."

Good opening.

I unpacked Road Response Kit.

Careful.

Set it down.

"Retired road response demonstrator. Bought surplus. False hangs near mark four around two runs in ten. Manual immediate trigger still does it. Orientation reversal still does it. I can see bead pause between three and four. I haven't opened track."

She looked at me.

Then tool.

"Good."

"Good it's broken?"

"Good you didn't fill it with lamp oil."

Pera vindicated.

She put lens down.

Did not touch yet.

"Inspection one bit. Opening two bits. Repair quoted after."

Reasonable.

I paid one bit.

She examined externally.

Ran bead with a little ivory pick through side slot.

Slow.

Stopped near fourth mark.

Again.

"Track burr."

"Certain?"

"No. Likely."

Excellent woman.

"Could also be bead flat. Need open to tell."

"Two bits?"

"To open and close without losing spring."

I paid.

Bits, not copper.

She opened back plate with proper driver.

Inside looked simpler than expected.

Tiny brass channel.

Black stone bead captured between guide lips.

Small return spring.

At fourth mark, one guide lip showed bright scratch.

She rotated bead with pick.

One side also had faint flat.

"Both."

"Of course."

"Burr catches flat when they meet. That's why intermittent."

"Fix?"

"Dress burr and turn bead. Might hold. Better bead replacement if you want reliable."

"Price?"

"Dress and reorient, one copper. Replacement bead three if I have right size. I don't."

One copper.

I had twenty-eight.

This was owned tool already.

Repair could make five-copper purchase useful.

But one-copper fix remained provisional.

"If you dress it, can we test before I trust it?"

"Obviously."

I liked her.

"Name?"

"Mera Tonn."

"Greg."

"Peg-Leg?"

Fuck this island chain.

I closed eyes.

"Who told you?"

"Vessa came through last night and bought lamp wick from my sister."

"Of course."

"You don't have peg."

"Thank you."

"Bad branding."

"Future-proof."

"Worse branding."

I paid one copper.

Twenty-eight to twenty-seven.

Mera used tiny stone file on guide lip.

Barely touched it.

Then rotated bead so flat no longer aligned same way under return.

No replacement.

No magic.

Maintenance.

She reassembled.

"Now test."

I did manual immediate reference twenty times.

All below mark one.

Not proof fixed forever.

Better.

Then another twenty.

One run rose just over one.

No false fourth-mark hang.

Mera said, "Forty isn't forever."

"I know."

"Write repair. If problem returns, bead."

I wrote:

**Ternhaven, Mera Tonn. Opened with proper tool. Burr at guide lip near mark 4 + faint bead flat. Burr dressed, bead reoriented. Post-service manual immediate: 40 runs, 0 false fourth-mark hangs; one slightly >1 mark. Provisional improvement only. Continue characterization before body/magic use.**

There.

Artifact improved through expertise and money.

Not miracle.

Still not authority.

Worth one copper.

Probably.

Mera pointed trial-leg bundle.

"That broken too?"

"No. Shop property."

"Whose shop?"

"Hark and Venn, Duskport."

She whistled.

"Far."

"Getting farther."

"Then don't fall in sea. Shipping property back annoying."

Practical sympathy.

I packed Lot 67.

Town noon.

Twenty-seven copper.

North road cart fifth ring.

Could stay main harbor for one copper maybe.

Could go north ferry hamlet for one.

Dorrin tomorrow four.

I walked harbor edge to decide.

Not long.

Maybe twenty minutes crutches.

Found kelp grading yard.

This was Ternhaven's real money.

Bundles arrived wet and got divided not just by species but by charge behavior.

Some stayed dull against red tester strip.

Some made strip glow orange.

One flashed white and worker immediately isolated whole basket.

"Bad?"

I asked from outside rail.

Grader said, "Expensive."

Of course.

"Why?"

"High charge kelp goes battery vats. Food kelp can't carry that much."

"Battery vats?"

"Dorrin."

Kelp Isles processed charge-rich kelp into something valuable enough for dedicated vats.

Maybe storage medium.

Maybe industrial charge.

I didn't know.

Wanted to.

Four copper.

Plus cart.

Plus bunk.

Road eating leg.

Counted.

Twenty-seven.

If north cart one, twenty-six.

North ferry bunk maybe one.

Twenty-five.

Dorrin four tomorrow.

Twenty-one.

Then food bits.

Then whatever after.

Still less than half North Reach.

No conversion.

Not home tomorrow.

But southward.

Merehook eventually.

Duskport.

Carrow.

Nothing wrong with that.

One copper.

Twenty-seven to twenty-six.

No Lio.

No Orric.

No named future friend required.

Actual low fern.

Knee height for standing people.

Chest height for me seated cart.

"Free if you don't count posts, thread, cleaning, storms, thieves, and waiting."

Economics universal.

North side drier.

Wind stronger.

That was new.

Island felt temporary under feet.

**DORRIN / THIRD RING / 4C / TIDE CHECK SECOND**

**OUTER ISLES FREIGHT / ASK MASTER**
