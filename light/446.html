THE INVESTOR

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Second ring said tide good.

Of course it did.

Would have been easier if weather made decision for me.

North Ferry board changed from chalk question to black tile.

**DORRIN / THIRD RING / 4C / RUNNING**

Twenty-five copper in pocket.

Twenty-three Vale.

Twenty copper already sitting in another city pretending to be future leg.

Four copper to keep going.

I stood under board long enough ferry clerk looked at me twice.

"You buying?"

"Thinking."

"Do it somewhere left."

Fair.

I moved left.

Thought.

Twenty-one after fare.

Then probably one copper bed.

Maybe food stayed bits.

Maybe Dorrin expensive.

Maybe return cost more.

Maybe work existed.

Maybe not.

Could go south.

One copper cart back Ternhaven harbor.

Three copper eventually Old Merrow if packet.

Then Lerrin.

Then Duskport.

Then home.

Not impossible.

Not failure.

Actually responsible.

I hated it.

Good sign? Bad sign?

Probably neither.

I pulled notebook.

Not because accounting needed drama.

Because money lied when feelings rounded numbers.

**Cash 25c.**

**Vale 23c.**

**Limb deposit paid 20c. Remaining quote 1 silver + 18c, mixed denomination unresolved.**

**Dorrin ferry 4c.**

**Likely remaining after fare 21c.**

Then under that:

**Why go?**

Charge-rich kelp.

Battery vats.

Bleed-pot system.

Kelp Isles.

Regional market.

New route.

Curiosity.

Then:

**Why not?**

Money.

Leg.

Return cost.

No job.

No obligation.

No one waiting there.

I stared.

That last one should have counted against going.

Instead it counted for.

Problem.

Or point.

I bought ticket.

Four copper.

Twenty-five to twenty-one.

Ferry clerk bit coin.

"Dorrin only?"

"Yes."

"Passenger deck forward. Pack under bench unless crew says move it. No touching bleed pots."

"Wouldn't."

"Everyone says that."

Boat workers everywhere had same religion.

The ferry was **Low Lantern**.

Wide.

Shallow.

Ugly in useful way.

Two square sails.

Long lattice racks over center deck.

Six ceramic bleed pots set between cargo lanes.

Blue seams glowed faint even daylight.

Cargo came first.

Wet kelp in open baskets.

Dry rope bundles.

Three sealed black crocks marked charge warning.

Crab cages.

One narrow crate with brass corners and no label.

Not mine.

Again.

Passenger deck forward had eight benches.

Maybe twenty people.

Workers mostly.

Two families.

One woman with ledger case.

Three young men carrying hooked kelp knives wrapped in leather.

Old man with a basket of onions.

Nobody looked adventurous.

Excellent.

Dock was dry.

Boarding ramp broad.

I considered trial limb.

Could use it for boarding, maybe hands free for pack.

Wet deck after departure still problem.

No.

Crutches.

Crewman took pack after asking.

I boarded.

Simple.

Ferry pushed off third ring.

Ternhaven north shore flattened behind us.

Kelp water opened ahead.

Not open water exactly.

Fields.

That was first difference.

Long dark bands stretched between low islands.

Some natural.

Some clearly arranged.

Lines of floating white markers.

Wood stakes.

Net corridors.

Boats moving between them like carts in roads.

Kelp farms.

Actual sea agriculture.

Low Lantern followed cleared lane.

Captain didn't steer by visible land alone.

He watched pole markers with colored caps.

Blue.

White.

Yellow.

No red.

I asked passenger beside me.

Woman with ledger case.

"Lane markers?"

She nodded.

"Depth and charge restriction. Blue ordinary. White shallow draft. Yellow high bleed."

"High bleed?"

"Kelp beds carrying too much residual charge for unshielded compass stones."

"So ferry has pots."

"So ferry has pots."

She looked at my crutches.

Then pack.

Then stopped caring.

Good.

"Dorrin worker?" I asked.

"Assessor."

"Kelp?"

"Everything wet people argue is kelp."

Messy enough answer I liked her.

"Greg."

"Nara Kesh."

Distinct name.

Thank god.

Nara opened ledger.

Conversation over.

Also good.

We crossed first yellow lane maybe half bell later.

Crew changed something at bleed pots.

Not opening.

Rotated ceramic collars.

Blue seam brightened.

Compass stone near helm flickered once.

Then settled.

I watched.

Captain noticed.

"Passenger."

Me.

"Yes?"

"You can look. Don't lean over cargo line."

"Understood."

Good boundary.

Bleed pot function clearer in motion.

Kelp baskets themselves had faint prickling light in wet folds.

Not glowing dramatically.

Little flashes when water ran.

Blue pot seams brightened as ferry passed yellow markers.

Then faded after.

Charge transfer?

Suppression?

Grounding into something?

Didn't know.

Could ask later.

Not invent.

Halfway, one of young kelp workers dropped metal hook.

It hit deck near black crock.

Nothing happened.

He swore anyway.

Crewman shouted.

"Wrap your steel!"

Worker: "It slipped."

"And if jar seam jumps?"

"It didn't."

"Great. Put that on tombstone."

Worker wrapped hook.

Human systems same everywhere.

Rules existed because somebody got tired of almost dying.

Sea changed closer Dorrin.

Islands lower.

Some barely more than platforms of root and stone.

Structures stood on piles.

Long drying frames.

Wind towers with hanging ceramic ribbons.

Floating workyards tethered between islands.

One island had no houses, only enormous black vats under roofs.

Battery vats maybe.

Nara looked up when I stared.

"Bleed works."

"Dorrin?"

"Outer yard. Main market ahead."

"Those charge storage?"

"Those prevent charge storage where you don't want it."

Correction.

"Where does useful charge go?"

"Depends grade. Some into ceramic cells. Some bled into sink stone. Some sold wet to people who like problems."

"People buy dangerous kelp wet?"

"People buy everything if they think margin is hiding in it."

True across worlds.

Dorrin appeared not as island town but cluster.

Five low islands connected by bridges, floating lanes, ferry slips, drying platforms.

Central market roof enormous.

Green-black, maybe kelp thatch.

Three tall lattice towers rose over it with blue glass at top.

No city wall.

No single harbor.

Water inside everything.

Low Lantern entered through marked channel.

Then stopped before dock.

Of course.

I sighed.

Nara glanced.

"First time?"

"Apparently my face says delays."

"This isn't delay."

Crew lowered long pole into water.

Second crew checked pot seams.

Captain waited.

Then one tower glass shifted blue to clear.

Ferry moved.

"What was that?"

"Charge gate."

Nara closed ledger.

"Dorrin doesn't let loaded kelp boats bunch inside market basin if bleed balance is high. They meter entries."

"Traffic light for magical seaweed."

She stared.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Need local metaphors.

Dock dry enough.

Disembarking easier.

Twenty-one copper.

Dorrin.

Immediately louder than Ternhaven.

Not people.

Things.

Wood racks knocking.

Ceramic bells.

Water slapping piles.

Winches.

Vent hisses.

Someone shouting lot numbers.

Market smelled salt, iodine, smoke, vinegar, wet rope, hot clay.

Good.

Different.

Very different.

I moved slowly off dock.

Board at entrance:

**DORRIN MARKET BASIN**

**PASSENGER LODGING -> ISLAND THREE**

**NORTH OUTER FERRY -> ISLAND FIVE**

**CHARGE GOODS DECLARATION BEFORE COVERED MARKET**

**UNMARKED WET KELP MAY BE SEIZED / TEST FEE TO OWNER**

Below:

**PUBLIC WORK BOARD / ISLAND TWO**

I noticed that.

Not because desperate.

Because twenty-one copper.

Maybe little desperate.

First lodging.

Island Three connected by low arched bridge.

Bridge wet but textured stone.

Crutches fine.

Bunk house charged **2c**.

Dorrin expensive.

Of course.

I asked.

"Anything one?"

Keeper pointed down lane.

"Net loft if space. One copper. Smells worse."

"How worse?"

"You stop noticing."

Perfect.

Net loft had space.

One copper.

Twenty-one to twenty.

Twenty.

Same number as deposit.

Interesting psychological cliff.

I paid.

Bed was rope platform above stacked dry nets.

Smelled tar and sea rot.

Could have been worse.

Probably would be later.

Pack stored.

Trial limb still wrapped.

Lot 67 safe.

I had afternoon.

Could wander market.

Could work board.

Could rest.

Money voted work board.

Island Two board covered jobs.

Most not mine.

**KELP HAUL / STRONG BACK / WET WORK / 7C DAY**

No.

**VAT SCRUB / WATER BOOTS PROVIDED / 5C HALF**

Tempting pay.

Bad limb/crutch environment.

No.

**LEDGER COPY / CLEAR HAND / 2C PAGE SET**

Maybe.

**LOT RUNNER / FAST FEET / 4C RING**

Funny.

No.

**DRY RACK TAG SORT / SEATED OK / 3C HALF SHIFT**

There.

Posted by **Rusk & Mere Charge House**.

Mere.

Not Merrow.

Fine.

I took chit.

Charge house on Island Four.

Worker at desk looked at crutches.

"You read marks?"

"If you show key."

"Write?"

"Yes."

"Count?"

"Usually."

She didn't laugh.

"Half shift pays three. Mistakes on red tags cost us more than you make, so don't guess."

"Good policy."

"You work here before?"

"No."

"Then slower than you think."

Also good.

Her name **Peth Orin**.

Voice clipped.

Work simple enough.

Dry rack tags came in baskets.

Ceramic slivers with punched edge codes and painted charge grade.

White ordinary food.

Blue utility fiber.

Yellow residual charged.

Red isolation.

Black rejection.

Numbers for weight lots.

My job sort by grade, then sequence rack numbers, flag duplicates or missing pair.

Seated table.

No magic.

No lifting beyond small baskets.

Perfect.

First basket twenty-seven tags.

One duplicate 18-yellow.

Missing 19-yellow.

I flagged.

Peth checked.

"Good."

Second basket had black tag painted over blue.

I stopped.

"Which wins?"

"Black."

"Even if blue under?"

"Especially if blue under. Means downgrade after retest."

Local rule.

Third basket two red tags stuck together by salt.

Separated carefully.

No problem.

Half shift maybe two bells.

Paid three copper.

Twenty to twenty-three.

That felt enormous.

Not actually enormous.

But direction changed.

For first time since North Reach, road didn't only eat.

Small thing.

Important.

Peth said, "Tomorrow?"

I hesitated.

Could earn again.

Could stay.

This is how city catches you.

Not prison.

Work.

Bed.

Interesting market.

Then suddenly week.

"Maybe."

"Maybe doesn't reserve chair."

"Fair."

"Board first ring."

Done.

Not buying.

Looking.

Rope fiber.

Charge gel in sealed crocks.

Dry conductive braid.

Storm-scorched fronds sold to artificers.

Kelp glass.

Thin green-black translucent plates made from ash and charged binder.

Plate bent slightly.

Returned.

"Gauge face, lamp shield, low-pressure viewing slit. Not building window unless you hate house."

"Cost?"

"This size six copper."

Problem list did not need six-copper weird glass.

Small brass clamps with ceramic jaw inserts.

Two copper each.

Used to shunt residual charge from narrow wet braid during repair.

Actual portable environmental/tool interaction.

Also tempting.

He said, "Depends what you clamp."

"Useful."

"You asked bad question."

Fair.

I did not buy.

Twenty-three copper now.

Do not reward earning three by spending six.

Training brain for money.

At one stall I saw artifact that genuinely made me stop.

Not flashy.

Palm-sized gray disk with four shallow grooves.

Mounted on cork.

Label:

**RESIDUAL FIELD WITNESS / OLD DORRIN DRYING HOUSE / 9C**

Problem list number three maybe.

Warning before environmental change reaches me?

"Records strongest directional charge crossing surface since reset. Grooves darken in quadrant."

Passive witness.

"Depends age."

"No."

"Known reference?"

"Old drying house used them for lane comparison, not absolute measure."

Interesting.

Nine.

No.

Still no.

Flat boards.

Standing.

Short transfers.

Not normal.
