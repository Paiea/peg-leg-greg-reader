THE DECKHAND

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Silt Mare arrived early.

Suspicious.

Black Reed did not do convenient without charging.

I was eating fried kelp edge outside Reed House when Mett Sarl shouted from quay.

"Dorrin boat! First ring!"

Yesterday: fifth-day second ring maybe.

Today: first ring.

Good.

Then Mett added:

"North load canceled!"

There.

Convenience invoice.

I got pack.

Trial leg wrapped.

Crutches.

Cash twenty-four.

Ten copper floor.

Return fare?

I reached quay.

Silt Mare boatmaster stood beside half-empty lattice racks looking irritated enough to ferment kelp.

"Dorrin?" I asked.

"Yes."

"Fare?"

"Three."

Expected.

"Why early?"

"Because north load isn't coming."

"Why?"

"Because north rack broke loose in night tide and half their cut is currently visiting somebody else's lease."

Material consequence.

"Anyone hurt?"

"No. Pride injured. Kelp missing."

"Boat still leaves?"

"If we can get off this dock."

I looked.

Problem.

Not Silt Mare.

Floating kelp.

A lot.

Dark ribbons packed against Black Reed's outer piles, pushed in by tide.

Loose field cut.

Maybe north load.

Maybe somebody else's.

It had wrapped around lower guide posts and drifted under Silt Mare stern.

Boatmaster said, "Rudder's clear now. Channel isn't."

Workers had long hooks.

They were pulling mats away from launch gap.

Wet work.

Not mine.

I paid fare anyway.

Twenty-four to twenty-one.

Ticket in pocket.

North mainland ferry tomorrow from Dorrin.

If we left.

I sat on quay edge outside work lane.

Mett came with hook.

"You going?"

"Trying."

"Good luck."

"Thanks."

He looked at wrapped trial leg.

"Could use extra hand on tally."

I looked at wet workers.

Bundles being hooked out, thrown onto slats, marked by colored lease ties when ties remained.

Some had no ties.

"Paid?"

"One copper if you stay until channel clears."

"I already bought ticket."

"Then sit and earn waiting."

Excellent.

"What tally?"

"Recovered cut. Tie color, bundle count, loose weight estimate. Don't assign owner if tie gone."

Familiar principle.

Different function.

"Fine."

I moved to dry crate.

Boatmaster shouted, "Passenger working doesn't delay departure."

"Wouldn't dream."

"Everyone dreams."

I took slate.

Blue tie.

Two bundles.

White tie.

One.

Loose mat.

Unknown.

Green tie, frayed.

Three partial.

Workers hauled.

Tide kept feeding more.

Not dramatic.

Then one hook caught.

Worker pulled.

Something pulled back.

He froze.

"Not kelp."

Everyone nearby stopped.

Hook line moved sideways.

Fast.

Worker let go.

Correct.

Hook vanished under mat.

Mett swore.

Water bulged.

Silver ridge.

Glassback?

No.

Smaller.

Wider.

Then head surfaced.

Flat triangular thing with translucent side fins and black mouth.

Kelp wrapped around body.

One hook snagged through mat beside it, not animal maybe.

Animal thrashed.

Silt Mare stern bumped pile.

Boatmaster yelled, "All passengers back!"

I was passenger.

Also tally worker.

Passenger won.

I moved back.

Crutches on wet-splashed boards.

Careful.

Animal hit mat again.

Kelp mass shifted.

One worker slipped to knee.

Caught pile.

Fine.

Another shouted, "Sailray!"

Name.

Useful.

"Cut mat!" Mett yelled.

Worker with knife started sawing loose kelp around trapped animal.

Sailray thrashed toward gap.

Wrong direction.

Toward dock.

Could not know dock bad.

Boatmaster grabbed pole and pushed mat outward.

Animal hit pole.

Pole snapped.

Not weapon fight.

Still action.

I watched.

Old-life combat brain offered trajectories.

Current body offered crate.

Use brain.

Not heroics.

The mat had tension.

Two workers pulling shoreward on opposite side while animal drove outward.

That pinned it between forces.

"Stop pulling shore side!" I yelled.

Mett looked.

"Why?"

"You're tightening wrap when it pushes out! Give it slack!"

Could be wrong.

But visible geometry.

Mett looked once.

Then shouted, "Drop hooks! Give slack!"

Workers released.

Kelp mat relaxed.

Sailray surged.

Almost free.

One ribbon still across fin.

Knife worker reached.

Too close.

Animal snapped tail.

Tail had glassy barb.

Oh.

"Back!"

He did.

Barb hit board where hand had been.

Good.

Now problem.

Can't safely cut close.

Could wait animal tire.

Could push whole mat channel out with boat poles.

But Silt Mare trapped by mat.

Mett said, "Need open fin."

Boatmaster: "Need not get stabbed."

Correct priorities.

I had line quietener.

Irrelevant.

WS-F/9 maybe water current direction, but no need.

HT-LP-4 no.

Lot 67 no.

Magic?

Maybe.

No.

Not experiment around animal/workers.

Crutch?

Long object.

Absolutely not sacrificing mobility tool into sailray barb.

Think.

Kelp ribbon over fin attached to larger loose mat.

If cut mat farther away, reduce drag without approaching animal.

"Can you cut ribbon two paces back?" I asked.

Mett followed line.

"Maybe."

Worker used hooked knife on long handle.

There.

Actual tool.

He caught ribbon farther from animal.

Sawed.

Sailray thrashed.

Rope-like kelp stretched.

Cut.

Mat separated.

Animal shot backward, fin still wearing short kelp strip, but free enough.

Tail slapped water.

Gone under outer piles.

Everyone waited.

No second attack.

Mett said, "Sailrays eat runners. Usually don't come inside lease posts."

"Usually."

"Loose cut brought it."

Maybe.

Likely.

Don't canon certainty.

Boatmaster looked at snapped pole.

"That's coming out somebody's freight."

Mett: "Which somebody?"

"Whoever owns kelp."

Mett pointed unknown loose mat.

"Excellent. Invoice ocean."

Good.

Workers laughed.

Tension dropped.

Then one worker looked at board.

Barb scratch.

Shallow groove in wood.

"Venom?" I asked.

Mett said, "Burns. Not usually kills."

"Good."

"Unless throat."

"Less good."

No one hit.

Back to work.

Channel still blocked.

Action did not solve labor.

Correct.

We cleared for another bell.

I tallied.

Unknown bundles became problem.

Lease owners arrived arguing ownership by cut pattern.

Mett refused assignment without ties or field marks.

Not another arbitration.

He simply put unknowns under basin hold.

Later problem.

At second ring, launch gap open.

Boatmaster shouted boarding.

Mett paid one copper.

Twenty-one to twenty-two.

"You yelled useful thing," he said.

"I sat useful too."

"Mostly that."

Fair.

I boarded Silt Mare.

Wet ramp.

Crutches.

Trial leg stayed wrapped.

Seat.

Black Reed pulled away.

I looked at quay.

Mett already arguing about unknown kelp.

No farewell.

Good.

Silt Mare headed Dorrin.

Outer islands passed backward.

Weir.

Lark.

At Lark, boat took three passengers and one cage of kelp runners.

They clicked like cutlery.

One escaped cage.

Tiny silver bug sprinted across deck.

Passenger screamed.

Crewman stepped on basket, not bug.

Bug ran under bench.

I lifted crutch tip.

No.

Not stabbing edible magic bug with mobility equipment.

Girl with cage caught it using hat.

"They bite?" I asked.

"Only if hungry."

"Is it hungry?"

"Always."

Great.

Dorrin appeared around fourth ring.

Basin towers first.

Then racks.

Then noise.

Felt almost big now.

Which was ridiculous.

I'd known it two days.

Travel compressed familiarity weirdly.

At ferry board:

**NORTH MAINLAND / SIXTH DAY / 6C / BERTHS 11**

Still.

Tomorrow.

I had twenty-two copper.

If buy ticket: sixteen.

Above ten floor.

Then bunk tonight one: fifteen after both.

Maybe food bits.

Mainland unknown lodging.

Thin.

Not impossible.

I asked clerk destination.

Had not actually asked before.

"North mainland where?"

She pointed line.

**BRINEWARD**.

"Brineward."

"Yes."

"What's there?"

She looked at me like ticket clerk asked tourism board question.

"Port."

"Useful."

"Salt road east. Shelf road north. Coaches inland when road dry."

"Work?"

"Port."

Right.

"Return?"

"Eighth day scheduled. Weather permitting."

Two-day minimum if went.

No dinner return.

Good.

Dangerous.

"Ticket now?"

"Six. Berths nine now."

Two sold.

I could wait morning.

Risk sellout.

Could buy now.

Cash twenty-two.

Ten floor.

Six ticket leaves sixteen.

Bunk one leaves fifteen.

Still five above floor.

I bought.

Six copper.

Twenty-two to sixteen.

There.

North mainland.

Actual.

Ticket folded in notebook.

Departure sixth-day second ring.

Lower boarding available if requested.

Good.

I paid net loft one copper.

Sixteen to fifteen.

Ten floor meant five discretionary.

Five.

Residual Field Witness seller was still market.

I did not go.

Growth.

Maybe fear.

Same behavior.

Could test.

No.

Actually yes.

Needed legitimate timing reference.

Not certified.

Already did forty clean after repair.

More immediate tests would increase confidence in sticky-failure removal.

Useful.

Dry flat board.

Same orientation ten, reversed ten.

All below mark one.

No fourth-mark hangs.

Cumulative post-repair immediate manual tests now sixty clean.

That mattered.

Still not calibrated.

Still operator manual.

Still no seconds.

**Lot 67 post-service immediate manual reference: +20 clean, 60/60 clean cumulative. No mark-four hang observed. Repeatability improved under immediate trigger. Still no interval calibration; no body/magic inference.**

Good.

Could next characterize actual delayed intervals using external reference someday.

Not tonight.

Trial leg had rested one day? Wore previous Black Reed day Ch448, Ch449 today no wear. One rest day effectively current day. Could maybe wear at loft, but travel tomorrow and boat. Better rest.

I cleaned liner instead.

Then magic.

Maybe.

Had rested since Old Merrow ridge external 136.

Clean loft conditions.

No people close? Shared loft.

Bad.

Could use yard downstairs?

Dry enclosed rack courtyard after work.

No need.

Important not turn availability into obligation.

Dinner.

Bits.

Brineward.

Six copper.

Fifteen cash left.

Ten floor.

Not desperate.

Thin.

Postage bits.
