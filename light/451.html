TEXT READER · CHAPTER 347

THE COURIER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Survey coach had six wheels.

That seemed excessive until I saw Shelf Road.

Then it seemed optimistic.

I arrived Brineward Survey Transit before first ring with pack, crutches, wrapped trial leg, fifteen copper, twenty-three Vale, and a temporary employment relationship with three sealed weather packets.

Woman at dispatch desk held them up.

"Dry."

"Yes."

"Sealed."

"Yes."

"You don't open them."

"That was implied by sealed."

She stared.

"I say it because people open them."

"Then useful."

Her name was **Mira Solt**.

No smile.

Good.

She handed me leather packet case with waxed flap and shoulder strap.

"Case is Survey property. Grey Post signs receipt. You copy road manifest at each staffed gate if changed. Driver owns movement. Gate officer owns road status. You own packets and paper."

"Excellent division."

"Don't improve it."

Brineward loved that instruction.

Coach driver was **Jeren Pike**.

Pike.

Not Jorren.

Names could rhyme without conspiracy.

He was narrow man with gray scarf and one missing front tooth.

"Lower seat," he said, pointing rear bench.

"Thanks."

"Don't thank me. Front axle throws mud."

"Still thanks."

Two other passengers rode under Survey permits.

A weather-glass technician named **Elsa Marr** with padded case.

Marr.

Again.

World had surnames.

And a road mason named **Kerr Holt** carrying tool roll and enough lunch for siege.

Nobody knew me.

Beautiful.

We left first ring.

Brineward flattened behind us.

Shelf Road climbed almost immediately.

Not mountain yet.

Long rising shelf above coastal plain.

Stone road, broad enough two freight carts, drainage trenches both sides.

Every half mile stood low posts with three ceramic fins.

White.

Amber.

Black.

I watched third one.

"Wind?" I asked.

Elsa looked.

"Shear posts."

"Measure?"

"Indicate."

Good distinction.

"White face means lower road wind within ordinary cart tolerance. Amber means high-side gusts. Black face turns when cross-shear exceeds freight rule."

"Charged?"

"Top fin lightly. Bottom two mechanical. Charge helps hold peak direction long enough to read after gust passes."

Useful.

Not exact instrument.

Road climbed.

At mile four, first staffed gate.

**LOW SHELF HOUSE**.

Jeren stopped.

Gate officer checked coach seal, road board, passenger count.

No change.

I copied manifest line anyway because job said each staffed gate if changed.

Actually no change meant no copy.

I almost copied.

Stopped.

Mira: if changed.

Good.

We continued.

At mile seven, sea behind us looked flat and stupidly far.

Brineward already small.

Carrow invisible by several systems.

Road bent north.

Wind increased.

Coach suspension earned all six wheels.

Front pair tracked shallow ruts.

Middle carried weight.

Rear pair kept long body from pitching as hard over stone joints.

Probably.

I wasn't wagonwright.

Ask.

"Why six?" I asked Jeren at water stop.

"Because four breaks more axles up here."

Simpler.

"Long wheelbase?"

"Middle axle takes belly load. Rear keeps packet cases from learning flight."

Good.

At mile ten, road changed.

Less grass.

More exposed gray stone.

Wind came sideways hard enough to push coat.

I was seated.

Fine.

Crutches tied under bench by my own straps.

Trial leg wrapped in pack.

Fine.

A shear post showed amber.

Next amber.

Third black.

Jeren slowed before gate appeared.

**THREE PIN GATE**.

Staffed.

Stone shelter.

Two coaches already waiting.

Gate officer raised hand.

Stop.

Jeren stopped.

I waited.

Nobody asked me to assess wind.

Beautiful.

Gate officer climbed aboard.

"Road status changed. High-side gust closure from Pin Twelve to Pin Sixteen. Minimum half bell."

There.

My job.

I opened ledger.

Original:

**BRINEWARD -> GREY POST / OPEN / HIGH WIND ABOVE M10 / SURVEY TRANSIT**

New:

**THREE PIN HOLD / HIGH-SIDE GUST CLOSURE P12-P16 / START SECOND RING LESS QUARTER / REVIEW HALF BELL**

I showed officer.

He corrected `high-side` to `cross-shear`.

I corrected.

Good.

No ego.

Packets dry.

We waited.

Half bell.

Wind did not improve.

Black fin stayed showing.

Officer had proper instruments inside shelter.

I did not touch.

Elsa did not touch either, despite weather technician.

Different authority.

Good world.

Kerr ate first lunch.

"That's first?" I asked.

"Road lunch."

"How many?"

"Depends road."

Fair.

Another quarter bell.

A freight driver started arguing.

Not dramatic.

He had salt medicine under priority mark.

Needed Marsh Shelf clinic before afternoon.

Gate officer said closure applied all high-sided freight.

Driver said his wagon was low-sided.

Officer said load stack made it high profile.

Driver said tarps could come off.

Officer said loose medicine crates in crosswind worse.

Correct procedure.

Human cost.

Clinic waiting.

No easy answer.

Then another wagon arrived from north.

Empty.

Low flat bed.

Gate officer looked.

Talked to drivers.

Could medicine crates transfer to low bed and send north driver back with them?

Medicine driver objected to custody transfer.

Priority seal required named carrier.

Gate officer checked rule book.

Could re-seal under emergency carrier substitution with two witnesses and clinic receipt.

That took time.

But moved medicine safely.

Not Greg solution.

I watched paperwork happen.

My manifest changed because road lane reopened only for low-profile authorized traffic under escort.

I copied:

**LIMITED RELEASE / LOW PROFILE ONLY / ESCORT PIN12-P16 / GENERAL COACH HOLD**

Jeren stayed.

Our coach high enough.

Medicine went.

Driver stayed behind with original wagon, furious and relieved at same time.

Good emotion.

Third ring approached.

We had been held more than one bell? Started second less quarter. Now near third. Roughly one and quarter rings maybe. Bell/ring system unclear. Don't calculate exact.

Survey contract delay pay only after first day at staffed gate.

Not relevant.

Jeren said, "If we don't clear by third-and-half, Grey Post before dark gets ugly."

"Return?"

He laughed.

"You were told same-day not guaranteed?"

"Yes."

"Believe them."

I did.

Ten copper floor.

Fifteen cash.

Pay five if delivered.

No pay until Grey Post.

Gate shelter had floor bunks for stranded Survey staff if overnight.

Wouldn't cost maybe? Infrastructure, but don't assume unless happens.

Wind shifted.

Black fin turned amber.

Then black again.

Officer checked inside.

Waited.

Another ten minutes? Don't use minutes if local time system. A short while.

Then amber held.

Officer came out.

"Test coach through Pin Twelve to Fourteen. Survey six-wheel only. No passenger transfer. Driver discretion."

Jeren said, "Mine?"

"Yours."

I disliked phrase test coach.

"Are we testing road?" I asked.

Jeren: "We're testing whether I hate my job today."

"Results?"

"Preliminary."

He checked wheel straps, axle pins, roof ties.

Gate officer asked passengers individually if we wanted remain at gate for next general release.

Elsa stayed aboard. Instrument delivery.

Kerr stayed. Mason job.

Me?

Packets.

Could remain and packets delayed.

Could ride authorized test transit.

Driver accepted.

Gate officer authorized.

This was not reckless improvisation.

I stayed.

"Crutches tied?" Jeren asked.

"Yes."

"Case strap around you, not bench. If coach rolls, bench goes somewhere too."

Comforting.

I changed strap.

We moved.

Pin Twelve.

Wind.

Hard.

Coach leaned.

Not tipping.

Enough body notice.

Jeren slowed to crawl.

Six wheels suddenly made sense viscerally.

Middle axle kept body from rocking as freely.

At Pin Thirteen gust hit.

Coach shifted half lane.

Kerr grabbed overhead rail.

Elsa hugged case.

I braced right foot and stump? Trial leg not on. Left residual limb not weight. Seat bracing with intact right + hip/back. Packet case across chest.

Jeren swore once.

Steered into gust.

Old-life combat brain woke.

Locked-in reserve maybe not needed.

But perception narrowed.

Wheel sound.

Body roll.

Next gust visible in grass? Sparse scrub flattened before hit.

I saw it.

"Left coming," I said.

Jeren already saw.

"I know."

Good.

I shut up.

Driver drives.

Gust hit.

Coach held.

Pin Fourteen shelter appeared.

We stopped behind stone wall.

Jeren exhaled.

"I continue if Fourteen officer clears north side."

Officer checked road.

North of Fourteen dipped behind ridge.

Less exposed.

Cleared to Pin Sixteen at slow transit.

We went.

No monster.

No broken axle.

No Greg heroics.

Still adrenaline.

At Pin Sixteen general road reopened behind us shortly after.

We had gained maybe little.

Mostly proved route.

Grey Post appeared late afternoon.

Not town first.

Towers.

Three thin weather towers on shelf edge, each with rotating glass vanes.

Then stone yard.

Coach sheds.

Low houses behind wall.

Road split:

**BRINEWARD SOUTH**

**HIGH SHELF NORTH**

**QUARRY ROAD WEST**

There.

Bigger map again.

Survey office had level rear entry as promised.

I got off by crutches.

Packets dry.

Seals intact.

Mira's case intact.

Desk clerk checked three seals one by one.

Signed receipt.

"Transit clerk?"

"Temporary."

"Best kind."

"Agreed."

He counted five copper.

Fifteen to twenty.

Good.

Real.

Not miracle.

I handed Survey case back.

Job done.

No field authority acquired by osmosis.

Desk clerk name **Oren Tull**.

He stamped my chit closed.

"Return coach?"

I asked.

"Tomorrow maybe. Today's returns held by shear closure."

"Maybe?"

"Depends road."

Of course.

"Cheap sleep?"

"Road House floor, two copper tonight. Closure crowd. Survey delay bunk only for active employees held under contract. Your delivery closed contract."

Excellent distinction.

No free protagonist roof.

Two copper.

Twenty to eighteen.

Still eight above floor.

Could afford.

I paid Road House.

Twenty to eighteen.

Wait, already said. Yes.

Road House floor was packed.

Freight crews.

Two quarry workers.

Woman with cage of pale birds that all faced windward even indoors.

Magic ecology.

I put pack under wall.

Trial leg still wrapped.

Rested two days since Brineward wear? Ch450 wore, Ch451 travel next day, so one day rest after wear by evening. Could leave.

Stable floor but crowded.

No need.

Crutches fine.

I went outside before dark.

Grey Post shelf edge view enormous.

South: road descending toward invisible sea.

North: land rose again in long gray steps.

High Shelf.

Wind towers clicked softly.

At public board:

**HIGH SHELF COACH / WEATHER PERMIT / 5C / NEXT DEPARTURE EIGHTH DAY**

Not seven from Brineward because Grey Post closer.

**QUARRY ROAD / WEST CUT / WORK WAGONS / PERMIT OR CONTRACT**

**WEATHER YARD DAY HAND / 3C HALF / DRY COPY + FLAG RESET / APPLY FIRST RING**

Flag reset sounded physical maybe poles. Dry copy possible.

Could ask.

Another notice:

**INSTRUMENT COMPARISON HOUR / PUBLIC BENCH / FIRST BELL AFTER DAWN / SMALL TIMERS, PRESSURE TILES, ROAD WITNESSES / 1 BIT BENCH FEE / NO CERTIFICATION**

Oh.

There.

Lot 67.

Legitimate delayed-interval reference opportunity.

Public comparison bench.

Not certification.

Small fee bits.

I read twice.

Tomorrow.

Return coach maybe.

High Shelf next day? Eighth day. What day now? Seventh? Brineward north mainland sixth day Ch450 arrival likely sixth. Ch451 next morning maybe seventh day. High Shelf departure eighth day tomorrow? Board says next eighth. If current seventh, yes next day. Need avoid calendar confusion. State can phrase next scheduled High Shelf coach on eighth day, exact relation likely following day.

Five copper.

Cash eighteen.

Floor ten.

If buy five leaves thirteen.

Then lodging maybe.

Thin.

Again.

Could work half for three.

Could compare Lot 67.

Could return south.

Could go north.

Grey Post had compressed choices into one board.

I liked it.

Bad sign.

I walked to weather-yard fence.

Not inside.

Public side had demonstration clock.

Mechanical pendulum? No.

A drip-wheel interval drum behind glass.

Marks around wheel.

Sign:

**PUBLIC REFERENCE DRUM. CHECKED EACH FIRST RING. COMPARATIVE USE ONLY. NOT LEGAL TIME STANDARD.**

Perfect.

Not tonight.

Could use tomorrow bench under staff procedure.

Lot 67 might finally move from immediate trigger to delayed intervals.

Still not body/magic.

Good.

Wind tower above clicked.

I thought about external attempt 136.

Could use Lot eventually to measure response delay maybe.

Not yet.

Characterize tool first.

Old me would have loved this place.

Current me also did.

Annoying continuity.

At Road House I ate stew with bits.

Kerr Holt appeared.

He had found mason bunk elsewhere then came for stew.

"You staying?" he asked.

"Tonight."

"High Shelf?"

"Maybe."

"Cold."

"Useful."

"That's not opposite of cold."

"I know."

He ate.

No invitation.

No party.

Good.

Elsa had gone weather office.

Jeren coach yard.

Temporary road people dispersed into functions.

I wrote home note.

**Grey Post. Shelf Road. Survey packets delivered, earned 5c. Cash 18c, Vale 23c. Wind closure delayed road but we got through under gate clearance. Nobody hurt. Ten floor intact. High Shelf exists north. Not committed. - Greg**

Then:

**There is a public instrument comparison bench tomorrow. This is unfortunately exciting.**

Postage bits.

Communication delay now ridiculous.

Cala might receive Dorrin note while I was already here.

Tavi might think I was on island when I was mainland shelf.

Home knew versions of me delayed by roads.

That felt stranger than distance.

At floor bunk, I checked money.

Eighteen.

Ten floor.

Eight discretionary.

High Shelf five.

Comparison bench bits.

Potential half-shift three.

Return unknown.

Limb money far away conceptually and literally.

Still mine.

Still waiting.

I touched wrapped trial build.

"Sorry," I whispered.

Man beside me said, "What?"

"Talking to leg."

He moved blanket slightly farther away.

Reasonable.

Courier.

That title actually fit more than recent ones.

I carried three packets from one office to another.

Got paid.

Nearly got blown sideways in process.

No secrets opened.

No kingdom saved.

Tomorrow I could test a broken old timing demonstrator against a public drum for one bit.

And maybe pay five copper to climb farther north.

Adventure had become very expensive errands.

Sori would probably say that counted now because dinner at home was no longer plausible.

Damn her.
