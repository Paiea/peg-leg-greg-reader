THE MEASURER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Instrument Comparison Hour had rules.

Good.

Rules meant someone had already made expensive mistakes before me.

Grey Post weather yard opened the public bench after dawn.

One bit.

I paid.

Bench clerk stamped my hand card.

"No certification."

"Understood."

"No legal claims from bench result."

"Understood."

"No opening instruments on the table."

"Mine already opens."

"Then don't."

"Understood."

Her name was **Vera Halm**.

Gray hair.

Ink on three fingers.

Voice like every extra noun cost money.

I liked her immediately.

Public bench held one drip-wheel interval drum behind glass, two mechanical reference flags, one pressure-tile cradle, one reset basin, and a chalk board with comparison procedure.

Not laboratory.

Not certification office.

Enough.

Other users included a road guard with two wind witnesses, a young artificer testing a pocket timer, and a farmer with a pressure tile that apparently blamed his wagon for something it could not explain.

Good crowd.

I set Lot 67 down.

Vera inspected exterior only.

"What are you testing?"

"Delayed interval repeatability."

"Against?"

I pointed to drip drum.

"Good. What are you not testing?"

"Seconds. Absolute accuracy. Magic response. Body response. Anything legal."

She nodded.

Passed first test.

Drip-wheel drum worked simple enough.

Reservoir released fixed drops into a weighted cup.

Cup tipped wheel one notch after accumulation.

Each marked interval repeated by mechanism.

Bench sign said it was checked each first ring against yard reference.

Comparative only.

Perfect.

Lot 67 had flexible tongue trigger and bead track.

Manual immediate trigger already sixty clean after repair.

Now I needed repeated delay.

Vera showed bench attachment.

Small clamp arm could release Lot tongue when drip wheel reached chosen notch.

That removed my hand from delayed trigger.

Better.

"Don't infer duration from bead mark," she said.

"I won't."

"Everyone says that."

"I've been told that about loose rope coils too."

She ignored me.

Procedure:

reset Lot.

Set drum to reference interval A.

Clamp tongue under release arm.

Start.

Drum reaches notch.

Arm releases tongue.

Read bead.

Ten repeats.

Then interval B.

Then maybe C if queue allowed.

I started A.

Run one: bead near mark two.

Two: mark two.

Three: just below two.

Four: two.

Five: two.

Six: slightly above two.

Seven: two.

Eight: below two.

Nine: two.

Ten: two.

No fourth-mark hang.

Good.

Not exact.

Consistent cluster.

I logged range.

Vera checked notebook.

"Don't average marks like linear scale."

"Wasn't going to."

"Good."

I absolutely had been about to think about average.

Saved.

Interval B.

Longer drum setting.

Run one: near three.

Two: three.

Three: slightly below three.

Four: three.

Five: three.

Six: three.

Seven: just above three.

Eight: three.

Nine: three.

Ten: below three.

Again cluster.

No sticky four.

Interesting.

Not certification.

Still interesting.

Queue behind me grew.

I asked Vera if third interval okay.

She looked.

"Five runs."

Fair.

Interval C longer again.

One: between three and four.

Two: similar.

Three: nearer four but below.

My stomach tightened.

Four: similar to one.

Five: similar.

No false snap.

No sticky pause visible.

Good.

I wrote:

**Grey Post public comparison bench. No certification. External drip-wheel release, not hand-triggered delay. Interval A x10 clusters around mark 2. Interval B x10 clusters around mark 3. Interval C x5 clusters between 3 and 4. No mark-four hang in 25 delayed trials. Combined post-service immediate 60 clean + delayed 25 clean. Scale non-linear/uncalibrated. Do not infer seconds.**

Vera read.

"Good."

Praise from Vera worth maybe half copper.

Not real half copper.

Don't invent conversion.

"Can I say it distinguishes longer repeated interval from shorter under this setup?"

She thought.

"Under this setup, today, yes."

"Not absolute."

"Correct."

"Not certified."

"Correct."

"Not body or magic."

She looked at me.

"Why would you use this on body?"

"Long story."

"Don't."

"That's current plan."

Good.

Lot 67 had upgraded from five-copper liar to five-copper thing that might actually do comparative delay.

Excellent.

Artifact win.

No fireworks.

Bench fee bits only.

Cash still eighteen.

I packed tool.

Grey Post morning board.

High Shelf coach still five copper.

Departure eighth day, second ring.

Weather check first.

Berths six.

Cash eighteen.

Ticket five leaves thirteen.

Ten floor.

Three discretionary after purchase.

Need lodging at High Shelf unknown.

Too thin.

Could break floor only with work plan.

Need work.

Weather Yard board still had half-shift three copper.

**DRY COPY + FLAG RESET**.

I asked clerk.

"Flag reset means climbing?"

"Some."

"Then no."

"Dry copy alone pays two."

There.

"What copy?"

"Yesterday closure sheets into route ledger."

Very similar to ledger work.

Useful.

Boring.

I looked farther down board.

**COACH YARD / LOAD STRAP CHECK / 2C / SEATED POSSIBLE**

Different enough.

"What's seated possible?" I asked yard master.

"Inspect removed straps. Mark fray, buckle damage, stretch. Don't climb coaches."

Good.

Two copper.

I took it.

Yard master **Sena Brakk**.

Big woman.

Long braid.

Talked enough for three people.

Excellent.

"Listen, they're going to hand you straps and every driver swears theirs is fine because if you mark bad they have to go requisition another and requisition window is all the way across yard, which is stupid, yes, but don't let their laziness become your eyesight. Red chalk if fibers cut, white if buckle bent, blue if stretched past gauge. If two problems, mark both. If unsure, put question knot here and I'll look. Do not invent fourth chalk because you feel artistic."

"I won't."

"Good. Last boy made green category. There is no green category."

"What did green mean?"

"He forgot."

Amazing.

I sat at dry yard table.

Straps came.

Simple work.

Not trivial.

First five fine.

Sixth had buckle tooth bent inward.

White.

Seventh frayed edge but not load fibers.

Question knot.

Sena looked.

"Surface. Pass."

Good.

Ninth visibly stretched.

Blue.

Driver argued.

Sena gave him requisition-window directions with enough detail to become a lecture.

He left.

Twelfth had cut fibers plus buckle bend.

Red and white.

No green.

I worked.

At midpoint coach arrived from High Shelf.

Mud gray.

One wheel rim chipped.

Passengers stiff.

Driver climbed down and said north road had frost pockets above Stone Mouth.

Frost.

New environment.

Grey Post cool.

High Shelf cold.

I listened.

Sena asked road status.

"Open. Slow after marker twenty-two. Quarry convoy coming down tomorrow."

High Shelf coach tomorrow still plausible.

Then strap pile included one strange silver-thread webbing.

I touched only because it had been handed to me.

Fibers shimmered.

"Charged?" I asked.

Sena said, "Memory weave."

"What remembers?"

"Stretch direction. Pull too hard one way, silver threads stay dark there until reset steam. We use it on instrument crates, not people."

Artifact-adjacent ordinary infrastructure.

I inspected.

One side had dark silver band.

Blue stretch.

Sena checked.

"Correct."

"Can it quantify?"

"No. Tells you where abuse happened, not how much unless you know weave lot."

Everything here loved directional residue.

Interesting regional technology family.

Residual Field Witness back Dorrin suddenly made more cultural sense.

Not isolated magical gadget.

A whole measurement tradition around preserving peak direction after event.

Good.

No purchase.

Half shift ended.

Two copper.

Eighteen to twenty.

Better.

Now High Shelf five leaves fifteen.

Likely lodging still leaves me above floor.

Thin but workable.

I walked board.

Berths four.

Two sold since morning.

Decision.

Return south remained possible.

High Shelf voluntary.

No one asked.

No job waiting.

Why go?

Because Shelf Road kept going.

Because quarry resonance stones.

Because weather yard.

Because old me wanted strange places.

Because current me had built enough cash through work to buy one more step without violating floor.

Because if I didn't, I could sit at Grey Post convincing myself prudence was destination.

Could still turn later.

I bought High Shelf ticket.

Five copper.

Twenty to fifteen.

Again fifteen.

My favorite miserable number.

Departure second ring tomorrow, weather permitting.

Ten floor intact.

Five discretionary.

Road House tonight still two copper if closure crowd persisted.

That would leave thirteen.

Still above floor.

I needed sleep.

Could cheaper worker loft exist?

Asked.

Survey clerk said weather crowd clearing, old coach shed floor one copper after night bell if no freight team took it.

No guarantee.

I waited.

Risk one-copper savings.

Not dramatic.

Night bell.

Coach shed had space.

One copper.

Fifteen to fourteen.

Good.

Four above floor.

Food bits.

Trial limb had rested since Brineward.

Coach shed floor dry.

Task: repack for colder road.

Actual reason.

I put trial limb on.

Medium foot.

Temporary ash shaft.

Still shop's.

Still not mine.

Cold-weather packing meant both hands useful.

I laid shirt, coat, wrap, notebook pouch.

Standing.

Crutches against bench.

First ten minutes easy.

Foot placement quiet.

Twelve.

Fifteen.

I carried folded coat seven steps to pack.

Returned eight with empty hands.

No issue.

At nineteen, one turn around crate happened without active foot-placement checklist.

I noticed after.

Funny.

Progress noticed by missing thought.

At twenty-three, hip fatigue.

Twenty-six, mild heat.

I wanted thirty.

Because round number.

Bad reason.

Stopped at twenty-seven.

Crutches.

Removed limb.

Skin check.

Upper pink mild.

Lower-front faint line.

At twenty minutes lower gone, upper mostly gone.

At thirty upper gone.

No pain.

Logged:

**Grey Post coach shed. 27 min dry board. Cold-road repack. Coat carry 7 steps; empty return 8. One clean crate turn around ~19 without active foot-placement checklist. Hip fatigue 23. Mild heat 26. Stopped 27. Lower clear 20; upper mostly clear 20, clear 30.**

Not farther than 28.

Better in another way.

Good.

No deliberate magic.

Tempting after tool bench.

Very tempting.

Lot 67 could distinguish delayed intervals under external release now.

That did not mean I should point it at magic the same day.

Need separate characterization.

Maybe repeat bench another day.

Maybe external physical trigger first.

Training brain wanted pipeline.

Road brain wanted sleep.

Road won.

Before bed I checked ticket.

**GREY POST -> HIGH SHELF / 5C / EIGHTH DAY SECOND RING / WEATHER PERMIT**

Cash fourteen.

Vale twenty-three.

Ten floor.

Four discretionary.

High Shelf lodging unknown.

Work unknown.

Cold known.

Excellent budgeting.

**Grey Post. Lot 67 finally tested against public comparison drum. 25 delayed trials clean, clustered by interval, no mark-four failure. Still not calibrated in seconds and not using it on magic/body yet. Earned 2c checking coach straps. Bought High Shelf ticket 5c. Cash 14c, Vale 23c. Ten floor intact. Trial build 27 min dry, fine. - Greg**

Then:

**I am apparently going somewhere colder on purpose. Please judge quietly.**

Postage bits.

I lay in coach shed beside three drivers and one snoring survey dog.

High Shelf tomorrow.

Measurer.

Maybe.

Morning I measured old artifact.

Afternoon I measured damaged straps.

Best instrument remained copper count.

Very accurate.
