THE EARNER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Adventure needed a job.

High Shelf had jobs.

Most of them wanted two good legs, strong lungs, hearing wraps, quarry certification, or a personality capable of waking before dawn cheerful.

I had one of those.

Barely.

Work board first ring:

**CUT THREE / SPOIL CART / 5C / STRONG BACK**

Still no.

**LOWER SCALE / LOAD TICKET CHECK / 2C HALF / SEATED**

Possible.

Boring.

**BELL STORE / RESONANCE FLAG FOLD + CASE PACK / 3C HALF / DRY BENCH / CAREFUL HANDS**

Better.

**MOTH HOUSE / CAGE WASH / 2C**

Already did.

No becoming local moth janitor.

I took Bell Store tab.

Cash fifteen.

Ten floor.

Three-copper Mere Ridge fare.

If I earned three, then bought fare, eighteen to fifteen.

Could still pay one-copper bunk and sit fourteen.

Four above floor.

Not luxurious.

Less stupid.

Good enough.

Bell Store sat behind lower cutting terrace, outside blue line.

Long stone room.

Shelves of cloth flags, bronze clappers, glass strips, padded cases, hearing wraps, and things I did not touch because labels included words like **phase** and **return-tone**.

Supervisor was **Pell Ardin**.

Pell again.

I refused to react.

Growth.

He had enormous eyebrows and spoke like every sentence had been revised twice before leaving mouth.

"Your task is not technical," he said. "I am stating that first because the room looks technical and people become ambitious when surrounded by nouns."

"I understand deeply."

"Good."

He pointed bench.

"Flags come back from cut lines. You check for tears, stiffness, obvious glaze contamination. Fold by color stripe. Pack in numbered cases. Anything questionable gets question tag. You do not decide whether contamination is active. You do not scrape glass dust off with fingernail. Someone did that."

"Still have finger?"

"Yes."

"Disappointing lesson design."

Pell blinked.

Then continued.

"Cases are light. Some require two-handed fold because signal stripe must not crease across conductive thread. Can you stand at bench?"

"For a while. I have a trial work leg. Dry floor?"

"Dry unless roof fails."

I looked up.

"Does roof fail often?"

"No. But I dislike guarantees."

Good man.

Trial limb had rested a full day.

Stable stone floor.

Useful two-handed task.

Crutches within reach.

Yes.

I fitted liner.

Temporary ash shaft.

Medium foot.

Still shop property.

Still not mine.

Still dramatically useful.

Standing with both hands free while folding signal cloth was exactly why I wanted the damn thing.

First ten minutes easy.

Flags mostly wool-like cloth with thin metallic threads along one edge.

White stripe.

Amber.

Blue.

Black.

Some smelled faintly metallic.

One had clear gray dust embedded along hem.

Question tag.

Pell checked.

"Correct. Cut Two glass residue. Wash crew."

"Dangerous?"

"Unknown until washed/tested. Therefore not yours to classify."

Beautiful.

I folded.

Packed.

Carried one empty case four steps to shelf.

Returned four.

No issue.

At about fifteen minutes, foot placement had gone quiet again.

Not absent.

Quiet.

Body knew enough that brain could pay attention to stripe alignment instead of narrating ankle-that-wasn't-there.

That was weird.

Good weird.

One flag had black stripe but case label said amber set.

I almost assumed worker mixed pile.

Stopped.

Tag said **B3 BLACK / WEST CUT**.

I asked.

Pell came.

"Good catch. Wrong return bin, not wrong flag."

He moved entire pile.

"If you'd silently corrected one flag, we'd have found rest later and wondered why one wandered."

"Systems hate helpful people."

"Systems hate undocumented helpful people."

Better.

At twenty minutes hip began reminding me arrangement was temporary.

Not pain.

Fatigue.

I shifted weight.

Kept folding.

At twenty-four, mild heat.

I finished current flag.

Sat.

No chasing twenty-eight because last time twenty-seven.

Today task different.

Cold outside.

Stone floor harder.

Enough.

Twenty-four minutes.

Crutches back.

Pell noticed.

"Done standing?"

"Yes."

"Good. Bench works seated."

No inspirational speech.

Perfect.

I logged later:

**High Shelf Bell Store. 24 min dry stone. Flag fold/case pack. Empty case carry 4 steps out + 4 return. Foot placement quiet by ~15. Hip fatigue 20. Mild heat 24. Stopped. Skin check pending.**

Work continued seated.

Then bell outside rang four short.

Pell froze.

Not alarm exactly.

Inventory attention.

Second sequence: two long.

He walked door.

"What is that?" I asked.

"Cut Four lost return tone."

"Meaning?"

"Means signal loop heard outbound bell and no confirmed return. Could be broken line, bad clapper, wind, worker missed pull, or actual cut condition."

"And?"

"And nothing until bell crew tells us."

Good.

He pulled two spare flag cases from shelf.

Worker arrived running.

"Need yellow return set and two hearing wraps."

Pell handed cases.

"Line break?"

"Unknown. West post not answering."

"Take question kit too."

Worker left.

I watched.

"Do lost return tones stop cutting?"

"Depends line. Cut Four is shared-face warning. So yes, until they know whether warning failed or condition changed."

Production interrupted again.

Different mechanism.

High Shelf built redundancy because rock could apparently lie in several dialects.

Pell sighed.

"Yesterday Six. Today Four. Cold wakes old faults."

"Mechanical?"

"Sometimes. Sometimes charge thread contracts. Sometimes moisture freezes in housings. Sometimes people tie knots like criminals."

"Technical category?"

"Very."

He returned bench.

No investigation Greg.

I folded cloth.

Half shift ended.

Three copper.

Fifteen to eighteen.

Good.

Skin check in washroom.

Lower-front faint line.

Upper pink.

At twenty minutes, lower gone, upper mostly gone.

At thirty, upper gone.

No pain.

Fine.

I added:

**Lower-front clear 20. Upper mostly clear 20, clear 30. No pain.**

That was enough limb today.

Now decision.

Mere Ridge cart board.

**NORTH CUT ROAD / MERE RIDGE / THIRD RING / 3C / OPEN / FROST SHADE ABOVE M6**

Third ring.

Today.

Could go.

Cash eighteen.

Three fare leaves fifteen.

Likely bunk one or two leaves thirteen-fourteen.

Floor intact.

I bought ticket.

Eighteen to fifteen.

There.

Again.

No prophecy.

No job contract.

Just enough margin after work.

I collected pack from Old Scale Shed.

No second bunk payment.

Checked stove ash.

Left.

At coach yard, Mere Ridge vehicle wasn't coach.

Covered cart with narrow benches and high wheels.

Driver **Iven Ro**.

Ro.

Fine.

Three other passengers.

Shepherd woman with folded crook.

Boy carrying sack of wool combs.

Man with two sealed clay jars strapped chest-to-back like unfortunate armor.

I asked lower step.

Iven pointed small rear ladder.

Not ideal.

Then side loading board.

"Use freight board."

Wider.

Less steep.

Good.

Trial limb wrapped again after morning wear.

Crutches primary.

Boarded seated transfer with driver holding pack after asking.

North Cut Road narrower than Shelf Road.

Less infrastructure.

Stone markers farther apart.

Quarry noise faded behind us.

That mattered.

High Shelf had been industry loud.

North road became wind and sheep bells.

Actual sheep.

White-gray animals on slopes with dark horns curling close to skull.

Some had little glass beads braided into neck wool.

"Decoration?" I asked shepherd woman.

"Storm beads."

"Warning?"

"Sort of. They click when fleece charge gets high."

"Sheep become weather instruments."

She looked offended.

"Sheep become sheep that don't surprise shepherd."

Better.

Her name **Hessa Pell**.

Pell.

I refused universe reaction.

Storm beads clicked softly when wool rubbed.

Road climbed less now.

Mostly followed ridge contour.

Frost shade at marker six was real.

Iven stopped once to scatter grit from bucket on inside turn.

No drama.

At marker nine, road split around shallow sink.

Left marked freight.

Right marked light cart.

Iven chose right.

Shepherd said, "Freight side broke?"

"Soft shoulder."

"Since when?"

"This morning."

"Will they fix?"

Iven shrugged.

"After somebody owns it."

Infrastructure politics universal.

We continued.

Mere Ridge appeared near evening as line of stone houses hugging broad saddle between hills.

Not impressive.

Exactly as quarry cutter promised.

Sheep.

Stone.

Wind.

Old signal house on highest knob, squat round tower with three empty-looking arms sticking roof.

Two inns.

One smithy.

One store.

Small road office.

That was town.

I liked it immediately.

Dangerous.

Cart stopped at **Ridge Post**.

Route board:

**SOUTH / HIGH SHELF / NEXT CART ELEVENTH DAY / 3C**

**NORTHWEST / CROWN VALE / 4C / ROAD OPEN**

**EAST / LARKEN LAKE / 5C / WEATHER HOLD POSSIBLE / DEPART TENTH DAY**

Larken Lake.

There.

Lake country got name.

Five copper.

Too much now without work.

Cash fifteen.

Ten floor.

Five discretionary.

Buying lake fare would put exactly ten before lodging.

No.

Crown Vale four leaves eleven before lodging.

Also no.

Money first again.

Inn prices.

Wind Lamb floor 2c.

Ridge Cot shared loft 1c.

Ridge Cot.

One copper.

Fifteen to fourteen.

Four above floor.

Again.

I was becoming very good at arriving places with fourteen copper.

Maybe business model.

Shared loft warm enough.

Low ceiling.

Smelled wool and onions.

I put pack down.

No limb again.

Already wore today.

Work board downstairs tiny.

**WOOL COUNT / MORNING / 2C**

**SIGNAL HOUSE CLEAN / HALF DAY / 3C / STAIRS**

No.

**ROAD OFFICE COPY / WEATHER RETURNS / 2C HALF / WRITING**

Paper again.

**SHEARING SHED TOOL TAGS / 2C / SEATED**

Maybe tomorrow.

Then separate handwritten card:

**OLD SIGNAL HOUSE: ONE-BIT PUBLIC VIEW / SECOND BELL / WEATHER PERMIT**

One bit.

Old signal house.

Artifact-ish infrastructure.

Cheap.

Could see.

Not buy.

Fine.

I ate stew with bits.

Hessa Pell from cart was there too, arguing with innkeeper about whether storm beads counted as livestock equipment for storage fee.

They did.

She lost.

No friendship.

At table beside me, old man complained lake road had been holding carts because east mist froze on marker glass.

Someone else said Crown Vale lower road warmer.

Route choice already forming without me asking.

I wrote home note.

**Mere Ridge. Earned 3c folding/packing quarry signal flags, then paid 3c north cart. Cash 14c, Vale 23c. Trial build 24 min dry work use, fine. Ten floor intact. Roads now split toward Crown Vale or Larken Lake. No ticket yet. - Greg**

Then:

**Adventure remains mostly accounting with scenery.**

Postage bits.

Cold but less industrial than High Shelf.

Stars clear.

Signal house on ridge dark.

Three roof arms pointed different directions.

No lights.

Retired maybe.

Wind moved sheep bells below.

I found sheltered wall behind inn.

Dry stone.

No one nearby.

Magic.

Maybe.

Long rest since external 136.

Safe bounded condition.

No Lot 67.

Simple external movement.

Light.

Not dangerous.

One pace.

Attempt 137.

Breathe.

Current body.

Push.

Could be wind.

Same distance.

Attempt 138.

Push.

Not rank.

Just another external response.

No third.

Good.
