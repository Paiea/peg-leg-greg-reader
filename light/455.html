TEXT READER · CHAPTER 348

THE SHEPHERD

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Sheep were louder than expected.

Not individually.

Collectively.

One sheep sounded stupid.

Forty sheep sounded political.

I woke at Ridge Cot to a wall of offended bleating outside.

Wind too.

Mere Ridge apparently believed mornings should contain multiple complaints.

Cash fourteen.

Vale twenty-three.

Ten-copper floor.

No Crown Vale ticket.

No Larken Lake ticket.

No magic today.

Probably.

I dressed.

Trial leg stayed wrapped.

Twenty-four minutes yesterday. Rest day.

Crutches.

Breakfast bits.

Work board first.

Money before scenery.

The road office had four tabs.

**WOOL COUNT / LOWER PEN / 2C / HALF**

**SHEARING SHED / TOOL TAG CHECK / 2C / SEATED POSSIBLE**

**SIGNAL HOUSE / CLEAN LOWER ROOM / 2C / STAIRS FOR UPPER ONLY**

**RIDGE FLOCK MOVE / EAST FENCE TO LOWER PEN / 3C / WALKING / UNEVEN GROUND**

No.

Three copper tempting.

Uneven ground not.

Tool tags sounded like more tags.

Signal House cleaning had potential, but public viewing second bell existed anyway.

Wool count.

I took it.

Lower pen sat behind Wind Lamb inn in a shallow stone hollow.

Woman from cart yesterday was there.

Hessa Pell.

She recognized me.

"Carrow man."

"Mere Ridge man for at least another day."

"That's not how being from somewhere works."

"Temporary citizenship."

She ignored that.

Good.

Work was not counting sheep by looking at sheep.

Thankfully.

Fleece bundles came from morning skirt table, each tied with colored cord by flock.

My job:

count bundles by cord color,

check storm-bead braid still attached to fleece sample tag,

note missing bead sets,

stack by buyer mark.

Do not grade fleece.

Do not decide bead condition.

If braid missing or beads cracked, question mark.

Rural paperwork with wool attached.

Better.

Supervisor **Noll Tere** had three sentences for every one asked.

"Blue cord is East Fence flock, but not all East Fence sheep are blue today because two pens got mixed before dawn, which is why you count the cord on the bundle and not whatever somebody tells you the sheep looked like, because nobody remembers sheep accurately once breakfast starts."

"Understood."

"Red cord lower wash. Yellow hill flock. Green is buyer hold, not flock, so don't count green as flock."

"Why green?"

"Because twenty years ago somebody was an idiot."

Useful history.

Work started.

Storm beads braided into tag cords were small cloudy glass pieces, each with tiny central thread.

Some clear.

Some faintly smoky.

One cracked.

Question mark.

One bundle missing bead braid entirely.

Question mark.

Hessa passed carrying two fleece bundles.

"You're doing that?"

"Apparently."

"Can you count?"

"Professionally?"

"Concerning answer."

She dropped bundles at table.

Shepherd humor less dry than Greg. Good.

Noll corrected my stack once.

Green buyer-hold cord had yellow flock cord underneath, and I had almost stacked by visible top cord.

"Green outranks flock for stack," she said.

"Even though not flock."

"Exactly."

"Terrible system."

"Yes."

Comforting.

Halfway through first ring? Mere Ridge time vague.

A boy ran in from east side.

"Hessa. Fence Three's down."

Hessa stopped.

"How much?"

"Two posts. Maybe three."

"Sheep through?"

"Some."

"How many is some?"

Boy looked offended by precision.

"More than five."

"Less than twenty?"

"Probably."

"Useful."

She put bundle down.

Noll said, "Go."

Hessa looked at me.

Not dramatic.

"Can he keep East count if I go?"

Noll: "He can count cord."

High praise again.

Hessa left.

I continued.

Ten minutes later, another worker came in.

"Need hands lower lane."

Noll looked around.

Two workers already out.

She looked at me.

I looked at crutches.

"Not sheep chasing."

"No," she said. "Could you sit gate table and mark returns?"

"Explain."

"They bring sheep back through lower lane. You mark flock cord color from neck bead braid and chalk ear stripe if braid missing. Don't decide ownership. Just record what comes through."

That I could do.

Distinct enough.

She paid wool-count half regardless? Not finished yet. We shifted function within same employer. Fine.

Lower lane gate was stone-packed, level enough for chair.

I sat.

Sheep returned in groups.

Not chased exactly.

Pressed by three shepherds and two dogs with terrifying work ethic.

Hessa at rear.

First six had blue bead braids.

East Fence.

Mark six.

Then one yellow.

Hill flock.

"Yellow," I called.

Hessa looked.

"Of course."

"Problem?"

"That idiot goes everywhere."

Sheep did not respond to accusation.

Then three with no visible braid.

Chalk ear stripe.

White.

White.

One had blue ear chalk from prior handling.

I wrote exactly that.

Do not infer.

A shepherd behind said, "That's Hobb's ewe."

I asked, "Do I write Hobb?"

Hessa shouted from lane, "No. Write mark. We'll sort names after."

Good.

Systems survived familiarity by refusing it when counting mattered.

Then problem.

One lamb came through with storm beads clicking sharply.

Not wind-chime cute.

Fast little glass ticks.

Another behind it too.

Hessa heard.

"Stop lane."

Dogs stopped somehow.

Magic.

Probably training.

She came forward.

Two lambs clicking.

One adult ewe behind them not clicking.

"Fleece charge?" I asked.

"Maybe."

She put back of fingers near wool without touching beads.

"High."

"Storm?"

Hessa looked east.

Sky pale.

Wind steady.

"Not enough for that."

Another shepherd said, "Fence line glass."

Hessa's face changed.

"Where did they get out?"

"East cut below old signal wire."

There.

Could be environmental infrastructure residue.

Hessa told boy, "Nobody touches wire posts. Rope the gap wider and bring them around south stone."

Boy left.

She separated clicking lambs into empty side pen.

"What happens?"

"We wait. If beads slow, fleece is holding local charge. If they keep climbing, we call signal keeper."

"Dangerous?"

"Annoying first. Dangerous if you make it worse."

Excellent rural doctrine.

No Greg magic.

No artifact.

No touching sheep.

I kept marking returns.

Three more clicking lightly.

Side pen.

Most normal.

Fence breach total eventually thirteen sheep.

Twelve East Fence blue.

One yellow hill idiot.

Four had clicking bead sets, two strong, two mild.

One missing braid.

No injuries.

No ownership dispute.

Just mixed flock and possible old signal-line charge contamination.

Hessa finished return count.

"Thirteen."

I showed sheet.

"Thirteen entries."

"Good."

"Yellow still yours?"

"Not mine. Nothing here is mine. I work for three families who all think the others own worse sheep."

Messier speech. Good.

The strong-click beads slowed after maybe quarter bell? Need local. After a while.

Hessa listened.

"Falling."

Another shepherd: "Then line residue."

Hessa: "Likely. Not proven."

I liked her.

Not enough to invite companion.

Signal keeper arrived anyway because boy had already gone.

Older woman named **Mara Fen**.

She carried insulated hook and small dull-glass plate.

No dramatic robes.

She checked side pen from outside.

Then went toward breach with Hessa.

I stayed gate.

Later Hessa returned.

"Old east signal wire dropped onto stone after frost. Still taking a little charge from post cap."

"Active line?"

"Retired line."

Of course.

"Why still connected?"

"Because removing ridge wire costs more than forgetting it exists until sheep find it."

Infrastructure realism.

"Fix?"

"Keeper isolated cap. Fence crew moves posts."

No Greg.

Good.

Noll came down.

"Count done?"

I gave sheet.

She checked.

Thirteen returned.

Four side pen charge notes.

One yellow flock mix.

One missing braid.

She nodded.

"You did both jobs."

"I did one job and then sat somewhere else."

"That's most work."

She paid original two copper.

Then another copper for gate return record because breach response had extended beyond half shift.

Not huge.

Real.

Fourteen to seventeen.

Good.

Seven above floor.

Now route choices changed.

Crown Vale four leaves thirteen.

Larken Lake five leaves twelve.

Still thin but possible.

Could earn more.

Or choose Crown because cheaper.

But macro-direction lake stronger.

Need actual pull.

I went road office.

Weather board changed.

**CROWN VALE / OPEN / 4C / NEXT CART ELEVENTH DAY FIRST RING**

**LARKEN LAKE / EAST ROAD / 5C / TENTH DAY THIRD RING / WEATHER HOLD LIFTED**

Today ninth? Greg arrived Mere Ridge Ch454 likely ninth day? Larken depart tenth day maybe tomorrow. Crown eleventh later. Good.

Under Larken notice:

**LAKE FERRY CONNECTIONS RESUME ON CLEAR WATER / NORTH SHORE ROUTE BOARD AT LARKEN**

There.

Lake isn't endpoint.

It is a hub.

North shore routes.

Water travel but inland.

Different.

Crown Vale board:

**WOOL MARKET / MILL ROAD / SOUTH VALLEY COACH CONNECTIONS**

Also useful.

More economic opportunity likely.

Larken Lake could be riskier.

Five copper.

Arrive with twelve.

Need lodging maybe one or two.

Could hit ten exactly.

No buffer.

Too thin.

Unless work on arrival guaranteed? No.

Could work tomorrow morning before third-ring departure.

Maybe.

Road office tabs next morning:

**SIGNAL HOUSE LOWER CLEAN / 2C / FIRST RING**

Still available.

Perfect.

Could earn two, then buy Larken five: seventeen +2=19, minus5=14. Better.

But ticket might sell out.

I asked clerk.

"Larken seats?"

"Six left."

"Can hold one without paying?"

"No."

"Deposit?"

"Two copper holds until second ring tomorrow. Balance three before departure. Deposit lost if you don't pay."

Interesting.

Could pay two now: seventeen to fifteen.

Then work two tomorrow back to seventeen, pay three to fourteen.

Floor preserved.

But commits route before job guaranteed? Signal cleaning tab posted, could fill.

Could take job now for tomorrow? Ask.

"Can I claim Signal House lower clean now?"

"Keeper hires at first ring."

No guarantee.

Could buy full ticket now and sit at twelve overnight, then one-copper loft another night? Wait current lodging was paid previous night only. Need tonight lodging if staying. Ridge Cot 1c -> eleven. That leaves only one above floor before departure. Too thin.

Could pay deposit two, lodge one -> fourteen. Four above floor. Tomorrow if no work, either pay remaining three -> eleven or abandon deposit and remain fourteen. Better optionality.

This is exactly why deposits matter.

I paid two-copper Larken hold.

Seventeen to fifteen.

Seat held until second ring tomorrow.

Not full ticket.

Good.

Then Ridge Cot loft another night one copper.

Fifteen to fourteen.

Same starting cash again.

Capitalism but more complicated.

But now I had option rather than finished travel.

This chapter should differentiate routes and make choice. We chose Larken provisional.

I spent one bit on Old Signal House public viewing because I was here and one bit is not copper.

Second bell.

Mara Fen was keeper.

Of course.

Old Signal House lower room had three huge wooden lever arms connected to roof signals by rods through ceiling.

Retired east wire map on wall.

There.

She pointed at line that had charged sheep.

"That one."

"Retired?"

"Mostly."

"Dangerous word."

"Accurate word."

Signal system historically sent ridge condition by mechanical arm positions plus lightly charged wire confirmation between posts.

If arm moved but charge echo failed, next station knew mechanical signal might not have carried cleanly through weather.

Redundancy.

Old east line retired when lake-road glass posts replaced it.

But cap at old Post Four remained tied into roof bleed because disconnect scheduled with full roof refit two winters ago.

Refit delayed.

So forgotten line still accumulated tiny residual charge under frost/wind cycles.

Enough to click storm beads after sheep brushed fallen wire.

Not enough for dramatic electrocution nonsense.

Still operational consequence.

"Why not remove it now?" I asked.

Mara said, "Because today I isolated it in ten breaths, and removing three miles of wire requires twelve people, two wagons, and permission from two road districts that disagree about who owns the middle mile."

There.

Somebody else’s story in one sentence.

"Do they disagree?"

"Constantly."

"About ownership?"

"About maintenance. Ownership gets very clear when somebody wants salvage money."

Perfect.

Public viewing moved on.

No need solve.

I asked about lake road glass posts.

She showed diagram.

New posts used sealed glass fins that changed opacity under sustained storm charge, no wire between stations.

More local, less network failure, but each post required direct inspection.

Tradeoff.

Old system could transmit status.

New system failed smaller.

Good infrastructure philosophy.

Lot 67 stayed packed.

No need.

Magic stayed rested.

No need.

Trial leg stayed wrapped.

Rest day.

Good.

At Ridge Cot, I wrote home.

**Mere Ridge, still. Earned 3c total doing wool count and return-gate recording after a fence breach. Thirteen sheep got out; four picked up charge from an old retired signal wire that wasn't fully disconnected. Nobody hurt. Cash 14c, Vale 23c after a 2c hold on Larken Lake seat and another 1c loft. Hold expires tomorrow second ring. Need 3c balance if I go. Ten floor intact. - Greg**

Then:

**Crown Vale is cheaper. Larken Lake has north-shore ferry connections. I picked the lake seat provisionally because apparently I enjoy making transportation decisions with insufficient money.**

I paused.

That sounded like old me.

Maybe current me too.

The road had stopped being a line.

Now it was options with prices.

Crown Vale promised mills, wool market, valley coaches.

Larken promised lake ferries and north-shore roads.

Neither was fake.

One was cheaper.

One pulled farther from everything I understood about coast and quarry.

I had paid two copper to keep that second door open.

Not entered.

Yet.

Shepherd.

Ridiculous title.

I had not herded a single sheep.

I counted them while professionals did the herding.

Which, honestly, was probably safer for sheep.

Tomorrow I needed two copper before second ring.

Or I needed enough sense to lose the deposit.
