TEXT READER · CHAPTER 413

THE CONTRACTOR

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Adventure started with crate padding.

Naturally.

Lakeward Glass Yard first ring smelled like wet wood, ash, and somebody else's expensive mistake.

I had ten copper.

Exactly ten.

Floor had stopped being policy and become current account balance.

Twenty-three Vale remained separate.

Leg money remained leg money.

Mirror Spring remained a sentence in notebook.

Before I let sentence become several days of bad terrain, I wanted two things.

Money.

Then Teren Sol's actual mouth.

Not Perrin's summary of it.

Glass Return Crate Pad paid two copper for one ring.

I took it.

Supervisor **Hala Renn** pointed at bench.

"Returned crates only. Pull old reed pad. Check frame for obvious split. New pad goes corners and base. If frame split, red chalk and stop. You are not a cratewright."

"I am collecting professions I am not."

"Good. Collect quietly."

Trial leg had rested.

Dry bench floor.

But I was leaving town after if job existed.

Unknown route.

Save skin.

Crutches.

I sat.

Crate padding was satisfying in way repetitive work sometimes became when endpoint obvious.

Old reed out.

Frame look.

No split.

New reed in.

Tie.

Stack.

Second crate had corner separation.

Red chalk.

Stop.

Third smelled like vinegar.

"Problem?" I asked.

Hala sniffed.

"Cleaning return. Fine."

Fourth had glass dust under base seam.

Question.

She swept sample onto black card.

"Old break residue. Crate cleared yesterday. Pad replacement only."

Fine.

No mystery.

No quest in crate.

Good.

At end of ring she counted two copper.

Ten to twelve.

I liked twelve better.

Still thin.

But floor had breathing room.

I found Perrin at east cart stand carrying graft knives in wrapped roll and arguing with man about axle space.

Not angrily.

Persistently.

"It's one roll."

"One roll becomes twelve rolls when everybody says one roll."

"Mine actually is one."

"That's what makes it dangerous."

Perrin saw me.

"You came."

"I came to ask your grandfather whether you invented his money."

"I rounded."

"Up?"

"No."

Interesting.

Cart master found knife roll space under bench for one bit.

Perrin paid.

"Morrow cart?" I asked.

"Second ring."

"Fare?"

"Two copper. Grandfather covers if you're coming about spring."

"He said that?"

"He said bring anyone willing to listen before talking."

"That's not same sentence."

Perrin exhaled.

"No. It isn't."

Good.

"Then I talk first."

"Fine."

I paid nothing.

We waited for cart.

Perrin did not fill silence well.

He tried anyway.

"The story isn't exactly how my grandfather tells it."

"Already encouraging."

"He says Iria didn't clear the spring."

There.

"What does he say?"

"She found where water went."

"Different."

"Yes."

"Where did it go?"

"Depends which time he tells it."

Better.

"And you want me to work for this man."

"I want somebody to go before lower pears drop."

That was honest.

"Why you?"

"Because my mother won't leave rows. Grandfather's knee won't take Three Sisters. My sisters are eleven and eight. Graft house already gave me yesterday afternoon because ferry was late. If I disappear three days, I lose placement."

"Could lose fruit instead."

"Yes."

No clean answer.

Cart arrived.

Morrow Orchard cart was flat farm wagon with canvas half roof and bins of empty produce trays going east.

Driver **Nemi Cross** checked Perrin's pass.

Perrin pointed at me.

"Possible spring hire."

Nemi said, "Possible doesn't ride account."

Correct.

Perrin opened mouth.

I said, "I'll pay two. If Teren hires me and travel is covered, he reimburses. If not, my trip."

Ten-floor problem.

Twelve to ten.

Exactly floor again.

But this was deliberate information cost, not casual spending.

Was that rational?

Maybe.

Could have demanded Teren come Lakeward.

But orchard clock mattered.

And I wanted to see channels.

Paid two.

Twelve to ten.

Adventure apparently hated liquidity.

Cart east.

Lakeward glass roofs receded.

Road climbed gently through orchards.

Rows first sparse.

Then continuous.

Pear-like trees? Avoid Earth exact maybe fruit types can be pears established by Perrin line. Fine.

Low stone channels ran beside road.

Some wet.

Some barely.

Workers lifted small wooden gates between terraces.

Not one irrigation system.

Many linked ones.

At first mile channel had steady finger-deep flow.

Second less.

Near Morrow, water moved thin over dark stone.

Not dry.

Low.

I crouched at public road crossing.

Did not touch.

Perrin said, "Yesterday higher."

"Measured?"

"Not by me."

Good correction.

"Who?"

"Gate keeper chalk board."

Morrow Orchard wasn't one farm.

Cluster of family holdings around packing shed, graft house, drying yard, and shared lower channel.

Morrow apparently district name older than current families.

Teren Sol sat under shade roof repairing basket handles.

Late sixties maybe.

Broad shoulders reduced by age rather than erased.

Left knee wrapped.

He looked at my crutches.

Then at Perrin.

"That's who you found?"

Perrin stiffened.

I liked Teren less immediately.

Useful.

"He found me," I said.

Teren looked at me again.

"Can you walk Three Sisters?"

"Don't know."

"Then why are you here?"

"To find out what you're actually hiring for before I decide whether your bad knee and my missing foot should negotiate terrain by proxy."

Perrin looked at ground.

Teren barked laugh.

Not warm.

But real.

"Sit."

I sat.

He did not know Peg-Leg Greg.

Excellent.

"Perrin says eight for route notes, twelve with sealed spring sample."

"Eight if you reach source and return useful notes. Twelve with district bottle filled and sealed correctly."

"Travel?"

"Lakeward to Morrow reimbursed if hired."

Good.

"Supplies?"

"Two days food. One blanket roll. Bottle deposit. Route copy."

"Two days?"

"Old path is day up, day back."

"When your knee worked?"

Pause.

"Yes."

"How long ago?"

"Nine years."

There.

Perrin looked away.

"Landslide?"

"Six years."

"So two days is story estimate, not current route estimate."

Teren's jaw moved.

"Three if slow."

"Unknown if blocked."

"Yes."

"Unknown if passable on crutches."

"Obviously."

"Not obvious enough if you're pricing two days food."

Perrin coughed into hand.

Teren stared.

Then said, "Three days food."

Good.

Not victory.

Basic planning.

"Turnaround condition?"

"What?"

"When do I stop trying and come back?"

"If path gone."

"Define gone."

"You enjoy this?"

"More than falling."

He rubbed wrapped knee.

"If Three Sisters lower bench is cut through, don't climb north face. Old upper track crosses loose shelf."

Specific.

"Any water crossing?"

"White Reed ford. Usually ankle. Could be knee after rain."

"Current?"

"Don't know."

"Weather?"

Perrin answered. "Dry two days. Ridge fog mornings. No rain posted."

Teren nodded.

"Sleeping Stone shelter?"

"Overhang. Dry unless east wind."

"People on route?"

"White Reed keeper sometimes. Three Sisters nobody permanent. Mirror Spring old catch house abandoned."

Now adventure.

"Why not send district team?"

"They are coming."

"Then why me?"

Teren leaned forward.

"Because three days is three days. Because lower fruit doesn't care whose seal is on paper. Because if source is running strong, I want to know before Halden families close another gate and call it conservation. Because if source is weak, I want Perrin's mother to stop accusing people who may not be stealing anything."

There.

Human problem.

"And because you want old path proven."

His eyes narrowed.

Perrin had warned me.

"I want people to remember there was a path before district road decided water begins where measuring box is."

"That's different job."

"Same mountain."

"Not same job."

Teren got angry quietly.

"You think because office draws new line the old one stops carrying water?"

"No. I think if you pay me to inspect source, I need to know whether you're paying for evidence or vindication."

Silence.

Perrin finally said, "Grandfather."

Teren looked at him.

Perrin kept going.

Messier now.

"This is what I meant. You keep saying walk it, walk it, walk it like if somebody puts feet on every old stone then everyone has to admit you were right about closing the lower marker council, and that's not what we need right now. We need to know if water is there. We need to know if something happened above Three Sisters. Mom doesn't care if the old path gets a plaque. She cares if Row Four drops fruit."

Teren's face changed.

Not defeated.

Hurt.

Good.

Somebody else's story.

Teren said, "Your mother learned that channel on my back."

"I know."

"You learned White Reed because I took you."

"I know."

"Then don't talk like memory is decoration."

"I'm not. I'm saying pears are dying while you argue about memory."

No sarcasm academy.

No clean winner.

I waited.

Eventually Teren looked at me.

"Evidence," he said.

"Route notes too?"

"Yes."

"Separate sections."

He frowned.

"What?"

"One page: route condition. One page: water observations. No mixing `old marker missing` with `upper family stole water`."

"Fine."

"Sample supplemental only."

"Fine."

"I do not accuse Halden families for you."

"Fine."

"I do not prove story."

Longer pause.

"Fine."

Perrin exhaled.

I wasn't done.

"Pay. Twelve with valid sealed sample. Eight if source reached and route notes returned but sample invalid or impossible. Four if I reach Three Sisters and route beyond is unsafe under agreed turnaround, with notes showing where and why."

Teren said, "Four for failing?"

"Four for useful route information that prevents next person from discovering same failure."

He hated it.

Thought.

"Three."

"Four."

"Three and your cart fare back."

"Four and cart fare already paid reimbursed now if hired."

Perrin whispered, "He's going to die bargaining before trail."

Teren ignored him.

"Four. Fare reimbursed."

"Good."

Contract.

Not paper yet.

We went packing shed office.

Morrow clerk wrote terms.

**MIRROR SPRING SUPPLEMENTAL WALK**

Employer: Morrow lower-channel cooperative, signed Teren Sol under emergency field-observation allowance? Need not invent legal detail too much. Better private cooperative job with district sample form separate.

Terms:

- route: Morrow -> White Reed -> Sleeping Stone -> Three Sisters -> Mirror Spring where safely passable;
- return route notes + water observations;
- 12c if valid district supplemental source sample returned with completed seal form;
- 8c if source reached and useful route notes returned but valid sample cannot be completed;
- 4c if Three Sisters reached and continuation judged unsafe under specified conditions, with useful route notes;
- Lakeward->Morrow 2c fare reimbursed upon hire;
- three days food + blanket roll supplied;
- district bottle deposit supplied by employer;
- no authority to alter gates, clear source, move markers, enter closed private channels, or represent district;
- Greg may turn back earlier for injury, weather, route loss, or equipment failure, but sub-Three-Sisters return has no guaranteed field pay beyond reimbursement/supplies consumed in good faith.

Good.

Teren paid my two copper reimbursement.

Ten to twelve.

Buffer restored.

He did not advance wages.

Fair.

"Bottle tomorrow," Perrin said. "District office runner comes Morrow first ring."

"Departure after?"

Teren said, "White Reed by midday if road dry."

"Road?"

"Farm track first part. Old path starts after keeper house."

"Guide?"

"Perrin can take you White Reed."

Perrin's head snapped around.

"I can?"

Teren said, "Graft house starts second ring tomorrow."

"And if cart back late?"

"Then I pay missed half."

Perrin looked suspicious.

"You hate paying missed half."

"I hate dry channels more."

There.

Short segment guide.

Not party.

Perrin could show maintained farm track to White Reed, then return for graft work if timing held.

"Does he know beyond White Reed?" I asked.

"To Sleeping Stone," Teren said.

Perrin said, "Barely. I was twelve."

"Then White Reed only."

Teren opened mouth.

I said, "You hired notes, not confidence theater."

He closed it.

Good.

Preparation.

Route sketch.

Old archive copy from Lakeward? Teren had hand copy.

Not map exactly.

Line with named markers, water branches, two old stone walls, Three Sisters contour scribble.

I compared to district archive notes copied yesterday.

Same sequence.

Different distances.

Teren's copy said White Reed to Sleeping Stone `one long climb`.

District archive said `approx. 3.4 road miles old measure`? Avoid modern miles? We have paces, road miles maybe prior. Better local distance uncertain. Use bells walking.

Teren: two bells when young.

Archive: no current time.

Fine.

I asked for physical landmarks.

White Reed: pale reed bed at channel fork.

Sleeping Stone: long dark slab over path, shaped like back under blanket.

Three Sisters: three narrow stone spires visible from lower hollow.

Mirror Spring: catch wall of black stone below white cliff seam.

Story names matched useful navigation.

Not magic.

Maybe.

Food:

flatbread.

dried fruit.

hard cheese.

smoked? Fine.

Three days modest.

Water flask.

Extra wrap.

Blanket roll.

No free expensive gear.

Teren offered old walking staff.

I looked at crutches.

"Inventory conflict."

He grunted.

Perrin laughed.

Sample bottle not yet.

Weather board at Morrow:

**RIDGE FOG MORNING / DRY DAY / EAST WIND LATE / NO RAIN WATCH**

Current only.

Three Sisters could differ.

Trial leg decision.

This mattered.

Crutches primary on unknown path.

Trial build could help on stable farm track and maybe free hands, but carrying extra weight plus unknown terrain.

Shop property.

Hark would murder me politely.

Already had been on boats.

Could leave at Morrow secured, but then if route includes stable sections I lose option.

Could carry wrapped, use only known dry stable stretches.

Weight cost.

I tested pack balance with it attached.

Manageable.

Not ideal.

Keep.

No wear today.

Skin rested.

Tomorrow maybe farm track only if dry.

Crutches on old path unless obvious stable ground.

Artifacts.

Line quietener.

Current reader.

Load tile.

Lot 67.

Did I need all?

I had been carrying them anyway.

Lot 67 could potentially compare timing if real interval question, but no reason yet.

Current reader maybe water/current direction? WS-F/9 slate current reader perhaps could be useful with flowing water, but limitations. Don't assume without state detail. Carry, characterize only if appropriate.

Load tile not obviously relevant.

Still carried.

No new purchase.

No money.

Magic.

Could test before trip.

No.

Save attention.

Protocol stayed 59 / 53 / 138.

Late afternoon Perrin took me lower channel.

Public cooperative section.

Chalk board nailed under roof.

Three readings across day.

Numbers meant local gauge marks, not flow volume.

Morning 6.2.

Midday 5.8.

Late 5.5.

Declining.

One day only.

Not trend beyond day.

Upper branch board? Different station, not visible.

"See?" Perrin said.

"I see this gauge declined today."

"You know what I mean."

"Yes. That's why I'm saying exact sentence."

He kicked dirt.

"You're irritating."

"Professionally now."

"Grandfather likes you."

"That's concerning."

"He doesn't."

"Better."

We walked rows.

Lower trees showed stress subtly.

Some leaves curled.

Fruit smaller? I couldn't know baseline.

Perrin knew.

He pointed Row Four.

"Those should be heavier by now."

"Could be water?"

"Could be heat. Could be root issue. Could be because this year's set was stupidly dense and Mom refused thinning enough because last year hail took half."

There.

Messy agriculture.

"So even source result won't explain fruit."

"No."

"Good."

He looked offended.

"Not good good. Evidence good."

"You talk like clerk."

"I've been paid as several."

At row end, older woman stood by gate.

Perrin's mother.

**Sera Sol**.

She had pruning knife and expression of person already informed about me and unconvinced.

"You're the walker."

"Apparently."

She looked at missing lower leg, crutches, pack.

Did not hide assessment.

"Teren sent a one-legged stranger up an abandoned path because he's too stubborn to wait for district."

"I negotiated."

"That doesn't improve sentence."

Fair.

Perrin said, "Mom."

"What? It's accurate."

I liked her more than Teren.

"Do you want me not to go?" I asked.

She answered too quickly.

"I want water."

Then slower:

"I want nobody hurt because my father thinks old stories are maps."

"Archive says places are real."

"Places being real doesn't make path safe."

"Agreed."

"If Three Sisters is bad, turn around."

"Contract says that."

"Contract is my father's handwriting with a clerk making it respectable. I'm telling you as person whose son will blame himself if you break your neck after he brought you here."

Perrin: "I wouldn't."

She looked at him.

"You absolutely would."

He shut up.

There.

Quest had people now.

Not just objective.

"I'll turn if route is beyond me," I said.

Sera nodded.

"And if spring is running strong?"

"I record that."

"Even if it means upper gates?"

"It means source appears strong at observation point. Upper gates would be separate claim."

She closed eyes briefly.

"Gods, my father hired a sentence."

"Twelve copper if sentence comes back with bottle."

She almost smiled.

Almost.

Then said, "Come eat. If you're walking tomorrow, I'm not letting Teren's travel ration be your dinner."

Food hospitality.

Not equipment windfall.

I accepted.

Family table.

Perrin's sisters **Lio** and **Mina** asked whether crutches made uphill harder.

"Yes."

"How much?"

"Depends hill."

"That's not number."

"Correct."

Perrin had told them.

"No."

"But people call you that."

"Unfortunately."

"Where's peg?"

"Excellent question."

Sera looked at Perrin.

Perrin became fascinated by bread.

Teren arrived late.

He and Sera barely spoke about water.

That told me they had spoken too much already.

After dinner Teren brought small black pebble.

Not magical-looking.

Smooth.

"From Mirror Spring?" I asked.

"My father said."

"You know?"

"No."

Good.

He put it on table.

"I used to carry it when I walked route."

"Want me to take it?"

He hesitated.

"No."

Better.

"Why show me?"

"So you understand I'm not asking because of a children's story."

"You're asking because story is how your family remembers a route."

"And because route is how we remembered water didn't start at our gate."

There.

Not sacred relic.

Not quest token.

Memory object.

"Bring your own rock if you need one."

"Already overstocked."

Fine.

**Lakeward east, then Morrow Orchard. I took a short field contract. Lower orchard channels are running low. **This is an actual adventure now, which was more charming when theoretical.**

Postage from Morrow? Could go with morning cart. Bits.

At loft, I laid gear out.

Crutches.

Pack.

Blanket.

Food.

Notebook.

Artifacts.

Empty place for district bottle.

No sword suddenly appeared because chapter had become quest.

Old me found that funny.

Old me also knew most quests began with worse preparation than people admitted later.

Contractor.

Not hero.

Not chosen.

Not even sure path existed in useful form.
