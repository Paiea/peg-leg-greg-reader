TEXT READER · CHAPTER 396

THE PATHFINDER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

My shoulder had improved overnight.

Which was not same as good.

I learned this by reaching for blanket corner with right hand and immediately reconsidering optimism as philosophy.

Sleeping Stone held gray morning.

East wind had pushed mist under overhang sometime before dawn. Not rain. Fine droplets on outer blanket edge and black stone lip.

I sat up slowly.

Neck fine.

Head fine.

Shoulder bruised dark across upper front, tender where stone had clipped me.

Arm forward to chest height.

Fine enough.

Out to side.

Fine enough until higher.

Overhead.

No.

Crutch load test seated first.

Plant right crutch.

Press gradually.

Ache.

Stable.

More pressure.

Still stable.

No sharp catch.

Good.

Standing load next.

Ugly but functional.

Yesterday's stone had purchased itself one morning of attention.

Fair.

Three Sisters remained ahead.

White Reed behind involved slide bypass, downhill travel, and admitting route had beaten me before contractual threshold.

That was not reason to continue.

Important.

Actual reasons:

shoulder functional;

weather dry enough;

food sufficient;

water sufficient;

route evidence still plausible;

Three Sisters was next major decision point.

Continue conditionally.

I ate flatbread and dried fruit.

Refilled ordinary flask from Sleeping Stone seep?

No.

Unknown quality.

I had enough water from White Reed plus Jonah's flask.

Do not create stomach adventure.

I recorded seep unchanged from night observation and packed.

District bottle remained sealed.

Trial limb remained wrapped.

Shoulder made extra carried weight more annoying, but rough route still made crutches safer than experimental gait.

I adjusted pack so blanket rode lower and trial build closer to spine.

Small difference.

Useful.

Before leaving, I looked again at orchard twine in shelter corner.

Cut clean.

Not ancient.

Could have been last month.

Could have been last year.

Could have been five years if dry enough.

I wrote:

**Sleeping Stone: human use later than original abandonment possible. Twine condition suggests not ancient. Timing unknown.**

No mystery stranger.

Just people using a place people supposedly stopped using.

Humans were inconsiderate toward clean historical narratives.

I left after first light.

Above Sleeping Stone, path did something stories rarely mentioned.

It became boring.

For almost a bell.

Thank gods.

Old channel climbed gently along hillside.

Trees thinner.

Ground dry under pale grass.

Occasional stone edging still visible.

My shoulder warmed with crutch use but didn't worsen.

I shortened stride.

More rests.

Not because route demanded drama.

Because body demanded arithmetic.

At first rest I checked map.

Sleeping Stone to Three Sisters had one useful line on Garrick's copy:

**keep water wall until wall turns inward; cross hollow where sisters show together.**

Story version Jonah remembered:

**When three sisters share one shadow, leave the old water and walk between them.**

Same instruction?

Maybe.

Different enough to matter.

The water wall continued.

Then stopped.

Not gradually.

One section simply ended at a broad fan of pale gravel.

Beyond it, hillside opened into shallow basin with three separate ridges visible through morning haze.

No three stone spires.

I stopped.

Map problem.

Could be wrong basin.

Could be Sisters not visible from this angle.

Could be haze.

Could be landmark changed.

Could be I had already drifted.

I backed to last certain point.

Important habit.

Last certain point: old channel wall, one large white-barked tree growing through outer stones, water wall curving slightly inward.

Garrick: wall turns inward.

Here.

So likely hollow.

Story: three sisters share one shadow.

No sisters.

I waited for haze to move.

It didn't care.

Could use sun? Morning light behind ridge. Shadow language might refer time of day, not route geometry.

Unhelpful now.

I scanned ground.

Two possible tracks.

Lower track followed gravel fan east.

Upper track crossed grass toward low saddle.

No current marker.

Old route copy showed channel line ending before Three Sisters, then single line through hollow.

Which line?

No scale.

Excellent.

I sat.

This was pathfinding.

Not walking where somebody already knew.

Evidence.

Lower gravel had wash channels.

Recent enough to cut grass edge.

Upper track had compacted soil patches but could be animals.

One old squared stone lay half buried near upper track.

Human infrastructure clue.

Not proof path.

I checked back side.

Shallow groove across top.

Could have held something.

Water infrastructure maybe.

Could also be old boundary marker.

I left it.

Then saw three black points appear when haze shifted.

Not ahead.

Farther north.

Three narrow stone spires on ridge shoulder.

Three Sisters.

From where I sat they were separate.

As sun cleared cloud edge, their shadows stretched downslope.

Not one shadow.

Story instruction likely depended time or position.

Garrick's practical instruction better:

cross hollow where sisters show together.

They showed together from upper track.

Lower gravel route angled away.

Upper.

I marked decision.

No triumph.

Just less wrong.

The upper track climbed toward saddle.

Shoulder objected.

I rested every short rise.

At one point I moved pack ten paces ahead, returned, then moved myself.

Slow.

Ridiculous.

Effective.

By midday Three Sisters filled skyline.

They weren't sisters up close.

They were three narrow basalt fins rising from broken ridge, each perhaps several stories tall, with different lean.

From below, two overlapped.

Third stood apart.

Hollow beneath them was broad bowl of grass, exposed stone, and old slide scars.

And there it was.

Lower bench.

Or what remained.

I stopped well back.

Contract threshold.

Three Sisters reached.

Four copper now possible if route beyond genuinely unsafe and notes useful.

That should not matter to safety judgment.

Which meant I needed to notice that it mattered.

I did.

Then put money away mentally.

Assess.

Old lower bench crossed left side of hollow beneath fins.

First third intact.

Middle section gone.

Not `narrow.`

Gone.

A slide scar dropped through it into ravine below.

Opposite bench resumed perhaps thirty paces across.

Loose north-face upper track visible as faint line climbing above scar.

Explicit no-go.

Garrick had said don't climb it if lower bench cut.

Lower bench was cut.

Contract answer looked simple.

Turn around.

Four copper.

Useful notes.

Quest fails safely.

I should have felt disappointed.

Instead I felt suspicious.

Not of Garrick.

Of word `route.`

Old path gone did not mean no path.

But contract had explicit warning because people died by inventing alternatives.

Do not become clever corpse.

I stayed back and surveyed hollow without approaching scar.

Three Sisters bowl had old water wall descending from right.

Not same wall as below? Maybe continuation.

A shallow seasonal channel cut through center grass.

Currently damp.

No meaningful flow.

At far southern side, below slide scar, trees thickened along contour.

Could be lower bypass.

Could be cliff.

Could be nothing.

Map did not show.

I moved along safe inner hollow, not toward slide edge.

At southern end found old stone steps.

Three.

Then five.

Then a descending line hidden under fern.

Not trail exactly.

Constructed.

Old.

Where go?

Down.

Potential bypass beneath scar.

Garrick had warned against north-face upper track specifically.

He had not mentioned southern descent.

That did not make it safe.

But it made it information.

I followed only until first turn, using crutches carefully.

Shoulder now more than ache.

Still stable.

Steps descended to narrow shelf under trees.

Shelf continued east beneath slide scar, hidden from hollow.

Old retaining stones.

Recent cut branches.

There.

Human use again.

Not ancient.

Somebody had maintained enough to cut branches.

I stopped.

Could be current route.

Could be hunter path.

Could terminate under unstable scar.

Need inspect without committing.

I left pack at top? No, never separate sample/gear too far. I had pack. Fine.

I descended another short section.

Shelf widened.

Then sound.

Water.

Not trickle.

Running water below.

I moved to safe opening between trees.

A narrow stream ran east under slide debris, emerging from broken stone culvert beneath lower bench line.

Interesting.

Old route's water had not vanished.

At least some water crossed beneath scar.

No source claim.

Could be local tributary.

Could be Mirror system.

I recorded.

Then noticed something else.

A plank.

Across stream.

Weathered but not rotten-looking from distance.

One end rested on old stone.

Other on newer stacked rocks.

Human crossing.

Recent enough arrangement maybe.

No footprints clear.

No person.

This changed decision.

Not `safe route exists.`

Only `somebody has used a lower crossing after bench failure.`

I could inspect.

Should I?

Shoulder.

Time.

Weather.

Contract.

Three days food.

Mirror Spring likely beyond.

If lower bypass crossed beneath scar and rejoined bench, I could continue without violating explicit north-face no-go.

But walking directly under active slide scar would be stupid.

Was shelf under scar or below stable toe?

Need geometry.

I backed up to hollow and looked from distance.

Southern descent curved below scar's lower toe, not beneath vertical release face.

Trees on shelf mature.

Retaining wall old.

Likely older alternate water-maintenance route.

Still risk.

Not same as upper loose track.

I ate.

Rested shoulder.

Checked sky.

East wind increasing.

No rain.

Haze thinning.

Three Sisters shadows now stretched differently.

At this angle, all three shadows touched across hollow floor.

Shared one shadow.

I laughed.

"Of course."

Story instruction wasn't telling me where route began.

It might be telling time.

When sun reached certain angle, shadows connected.

And where they connected?

The joined shadow crossed southern descent mouth.

That was too neat.

Dangerous neat.

I did not let it become proof.

But people without clocks could encode departure timing or route visibility in story.

Maybe.

Or coincidence.

Write both.

**At current season/day position, three spire shadows join across hollow near southern descent after midday. Story phrase may encode timing/position. Not established.**

This was why stories survived.

Not because they were precise.

Because sometimes precision wore badly and image wore well.

Decision.

I could stop here.

Contract would pay four.

Safe.

Useful.

Or inspect lower bypass far enough to determine whether it rejoined old route without entering unstable toe.

Shoulder functional but accumulating pain.

I had daylight.

Weather dry.

Food.

No companion.

That last one cut both ways.

Nobody to help.

Nobody to pressure.

I set turnaround:

one bell maximum;

no travel beneath fresh bare scar;

no loose slope;

no jump/gap requiring crutch commitment;

if shelf narrows below safe crutch placement, return;

if crossing plank questionable, do not use;

if shoulder worsens sharply, return to Sleeping Stone.

Good.

I descended southern steps again.

At stream crossing, plank looked worse close.

One side cracked.

No.

But stream itself narrow.

Maybe three paces.

Water below knee depth? Hard to tell. Fast enough to matter.

Stones.

Crutches and current.

No.

Could cross upstream where wider/shallow?

I followed bank without committing.

Thirty paces south, stream spread over flat stone into broad ankle-deep sheet.

Better.

Bottom visible.

Still slippery.

Could use trial limb?

Absolutely not.

Crutches.

Need three points.

Shoulder.

This was actual decision.

I tested first crutch tip on submerged stone from bank.

Held.

Second.

Held.

Step right foot.

Water cold.

Left side absent lower leg meant body geometry strange but familiar.

Crutch placement wide.

Move.

One.

Pause.

Two.

Right shoulder complained under load.

Not sharp.

Three.

Halfway.

Water pushed crutch shaft, not enough to move tip.

Then upstream pulse.

Not flood.

A sudden stronger surge, maybe release from pocket or gate? No gate here known.

Water rose around right ankle and hit both crutches harder.

I froze.

Bad.

Moving during unknown surge worse.

Plant.

Lower center.

Wait.

Surge lasted seconds.

Then eased.

No magic.

No need.

I backed out.

Same route.

Slow.

Returned south bank.

Decision made.

Not today.

Could perhaps cross after water settled, but shoulder already had one warning.

And unknown pulses made shallow ford less predictable.

Useful information.

I recorded time, weather, approximate water rise, no claim cause.

Three Sisters lower bench cut.

North-face upper track no-go.

Southern old stone descent exists and shows recent branch cutting.

Lower shelf reaches stream crossing below slide toe.

Plank cracked/untrusted.

Shallow alternate ford exists south but experienced short unpredicted surge during test; aborted crossing.

That was enough.

Contract threshold met.

Four copper likely.

But Mirror Spring remained beyond.

Could I camp Three Sisters and try morning lower water?

Three-day food.

Day one Morrow to Sleeping Stone.

Day two Sleeping Stone to Three Sisters.

If camp here, day three would be attempt beyond plus return impossible to Morrow same day likely.

Food nominal three days, not cliff edge.

Could ration.

But shoulder.

And sample return.

Adventure had finally reached the point where continuing required changing plan, not merely walking harder.

Good.

I climbed back into hollow.

Needed shelter.

Three Sisters had no named shelter on map.

Sleeping Stone behind maybe one bell plus shoulder, more downhill than uphill? Could return there safely before dark if I left now.

That preserved option to turn back White Reed tomorrow.

Or maybe try Three Sisters again morning from Sleeping Stone? Waste distance.

Better to find safe hollow camp only if weather dry.

East wind.

No rain watch.

But exposed hollow.

I inspected southern wall and found shallow rock lip, not Sleeping Stone quality but enough windbreak.

Ground dry.

No overhead loose rock.

Camp.

This was risk but reasonable.

I chose it.

No fire.

Food.

Water.

Shoulder rest.

I checked bruise.

Darker.

No new swelling obvious.

Crutch load after rest still stable.

Good.

Trial limb stayed wrapped.

District bottle stayed sealed.

Mirror Spring remained unsampled.

Magic stayed 139.

No reason to make water surge about me.

At dusk, Three Sisters became black against pale sky.

Their shadows disappeared because everything became shadow.

Story less useful then.

I wrote route notes carefully.

Not because contract.

Because if I turned back, notes were what made failure useful.

Then I noticed one detail I had missed.

The recent branch cuts on southern descent.

Cuts were angled clean.

Not weather break.

At least several.

Some leaves on cut twigs were still dull green.

Recent.

How recent?

Days maybe.

Weeks maybe depending plant.

Not years.

Someone had used or cleared lower bypass recently.

That did not mean safe.

But it meant tomorrow's choice wasn't between known dead route and stupid improvisation.

There was current human behavior to understand.

No person appeared.

No camp smoke.

No convenient guide.

Just cuts.

I added:

**Southern descent vegetation cut recently enough leaves remain partly green. Identity/purpose unknown. Do not infer route safety.**

Then one more line:

**Question tomorrow: observe stream before first ring. If baseline lower/stable and shoulder improved, consider bounded crossing attempt only if return margin remains credible. Otherwise turn back and claim Three Sisters threshold.**

Pathfinder.

Not because I found path.

Because I found where certainty ended and still had to decide what counted as a road.

Three Sisters stood above me.

Mirror Spring somewhere beyond.

For first time since accepting contract, I could earn money by going home.

That made continuing more honest.

Tomorrow I would find out whether honest was enough.
