Three Rays mark stamped into one lower bracket.

Nothing mounted inside.

Adjustable mounts.

Maybe.

Could have held glass strips at different heights.

Could have held signs.

Could have held bells.

I drew it.

No conclusions.

There.

Isolation.

Not as complete as Mirror route.

Road still wide.

But no maintained marker behind line of sight.

I felt it anyway.

Choice becoming place.

The ridge rose gradually.

Fog below, clear above.

Sun bright enough to make wet leaves irritating.

At what might once have been Marker Eight, only a foundation remained.

Square socket.

Broken number stone nearby, face down.

Could be decorative.

No reason to marry theory yet.

I kept going.

One bell beyond Seven, road split around a low rock spine.

Right branch higher, exposed.

No map beyond Seven.

No marker.

Both looked used.

This was first real route ambiguity.

Evidence.

Old drainage channel followed lower branch.

Iron post remnants followed upper.

Which belonged to road?

Maybe both.

I backed fifty paces.

Last certain Three Rays feature: west-facing slab.

From there, line of road aimed toward upper branch.

Lower branch looked like later water/service path.

Upper more likely main road.

I chose upper.

Recorded.

Twenty minutes later, I regretted confidence.

Not because wrong.

Because outer edge started failing.

Road narrowed against steep slope.

Old retaining wall below had bulged outward.

Several paving stones tilted toward drop.

No barrier.

No recent tread.

I stopped before it.

Could cross inside edge.

Maybe.

Distance fifteen paces.

If wall failed, inside edge might go too.

No need.

Turn around?

Reasonable.

But lower branch could bypass.

Backtrack.

Try lower.

I did.

That was exploration, apparently.

Mostly returning to places you had already walked because first guess developed opinions.

Lower branch descended into trees.

Damp channel beside it carried thin water.

Path narrower but ground more stable.

Not bell.

A repeated light knock.

Wind-driven.

Clink.

Pause.

Clink.

I slowed.

No person voices.

No machinery rhythm.

Not bridge.

Service underpass maybe.

Real.

Still mounted.

Three Rays stamp at top.

I stopped well back.

That was road structure.

Do not remove.

Cloudy strip maybe same family as Lakeward half-wheel's glass.

Not painted surface.

Suspended or layered.

Interesting.

Could be weathered glass thickness.

At one position, all three bands lit faintly.

At another, only lower.

No magic sensation obvious.

Could use line quietener? Irrelevant.

Current reader? Maybe not.

HT-LP-4? No.

Eyes sufficient.

I recorded.

Then noticed stone arch had two grooves in floor aligned with bracket.

Wheel grooves?

Guide lines?

If cart stopped here, glass could be viewed from fixed position.

Maybe route-check station.

This was stronger.

Not full system.

But physical design.

I got closer without touching bracket.

Three bands.

One cracked corner.

Bracket adjustable holes like iron frame earlier.

Sunlight intermittent through canopy made readings inconsistent.

Original road likely had fewer trees.

Again.

Current landscape corrupting old system.

Good.

Then clink stopped.

Wind died.

Fog rose.

Fast.

Not from sky.

From lower valley.

Gray pushed between trees and across branch in sheets.

Morning ridge fog returning early?

Could happen.

No rain watch did not mean no fog.

Turnaround rule.

If fog erased known return line, stop and wait rather than invent geography.

Behind me, maybe forty paces.

Beyond that branch curved.

Need return now before visibility worsened.

I turned.

Fog thickened faster than I liked.

Road underfoot still visible ten paces.

Then six.

Then four.

No panic.

Old me knew fog.

Rule one: don't speed because scared.

Rule two: maintain contact with known feature line.

Keep channel.

Crutch tips on stone, not soft edge.

Move.

Problem.

Upper and lower split both disappeared into gray.

I knew I had come from lower.

Which direction from junction led west?

I had turned around once already. Orientation memory good, but fog makes confidence expensive.

West-facing slab farther back if correct branch.

Fresh.

Mine.

Follow.

Good.

No ancient magic required.

Except wind shifted.

Sound.

Not animal.

Stone on stone.

Retaining failure?

Not enough to fall.

Enough.

No dramatic cliff collapse.

A section maybe six paces long slumped downslope with dirt and stones, disappearing into fog.

I was one pace behind failure line.

Good.

Body started rotating downhill.

This was different from rock fragment.

No time for magic maybe.

Use right foot pivot.

Throw left crutch uphill.

Get chest toward slope.

I did.

But held.

Not elegant.

Stopped.

Outer road continued rattling away.

No second movement immediate.

Right shoulder sharp now.

Not pop.

Not weakness.

Pain.

Crutches intact.

Right knee bruised maybe.

No open skin.

Pack still secure.

No magic.

Good.

"You hurt?"

"Didn't see anyone."

"No."

"Good."

"Also found mounted cloudy glass strip in old stone arch on lower branch east of split. Three Rays stamp. Still in bracket."

Shade glass.

"Don't know. Note says `shade glass`, that's all I remember."

"Can I see note?"

"Tomorrow. I'm closing Sunroad west access first."
