TEXT READER · CHAPTER 380

THE INTERMEDIARY

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The first useful thing I did that morning was not buy anything.

Progress remained humiliating.

Carrow's north-market equipment board had changed while I was east.

Some notices gone.

Some new.

Most ordinary.

**TWO HANDCART WHEELS / IRON RIM / BUY OR TRADE**

**THREE ROOF LAMPS / ONE NEEDS WICK HOUSING**

**MILL SOUTH / NEED ONE DRY SHAFT COUPLER / CURRENT PATTERN ONLY**

Then:

**NORTH FREIGHT YARD SEEKS 4 YARD SWAY SHOES / OLD DISTRICT PATTERN ACCEPTABLE / OPEN DISPOSAL OR CLEAN PRIVATE CHAIN REQUIRED / CURRENT LOAD RATING NOT REQUIRED / BENCH FUNCTION MUST PASS / OFFER TO 8S FOR FOUR**

I stopped.

Not because artifact.

Because wording.

Open disposal.

Clean private chain.

Current load rating not required.

Bench function must pass.

Buyer knew exactly what problem it was willing to own.

Good buyer.

I copied notice.

Then saw another one three rows lower.

**CROSS STREET SURPLUS / 5 OLD YARD SHOES / DISTRICT MARKS / SELL LOT / 10S / NO SPLIT**

I stared.

Maybe same thing.

Maybe not.

Could be wrong pattern.

Could be stolen.

Could be five dead lumps with district marks and ambition.

Could be exact mismatch.

Buyer wanted four.

Seller had five.

Price gap existed.

Paper gap likely existed.

Function gap existed.

And I had enough silver to buy the seller lot if I wanted to be stupid.

That was no longer the same thing as having a reason to own it.

Good.

No inventory.

I took both notices down only long enough to copy exact wording, then pinned them back.

Ownership of paper remained with society.

Cross Street Surplus was three lanes south.

I went there first.

Two crutches.

No trial hardware.

No magic.

No reason to complicate morning with body experiment.

The surplus stall occupied half an old cooper shed.

Iron hooks.

Cart latches.

Lantern cages.

Old gate rollers.

Three chipped heat stones in a basket labeled **WARM SOMETIMES**.

Honest merchandising.

The yard shoes were under bench.

Five black iron housings, each about two hands long, with thick flat base, paired side jaws, and dull gray ceramic bar inside.

Broad category familiar.

Old freight yards used them under temporary load frames or beside rails to damp lateral sway.

Not magic enough to impress anyone.

Useful enough to prevent expensive things from becoming interesting.

Seller was a woman maybe forty with scar across one eyebrow and absolutely no interest in my life.

Good.

I showed copied notice.

"These yours?"

"Yes."

"All five same lot?"

"Four are. Fifth came with them."

There.

Already worse.

"Which four?"

She pointed.

The fifth had slightly different base casting.

"District source?"

"Old south warehouse clearance."

"Receipt?"

She reached under counter and produced folded sale sheet.

Good.

Not defensive.

I read.

**SOUTH WAREHOUSE DISPOSAL / LOT 31 / FOUR SWAY SHOES / MISCELLANEOUS FASTENERS**

Four.

Not five.

"Fifth isn't on receipt."

"No."

"Where from?"

"My brother bought it with a rail cart years ago."

"Paper?"

"No."

There.

Buyer wanted clean release or private chain.

A brother's rail cart might be clean private chain.

Might not.

"Why sell five together?"

"Because I want them gone."

"Why one silver six?"

"Because four work."

"You tested?"

"Clamped to bench. Pulled frame sideways. Four damp. Fifth barely."

Interesting.

"Which fifth?"

"The one with different base."

"So four receipt-matched ones work?"

"Yes."

"Current rating?"

"No."

"Service marks?"

"Old."

"Voided?"

She looked at me.

"What?"

There.

Not her domain.

I crouched enough to see side plates.

Each of the four matched units carried district oval and three short punch marks crossing lower edge.

Void maybe.

Could be inspection.

Could be damage.

Do not invent.

"Do you know what those punches mean?"

"No."

"Buyer notice says open disposal or clean private chain."

"I have disposal receipt."

"Receipt says four. That's good. But buyer may want docket or void status on district marks."

She folded arms.

"Why?"

"Because if marks look active, their own records people may refuse them."

"They're old iron shoes."

"Yes."

"So?"

"So offices enjoy paper."

She considered.

"What do you want?"

Good.

Real transaction.

"Two silver if I bring a buyer who completes at eight silver or more for the four receipt-matched shoes."

Her eyes narrowed.

"Two silver?"

"Only if sale completes."

"You found buyer already."

"I found notice."

"That's not worth four."

"Then you can answer notice yourself."

Silence.

I liked that.

She looked at my copied buyer notice.

"One."

"Two."

"One silver and one bit for copying."

"Two silver. You keep fifth."

"Why would I keep worst one?"

"Because buyer asked four and your receipt proves four."

She disliked that.

Exactly.

"If buyer takes all five?"

"Then your problem gets worse because fifth has weaker chain."

"Maybe buyer doesn't care."

"Then negotiate it separately. My fee is for four receipt-matched units."

There.

Scope.

She stared long enough that I wondered whether I had become annoying professionally.

Probably.

"Two if they pay nine silver," she said.

"Two if they pay eight silver or more."

"One if eight. Two if nine."

I thought.

Seller incentive.

My incentive.

Could distort me toward pushing buyer one copper higher.

Not automatically wrong.

But buyer should know I am paid by seller.

Important.

"Fine. Two silver if completed at eight silver or more. You pay records bits if we need them."

She nodded.

"Written."

She found scrap paper.

Good seller.

We wrote:

**Greg introduction / transaction assist. Seller pays 2s if four receipt-matched sway shoes sell for at least 8s. Seller covers agreed public-record copy/inspection bits. No fee if no completed sale. Fifth shoe excluded unless separately agreed.**

She signed.

I signed.

No modern broker license speech.

No ownership transfer.

No promises about function.

I told her one more thing.

"Buyer needs bench function."

"Four work."

"I am not certifying that."

"I didn't ask."

Good.

North Freight Yard was across river-side market.

Buyer notice belonged receiving office.

I arrived before midday.

Showed copied seller sheet, not seller's original receipt.

Receiving clerk read.

"Five?"

"Four are on district disposal receipt. Fifth separate and excluded."

"Price?"

"Seller asks one silver six. She will transact four at one silver two minimum if other terms clear."

That was not exactly seller ask.

But signed fee terms established minimum, not offer.

Careful.

"Correction. Seller has not offered one-two. She agreed my commission only if completed sale reaches one-two or more."

Clerk looked at me.

"Difference?"

"Yes."

"Annoying difference."

"Important difference."

He sighed.

"Buyer is yard quartermaster."

Role, not name.

Good.

Quartermaster came.

Older man with heavy apron and left hand missing two fingertips.

No need explain.

He read notice.

"Four same pattern?"

"Looks same broad pattern. I have not measured."

"South warehouse?"

"Receipt says South Warehouse disposal lot thirty-one."

"Open disposal?"

"Receipt says disposal. I have not verified local status class yet."

"Current cert?"

"None established."

"Function?"

"Seller says four damp under bench pull. Buyer notice says your bench test."

He looked at me properly.

"You seller?"

"No."

"Buyer?"

"No."

"Then what are you?"

There.

Title.

"Intermediary."

That sounded too adult.

He frowned.

"Broker?"

"Recent diagnosis."

Elian's line came back.

Fine.

"Paid by who?"

"Seller. Two silver if sale completes at eight silver or more."

His face changed.

Good reason.

"So you want my price higher."

"Yes."

"Why tell me?"

"Because otherwise you should assume worse."

He laughed once.

Not warm.

But fair.

"Good. Then I want seven silver."

"Your posted offer says to eight silver."

"To."

"Not at."

"Correct."

Negotiation.

Excellent.

I had no authority to accept.

"Bench test first?"

"Paper first. I'm not moving yard time for stolen district gear."

There.

Friction.

Actual.

"Receipt enough?"

"Not for our ledger. Need docket or void note."

Exactly Ch483.

I did not smile.

Would have been unbearable.

"Seller agreed to pay public-record bits."

"Good."

"Can your office check?"

"We can, but not today."

"Why?"

"Clerk who knows old south warehouse index is off."

Human system.

"Tomorrow?"

"Maybe."

Maybe was expensive.

"District records can search lot."

"Then do that."

"Fee?"

"Seller pays records bits. My time is on my commission."

He nodded.

"Bring docket copy. If open disposal and official marks voided or sale-cleared, we bench four."

"Price still open."

"Obviously."

Good.

I went back seller.

Explained.

She swore at records.

Longer than necessary.

Not Greg-style.

Actual speech.

"Every time district sells old yard iron they stamp a receipt, punch three holes somewhere no one explains, put the real answer in a book across town, then ten years later everybody acts surprised that nobody remembers which hole means what. I bought the whole cart because the warehouse foreman said cleared. He said cleared. He was standing under a sign that said clearance. What else was I supposed to ask, whether the word clearance had been cleared?"

I waited.

She finished.

"Records bits," I said.

"Fine."

Good.

I did not make records office a chapter again.

I already knew route.

I already knew question.

Lot thirty-one.

South Warehouse.

Disposal docket.

The clerk took one bit to inspect and another to copy.

Seller reimbursed both bits.

Docket:

**SOUTH WAREHOUSE / OPEN DISPOSAL / LOT 31**
**FOUR SWAY SHOES**
**SERVICE AUTHORITY VOIDED BY THREE-PUNCH LOWER-SEAL STRIKE**
**SOLD AS-IS / NO CURRENT LOAD CERTIFICATION**
**PRIVATE TRANSFER PERMITTED**

There.

Three punches.

Not active inspection.

Void strike.

Exactly what buyer needed.

No mechanism claim.

No function claim.

No fifth shoe.

I carried copy to North Freight Yard.

Quartermaster read.

"Good."

One word.

Worth two bits.

"Bench?"

"Bring seller tomorrow second bell."

"Transport?"

"Her problem."

Correct.

I returned seller.

She had a handcart.

Also correct.

Next morning we rolled four shoes to North Freight Yard.

I did not help pull cart.

Two crutches.

Seller had one stall hand pull.

I carried paper in shoulder bag.

Hand economy still irritating.

At yard test bench, buyer's mechanic took over.

Not me.

He checked housings.

Measured jaw openings.

Looked at ceramics.

Tapped one lightly with small wood handle.

No mystical scan.

First shoe clamped under short side frame.

Mechanic hung weighted bar from frame and pulled sideways with rope through floor ring.

Released.

Frame moved.

Shoe resisted and settled motion faster than bare frame baseline.

Useful.

No precise rating.

Second similar.

Third squealed.

Mechanic stopped.

Opened housing cover.

Dirt and old grease.

"Function?"

Seller said, "It worked at my bench."

Mechanic said, "It may. Not like this."

He asked seller permission to wipe old grit.

She said yes.

He cleaned track with rag.

Reclamped.

Third damped, but slower.

Mechanic marked **WEAK / FUNCTIONAL**.

Fourth did nothing first pull.

Seller's face hardened.

"Again."

Mechanic did.

Nothing.

He checked jaw seating.

One jaw had not fully closed due bent latch.

"Latch."

"Repairable?" quartermaster asked.

Mechanic said, "Probably. Not testing repair today."

Good.

Fourth failed current bench test.

Now deal friction changed.

Buyer wanted four.

Seller had three pass, one failed, one excluded undocumented weak unit back at stall.

No sale at posted condition.

This was where intermediary could become liar.

I did not.

Quartermaster said, "Three at five silver."

Seller said, "No."

I stayed quiet.

"Five each?" she asked.

"Five total."

"Get bent."

Good.

He pointed at failed fourth.

"I'm buying three useful shoes and one future repair if I take four."

"You're not taking four at five silver."

"Then no deal."

Could end.

My fee zero.

Correct.

I thought.

Not about squeezing.

About transaction.

Buyer needs four.

Seller has fifth weak but working, undocumented.

Fourth failed due latch likely repairable but not proven.

Could seller repair and return later?

Could buyer buy three now, fourth conditional after repair?

Buyer notice wants four.

Maybe quantity timing flexible.

Ask.

"Do you need four today?"

Quartermaster looked at me.

"Need four before next loading frame goes live sixth day."

There.

Time.

"Would you buy three now and fourth after seller repairs latch, same lot chain?"

"Maybe."

Seller said, "I'm not splitting lot."

I looked at her.

"You already brought four."

"Price was for four."

"Yes."

"This isn't price."

Correct.

"What price for three now plus repaired fourth later?"

She thought.

Buyer impatient.

Good.

She said, "Nine silver for all four, fourth delivered functioning before sixth day."

Quartermaster laughed.

"No."

"Your notice says to eight."

"For four passing now."

"Then eight with fourth later."

He considered.

Mechanic said, "Latch is probably simple."

"Probably isn't pass."

"Correct."

Quartermaster looked at seller.

"Seven silver. Three now. Fourth after pass. One silver held until the fourth passes."

Seller snapped, "Withheld from what? You already cut price."

Good question.

They argued.

Not elegantly.

Seller talked too much.

Quartermaster repeated same three sentences.

I listened.

Actual gap became structure, not price.

Seller wanted payment enough now to justify repair.

Buyer wanted leverage to ensure fourth arrived.

"Separate the fourth," I said.

They both looked at me.

"Three shoes sale now. Fourth is conditional purchase after pass. Stop pretending one agreement has to carry both."

Seller frowned.

"Then three price?"

"Your decision."

Quartermaster said, "Six silver for three. Two silver for fourth after pass."

Seller looked.

"Seven for three. One for fourth."

"No."

"Six for three. Two for fourth. You collect the fourth only when it passes here."

Seller thought.

That recreated buyer's posted eight-silver total.

But delayed fourth risk on seller.

My commission agreement: completed sale reaches eight silver or more.

Partial three sale did not complete my condition.

Good.

I said, "My fee only if fourth completes and total reaches eight silver."

Seller glared.

"I'm aware."

Good.

She accepted.

Written.

North Freight Yard bought three now for six silver.

Fourth remained seller's.

Conditional: buyer would buy fourth for two silver if it passed same bench before sixth-day frame deadline.

No obligation to buy failed fourth.

No seller penalty beyond no second payment.

Clean.

Seller took failed shoe back.

No fee to me yet.

Important.

I had done work and earned zero so far.

That felt correct.

Two days later seller sent runner to home.

Not because protagonist.

Because she owed potential commission and knew address from written sheet.

"Passes tomorrow," note said.

No detail.

Next morning I went North Freight because if fourth passed, transaction completed and my fee triggered.

Seller arrived with fourth.

Latch had new pin and old bent tongue straightened.

Who repaired it did not matter.

Mechanic bench tested.

First pull.

Damped.

Second.

Damped.

He marked **PASS / CURRENT BENCH ONLY / NO LOAD CERT**.

Quartermaster paid two silver.

Total deal: eight silver.

Seller paid me two silver under agreement.

There.

Two silver.

Not a day-labor fee.

A commission.

I had not sold day.

I had connected existing demand and supply, narrowed title uncertainty, protected buyer's test authority, helped restructure a failed four-unit sale into staged transaction, and waited to get paid until actual friction cleared.

Still bounded.

Different engine.

Seller counted my coins.

"Two silver."

"Yes."

"You should have taken one."

"No."

She snorted.

Quartermaster said, "You cost less than yard clerk losing half day."

That was compliment shaped like accounting.

Better.

"Need anything else?" I asked.

"No."

Good.

No automatic deal pipeline.

Seller said, "I have fifth shoe."

"No."

"Didn't even ask price."

"No paper."

"Brother's rail cart."

"Find chain first."

"It works."

"Buyer needed clean chain."

"You've become boring."

"Professional growth."

She left.

No fifth-shoe side quest.

I stood by yard wall with eighteen copper.

Ten floor.

Eight deployable.

Exactly the same safe artifact ceiling I had carried into stillhook auction at eighteen.

Interesting.

But context had changed.

Then, eighteen had been whole purse after travel work and pending fee.

Now I had reached eighteen again by closing a transaction whose total was silver-scale without owning the goods.

Not rich.

Not even thirty-copper reserve.

Still nowhere near paying Vale responsibly.

Still not buying artifacts.

But direction.

I wrote notebook:

**North Freight / sway-shoe transaction**
**Seller: 4 receipt-matched units + 1 excluded weak/unclear-chain unit**
**Buyer: needed 4 old-pattern shoes; clean release; current load cert not required; bench function required**
**Friction 1: district marks / release**
**Resolved: open-disposal docket + three-punch void strike**
**Friction 2: 1 of 4 failed bench due latch**
**Resolved commercially, not technically: 3 sold now, 4th conditional after repair/pass**
**Final completed price: 8s**
**My fee: 2s**
**Silver +2s; ordinary copper unchanged**
**I owned none of it.**

Then:

**Do not confuse facilitating with certifying.**
**Records office owned title record. Buyer mechanic owned bench function. Seller owned repair. Buyer/seller owned price. I owned connection + scope + structure.**

That line mattered.

Support.

Actual support.

People doing their own jobs better because boundaries aligned.

Not mystical.

Not heroic.

Profitable enough to notice.

I went home.

Lyssa was eating standing over table because apparently sitting had become optional in our household.

She saw coins.

"Paper made money."

"Eventually."

"How much?"

"Three copper."

"How much stuff?"

"One silver two copper transaction."

She stopped chewing.

There.

Scale.

"Yours?"

"No."

"Good."

"Why does everybody say that?"

"Because you like objects."

Fair.

I explained shortest version.

Four yard shoes.

Paper clear.

One failed.

Split transaction.

Repair.

Pass.

Seller paid three.

Lyssa nodded.

Then said, "So you got paid because two stubborn people needed a third stubborn person."

I thought.

"Yes."

"Useful profession."

Sori and Antonius both would hate that summary because it was better.

I counted money again.

Eighteen copper.

Twenty-three Vale.

Bits unspecified.

Magic fifty-nine, fifty-three, one-forty-seven.

No limb update.

No theatre update.

No Hessa.

No debt payment.

No artifact purchase.

Three copper earned.

Silver-scale value moved.

More importantly, I had learned what part was mine.

Not the iron.

Not the ledger.

Not the repair.

Not the test.

The gap.

Sometimes a gap was just empty.

Sometimes it was where money got stuck.

That was worth learning.

I closed notebook.

For once, being in the middle had paid.
