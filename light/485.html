TEXT READER · CHAPTER 380

THE ORGANIZER

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

The South Road notice was still there.

That annoyed me more than if it had disappeared.

**PRIVATE HOUSE REQUIRES THREE SEASONAL FIELD CREWS / SOUTH ROAD / ORGANIZER FEE AVAILABLE / REFERENCES REQUIRED**

Same paper.

Different pin.

Somebody had moved it from the lower corner of the private board to eye level.

No new date.

No urgency strip.

No promise that the work still existed.

Just enough persistence to make pretending I had not copied it weeks ago embarrassing.

I had a mixed purse now: ordinary copper for daily life and enough silver that tiny jobs no longer defined the whole problem.

Vale still open.

Bits unspecified.

Magic fifty-nine, fifty-three, one-forty-seven.

Hark & Venn still had the trial hardware.

No Hessa note.

No theatre runner.

No buyer waiting for me to solve a paper problem.

Good.

I took the notice down long enough to read the tiny line at bottom I had ignored the first time.

**INQUIRE SOUTH ROAD FIELD OFFICE / LOWER GATE / THIRD BELL TO FIFTH**

There.

Actual place.

I copied that too.

Then pinned notice back.

I did not apply immediately.

That felt responsible.

Also suspicious.

I went to theatre first.

Not for work.

Rinna was at side table arguing with a supplier receipt.

The supplier receipt was losing.

She looked up when I stopped outside the work lane.

"You're not on call."

"Correct."

"Then why are you standing there?"

"Reference."

Her face became less welcoming.

Fair.

"For what?"

I showed copy of South Road notice.

She read it.

"Field crews."

"Yes."

"You know field work?"

"Not enough to supervise field work."

"Good start."

"Notice says organizer. I don't know what that means yet."

"Then why do you need me?"

"Because if they ask whether I can keep people, timing, objects, and changing instructions from becoming six separate disasters, you have evidence."

Rinna stared at me.

Then at notice.

"You want me to say you can run a field crew?"

"No."

"Good."

"I want you to say exactly what you can support."

That interested her.

She leaned back.

"Prompt-side work?"

"Yes. Theatre calls generally too, if you think true."

"You keep notes."

"I have been accused."

"You follow scope when scope is clear."

I waited.

"Usually."

Cruel.

"You don't move other people's authority around just because you're bored."

"That sounds flattering."

"It isn't."

"Fine."

"You notice when two people are working from different versions."

"Yes."

"And you ask before touching things now."

Now.

Important.

"Would you sign that?"

"Not those words."

Of course.

She found scrap paper.

Wrote:

**Gregory has worked bounded theatre support under changing rehearsal conditions. He keeps current instructions, asks when authority is unclear, and can coordinate cues, entrances, prop-state handoffs, and light work within assigned scope. This is not a reference for fieldcraft, hiring, safety inspection, or crew supervision outside theatre.**

Then signed Rinna.

Perfect.

"Thank you."

"If they hire you to harvest something and you kill it, that is not my fault."

"Strong confidence."

"Go away."

I went to Vale.

Antonius saw the notice and sighed before I asked.

"No."

"You don't know question."

"Reference."

"How?"

"You brought paper."

Fair.

I put Rinna's note beside notice.

He read it.

"Good reference."

"I know."

"What do you want from me?"

"Narrower thing. Written agreements. Scope. Money. Whether I do what I said after I sign."

"You still have an active account with me."

"Excellent evidence of ongoing relationship."

"That is one interpretation."

He did not reach for paper.

"Why should I reference you?"

Good question.

"Because you can say no."

He almost smiled.

"Better answer."

Then he did reach for paper.

**Gregory keeps unusually detailed records for his age, reads terms before agreeing more often than he used to, and distinguishes observed fact from preferred explanation when forced. I can confirm he has maintained an active account with this office and has honored agreed reporting/credit terms to date. I do not certify field competence, hiring judgment, solvency, or future performance.**

Signed Antonius Vale.

"Solvency."

"You asked for honest."

"I did."

"Try not to regret it."

I folded both references.

South Road field office sat just beyond Lower Gate where city stone gave way to packed road and drainage ditches.

Not countryside.

Not yet.

Carrow still pressed behind me.

Warehouses thinning.

More carts.

More mud.

The office was a long single-story building with three open windows and a porch wide enough for workers to stand without blocking road.

Good design.

I arrived on two crutches halfway between third and fourth bell.

One woman sat behind front table.

No need name.

She wore a brown vest, gray shirt, and expression of someone who had already answered the same question thirteen times.

I showed notice.

"Still open?"

"Maybe."

Strong start.

"What decides?"

"Whether you're an organizer or another person who thinks organizer means shouting at laborers."

Better.

"What does it mean here?"

She looked at my crutches.

Then references.

Then me.

"Sit."

I did.

"Private house has three seasonal crews already contracted. Not yours. You do not hire them. You do not set their wages. You do not fire them. Each crew has its own lead."

Good.

"Then what am I organizing?"

"Four days. Three field sections. Two supply carts. Water. Meal issue. Start order. Crew arrival. Tool issue records. Field handoff. If one section isn't ready, you move sequence without making three crews stand around blaming each other."

There.

That was familiar.

Not fields.

State.

"Who owns work method?"

"Crew leads."

"Safety call?"

"Crew lead for ordinary work. House field steward can stop any section."

"Tool condition?"

"House tool keeper."

"Transport?"

"House carts and one contracted wagon. Organizer tracks, does not drive unless separately agreed."

"Worker disputes?"

"Crew lead unless schedule or house provision caused it."

"Weather?"

"Field steward."

"Pay disputes?"

"Office."

Excellent.

Clear authority.

"How many workers?"

"Depends day. Around twenty across all three crews."

Not my twenty.

Important.

"What are fields doing?"

"Seasonal drainage and late-cut cleanup before wet turn. Ditch clearing. Cut brush. Pull old stakes. Reset access paths. Some bundling. No crop judgment from organizer."

Better.

"Fee?"

She slid a sheet across table.

**ORGANIZER TERM / FOUR DAYS**

**6S ON ACCEPTED START PLAN + CONFIRMED CREW/TRANSPORT CONTACTS**

**12S ON COMPLETION OF FOUR-DAY HANDOFF IF ALL DAILY ORGANIZER RECORDS ACCEPTED**

**NO COMPLETION FEE IF ORGANIZER ABANDONS TERM WITHOUT HOUSE RELEASE**

**HOUSE COVERS AUTHORIZED MESSAGE / RECORD / LOCAL CART COSTS**

Eighteen silver total.

I read again.

Actually silver.

More importantly, I was not being paid to clear a ditch.

I was being paid so twenty people and several moving parts did not waste four days.

"What counts accepted record?"

"Start roster. Actual arrival. Section assignment. Tool issue discrepancies. Cart delay. Work handoff. Anything house steward marks relevant. Not a diary."

"What if crew lead changes sequence for field reason?"

"You record it and adjust shared schedule."

"What if I disagree?"

"If it's field method, you don't matter."

I liked her.

"References required."

I gave both.

She read Rinna first.

Longer than I expected.

Then Antonius.

She looked at me.

"You owe Vale?"

"Yes."

"How much?"

"I haven't reconciled the whole account recently. Enough that I should."

"Does this job require you to front house expenses?"

"Sheet says no."

"Good."

No shame.

Just risk.

"Why field crews?"

"Because notice existed."

"Bad answer."

"Because I spent a long time thinking useful work meant personally doing task. Recently I have been more useful connecting people and keeping separate expertise aligned. I wanted to know if that transfers to more people."

She watched me.

"That's a speech."

"Unfortunately."

"Shorter."

I thought.

"I think I can keep three competent crews from waiting on the same cart."

There.

She nodded once.

"Better."

Interview continued.

Not dramatic.

She gave scenarios.

Two crews arrive, third late.

Water cart missing.

Field steward closes lower section.

One crew lead wants to borrow tools issued to another crew.

Contract wagon leaves early because driver claims route changed.

I did not solve everything brilliantly.

On tool borrowing I said organizer could reassign.

Wrong.

"Tool keeper owns issue."

Right.

On closed field I initially moved crew immediately.

She asked whether alternate section was ready.

I had not checked.

Good.

On late crew I said start others if their work independent.

Correct enough.

On missing water cart I asked whether work could begin safely without it.

"Field steward decides."

Right.

On wagon I asked who contracted it and what written route term said.

That got a tiny nod.

At end she said, "You are not first choice."

Good.

"Who is?"

"Woman who did spring turn. She is north with family."

"Second?"

"Man from Miller Lane. He wants hiring authority we are not giving him."

Interesting.

"So?"

"You can have provisional term if references verify today. Start plan due by first bell tomorrow. If plan accepted, six silver. If first morning goes badly enough, house can release you and you keep only accepted-plan payment."

"Fair."

"Don't say fair until you read whole term."

Antonius disease.

I read whole term.

Four pages.

Not exciting.

Useful.

No hidden worker wage liability.

No requirement I guarantee attendance.

No authority to discipline crews.

House could release for material organizer failure.

I could withdraw before start plan acceptance with no fee and no penalty.

After start, abandoning without release forfeited completion fee.

No invented fine.

I signed.

She signed for field office.

"Now references."

She sent a runner.

House expense.

I waited.

Luxury again.

Rinna's answer returned first.

Confirmed.

Antonius's second.

Confirmed.

The woman at table handed me a packet.

Crew contact sheets.

Three leads, names on paper.

I did not need their names in my head yet.

Better categories first.

North crew.

Middle crew.

Lower crew.

Each had meet point, expected headcount range, tool needs already agreed, and one note from prior organizer.

North crew liked to start early and hated waiting for carts.

Middle crew had one older worker who carried their own ditch gauge.

Lower crew had two workers joining only second day.

Not problems.

Facts.

Transport sheet:

Cart A water and meal.

Cart B tools and spoil baskets.

Contract wagon for cut brush from lower turnout afternoon only.

Field sections:

North ditch.

Middle access line.

Lower drainage cut.

I asked for map.

She gave one.

I asked for current readiness.

"Field steward checks at dusk."

"Can I see results?"

"Posted here after sixth bell."

Good.

I spent rest of afternoon meeting crew leads.

Not recruiting.

Not motivational speech.

Just contact.

North crew lead was a compact woman with mud dried to one boot and no patience for introductions.

"You organizer?"

"Provisional."

"Can you make carts arrive?"

"No."

She stared.

"Can you tell me before they don't?"

"Sometimes."

"Better."

Good relationship start.

Middle crew lead talked too much.

A lot too much.

He explained, without apparent need for air, that his people could clear ditch, path, old stake, light brush, medium brush, some heavy brush if the roots were not stonebound, but they were absolutely not hauling wet spoil uphill again because last season somebody had called that "temporary repositioning" and temporary had lasted three hours and two backs and one argument with a mule.

I let him finish.

"Wet spoil uphill is not in your sheet."

"Good."

"If it appears?"

"We discuss before anybody decides language changes weight."

Excellent.

Lower crew lead was formal.

"Our contracted headcount on first day is six. Second day may be eight. I will confirm at evening bell. We require the two long-handled ditch hooks listed. If unavailable, I prefer reassignment before substitution."

"Because?"

"Short hooks change body position."

"Understood. Tool keeper owns issue. I'll flag availability, not invent replacement."

He nodded.

Different speech.

Different people.

By sixth bell I returned field office.

Dusk readiness sheet posted.

North ditch: **READY**.

Middle access line: **READY**.

Lower drainage cut: **HOLD / WATER STILL HIGH / RECHECK FIRST LIGHT**.

There.

Plan changed before plan existed.

Original packet assumed north, middle, lower start together.

Could assign lower crew elsewhere temporarily.

But alternate work needed readiness.

I asked field steward.

Unnamed man with coat tied around waist and mud to knees.

"If lower still held at first light, what work can that crew legally start?"

"North spoil bundling after north crew clears first hundred pace. Or old-stake pull on middle south edge."

"Which creates least interference?"

"Depends pace."

"Can lower lead choose at start based on actual progress?"

"Yes, if I approve section."

Good.

So plan did not force one.

It created decision point.

I wrote:

**FIRST BELL**

North crew -> North ditch.

Middle crew -> Middle access line.

Lower crew -> report Lower Gate, hold assignment until field-steward first-light status.

If lower cut opens -> Lower drainage.

If still closed -> steward selects North bundling or Middle old-stake edge based on readiness; lower crew lead owns work method.

Cart A -> water/meal first North turnout, then Middle, then Lower Gate hold point unless steward redirects.

Cart B -> tool issue at Lower Gate before crews split; long ditch hooks reserved to Lower crew only if lower drainage opens, otherwise remain tool-keeper custody.

Contract wagon -> lower turnout afternoon only, confirm after first-light lower status.

Then communication.

One runner token to North turnout.

One to Middle.

Field office runner held for wagon notice if lower status changes.

I did not need people sprinting because I had failed to decide where information moved.

That felt old.

Not old life exactly.

Old reflex.

I took plan home.

Lyssa was eating over work table.

Bad sign.

"You got job?"

"Provisional organizer term."

She looked up.

"Organizer."

"Apparently."

"People?"

"Around twenty."

She stopped chewing.

I enjoyed that too much.

"You are in charge of twenty people?"

"No."

"Good."

"Three crew leads are in charge of their crews. Field steward owns field decisions. Tool keeper owns tools. Office owns pay. I own schedule and shared failure."

"Shared failure."

"Strong title."

"How much?"

"Six silver if they accept start plan. Twelve more after four-day handoff if records accepted."

She did math with face.

"Eighteen silver."

"Yes."

"And if you don't ruin crops, the term actually matters."

I looked at her.

"There are no crops in my authority."

"You know what I mean."

I did.

Eighteen silver total.

A different tier from the copper jobs I had been stacking.

If.

Not mine yet.

Important.

"Six first," I said.

"Good."

She went back to eating.

"Also drainage."

"Less romantic."

"Much."

I slept badly because apparently management had discovered anticipation.

First bell came cold and gray.

I reached Lower Gate early.

Two crutches.

Shoulder bag.

Plan.

No trial leg.

No magic.

I was not walking fields if I could avoid it.

Organizer did not mean personally inspecting every ditch.

Field steward arrived with first-light status.

Lower drainage cut: **HOLD**.

Water had dropped, not enough.

Good.

Plan branch B.

Then Cart B did not arrive.

Of course.

North crew arrived.

Middle crew arrived.

Lower crew arrived with six.

No tools.

One cart missing.

Cart A water and meal was there.

I checked time.

Then asked field office.

Cart B axle pin broken three streets north.

Repair underway.

Estimated half bell.

Maybe.

North crew lead said, "We have our hand tools. We need baskets later."

Middle lead began a long explanation about how his crew technically had enough hooks to start but not enough spare handles if one cracked and one always cracked when somebody said there were enough handles.

Lower lead said, "Without long hooks, lower cut is irrelevant. For alternate old-stake work we have personal pry bars."

There.

Capability distributed.

I asked field steward.

"Can lower crew start middle south-edge stake pull now?"

He looked at Middle lead.

"If your crew stays north half until second bell."

Middle lead inhaled for speech.

I raised hand.

"Can you?"

He stopped.

"Yes."

"Then lower takes south-edge stakes until Cart B arrives or lower drainage opens."

Lower lead nodded.

North crew lead said, "We're starting."

"Yes."

They left.

No applause.

Good.

I sent one runner to Cart B repair point for update.

House token.

Then Cart A driver said his route sheet listed Lower first.

My plan said North first.

Old version.

There it was.

Different versions.

Theatre again.

I showed signed start plan.

Driver showed route sheet from yesterday.

Neither of us wrong.

Office had failed update.

I did not yell.

Growth.

"Can you run North first without breaking your cart agreement?"

"Agreement says field office route."

"Then office can correct."

We walked five yards to table.

Office woman wrote amended route.

Driver signed receipt.

North first.

Problem gone.

Not heroic.

Useful.

By second bell, North ditch was moving.

Middle access line moving.

Lower crew pulling old stakes and complaining that whoever drove them last year had hated future generations.

Cart B arrived after repair.

Tool keeper checked pin before releasing cart onward.

Not me.

Good.

Field steward rechecked lower water.

Still hold.

So long hooks stayed with tool keeper.

No reason to issue them just because packet said Lower crew.

By third bell, the lower crew had cleared enough old stakes that Middle lead wanted south edge early.

Conflict.

Not fight.

Timing.

I asked both leads what they needed.

Middle lead gave six sentences.

Lower lead gave two.

Result: lower could finish current thirty paces, then move to North spoil bundling. Middle could expand south afterward.

Field steward approved.

Done.

At midday I realized I had not eaten.

Bad management.

Of self.

I took meal from house issue like everyone else entitled by contract.

Sat on overturned basket near gate.

Hands tired from crutches and paper.

Right leg fine.

Shoulder ordinary.

No magic fatigue because I had not done anything stupid.

North crew lead passed with water cup.

"Cart came."

"I noticed."

"You told us before it came."

"That was goal."

"Good."

Then she left.

That was probably more satisfying than praise should have been.

By fourth bell, field office woman called me over.

She had my start plan.

Marked in three places.

"Accepted."

Six silver on table.

There.

Not completion.

Not eighteen.

Six.

I counted once.

She said, "Your actual-arrival record is missing Cart A route correction time."

I looked.

She was right.

I had written amendment, not time.

"Fix."

I did.

"Also lower crew alternate assignment changed at second bell. Record who approved."

"Field steward."

"Write it."

I did.

"You're doing fine."

Dangerous sentence.

I waited.

"That does not mean relax."

There.

Safer.

"Three more days."

"Yes."

"Completion fee is not earned."

"I know."

"Good."

I put six silver away.

Operating reserve intact.

Twelve more silver still conditional.

Closer.

Not there.

At fifth bell the lower drainage finally opened.

Too late to move crew there productively that day.

Field steward said tomorrow first bell.

Lower lead agreed.

Middle lead had opinions about whether lower water would rise overnight based on cloud shape, ditch smell, one frog, and his grandmother.

Field steward listened politely.

Then said, "We check at first light."

Hessa would have approved.

I did not tell anyone.

At close I wrote actual day state.

North: ditch clear through marked section, spoil bundling partly done.

Middle: access line clear through north half, south-edge stakes partly cleared by Lower crew before handoff.

Lower: drainage closed until fifth bell, no drainage work performed, alternate stake work completed in bounded section.

Cart A: old route sheet corrected at first bell plus.

Cart B: delayed by axle pin repair, arrived before second bell plus.

No worker injuries reported to organizer.

No claim that none existed outside reports.

Tool keeper retained long hooks.

Contract wagon not called because lower brush haul did not start.

Tomorrow: lower drainage only if steward opens at first light.

There.

Not a victory.

A current state.

I knew this feeling.

Old life before a fight when everyone had different capabilities, different timing, and enough ego to make geometry political.

But this was not battle.

Nobody was dying.

Nobody needed buff timing.

Nobody needed me to make them stronger.

They needed water in right place.

Tools not issued to wrong crew.

A cart route updated before driver left.

A field held because water was high.

Three leads allowed to remain better at their jobs than me.

Support without power.

I almost wrote that.

Did not.

Too neat.

Instead I wrote:

**Organizer day 1: plan is not instruction to reality. Plan is shared version until reality changes. Keep authority attached to person who can actually decide.**

Still a little neat.

Fine.

At home Lyssa was not there yet.

Her work had its own schedule.

Good.

I put six new silver beside the rest.

Not eighteen silver for the term.

Not yet.

Twelve completion silver sat in someone else's future obligation, contingent on three more days and acceptable records.

I did not count it.

Growth.

Then I looked at Antonius's reference again.

**reads terms before agreeing more often than he used to**

Rude.

Accurate.

Tomorrow lower field might flood again.

A crew might come short.

Cart B might break something more expensive than a pin.

Middle lead might explain weather to death.

None of that belonged to me yet.

What belonged to me was current state, current scope, and next bell.

For once, that was enough management.
