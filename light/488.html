TEXT READER · CHAPTER 385

THE ACTOR

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Twenty copper lasted through breakfast.

This was less impressive than thirty-two.

It was also more useful than nine.

I decided not to improve it before noon.

Dangerous experiment.

Lyssa had already left for work.

The room was quiet except for street carts, somebody arguing about onions downstairs, and one pigeon that had developed a personal grievance against the sill.

I ate bread.

Used the last of yesterday's onions.

Did not calculate return on onions.

Growth.

Vale eleven.

Hark & Venn nothing due.

Magic fifty-nine, fifty-three, one-forty-seven.

No South Road contract.

No buyer.

No seller.

No one waiting for me to fix movement of anything.

I had become temporarily unemployed in four separate disciplines.

Excellent.

The theatre runner found me before second bell.

Not the same visitor from before.

Actual theatre runner.

Young man, narrow shoulders, damp hair, carrying three folded notices and breathing like he had decided stairs were political.

"Greg?"

"Unfortunately."

"Rinna says come."

"Why?"

"She said not to explain because you'd ask questions."

I looked at him.

"That's insulting."

"She was right."

Strong runner.

I took crutches.

No bag beyond notebook.

No money pouch beyond bits and two copper because leaving home with nothing had become unnecessary performance.

The theatre smelled like dust, glue, old cloth, and somebody boiling something that should not have been boiled indoors.

Home-adjacent.

Rinna stood near stage with script in one hand.

Nella sat on edge of platform wearing rehearsal trousers and one shoe.

The other shoe was beside her.

I pointed.

"Loss?"

"Choice."

"Concerning."

"It hurts."

"Less concerning."

Rinna held script toward me.

"No."

"I haven't asked."

"You came holding pages."

"That is not consent."

"Read."

There it was.

I took pages.

Not prompt copy.

Scene.

Actual scene.

Five pages.

My role marked **HARROW**.

I knew Harrow.

Not personally.

Character.

Older younger brother.

Which was already annoying phrase.

The play had been in rehearsal while I traveled. I had seen pieces before leaving and bits when I substituted prompt-side after returning.

Nella played Mara Vale.

Not Vale related to Antonius.

Probably.

Different Vale.

Need not investigate.

Harrow was her brother, recently home after six years away, pretending he had come back because their father was sick when he had actually come back because the river company had collapsed and taken his money.

Good.

Normal family disaster.

"Where is Pell?" I asked.

"Sick," Rinna said.

"Renn?"

"Back."

Good.

One uncertainty resolved casually.

Renn was apparently alive and working again.

No ceremony.

"Why me?"

"Because you know scene traffic, you're here, and Nella needs another body with enough words to run the second half."

"Enough words."

"Do not become proud."

Nella said, "Pell does it better."

"Thank you."

"He does."

"I believed first time."

She looked at me.

Good.

Not everyone cushioning ego.

Rinna tapped page.

"This is rehearsal. You are not replacing Pell. Pell is still Harrow unless illness grows ambition. We need Nella's scene. You read Harrow."

"How much acting?"

"Some."

"Terrible unit."

"More than table read. Less than costume."

Useful.

I put crutches at side of stage.

That created immediate problem.

Harrow crossed from window to center table, then to door, then back.

I did not.

Current me had two crutches.

No trial limb.

No theatrical miracle.

Rinna watched me look at blocking marks.

"Adapt."

"Authority?"

"Acting blocking for this rehearsal only. Do not redesign scene."

There.

I moved chair closer to center line.

Kept one crutch against it.

Other stayed under right hand.

Could stand, shift, sit, turn.

Not cross quickly.

Harrow would become less mobile in this run.

Fine.

Nella put second shoe back on.

Apparently pain had negotiated.

We started.

Scene opened with her folding father's shirts.

Harrow entered.

My first line:

"I thought you would have burned those."

I said it.

Correctly.

Nothing happened.

Nella looked at me.

In character maybe.

Then said her line.

"He still wears them."

I said, "He still thinks he does."

Good line.

I liked it.

I said it like I liked it.

Rinna stopped us.

"No."

"What?"

"You're showing us line."

"I spoke line."

"You presented it."

Nella said, "You made face before it."

"I have face."

"Yes."

Rinna pointed at start.

"Again. Harrow has been in house ten minutes. He knows father is worse than letter said. He is trying not to let Mara ask why he came home. Stop acting like audience deserves warning when good sentence arrives."

Cruel.

Accurate.

Again.

"I thought you would have burned those."

"He still wears them."

"He still thinks he does."

Better.

Not much.

Nella folded shirt.

She did not look up.

Her next three lines were longer than mine.

She spoke while folding.

Not cleanly.

Character thought changed halfway through.

"Mrs. Pell says he asks for tea and then forgets why it's there, and yesterday he told me Mother would be angry if I left the window open, which would be interesting if Mother had not been dead nine years, and I shut it anyway because I was tired."

The line landed because Nella did not polish it.

She sounded tired.

Actually tired.

I forgot next line.

Not entirely.

It existed somewhere.

Prompt-side instinct looked for cue copy that was not there.

Renn whispered from side:

"Then why write me?"

Right.

I said, "Then why write me?"

Late.

Nella looked up.

"Because I wanted you home."

That was scene.

Simple.

My next line was supposed to be:

"You wanted help."

I said it too fast.

Rinna stopped.

"No."

I looked at page.

"It's four words."

"Yes."

"Useful."

"Not when you use them to escape."

Nella said, "You sound like Harrow is cleverer than her."

"He thinks he is."

"Not there."

I looked at her.

She pointed at page.

"She just said she wanted him home. He doesn't know what to do with that. Pell lets it sit."

Pell.

Better actor.

Good.

"What do you do?" I asked.

Nella frowned.

"When?"

"As Mara. After saying it."

"Nothing."

"Nothing?"

"Yes."

She demonstrated.

"Because I wanted you home."

Then she just looked at me.

No gesture.

No cleverness.

Uncomfortable.

I understood.

Hated it.

Again.

"Then why write me?"

"Because I wanted you home."

Silence.

Longer.

Not theatre-long.

Human-long.

I felt impulse to fill it.

Joke.

Move.

Look away.

Harrow probably felt same.

Good.

"You wanted help."

This time it came smaller.

Nella's answer:

"I did too."

There.

Better.

Rinna did not stop.

We continued.

Harrow tried to make father practical.

Meals.

Doctor.

Money.

Roof.

Mara refused to let him turn return into maintenance contract.

Good scene.

I liked play more now.

At page three, Harrow crossed to door in original blocking.

I could not make that distance smoothly while holding pages and using crutch.

I tucked pages under arm.

Bad.

Lost place.

Rinna stopped.

"Pages on table."

"Then I can't see line at door."

"Don't go door."

"Blocking says door."

"Rehearsal says current body."

That was simple.

Nella said, "Pell goes door because he wants to leave."

"I also want leave."

"Then make me believe it from chair."

Hostile craft.

Good.

I sat.

Harrow's next line was:

"I can send money."

Mara:

"From where?"

Then Harrow lied.

"North office kept me."

Important.

I said lie too smoothly.

Nella laughed.

Out of character.

"That is Greg lie."

"What?"

"You say bad thing like you've already prepared defense."

"I have experience."

"Exactly."

Rinna said, "Harrow hasn't practiced this lie enough."

Interesting.

I tried again.

"I can send money."

"From where?"

"North office kept me."

Too hesitant.

Rinna shook head.

"Now he's twelve."

"Useful spectrum."

"Find middle."

I did.

Three more times.

Not exciting.

Third was worse than second.

Fourth finally had tiny break between North and office.

Like Harrow selected lie while speaking.

Nella's face changed.

She heard it.

"Kept you doing what?"

I answered.

"Accounts."

"Whose?"

"River."

"Which river?"

There.

Character trapped.

I smiled.

Rinna stopped.

"Why are you smiling?"

"Good trap."

"Greg is enjoying writing. Harrow is losing."

Right.

Again.

No smile.

Harder than magic.

Magic at least did not care about expression.

Scene pushed Harrow toward admitting company failed.

He still did not admit money.

Mara knew anyway.

Nella had one line:

"You came home poor and decided shame would be less visible if you carried groceries."

Excellent.

I laughed.

Everyone stopped.

Nella stared.

"That is my line."

"I know."

"Then stop appreciating it."

"Good line."

Rinna said, "Audience can appreciate it. Harrow cannot."

"I disagree."

"Harrow is being disemboweled."

"Metaphorically."

"Act like it."

Again.

This time line hit.

"You came home poor and decided shame would be less visible if you carried groceries."

I felt stupidly seen.

Not because exact.

Because mechanism.

Come back useful enough and maybe nobody asks what failed.

That thought did not belong only Harrow.

Dangerous.

I did not use it as revelation.

I let it make eye contact harder.

Nella saw change.

She kept going.

Good actor.

"Did you think Father would care?"

"I didn't come for him."

First true line.

Nella stopped folding.

"Mara knows that?" I asked.

Rinna said nothing.

Nella said, "She does now."

We ran rest.

Scene ended without reconciliation.

Mara handed Harrow one of father's shirts.

He took it.

Not hug.

Not forgiveness.

Just object.

Harrow said:

"This won't fit me."

Mara:

"I know."

Blackout.

Good.

I sat holding rehearsal shirt.

Rinna said, "Again from 'You came home poor.'"

No praise.

Excellent.

We did it again.

Then again.

By fourth run, my shoulder started complaining from repeated stand-sit with crutch.

Not pain.

Work.

I shifted chair half foot.

Rinna noticed.

"Better?"

"Yes."

Nella said, "You can stay seated after lie."

"Does that change scene?"

"It changes your height."

"Deep."

"It makes Mara stand over you."

I looked at Rinna.

She considered.

"Try."

Specialist authority.

Not mine.

We tried.

Better.

I hated this profession.

Pell's original movement made him walk away when exposed.

My current body made escape cumbersome.

Sitting made Harrow trapped.

Not universally better.

Different.

Rinna said, "Keep for today."

Today.

Good boundary.

At break Renn brought water.

Alive, apparently.

"Recovered?"

"Mostly."

"Your illness inconvenienced me."

"I heard you survived prompt."

"Barely."

He sat nearby.

"Bell seven?"

I stared.

News traveled.

"Nella betrayed me."

Nella from across room said, "You missed it because you watched me."

Renn nodded.

"Understandable."

Traitor.

I drank.

No one asked about money.

No one cared I had paid Vale twelve yesterday.

Theatre possessed its own scale.

A missed bell mattered here.

A line landing mattered.

A shoe hurting mattered.

Pell being sick mattered because scene needed Pell.

Good.

After break, Rinna took pages away.

I objected.

"You know section."

"I know approximate section."

"Good."

"Not same."

"Act."

Cruel.

Without pages, I lost exact word in second exchange.

Nella corrected in character.

I recovered badly.

Rinna did not stop.

Interesting.

Scene survived.

Next run I overcorrected and anticipated cue.

Nella held silence longer just to punish me.

I entered early anyway.

She raised eyebrow.

Character could have done that.

Maybe Nella.

Both.

After run she said, "You keep trying to help me get to my line."

"I support."

"Don't."

"Hostile to teamwork."

"My pause belongs to me."

There.

Good.

That sentence lodged.

My pause belongs to me.

Support instinct could become interference if I optimized other person's timing for them.

Theatre version of management mistake.

Annoying repetition across life.

I did not write it down.

Growth.

Next run I let her pause.

It felt too long.

Then it didn't.

By fifth bell Pell arrived.

Sick enough to look offended by air.

Rinna said, "Go home."

Pell said, "I came to watch."

"Home."

"I can sit."

"You can sit at home."

Nella waved.

"Greg is ruining your part."

Pell looked at me.

"How badly?"

"Professionally."

He smiled weakly.

Then coughed.

Rinna physically pointed toward door.

Pell left.

Good bossing inside her authority.

We did one last run.

This time no stops.

Not because perfect.

Because Rinna wanted whole shape.

I missed one word.

Nella stepped on one line.

A chair leg caught hem.

Renn dropped something backstage.

Scene still worked.

At "You came home poor," I did not think about my own money.

At least not first.

I thought about Harrow.

That was probably acting.

At final shirt exchange, Nella handed it to me.

"This won't fit me."

"I know."

I held shirt.

No blackout because rehearsal.

Silence.

Rinna closed script.

"Fine."

High praise.

"How fine?"

"Enough."

Nella sat and removed painful shoe immediately.

I pointed.

"Victory."

"Blister."

"Less poetic."

"Better."

Rinna gave me one copper.

I looked at it.

"No."

She frowned.

"What?"

"I wasn't on paid call."

"You worked half day."

"You asked read."

"That is work."

There.

Old mistake.

Thinking theatre participation became gift if informal.

No.

Labor.

"Fine."

I took copper.

Cash twenty-one.

Vale eleven.

No debt payment.

Good.

"Does this make me replacement Harrow?"

"No," Rinna said.

"Excellent."

"Pell is better."

"Everyone committed to clarity."

Nella said, "You got less bad."

I bowed from chair.

"Finally."

She threw folded stocking at me.

I missed.

Acting did not improve reflex.

Outside the theatre, evening traffic had thickened around the market turn. I waited for a handcart to clear instead of trying to angle both crutches through the narrowing gap. The driver apologized after he had already blocked everything.

"No injury."

"Still sorry."

"Accepted."

He moved.

I moved.

Nobody wrote it down.

That felt almost luxurious after South Road.

My hands were tired from the rehearsal more than I expected. Not from carrying. From standing, sitting, pages, crutch, shirt, chair, repeated resets. Acting apparently had logistics too.

Of course it did.

On way home I spent one bit on hot bread.

Not because income.

Because bread was hot.

Important distinction.

Lyssa got home later.

She found me trying Harrow's line while fixing my shirt seam with the bad thread she had rejected.

"Why are you talking to yourself?"

"Acting."

"Same question."

I told her I had read Harrow.

She knew play enough.

"The broke brother?"

"Yes."

She looked at me.

"No."

"What?"

"Do not make face."

"I didn't."

"You did."

"Everyone is director."

"Did you do good?"

"Less bad."

"That sounds believable."

I showed one copper.

"Paid rehearsal."

"Good."

No question about Vale.

No accounting.

She picked up my repaired seam.

Pulled once.

Thread held.

She looked annoyed.

"Still bad thread."

"Working bad thread."

"Not same as good."

Hessa had colonized sewing too.

We ate hot bread.

I told her one useful thing from rehearsal.

"Nella said her pause belongs to her."

Lyssa nodded immediately.

"Yes."

"Why everyone knows things before me?"

"Because you talk during pauses."

Cruel.

Accurate.

I considered response.

Did not make one.

Lyssa smiled.

There.

Twenty-one copper.

Eleven Vale.

Bits unspecified.

Magic unchanged.

Work limb unchanged.

No deal.

No organizer contract.

No artifact.

Renn was back.

Pell was sick but still Harrow.

Nella was better actor than me.

Rinna had paid me for work I almost treated like favor.

And for half a day I had not been intermediary, coordinator, debtor, verifier, patient, or experiment.

I had been actor.

Not excellent.

Not secretly brilliant.

Not transformed.

Just good enough to make scene better once I stopped trying to stand outside it and manage what everyone else was doing.

That was plenty.

Tomorrow could become complicated again.

Tonight there was hot bread and bad thread and Lyssa deliberately leaving a pause open after I had just learned not to fill one.

I lasted maybe three seconds.

"What?"

She laughed.

Failure remained available.
