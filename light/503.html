TEXT READER · CHAPTER 392

THE AMATEUR

Text-only reading · no chapter illustrations

Three weeks after soup, the leg was still not finished.

Good.

Masta had changed two things.

Venn had changed one thing and claimed Masta's second thing was actually his first thing viewed from wrong side.

I had refused to adjudicate.

Professional growth.

The socket lasted longer before upper outside warmed.

The foot behaved better on Carrow cross-slope.

Long streets still belonged mostly to crutches.

Short indoor routes and some flat outside work belonged to the leg.

Stairs depended.

Rain depended.

Fatigue depended.

Everything depended.

Masta had infected my life.

The leg had stopped being new before becoming final.

That helped.

It lived beside door.

Crutches lived there too.

No one had held ceremony.

Excellent.

Mail arrived at breakfast.

Three pieces.

Too many.

I was eating eggs.

Lyssa was already working at table.

Tavi had left for school after accusing me of making toast too dark.

Incorrect.

Toast had ranges.

The district runner gave me first envelope.

Lakeward mark.

There.

I signed receipt.

No dry mouth this time.

Some.

Less.

Lyssa glanced over.

Not stopped working.

Also progress.

I opened it.

**LAKEWARD DISTRICT HERITAGE RELEASE OFFICE**

Same office.

Different paper.

My factual statement had been received and entered.

No personal appearance was presently required.

No penalty determination was enclosed.

The marked Three Rays lot remained under district review as part of property-release reconciliation.

If further statement or appearance was required, notice would follow through registered relay.

That was all.

Not cleared.

Not charged.

Not solved.

Administrative limbo with better wording.

I read it twice.

Then put it beside plate.

Lyssa said, "Anything you have to do?"

"No."

"Today?"

"No."

She nodded and kept working.

Correct.

Second envelope was from Merrin.

Duskport.

My brain changed speed.

Not because danger.

Because possibility.

I opened.

One-page urgent notice.

A retired coastal survey instrument had entered a broker sale from a northern estate.

Early Veyr-adjacent housing.

Not confirmed Veyr response ring.

Outer mark damaged.

Certification void.

Needle assembly present.

Glass cracked.

Seller allowed inspection before close.

Four days.

Reserve not listed.

Merrin had underlined one line:

**MAY MATCH CATEGORY 1 / AUTHENTICATION UNCERTAIN.**

There.

Not perfect.

Better.

A thing worth looking at.

Maybe.

Duskport passenger timetable lived in my head now.

Leave tomorrow morning.

Arrive afternoon.

Inspect next day.

Plenty before close.

Could leave today and get extra day.

Could send Merrin one bit to inspect seller paperwork first.

Could ask for photographs if estate had someone who could draw badly.

No.

Photographs did not exist.

Wrong life.

Excellent catch.

I reached for notebook.

Stopped.

Not because I had become saint.

Because third envelope remained.

Carrow paper.

No seal.

Nessa's handwriting.

That was dangerous in different direction.

I opened.

**Need your ugly shoulder harness tomorrow if you still have it. Jori says the new counterweight rig can clear the side door if we hang from wrong point. I say he is lying. Bring harness after second bell if free. If not, don't.**

No greeting.

Perfect.

I still had ugly shoulder harness.

Not in this room.

Theatre property.

Maybe she meant mine from road rig.

I knew what she meant.

Mostly.

Could go tomorrow.

Could also leave Duskport tomorrow.

There.

Conflict.

Tiny.

Real.

No destiny.

I set both letters down.

Lyssa threaded needle.

"You got face."

No.

Absolutely not.

"You people need new observations."

"Which one?"

"Merrin has a maybe-instrument in Duskport. Four days."

"And?"

"Nessa wants help tomorrow."

"And?"

"I can do both if I leave after theatre."

Lyssa bit thread.

"Then why face?"

There.

I looked at letters.

Because doing both was possible.

Because once something was possible my brain treated not doing it as loss.

Because if I left after theatre, I could still inspect.

If I skipped theatre, I got more time.

If I skipped Duskport, another buyer might get instrument.

If I sent Merrin first, I could reduce uncertainty.

If I stayed home, nothing collapsed.

Annoying range of choices.

"I haven't decided," I said.

Lyssa nodded.

No analysis.

No advice.

Just answer accepted.

Then she held up my bag.

Not travel bag.

Small everyday shoulder bag.

One strap had torn halfway through at seam.

"When did this happen?"

I looked.

"Yesterday?"

"You've been carrying it like this?"

"It still carried."

"That is not answer."

"It is function report."

"Do you want me to fix it?"

Immediate yes.

She could do it in three minutes.

Maybe two.

Her stitches would hold.

Mine would look like legal dispute.

I looked at dark garment under her hands.

Paid work.

Then bag.

"No."

Lyssa looked up.

I regretted everything.

"Show me."

Her expression changed.

Not emotional.

Suspicious.

"Show you what?"

"How to fix it."

"You know how to tie things."

"That is not sewing."

"You have watched sewing for years."

"Watching competent person do thing is famous substitute for skill."

She smiled.

"You're serious."

"Unfortunately."

She pushed small scrap toward me.

"Not on bag first."

Reasonable.

I sat.

Trial leg on.

Both feet under table.

Crutches against wall.

Needle looked smaller than most weapons I had respected.

Thread looked malicious.

Lyssa showed me once.

Not slow enough.

"Again."

She showed me again.

Needle through.

Loop.

Pull.

Spacing.

Nothing complicated.

Which meant failure would be personal.

Excellent.

I tried scrap.

First stitch leaned.

Second too far.

Third pulled fabric into little hill.

Lyssa watched.

"Why are you strangling it?"

"I want it secure."

"Secure is not same as angry."

That sounded fake-deep enough that I objected.

"Do not turn this into philosophy."

"I am telling you stop pulling thread so hard."

"Better."

She took scrap.

Flattened it.

Showed tension.

Returned needle.

I tried again.

Less bad.

Still bad.

Good.

No hidden mastery.

No old-life sewing memory appearing from void.

No system message.

Hands learned because hands did thing.

Tavi came back early because school released after roof leak in side room.

Apparently buildings also had bodies.

He entered.

Saw me sewing.

Stopped.

"Why?"

Excellent child.

"Bag broke."

"Lyssa can do it."

"Yes."

He stared.

"Then why?"

I pointed needle at him.

"Go wash hands."

He left laughing.

Hostile household.

I practiced until stitches looked merely cheap.

Then moved to bag.

Lyssa said, "Pin first."

"I don't need."

She looked at me.

I pinned first.

Growth had become unbearable.

The Merrin notice stayed beside my elbow.

Four days.

Nessa's note stayed under it.

Tomorrow.

Lakeward sat nearest eggs and no longer required anything.

Three different futures on one table.

No prophecy.

Mostly postage.

I stitched torn seam.

Slow.

Bad angle because bag material thicker.

Needle stuck.

I pushed harder.

Lyssa said, "Don't."

I stopped.

"Why?"

"You'll bend it."

"I have bent stronger metal."

"That isn't argument."

Fair.

I backed needle out.

Changed angle.

Through.

Pull.

Not too hard.

Next.

Tavi returned with wet hands.

"Can I have food?"

"You know where food is."

"Can you carry it?"

"No."

He grinned.

Soup certification had damaged authority permanently.

I looked at Merrin notice again.

Could leave today.

Still.

It was barely after breakfast.

Coach at third bell.

Duskport room no longer prepaid.

Pell would charge.

Merrin could meet.

I could inspect.

This was exactly kind of opportunity I had built money and contacts to catch.

Important.

I wanted it.

Also important.

I folded notice once.

Put it in notebook.

Not buried.

Not discarded.

Tomorrow still worked.

Maybe.

If another buyer moved early, then not.

That was allowed.

This thought hurt slightly.

Useful.

I told Lyssa, "I'm not leaving today."

She did not look up.

"Okay."

That was it.

No reward.

No proof.

No one rang bell because man delayed train one day.

I picked up Nessa's note.

Tomorrow after second bell.

Then maybe Duskport.

Maybe not.

Depends.

Damn Masta.

At noon I wore work leg to market.

Crutches came too.

One stayed folded under arm for first block.

Both came down when rain started and stones slicked.

No shame.

No test.

Just weather.

I bought bread.

Tavi complained it was wrong loaf.

He ate it.

In afternoon I went by theatre to tell Nessa I could come tomorrow.

She was not there.

Jori was.

He said, "What?"

"Nessa note."

"About harness?"

"Yes."

"She's right."

"About what?"

"I'm lying."

There.

Useful preparation.

I went home.

Lyssa had finished paid garment.

My bag still waited.

I sat.

Put trial leg aside because socket had warmed after market.

Crutches against table.

Needle.

Thread.

Bag.

Merrin notice inside notebook.

Lakeward letter filed.

Tomorrow had enough work without becoming today.

I started seam again.

First stitch through clean.

Not straight.

Acceptable.

I pulled.

Too loose.

Lyssa glanced over.

Said nothing.

I tightened slightly.

Better.

Then I tried second stitch.

The second stitch was worse.
