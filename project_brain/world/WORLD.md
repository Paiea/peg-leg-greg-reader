# PEG-LEG GREG — WORLD

Reusable setting/system facts only. Prefer manuscript evidence over completionist lore.

## Carrow

Carrow is Greg's current city and the center of much of his ordinary second-life work, relationships, commerce, magic investigation, and theatre connection.

Treat it as a working city with overlapping economic and social systems, not a fantasy-theme backdrop. Businesses, suppliers, roads, rooms, schedules, debt, carts, labor, weather, and reputation matter.

## Theatre ecology

The theatre/company operates through ordinary constraints:

- rehearsal and performance schedules
- paid work
- venues of uneven quality
- costumes and props
- oil / lighting supplies
- carts and transport
- local halls and businesses
- ticket capacity and deposits
- backstage movement and stage geometry
- company hierarchy and personalities

A venue may have leaks, bad storage, compromised entrances, limited wings, audience-space constraints, or improvised solutions. Practical stagecraft should remain physically grounded.

## Commerce / money

- Currency is copper / silver / gold.
- No conversion rate is established.
- Cash, supplier trust, deposits, debt, and information about money movement can matter independently of combat power.
- Businesses can become information nodes because they notice customers, deliveries, warnings, timing, and unusual questions.

## Social information

Recent chapters establish a useful world principle: information can travel through ordinary commercial relationships before institutions formally act.

This does not establish a universal intelligence network. It establishes that neighbors and suppliers can notice pressure and communicate when given a reason and a route.

## Travel / roads

Travel is materially constrained by weather, road quality, wagons, accessibility, lodging, and the physical work of moving people and theatre equipment. Greg's crutch use and weight transfer should remain real constraints in these environments.

## Institutions / hidden structure

Do not invent a complete hierarchy behind Bren or the current pressure arc. No boss, organization, territory, or Vale connection is established.

Prefer discovering institutions through consequences, paperwork, money, labor, rules, witnesses, suppliers, schedules, and repeated behavior rather than exposition dumps.

## Worldbuilding rule

Build the world when the story creates pressure for an answer. Preserve ordinary systems because they are often more useful than adding another magical institution.
