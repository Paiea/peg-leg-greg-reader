# R2 Audio Score

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL AUDIO-NATIVE PERFORMANCE LAYER**

The Audio Score is a derived performance transcript for Listen. It is not the clean written rendition and must not replace `r2/assets/written/`.

## Authority

Story State / Performance and Greg Experience remain factual authority. Clean written chapters remain the readable rendition. The Audio Score may substantially reshape wording, pacing, repetition, punctuation, lineation, and thought order inside Greg's moment-to-moment experience so the spoken result carries processing time and prosodic shape.

The Audio Score must preserve:

- what happened
- who acted
- who knew what
- dialogue ownership and binding language
- prices, terms, deadlines, quantities, locations, and causal relationships
- Greg's actual uncertainty rather than upgrading memory into fact

It may add no new story facts merely to create liveliness.

## Core goal

> **Compose the voice input, not page prose.**

The score is allowed to look awkward, repetitive, unfinished, or badly edited when those features improve the spoken experience. Readability is secondary. Audible cognition is primary.

## Processing time

Do not jump directly from evidence to a clean conclusion when Greg would plausibly process his way there.

Useful processing may include:

- repeated words with changed meaning
- false starts
- abandoned explanations
- questions Greg immediately revises
- unnecessary-but-characterful classification
- sensory or memory detours
- returning to an earlier thought without a transition
- saying the obvious because the obvious has only just become obvious

Extra words must buy cognition, orientation, anticipation, humor, emotional absorption, or rhythmic space.

## Cadence system

Do not build one universal fragment cadence. Chapters and scenes should move through different cadence zones.

### Upbeat / continuation

Use when a thought is still moving or the voice should resist a full landing:

- commas and dependent clauses
- repeated setup words
- questions
- an unfinished phrase followed by `...`
- a long sentence that accumulates thought before interruption

Example:

`and maybe, maybe it was coming?`

### Downbeat / landing

Use when a thought should resolve or strike cleanly:

- short declaratives
- important content word placed phrase-finally
- a hard correction such as `No.` when earned
- the final item of a list

Example:

`No. Knee.`

### Suspension

Use `...` selectively when the thought is genuinely hanging, searching, or refusing to resolve. Do not decorate every paragraph with ellipses.

### Reset

A blank line plus a new grammatical shape can signal a real cognitive reset. A reset may also come from abrupt subject change rather than an announced transition.

## Thought webbing

Greg may leave a thought because another perception, memory, or association catches him.

Do not always explain the transition. He can move:

`body pain -> ceiling stain -> memory -> wait, knee -> body pain`

and return later.

Thought webbing should feel motivated by attention, not random surrealism.

## Prosodic wording

Prefer normal language controls before exotic notation:

- questions for rising / unresolved contours
- statements for falling / resolved contours
- lists whose earlier items continue and final item lands
- contractions and ordinary spoken syntax
- phrase-final placement for the word that should carry weight

Nonstandard spelling, clipped words, vocalizations, or phonetic respelling are allowed only as auditioned exceptions. They are never the default and must not alter lexical identity or story meaning.

## Action scenes

Do not bury physical causality under internal chatter.

During fast action, processing space should expose decisions, wrong models, corrected load direction, attention shifts, and motor interrupts. It should not slow every strike into commentary.

External commands and other characters' expertise keep their ownership.

## Dialogue scenes

Preserve exact dialogue when its wording creates relationship state, prices, promises, contracts, threats, refusals, or domain authority.

Greg's surrounding thought may expand, loop, or interrupt itself, but it may not steal another character's reasoning.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- `Wait. No. Anyway.` repeated as a house tic
- making every sentence short
- making every paragraph a question-answer pair
- polished punchlines after every realization
- fake slang or rapper voice
- phonetic misspelling as decoration
- adding filler that buys no processing time
- making all chapters share the same rhythm

## Calibration target

The current preferred voice behavior came from a Chapter 1 probe shaped roughly like:

`I woke because my back didn't hurt, and I didn't move because maybe... maybe it was coming?`

`No.`

`My back should hurt.`

`Lower back.`

`Then shoulder?`

`No. Knee.`

The important lesson is not those exact phrases. It is the movement between continuation, rise, correction, fall, repetition, and suspension.

## File policy

Audio Score chapters live in `r2/assets/audio-score/` and include the clean source path and blob SHA in their headers. They are derived artifacts. Clean written files remain untouched.
