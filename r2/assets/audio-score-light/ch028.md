# Chapter 28: Eventually

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Jorren came back while I was buying onions.

This was rude for several reasons.

The first was that I had spent most of the morning not thinking about roads and was doing reasonably well.

The second was that I had one hand full of produce when someone behind me said, "Those look terrible."

I knew the voice before I turned.

My body knew it too.

Everything in my chest released at once.

Too much.

Far too much.

For one ugly second I was fifty-nine again, hearing boots outside a door after a contract that had run two days late.

Roads taught you that `eventually` was not a promise.

People came back with scars.

People came back missing gear.

People came back furious.

Sometimes somebody else came back carrying their things.

None of that belonged to Jorren yet.

That was the problem.

The feeling arrived before current evidence could stop it.

I turned.

Jorren stood beside a cabbage cart with a pack over one shoulder and road dust built into everything else.

Alive.

Annoyingly alive.

Same broad chest.

Same grin.

Different boots.

The new pair from before he left had been resoled once already, darker leather stitched around the heel. His sword belt rode higher. The loose ends of his pack straps had been cut short and tied back instead of slapping against his legs like they used to.

A narrow pale line crossed the outside of his left hand.

Not new enough to be interesting to anyone except me.

I stared at it anyway.

Jorren looked at the onions.

Then at me.

"You going to say something?"

There were several available answers.

`You're back` felt too obvious.

`I was worried` felt worse.

I chose the oldest defense available to men with functioning mouths.

"Eventually took you long enough."

His grin widened.

"You remembered."

"I remember insults better than people."

"Healthy."

He shifted the pack higher.

The motion made him wince.

Small.

Right shoulder.

I noticed.

Did not ask yet.

Growth through cowardice.

The onion seller cleared her throat.

Right.

Commerce.

I paid one copper for more onions than one man should own and received them in a net bag that immediately tried to escape through my fingers.

Jorren watched.

"You cook now?"

"No."

"Then why onions?"

"Noll used the last ones."

"Noll."

There it was.

A current name Jorren did not know.

I liked that.

Then disliked that I liked it.

"Neighbor."

Jorren nodded as if this explained anything.

"You have a neighbor?"

"Most rooms do."

"One you buy onions for?"

"It became complicated."

"You were gone two weeks."

"Carrow is efficient."

We started toward Lower Rook.

I wanted to ask him everything at once.

North road.

Caravan.

Who the scar belonged to.

Why the shoulder hurt.

Whether Meral's warning about the route mattered farther north.

Whether he had slept badly.

Whether he had met anyone.

Whether he had missed Carrow.

Whether he had missed me.

The last question was obviously defective and could remain in storage.

Jorren solved the problem by talking first.

"Caravan went farther than they said."

"Of course."

"Company split at Doss Ferry. I stayed north with the grain wagons."

"More pay?"

"Eight days."

"Good."

"Then six with another escort east."

I looked at him.

"You changed companies?"

"They paid."

Strong philosophy.

"Then bridge crew for three days."

"Guarding?"

"Mostly watching them argue about stone."

"Dangerous."

"Someone stole breakfast."

I looked at the scar again.

"That?"

"No."

"Breakfast thief?"

"Dog."

I stopped walking.

Jorren kept going three steps before turning.

"Large dog."

"Did you fight it?"

"Cook did."

"Did he win?"

"No."

I started walking again.

The world had remained properly organized while I was gone.

The scar came from a crate nail.

The shoulder came from trying to catch a sack that had already decided to fall.

"Why?"

"It was falling."

"Yes. That is usually when people decide not to stand under them."

"It had flour."

"Critical cargo."

"They paid me."

This remained his strongest argument.

Lower Rook looked unimpressed by his return.

Laundry over the courtyard.

One repaired section around the cookroom flue still lighter than the rest of the wall.

A child sitting on the pump base eating something with both hands.

Somebody had moved the fire buckets from the stair landings to hooks on the wall.

Nobody had voted.

The building had acquired policy by repetition.

Jorren stopped beneath the rear stairs.

"This where you live?"

I heard the question I wanted instead.

`This is what you built while I was gone?`

That was not what he had said.

"Third floor."

"Nice."

"You haven't seen it."

"Building's still standing."

"Recently tested."

Noll leaned out of the cookroom doorway at exactly the wrong moment.

"Greg!"

Jorren looked at me.

I closed my eyes.

Noll held a wooden spoon in one hand.

"You get onions?"

I lifted the bag.

"Eventually."

"Those look terrible."

Jorren started laughing.

I looked from one man to the other.

Something unpleasant had happened very quickly.

Noll came closer.

He wore an apron that had once been white and now contained several arguments about broth.

His eyes went to Jorren's pack.

Then sword.

Then face.

"Road?"

"Road," Jorren said.

Good. Apparently there was a dialect.

I lifted one hand.

"Jorren. Noll. He lives next door."

Noll looked at me.

Not offended.

Worse.

Interested.

"Next door."

I heard the problem after the words had already left.

Jorren did not.

He held out his hand.

"Jorren."

Noll shook it.

"Noll. I saved his building."

"We saved the building."

Noll ignored me.

Jorren looked at the repaired flue.

"From fire?"

"Mostly from Greg. Fire was secondary."

"I cut one laundry line."

"Three shirts."

"Some were burning."

"Not mine."

Jorren looked at me.

"You became worse at stories while I was gone."

"Apparently everyone has been discussing this."

Noll took the onions out of my hand.

Just took them.

"You eating?"

Jorren looked at him.

"Is that for me?"

"Depends. You got money?"

"Some."

"Then yes."

They started toward the cookroom.

I remained in the courtyard for half a second holding nothing.

This was my reunion.

Apparently I had lost procedural control.

I followed.

Noll's meal was onions, potatoes, and something he called sausage because truth had limits.

Jorren ate two bowls.

He paid one copper without arguing.

I paid one too because the onions had not magically converted into ownership.

Noll sat after the rush with us and asked better questions than I had.

Not where Jorren went.

Which road had the worst mud.

Whether the north inns watered beer.

If the dog kept the breakfast.

It had.

Jorren asked about the fire.

Noll told the story badly.

This made me like him more and irritated me because I had wanted to tell it.

He gave the support plane approximately half a sentence.

The laundry received five.

"Those shirts mattered," he said.

"One was already on fire."

"Still a shirt."

Jorren nodded.

"Property rights."

"Exactly."

I ate potato and considered leaving both of them to each other.

After dinner Jorren carried his pack upstairs.

My room was exactly as badly arranged as it had been yesterday.

This felt suddenly personal.

Field pack beside the door.

Sword rack improvised from two wall hooks and optimism.

Wet-weather cloak folded badly on the chair.

One chair.

Right.

I had not acquired later infrastructure merely because someone visited.

Jorren put his pack down.

Looked once around the room.

His eyes stopped on the good sword.

"New."

There.

Finally.

"Yes."

He picked it up without drawing it.

Good manners survived roads.

"Expensive?"

"Unfortunately."

"Worth it?"

I thought about the hooktail.

The West Cut.

Five days of rain without drawing it once.

"Yes."

Jorren set it back.

His eyes moved to the field pack.

Fresh socks visible at the top.

Then Mara's Halden envelope.

Then Tavin's letter beside it.

He did not ask about either.

I almost wished he would.

Instead he sat on the floor with his back against the bed.

"Guild beds are full."

"Of course they are."

"Road crew in from east."

"Inn?"

"Two silver."

I laughed.

He did not.

Right.

"One night," I said.

Jorren looked up.

"Floor?"

"You thought I had a guest room?"

"I thought you became important."

There it was.

The stupid little part of me that had wanted the sentence arrived just in time to discover he was joking.

"I became solvent. Different profession."

A knock hit the doorframe.

Noll stood there holding a folded blanket.

Not wet.

Mostly not smoke-colored.

"For road."

He handed it to Jorren.

Not me.

Jorren took it.

"Thanks."

"He'll steal it," I said.

Noll looked at me.

"You still have my soap."

"You have my soap."

"Shared custody."

Then he left.

Jorren unfolded the blanket.

"Good neighbor."

I looked at the doorway after Noll disappeared.

"Apparently."

The word felt too small now.

Annoying.

Jorren lay down on the floor with his coat under his head and Noll's blanket over his legs.

He was asleep in less than ten minutes.

Road skill.

I stayed awake longer.

Not because he snored.

He did not.

That felt suspicious.

Through the wall, Noll dropped something heavy and swore.

On the floor beside my bed, Jorren breathed slowly enough that I could hear the difference between sleep and silence.

Eventually, apparently, had a sound...
