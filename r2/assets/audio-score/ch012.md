Mara was sitting on the floor outside my room.

And that was how I learned I'd been avoiding Mara.

Which...

No.

Avoiding sounds deliberate.

Conscious avoidance has a plan in it. You decide not to go somewhere. You decide not to answer something. You know what you're doing and then do it badly on purpose.

This was worse.

I reached the top of the stairs and stopped.

She looked up.

Dark hair tied badly behind her head. Gray work dress. Ink on two fingers. One boot unlaced.

The boot bothered me for some reason.

No.

Not the boot.

The waiting.

She'd been there long enough to stop caring about the boot.

I knew her face.

Immediately.

Thank God.

No.

Relief first.

Then shame because relief was not the correct emotion when somebody had been sitting on a hallway floor waiting for you to remember they existed.

"There you are."

Her voice hit somewhere old.

Not old old.

Not forty years.

Earlier.

Heat.
River steps.
Somebody throwing plum pits from a roof.

Gone.

I reached for it and it was already gone.

"Mara."

She stood.

The fact that I knew her name annoyed her.

"Congratulations."

Fair.

No.

Not doing that.

I unlocked the door.

She didn't move.

"You got my letter?"

There it was.

Letter.

Drawer beside the bed.

I had recognized the handwriting on my first morning.

Recognized handwriting.
Didn't remember the conversation in the first line.
Read the date.
Read the line.
Stopped.

I'll come back when the world makes more sense.

That had been the plan.

The world had declined.

"Yes."

"And?"

And...

Nothing.

I looked at her.

She looked at me.

No door appeared inside the door I'd just opened.

"I didn't answer."

"I noticed."

"I should have."

"Also noticed."

Arms crossed.

I had negotiated with Antonius Vale while owing him money.

This was worse.

Why was this worse?

Because Vale wanted money.

Mara wanted an explanation and I did not own one.

"Do you want to come in?"

"Do you?"

That should have been easy.

Yes.

Obviously yes.

My brain still took too long.

"Yes."

She came in.

Looked around once.

The room was different now.

New basin.
Better rag.
Training clothes folded instead of chair-shaped.
Arlo numbers under a cup.
Meral's marked cord by the window.
Cheap sword against wall.

Six weeks of me, apparently.

Mara picked up the cord.

"What's this?"

Magic practice.

She looked at me.

"You hate magic practice."

...

Do I?

Did I?

She saw it happen.

Her face changed.

"You do."

Did.

"Six weeks ago."

Six weeks.

Right.

Not a lifetime.

She put the cord down.

"You missed Tavin's send-off."

Tavin.

Nothing.

My stomach tightened before my face figured out what to do.

Mara saw that too.

"You missed it."

"I don't remember Tavin."

Silence.

Wrong answer.

No.

Only honest one.

"What?"

I know the name should mean something.

"He lived across from us for twelve years."

Us.

There was a whole building inside that word and I didn't have the key.

"He taught you cards."

Cards.

Oh.

White Dog.
Dockworker protecting winnings.
Man selling tilt.
Forty years of instincts.

The beginning was gone.

The person who put the first cards in my hands was gone from my memory and apparently alive enough to have a send-off I missed.

I sat down.

Mara didn't.

"Where did he go?"

River company. South branch.

Alive.

Good.

Too good.

Relief showed.

Mara's anger sharpened.

"Why are you looking relieved?"

Because I thought you were about to tell me he died.

Because absence means dead too often in my head.

Because somebody I don't remember being alive feels like getting him back.

Can't say any of that without sounding insane.

"I thought something worse happened."

"Worse than you forgetting him?"

No answer.

Mara sat.

Not because the conversation got better.

Anger has knees too, apparently.

"Did you get hit in the head?"

Maybe.

Not recently.

No.

Fever?

No.

Spell accident?

Not that I know of.

"Then what is this?"

This.

She meant me.

I wanted a model.

Memory damage.
Temporal displacement.
Identity discontinuity.
Forty years inside nineteen.

All technically closer than anything I could say.

None belonged in this room.

I could lie.

Head injury.
Trauma.
Bad dreams.
Something ordinary enough to fit.

Problem.

She knew me.

Not me now.

The boy I was supposed to be.

A lie would have to survive somebody who knew the original better than I did.

"Something happened to my memory."

Her anger moved.

Not gone.

Moved.

"What kind of something?"

I don't know how to explain it.

"Try."

Hands.

Young hands.

Still strange when I remember to notice them.

"I remember things that happened a long time ago better than things that happened recently."

True.

Wrong direction.

"How long ago?"

Almost laughed.

Didn't.

"Long."

She hated that.

"Greg."

There were versions of my name.

Sella: administratively irritating.
Jorren: about to do something funny or stupid.
Arlo: warning label.

Mara...

Mara said it like I had left something unfinished.

I remembered that.

Not scene.

Pattern.

"I remember you."

Nothing.

"Not enough."

Still nothing.

Then the sentence came from somewhere I hadn't prepared.

"I remember you. Not in the order you deserve."

She looked away.

That was worse than anger.

I let it stay quiet.

For once.

After a while she put a brass key on the table.

Small.

I knew it.

No idea what it opened.

She watched me not know.

"Your old room."

I looked around.

This is my old room.

"Before this one."

Ah.

Almost a smile.

"My mother's back room. You stayed there after your parents died."

And the room changed without moving.

Parents died.
Mara's mother.
Back room.

Rain against shutters.
Bread cut too thick.
A woman with flour on one wrist telling someone not to bleed on the good cloth.

No face.

Mara. Fifteen? Sixteen? Sitting on a windowsill, knees up.

Gone.

I closed my hand around the key.

"Your mother let me stay there."

Eight months.

Eight months.

I remembered princes better.

That was obscene.

Mara softened.

I wished she hadn't.

Anger was easier.

Kindness put weight on the missing parts.

"She's alive," Mara said.

I looked up too fast.

Damn it.

She saw.

"Why wouldn't she be?"

No present reason.

None.

"I don't know."

"You're frightening me."

There.

No joke.

No model.

I did that.

"I'm sorry."

"Stop saying things like someone twice your age."

That almost got me.

Look down.
Don't smile during apology.

Mara exhaled.

"I leave in four days."

Room tight again.

Where?

Halden.

Halden.

River city west of low ranges. Textile warehouses. Later glasswork. No catastrophe jumped forward.

Good.

No.

Absence of remembered catastrophe is not safety.

Still... good enough for one breath.

"Ledger position with Vey Shipping."

She studied me.

"This is the part where you say I hate boats."

Wait.

"I hate boats."

"Do you?"

"Very much."

Good to know.

She laughed once.

Tiny.

Unwilling.

First thing in the room that felt like something opening.

"I told you about it before Tavin left."

I don't remember.

"I know."

Less sharp.

She looked at the cord.

Sword.

Me.

"You said you'd come."

...

To Halden?

Eventually.

Not forever.

Carrow was getting small, apparently.

Young Greg wanted out.

That made sense.

I had forgotten wanting it.

"Were we..."

Stop.

Coward.

"Were we what?"

Together?
Dating?
Sleeping together?
In love?

Too many nouns. Not enough memory.

"Close."

Complicated face.

"Yes."

Not enough.

She knew it wasn't enough.

"Greg."

"I'm trying not to invent the part I don't remember."

That changed something.

She looked down.

"We kissed twice."

Oh.

"Twice?"

"You counted too."

That sounded like me.

"Did it go badly?"

"The first one was terrible."

Good.

"Why is that good?"

Seems credible.

She laughed despite herself.

Covered her mouth like she regretted giving me progress.

Second was better.

I remembered neither.

And that hurt in a stupid little way.

Not wife.
Not great lost love.

Two kisses.

A room.
A neighbor who taught cards.
A woman who fed me after my parents died.

Ordinary things.

History hadn't bothered preserving them.

Neither had I.

Mara stood.

"I'm not asking you to come to Halden."

I nodded.

"I was. Before. I'm not now."

Fair.

Still hurt.

"I understand."

"Do you?"

Enough.

She picked up the key.

Stopped.

Put it back.

"Keep that."

Why?

"Because my mother will hit you with a spoon if you make her explain you to yourself."

Something warm behind ribs.

No system.

Good.

She went to the door.

I stood.

"Four days?"

Morning barge.

"I want to see you before you go."

Long look.

"Then do."

That's it.

No appointment.
No promise.
No protection against a contract, sword, filter, future catastrophe, or my own brain becoming interesting at exactly the wrong time.

A deadline.

She left.

I stayed in the doorway until the steps were gone.

Brass key in my hand.

East Verrel.
Dragonfire.
Market prices from dead cities.
S-class barriers.

Two kisses?

Nothing.

That was not a problem I could solve by remembering harder.

Four days.

I wrote it down.

This time I wrote it down.
