I was early.

Let's establish that first.

Early.

Not celebrate it.

Celebration creates expectations.

Recognition only.

The river was still gray when I reached Mara's mother's house.

Bakers awake.
Teamsters awake.
People with healthier relationships to morning rolling barrels toward docks.

I had eaten.

Also important.

No, not important.

Worth noting.

Door opened before I reached for key.

Mara's mother in apron.

"You ate?"

Yes.

Suspicious look.

"What?"

"Nothing."

That nothing contained a lot.

She let me in.

Mara upstairs, last crate, rope pulled tight.

Her room had crossed some line overnight.

Yesterday: packing.

Today...

Someone had left and forgotten to leave.

Bare mattress.
Empty shelf.
No blue cup.
Map gone.

Pale square on plaster where paper had kept years of smoke away.

Mara kneeling beside crate.

"You're early."

Apparently.

"Should I be worried?"

Probably.

She snorted.
Handed me rope.

I finished knot.

She checked it.

Retied it.

I chose growth and did not take that personally.

Crates downstairs.

One at a time.

Boatman came through kitchen exactly as promised and complained books were malicious cargo.

Mara had three crates.

He charged extra for third.

She argued.

One copper came off.

I stayed out of it.

That may have been the greatest evidence of personal development in the room.

Her mother packed food into a bag large enough for siege logistics.

Mara protested.

Two apples went in.

Argument over.

Western river quay.

Not passenger dock.

Working dock.

Cargo first.
People around it.

Mara's crates under canvas beside lamp oil barrels, six deeply offended chickens in a cage, and a locked metal box owned by a man who watched everybody else too much.

Vey Shipping mark in yellow on barge.

I knew Vey.

Later Vey.

Bigger offices.
Faster hulls.
Warehouses in six river cities.

This Vey had a patched sail and a deck hand bailing yesterday's rainwater with a cooking pot.

The future kept embarrassing itself in advance.

Mara gave clerk travel paper.

Name.
Crates.
Ledger position.
Destination.

Halden.

There.

Not a circle on a wall anymore.

Place she was actually going.

Her mother hugged her immediately.

No restraint.

Mara endured one second and then gave up and hugged back.

I looked toward river.

Not discomfort.

Maybe some discomfort.

But mostly because some moments belong to the people actually in them.

When I looked back her mother was crying and pretending wind had become very specific.

Mara came to me last.

That seemed right.

Neither of us moved.

One second maybe.

"You came."

Twice.

"Don't get ambitious."

Smile.

Then it disappeared by itself.

She looked at me carefully.

"I meant what I said yesterday."

Letters.

Not making her stupid.
Not inventing certainty.

I know.

"Do you?"

There she was.

Enough to be nervous.

Almost pleased.

"Good."

Deck hand called passengers.

Mara looked at barge.

Back to me.

"I don't know what happened to you."

No answer.

Don't build one.

She kept going.

"I don't know if you're going to remember more. I don't know if you're going to become somebody I recognize again."

I could feel an answer assembling.

Of course I could.

Explain.
Reassure.
Make model.
Make it neat.

She saw my face.

"Don't."

I stopped.

Good command.

"I also don't know if that's what I want."

...

That landed deeper.

Not cruel.

Better than cruel.

True.

Young Greg had belonged to her in some unfinished way.

Not belonged.

No.

Wrong word.

Young Greg had been somebody she knew.

Somebody she had almost become something with.

And I was not that boy preserved in glass waiting for memory to finish loading.

She did not owe him a reconstruction project.

Mara looked at river.

"Maybe I like parts of this version better."

I blinked.

"That seems irresponsible."

"There he is."

We laughed.

Then didn't.

Barge master shouted one minute or swim to Halden.

Mara stepped closer.

And for one stupid second I expected a kiss.

Why?

Memory supplied none.

Expectation supplied plenty.

She hugged me instead.

Quick.

Harder than expected.

My arms came around her late.

One heartbeat.

Late.

That bothered me.

Then...

No.

It didn't.

Not after the second breath.

She let go.

"Write something true."

Not often.
Not weekly.
Not promise.

True.

That's a different instruction.

"I can try that."

"Terrifying."

She climbed aboard.

Her mother called three instructions after her before loading plank moved.

Mara answered none.

Poles down.

Wood creak.

Water along hull.

For several yards departure looked stupidly ordinary.

Boat moved.

That's all.

Then current caught.

Barge turned to midriver.

Mara at stern rail, one hand on canvas over books.

Other hand up.

Mine too.

No last words.

Too much water too quickly.

Lower bridge.

Yellow mark.
Patched sail.
Six furious chickens.

Smaller.

Smaller again.

Part of river traffic.

Gone around bend.

Her mother stayed beside me after there was nothing left to see.

Or nothing left to reasonably claim we were seeing.

"She'll be fine," I said.

She looked at me.

"I know."

Right.

That wasn't reassurance she needed.

Not everything needed me to supply the final sentence.

We walked back.

Kitchen.

Small bundle on table.

Cards.

Old deck.
Soft edges.
Burned corner on seven of cups.

I knew the deck before I knew why.

Hand on top card.

Tavin.

And there...

Something.

Back room table.
Rain.
Mara complaining because dried beans were stakes and Greg had eaten one of the red ones to prove some point history correctly decided not to preserve.

A man's hand slapped mine away from pot.

Laughter.

Gone.

I opened my eyes.

Mara's mother had seen enough.

"Those were yours."

Tavin's.

Head tilt.

"His first. Yours after he bought a better deck."

Of course.

People improved equipment in every timeline.

Seven of cups.

Burned corner.

"What happened?"

"You tried to cheat at candle tricks."

Did it work?

"You set the card on fire."

Ah.

More credible.

She pushed deck toward me.

"Take them."

I did.

No argument.

Outside, morning had become day without asking me.

Guild board had work.
Arlo awake.
Ivena probably awake earlier.
North Gate still standing.
Purse still needed help.

All true.

I went to my room first.

Meral's marked cord by window.

Brass key beside it.

Old cards on table.

Three objects.

Three versions of me?

No.

Don't do that.

I could feel the theory trying to happen.

Cord means future skill.
Key means forgotten boy.
Cards mean...

Stop.

I ate one of the apples Mara's mother had shoved into my pocket instead.

Crunch.

Good.

Apple was allowed to just be an apple.
