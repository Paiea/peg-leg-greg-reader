There was a letter under my door.

I saw the handwriting before I saw the Halden stamp.

Which seemed backwards.

Still true.

One hand on stair rail.

Warehouse dust gray on my fingers from counting grain sacks for a broker who apparently believed arithmetic improved when supervised.

Letter clean.

My name on the front.

Greg. Lower Rook Street. Carrow.

No Mara anywhere.

Didn't need it.

The last address line tilted upward because Mara always got impatient with addresses before they were finished.

Apparently Halden had not repaired that.

I could have picked it up.

Instead I went downstairs.

Washed my hands at the courtyard pump.

Came back up.

Letter still there.

Good.

Avoidance had become less ambitious.

I picked it up.

Heavier paper than mine.

Not expensive.

Different office stock.

Halden stamp.
Vey route mark.
Date four days after the Carrow stamp on my letter.

I counted without meaning to.

Mine reached Halden.

Mara had it long enough to answer.

Then this crossed back on another packet.

Time had happened somewhere I could not see.

That trick again.

Inside, support receipt still tucked under brass key so I would stop checking the date every morning.

It had not worked.

First Deepwinter closer.
Still weeks.

Arlo and Ivena had not waited either.

Tannery paid through trial and Ivena extended the one-unit service arrangement without asking whether I had thoughts.

I did.

She had not asked.

Healthy system.

Units leaving workshop one at a time.

Fittings arrive.
Housing survives firing.
Unit leaves.

Nobody needed me yesterday.

Today a grain broker needed me to count sacks.

And Mara wrote back.

I sat on bed.

Opened envelope badly because I tried to preserve the fold and tore a corner anyway.

Two sheets.

Two?

Excessive.

Then I remembered Mara owned more books than was financially responsible.

Fair.

First page.

"Greg,

No. Halden does not smell better than the lower quay."

I laughed before deciding whether I should.

"It smells different. Wet wool near the east warehouses. Coal smoke when the wind comes off the hill. The cooper under our office burns something every morning that I am almost certain is not wood. Pell eats onions at the ledger desk and claims that does not count as a smell because he likes them. He is wrong."

Pell.

I stopped.

I did not know Pell.

And that hurt.

Not badly.

Correctly.

Mara had acquired a person whose onion habits already deserved postage.

Her life had Pell.

Mine had Meren Voss and a commercial ward seam full of wool grease.

Reasonable exchange.

Keep reading.

"The job is mostly numbers so far. I knew that before I came. Knowing it and sitting through eight hours of somebody else's corrections are different things. Vey's Halden office is three rooms over a cooper's shop. The west room tilts enough that the ink runs toward the wall if the desk is not level. We share the rear store room with another carrier because apparently a company can own three barges and still argue over shelf space."

Three barges.

My brain grabbed it immediately.

Later Vey: six river cities. Faster hulls. Dedicated packet halls. Scheduling office in Carrow large enough that I once got lost looking for the correct complaint desk and found two.

Three barges now.
Shared storage.
Tilted room over cooper.

Expansion.
Consolidation.
River network.
Merchant house.

No.

Stop.

Mara did not send me evidence for an investment thesis.

She sent me her office.

The floor tilts.

That is the thing.

Back to letter.

"The blue cup survived. Mother was wrong. Halden has cups, but none of them are the blue one."

I smiled.

"Your rope story was terrible. I read it twice. I am glad you listened when Orra told you to cut it. I am also aware you crossed out `she was right.` Crossing out words does not make them invisible. You used to know that."

Right.

Ink remained ink.

Revolutionary technology.

I had mailed her the edit and the thing being edited.

"Mother told me she gave you Tavin's cards. I do not remember why you burned the seven either. Tavin said you cheated. You said the card was defective. I believed Tavin."

Unfair.

Probably accurate.

I picked up the deck.

Seven of cups.
Burned corner.
Defective card.

Nothing came back.

Fine.

Put it down.

Next paragraph shorter.

"You did write something true."

I read that again.

Immediately found three ways to ruin it.

Then next line supplied a fourth.

"You also wrote it like someone might grade you afterward."

...

I stared.

"River mistake. Business update. Cards. Money. Proof that you are trying. Greg, I asked for true. I did not ask for a report proving you had behaved correctly."

Cold plaster against my back.

First impulse: defense.

Let it finish.

I didn't write to prove anything.

I wrote because she asked.

I told the truth.

I included what happened.

What else was I supposed to...

Second page.

Of course.

"Do not answer this with a worse letter where you explain your feelings correctly. I will throw it in the river."

I laughed.

Louder this time.

"Next time tell me one thing you would leave out if you were trying to make the letter useful."

Then:

"What you ate. Something stupid Sella said. Whether the room is cold. A person I do not know yet. Something you wanted to tell me before you decided whether it mattered."

There.

I stopped.

Useful.

Of course.

I had turned truth into a requirement.

No lying.
No false certainty.
No pretending memory existed.

Meet constraints.
Submit.

Mara asked for contact.

I sent documentation.

Annoyingly clear once somebody else wrote it down.

One more paragraph.

Read it before building a response.

"I do not know how often I will write. The first week here was longer than it was supposed to be and next week looks worse. If you write again, I want to read it. Those are the two things I know."

Two things.

I hated the phrase on sight.

Not because of Mara.

Because somewhere in my room nineteen-year-old Greg had probably already found a way to make it personal.

She signed Mara.

No promise.
No miss you.
No come west.
No answer to a question I had not asked honestly enough to deserve one.

Just Mara.

I set both sheets on bed.

Picked them up immediately because blanket corner was damp from laundry.

Important priorities recovered fast.

Letter on table beside Tavin's cards.

Obvious next action: write back.

I did not.

Not restraint.

I had no idea what to say.

Different.

I went downstairs and bought dinner.

Not because Mara said report food.

Because hungry.

Still useful to verify.

Two-copper stew at south corner.

Beef, if beef was aspirational.

Too much pepper.
Not enough meat.
One potato had given up structurally and become broth.

I ate all of it.

Boy at next table dropped spoon.

Looked at mother.
Picked it up.
Wiped it on shirt.
Kept eating before she turned around.

Competent field work.

I almost saved that for the letter.

Then realized what I was doing.

No.

Eat.

Carrow moved around me.

Cart with two filter housings went past toward East Mill.

Arlo's mark on crates.

Not mine.

Driver woman I didn't know.

Ivena arranged delivery yesterday after the fact.

Tannery trial extended.
One unit.
Matched spare.

Nobody built the network in my head.

Nobody asked me to.

Across street, city workers argued about North Gate restriction while carrying a ladder south.

Couldn't hear details.

Gate still standing.

Also moving through city without me.

Stew still too peppery.

Analysis did not fix it.

Back upstairs.

Blank sheet.

Same shape as before.

Different problem.

Last time: not enough true content.

Now:

Too much.

Meren.
Apothecary coupling.
Iron drain.
Six copper.
Tannery extension.
Vey's three barges.
Kesra in ledger.
Red roof.
Future I still couldn't prove.

All useful.

Mara had given examples.

What you ate.
Room cold.
Person I don't know yet.
Something before you decide it matters.

Room was cold.

That sounded insulting as correspondence.

Then I noticed my left foot tucked under my right leg because floorboard near bed pulled cold through sole.

I had been doing it for days.

Maybe longer.

Young Greg too?

No memory.
No importance.

Just cold.

Pen.

"Mara,

The room gets cold before dawn. I keep tucking one foot under the other when I sit on the bed. I do not know if I always did that."

Stop.

Ridiculous.

Good sign?

No.

Don't grade.

"I bought stew tonight. Too much pepper. I ate all of it."

My hand wanted to add why I had money for stew.

Ward shift.
Six copper.
Plane.
Progress.
Context.

Useful.

Leave it out.

For two breaths omission felt dishonest.

Then merely unfamiliar.

One more.

"There is a woman who drives Arlo's deliveries now. I do not know her name. That bothered me for a second, then it did not."

Too interpretive.

I crossed out second sentence.

Ink remained ink.

Mara would see it.

Fine.

Then beneath:

"Her name is Dena. I asked yesterday and forgot until now."

Better.

Not because it proved anything.

Because Dena existed.

I had met her beside a cart and immediately converted her into delivery capacity.

Mara would probably enjoy that indictment more than I did.

I almost explained.

Didn't.

Letter short.

I hated that too.

First one took half a day and a river emergency.

This had cold feet, bad stew, and Dena.

I reread.

No declaration.
No report.
No request for reassurance that Mara received the relationship correctly.

Signed Greg.

Then blue cup.

Turn page.

"Your mother was still wrong about the cup. I am willing to testify."

There.

That one I wanted her to read.

Packet office closed.

I knew because I checked.

Different kind of progress.

Letter beside Halden envelope.

Waiting until morning did not feel like failure.

At dawn, clerk behind counter.

Same clerk?

Maybe.

I had started respecting confidence in faces before breakfast.

"Halden?"

Yes.

One copper.

Same rate.

Vey sack looked newer than last one.

Or cleaner.

Dangerous confidence again.

Stamp.

Drop.

Paper against paper.

No ceremony.

This time I did not ask when it would arrive.

The route knew more about that than I did.

Outside, cold enough for both hands in coat pockets.

Support receipt touched one hand.

Mara's Halden envelope folded in the other.

I left them there.

Breakfast.
