Noll stopped sleeping on my floor after one night.

The smoke stayed longer.

By third morning Lower Rook Street smelled less like burned building and more like building that had recently lost an argument with water.

Progress.

Cookroom stove still cold.

Buckets still on every landing.

Nobody moved them.

Noll found a mattress somewhere.

It had opinions about fleas.

Still technically a mattress.

I had given him soap twice since the fire.

That part of history was recovering beautifully.

I left before breakfast because corner stall raised egg prices again and I preferred being insulted by the Guild for free.

Sella looked up.

Then my coat.

"You still smell burned."

Building fire.

"I heard."

Of course.

Carrow Guild knew structural emergencies, monster sightings, unpaid hazard slips, marriages that changed contract names, and at least three kinds of public embarrassment before breakfast.

Sella moved work chits.

"You here for another ward shift?"

No.

The answer came fast enough to surprise me.

Meren's work paid.

Useful too.

I'd do it again.

Different from wanting to spend the next several weeks in Carrow waiting for Kesra Dain to arrive and tell nineteen-year-old me how badly I performed the first five inches of a discipline fifty-nine-year-old me once mastered.

Assessment still weeks.

Weeks...

Weeks are a lot of life if you stop treating them like a hallway.

I looked at board.

City work left side.

Warehouse checks.
Inside-wall escort.
Commercial ward hands.
Tannery response I avoided on principle.

Field postings farther right.

Fewer.

Colder.

Better handwriting somehow.

WEST CUT ROUTE VERIFICATION. FOUR DAYS MINIMUM. LIGHT ESCORT. SURVEY PRIORITY.

"That road still exists?"

Sella leaned back.

"Apparently that's what they're paying someone to find out."

West Cut.

Old quarry road.

Good.

"Then you walk back and write down that it is gone."

Maybe six.

Old quarry road?

Drainage.

Road.
