Pate heard them first.

One ear toward road.

Then the other.

Mule lifted his head out of whatever argument he had been having with sleep and went completely still.

I watched him.

Rainwater tapping from quarry lip.

Leaves moving below overhang.

Nothing else.

Pate's nostrils widened.

There.

Not sound exactly.

Something under sound.

Rhythm too slow for rain.

Too irregular for wheels.

Hand to sword.

Listen harder.

Click.

...

Click-click.

Stone on stone somewhere down the Cut.

Then hooves.

A lot of hooves.

I knew the noise before memory bothered making a picture.

"Fuck."

Pate agreed.

Lead rope tight against stake.

Up.

Edda slept nearest road, hand beneath cloak, spear where one hand could find it before both eyes opened.

Touch boot.

Eyes open.

No confusion.

Useful woman.

"What?"

Glasshorns.

She was upright before word finished.

"Here?"

Coming through Cut.

She listened.

Clicks easier now.

Horn against horn.
Hoof against limestone.

Not close.

Closer than I wanted.

How many?

Don't know.

Edda looked at Pate.

Pate had stopped pretending bravery.

"Enough."

Yes.

Harl woke because Edda kicked his bedroll.

Less gently than I had touched hers.

"Up."

He made a noise suggesting road office did not pay consciousness at this hour.

"What happened?"

Herd coming.

That fixed him.

"What herd?"

Glasshorns.

He stared.

Then road.

"They don't come this low."

Click-click.

Current evidence remained rude.

Edda pointed uphill.

Move mule and gear above road.
Now.

Harl looked at slope.

"Pate hates loose stone."

"Pate is about to hate something else more."

Also true.

Move.

No shouting.

No fire because rain had denied us one.

Bedrolls badly rolled.
Cook tin shoved in.
Survey case first because Harl would rescue it before us if forced.

Pate required no persuasion.

Mule had suddenly decided slope was excellent.

Harl led him toward narrow shelf above Cut.

Loose stone under hooves.

Pate tried too fast.

Harl caught halter near cheek.

"Easy."

Pate disagreed.

"Easy anyway."

Mule considered murder.

Obeyed.

Edda behind load, keeping pannier off rock.

I carried survey case.

Harl noticed.

"Don't drop that."

I was planning to throw it at herd.

"Don't."

Glad we discussed.

Gear on shelf.

Road below dark.

Sound wasn't.

More hooves.

Low breathy grunt between quarry walls.

Pate pulled to back of shelf.

Harl did not tie him hard to scrub pine.

Good.

Frightened mule plus hard tree is fear converted to injury.

Lead twice around Harl's own forearm.

Near shoulder.

Also good.

Enough years around bad animal handling to appreciate when nobody needed my advice.

Edda at edge, spear in hand.

Low.

"What do they do?"

Keep moving.

If see us?

Keep moving.

If Pate panics?

More our problem than theirs.

Pate had reached same conclusion.

Aggressive?

Not unless we make road theirs and ours at same time.

First pale light around bend.

Not lantern.

Low.
Soft.

White-green smear between bare trunks.

Another.

Five more.

Harl stopped asking.

Glasshorns came out of dark.

Name made them sound delicate.

They weren't.

Lead animal almost Pate's shoulder height. Narrow belly. Heavy chest. Dark winter coat wet with rain. Horn sheaths long, swept back.

Not glass.

People had named things badly for centuries.

Outer sheath translucent enough for faint light inside to show cloudy bands.

Wet limestone made glow brighter.

Lead stepped into open stretch below.

Stopped.

Pate made a tiny sound.

Glasshorn lifted head.

Edda's spear stayed low.

Good.

I remembered a Silver fighter once seeing lead glasshorn stop and deciding herd was preparing attack.

Shield up.

Lead animal interpreted that as new landscape in route.

Rest of herd interpreted lead stopping as permission to arrive all at once.

Shield survived.

His ankle didn't.

Below us, animal smelled air.

Ears toward Pate.

Then me.

Nothing happened.

That was the hard part.

Nothing happening can feel unfinished when you are holding a weapon.

Like maybe you should complete it.

Don't.

Animal lowered head.

Walked on.

Three behind it.

Then eight.

Then enough that counting became a job nobody needed.

Loose line.
Two abreast where wide.
Single where pinch.
Young ones inside.

One juvenile with little horn nubs, almost no light.

One limping right rear.

Nobody fixed it.

Herd left room anyway.

Not kindness.

Bodies moving together learning space.

Pate breathing worse.

Harl leaned shoulder into him, spoke quietly into ear.

Couldn't hear words.

Didn't need to.

Mule stopped pulling back.

Edda looked over.

Harl nodded once.

Larger glasshorn around bend.

Male probably.

Wider horn sheaths.
Pale light nearly to tips.
Mud on flank.
Broken point left horn.

Stopped directly below shelf.

Pate jerked.

Harl's boots slid half step.

I moved toward mule.

Harl shook head once.

His animal.

Right.

Stop.

Glasshorn looked up.

Pale horn light touched Edda's face, spearhead, Harl's patched coat, underside of Pate's jaw.

It could see us.

Maybe smell oat cakes.

Did glasshorns like oat cakes?

Question did not deserve experiment.

Male snorted.

Scraped front hoof.

Edda's fingers shifted on spear shaft.

Not raising.

Ready.

Then herd called farther ahead.

Low.

Felt more than heard.

Male turned.

Walked on.

I let breath out through nose.

Edda heard.

"You were worried."

I said not aggressive.

Didn't say polite.

"Useful distinction."

Harl made a strangled noise that may have been laughter or Pate stepping on him.

Herd kept coming.

Twenty minutes?

Thirty?

Time gets strange when every pale horn around bend could be last and keeps not being last.

We stood.

Legs stiff.
Rain down collar.
Edda shifted twice.
Harl's arm probably numb from Pate leaning.

Nobody complained.

Last animal seemed small female, one sheath cloudy almost opaque.

Stopped to lick water off quarry wall.

Migration refreshments.

Then followed herd.

Clicks faded.

Lights disappeared one by one.

We waited.

Old field rule.

Last animal is not always last animal.

One minute.

Two.

Then juvenile came around bend running hard enough to slide on wet stone.

Edda looked at me.

Now?

Still no.

Juvenile recovered, noticed none of us because it was busy being late, and ran after herd.

Little horn nubs flashed twice between trees.

Dark again.

Edda waited another minute.

"Now?"

Probably.

"Probably?"

You want certainty, hire Harl.

Harl offended.

"I certify roads."

Exactly.

Pate urinated on shelf.

Discussion over.

We did not move camp back down.

Edda decided before anyone suggested it.

Road could have road until morning.

I returned to watch from shelf.

Harl stayed awake because Pate wouldn't settle.

Edda sat beside me, cloak around shoulders.

"You've seen those before."

Yes.

A lot?

Enough.

She looked.

Dark made lying easier.

Also less useful.

"More than somebody your age should have."

No safe answer.

"I've had a complicated month."

Long stare.

Snort.

"That was a terrible answer."

I know.

She didn't ask again.

I liked her more.

Dawn made road different.

Not destroyed.

Used.

Hundreds of split hoofprints through wet center.
Mud pushed shoulders.
Pale hairs on thorn bush.

Harl at stone pinch with measuring rod before Edda finished half breakfast.

What are you doing?

"Measuring the same road after animals walked on it."

Did it get wider?

"No."

Then I saved you time.

Ignored.

He measured churned shoulder.
Rock face to drop.
Walked fifty paces back.
Looked at hoof line.

"They always use this?"

Question to me.

I don't know this exact Cut.

Important.

I knew glasshorns.

I knew later roads.

Not every year between.

They use limestone corridors after first cold rains.
Usually moving down from high ground.

"Usually?"

Usually.

How long?

Days.
Sometimes longer.
Depends herd and weather.

Harl finally looked at me.

"That is extremely unhelpful."

You asked animal question.

"I asked a road question."

Animals didn't.

Edda made sound into oat cake.

Harl wrote something.

Not my answer.

Good.

He walked downhill where fresh tracks entered Cut from side slope.

Checked.
Walked farther.
Returned.

"Current corridor use."

Meaning?

"I cannot call this reliable for pack traffic until we know where they enter and where they leave."

Does that kill route?

No.

Board tap.

"It makes the route seasonal until proven otherwise."

Pack shelf.

Pate came down loose stone carefully.

Stopped at road.

Smelled every hoofprint like gathering evidence for lawsuit.

Harl let him.

Edda checked bend.

Survey not done.

Quarry spur.
West slope.
Whatever rain had done.

Socks almost dry.
Shoulders hurt from stone sleep.
Sword unused.

Good sword.

Edda walked beside me after Harl and Pate moved ahead.

"You have first watch tonight."

I had second last night.

"And you found the herd."

That is not a reason for more watch.

"It is exactly a reason."

I considered arguing.

Harl called road narrowing ahead.

Edda moved past me.

Conversation over.

I followed west.
