The second letter had spent almost a month under lamp oil.

Less dramatic than losing it in a fire.

More insulting.

I found it the morning after my day off because the table had stopped being arranged and started negotiating territory.

Sword oil beside support receipt.

Waxed thread under brass key.

Two loose copper on Mara's Halden envelope because apparently money became safer when placed on correspondence.

Lamp-oil receipt I had kept for reasons nobody alive could defend.

Move receipt.

Envelope.

Stop.

Not Mara's handwriting.

Cheaper paper.
Folded once before someone decided it deserved envelope.

My name on front without street.

Greg.

That's all.

Whoever sent it expected packet office to know where I lived.

I disliked that immediately.

Not because unreasonable.

Because somebody else apparently knew how easy I was to find before I did.

Freight-yard stamp in corner.

South Latch.

River company.

Then name.

Tavin.

Hand slapping mine away from dried beans.
Rain against back-room window.
Burned seven.
Mara saying Tavin claimed I cheated.

Then nothing useful.

No face.
No voice.

No reason the name should tighten my chest anyway.

I sat.

Chair complained.

Good.

Current furniture reliable.

Date.

Eleven days before I woke in this body.

I checked twice because arithmetic easier than opening it.

Eleven days.

Young Greg received this.

Or should have.

Maybe read it already and put it under receipt.

Maybe delivery clerk slid it under door after whatever happened to him happened.

Maybe I had been carrying a stranger's unopened mail around since Chapter One of existence.

I did not know.

Crowded category.

Open.

First line solved voice problem.

"Greg, if you are still pretending Carrow is too small for you, Venn's crew needs another body."

I laughed.

Stopped.

Whoever Tavin was, he had already annoyed me accurately.

Keep reading.

"Not Guild. Freight company. South Latch to Rell Ford and back, maybe six weeks if the river stays stupid. Mostly wagons beside the water because the shallow barges cannot take the stone cargo this season. Two guards already. They want somebody who can carry and stand watch without inventing problems. I lied about you."

There he was.

Not remembered.

Present enough to be rude from paper.

Pay decent.

Food not.

Foreman terrible in a way Tavin apparently considered proof of reliability.

Then worse.

"You said Halden first if Mara goes. If that is still the plan, ignore this. If you are actually serious about getting out on the road instead of talking about it in her mother's back room, write me before the end of the month."

Once.

Again.

Put letter down.

Room suddenly had too many versions of me in it.

Halden first if Mara goes.

Of course.

Nineteen-year-old boy plans to follow girl west.

Simple.
Embarrassing.
Common enough for whole songs by men who should know better.

Except letter didn't say only that.

Tavin had a six-week freight lead because I had apparently been talking about roads before Mara left.

Enough talking that another person got tired and tried to make me prove it.

I looked at field pack by door.

Cleaned.
Repaired.
Ready to become packed.

I had thought that was mine.

Not pack.

Wanting.

Roads.
Work outside Carrow.
Somewhere larger than room.

I had been careful with that desire because it had a clean explanation.

Old Greg was an adventurer.

Of course Old Greg wanted roads.

Old Greg remembered contracts, bad inns, support work, rain under armor, people packing six days food for eight-day routes.

Current Greg could want roads because current Greg remembered them.

Clean.

Then young Greg ruined it before dying.

I was irritated with him.

Difficult because he was me.

Probably.

Mostly.

Enough for legal purposes.

The dead boy stole my excuse.

Pick letter up.

Tavin kept going like he had not complicated my theory of self.

"Do not bring the red blanket unless you finally washed it."

Wardrobe.

No red blanket.

One historical problem solved through disappearance.

"Also you still owe me two copper from cards. Mara says you claim the burned seven invalidated the hand. It did not. You burned it after I won. That is not how rules work."

I stood.

Cards under Mara's letter.

Deck.

Seven of cups on top because apparently subconscious arrangement was becoming a service.

Burned corner.

Letter beside card.

No memory.

Still defective.

Two copper from table onto card.

This improved nothing.

Pleased me anyway.

Last paragraph: South Latch office. Don't use Kell's packet line because they lose half mail and charge the other half for privilege.

Then Tavin.

No miss you.
No careful explanation of relationship.
No request to become person who understood joke.

Just Tavin.

I sat with letter and two copper on burned seven.

Month-end deadline passed.

Not yesterday.

Enough.

Venn's crew gone, full, or halfway to Rell Ford.

First impulse: can I catch them?

Embarrassing.

Second: does pay justify trying?

Different embarrassment.

Job dead.

Wanting not.

I almost wrote that sentence down.

Then heard Mara from four days of packet travel away.

Do not answer this with a worse letter where you explain your feelings correctly.

Fine.

Clean sheet.

Paper had become expensive since women started grading my emotional competence.

"Tavin,"

Stop.

What does he deserve?

Mara saw me fail to remember kissing her.

Tavin sent freight lead and demanded two copper.

Not same relationship.

Write:

"I found your letter late. The job is probably gone."

True.
Useful.
Dangerous start.

Hand kept moving.

"I do not remember you properly."

I stared.

Too much.

Not false.

But imagine receiving it.

Hello. Your friend has misplaced you. Thank you for employment opportunity.

Cross it out.

Ink remains ink.

Mara established technology.

Turn page.

Paper has two sides.

Emotional honesty can pay rent like everyone else.

Again.

"Tavin,

I found your letter late. The freight job is probably gone. I am still in Carrow. I have been out on the road anyway."

Better.

Then hand wanted proof.

Five days west.
Glasshorns.
Road office.
Field pay.
Good sword.

Look, Tavin.

I became the person you accused me of only pretending to want to be.

Pen down.

That's what I hated.

I wanted him to know.

Not because information mattered.

Because I wanted this faceless man to read it and think yes, Greg finally did something.

No professional boundary around that.

No correct angle.
No five-inch version.

I got up.

Opened window.

Cold air immediately made room worse.

Useful.

Lower Rook below.

Cart wheel hit same loose stone twice because driver reversed and tried mistake again.

Someone shouting onions.

Woman upstairs shaking rug into courtyard with personal conviction.

Next door: thump.

Another.

Noll through wall.

"You alive?"

Why?

"You haven't dropped anything in an hour."

I am becoming graceful.

"No."

Fair.

Pause.

"You got soap?"

Eyes closed.

Still.

Door opened next door.

Mine three seconds later because apparently this was postal system now.

Noll in hall with towel.

Both boots today.

Progress.

Soap.

He looked past me at table.

Two copper on burned card.

"Gambling before breakfast?"

Debt.

"Yours?"

Apparently.

Noll leaned just enough to see letter, not read.

"Mara?"

No.

He waited.

I waited harder.

Shrug.

"Dangerous."

What is?

"Other people."

Then downstairs with my soap.

I stood in doorway.

Wisdom or theft.

Probably theft.

Back at table.

Half-letter.

I did not finish it.

Did not tear it up either.

That felt important enough that I refused to explain why.

Tavin's old letter beside Mara's.

Unfinished reply under burned seven.

Two copper on top.

Paperweight for debt I could not remember earning.

Outside Noll shouted somebody used all hot water.

Someone shouted water did not become his because he wanted it first.

Argument improved from there.

I closed window.

Road could wait until after breakfast.
