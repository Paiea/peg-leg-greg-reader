I took Jorren to Mill Street because I wanted him to see the filtration venture.

True.

Incomplete in a way Mara would find irritating from Halden.

I wanted him impressed.

That took longer to admit.

Not by filters exactly.

By me.

Worse.

Jorren met me after midday with new shoulder strap and half loaf.

"You eat?"

Some.

He tore loaf in half and gave me smaller piece.

Friendship remained adversarial.

Mill Street smelled wet wool, kiln smoke, mineral damp from businesses boiling things for money.

Jorren looked around.

"This your exciting secret?"

No.

"Good."

You have become demanding.

"Roads."

Apparently roads explained everything now.

Arlo's workshop door open.

Before we reached it, familiar cart rolled out.

Dena driving.

Two clean ceramic chambers upright behind her.

One stained return in rear cradle under oilcloth.

She saw me.

Then Jorren.

Eyes to new strap.

"New leather."

Jorren looked.

Yes.

"Oil it before rain."

I know.

Dena nodded.

Professional relationship established.

I lifted hand.

Hello, Dena.

"Greg."

Mule moved.

Jorren watched cart go.

"Who is that?"

Dena.

"I gathered."

Service runs.

"For this?"

He pointed at workshop.

Yes.

There.

We had not entered and cart already made my explanation late.

Inside, Arlo beside kiln with hands black to wrists.

Not soot.

Slip.

Fresh chamber bodies on boards by warm wall.

Ivena at bench with ledger, three client tags, and expression suggesting somebody recently asked for a discount.

Neither surprised by me.

Jorren got attention.

Arlo looked at sword.

Boots.

Face.

"Road friend?"

I disliked how accurately people identified categories I had struggled to name.

Jorren.

Hand out.

Arlo looked at black hands.

Jorren did too.

"Later."

"Good."

Ivena closed ledger.

"Does he have money?"

Jorren looked at me.

"This the woman?"

Which woman?

"The one who asks if you brought money."

Ivena's eyes moved to me.

"You talk about me?"

Only under financial duress.

"Good."

Back to Jorren.

Do you?

Some.

"Then welcome."

I had imagined this differently.

Not consciously.

That would be embarrassing.

But somewhere between Lower Rook and Mill Street I had built a version.

Open door.
Show Jorren four ugly ceramic units that became actual orders.

Explain first failure.
Bypass.
Ivena's wash.
Arlo's chambers.
Service model.

Maybe road-version me would look less like man who spent free time irritating posts with sword.

Instead Dena drove past before I said anything and Ivena was checking whether Jorren qualified economically as a person.

Arlo wiped hands.

"What did you bring him for?"

To see shop.

Arlo looked around.

"It's here."

Jorren laughed.

I hated everyone.

I picked up finished chamber.

"This is the clean half of the service unit. Customer keeps two. One runs while the other comes back for cleaning."

Jorren took it.

Heavier than it looked.

Turned once.

"Fragile?"

"Less than the first ones," Arlo said.

Jorren checked thick collar.

Then street where Dena disappeared.

"How many break on the cart?"

I knew approximately.

Arlo knew better.

"One so far."

Ivena added:

"Not cart. Customer dropped it off the wash platform."

They say that?

"After Dena asked why the impact mark was on the top edge."

Jorren smiled.

"I like Dena."

Of course.

I took chamber back.

"The ceramic isn't the important part."

Arlo looked.

Correction.

"It is an important part."

"Good recovery."

Jorren moved to service tags.

Three wooden slips.

Purple.
Blue.
Black.

Client codes.

What's this?

Ivena answered.

Fixed service days.

East Mill second and fifth.
Lower bridge third.
Dyehouse pickup whenever her workers stopped pretending they'd remember to send dirty chamber back.

I looked at her.

Fixed days?

Yes.

Since when?

This week.

I had been on West Cut.

Then asleep.

Then day off.

Then Tavin under lamp oil.

Then Jorren in onions.

Commerce continued being rude.

Why?

Ivena opened ledger.

Not because needed.

Because numbers improved accusation.

Dena making extra crossings because customers called whenever running chamber slowed, even when matched spare was already clean beside it.

On-demand service became three half-empty cart trips in one week.

Fixed days: customer swaps to spare, tags dirty, waits unless both have problem.

And if both fail?

"Runner."

How fast?

"They pay for fast."

Ivena.

I looked at tags.

Future version liked fixed routes.

Eventually.

Part of me wanted to argue three customers was too early for schedule rules.

That part wasn't entirely wrong.

What if wool washer only needs service once that week?

"Then Dena has one less chamber."

Cart still goes.

"Because the soapworks still exists."

Probably.

Ivena stared.

"Do you have information?"

No.

"Then don't kill my customer."

Jorren looked down so I wouldn't see smile.

I saw it.

You think this funny?

"Very."

You've been here four minutes.

"Strong four minutes."

Arlo took chamber from me.

"Dena wanted the fixed days."

I looked.

That mattered.

Why?

"Ask her tomorrow."

Fair.

I hated fair answers when showing someone around.

Jorren went to cart rack near door.

Two padded cradles.
Spare tie strap.

Thumb into padding.

"Wool?"

Old blanket, Arlo said.

Works.

"Dena cut it."

Smart.

I stood in workshop while friend admired blanket inside cart cradle.

This was not impression I planned.

I had become a man with a business whose exciting feature was responsible packing.

Jorren looked back.

"Which part did you make?"

There.

Finally.

I opened mouth.

Arlo first:

"Bad disk."

Ivena:

"Money."

I looked at both.

I also had filtration idea.

"Bad disk idea," Arlo said.

It filtered.

"Until Ivena used it."

Ivena pointed with pen.

"I improved it by needing it to work."

Jorren nodded seriously.

"Important role."

"Thank you."

Arms crossed.

Dena wasn't here, depriving room of fourth accusation.

Then Arlo ruined joke by looking at ledger.

"Greg saw the first waste shimmer."

No smile.

Fact.

"And he paid for enough clay that I could break three versions without closing the shop."

Ivena added:

"He noticed the first hot-run failure was different before either of us wanted to admit we'd sold something unfinished."

You admitted quickly.

"After I shouted at you."

True.

Jorren looked from them to me.

This should be the moment.

Admiration available.

Instead:

"You got yelled at into a business."

I stared.

Arlo sat down because laughing while standing became inefficient.

Ivena made a sound I'd never heard and covered it by coughing.

I hated sentence because not entirely wrong.

I contributed more.

"I'm sure."

Worse.

Jorren picked spare tie strap.

"This buckle is backwards."

Arlo stopped laughing.

Looked.

Strap rethreaded wrong side after washing.

Would hold.

Would jam under load if pulled tight.

Road problem.

Jorren flipped it.

Threaded correctly.

Set back.

Nobody praised him.

He didn't need it.

Workshop continued.

Ivena reopened ledger.
Arlo went back to wet chambers.
Bell somewhere East Mill.
Jorren finished bread.

I stood there with stupid warm feeling I expected when he admired my importance.

It arrived sideways.

Arlo trusted me enough to insult first design without protecting ego.

Ivena remembered exactly where I was useful and exactly where expensive.

Dena changed service days without asking.

Jorren fixed a strap within ten minutes of entering.

None of that was the speech I wanted.

I was still a little annoyed.

Jorren nudged my boot.

"You buying lunch?"

You ate half a loaf.

"That was before business."

You did no business.

He pointed at strap.

"Consulting."

Ivena looked up.

"How much?"

Jorren considered.

Lunch.

"Greg pays."

Agreed.

They shook hands.

I had brought Jorren here to show him something of mine.

I left owing him lunch...

To my own business partner.
