Jorren came back with mud on only one boot.

Impossible enough that I distrusted the entire man.

He was sitting in Lower Rook cookroom when I came down for breakfast, eating eggs from Noll's bowl.

Noll was not there.

Brass Spoon had swallowed another evening and apparently part of the morning after it.

The bowl remained vulnerable.

Jorren looked up.

"Morning."

I stopped on last stair.

Chest first.

Again.

Less violent than onion market.

Still enough.

Back.

Current evidence.

Wet cloak folded on bench.

Hair clean enough to suggest water before food.

One boot brown to ankle.

Other almost respectable.

Pale scrape across jaw.

Right shoulder moved normally when fork rose.

I checked without meaning to.

Jorren saw.

"Sack won."

"You caught another one?"

"No. Shoulder stopped hurting."

"Then why say sack won?"

"You looked."

My face remained public infrastructure.

I sat across.

"Why one boot?"

"Cart rut."

"Only one foot?"

"Talent."

"Finally."

Bread plate pushed toward me.

"Four days?"

"Five. Rain east."

Some of mud explained.

He kept eating.

I waited for road story.

Nothing.

Inconsiderate.

"Millstones survive?"

"Mostly."

Mostly.

One chipped. Driver hit bridge rail. Jorren asleep.

"You admit that?"

"Wasn't my watch."

Strong defense.

Vell paid on return. Axle pin worked loose outside Rellin Cross. Half day lost. East rain turned last miles into mud. Jorren stepped into rut pushing wheel and buried only left boot.

Jaw scrape from same wagon. Loose side board.

"You lose fights with cargo often?"

"Cargo pays better than people."

"Doesn't apologize either."

"People don't."

Fair.

Cookroom door opened. Second-floor woman came in, saw Jorren using Noll's bowl.

"He'll complain."

"I know."

She nodded.

Apparently building had accepted him during my absence too.

Annoying.

I ate bread.

Jorren finished eggs.

Then looked at me.

Not casual.

Not serious.

Direct.

"Road."

My hand stopped halfway to bread.

One word.

Different this time.

"What road?"

"North-east. Doss Ferry. Maybe farther if return load changes."

Answer arrived before details.

Yes.

Road.

Seven days. Maybe ten.

Cold mornings.

New towns.

A pack used for something besides sitting beside my door.

Wait.

"Who?"

"Vell."

Again.

He likes being paid. Jorren likes being paid.

Beautiful partnership.

Vell moving north-east in two days.

Grain out.

Brass fittings and two crates machine parts back if sale closes.

Six days minimum.

Eight likely.

Ten if return cargo changes or river crossing backs wagons.

Two guards because brass is easier to steal than stone.

Pay?

Better than West Cut.

Better than millstone run.

Not life-changing.

Enough.

Food included except two midday meals.

Drivers knew route.

Vell had paid clean before.

Exactly kind of road I had wanted Jorren to ask me about.

Rude.

"When do you need an answer?"

"Now works."

Not deadline.

"Tonight. Vell wants names tomorrow."

Bread.

Mara.

Five days, maybe six when letter arrived.

One day gone.

Four or five now.

Jorren's road left in two.

Six days minimum.

Arithmetic refused improvement.

I could leave.

Mara said if packet behaved.

Packets did not always behave.

River could delay her.

Vey could keep her Halden.

Carrow office might need her second day instead of first.

My answer already gone west.

**Yes. Dinner. I'll be here.**

Specific enough to become inconvenient.

Jorren ate bread.

Did not fill silence.

I disliked him for that too.

"How far north-east?"

"Doss. Maybe Kelm turn if return cargo isn't ready."

Kelm.

Old memory pulled road loose.

Low hills.

Stone inn with terrible mattresses.

Spring outside town, cold even in summer.

Had young me seen it?

No idea.

Maybe current Kelm had no stone inn yet.

Maybe spring had different name.

I wanted to know.

Actual wanting.

Not professional value.

Not future verification.

I wanted to go north-east with Jorren and sleep badly somewhere not my room.

That made other want worse.

Mara in Carrow.

Dinner.

Her face when she read I'll be here.

If letter reached her.

Maybe it wouldn't.

That thought arrived dressed as reasonable.

I recognized it anyway.

"Mara's coming."

Jorren blinked.

Then smiled.

"Ah."

"Don't."

"Didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

He looked delighted by theft.

"When?"

I told him.

His eyes did arithmetic.

"You'll be gone."

"Yes."

"Could come back early," I said.

Head shake.

Not if brass loads.

If not?

Maybe sixth day.

Still bad.

"Mara could be late."

"Probably."

Bastard.

I stood.

Not because standing improved math.

Because sitting had become associated with choosing.

Empty plate to wash tub.

Rinse too carefully.

"You asked," I said.

"Yes."

"Like I told you."

"You told me."

"You said next one."

"I remember."

I turned.

"I hate that this is a good road."

He considered.

"Could ask you about a bad one."

"No."

"Difficult customer."

Laugh once.

Does not solve.

Upstairs.

Jorren did not follow.

Helpful.

My room had evidence.

Mara's new letter under brass key.

No Tavin reply.

No two copper on burned seven.

Field pack by door.

Ready.

I crouched. Opened it.

Fresh socks.

Waxed thread.

Oil.

Bedroll straps repaired.

Everything for six days except food.

Maybe eight.

The pack wanted nothing.

I wanted both.

Floor beside it.

Young Greg wanted roads before Old Greg arrived.

Current Greg had said yes to dinner before this road existed.

Neither fact volunteered to disappear.

Mara's letter.

**If you are here, I want dinner.**

My answer already in a sack.

Maybe west road now.

Maybe Halden tomorrow.

Maybe under another clerk's tea.

Still mine.

Put letter down.

Then do something stupid enough to clarify.

Pack.

Not fully.

Socks.

Oil.

Thread.

Bedroll.

Tighten top.

Lift.

Weight across shoulders.

Good.

Familiar.

Leaving easy.

Walk across room.

Back.

Mara's letter still on table.

Take pack off.

Not dramatic.

Hits floor harder than necessary.

Knock from next door.

"You break something?"

"No."

"Then quieter."

"You throw pans at midnight."

"Work."

"That was here."

"Recovery."

Apparently Jorren had infected building with professional vocabulary.

Unpack bedroll.

Leave socks.

Why?

No idea.

Compromise by laundry.

Back downstairs.

Jorren moved from eggs to tea.

Look once.

Wait.

"No."

Word hurt exactly as much as it deserved.

"All right."

That's it?

"You said no."

I know.

"Do you want negotiation?"

"No."

"Then good meeting."

"Who are you asking?"

There.

Next ugly want.

I declined.

Still wanted to know how replaceable I was.

Human dignity remained aspirational.

"Beren first."

Of course.

He's south.

Came back yesterday.

I did not know.

Jorren did.

"You already talked to him?"

"Saw him at Guild."

"About this?"

"No. I was asking you."

That should help.

It did.

Annoyingly little.

He had asked me first.

I wanted that.

I said no.

Now road would continue with somebody else.

No useful action attached.

Awful design.

"Mara here long?"

One night definitely. Maybe two.

Work. Vey. Ledgers. Everyone blaming everyone.

"Sounds like her."

There.

Young-Greg world again.

Jorren knew Mara in a way I did not fully know either of them.

Tavin too.

Old relationships moving through current room.

"I'm going Guild."

"Beren?"

"Eventually."

Napkin at him.

Caught.

Road reflex.

"Why?"

"Principle."

"No."

"Next one too?"

Surprise.

"Yes."

"All right."

Then left.

Again.

Not road yet.

Just cookroom door.

Still irritating.

Paused at my door.

"Road?"

He pointed at half-packed field pack.

Fresh socks still inside.

Everything else returned.

"No."

"Mara?"

"Yes."

Nod.

No mockery.

"Eggs."

"Jorren ate them."

"I know."

"Guild."
