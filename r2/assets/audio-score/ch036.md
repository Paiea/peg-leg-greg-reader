Noll's fourth trial night made Lower Rook too quiet before it made him employed.

I discovered this at sixth bell with a book open to the same page long enough to qualify as a relationship.

Nothing through wall.

No pan.

No chair.

No argument with clothing.

No door opening because some object in my room had become temporarily communal.

Just plaster.

I read first paragraph again.

Page remained innocent.

Mara due in two days.

Maybe three.

I had checked Vey desk once that morning.

Only once.

Restraint of this magnitude deserved institutional recognition.

Unavailable.

Clerk said no Halden packet.

I had not returned.

Yet.

Instead now listening to wall because apparently staying in Carrow had created hobbies.

Book closed.

Brass Spoon across East Market.

It had food.

Both facts useful.

Coat on.

This was not checking on Noll.

Dinner lived there.

Noll happened to work where dinner lived.

Completely different activity.

The Brass Spoon looked more expensive at night.

Lamps in both windows.

Clean glass.

Painted spoon over door, once gold, now mostly ambition.

Every front table occupied.

Register woman looked at me.

"One?"

"Apparently."

Smallest table in room.

Immediately understood why available.

One leg short.

Menu scrap under it.

Current engineering.

Kitchen hatch beyond side counter.

Heat first.

Then voices.

Then Noll carrying three bowls at once.

Almost called name.

Did not.

He moved past hatch before seeing me.

Useful.

Gave me several seconds to realize I had never watched him work somewhere he did not live.

At Lower Rook, Noll occupied space like rent included collisions.

Doors with hips.

Knives wherever cutting ended.

Words while carrying hot things.

Swearing at furniture with no expectation of reform.

Here?

He turned before corners.

Not elegant.

Enough.

Server behind him with plates.

Half-step without looking.

Plates missed shoulder.

Bowls on pass.

Towel.

Middle rim wiped.

All three out.

Then hatch.

Saw me.

Stopped half a breath.

Suspicious.

I raised hand.

He looked away.

Good reunion.

Woman to table.

Dark hair pinned high enough to survive steam. Sleeves rolled. Thin burn scar along forearm.

"You Greg?"

Noll had betrayed me before appetizers.

"Depends who is asking."

"Vessa."

There.

Current Vessa younger than whatever later memory tried to supply. Less interested in my uncertainty.

"He said you eat."

"Frequently."

"What?"

Board.

Stew.

Fish.

Dumplings.

Prices designed by people who believed sitting indoors was luxury service.

"Dumplings."

Vessa looked kitchenward.

"Of course."

"Bad?"

"For him, maybe."

Gone before explanation.

Noll looked through hatch again.

I smiled.

He mouthed something.

Too far.

I chose welcome, dear friend.

A spoon hit kitchen wall.

Metal. Plaster. Hard.

Nobody ducked.

Broad man near stove, one hand empty, expression innocent.

Vessa turned.

"Domm."

"Slipped."

"Keep next one in your hand."

Domm looked at spoon on floor.

"That one isn't."

"Find it."

He found it.

Noll did not turn.

That got me.

Not spoon.

Noll.

At home he would have commented before sound finished crossing wall.

Here he was cutting something green while another cook reached for salt.

Different room.

Different Noll.

Same eyebrows when too much salt disappeared.

Better.

Dumplings arrived in broth.

Six.

Folds tighter than home trial. Still not identical.

One leaned left.

I ate that one first out of solidarity.

Wrapper thinner.

Pork. Cabbage. Onion. Vinegar sharp at end.

Good.

Very good.

Do not look at hatch immediately.

Pathetic.

Wait until second dumpling.

Noll was looking.

One nod.

His mouth moved.

Small.

Then Vessa shoved tray at him and he disappeared.

Rush thickened.

Three new tables.

Two left.

Five came in.

Someone complained fish had bones.

Server stared long enough that I respected her professionally.

Kitchen in pieces.

Noll missed a call once. Vessa repeated louder. He answered.

One wrapper dropped. Domm stepped on it. Noll called him bastard without slowing.

Pan handle burned side of finger.

Swear.

Cold water.

Back to board.

I shifted in chair.

Not standing.

Close.

Nobody else moved.

Vessa looked once.

"Skin?"

"Fine."

"Then don't grab that handle again."

"Excellent plan."

Working.

I sat back.

Friendship had become physically inconvenient from across dining room.

Finish dumplings.

Beer.

Charged before delivery.

Trust remained expensive.

Late bell.

Four tables.

Kitchen volume changed.

Same people. More words between movements.

Noll told Domm sauce looked diseased.

Domm threw towel.

Noll caught it.

Progress in ammunition safety.

Vessa brought bill.

"Does chair have separate line?"

"Should."

"Table too?"

Hand against wobbling edge.

Menu scrap shifted.

"Not that table."

Justice.

Paid full anyway.

Vessa took coins.

"He says you gave him pork money."

Hatch.

Noll cleaning board with exaggerated concentration.

"Loaned."

"Two copper."

"Correct."

"He has it."

"Wonderful."

"He keeps asking when to give it back."

"Preferably before one of us dies."

Expression unchanged.

"You two related?"

"No."

"Unfortunate."

Gone.

Still deciding insult when Noll came out.

Hair damp. Apron stained three directions. Flour on cuff. Burned finger pink, nothing more.

He sat.

Reached pocket.

Two copper hit table.

One rolled toward short leg and stopped at menu scrap.

"You could have done this before I paid twelve copper for dinner."

"No."

"Why?"

"Funny."

Fair.

Coins into purse.

Tavin's debt had bought pork, turned into dumplings, gone to Brass Spoon, returned after charging me admission.

I would explain none of this to Tavin unless legally compelled.

Noll leaned back.

"Well?"

"Expensive."

"Dumplings."

"Also expensive."

Kick under table.

"Very good."

Corner of his mouth failed to stay still.

"Fold holds better."

"Vessa."

"Vinegar too?"

"Vessa."

"Did you contribute anything?"

"Pork."

"My pork."

"Paid back."

Right.

Noll looked toward kitchen.

"She keeping you?"

"Don't know."

Fourth night.

He can count. Evidence mixed.

"Domm says I open wrappers when pot gets crowded."

"Do you?"

"Three tonight."

"Out of?"

"Forty-eight."

My brain began percentage.

Noll pointed.

"No."

Stop.

He had learned dangerous skills.

"You like it?"

Short consideration.

"Yes."

There.

No joke around it.

Hours bad.

Hot fat smell.

Pay better.

If she keeps him, five nights.

"You want five?"

Face shift. Same one from pork question.

"Four."

Why four?

Maud gives carter supper fifth night.

Brass Spoon pays more.

Enough more that fifth shift is not sentimental rounding error.

"Then why keep carters?"

Frown.

"Because I live there."

Vessa arrived.

Noll sat straighter.

Barely.

"Four."

Blink.

"What?"

"Four nights. Same days after this week. Fifth if I ask before noon and you want it."

"You said five."

"You said four."

"I said I wanted four."

"Yes."

He looked offended by effectiveness of communication.

Trial rate another week. Then two copper more each night if wrappers stop opening.

"Three tonight."

"Four."

"No."

Kitchen.

Domm hand up.

"Four."

"Traitor."

"I counted."

"First time all night."

Two copper more if three or fewer.

Negotiation over failures.

Noll somehow tried to move number down and briefly argued it up.

Vessa waited him out.

"Fine. Three."

Hands.

Done.

No paper.

No Guild mark.

No ceremony except grease on both hands.

Vessa looked at me.

"You taking him home?"

"He knows where it is."

"Good."

Gone.

Noll watched her.

Then me.

"Four."

I smiled.

"Four."

He tried not to.

Failed worse.

We left after chairs went upside down.

Noll carried paper parcel.

Bones.

For broth.

At Lower Rook.

Where else?

Cold streets home.

River turn.

"Mara tomorrow?"

"Maybe."

His face intolerable.

"Actual maybe. River."

"Still maybe."

"You have become very wise since employment."

"Four nights."

"Yes, Noll."

At Lower Rook bones went downstairs first.

Cold box.

Tomorrow's broth.

Then stairs.

My door.

"You going Vey in morning?"

"Why?"

"You went today."

Once.

"Twice yesterday."

How know?

I told him.

Right.

Self-betrayal.

"Maybe."

Noll unlocked door.

"Dangerous."

"Go to bed."

Door closed.

Chair scrape once.

Then nothing.

Four nights a week, that silence would arrive late now.

I stood in my room with two copper back in my purse and listened to it for a while...
