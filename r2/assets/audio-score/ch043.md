I wrote the date down because otherwise I could pretend I had never said it.

Firstday of Spring, Year 313.

Cheap paper drank ink around the edges.

I stared.

Then underneath:

**One year from today, I am going to stand at the Black Stair.**

Noll read it upside down from second chair.

He had entered while I was still deciding whether sentence needed probably.

It did not.

Onion smell.

Chewing.

"Black Stair."

"Yes."

"Far."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Rude question.

I had prepared for danger.

Expense.

Guild regulations.

Monsters.

Winter roads.

Old magic.

Death, potentially.

Not why.

"I want to go."

Noll waited.

Apparently passed.

"Okay."

Another bite.

I looked at him.

"That's all?"

"You wrote it."

Most people would point out Bronze.

"You know."

No party.

"You know."

Westreach does not usually take random nineteen-year-olds with sword and unreliable memory.

Noll swallowed.

"Do you know?"

"I assume."

"Then ask."

Annoying.

Useful.

Fold once. Coat.

Room had changed in ways too small to notice until leaving.

Two chairs.

Tavin's letter beneath burned seven.

Mara's old note under room key.

Field pack open beside bed because I had not decided whether wanting road meant keeping it ready.

Outside, spring rain stopped before dawn.

Street still held it.

Water in ruts. Roof drips. Woman beating blanket over railing and cursing weather like spring arrived specifically to inconvenience laundry.

Noll followed downstairs.

"Work?"

"Guild."

"Same."

"Cruel."

Maud stopped me front room.

"You eating?"

"Guild has food."

"Guild has lies."

Bread heel into hand anyway.

Ate walking.

Carrow on Firstday of Spring did not look transformed.

Apparently city missed ceremonial notice.

Gray sky. Wet stone. Carts. People stepping around puddles with personal resentment.

Guild front board had changed.

Enough.

Winter notices stripped. Fresh postings beneath painted green stripe.

Road season.

I stopped outside.

One large sheet behind glass.

**WESTREACH EXPEDITION, YEAR 313**

There.

A full year away and already taking wall space.

Not recruitment exactly.

Information.

Field applications open.

Sponsor declarations late autumn.

Skills registry review winter.

Final crew selection before departure.

Departure late winter.

Target arrival Black Stair, Firstday of Spring.

My stomach tightened.

Not because impossible fact.

Because date on my paper existed outside my room.

Somebody else printed it first.

Inside.

Sella mug, route tokens.

Look at face.

Then Westreach notice through door.

"No."

"I didn't ask yet."

"Still no."

Excellent service.

Paper on desk.

She read date.

Sentence.

Expression shifted.

Professional evaluation.

Worse than amusement.

"You want a Westreach slot."

"I want to stand at the Black Stair."

"Same road."

"Not necessarily same job."

"Almost certainly same expedition unless you plan crossing western passes alone in winter."

I do not.

Good.

What require?

"Depends what you are."

"Tall."

"Already overrepresented."

Bronze.

"Currently."

There.

One word hit too hard.

Usually Silver minimum adventuring roster. Gold leads some sections. Bronze gets on through trade, animal handling, freight, medicine, mapping, specialist craft, sponsor labor, or somebody deciding they're worth problem.

Specialist magic?

Possible.

Ward work?

Possible.

Force?

"What force?"

Fair.

Current magic category understood better by fifty-nine-year-old memory than nineteen-year-old execution.

Small support. Load redirection. Some sensing.

Documented?

Not much.

"Then not much."

Clean.

Blank Westreach interest card.

Not application.

Interest.

Distinction offended me.

"This puts your name in seasonal registry. It does nothing else."

Cost?

One copper.

For paper?

"You can make your own expedition."

Paid.

Sella wrote `GREG`.

Looked unimpressive in black ink.

"What should I do?"

"Work."

Specific.

Get field marks from people not friends. Take roads. Take jobs where somebody can say what I did. Stop treating sword like private evidence. If I want Silver, ask what Silver needs instead of imagining it. If weird magic real, make useful where somebody else owns problem.

That last one landed.

Either she had become wiser or more targeted.

Timeline?

Four lines on Westreach sheet.

D274 candidate office wants real path. Rank, sponsor, trade, whatever.

D304 crew trials.

D316 final freight and role commitments.

D326 west gate departure, weather permitting.

Four dates copied to back of my paper.

One-year goal acquired smaller teeth.

Sella watched.

"Writing dates does not do them."

"I suspected."

"Good."

Again.

Fold paper.

"Contract board?"

Point.

Road jobs on right.

Eyes went there first this time.

Progress perhaps.

Most excluded me fast.

Silver escort north.

River patrol needing endorsement.

Five-day freight run wants prior mule experience.

Mapping crew wants ward reader, Bronze accepted, seven days, and I have no idea whether sensing qualifies.

Then left side.

**STONEHOOK FREIGHT, WEST DISTRICT**

**DAY LABOR / LOAD WARD ASSIST / THREE DAYS POSSIBLE**

**BRONZE ACCEPTED**

Pay ordinary.

Work ugly.

Exactly what Sella meant.

Stonehook?

River machines. Kiln clay. Metal fittings. Crane yard.

Danger?

"Everything with six men and a suspended crate is danger if they get creative."

Ward assist?

Old reader sick. Rigger tells me what not to touch.

Good enough.

Token.

Sella wrote `Bronze / sensing`.

Pause.

Then `sword` smaller.

I noticed.

She noticed me noticing.

"Don't become unbearable."

"Too late."

Stonehook west of fish market where streets widened for freight and every building looked hit by wagon at least once.

Yard announced itself by sound.

Chain.

Timber.

Hammer on iron pin.

Men shouting numbers.

Something heavy hitting ground hard enough pigeons left three roofs.

Open gate.

Woman gray scarf, short mallet, looked up from hoist base.

"Guild?"

Token.

"Greg."

"Mira."

Read Sella notes.

Snort.

"Sensing. Sword. Which one lifts?"

"Neither, mostly."

"Then hands."

Wheel chocks under shed.

Move those. Come back.

I moved chocks.

Something satisfying about ambitious one-year expedition beginning with carrying wood twenty feet.

Did not say it.

Sounded like chapter ending and barely morning.

Stonehook did not care about narrative structure.

By midday, three lessons.

River clay gets heavy when wet.

Every crane crew thinks every other crane crew ties bad knots.

Mira owns yard whenever something hangs above person's head.

Not wardwright.

Rigger with twenty years of not dropping things often enough to stay employed.

Counts.

My job: watch old bronze load plates by hoist heads. Tell her whether charge stayed even while test weights moved.

Magic simple.

Simple not easy.

Each plate low field across pulley supports. Did not hold load. Warned when tension relation shifted.

Old Greg remembered systems that actively compensated.

These did not.

Current Stonehook used cheap reliable warning because cheap reliable survived accountants.

North crane plate pulsed when empty hook swung in wind.

"Bad?"

Watch again.

"Maybe too sensitive."

"Maybe?"

"Empty hook isn't enough evidence."

"Better answer."

Apparently she attended same school as everyone else in Carrow.

Half-weight test crate with broken brick.

Tag line in my hand.

Crane takes strain.

Plate pale green.

Steady.

Mira pushes crate sideways with pole.

Amber flash.

Green.

"Reads lateral."

"Supposed to."

"Then maybe fine."

"Maybe again."

Ignore.

By second bell, shoulders hurt from rope and boots had clay in seams.

Mira signed token for one day.

One field mark.

Tiny.

Real.

"Tomorrow?"

Look at yard. Crew securing crate taller than me beneath south hoist.

"Yes."

"First bell. Eat before."

On walk home, paper out once.

Not for date.

Back now had four deadlines. Front one sentence.

One year from today.

Black Stair still impossibly far.

Stonehook ink on Guild token.

Smaller than dream.

Heavier than one.

Lower Rook.

Noll in second chair again.

"Work?"

"Yes."

"Black Stair?"

"Not today."

Nod.

"Tomorrow?"

"Also probably not."

"Slow."

"Extremely."
