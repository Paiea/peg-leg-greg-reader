By fourth morning, Stonehook had taught my palms where rope lived.

Two small blisters flattened into sore pink skin. Shoulders hurt in exact places Old Greg's body had stopped complaining about decades later. Clay had worked permanently into welt around right boot.

Mira had signed three day marks on Guild token.

Not endorsements.

Marks.

I learned distinction because I asked.

"An endorsement means I would put my name next to yours when something expensive is hanging."

"And three marks?"

"Means you showed up three days."

"Glowing praise."

"Show up thirty."

So I came back.

Spring changed too.

Rain stopped. Morning light reached west yard before first bell, weak but real. Fish market worse in warmth. River cart lost winter canvas. Boy outside Stonehook sold lemon water instead of hot root tea and looked betrayed by inventory.

Inside, south hoist already wrong.

I knew because Mira was standing under nothing.

She never stood under live hoist line unless hook down and tied.

Today hook rested on paving beside boots.

Bronze load plate above glowed faintly despite no load.

"Morning."

"Look."

Look.

Weak amber shimmer at lower edge.

Three days ago I would have reached for magic immediately.

Today mechanical first.

Progress came embarrassingly fast when lesson involved not being crushed.

"Hook line has tension?"

Only own weight.

Wind? None.

Started overnight?

Night crew wrote it at fourth bell. Flickered twice lowering empty rig. Tied off. No load since.

Plate could be bad.

Could.

Bracket moving.

Could.

Line running wrong through sheave.

Mira waited.

"I hate this game."

"Good."

Field did not feel damaged.

Pulled.

Slightly.

Left.

"Why backed?"

Better question.

Locking wedge still present. Seat showed bright metal where repeated motion polished edge.

"Something is walking housing sideways."

Load?

Maybe line alignment.

Mira looks yard floor.

South hoist modified last year with longer swing arm.

Old structure carrying new geometry.

Familiar problem in every century.

Down.

Cost money.

Three kiln-clay crates waiting.

Mira: wait.

North hoist booked.

"Guild says ward?"

"Ward is reporting real movement."

"Can you fix?"

"I can tell you it isn't lying."

Carpenter and ironworker.

Cover off.

Real cause after another hour: extended swing arm pulled sheave housing off-axis when load moved beyond certain yard angle. Pin not defective. Modification changed path enough every heavy lateral swing worried it loose a fraction.

"New bracket."

"Her hoist."
