Thirteen days gave me a new scar on my left thigh and Jorren a desk.

Mine came from a thorncat outside North Edge Waystation.

His came from Meln deciding anyone who could count sacks without stealing deserved to sit occasionally.

I considered my progression more glamorous until I saw the desk.

It had a drawer.

"You have a drawer."

Jorren looked up from route sheets.

"Yes."

"What do you keep in it?"

"Things."

Not answer.

Drawer open.

String.

Two pencils.

Knife.

Half dried apple.

One sock.

"Why sock?"

"Dry."

Drawer closed.

"I expected institutional secrets."

"Sock is secret."

Current Jorren had spent almost two weeks getting better at factor work while I took three Guild contracts, one road, and two days limping after thorncat clawed through trousers.

Wound shallow.

Scar impressive because bodies enjoyed poor proportionality.

Another blade note from North Edge lead:

`FAST. DOES NOT FREEZE. NEEDS TO STOP CHASING AFTER FIRST SAFE LINE.`

I objected because I chased exactly nine steps.

Sella asked how many ordered.

Zero.

Objection died.

New boots stopped feeling new.

Heel caps road-scratched. Leather darkened from rain. I no longer noticed every time I looked down.

Maybe clearest sign time had passed.

Objects stopped announcing themselves.

Today I was not at Meln's yard for furniture.

Waiting for Venn freight train from South Latch.

Tavin's letter arrived eight days ago.

Started:

**Your memory letter was terrible. Better.**

Then corrected another thing.

I had not missed his send-off.

I had been late.

Drunk later.

Apparently cried after three beers and tried to challenge someone twice my size because nineteen-year-old Greg had been committed to making every social situation expensive.

No memory returned.

Fact settled anyway.

I had come.

Late was different from absent.

Then Tavin wrote South Latch freight line might put him in Carrow around D041 if weather and axle gods allowed.

I checked date seven times.

Not obsessively.

Calendar discipline.

Jorren had heard enough.

"He'll come."

"Maybe."

"You say maybe when you want yes."

"You own a sock drawer. Your judgment is compromised."

Yard bell.

Three wagons entered East Row.

Blue-green Venn canvas.

My body stood before decision.

Jorren smiled.

"Go."

"You're coming."

"Working."

There.

"Later?"

"Later."

I left him at desk.

Venn wagons into receiving lane under ordinary noise.

Drivers shouting. Clerks waving flags. Barrel cart complaining Venn blocked turn.

Scan faces.

First wagon unfamiliar.

Second.

Third.

Someone climbed down.

Lean.

Brown coat too large at shoulder.

Red scarf stained seal wax.

Hair shorter than version my mind had failed to invent.

He landed, slipped in grain dust, windmilled once, recovered, immediately checked if anyone noticed.

I noticed.

He saw me.

Recognition did not arrive like memory.

It arrived like my body had been waiting for a shape.

Broken nose slightly left.

Scar through eyebrow.

Eyes that knew me before I knew what to do with them.

Tavin stared.

"You got tall."

I laughed.

"You didn't."

Grin.

Cross lane.

I expected joke.

Punch to shoulder.

"Ow."

"Good."

Then hug.

Hard.

No warning.

Arms came up automatically.

For a moment yard disappeared behind wool, smoke, road smell.

No missing memory filled in.

Did not need one.

Tavin released first.

Looked me over.

"You look terrible."

"I own new boots."

"Worse."

"You smell like wax."

"Fuck wax."

Good.

Woman shouted from wagon.

"Tavin! South manifest!"

Eyes close.

"Work."

"Go."

Finger at me.

"Don't leave."

Words hit harder than intended.

"I'm not."

"Good."

He ran back.

I stayed because no job and because leaving would be insane.

Then yard gave me one.

Receiving clerk recognized me from Meln jobs and shoved chalk board into hands.

"You free?"

"Technically."

"Count Venn eleven through seventeen. Rami's sick."

"Pay?"

Half day.

Six copper.

I took chalk.

Tavin watched from wagon and laughed.

"Of course you're working."

"Apparently."

For next hour we occupied same yard separately.

That was stranger than reunion.

Tavin knew freight.

Not vague future-memory knowledge.

Current hands.

Seal numbers. Canvas corners. Cracked strap. Water damage argument. Moving through crew without waiting for me to validate any of it.

I counted packages.

Eleven.

Twelve.

Thirteen.

At fifteen, crate mark did not match board.

Call clerk.

Tavin heard.

"South copy wrong."

"How know?"

"Because I wrote it wrong."

"Excellent system."

"I crossed it out."

Manifest correction existed.

Our copy made before correction.

Current logistics defeated blame with timing.

Then wagon two moved.

Nobody meant it to.

Rear wheel chock cracked.

Sharp wood snap.

Wheel rolled.

Driver shouted.

Slight slope.

Heavy barrels.

Six inches.

A foot.

Loader between rear wheel and stacking platform.

Tavin jumped from cart bed.

I dropped chalk.

No magic first.

Spare chock three steps away.

Kick toward wheel.

Miss by inches.

Tavin catches with boot, shoves down.

Wheel hits wood.

Chock skids.

Not enough.

"Brake!"

Driver hauls lever.

Catches late.

Wagon jolts.

Top barrel shifts against rail.

Rope snaps.

Barrel rolls off.

Heavy.

Tavin under line.

Now magic.

Force plane without deciding whether Stonehook taught it or memory did.

Angled.

Not stop.

Barrel hits.

Mana buckles sideways.

Wrists hold better than D004.

Still not enough to arrest.

Enough to change direction.

Barrel into empty pallet instead of Tavin's knees.

Wood breaks.

Barrel does not.

Tavin freezes half heartbeat.

Then back.

Wagon stops.

People move.

Secondary chocks. Barrels secured.

Tavin comes toward me.

Expected thanks.

Punches same shoulder.

"Stop doing that."

"What the fuck was that?"

"Force."

"You can do that?"

"Some."

Broken pallet.

Bronze tab.

Me.

"Your letter omitted useful information."

"Your letter omitted eyebrow scar."

Fingers touch it automatically.

"You don't remember?"

"No."

Expression changes.

Not pity.

Quieter.

"Fence."

"What?"

"You always said fence when you didn't want to explain scars. That one's actually fence. Fell drunk climbing Sorn's yard wall."

Laugh.

Tension breaks.

"You are a terrible historian."

"Accurate historian."

"Why were you climbing?"

"You bet me."

Of course.

Freight lead shouts Tavin again.

He turns.

"Later."

"Later."

This time word feels less like uncertainty.

Incident costs two hours.

Broken chock replaced. Barrel inspected. Pallet charged pending argument. Chalk board recovered from mud.

I finish count.

Tavin finishes unload.

Work after adrenaline.

Third bell, released until morning.

Walk toward Lower Rook carrying small travel bag between us because strap tore and he refused porter.

"Two chairs?"

"Yes."

"Your letter was real?"

"Most of it."

"Mara?"

"West."

"Still?"

"Halden."

"You and her?"

"Seeing each other."

Tavin stopped in middle of street.

Cart driver cursed.

We moved.

"Again?"

My stomach tightened.

"Again?"

His face changed.

"Shit."

"Tavin."

"You really don't remember that part."

"No."

Spring shower. Awning. People moving around us.

"What happened?"

Face rub.

"You liked her. She liked you. You fought. You tried to decide something for her. She decided something without you. Both complained to me separately until I considered murder."

"Specific."

"That's all I know."

"Did we sleep together?"

His eyes widen.

Then laughter hard enough he leans on wall.

"What?"

"Finally."

"Finally what?"

"You asked a normal fucking question."

"Answer."

"No idea."

I shoved shoulder.

Almost dropped bag.

"Careful. Freight professional."

"Fraud."

Lower Rook.

Maud looks at Tavin.

"No."

He stops.

"I haven't done anything."

"You look like trouble."

"Greg lives here."

"Exactly."

Laugh.

Noll through side door in blue apron.

Sees Tavin.

Stops.

This mattered more than whether I remembered.

"Tavin."

"Noll."

Tavin grins.

Noll punches chest.

Then hug.

Current relationships existed where I had holes.

Pain real.

Relief real too.

I stood there holding half Tavin's bag like idiot.

Noll looked at me.

"Food."

Of course.

Upstairs.

Three people. Two chairs. One bed.

Tavin steals from my bowl.

Noll steals from his.

Justice.

Later Jorren arrives after factor shift because Noll sent runner without asking.

Jorren and Tavin insult coats five minutes before cards appear.

Nobody asks me to mediate their history.

At midnight Maud hits ceiling with broom.

Voices lower.

Tavin sleeps floor, coat as pillow.

I lie awake listening to breathing.

Forty-one days into year, Black Stair felt farther than D001.

Not because I was failing.

Because current life was getting heavier.

I had not expected progress to make leaving harder...
