# Chapter 13: The Good Sword

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Batch rehearsal: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/011-013-story-batch.md`

---

The sword was still there.

This felt personal.

Same smith.

Same rack.

Same plain leather grip and narrow guard.

Nothing decorative enough to justify the price.

I picked it up.

The balance point sat where it should.

Not where my old cheap sword had taught my wrist to expect it.

I raised the blade.

Overhead.

Stopped before the cut.

The smith watched me from behind the bench.

"You going to buy it this time?"

"I'm evaluating whether it became cheaper."

"It didn't."

Reliable man.

I made the cut.

The blade moved through the line without pulling my shoulder forward.

Recovery came back cleaner too.

Not clean.

Cleaner.

That distinction cost money.

I tried again.

Same result.

My old sword had forced small corrections into every repetition. Grip pressure. Wrist angle. Shoulder tension. The errors had become familiar enough that I no longer noticed all of them until the better tool removed the argument.

I hated useful evidence.

"How much?"

The smith told me the same number.

I counted my road pay.

Then counted what remained if I bought it.

Food.

Room.

Some yard time.

No proper run of support lessons unless I found somebody charitable, stupid, or both.

Nothing meaningful for Arlo's next production problem.

Less reserve than I had wanted after finally learning what reserve felt like.

Mara left in three days.

The sword had nothing to do with Mara.

My brain tried to put them on the same ledger anyway.

I stopped it.

Different systems.

I bought the sword.

The smith wrapped the old one without asking whether I wanted to trade it.

"You keep bad tools?"

"Apparently."

He shrugged.

Maybe he did not understand that an object could become evidence simply by disappointing you long enough.

I carried both to the Guild.

Sella looked at the new sword.

Then my face.

"You spent the road money."

"Some."

"Most?"

I put my token on the counter.

"Do you have work?"

She looked at the sword again.

That answered her question.

The work arrived before she found a polite way to judge me.

A runner came through the front door hard enough to hit the frame with one shoulder.

Tannery district.

Animal loose in a storage yard.

Two workers hurt.

City watch had the street blocked, but the thing had gone through one fence already and the next yard kept horses.

Sella was moving before the runner finished.

She pulled three contract slips from beneath the desk.

"Bronze response. Immediate."

I took one.

The new sword felt heavier on my belt because money had weight too.

Two other Bronze fighters met me outside.

One was a woman named Edda who carried a short spear and wore boiled leather dark with old stains. The other was a younger man I had seen in the yard but never spoken to.

He had a sword longer than mine and the expression of somebody delighted to finally need it.

Bad combination.

We ran.

The tannery district announced itself two streets early.

Wet hides.

Lime.

Smoke.

Rendered fat.

I had smelled worse things.

Few had worked harder.

The city watch had closed a lane between two warehouses. Workers stood behind a cart barricade pointing toward a fenced yard full of stacked hides, barrels, and low timber sheds.

Something hit the inside of the fence.

Wood shook.

The young Bronze smiled.

Edda did not.

I liked Edda more.

"What is it?"

"Hooktail," one watchman said.

I knew hooktails.

Sort of.

Common scavenger outside towns. Dog-sized when young, larger when fed well. Broad shoulders. Long rear tail ending in a hard curved spur used for digging roots, opening carcasses, and convincing people not to stand behind them.

This one had apparently arrived in a hide shipment half-starved, escaped when the workers opened the cart, and discovered a district containing enough animal smell to destroy its remaining judgment.

Not evil.

Not magical.

Hungry and terrified.

The two injured workers had tried to corner it with poles.

Excellent plan if the objective was identifying the workers most likely to be injured.

Edda asked where it was now.

A watchman pointed toward the far shed.

It had gone inside five minutes ago.

One door.

One broken wall panel.

Next yard beyond that kept six draft horses.

There was our clock.

Edda looked at us.

"I push it out. You two keep the lane. If it turns, don't chase the tail."

The young Bronze nodded immediately.

I did too.

Edda noticed the difference.

"You've seen one?"

"Later. Larger."

She stared at me.

Right.

"Once."

Better.

We entered the yard.

Workers had left tools everywhere when they ran.

Reasonable.

Bad footing.

Hide racks narrowed the center into lanes. Barrels blocked the left wall. The shed sat at the far end, door hanging open.

I drew the new sword.

No drag at the wrist.

Nice.

Not relevant yet.

Edda took the left side with her spear low.

The young Bronze took right.

I stayed center, several steps back.

No formation discussed.

Still a formation.

Edda reached the shed.

Tapped the spear shaft against the doorway.

Nothing.

Again.

A low scraping came from inside.

Then breathing.

Fast.

The hooktail came out sideways.

Not charging us.

Trying to leave.

Gray-brown hide. Shoulders near my waist. Long body. Tail curved behind it like a farmer's hook made of bone.

It saw three armed people and changed its mind too late.

The young Bronze stepped forward.

Wrong.

Edda shouted, "Hold!"

He heard the word.

His body had already committed.

The hooktail spun.

Tail first.

The spur crossed the space where his thigh had been.

He jumped back badly and hit a hide rack.

The rack tilted.

I caught it with my left hand.

Several wet hides slid off anyway and landed at my feet.

The hooktail bolted toward the broken wall panel.

Horse yard.

I moved to cut it off.

Not attack.

Position.

The new sword stayed close to my side without fighting me.

The animal lowered its head.

I had one second to decide whether it would turn.

It did not.

Fine.

Side step.

Small.

The hooktail passed close enough that I smelled blood under the tannery stink.

Old cut along its flank.

Shipment injury.

It was not having a good day either.

The tail followed.

I expected it.

That mattered.

I brought the sword down across the hard curve before the spur reached me.

Not overhead.

Short diagonal.

Steel hit bone.

The new blade stopped where I told it.

The impact kicked the tail lower.

The spur carved dirt instead of my knee.

Clean.

For half a heartbeat I admired the tool.

The hooktail hit me with its shoulder.

I went backward into a barrel.

Air left.

Right.

Still an animal attached to the tail.

Edda's spear struck the ground in front of its face.

Not stabbed.

Blocked.

The hooktail turned away from the point and ran toward the center lane.

Exactly where she wanted it.

The young Bronze had recovered.

He raised his long sword two-handed.

Too much blade for the lane.

I saw the hide rack beside him.

"Low."

He hesitated.

The hooktail came.

"Low!"

He dropped the sword across his body instead of swinging.

The animal hit steel and changed direction.

Ugly use of a sword.

Correct enough.

Edda stepped behind it.

Her spearhead touched the flank.

The hooktail spun again.

Tail swept.

Edda jumped the first pass.

The second came faster.

She could not clear it.

I was already moving.

New sword up.

The spur hit my blade near the upper third.

The old sword would have dragged my wrist outward.

This one did not.

I turned the impact.

Recovery.

Back to center.

For once, the blade arrived before the next problem.

Edda saw the opening.

Her spear drove into the hooktail's shoulder behind the front leg.

Deep.

The animal screamed.

Not heroic sound.

Terrible one.

It twisted against the shaft.

Edda lost the spear.

The hooktail ran with it hanging from the wound.

Toward the broken panel.

Toward horses.

No time to admire anything.

The panel opening was narrow.

One body width.

I got there first.

Not because I was faster than the animal.

Because it had to turn around a stack of barrels.

Present terrain.

I stood in the opening.

The hooktail charged.

Stupid place to stand.

Necessary one.

I raised the sword.

Overhead cut available.

Open enough above me.

Perfect tool.

Perfect line.

The hooktail lowered its head.

And I saw the problem.

If I committed straight down and missed the skull, the body still hit me.

If I killed it cleanly, several hundred pounds still hit me.

Wall thinking.

Different shape.

I stepped out of the opening instead.

The hooktail took the escape.

Not into the horse yard.

I had moved to the inside edge and left the wider lane back toward the street open.

Edda understood immediately.

"Gate!"

The watchmen outside pulled the yard gate wider.

People cleared.

The hooktail ran through.

Across the blocked street.

Straight toward the empty lane leading away from the horses.

Edda grabbed a dropped pole and followed far enough to keep it moving.

The young Bronze looked at me.

"We're letting it go?"

For the moment.

City watch had another barricade at the far end and open slaughter fields beyond that.

Better place to finish the problem.

We followed at a distance.

The hooktail reached the second barricade, found people again, turned toward the open field exactly as hoped, and ran.

Edda stopped us at the street edge.

"Leave it."

The young Bronze looked disappointed.

I did not.

The animal was injured, outside the crowded district, and moving away from people.

A field crew could track it later if the city wanted the carcass or if it circled back.

The immediate problem had changed state.

Done.

We returned to the yard.

One broken rack.

Several dirty hides.

Edda's spear needed repair.

My ribs hurt where the barrel had introduced itself.

The new sword had a pale scrape near the upper third.

Nothing dangerous.

I ran my thumb beside it.

The blade had done exactly what I paid for.

Twice.

It had not stopped me from standing in front of an animal that could have run through me.

That part had been mine.

I put the sword away.

No speech required.

The Guild paid hazard rate because the hooktail had crossed into a second commercial yard.

Sella counted the coins onto the counter.

The night work returned a small portion of what I had spent that morning.

Finance remained insulting.

She looked at the new scrape on the blade.

Then at me.

"Worth it?"

I thought about the tail strike returning to center without pulling my wrist wide.

The clean diagonal.

The moment I almost confused having the right tool with having the right answer.

"Yes."

That was enough.

Back in my room, I counted what remained.

Food.

Rent.

A little yard time.

Not enough for the support instructor I had asked about before leaving with Jorren.

Not anymore.

I could earn more.

Later.

The marked cord sat near the window.

I left it there.

On the table beside it was the brass key Mara had returned.

Three days had become two.

That mattered more tonight...
