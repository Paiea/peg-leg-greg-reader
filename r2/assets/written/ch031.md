# Chapter 31: The Next Road

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Jorren bought dried meat after lunch.

I noticed because I had just paid for lunch and was still emotionally invested in every form of food entering his possession.

We had left Mill Street with Ivena's laughter behind us and two bowls of lentil stew inside us.

Jorren had eaten most of the bread too.

Then he stopped at a provision stall near South Market and pointed at a row of smoked strips hanging from hooks.

"Half pound."

The seller wrapped it in paper.

Jorren added oat cakes.

Then wax.

Then a small packet of salt.

I looked at the pile.

"You planning to survive winter before dinner?"

"Road."

One word.

Too casual.

I looked at him.

He was counting copper into the seller's hand.

"What road?"

"East road."

"When?"

"Today."

The seller handed him change.

Jorren checked it once.

I stood there with my hands empty and the very specific sensation that somebody had moved a stair while I was using it.

"Today."

"Yes."

"As in later today."

"That's usually how today works."

I disliked him immediately.

The provision seller looked between us and decided another customer deserved more attention.

Reasonable woman.

Jorren tucked the food into his pack.

New shoulder strap.

Oil still bright where the leather bent around the buckle.

He had bought that yesterday.

I had known he bought it.

I had apparently failed to ask the obvious next question because I had been busy showing him a business.

"You took a contract?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"This morning."

Morning.

While I had been deciding whether the workshop looked impressive enough.

"From Sella?"

"No."

That made it worse in a way I did not immediately enjoy examining.

Jorren shifted the pack onto one shoulder.

"Grain factor from the north run. Vell. He has two millstone wagons going east to Rellin Cross. One of his regular guards broke an ankle yesterday."

"And he found you."

"At the Guild."

"Specifically."

Jorren looked at me.

"That is usually what finding someone means."

I ignored that.

"How long?"

"Four days if the road stays dry. Five if it doesn't."

"Pay?"

He told me.

Decent.

Better with food included.

Not stupid money.

Not insult money either.

A real rate for a man who had already worked with the client and apparently not ruined enough grain to become unemployable.

"Half now?"

"Quarter. Rest at return."

"Why only quarter?"

"Because Vell pays quarter."

"That's not a reason."

"It has worked so far."

I opened my mouth.

Closed it.

The rate was fine.

The route was ordinary.

The client had paid him before.

Jorren knew the work.

Nothing available to attack except my own mood.

We started walking.

Not toward Lower Rook.

Toward East Gate.

I noticed after half a street.

"Your gear?"

"At the Guild stable."

Of course it was.

He had already packed.

That was why the blanket had been folded yesterday morning.

That was why he had only committed to breakfast with Noll.

That was the small hesitation when Noll asked whether he would be there tomorrow.

Current evidence had been sitting in my room wearing boots and I had apparently chosen not to look at it.

Annoying discipline.

"You were going to tell me?"

Jorren looked over.

"I am telling you."

"After you accepted."

"Yes."

There was no defensive tone.

That irritated me more.

"You could have mentioned it yesterday."

"I didn't have it yesterday."

"This morning, then."

"You said Mill Street after midday."

"That did not prohibit sentences before midday."

Jorren stopped walking.

A woman behind us carrying a basket almost ran into him and called him something rude.

He moved aside.

"Greg."

I knew that tone.

Not from memory.

From sparring.

The tone before he asked why I was making a simple thing difficult.

I hated that I had a category for it.

"What?"

"Do you want the job?"

Easy question.

"No."

Too fast.

He waited.

I added, "I have the support assessment coming. The filtration work is here. I just came off five days west."

All true.

"Then what are we arguing about?"

There it was.

I had several answers and disliked all of them.

You came back.

I thought back meant longer.

I thought we might take something together.

I thought I had more time before you became somebody else's expected person again.

None survived contact with my mouth.

I pointed at his right shoulder instead.

"You caught a sack badly two days ago."

Jorren stared at me.

"Three."

"Excellent. Fully healed by mathematics."

"It's sore."

"You winced yesterday."

"I also slept on your floor."

"Noll's blanket was excellent."

"Blanket didn't fix the floor."

We resumed walking.

Apparently the argument was coming with us.

I moved to his right side without meaning to.

His pack sat higher on that shoulder now because of the new strap.

Maybe too high.

"Loosen that one notch."

"No."

"It's pulling across the sore side."

"It was pulling before. This is better."

"Better does not mean good."

"You sound like every instructor you've complained about."

"They were often right."

"You hated them anyway."

"Also often right."

Jorren laughed once.

I did not.

East Gate traffic thickened around us.

Wagons waiting for inspection.

Two drovers arguing about axle width.

A woman carrying a basket of live chickens through a line that had clearly not been designed for chickens.

The road beyond the arch ran pale under weak afternoon sun.

Dry today.

Clouds farther east.

The Guild stable sat just inside the gate wall.

Jorren's pack roll, bedroll, and sword were already stacked beside two larger freight packs.

A man in a green wool cap stood with one foot on a wagon hub checking a list.

Broad shoulders.

Gray beard.

Ink on two fingers.

He saw Jorren and lifted the list.

"There you are."

Not `who is that?`

Not `you made it.`

There you are.

Expected.

Jorren raised one hand.

"Vell."

The grain factor looked at me.

Then at Jorren.

"Second?"

For one half-second my chest did something stupid.

Jorren shook his head.

"Friend."

Friend.

Better category than neighbor.

Worse timing.

Vell nodded once and returned to the list.

No recruitment.

No sudden open slot.

The universe remained cruelly well staffed.

Jorren went to his gear.

I followed because leaving would have looked strange and staying felt worse.

He checked the bedroll ties.

Waterskin.

Food packet.

Knife.

Wax.

Spare cord.

He did not ask me to inspect anything.

I saw three things I would have packed differently.

Two were preference.

One was probably actually worse.

"Your cook tin is outside the roll."

"I know."

"It'll bang."

"I know."

"That will become annoying after an hour."

"Greg."

"What?"

He looked at me.

Then the cook tin.

Then back.

"Do you want me to stay?"

Direct hit.

No setup.

No professional courtesy.

I laughed because my body was a traitor.

"For a cook tin?"

Jorren waited.

I could have lied.

Not dramatically.

A small social lie.

Say no.

Say I was checking the gear.

Say five days east was nothing.

All of those would have been easier.

Mara had made easy answers expensive lately.

"A little," I said.

The words felt ridiculous as soon as they existed.

Jorren's face changed.

Not softened.

Not pity.

Just attention.

I hated that too.

"I came back."

"For two days."

"Yes."

"That's barely back."

"I don't know the minimum."

"Three."

He smiled.

Tiny.

I did not.

"I thought we might do something."

"We did."

I looked at him.

"You ate my lunch and got me laughed at by a ceramic worker."

"Busy schedule."

"I meant road."

There.

Jorren tightened one bedroll strap.

"Then ask me when there's a road."

Simple.

I had not.

I had wanted him to infer the invitation from my existence.

Terrible system.

"You already took this one."

"Yes."

"Without asking me."

He stopped with one hand on the strap.

Not angry yet.

"Greg."

I knew immediately that I had found the wrong sentence.

Too late.

"I'm not saying you needed permission."

"Good."

"I'm saying you could have asked."

"Why?"

"Because we work well together."

"So do me and Beren."

A name I did not know.

Of course.

Jorren continued before I could ask.

"He took a cart south this morning. Vell needed one guard, not two. He asked me. I said yes."

One guard.

There.

The whole tragedy reduced to staffing.

I felt stupid enough to become angry again.

"One Bronze for two millstone wagons?"

"Drivers are armed. Vell has a crossbow. East road is patrolled to Rellin. They want somebody who can stay awake and not invent monsters."

"You invent dogs."

"Dog was real."

"Breakfast thief."

"Also real."

My mouth wanted another argument.

None deserved release.

Jorren picked up the pack.

The sore shoulder dipped once.

I saw it.

He saw me see it.

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

"My face is private."

"Not that one."

He shifted the load until it sat better.

There.

Better.

Without me.

Vell called his name from the wagon.

Jorren lifted one finger.

Then looked back at me.

"Four days. Maybe five."

Eventually.

My chest tried the old thing again.

Roads.

Doors.

People returning with other people's gear.

I knew too much about departures in general and almost nothing about this one.

That was not Jorren's problem.

Unfortunately, it remained mine.

"Try not to catch any millstones," I said.

He smiled.

"Try not to buy a kiln."

"I don't own enough of the business to make that mistake alone."

"Good."

He started toward the wagon.

Then stopped.

Turned back.

"Greg."

"What?"

"Next one, ask."

I stared at him.

He did not explain what he meant.

Friendship remained merciful.

"Fine."

"Terrible answer."

"Go guard rocks."

"Millstones."

"Already shaped rocks."

He climbed onto the rear step of the second wagon.

Vell called something to the driver.

The wagon moved before Jorren had fully settled, because apparently nobody respected emotional timing anymore.

He caught the side rail with one hand.

Good hand.

No flour sack involved.

I stood beside the stable wall while the two wagons rolled toward the gate.

Jorren did not wave.

Neither did I.

The last thing he did before the arch swallowed him was reach down and move the cook tin inside the bedroll straps.

Bastard.

I laughed anyway.

Then the wagon was outside.

Road noise took it.

I walked back to Lower Rook alone.

Not tragically.

Carrow refused that kind of cooperation.

A cooper blocked half the street with hoops.

Someone had spilled cabbage leaves near South Market and three pigeons were fighting over one piece as if winter depended on it.

The woman at the onion stall recognized me.

"Need more?"

"No."

She looked surprised.

Reasonable.

Lower Rook smelled like broth by the time I reached it.

Noll was in the courtyard carrying a bucket toward the cookroom.

He looked behind me.

"Road?"

"Road."

"You going?"

"No."

He nodded.

That was all.

He carried the bucket inside.

I went upstairs.

My room looked larger with nobody on the floor.

One chair.

One bed.

One field pack beside the door.

The folded reply to Tavin still sat under the burned seven.

Two copper on top.

I took the coins off.

Read the half-letter.

**I found your letter late. The freight job is probably gone. I am still in Carrow. I have been out on the road anyway.**

Still true.

I picked up the pen.

For a moment I wanted to add everything.

Jorren came back.

Jorren left again.

I wanted him to ask me first.

I wanted to go and did not want that road.

I had a business that could embarrass me in front of friends.

A man next door who was apparently no longer just a neighbor.

Mara three cities away correcting my letters by existing.

Too much.

Tavin had asked whether I was serious about roads.

I wrote one line.

**You were right that I wanted them. I am less sure now what I mean by that.**

I read it.

Dangerously close to a sentence that knew what it was doing.

I left it anyway.

Then I added:

**I still dispute the two copper.**

Better.

I did not fold the letter.

Did not send it.

Noll dropped something through the wall.

A pan, probably.

Then swore with enough force to identify the object as expensive.

I put the pen down.

The room stayed mine.

Jorren stayed gone.

The onions, unfortunately, stayed too...
