# Chapter 34: The Dark Loft

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Story search: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/034-story-search.md`

---

The warehouse owner wanted us to burn the moths.

This was why people hired adventurers.

Not because adventurers always knew what to do.

Because occasionally the owner had already selected the worst available option and somebody needed legal permission to say no.

"They're called cinderwings," he said.

I looked up at the loft.

I knew.

That was the interesting part.

Twenty, maybe thirty of them clung to the rafters around three cheap mana lamps.

Hand-sized.

Brown-black wings folded flat against pale bodies.

Tiny orange lines pulsed near the joints whenever one of the lamps flickered.

Dry rope hung beneath them.

Canvas rolls beside the rope.

Wooden rafters above all of it.

An excellent place to introduce fire.

The owner held a long pole with a rag wrapped around the end.

Oil-dark rag.

Of course.

"We light this, knock them down, stamp them out."

Jorren looked at me.

I looked at the pole.

"Please don't."

The owner frowned.

"They burn."

"Everything in this room burns."

He considered the rope as if seeing it for the first time.

"Guild says they're a fire hazard."

"Yes."

"So burning them removes the hazard."

There were arguments that could not be improved by additional logic.

Sella's contract sheet had been more useful.

**CINDERWING CLUSTER. DRY-GOODS LOFT. REMOVE WITHOUT STRUCTURAL FIRE.**

The last four words existed because history had happened to someone else first.

Good institution.

I pointed at the lamps.

"Turn those off."

The owner hesitated.

"Then we can't see."

"We have daylight."

Two high shutters on the river side.

Currently closed.

One narrow window over the loading floor.

Also closed.

"Open the shutters. Put the mana lamps out."

"Why?"

Because cinderwings fed on low charge.

Because cheap mana lamps leaked exactly the kind of soft waste they liked.

Because clusters stored it in the wing joints.

Because if you hit thirty charged cinderwings with a burning rag, they dumped everything at once and converted a loft into a lesson.

Later warehouse codes covered this.

Blue shutter paint around lamp lofts.

Ceramic drain cups under fixtures.

Quarterly checks.

No open flame clearance inside dry storage.

Boring future competence.

I had seen three loft fires before people stopped needing to relearn it.

Maybe four.

"They'll be less active in the dark," I said.

True enough.

"And less charged."

Also true.

The owner looked at my Bronze token.

There.

Current rank meeting later experience.

"You done this before?"

Not in this body.

I disliked the question less than I used to.

"Yes."

Clean answer.

Jorren looked at me.

He knew enough not to ask when.

The owner lowered the fire pole.

Progress.

We opened the river shutters first.

Cold daylight cut across the loft.

The cinderwings shifted.

Not flight.

Just wings tightening.

I waited.

No heat flash.

Good.

"Now lamps."

One warehouse worker reached for the nearest switch plate.

I stopped him.

"All three together."

Why?

Because if one stayed lit, the cluster would concentrate around it.

That was useful later.

Not while dry rope sat beneath thirty startled bodies.

Three workers took positions.

One.

Two.

Three.

Dark.

Not actually dark.

Daylight from the shutters made everything gray.

The cinderwings became ordinary brown shapes against timber.

Orange lines faded slowly.

Jorren watched.

"How long?"

"Until the joints stop glowing."

"Very technical."

"I contain multitudes."

Ivena had infected me.

Five minutes.

Maybe seven.

The warehouse owner became impatient at four.

Predictable ecology.

While we waited, we moved the rope coils and canvas out from beneath the cluster.

Actual labor.

Heavy coils.

Dust.

One crate labeled **DRY TEA** that smelled strongly enough of fish to challenge several legal definitions.

Jorren carried it without comment.

Professional restraint.

By the time the floor beneath the rafters was clear, the last orange pulse had faded.

Now bait.

Later crews used purpose-made ceramic draw lamps.

Current warehouse did not own one.

Fine.

I had asked the owner for the cheapest hand lamp he could afford to lose.

He had produced something insulting even by that standard.

Small ceramic cup.

Cracked glaze.

Weak charge stone under the wick plate.

Perfect.

We set it outside the open river shutter on a plank resting across two crates.

Not inside.

Outside.

Away from dry goods.

The owner watched like I was feeding coins to birds.

I opened the charge just enough for a faint blue light.

Then waited.

Nothing happened.

Thirty seconds.

A minute.

Jorren shifted his weight.

The owner inhaled to become a problem.

One cinderwing opened its wings.

Orange line.

Faint.

It lifted.

Not fast.

Lazy spiral toward the brighter charge.

Out through the shutter.

Landed on the brick beside the bait lamp.

Then another.

Then four.

There.

The owner's mouth closed.

Clean win.

No trick.

No present-world exception.

Cinderwings still behaved like cinderwings.

I had known.

That felt good.

I allowed it.

The cluster bled out over ten minutes.

One by one at first.

Then groups.

The outside wall became covered in brown wings around the little blue lamp.

Jorren leaned near the shutter.

"Now what?"

"We move the lamp away from the building. Slowly."

"And they follow?"

"Mostly."

"Mostly."

"Nature dislikes guarantees."

We had a worker carry the plank from below while Jorren and I watched the loft.

The lamp moved ten feet along the loading yard.

Cinderwings lifted and followed.

Another ten.

Followed.

The owner stared.

"Where are you putting them?"

Good question.

Not somebody else's warehouse.

Important.

The river embankment had a strip of stone and damp weeds between two loading lots.

No dry goods.

No wooden sheds.

We placed the lamp there and reduced the charge until it barely glowed.

The cinderwings settled on the stones and weeds.

They would disperse as the charge died.

Probably.

Later city pest crews used ceramic boxes and removed them farther.

We were Bronze adventurers with a terrible lamp.

Scale mattered.

Back in the loft, seven cinderwings remained.

Always did.

Nest material around the lamp brackets.

Dusty silk mixed with black flakes from years of charged soot.

The leftover insects were sluggish now.

No stored glow.

We put on gloves.

Jorren held a sack beneath the first bracket while I scraped the dead nest material free with a wooden paddle.

No metal.

No sparks.

One moth dropped.

Jorren flinched.

It crawled across the sack.

"Dangerous?"

"To your dignity."

He disliked that.

Good.

We cleared all three lamps.

Then checked the rafters.

Second cluster?

No.

Egg cases?

Three.

Those mattered.

Small gray ridges in cracks above the warmest lamp.

We scraped them into a ceramic jar.

The warehouse owner wanted to know if he could sell them.

I looked at him.

"To whom?"

He had no answer.

"Then probably not."

The future contained somebody who bought cinderwing residue for low-grade heat charms.

I remembered that vaguely.

Maybe.

Wrong place to manufacture a market from fog.

We sealed the jar and handed it to the Guild pest clerk per contract.

No artifact business.

No sudden fortune.

Just eggs removed from dry timber.

The final step was the lamps.

All three leaked.

Not dangerously.

Enough to invite the next cluster eventually.

I showed the owner the black charge soot around the fittings.

"Clean these. Better seals if you can afford them. Keep the river shutters open during the day."

He frowned at the cost.

Of course.

"How often?"

"Check monthly until you know how fast the soot returns."

Current best answer.

Not forty-year standard.

He wrote it down.

That mattered more than I expected.

The contract paid four copper each.

Two hours of work.

No injuries.

No fire.

No sword drawn.

Jorren and I walked back toward the Guild with the river wind behind us.

"You really knew that," he said.

"Yes."

No probably.

No correction.

He waited.

I waited too.

"You going to explain?"

"Complicated month."

Jorren groaned.

"Still using that?"

"It has excellent range."

He kicked a loose stone off the road.

"You should charge more when you know things like that."

Interesting.

"We took the posted rate."

"I know."

"You want to renegotiate after?"

"No."

"Then what?"

Jorren thought about it.

"Next time, if Sella says cinderwings and you already know how to clear them in half the time, maybe the Guild should know that before it prices us like two idiots with sacks."

There.

Different kind of progression.

Not stronger magic.

Information with economic value.

I looked back at the warehouse.

No smoke.

Good.

"Maybe."

Jorren smiled.

"That's the face."

Apparently the face had survived future knowledge too.

At the Guild, Sella signed the clearance sheet.

I told her the cluster was removed without fire.

She looked at the line.

Then at me.

"You sound proud."

"I am."

That surprised both of us.

I let it stand...
