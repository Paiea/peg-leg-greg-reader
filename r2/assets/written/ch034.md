# Chapter 34: The Correspondent

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Mara wrote that my stew sounded criminal.

This was the first line.

I appreciated efficient correspondence.

The letter arrived under my door while I was asleep badly.

Not late.

Badly.

Noll had come home after midnight from his first Brass Spoon shift, dropped something against the shared wall, then remained suspiciously silent for long enough that I started listening.

Eventually his boots hit the floor.

Then the bed complained.

Then snoring.

Home had changed schedules without changing volume.

I slept after that.

At dawn, paper scraped beneath the door.

I opened one eye.

Halden stock.

Mara's hand.

No need to stand yet.

I lay there looking at it.

The room was cold.

My left foot was tucked under the other leg beneath the blanket.

Apparently that detail had survived being reported.

I reached down from the bed and missed the letter by three inches.

Second attempt.

Caught it.

Dignity preserved because no one witnessed the first.

The envelope had a Vey mark at the corner and a packet stamp two days older than I expected.

Fast route.

Or fortunate handoff.

I started counting travel time.

Stopped after one calculation because Mara had not sent me a logistics exam.

Progress remained expensive.

I opened it.

**Greg,**

**Your stew sounds criminal. The potato surrendering into the broth does not make it better. Pell says pepper hides bad meat. Pell also eats onions at the desk, so his authority remains limited.**

I smiled.

Pell remained alive.

I still had never met him.

That fact hurt less this time.

I kept reading.

**The cold foot thing is familiar. You used to steal the warm side of the bench behind Mother's stove and insist it was because the lamp was better there. The lamp was on the other side. I did not tell you because you were easier to move if you thought the lie was yours.**

I stopped.

There.

Young Greg.

Cold feet.

A bench behind Mara's mother's stove.

No memory.

The detail did not arrive like a missing puzzle piece clicking into place.

It arrived like someone describing a room I had once rented and forgotten to photograph.

I could picture it anyway.

Not correctly.

Warm boards.

A stove.

Mara younger by several years, perhaps already annoyed.

Me inventing a lamp excuse because admitting cold feet apparently exceeded masculine infrastructure.

I put one foot flat on the floor.

Cold.

Pulled it back under the blanket.

Historical verification complete.

I kept reading.

**Dena is a better name than `the woman who drives Arlo's deliveries.` Please try to keep it.**

I looked at the wall as if Mara could see through it.

Unfair.

**The blue cup remains blue. Mother remains wrong. I am prepared to accept your testimony if this reaches litigation.**

Better.

Then the handwriting changed.

Not dramatically.

Tighter.

Less space between lines.

Work had entered the letter.

**Vey is sending me to Carrow.**

I sat up fully.

There were sentences whose meaning arrived before grammar finished.

Carrow.

Mara.

Here.

I read the line again because the first reading had been mostly body.

**Do not become strange. It is work.**

Too late.

**The east packet numbers are wrong in a way everyone has agreed is someone else's fault. Halden says Carrow handoff. Carrow says river delay. River office says warehouse hold. One of them may even be right. Vey has decided I should go look at the ledgers until somebody becomes embarrassed enough to tell the truth.**

That sounded like Mara's job had found its natural weapon.

**I leave Halden in five days. If the packet behaves, I should reach Carrow two days after that. Maybe three. I have one night definitely. A second only if the office keeps me. Then I go back west.**

I counted.

Of course I counted.

Letter stamp.

Two days already gone.

She left in three.

Arrived in five or six.

Maybe seven if river stupidity earned promotion.

I knew where I was supposed to be during those days.

Nowhere.

That was suddenly a thing.

The next line waited.

**If you are here, I want dinner.**

No `maybe`.

No `if you want.`

If you are here.

I want dinner.

I read it twice.

Then a third time because apparently confidence did not reduce literacy requirements.

My chest felt warm in a room that remained objectively cold.

That was embarrassing.

Also irrelevant.

The letter continued.

**A real dinner. Not a bowl you bought because somebody said the word beef near it. I am willing to pay half if that protects your pride. I am not willing to spend the only evening arguing about whether I am allowed to pay half.**

I laughed.

Then stopped because my imagination had already moved past dinner.

Mara outside Lower Rook.

Mara climbing the rear stairs.

Mara standing in this room again.

The room had not improved enough for that thought.

One chair.

Bed.

Table.

Letters.

Sword.

The same blanket she had sat near last time.

My eyes went to the bed before I could prevent it.

Right.

Nineteen.

Also fifty-nine.

Neither age had apparently solved wanting a woman who had kissed me twice before I could remember either kiss.

I wanted to kiss her.

That was the cleanest version.

Not recover a kiss.

Not verify old intimacy.

Not repair a missing history.

Kiss Mara when she was here.

Current Mara.

Ink-stained thumb probably.

Blue cup still in Halden.

Too many books.

Work enough to make one night definite and a second uncertain.

I looked at the bed again.

My body offered several additional suggestions.

I got up.

Cold floor helped.

Not enough.

Fine.

I was an adult man in a nineteen-year-old body with an attractive woman coming to the city.

Pretending desire had not survived reincarnation would be stranger than having it.

What I did with it remained a later problem involving another person.

That distinction I trusted.

Mostly.

I read the rest.

**Tell me if you will actually be here. Do not say `probably.` I have enough probably in the warehouse ledgers. Send the answer to Halden if you post tomorrow. After that, mark it HOLD CARROW / VEY EAST DESK and they will keep it for me.**

Then:

**Also, I am glad Dena exists. That was a better letter.**

No explanation.

No grade.

Just that.

**Mara**

I sat on the edge of the bed with the sheet in both hands.

There were five or six days before she arrived.

Maybe seven.

Enough time for something to happen.

That was how time worked.

Noll could finish or fail his trial.

Jorren could return.

The filtration venture could decide it needed another argument.

The Guild could offer something.

A road could exist.

Anything could.

Mara had not asked whether my calendar might become interesting.

She had asked if I would be here.

I looked at the field pack beside the door.

Ready to become packed.

I had been proud of that.

Still was.

The pack did not make decisions.

Annoying object.

I found paper.

Not the back of anything important this time.

The first answer came too quickly.

**Yes.**

I stared at it.

Mara had specifically prohibited `probably.`

I appreciated clear requirements.

I added:

**Dinner. I'll be here.**

That was enough.

Probably.

No.

Dangerous word.

I signed my name.

Then sat there with the letter in front of me and felt the shape of the promise after making it.

Small.

Specific.

One evening in Carrow.

Be here.

Dinner.

People made commitments larger than that before breakfast every day.

Some of them even survived.

I folded the paper.

Noll hit the wall once from next door.

Not knocking.

Accidental.

Then his door opened.

Footsteps crossed the hall.

A knock came against my frame.

"Open."

Noll opened it anyway.

He looked terrible.

Hair wrong.

Eyes half awake.

Small red mark across the back of one hand.

His shirt smelled faintly of hot fat from several feet away.

"You alive?" I asked.

"No."

"Good shift?"

He looked at me.

"No."

"Bad shift?"

"No."

Strong report.

He noticed the letter.

"Mara?"

"Yes."

"Dangerous."

"Apparently."

Noll leaned against the frame.

"She coming?"

I looked up.

"How did you get that from one envelope?"

"Face."

My face had apparently lost privacy rights across multiple relationships.

"Five or six days."

Noll nodded.

No grin.

No adolescent noise.

I had expected something worse and was almost disappointed.

"You need room?"

I stared at him.

There it was.

Much worse.

"Go to sleep."

"Just asking."

"No, you're not."

"I can eat downstairs."

"You already do."

"Late."

"Noll."

He smiled.

Tired enough that it barely formed.

Then he saw the folded reply.

"You sending?"

"Yes."

"Today?"

"Yes."

"Good."

He turned away.

I waited for the rest.

Nothing.

"How was Brass Spoon?"

Noll stopped in the hall.

"Hot."

"Useful."

"Domm threw spoon."

I blinked.

"At you?"

"Wall."

"Why?"

"Lost count."

That opened several questions.

Noll had already closed his door.

Apparently his outside life had acquired flying utensils while I slept.

I put Mara's letter beside the burned seven.

The card now had no coins on it.

Tavin's reply was gone too.

The table looked emptier.

Mara's new letter took their place badly.

I got dressed.

The packet office opened at first bell.

I arrived before the clerk had finished tying the Halden sack.

Different clerk.

Definitely.

Probably.

I handed over the letter.

"Halden. Vey office."

She weighed it.

One copper.

I paid.

No debate.

No revision at the counter.

She stamped it.

Dropped it into the sack.

There.

`Yes` had become harder to retrieve.

I liked that.

Then I disliked liking it because it felt too much like proving something to myself.

I left before the thought organized.

Carrow was gray with morning cloud.

Market awnings still wet from overnight mist.

A Vey wagon rolled past toward the river with three sealed packet boxes strapped under canvas.

For once I did not wonder whether one contained anything important.

Mine was already moving the other direction.

I bought breakfast.

Not stew.

Standards had risen under external pressure.

Eggs.

Bread.

One sausage that admitted what it was.

I ate near the window of a small market room while the city filled around me.

Five or six days.

I was going to be here.

Not because I had nowhere else to go.

That felt different enough that I left it alone...
