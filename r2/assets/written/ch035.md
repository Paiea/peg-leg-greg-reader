# Chapter 35: The Promise-Keeper

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Jorren came back with mud on only one boot.

This seemed impossible enough that I distrusted the entire man.

He was sitting in the Lower Rook cookroom when I came downstairs for breakfast, eating eggs from Noll's bowl.

Noll was not there.

Brass Spoon had swallowed another evening and apparently part of the morning after it.

The bowl remained vulnerable.

Jorren looked up.

"Morning."

I stopped on the last stair.

Chest first.

Again.

Less violently than the onion market.

Still enough.

He was back.

Current evidence.

Wet cloak folded over the bench.

Hair clean enough to suggest he had found water before food.

One boot brown to the ankle.

The other almost respectable.

A pale scrape crossed his jaw.

Right shoulder moved normally when he lifted the fork.

I checked without meaning to.

Jorren saw.

"Sack won."

"You caught another one?"

"No. Shoulder stopped hurting."

"Then why say sack won?"

"You looked."

My face remained a public institution.

I sat across from him.

"Why one boot?"

"Cart rut."

"Only one foot went in?"

"I was talented."

"Finally."

He pushed the bread plate toward me.

I took a piece.

"Four days?"

"Five. Rain east."

That explained the mud.

Some of it.

He kept eating.

I waited for the road story.

Jorren did not provide one.

This was inconsiderate.

"Millstones survive?"

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

"One chipped."

"How?"

"Driver hit bridge rail."

"You?"

"Was asleep."

I looked at him.

"You admit that?"

"Wasn't my watch."

Strong defense.

Vell had paid on return.

One axle pin worked loose outside Rellin Cross and cost them half a day.

The east rain turned the last two miles into mud.

Jorren stepped into a rut while pushing a wheel and managed to bury only the left boot.

The jaw scrape came from the same wagon when a loose side board swung back.

"You lose a fight with cargo often?"

"Cargo pays better than people."

"Doesn't apologize either."

"People don't."

Fair.

The cookroom door opened.

A woman from the second floor came in, saw Jorren using Noll's bowl, and said, "He'll complain."

"I know," Jorren said.

She nodded.

Apparently the building had accepted him during my absence too.

Annoying.

I ate bread.

Jorren finished the eggs.

Then looked at me.

Not casual.

Not serious either.

Just directly.

"Road."

My hand stopped halfway to the bread plate.

One word.

Different this time.

"What road?"

"North-east. Doss Ferry. Maybe farther if the return load changes."

I felt the answer before I knew enough to deserve it.

Yes.

Road.

Seven days, maybe ten.

Cold mornings.

New towns.

A pack that existed for reasons other than sitting beside my door.

I made myself wait.

"Who?"

"Vell."

"Again?"

"He likes being paid. I like being paid."

"Beautiful partnership."

Jorren tore the last piece of bread in half.

Gave me the smaller one.

Stable friendship.

Vell had another movement north-east in two days.

Not millstones this time.

Grain out.

Brass fittings and two crates of machine parts back if the Doss factor finished the sale.

Six days minimum.

Eight more likely.

Ten if the return cargo changed or the river crossing backed up wagons.

Two guards requested because the brass cargo would be easier to steal than stone.

"Pay?"

Jorren told me.

Better than the West Cut.

Better than his millstone run.

Not life-changing.

Enough to matter.

Food included except two midday meals.

Driver crew knew the route.

Vell had paid clean once already.

It was exactly the kind of road I had wanted Jorren to ask me about.

That was rude.

"When do you need an answer?"

"Now works."

"That's not a deadline."

"By tonight. Vell wants names tomorrow."

I looked down at my bread.

Mara.

Five days, maybe six when I read the letter.

One day had passed since.

Four or five now.

Jorren's road left in two.

Six days minimum.

The arithmetic did not improve with repetition.

I could leave.

Mara said if the packet behaved.

Packets did not always behave.

The river could delay her.

Vey could keep her in Halden.

Carrow office could need her second day instead of the first.

My answer had already gone west.

**Yes. Dinner. I'll be here.**

Specific enough to become inconvenient.

Jorren ate his half of the bread.

He did not fill the silence.

I disliked him for that too.

"How far north-east?"

"Doss. Maybe Kelm turn if return cargo isn't ready."

Kelm.

The name pulled a road out of older memory.

Low hills.

A stone inn with terrible mattresses.

A spring outside town where later adventurers stopped because the water stayed cold even in summer.

I had not seen it young.

Maybe current Kelm had no stone inn yet.

Maybe the spring had a different name.

I wanted to know.

Actual wanting.

Not professional value.

Not future verification.

I wanted to go north-east with Jorren and sleep badly somewhere that was not my room.

That made the other want worse.

Mara in Carrow.

Dinner.

Her face when she read `I'll be here.`

If the letter reached her.

Maybe it would not.

That thought arrived wearing reasonable clothes.

I recognized it anyway.

"Mara's coming," I said.

Jorren blinked.

Then smiled.

Not large.

Enough.

"Ah."

"Don't."

"Didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

He looked delighted by the theft.

"When?"

I told him.

His eyes moved once, doing the same arithmetic.

"You'll be gone."

"Yes."

"Could come back early," I said.

Jorren shook his head.

"Not if brass loads."

"If it doesn't?"

"Maybe sixth day."

Still bad.

"Mara could be late."

"Probably."

I looked at him.

He kept his face innocent.

Bastard.

Mara had banned that word from her ledger life and apparently the universe had assigned Jorren enforcement.

I stood.

Not because standing improved arithmetic.

Because sitting had become associated with choosing.

I carried my empty plate to the wash tub.

Rinsed it.

Too carefully.

Jorren remained at the table.

"You asked," I said.

"Yes."

"Like I told you."

"You told me."

"You said next one."

"I remember."

I turned.

"I hate that this is a good road."

Jorren thought about that.

"Could ask you about a bad one."

"No."

"Then difficult customer."

I laughed once.

It did not solve anything.

I went upstairs.

Jorren did not follow.

That helped.

My room had acquired evidence since he left.

Mara's new letter under the brass key.

No Tavin reply.

No two copper on the burned seven.

Field pack beside the door.

Still ready.

I crouched and opened it.

Fresh socks.

Waxed thread.

Oil.

Bedroll straps repaired.

Everything necessary for six days except food.

Maybe eight.

The pack wanted nothing.

I wanted both.

I sat on the floor beside it.

Young Greg had wanted roads before Old Greg arrived.

Current Greg had said yes to dinner before this road existed.

Neither fact volunteered to disappear.

I picked up Mara's letter.

**If you are here, I want dinner.**

My answer was already in a sack.

Maybe at the west road by now.

Maybe Halden tomorrow.

Maybe sitting under another clerk's tea.

Still mine.

I put the letter down.

Then I did something stupid enough to feel clarifying.

I packed.

Not fully.

Socks.

Oil.

Thread.

Bedroll.

I put them in, tightened the top, lifted the pack, and stood.

The weight settled across my shoulders.

Good.

Familiar enough to make leaving easy.

I walked once across the room.

Then back.

Mara's letter remained on the table.

I took the pack off.

Not dramatically.

It hit the floor harder than necessary.

The sound brought a knock from next door.

Noll's voice.

"You break something?"

"No."

"Then quieter."

"You throw pans at midnight."

"Work."

"That was here."

"Recovery."

Apparently Jorren had infected the building with professional vocabulary.

I unpacked the bedroll.

Left the socks inside.

Why, I did not know.

Compromise by laundry.

I went back downstairs.

Jorren had moved from eggs to tea.

He looked at me once.

Waited.

"No," I said.

The word hurt exactly as much as it deserved.

Jorren nodded.

"All right."

That was it.

I stared at him.

"That's all?"

"You said no."

"I know."

"Do you want negotiation?"

"No."

"Then good meeting."

I sat again because apparently anger had brought me back to furniture.

"Who are you asking?"

Jorren took a drink of tea.

There.

The next ugly want arrived immediately.

I had declined.

I still wanted to know how replaceable I was.

Human dignity remained aspirational.

"Beren first."

Of course.

"He's south."

"Came back yesterday."

I did not know that.

Jorren did.

"You already talked to him?"

"Saw him at Guild."

"About this?"

"No. I was asking you."

That should have helped.

It did.

Annoyingly little.

"He'll take it?"

"Maybe."

"You know his rate?"

Jorren looked at me.

"Greg."

"Right."

I put both hands flat on the table.

He had asked me first.

I had wanted that.

I had said no.

Now the road was going to continue with someone else.

The realization had no useful action attached.

Awful design.

Jorren finished the tea.

"Mara here long?"

"One night definitely. Maybe two."

"Work?"

"Vey. Ledgers. Everyone blaming everyone."

"Sounds like her."

There.

Young-Greg world again.

Jorren knew Mara in a way I did not fully know either of them.

"You knew she liked ledgers?"

"Everyone knew."

"I did not."

"You forgot."

No cruelty.

Still landed.

I looked toward the stairs.

"Tavin wrote too."

Jorren's eyebrows rose.

"When?"

"Before I woke. Found it late."

"He still south?"

"Apparently. Sent reply yesterday."

"You owe him money?"

I stared.

"Why does everyone know that?"

Jorren smiled.

"Because you complained about the seven for years."

"I have evidence it was defective."

"It's burned."

"Exactly."

"You burned it."

Conspiracy remained geographically robust.

Jorren stood.

"I'm going Guild."

"Beren?"

"Eventually."

I threw a napkin at him.

He caught it.

Road reflex.

"Tell him the pay's bad."

"Why?"

"Principle."

"No."

He put the napkin on the table.

Then looked at me.

"Next one too?"

The question surprised me.

I had assumed one declined invitation used up the mechanism somehow.

"Yes," I said.

Jorren nodded.

"All right."

Then left.

Again.

Not on the road yet.

Just through the cookroom door.

Still irritating.

I stayed at the table.

The second-floor woman came back in for water.

She looked at Jorren's empty bowl.

"He pay?"

"For what?"

"Eggs."

I looked at the bowl.

"No."

She held out her hand.

"One copper."

"They were Noll's eggs."

"Noll bought from me."

Current economy had found me.

I paid.

That evening Noll left for his third Brass Spoon trial shift wearing a cleaner shirt than yesterday.

He paused in my doorway.

"Road?"

I looked up from the table.

"What?"

He pointed at the half-packed field pack beside the door.

Fresh socks still inside.

Everything else back where it belonged.

"No."

Noll nodded.

"Mara?"

"Yes."

He nodded again.

I waited for mockery.

None arrived.

"You owe me one copper," I said.

"For?"

"Eggs."

"Jorren ate them."

"I know."

"Then ask Jorren."

"He left."

"Road?"

"Guild."

"Then eventually."

I hated everyone.

Noll went downstairs.

His footsteps disappeared into the courtyard.

The room became quiet again.

On the table, Mara's letter sat beside the burned seven.

Beside the door, my pack contained two pairs of socks and no plan...
