# Chapter 36: The Neighbor

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Noll's fourth trial night made Lower Rook too quiet before it made him employed.

I discovered this at sixth bell while sitting in my room with a book open to the same page for long enough to qualify as a relationship.

Nothing came through the wall.

No pan.

No chair.

No argument with clothing.

No door opening because he had decided some object in my room was temporarily communal.

Just plaster.

I read the first paragraph again.

The page remained innocent.

Mara was due in two days.

Maybe three.

I had checked the Vey desk once that morning.

Only once.

Restraint of this magnitude deserved recognition from institutions currently unavailable.

The clerk said no Halden packet.

I had not returned.

Yet.

Instead I was now listening to a wall because apparently staying in Carrow had created hobbies.

I closed the book.

The Brass Spoon was across East Market and had food.

Both facts were useful.

I put on my coat.

This was not checking on Noll.

I was going to dinner.

Noll happened to work where dinner lived.

Completely different activity.

The Brass Spoon looked more expensive at night.

Lamps in both front windows.

Clean glass.

A painted spoon over the door that had once been gold and now mostly suggested ambition.

Inside, every table near the front was occupied.

The woman at the register looked at me once.

"One?"

"Apparently."

She pointed toward the rear wall.

I took the smallest table in the room and immediately understood why it was available.

One leg was short.

I folded a scrap of menu paper under it.

Current engineering.

The kitchen hatch opened beyond the side counter.

Heat came through first.

Then voices.

Then Noll carrying three bowls at once.

I almost called his name.

Did not.

He moved past the hatch before he saw me.

That was useful because it gave me several seconds to realize I had never watched him work somewhere he did not live.

At Lower Rook, Noll occupied space like rent included collisions.

He opened doors with hips.

Put knives down wherever the cutting had ended.

Spoke while carrying hot things.

Swore at furniture with no expectation of reform.

Here, he turned before corners.

Not elegantly.

Enough.

A server came through behind him carrying plates and Noll moved half a step without looking.

The plates missed his shoulder.

He put the bowls on the pass, reached for a towel, wiped the rim of the middle one, and sent all three out.

Then he looked through the hatch.

Saw me.

Stopped for perhaps half a breath.

His expression became suspicious.

I raised one hand.

Noll looked away.

Good reunion.

A woman came to my table.

Not the register woman.

Dark hair pinned high enough to survive steam. Sleeves rolled above both elbows. Thin burn scar along one forearm.

"You Greg?"

Noll had betrayed me before appetizers.

"Depends who is asking."

"Vessa."

There.

The name from his dumpling trial.

Current Vessa was younger than whatever old restaurant-memory had tried to hand me and looked less interested in my uncertainty.

"He said you eat."

"Frequently."

"What?"

I looked at the board.

Stew.

Fish.

Dumplings.

Prices designed by people who believed sitting indoors was a luxury service.

"Dumplings."

Vessa looked toward the kitchen.

"Of course."

"Is that bad?"

"For him, maybe."

She left before explaining.

Noll looked through the hatch again.

I smiled.

He mouthed something.

Too far to hear.

I chose `welcome, dear friend.`

A spoon hit the kitchen wall.

Metal against plaster.

Hard.

Nobody ducked.

A broad man near the stove stood with one hand empty and an expression of complete innocence.

Vessa turned from the register.

"Domm."

"Slipped."

"Keep the next one in your hand."

Domm looked at the spoon on the floor.

"That one isn't."

"Find it."

He found it.

Noll did not even turn.

That got me.

Not the spoon.

Noll.

He had heard it.

Obviously.

At home he would have commented before the sound finished traveling through the wall.

Here he was cutting something green into a bowl while another cook reached past him for salt.

Different room.

Different Noll.

Same eyebrows when the other cook took too much salt.

Better.

My dumplings arrived in broth.

Six.

The folds were tighter than the test one he had brought home.

Still not identical.

One leaned slightly to the left.

I ate that one first out of solidarity.

Wrapper thinner than his Lower Rook version.

Pork, cabbage, onion.

Vinegar sharper at the end.

Good.

Very good.

I did not look at the hatch immediately.

That would have been pathetic.

I waited until the second dumpling.

Noll was looking.

I nodded once.

His mouth moved.

Small.

Then Vessa shoved a tray toward him and he disappeared.

The rush thickened after that.

Three new tables.

Two people left.

Five came in.

Someone near the door complained that fish had bones.

The server stared at him long enough that I respected her professionally.

I watched the kitchen in pieces.

Noll missed a call once and Vessa repeated it louder.

He answered.

He dropped one wrapper.

Domm stepped on it.

Noll called him a bastard without slowing down.

He burned the side of one finger on a pan handle, swore, stuck the finger under cold water, then returned to the board.

I shifted in my chair.

Not standing.

Close.

Nobody else moved toward him.

Vessa looked once.

"Skin?"

Noll checked.

"Fine."

"Then don't grab that handle again."

"Excellent plan."

He kept working.

I sat back.

Friendship had become physically inconvenient from across a dining room.

I finished the dumplings.

Ordered beer.

The server charged me before bringing it.

Trust remained expensive.

By late bell, only four tables remained.

The kitchen changed volume.

Same people.

More words between movements.

Noll told Domm his sauce looked diseased.

Domm threw a towel.

Noll caught it.

Progress in ammunition safety.

Vessa came to my table with the bill.

I looked at the total.

"Does the chair have a separate line?"

"Should."

"Table too?"

She pressed one hand against the wobbling edge.

The menu scrap shifted.

"Not that table."

"Finally, justice."

I paid full price anyway.

Vessa took the coins.

"He says you gave him the pork money."

I looked toward the hatch.

Noll was cleaning a board with exaggerated concentration.

"Loaned."

"Two copper."

"Correct."

"He has it."

"Wonderful."

"He keeps asking when to give it back."

"Preferably before one of us dies."

Vessa's expression did not change.

"You two related?"

"No."

"Unfortunate."

Then she walked away.

I was still deciding whether that had been an insult when Noll finally came out.

His hair was damp at the temples.

Apron stained in three separate directions.

One cuff had flour on it.

The burned finger looked pink, nothing more.

He sat across from me.

Then reached into his pocket.

Two copper hit the table.

One rolled toward the short leg and stopped against my folded menu scrap.

I looked at the coins.

"You could have done this before I paid twelve copper for dinner."

"No."

"Why not?"

"Funny."

Fair.

I put the coins in my purse.

Tavin's debt had bought pork, turned into dumplings, gone to The Brass Spoon, and returned after charging me admission.

I would explain none of this to Tavin unless legally compelled.

Noll leaned back.

"Well?"

"Expensive."

"Dumplings."

"Also expensive."

He kicked my boot under the table.

"Very good."

His face tried not to move.

Failed around one corner.

"Fold holds better."

"Vessa."

"Vinegar too?"

"Vessa."

"Did you contribute anything?"

"Pork."

"My pork."

"Paid back."

Right.

Noll looked toward the kitchen.

Vessa was counting the register with the other woman.

"She keeping you?" I asked.

"Don't know."

"Fourth night."

"I can count."

"Evidence mixed."

He looked at me.

"Domm says I open wrappers when pot gets crowded."

"Do you?"

"Three tonight."

"Out of?"

"Forty-eight."

I started calculating.

Noll pointed at me.

"No."

I stopped.

He had learned dangerous skills.

"You like it?"

He considered.

Not long.

"Yes."

There.

No joke around it.

"Hours are bad."

"You come home smelling like hot fat."

"Better than your room."

"Impossible."

"Pay's better."

"Than Lower Rook?"

"Yes."

He looked at the front window.

Outside, East Market was nearly empty.

"If she keeps me, five nights."

"You want five?"

Noll's face changed in the small way it had when I finally asked why he needed pork.

"Four."

"Why four?"

"Maud gives me carter supper fifth night."

Maud Pell ran Lower Rook like ownership was mostly a form of shouting.

"Brass Spoon pays more."

"Yes."

"How much more?"

Noll told me.

Enough that the fifth shift was not a sentimental rounding error.

Not enough to explain the answer by itself.

"Then why keep the carters?"

He frowned.

"Because I live there."

Vessa arrived.

Noll sat straighter.

Barely.

"Four," she said.

He blinked.

"What?"

"Four nights. Same days after this week. Fifth if I ask before noon and you want it."

Noll looked at her.

"You said five."

"You said four."

"I said I wanted four."

"Yes."

He appeared offended by the effectiveness of communication.

Vessa continued.

"Trial rate one more week. Then two copper more each night if I stop finding opened wrappers in the pot."

"Three tonight."

"Four."

"No."

Vessa looked toward the kitchen.

Domm lifted one hand.

"Four."

Noll turned.

"Traitor."

"I counted."

"First time all night."

Vessa waited.

Noll looked back at her.

"Two copper more?"

"If three or fewer."

"You just said none."

"Terms changed."

I liked her immediately.

Noll did not.

"Two or fewer."

Vessa's eyebrows rose.

"You are negotiating the number of failures upward?"

"Down."

"From three."

"You said three."

"I did."

Noll stopped.

Thought.

"Fine. Three."

Vessa held out her hand.

Noll shook it.

Done.

No paper.

No Guild mark.

No ceremony beyond grease on both hands.

Vessa looked at me.

"You taking him home?"

"He knows where it is."

"Good."

She left.

Noll watched her go.

Then looked at me.

"Four."

I smiled.

"Four."

He tried not to smile back.

Failed worse this time.

We left after the chairs went upside down.

Noll carried a paper parcel Vessa had shoved at him before the door.

"What's that?"

"Bones."

"Employment benefit?"

"Broth."

"At Lower Rook."

"Where else?"

We walked through cold streets toward home.

At the river turn, Noll looked at me.

"Mara tomorrow?"

"Maybe."

His face immediately became intolerable.

"Actual maybe. River."

"Still maybe."

"You have become very wise since employment."

"Four nights."

"Yes, Noll."

"Four."

He said it once more because apparently numbers improved with repetition.

At Lower Rook he took the bones downstairs before going up.

Cold box.

Tomorrow's broth.

Then we climbed.

At my door, he stopped.

"You going Vey in morning?"

I looked at him.

"Why?"

"You went today."

Betrayal.

"Once."

"Twice yesterday."

"How do you know that?"

"You told me."

Right.

Self-betrayal.

"Maybe."

Noll unlocked his door.

"Dangerous."

"Go to bed."

He went inside.

The door closed.

A chair scraped once.

Then nothing.

Four nights a week, that silence would arrive late now.

I stood in my room with two copper back in my purse and listened to it for a while...
