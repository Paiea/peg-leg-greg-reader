# Chapter 37: The Tenant

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Story search: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/037-story-search.md`

---

My room became too small on a Tuesday.

It had been the same size on Monday.

This was financially suspicious.

I was sitting on the bed with one boot off when somebody knocked.

Not Noll.

Noll knocked like the door had personally withheld soap from him.

This was two short taps.

Patient.

Administrative.

I put the boot back on because experience had taught me that authority arrived when your feet were least prepared for it.

The woman outside was named Maud Pell.

I knew this because she collected rent every fourth week and had been doing so for months without becoming important enough for my later memory to preserve her.

Current me had learned the name after the fire.

Useful improvement.

Maud was perhaps fifty, square through the shoulders, with gray hair pinned behind both ears and a ledger tucked under one arm.

She looked past me into the room.

Not rudely.

Professionally.

Bed.

Table.

Chair.

Sword.

Field pack beside the door.

Two shirts drying from a cord near the window.

One pair of socks I had paid enough for to resent seeing them become ordinary.

"You planning to stay?" she asked.

This seemed like a dangerous opening question.

"In the building?"

"Ideally."

Good.

"Yes."

Maud nodded once.

"Third floor front is empty tomorrow."

I waited.

She waited too.

Apparently I was expected to know what this meant.

"Congratulations?"

"Beren's moving in with his sister."

Beren.

Third floor front.

I pictured him slowly.

Older man with a cough and two wicker baskets he repaired badly in the cookroom.

Had once blamed Noll for stealing a spoon that was later found inside Beren's own boot.

I remembered the accusation better than the apology.

Reasonable distribution.

"You want the room?" Maud asked.

Ah.

There it was.

"Why me?"

"You pay."

Flattering.

"Mostly on time."

Less flattering.

"And moving you upstairs is easier than finding somebody outside before next rent day."

There was the romance.

I looked behind me.

My current room had not shrunk.

The field pack still fit beside the door.

The sword still fit beside the chair.

The bed remained technically capable of containing one person if that person did not expect dignity from his knees.

"How much?"

Maud named the rent.

Three copper more each week than I paid now.

I stared at her.

"For stairs?"

"For space."

"Stairs are included?"

"As many as you want."

Generous building.

Three copper a week.

Twelve in four weeks.

One support-assessment deposit, every month, forever.

Not forever.

That was how bad budgeting became religion.

I looked at the field pack.

Three copper was also less than one clean cinderwing job had paid me for two hours.

Not guaranteed work.

Different category.

Recurring expense against irregular income.

Better question.

"Can I see it?"

Maud was already turning toward the stairs.

Apparently this counted as negotiation.

The third floor felt farther away carrying nothing.

Important research.

Beren's door stood open.

The room beyond was not impressive.

I liked it immediately.

Also dangerous.

Maybe half again as large as mine.

Not enough to host nobility.

Enough to walk around the bed without touching it.

Two windows instead of one.

One faced the street.

The second faced the narrow yard behind the building where Noll's laundry had previously endangered my sword practice.

There was a shallow alcove beside the rear window.

Not a room.

Just three feet of wall set back around an old chimney breast.

A shelf had been built into one side.

The shelf was crooked.

I forgave it.

My field pack would fit there.

So would boots.

Wet road coat below the window.

Drying cord across the alcove instead of above my bed.

I was already furnishing a room I had not agreed to pay for.

Bad sign.

Beren's bed frame remained against one wall because it apparently belonged to the building.

Narrower than mine.

Better wood.

One floorboard near the window had been replaced recently.

The plaster above it showed an old water stain.

"Roof leak?"

"Was," Maud said.

"Was?"

"Reshingled that corner in summer."

I looked at the stain.

Current evidence.

Dry.

No new edge.

Fine.

The room's greatest luxury stood in the center.

Nothing.

Six feet of empty floor.

Maybe seven if I moved the chair.

I drew an imaginary line from the street window toward the interior wall.

Overhead cut.

Recover.

Side step.

No basin.

No coat.

No chair unless I became ambitious.

Maud watched my eyes.

"No chopping the wall."

"I have improved."

"That's not an answer."

"No chopping the wall."

Acceptable lease language.

I walked to the rear window.

Lower Rook looked different from one floor higher.

Not better.

Roofs instead of awnings.

More sky between chimneys.

The yard smaller from above.

Noll came through the back door carrying a bucket while I was looking down.

He glanced up.

Saw me.

Stopped.

"Why are you in Beren's room?"

"Crime."

"Need help?"

Good neighbor.

"Rent."

He looked at Maud behind me.

"Worse."

Then carried his bucket inside.

Maud did not react.

Experienced manager.

I touched the window latch.

Worked.

Dangerous room.

"Beren out tomorrow?"

"By noon."

"Deposit?"

"You already have one downstairs. Moves with you."

There.

Interesting.

No new capital barrier.

Just recurring cost.

"Same notice terms?"

"Same."

"Same cookroom?"

She looked at me.

"Unless you've discovered another one in the building."

Not yet.

"Heat?"

"Same stove line. Front room stays warmer than yours."

Value.

Winter coming.

The room wanted to sell itself.

I distrusted any asset willing to do that much work.

"I need to think."

Maud nodded.

No pressure.

That made the pressure worse.

"Before supper. If not, I put the note outside."

Current market deadline.

Fair.

I went downstairs.

My room received me without comment.

Same crack in the basin.

Not the original crack.

I had added to that history on the first morning of my second life.

Technically first morning of my first second life.

No.

I refused the sentence.

The table held Mara's last letter beneath the old card deck.

Tavin's letter sat folded beside it now.

The marked support cord hung near the window.

The field pack occupied the floor beside the door.

My good sword leaned where the cheap sword had leaned on the morning I woke nineteen.

Same room.

Different inventory.

I sat.

Three copper more each week.

I counted the wrong way first.

How much money did I have now?

Enough.

That was not the question.

How much did I earn?

Irregular.

Road work.

Ward hand.

Two-hour creature jobs.

Two-day relay repair.

Warehouse counts when life became less interesting.

Nine copper from ownership that might not repeat on schedule.

Could I afford the room if I had a bad month?

Probably.

Bad word.

I recalculated with no venture distribution at all.

Then without a good field contract.

Then with food becoming annoying because winter made everything annoying.

Still possible.

Not comfortable.

Possible.

What did the extra rent buy?

Space.

Warmth.

Drying.

Practice.

A shelf.

The shelf should not have mattered.

It did.

My current table had become three systems stacked on one another.

Letters when I wrote.

Sword oil when I cleaned.

Cards when I wanted to stare at evidence from a life I did not fully remember.

Food when I gave up on all three.

The new room had enough floor and wall that objects could stop taking turns being my life.

That sounded suspiciously like adulthood.

I had already done adulthood once.

Mixed results.

Someone knocked again.

This time Noll.

Correct knock.

I opened the door.

He came in without invitation because relationship boundaries had apparently achieved maturity.

"You moving?"

"Considering."

He looked around my room.

Then at me.

"Why?"

"More space."

"You have space."

He pointed at the six inches between my bed and chair.

"That's a path."

"Luxury."

"Three copper more."

He knew already.

Of course he did.

Buildings processed information faster than Guild clerks.

"Worth it?" I asked.

Noll looked confused.

"To me? No."

Good answer.

"Why?"

"I don't own a sword I swing indoors."

Fair.

He sat in my chair.

Now the room had no remaining floor.

Demonstration.

"Beren room's warmer," he said.

"Maud said."

"Window leaks air at bottom."

There.

Local inspection report.

"Bad?"

"Stuff rag in it."

Fine.

"Roof?"

"Fixed."

Confirmation.

"Mice?"

"Same mice."

Important continuity.

I leaned against the wall.

"Would you move?"

Noll shook his head.

"My room's near cookroom."

That mattered to him.

Not me.

"You going somewhere?" he asked.

"Upstairs."

"No. I mean you keep putting that bag by the door."

I looked at the field pack.

"Sometimes."

"Then take the room."

Simple.

I waited.

"Why?"

"Because every time you come back, your wet shit is in the hall."

There was the emotional core.

Beautiful.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

He stood.

At the door he stopped.

"If you move, can I have that shelf?"

I looked at my current shelf.

One board above the table.

"It belongs to the room."

"Could accidentally belong to me."

"Talk to Maud."

He looked offended by procedure.

Then left.

I decided before supper.

Not because of Noll.

Mostly.

Maud was in the cookroom checking the repaired stove door when I found her.

"I'll take it."

She held out her hand.

I stared at it.

"What?"

"Key."

Of course.

Apparently decisions could become architecture quickly.

I gave her my current room key.

She gave me the third-floor key from her apron pocket.

No contract rewrite.

Same terms, new room number, higher rent entered into her ledger.

She wrote the amount.

Three copper more.

Every week.

The number looked worse in ink.

Good.

Recurring costs should remain visible.

"Tomorrow after noon," she said.

"I know."

"Don't move before Beren's gone."

"Wasn't planning to."

"Noll once moved a chair into an occupied room because he thought the man had left."

I paused.

"Did he?"

"Man was sleeping."

Excellent building.

The move took less than an hour.

This was humiliating.

I had believed I owned more things.

Bed linens.

Clothes.

Table contents.

Sword.

Field pack.

Two cooking bowls.

One cup.

Wash things.

Old cards.

Letters.

Marked cord.

A cheap chair I had apparently paid for at some point and forgotten.

Noll carried the table upstairs because he claimed my long arms made me bad at corners.

This was not obviously true.

It became true on the second landing.

We rotated.

Lifted.

Nearly took a strip of plaster off the wall.

Maud shouted from below without seeing us.

"No wall."

Disturbing woman.

We succeeded.

The field pack went into the alcove.

Boots beneath it.

Road coat on a peg near the rear window.

Sword beside the bed at first.

Then I moved it to the wall near the open practice line.

Better.

Letters on the built-in shelf.

Cards beside them.

The marked support cord hung from the shelf edge.

My old table became only a table.

I stood in the middle of the room.

Nothing touched me.

Suspicious.

Noll leaned in the doorway.

"Rich."

"Three copper richer every week, apparently."

"Opposite."

Fair.

He looked at the open floor.

"Do the stupid sword thing."

"It's not stupid."

"Then do the important sword thing."

I drew the blade.

Checked the windows.

Checked Noll.

"Stay there."

"Very reassuring."

Overhead cut.

Arrive.

Recover.

No chair.

No basin.

No coat moving from displaced air.

My eyes came up.

Noll was still in the doorway.

The sword was ready.

There was room for the next step.

I did it again.

Then stopped before the room became a lesson.

Sheathed the sword.

Noll looked disappointed.

"That's it?"

"Walls cost money."

He nodded solemnly.

Growth recognized growth.

That night the room sounded different.

Same building.

Same pipes.

Same voices through walls.

Noll coughing one floor below.

Somebody dropping a pan in the cookroom.

Street carts outside.

But the angles had changed.

The bed faced another direction.

Moonlight came through a second window.

The field pack was no longer between me and the door.

For one stupid second, I missed the old room.

Not badly.

Just enough to notice.

I could have stayed there for years because it was the room where I woke up.

That would have been a terrible reason.

Memory did not need rent-free property.

I turned over.

The new window leaked cold air along the bottom exactly where Noll said it would.

Tomorrow I would find a rag.

Three copper more a week and the wind was still included.

Good.

I could afford improvement.

Apparently perfection remained outside my lease...
