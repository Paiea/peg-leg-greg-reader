# Chapter 37: The Companion

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Mara arrived one day late and I heard her before I saw her.

This was appropriate.

The Vey packet office occupied the ground floor of a narrow building near the river. Their east desk was upstairs, reached by a stair designed by somebody who hated boxes.

I had checked once that morning.

No Halden arrival.

The clerk had looked at me with recognition and then with pity, which I resented in that order.

I left.

At seventh bell, I returned because I happened to be on the same street.

This was almost true.

Before I reached the counter, a woman's voice came through the ceiling.

"Then the date is wrong."

Another voice answered something I could not make out.

Mara again.

"No, the date being written clearly does not make it less wrong."

There.

My chest did the stupid thing before the clerk looked up.

"Halden came in," she said.

"I noticed."

"If you're Greg, upstairs."

I stopped.

"How do you know who I am?"

The clerk looked toward the ceiling.

"She asked twice."

Oh.

That helped too much.

I climbed.

The stair turned halfway and narrowed enough that a man coming down with a document box had to flatten himself against the wall.

"Good design," I said.

"Vey doesn't own the building," he said.

Apparently blame had jurisdiction.

The upper office smelled like damp paper, ink, and whatever somebody had eaten at a desk hours earlier.

Three worktables.

Shelves along one wall.

A rear window looking toward the river.

Mara stood beside the middle table wearing her travel coat open and her hair tied back badly enough that one side had mostly escaped.

One hand rested on a ledger.

The other held a loose page toward a Carrow clerk.

The blue cup sat beside them.

Of course it did.

She had carried it across two cities and placed it in the middle of an accounting dispute.

Mara looked up.

Her face changed before either of us spoke.

Tired first.

Then something warmer.

Still tired.

Good.

"You came."

"Eventually."

Her eyes narrowed.

"Wrong person."

"Jorren infected me."

"Dangerous man."

I walked over.

For a moment I did not know whether to hug her.

Six weeks ago she had sat in my room and told me I had forgotten two kisses.

Since then we had written letters about cold feet, bad stew, Dena, work, and a blue cup.

That was enough relationship to make standing three feet apart feel stupid.

Mara solved it.

She put the loose page down and hugged me.

Quick.

Both arms.

Current.

My hands found her back a fraction late.

Then she stepped away.

"You smell like outside."

"You smell like Vey."

"Ink?"

"Onions."

Mara looked at the other clerk.

He raised both hands.

"Not me."

"Pell's influence has spread."

I smiled.

The clerk looked between us.

"Dinner?"

Mara looked at the ledger.

"After this."

Right.

She had not traveled east to make my evening begin on schedule.

I stood there for one second too long.

Mara noticed.

"You can sit."

There were three chairs.

One held a stack of tied packets.

One had a cracked back.

The third was beneath the clerk.

"Luxury office."

"You should see Halden."

I moved the packet stack to the floor and sat.

That became my contribution to Vey Shipping.

For the next twenty minutes, Mara worked.

Not in a way designed for observation.

She read the Carrow handoff ledger, compared dates against two packet slips, and got increasingly irritated at a line marked `warehouse hold` without a warehouse number.

The local clerk, Emmet, explained that Carrow used the river-day date for mixed loads when the physical box moved after midnight.

Mara said that was stupid.

Emmet agreed.

Then pointed out that the Halden office had entered one of the dates in departure-day format instead.

Mara checked.

Stopped.

"That's ours."

"Yes."

She rubbed both eyes with the heels of her hands.

"I hate everyone."

Emmet nodded.

"Gets easier."

"Does it?"

"No."

Mara looked at me.

"You enjoying this?"

"Immensely."

"Why?"

Because she had been wrong once and the world remained standing.

Because she looked competent without looking finished.

Because the woman who had corrected my letters could be corrected by a clerk named Emmet and simply continue.

I chose the least dangerous answer.

"You came all this way to discover both offices are idiots."

"I suspected. Evidence matters."

Emmet pointed at her.

"That part I like."

She went back to the ledger.

By the time Mara closed it, the window had gone dark enough to reflect the room.

Emmet tied the corrected slips into one bundle.

"Tomorrow?"

"First bell."

He grimaced.

"Second."

"First."

"I have children."

Mara looked at him.

"Second."

He smiled.

Messy institution.

Mara finally picked up the blue cup.

I stared at it.

She caught me.

"What?"

"You actually brought it."

"Mother remains wrong."

"Halden has cups."

"Exactly."

"This proves nothing."

"It proves I was committed enough to carry ceramic on a Vey wagon."

"That proves several things."

She put the cup into her travel bag.

I took the ledger case she had left beside the desk.

This time I asked with my hand first.

Mara let me carry it.

We went downstairs.

The clerk at the counter looked up.

"Dinner?"

I looked at Mara.

"Does everyone know?"

"You checked twice yesterday."

Betrayal had become institutional.

"Once today."

"Improvement."

Mara laughed.

Outside, Carrow had gone cold enough that our breath showed near the river.

"Real dinner," she said.

"You remember your own demands."

"I wrote them down."

"Cheating."

"Reliable."

I took her to The Brass Spoon.

Partly because the food was good.

Partly because Noll was working and I wanted Mara to see that.

I admitted the second reason to myself only after we had already turned into East Market.

Too late to choose nobility.

Vessa saw us from the register.

Her eyes went from me to Mara and then to the last empty two-person table near the rear.

"There."

"Good evening to you too."

"Table goes if you keep talking."

We sat.

Mara looked toward the kitchen hatch.

"That's Vessa?"

"How do you know Vessa?"

"You wrote her name once."

Had I?

Apparently my letters contained more people than I remembered sending.

"Terrifying."

"You wrote that too."

Consistent evidence.

The hatch opened.

Noll came through carrying two plates.

He saw me first.

Then Mara.

His whole body stopped while the plates stayed level.

"Mara?"

She turned.

Her face changed in a different way than mine had upstairs.

Recognition without calculation.

"Noll."

He looked at his apron as if checking whether he was still himself.

"You left."

"Yes."

"Long time."

"Four years."

Noll considered.

"Still long."

Mara smiled.

"You got tall."

"So did Greg."

She looked at me.

"Barely."

"I am six foot three."

Neither cared.

Noll's eyes went to her travel bag.

The blue rim of the cup showed at the top.

"That cup?"

Mara stared.

"You remember it?"

"Your mother hated it."

"She was wrong."

"Yes."

Immediate alliance.

I had worked months for less agreement.

Vessa shouted, "Noll."

He looked toward the kitchen.

"Work."

Then he disappeared through the hatch.

Mara watched him go.

"You didn't know I knew him."

"No."

"He used to throw apple cores at Tavin," she said.

"Why?"

"Tavin threw them back."

"This is not better information."

"You participated."

"Of course I did."

Dinner arrived before the story could become criminal evidence.

Mara ordered fish.

I ordered stew because I believed in rehabilitation.

Noll sent dumplings we had not ordered.

Vessa charged us for them.

I respected her consistency.

The first part of the meal was easy.

Mara told me about Corren, the Vey driver who had sung one verse for six hours while their axle dragged them a day behind schedule.

I told her Dena had charged me one copper for using four minutes of her route to post Tavin's letter.

"Good."

"You still don't know enough to support that ruling."

"You cost four minutes."

"I paid."

"Then justice occurred."

"This is why I avoid telling you things."

"No, you don't. You write them and then cross out the explanation."

Fair.

We ate.

Eventually Mara asked, "Jorren?"

Not `where is he.`

Just his name.

I put my spoon down.

"Road."

Her eyebrows moved.

"With you?"

"No."

The answer came too quickly to hide anything.

"Why?"

There were several versions.

I had work.

I was staying in Carrow.

Timing.

All true enough to become useless.

"I told you I'd be here."

Mara's face lost the smile.

"Greg."

"He asked after."

"Asked what?"

"Doss Ferry. Maybe farther. Good pay. Six to ten days."

She stopped eating.

"And you said no because I was coming."

"I said no because I had already said yes to you."

"That sounds nicer."

"It is also what happened."

Mara wrapped both hands around her cup.

Restaurant cup.

Blue one remained safely litigated in the bag.

"I didn't ask you to stop taking roads."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Yes."

She waited.

I hated waiting when the other person knew it worked.

"I wanted the road," I said.

There.

Her expression changed.

Not guilt exactly.

Closer.

"Then why didn't you go?"

"Because you were coming."

"That's the same answer."

"Yes."

"You said you'd be here before the road existed."

"Yes."

"And if I had been two more days late?"

I had considered that.

Of course I had.

"I would have become unpleasant."

"At me?"

"At weather. Vey. Axles. Jorren. The city. Whoever was closest and defensible."

That got one reluctant breath of laughter.

"Healthy."

"I have a system."

Mara looked down at the table.

"I don't want to owe you Doss Ferry."

"You don't."

"Then don't use me to make it worth missing."

That one found the exact place I had been hiding the evening.

Mara here.

Good fish.

Noll through the hatch.

Proof.

I picked up my spoon.

Put it down again.

"I was doing that."

"I know."

"Annoying."

"Also know."

For several bites, neither of us improved anything.

The stew was actually good.

That felt rude.

Noll came out once carrying three plates, saw our faces, and kept walking.

Excellent friend.

Mara eventually asked whether I was angry.

"Yes."

She looked up.

"At me?"

I checked.

Really checked.

"A little at the possibility of you. Not at you sitting here."

Mara considered that.

"I don't know what that means."

"Neither do I."

Better than explaining something false.

"Mostly I'm angry the road existed at the wrong time. And that Jorren can ask Beren instead."

"Who is Beren?"

"Exactly."

She laughed.

The awkwardness stayed.

Just with room around it now.

We talked about other things.

Not to avoid the road.

Because dinner contained more than one subject.

I told her what glasshorns sounded like at night after realizing I had barely noticed sound while watching them.

She told me about Sera at the Halden office, who cheated at darts and denied it with enough confidence to make witnesses doubt arithmetic.

Pell had brought Mara onion soup when she was sick.

I disliked him less and resented that too.

By the time Vessa brought the bill, most tables had emptied.

Mara reached for it.

I reached at the same time.

Her fingers landed across mine.

Warm.

Ink stain still along the side of her thumb.

Neither of us moved immediately.

"Half," she said.

I looked at our hands.

Then her.

"Half."

Mara smiled.

We paid.

Outside, East Market had gone almost quiet.

Cold air made the warmth from her hand more obvious after it was gone.

We started toward the Vey office.

Three streets in, Mara slowed at the turn toward Lower Rook.

She looked down it.

"Still there?"

"The building? Mostly."

"Your room."

"Third floor."

"Same one?"

"Yes."

Mara looked at me.

"Show me."

My body understood the request before my brain located safe language.

"Work at second bell."

"Emmet has children."

"You already corrupted the schedule."

"Improved it."

She turned toward Lower Rook.

Not waiting for permission.

I followed.

The road I had missed ran north-east without me.

Tonight, apparently, I was walking south with Mara and still allowed to be annoyed about both...
