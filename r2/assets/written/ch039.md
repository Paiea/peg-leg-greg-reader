# Chapter 39: The Visitor

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Mara canceled dinner with six words and an insult.

The note arrived at Lower Rook after midday.

A Vey runner found me in the courtyard arguing with Maud Pell about whether a loose stair rail qualified as a building feature.

Maud said yes.

I said criminally.

The runner held out folded paper.

"Greg?"

Maud looked at me.

"Depends."

"It says Greg."

"Then unfortunately."

I took it.

Mara's handwriting.

No envelope.

**Ledger mess worse. No dinner. If you come, bring food. Not stew.**

Six words before the insult depended on whether `ledger` and `mess` were emotionally one concept.

I counted anyway.

The runner waited.

"Answer?"

"Did she ask for one?"

He shrugged.

Professional packet philosophy.

I folded the note and put it in my coat.

Maud pointed at the rail.

"Still feature."

"Someone will die."

"Not yet."

Impossible management.

I went upstairs.

The room looked exactly like it had after Mara left.

That was annoying.

Bed still rumpled near the wall where we had sat.

Burned seven beneath her letter because she had moved it.

Two keys on the ring.

Field pack with two socks.

No evidence that the night had changed the laws of matter.

I had expected a second dinner.

Not promised.

Expected.

Mara's letter had said one night definitely, second only if the office kept her.

The office had kept her.

Apparently I had interpreted that as `more Mara` rather than `more office.`

Poor reading.

I spent the afternoon doing three things I would have done if Mara were still in Halden.

First, I went to Mill Street.

Arlo needed two brass collars carried from the machinist because Dena's cart was on route and the apprentice he usually bribed with bread had acquired a fever.

I carried them.

Heavy enough to be irritating.

Not heavy enough to become adventure.

Ivena asked whether I had seen Mara.

I stared at her.

"How do you know she's here?"

"Dena."

Of course.

"How does Dena know?"

"Vey wagon at river office."

There was no privacy left in Carrow, only varying transport speeds.

"Yes."

Ivena looked at my face.

"Good?"

Dangerous question.

"Complicated."

She nodded.

No follow-up.

I liked her more for that.

Second, I went to the Guild.

The board had one road job I would have taken if it left next week.

It left tomorrow.

South, not north-east.

Two nights.

Bad pay.

I still read the entire slip.

Then put it back.

Not because of Mara.

Mostly because the pay was insulting.

I refused to investigate the percentage.

Third, I bought food.

Not stew.

The river market sold hand pies from a stall that filled them with beef, onion, and enough pepper to prove quality by violence.

I bought four.

Two for Mara.

One for me.

One because arithmetic near food remained unreliable.

At fifth bell, I went to Vey.

The downstairs clerk saw the paper bag.

"Upstairs."

"No interrogation?"

"She said food."

"Everyone knows everything."

"Yes."

Efficient institution.

Upstairs was worse than yesterday.

Not emotionally.

Physically.

Document boxes occupied the floor around the middle desk.

Two ledgers lay open on the window ledge.

Emmet had removed his coat and rolled his sleeves to the elbow.

Mara had tied her hair back again and failed more completely this time.

Ink marked three fingers.

The blue cup sat near her elbow with something brown at the bottom.

Tea, hopefully.

Mara looked up when I entered.

Her face warmed.

Then she saw the bag.

"Not stew?"

"I can leave."

"Come here."

Different use of the phrase tonight.

Still effective.

I put the pies on a clear corner of the desk.

Clear by local standards.

There was one loose packet slip beneath them.

Mara pulled it out before grease could reach the paper.

"Food area," I said.

"No such thing."

"Hostile workplace."

Emmet took a pie without asking.

I looked at him.

"That was Mara's."

"There are four."

"You don't know the allocation."

"I have children."

Apparently that remained a universal bargaining position.

Mara pushed a second pie toward him.

"For later."

He looked at her.

"Now Greg has two."

"Greg bought them."

"Exactly."

Corruption.

I took the remaining bag to the window ledge and sat on the one chair currently free of paper.

Mara opened a pie with one hand and a ledger with the other.

"What got worse?"

She pointed with the pie.

"Nothing."

"Your note said worse."

"We know more now."

"That usually makes problems worse."

"Exactly."

Emmet slid one sheet across the desk.

Three dates.

Three route marks.

A warehouse number.

Mara tapped the middle line.

"Carrow logged the packet box on fourth day. River departure says fifth. Halden received on ninth."

"Missing day?"

"Maybe."

She frowned at the word as if it had violated office policy.

"Could be warehouse hold. Could be midnight transfer. Could be the river clerk copied departure instead of load."

Emmet said, "Quay mark."

Mara looked at him.

"What?"

He pointed at a small mark beside the warehouse number.

Two diagonal strokes.

"That's East Quay night cage."

"I thought that was warehouse four."

"Warehouse four is one slash with a hook."

Mara leaned closer.

Looked.

Then closed her eyes.

"I hate your city."

"Our city," I said.

"Not helping."

Emmet smiled into his pie.

Mara took the ledger from him.

"So the box sat overnight because river gate closed."

"Likely."

"Likely or yes?"

"If you want yes, find the night-cage book."

Mara looked around.

"Where?"

Emmet pointed at a stack behind her chair.

Bottom.

Of course.

I stood automatically.

Mara put one hand out without looking.

"No."

I stopped.

"I can lift a book."

"You don't know which one."

"Labels exist."

"Not good labels."

Emmet nodded.

"Very bad."

Mara moved the first two ledgers herself.

I sat back down.

Annoyed.

Not because she was wrong.

That did not improve it.

I ate my pie.

For the next hour I watched enough work to become bored.

The first twenty minutes were interesting because Mara was there.

Then dates became dates.

Emmet found the night-cage book.

Mara confirmed the packet had sat from late fourth day to first bell fifth.

That removed one missing day and created a different question about why Halden had recorded the handoff as warehouse delay.

A Carrow runner arrived with another sheet.

Mara read it twice.

Swore quietly.

Emmet said, "Told you."

She said, "You told me three possible things. One being right is not prophecy."

I liked her professionally and personally at the same time.

Inconvenient overlap.

At some point Mara ate the second half of her pie cold.

I gave her mine because I had accidentally bought enough for that to become possible.

She took it without gratitude theater.

Better.

The office lamps were fully lit by the time Emmet tied the last corrected packet bundle.

He stood.

"Tomorrow, first bell?"

Mara looked at him.

"You negotiated second today."

"Solved enough."

"We still have the Halden correction."

"You can write it on the wagon."

Mara stared.

Emmet put on his coat.

"My children remember me by shape."

"Go."

He went.

The door closed behind him.

For the first time all evening, Mara and I were alone.

Not in my room.

Not on a bed.

Two desks, eight boxes, three open ledgers, and one stolen pie wrapper between us.

Mara leaned back in her chair.

Her eyes closed.

I watched her for perhaps three seconds.

Then five.

"You're staring."

Eyes still closed.

"No."

"Greg."

"You cannot prove it."

She opened one eye.

"Better room last night."

My entire body remembered before I answered.

"Strongly agree."

Mara smiled.

Tired.

Not enough energy to hide it.

I went to her desk.

Stopped on the other side.

She looked up.

"You canceled dinner."

"I know."

"I am disappointed."

Mara waited.

"That's all?"

"I was told not to approve applications."

Her smile widened.

"Learning."

"Dangerous claim."

"Very."

She reached across the desk and took my hand.

Not much.

Fingers around two of mine.

Ink touched my knuckle.

"I'm disappointed too."

There.

Still helped.

Still unfair that it helped.

"When do you leave?"

Mara looked toward the window.

River dark beyond it.

"First bell."

I had known she might.

Knowing and hearing were different activities.

"Tomorrow."

"Yes."

"You got one day late and leave one day early."

"Vey efficiency."

"Criminal."

"Write a complaint."

"To who?"

"Pell. He likes paper."

"And onions."

"Unfortunately."

I squeezed her fingers once.

"Second night lost."

"Yes."

Mara did not make the loss smaller.

I looked at the ledgers.

"You staying here until when?"

"Until I finish the Halden correction."

"How long?"

"An hour if the numbers behave."

"They haven't."

"Then two."

I wanted to stay.

Not help.

Just stay.

That felt pathetic enough to be true.

"Can I sit?"

Mara looked at the chair by the window.

"You were already sitting."

"Can I continue sitting?"

"If you stop asking what the numbers mean."

"Cruel."

"Terms."

I returned to the chair.

For another half hour, I read my book while Mara worked.

Actually read this time.

Mostly.

Page, Mara, page, river, Mara.

The rhythm improved when I stopped pretending the order was different.

Eventually she said, "Done."

I looked up too fast.

Mara caught it.

Said nothing.

She stacked the corrected sheets, tied them, and put the bundle into the top document box.

Then stood.

"Walk?"

"Yes."

We went downstairs.

The packet clerk had already closed the front shutters and left the side door unlatched for the upstairs office.

Outside, the river air was colder than last night.

Mara slipped her hand into mine before we reached the corner.

No hesitation this time.

We walked only one block.

Her lodging was above the Vey office.

Terrible design for romance.

Excellent for payroll.

At the side door Mara stopped.

"First bell," she said.

"River stairs?"

"Yes."

"I'll be there."

Mara raised one eyebrow.

I heard it too.

Another promise.

This one I meant enough to leave alone.

"Don't be early," she said.

"How early is early?"

"Before me."

"Unhelpful."

"Good night, Greg."

She kissed me.

Short.

Current.

Then went inside before I could turn one kiss into negotiation.

I walked back to Lower Rook alone.

Noll was still at The Brass Spoon.

Jorren was somewhere north-east with Beren and Vell.

Mara was one street behind me finishing a night in a room I could not enter just because I wanted to.

Tavin's letter was somewhere south.

My room was quiet when I opened it.

The two keys clicked together in the lock.

I put Mara's six-word note on the table beside the burned seven.

Then I went to bed before first bell could become another thing I checked twice...
