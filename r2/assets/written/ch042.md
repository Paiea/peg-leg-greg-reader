# Chapter 42: The Correspondent

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Story search: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/042-story-search.md`

---

Tavin's reply cost him four copper to send.

I knew this because he complained in the first sentence.

**Greg, your letter followed me to Rell Ford, then back to South Latch, then north again because Venn's clerk has the map sense of boiled cabbage. Four copper. You now owe six.**

I read it twice.

Not for meaning.

For pleasure.

The packet had arrived at Lower Rook folded beneath my door sometime while I was out helping guard a dead shardback from children and dogs.

Current life had improved its variety.

The envelope was thicker than Tavin's first one.

Not because he had written more.

Because somebody had stamped it repeatedly.

South Latch.

Rell Ford.

South Latch again.

Then Carrow.

Three route marks crossed one another on the back like the letter had been trying to escape.

I sat at the table in my larger room.

New table position.

Same burned seven of cups beside the brass key.

The two copper I had ceremonially set aside for Tavin still sat under the card.

Apparently debt could appreciate through postage.

I continued.

**The stone run is done. Nobody died. The foreman remains unbearable, which means the company has renewed him. I was right about the food. I was wrong about six weeks. It took seven because a wagon axle split outside Rell and we spent two days learning that local ash wood is not wagon oak no matter how confidently a man says it is.**

Useful field history.

**I got your letter after we came back south. Then Venn sent me north with lamp glass because somebody thinks Deepwinter is a good time to transport breakable things on frozen roads. I am writing from a room above the Blue Pike where the floor slopes enough that my boots moved during the night.**

Tavin had continued existing.

Good.

I had known he would.

Different thing from evidence.

The next paragraph was shorter.

**You said you don't remember talking about roads. You did. Too much.**

There.

I leaned back.

Tavin continued.

**You wanted Halden because Mara wanted Halden. You wanted Rell Ford because I said the pay was better. You wanted the coast because you had never seen it. You also wanted to come back to Carrow with enough money that nobody could tell you where to sleep. Those were separate plans. You kept combining them when drunk.**

I stopped.

Enough money that nobody could tell you where to sleep.

I looked around the room.

Three copper more a week.

Two windows.

Drying alcove.

Practice space.

Nobody had told me where to sleep.

Young Greg had apparently set his ambitions at a scale I could actually afford.

That hurt in a useful way.

Not prophecy.

Not destiny.

Rent.

The letter went on.

**You were never going to stay in Mara's mother's back room forever. Mara knew that. Her mother knew that. You knew it on good days. On bad days you acted like leaving meant proving you had not needed any of them. That part was stupid.**

I stared at the sentence.

Tavin's handwriting did not become more compassionate because the content had.

Same uneven letters.

Same hard downward stroke on every t.

Good.

He had not transformed into a historian because my memory was broken.

One correction.

Enough.

**If you really do not remember saying any of this, I believe you because lying about forgetting me would require more effort than you usually spend on friendship.**

There he was again.

I laughed.

Noll knocked on the wall from next door.

Not because he understood.

Because Lower Rook believed sound was communal property.

I knocked back once.

Relationship maintenance.

Tavin's letter moved to work.

**I am with Venn through the thaw unless they fire me or promote me by mistake. We run South Latch, Rell Ford, and two smaller river depots you would hate. After thaw there may be a coast run. There may not. Freight is not prophecy.**

I disliked the last sentence on principle.

Accurate anyway.

**If you come south, use Venn's office. If I am gone they will know which route I am on unless the clerk with boiled-cabbage maps has taken over the ledger. Do not come because you think I have work waiting. I do not. Come if you want to see me or if Carrow finally becomes as small as you used to claim.**

No job waiting.

No deadline.

Just a friend somewhere south.

I kept reading.

**Mara went west, then. I heard from her once before Halden. She said the Vey office hired her. Good. She always wrote cleaner numbers than you.**

I paused.

Mara had written Tavin before leaving.

That made sense.

They had both existed in the same young life.

I did not need to be the bridge between them.

**If you write her, tell her I still have the blue scarf she says I stole. She gave it to me because she hated it. This distinction matters.**

Useful dispute.

**As for the cards: two copper remains owed. I reject your evidentiary theory about the burned seven. Interest is now dinner if you ever appear in person. I will choose the place because you have proven financially irresponsible around food.**

I looked at the two copper under the card.

Six, according to Tavin.

Plus dinner.

Debt expansion had become relational.

The final lines were simpler.

**You sound strange. Not bad. Strange.**

**Write when you have something worth saying or nothing worth saying. Both are allowed.**

**Tavin**

I sat with that for a while.

Mara had told me to write something true.

Tavin had apparently lowered the standard to something or nothing.

Different relationships should not require the same language.

Obvious.

Still useful.

I read the road paragraph again.

Halden.

Rell Ford.

Coast.

Come back with enough money that nobody could tell you where to sleep.

Young Greg had been nineteen in a small room making too many plans at once.

That part I remembered from current experience.

The difference was he had not known which future would survive contact with money, people, weather, or his own fear.

Neither did I.

That was increasingly fair.

I took out paper.

No hesitation this time.

Not much.

I wrote:

**Tavin,**

**Four copper postage is your fault for being mobile. I recognize only the original two. Dinner may be negotiated.**

Good start.

**North Gate reopened one lane. I moved upstairs in the same building. The room is larger. Nobody tells me where to sleep except the landlord, indirectly, through rent. So apparently one of my plans worked.**

I stopped.

That was more personal than I expected.

Fine.

**I passed the support intake. Low reserve. Strong short control. Irregular experience. The examiner made that sound like a diagnosis. She was probably right.**

No need to name Kesra.

Tavin did not need future-history contamination for postage.

**I am still Bronze. I have been taking road work, ward work, warehouse work, and one job where I was paid to keep a dog away from a dead shardback. The dog performed better than several employers.**

I considered mentioning Mara.

He already knew enough.

Still.

**Mara is working farther west now, maybe toward Bracken route accounts. I will tell her about the scarf if I remember. I assume she will convict you.**

Then the road.

The actual question.

**I am not coming south now. That is not the same as no.**

I read the sentence.

Kept it.

**If you are still with Venn after thaw, write where. I want to see the coast eventually. I also want to know whether you actually remember me better than Mara does or are simply more confident.**

Too sharp?

No.

Tavin could survive confidence.

**Greg**

I did not include coins.

Wisdom remained.

At the packet office the route clerk looked at the South Latch address.

"Venn again?"

"Yes."

She checked the board.

"Three copper if it catches tomorrow's freight sack. Four if you want route-forwarding paid through Rell."

I stared at her.

Tavin had somehow been right about postage becoming debt.

"Four."

She held out her hand.

I paid.

"You know where he is?"

"Not exactly."

"Then four is smarter."

Expensive praise.

She stamped the letter.

Current relationship became logistics.

Again.

Outside, sleet had turned to dry cold.

The north road bells were louder now that the gate was open.

South traffic moved too.

Different route.

Different person.

I could go south.

Not today.

Not because a job waited.

That made the direction more real, somehow.

A road did not need to be urgent to exist...
