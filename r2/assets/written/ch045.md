# D171: Under the Quarry

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

The old door was under forty feet of quarry.

This was enough to make me interested before anyone explained why that was a problem.

"No," Kessa said.

I looked at her.

"I haven't said anything."

"Your face did."

Public institution again.

We stood on the third cut of North Quarry beneath a wall of gray slate where six days earlier a blasting charge had opened a hole that was not supposed to exist.

Not a cave.

Too square.

Not a mine.

Too old.

The quarry workers had cleared loose stone from the opening and discovered dressed blocks behind the natural seam. Then someone pushed a lantern through and saw steps.

That was when responsible adults stopped touching things.

Mostly.

One quarry apprentice had gone down seven steps before the foreman dragged him back by the belt.

He had been offended by safety for the rest of the day.

The Guild paper called it **subsurface constructed void, unknown age, possible residual warding**.

Excellent phrase.

Ruins became much less romantic when insured.

Edda stood beside the opening with her short spear and a helmet I had never seen her wear in Carrow.

"You smiling?" she asked.

"No."

Jorren answered for me.

"Yes."

Betrayal.

I had spent most of the last six weeks doing work whose biggest mysteries were loose anchors, bad drainage, dishonest inspection dates, and why Kessa continued to own a ladder one inch shorter on the left side.

Kessa claimed the floor was always wrong.

Statistically aggressive.

The quarry had changed around us during those weeks.

Summer heat had gone.

Morning breath now showed in the upper cut before sunlight reached it.

The waste pile Savi had complained about had been moved downhill and fenced. No needleback had returned.

A new timber shed stood beside the tool yard.

The foreman had installed a bell for blasting shifts after two workers missed the old hand signal in fog.

I knew these things because North Quarry had become one of my routes.

That was probably why Sella had put my name on the paper before I reached the Guild that morning.

Kessa for rigging and structure.

Edda for anything alive that objected.

Jorren because road and quarry crews trusted him with rope, watches, and people who became stupid near holes.

Me because I could shape under load and had apparently developed a reputation for asking whether the stone wanted to remain stone.

I had not phrased it that way.

Sella had.

Malicious clerk.

The fifth person was Harl Neven, quarry master for the north cuts.

He was short, wide, bald, and uninterested in Guild hierarchy because everything above our heads belonged to his problem.

I liked him immediately.

Harl pointed into the opening.

"We own down to natural stone. We don't own whatever this is."

"Who does?" Jorren asked.

"If it's old city, city. If it's old crown, crown. If it's older than crown, everybody argues until winter."

"And quarry?"

"Quarry owns the hole we made getting there."

Clear jurisdiction.

Kessa crouched at the opening.

The first visible stair was stone, worn at the center despite being buried longer than anyone currently working the quarry could account for.

A thin copper line ran along the right wall.

Green with age.

Broken in two places near the blast opening.

I looked at it.

"Ward?"

Kessa said, "Maybe."

Edda looked at me.

"You asking because you know?"

"No."

"Good."

Everyone had learned.

We tied a safety line outside.

Not around each person.

Around a waist belt on whoever went first, with enough slack to move and enough people outside to make falling socially difficult.

Harl stayed above.

His quarry.

His workers.

His decision if the cut became unsafe.

Edda went first.

Not me.

Very unfair.

She descended ten steps.

Stopped.

"Landing."

Kessa followed.

Then me.

Jorren last.

The air changed after the fifth step.

Cooler.

Dry.

Quarry dust smell vanished and something mineral replaced it.

Old stone.

Copper.

A faint sourness I associated with dead ward compound and later buildings I had not yet visited in this life.

I did not trust the association.

Current nose first.

The stair ended at a landing perhaps eight feet square.

Door ahead.

Actual door.

Stone frame.

Two heavy wooden leaves reinforced with blackened iron straps.

The wood should have rotted.

It had not.

That was the first magical thing.

Not glowing.

Not whispering.

Still being wood.

Edda touched nothing.

Excellent adventurer.

Kessa crouched near the floor.

A narrow groove crossed the threshold and disappeared beneath both walls.

"Drain?" Jorren asked.

"Maybe."

"Everyone says maybe down here."

"Keeps us alive," Edda said.

I held the lantern closer to the right wall.

Copper line descended from the stairs and entered a ceramic block beside the door.

Three more lines left the block.

One up.

One down.

One around the frame.

"Meren would enjoy this," I said.

"Meren enjoys invoices."

"Also cracked ceramics."

"Because of invoices."

Fair.

No symbols I recognized.

Not decorative.

A real wedge with graduated marks cut along one side.

Kessa leaned closer.

"Don't."

I had not moved.

Again.

"My face?"

"Your hand."

I moved it back.

Growth remained uneven.

Edda checked the edges of the landing.

"Anything alive?"

Jorren asked.

"Us."

"Comforting."

This was excellent.

No one tried to open it.

Kessa traced cracks in the frame without touching them.

I held the lantern.

Edda listened.

"Load's wrong."

Barely.

Enough that the horizontal iron strap no longer lined perfectly with its opposite.

"Sag?"

"Maybe."

Kessa smiled.

Us.

Human improvement again.

Good quarry master.
