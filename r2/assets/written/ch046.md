# Year Chapter 04: The Road Hand

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D017 / SPRING`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 8`

`LOCATION: West Spur road`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

Eight days after South Four, the bite bruises on Lessa's shin had gone yellow and my sword had acquired a better edge than before the hounds damaged it.

This seemed unfair to Lessa.

She told me so when we crossed in the Guild lobby.

"Your sword improved. My leg became ugly."

"You could sharpen your leg."

"I regret meeting you."

"Vaska says Silver team cleared the nest."

"I know. I was there."

I stopped.

"You went back?"

"Healer support."

There it was.

Life continuing in places I had left.

The Silver crew trapped three more hounds and found an egg clutch in an old warm-water branch. City moved the eggs rather than destroy them after someone from river ecology complained.

I had spent eight days imagining South Four as a completed event because my part ended.

It had not.

Lessa had been back twice.

Vaska probably more.

"How's leg?"

"Fine."

"Actually?"

"Actually fine."

She kicked my boot lightly to prove it.

Then left for another job.

I stood in the lobby holding a road token.

First actual road.

Two days, maybe three.

West Spur mill hardware run.

Driver: Ossa Marel.

I knew the name only because Sella had said, "If Ossa tells you to move, move before asking why."

This was apparently a recommendation.

At East Gate, Ossa was under her cart.

Only boots visible.

Small boots.

A wrench struck wood.

"Ossa?"

"If you're Greg, hold mule."

"How do you know?"

"You're tall and early enough to be anxious."

Carrow had developed face-reading into a regional craft.

I took the mule's lead.

The animal was gray, patient, and named Murn according to a brass tag on harness.

"Morning, Murn."

He ignored me.

Ossa slid out.

She was smaller than I expected from the confidence of her profanity. Dark hair cut at jaw. Work coat patched at both elbows. Hands black with hub grease.

"Cart knowledge?"

"Minimal."

"Mule?"

"Also minimal."

"Road?"

"Some. Maybe too much in wrong direction."

She stared.

"Sella said weird."

"Sella talks too much."

"Good. We agree."

She took lead and handed me a wooden wheel chock.

"Put behind left rear."

I did.

"Why?"

"Now you can ask."

I liked her immediately.

The cart carried two wrapped mill belts, a crate of ceramic bearings, six sacks of lamp-black pigment, and one box of small ward pins that Ossa told me not to drop because customer counted them individually out of spite.

My official role was second body and Guild guard.

Fourteen copper plus food.

Road mark if Ossa signed.

The West Spur itself began disappointingly close to vegetable farms.

No mountains.

No monsters.

No mysterious horizon.

Mud.

Spring hedges.

Children walking beside goats.

A man repairing a wall.

I loved it.

Not every second.

The first hour my pack strap rubbed collarbone.

The second hour Murn farted directly beside me twice.

At third bell a light rain began and found the narrow gap between hood and neck.

Road desire survived all three.

Ossa drove some stretches and walked others. She made me take the driver's board on a flat mile after asking whether I could hold reins.

"Probably."

"Bad answer."

"I can hold reins. I cannot claim I know Murn."

"Better."

She handed them over.

The mule immediately slowed.

"He knows," I said.

"Knows what?"

"New management."

"He knows you let him."

Ossa clicked tongue once.

Murn sped up.

I tried.

Nothing.

She laughed for a full minute.

Power fantasy delayed.

We stopped near noon at a farmhouse that sold travelers soup through side window. Ossa knew the woman by name.

"Mira."

Different Mira than Stonehook? The world could contain two, but reader confusion was pointless. Her name was Yessa.

"Ossa. Same mule?"

"Unfortunately."

Yessa gave Murn apple peel.

The animal suddenly possessed personality.

"Traitor," I said.

He ate.

Road repeated people.

That was what I had not understood from maps.

Not distance.

Relationship infrastructure.

Ossa knew which farm had soup, which culvert usually flooded, which gate keeper accepted exact toll and which one pretended not to have change.

Old Greg remembered West Spur as a line.

Ossa knew it as names.

After lunch the road climbed toward low ridge.

Rain strengthened.

Water moved in ditches.

At the south shoulder, Ossa stopped the cart.

I saw the problem a heartbeat later.

The outer road edge had slumped.

Not collapsed fully.

A long crescent crack ran parallel to the wheel rut, twenty feet or more.

Fresh mud at bottom of ditch showed one section had already fallen.

"Walk it," Ossa said.

We tied Murn and went ahead on foot.

The north edge pressed against a bank with scrub and two large stones. Enough room for a cart if the damaged shoulder held.

Maybe.

I crouched beside crack.

Poked with stick.

Soft beneath.

"No."

Ossa looked.

"You know road?"

"I know gravity."

"Useful."

She walked farther, then looked uphill.

A field lane cut over ridge through a gate.

"Marr."

"Person?"

"Disease."

We backed the cart thirty yards to lane.

The gate was chained.

An old man in red cap appeared from nowhere before Ossa touched it.

"Four copper."

"Two."

"Rain. Four."

"Rain is free."

"Not on my field."

"Three."

"Five because you started at two."

I watched economic warfare.

Marr looked at me.

"Who's giant?"

"Greg."

"He damages field, six."

"I haven't moved."

"Preventative."

I liked him against judgment.

Ossa paid three and made him sign route paper.

Marr complained while producing ink immediately.

The field lane climbed wet grass.

Murn disliked it.

Cart wheels slipped.

Ossa walked bridle. I pushed rear rail on steepest section.

Mud entered left boot over ankle because one rut hid water.

At barn, barking started.

Two dogs behind fence.

One jumped.

Not over.

Through a broken board.

Lean black dog came straight at Murn.

Mule kicked.

Ossa pulled bridle.

I stepped between without thinking.

The dog changed target to me and leapt.

My hand went to sword.

Stopped.

Not channel hound.

Farm dog.

I caught collar instead.

Bad idea physically.

Momentum twisted shoulder.

Dog snapped near wrist.

Leather bracer took teeth.

"Pike!"

Marr ran from gate, surprisingly fast.

Of course the dog's name was Pike.

I held collar with both hands, keeping head angled away.

Pike's paws churned mud.

Marr reached us and grabbed scruff.

"You bastard."

Dog wagged at him.

"Your dog has poor professional boundaries," I said.

Marr inspected bracer.

"Bite?"

"No."

"Good leather."

"Bad dog."

"Good dog. Bad taste."

Ossa had Murn settled.

She looked at sword hand, then dog.

"Didn't cut him."

"I noticed he wasn't a drain monster."

"Some Guild don't."

That was depressing.

We continued.

Field lane rejoined road above slump.

Rain eased by late afternoon.

My left boot stayed wet.

Murn forgave nobody.

The mill lay another three miles west beside narrow river, two water wheels turning under gray sky.

We unloaded until dark.

Not dramatic.

Belts first. Bearings second. Pigment under shed. Ward pins counted individually by a clerk who proved Ossa had not exaggerated.

One pin missing.

The clerk became excited.

Ossa opened crate false bottom.

Pin stuck in paper fold.

His disappointment was beautiful.

We slept in mill loft.

Dinner was oat stew and bread.

I removed wet sock and hung it near stove.

Ossa repaired a harness strap with waxed thread.

"Still want road?" she asked.

"Yes."

"First day."

"You sound like Sella."

"Insult."

"Agreed."

She bit thread clean.

"Why Black Stair?"

I looked at her.

"Everyone knows?"

"You checked date three times."

The folded paper had come out at toll gate and mill office.

Apparently secrets required better habits.

"Because I want to know if I can get there as me."

Too honest.

Ossa stopped sewing.

"Who else would go?"

Complicated.

"Old version of me."

She considered this without context.

"Dead?"

"Sort of."

"Then bad competition."

Simple.

I laughed.

Outside, mill wheel turned steadily.

My boot steamed by stove.

The Black Stair was still 348 days away.

Today I had traveled maybe fifteen miles and paid Marr three copper to avoid falling into a ditch.

The scale difference should have embarrassed me.

Instead it made me hungry for morning.
