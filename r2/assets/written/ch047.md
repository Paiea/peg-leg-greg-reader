# Year Chapter 05: The Trainee

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D028 / SPRING`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 11`

`LOCATION: Carrow / Lower Rook`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

Eleven days changed the bruise on my forearm from Pike's teeth from purple to yellow and changed Noll's apron from brown to blue.

He had bought the second one himself.

That mattered to him more than I understood.

"Better," he said.

He stood in Lower Rook courtyard before dawn tying it over his work shirt.

"You paid for fabric because the restaurant's free apron offended you."

"Yes."

"How much?"

"Four."

"Robbed."

"Your boots cost twenty-one."

I looked down.

New boots.

He had me.

The old pair sat upstairs drying even though they were no longer wet. Three short road jobs and four freight shifts had taught me exactly which seam failed first. The left heel had worn thin enough that cobbler recommended patching before summer.

Instead I had spent twenty-one copper on a heavier pair with replaceable heel caps and better ankle wrap.

Expensive enough to resent.

Good enough that I had worn them around Carrow for three days before trusting them on a road.

The money came from work.

That felt different from simply possessing money.

My Guild token now carried seven marks and three signed notes.

Stonehook.

South Four.

Ossa's one-line review after West Spur:

`LISTENS AFTER FIRST ARGUMENT. USEFUL WITH LOAD. DO NOT LET HIM DRIVE MURN ALONE.`

I had objected to the first sentence.

Sella said the second mattered more.

I objected to that too.

Spring itself had finally stopped pretending to be winter.

The courtyard tree had small green leaves. Morning rain smelled warmer. Laundry hung outside without freezing stiffness. Market sellers had strawberries at prices designed for nobility.

Jorren returned to Carrow six days after my West Spur run.

I learned two days later because he did not come find me.

That hurt more than I expected.

Not because he owed me.

Because some part of me had assumed I would be first stop.

Instead he slept half a day, washed, delivered Beren's papers, then took temporary work at Meln's factor yard because a clerk there had broken his ankle and somebody remembered Jorren could count.

I found him on D024 standing on a platform shouting numbers at flour sacks.

He looked happy.

Not glowing.

Not transformed.

Busy.

Current life had used him before I could.

Today he was coming to Lower Rook for breakfast after first shift.

Noll left before him.

"Close tonight," Noll said.

"Again?"

"Three nights now."

"They trust you with close?"

He looked offended.

"Why not?"

"No reason."

There was a reason. I still had a twelve-year-old version of Noll lodged in memory beside the current one.

Current Noll had keys to Brass Spoon back door.

Old Greg's memories were not only missing people. Sometimes they kept people too small.

"Good luck," I said.

Noll shrugged and left.

I went upstairs to pack for nothing.

That was the problem.

No road contract today.

I had checked the Guild board yesterday and again this morning before sunrise.

Two available routes required Silver.

One three-day courier escort had been claimed.

A five-day quarry run wanted archers.

I owned a sword and opinions.

Neither counted.

So I cleaned my pack.

New boots.

Dry socks.

Oilcloth repaired after field lane.

Knife sharpened.

Sword chip gone.

I had begun accumulating the habits of somebody who might leave while remaining in the room.

Mara's last letter sat on table.

Arrived D022.

Six days ago.

She wrote about Halden rain, Vey changing one freight schedule three times, a woman named Sera threatening a clerk with a ledger, and a blue cup she had broken by dropping it on stairs.

No declaration.

No relationship contract.

At bottom:

**You keep writing about roads as if they are women you are trying not to admit you like. Go on another one.**

Then:

**Come west when you actually have time. Warning first.**

I had read `come west` more often than the rest.

Apparently my selective attention remained healthy.

Jorren knocked once and entered before answer.

He had changed coat.

Not new.

Patched green cloth at left elbow where old brown patch had been.

"You bought boots," he said.

"You changed patch."

"Mine cheaper."

"Unproven."

He sat in second chair.

Everyone did.

"Road?" I asked.

"No."

"Factor?"

"Yes."

"How long?"

"Meln asked through summer."

There.

I had spent eleven days assuming Jorren's next road waited around a bend.

He had accepted a season of local work.

"You said yes?"

"Yes."

My disappointment arrived before generosity.

Ugly but true.

"You like it?"

"Some."

"More than road?"

Jorren looked at me.

"Why choose?"

Excellent answer.

I hated it.

He worked factor mornings, could still take occasional short route if Meln released him, and liked sleeping in same bed for more than three nights.

"Beren?"

"He said boring."

"Is he right?"

"Yes."

"And?"

"Still doing."

There.

Current Jorren allowed boring things.

I was still treating every choice as identity evidence.

We went downstairs for breakfast because my room contained no food except dried oats and one hard cheese rind.

Maud served eggs.

Jorren ate four.

"Road starved you?"

"Factor work."

"Counting burns calories?"

"Arguing."

Fair.

After breakfast he had only an hour before yard.

I wanted to drag conversation longer.

Instead we walked east together.

Carrow had grown greener during his absence. Vines on one wall. Market awnings open. Children playing in drainage water with sticks.

At East Square, a crowd had formed around a Guild demonstration yard.

Not event exactly.

Spring assessment day.

Bronze members could test practical modules for endorsements: rope climb, weighted carry, basic ward recognition, blade control, casualty drag.

I stopped.

Jorren followed gaze.

"You want."

"I have time."

"Same."

"You need factor."

"Yes."

He clapped my shoulder.

"Do it."

Then left.

No watching.

No validation.

I registered anyway.

Two copper.

The assessment was less glamorous than imagined.

Carry sandbag fifty yards.

Climb twelve-foot rope.

Identify which of four simple ward plates was overloaded.

Drag a weighted dummy around barrels.

Then blade lane against padded target arms that swung from hinges.

My body did well.

Not perfectly.

Sandbag awkward because long arms made shoulder placement strange.

Rope easy enough until wrists remembered Stonehook and weakened near top.

Ward plate obvious.

Casualty drag easier than expected because Old Greg had moved people more times than I wanted to remember.

Blade lane was where things changed.

Instructor demonstrated sequence.

Six targets.

Step, parry, cut, turn, duck, thrust, disengage.

Bronze safety pace.

I watched once.

Then went through.

First target too slow.

I had to wait for it.

Second, I cut inside hinge before padded arm finished swing.

Third, ducked late deliberately because if I moved at remembered pace I would outrun apparatus.

The instructor frowned.

"Again. Full."

"Full what?"

"Your pace."

I looked at wooden arms.

"They'll break."

"Don't hit hard. Move fast."

That I could do.

Second run.

I stopped thinking.

The lane shortened.

Parry barely touched first arm.

Blade traveled to second in same turn.

Duck.

Step inside third.

Reverse grip? No. Current practice did not need flourish.

I passed six targets before final arm completed swing.

Silence around lane.

Not whole yard.

Enough.

Instructor looked at my Bronze tab.

Then me.

"Where trained?"

Memory offered too many answers.

"Long story."

"Useful?"

"Probably not."

"Name."

"Greg."

He wrote something on assessment card.

"Again with live partner."

There.

A Silver assistant named Callen stepped into ring with padded sword and face guard.

"Control only," instructor said.

Callen nodded.

I felt excitement.

Simple.

No shame.

We touched blades.

Callen attacked first.

Competent.

Current-world competent.

Not slow apparatus.

I gave ground one step, caught second strike, turned wrist, and hit his shoulder before he recovered.

One point.

He blinked.

Again.

This time he feinted low and came high.

I knew before shoulder moved.

Old experience.

I stepped inside and put padded edge against ribs.

Two.

Callen smiled.

Not angry.

"Again."

Third exchange lasted longer.

He adapted.

Good.

He crowded distance and used body instead of blade.

I nearly tripped over boundary rope.

Recovered badly, took hit on upper arm.

One for him.

I laughed.

Actually laughed.

This was fun.

Fourth exchange, I stopped trying to preserve mystery.

Three beats.

Bind.

Shoulder check.

Turn his blade past hip.

My padded sword rested at throat.

Callen froze.

The instructor said, "Enough."

I stepped back immediately.

Breathing faster.

Happy.

There was no corrective disaster.

Nobody fell through floor.

Nobody told me I was arrogant.

Callen removed face guard.

"Bronze?"

"Yes."

"Not with blade."

I liked that sentence so much it became embarrassing.

I accepted embarrassment later.

Instructor wrote on card:

`BLADE CONTROL: SILVER TRAINING RANGE. FIELD RECORD INSUFFICIENT FOR RANK REVIEW.`

There.

Not promotion.

Evidence.

I carried that card to Guild desk with stupid care.

Sella read it.

"You look unbearable."

"I said nothing."

"Worse."

She added it to my file.

"What does this unlock?"

"Some Bronze combat jobs will take you with blade endorsement. Doesn't make you Silver."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Today yes."

She nodded.

I checked road board.

One new posting had appeared while I tested.

**NORTH EDGE WAYSTATION / TWO DAYS / BEAST PRESSURE / BRONZE WITH BLADE ENDORSEMENT ACCEPTED.**

I touched my new assessment card.

Then the posting.

Eleven days ago I had wanted a road and had none.

Now one existed because I spent a morning proving something I already thought I knew.

I took the token.

Sella smiled into her mug.

I did not ask why.
