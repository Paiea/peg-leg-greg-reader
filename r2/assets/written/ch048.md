# Year Chapter 06: South Latch

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D041 / SPRING`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 13`

`LOCATION: East Warehouse Row, Carrow`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

Thirteen days gave me a new scar on my left thigh and Jorren a desk.

Mine came from a thorncat outside North Edge Waystation.

His came from Meln deciding that anyone who could count sacks without stealing should be allowed to sit occasionally.

I considered my progression more glamorous until I saw the desk.

It had a drawer.

"You have a drawer."

Jorren looked up from a stack of route sheets.

"Yes."

"What do you keep in it?"

"Things."

"That is not an answer."

He opened it.

String.

Two pencils.

A knife.

Half a dried apple.

One sock.

"Why sock?"

"Dry."

I closed the drawer.

"I expected institutional secrets."

"Sock is secret."

Current Jorren had spent almost two weeks getting better at factor work while I took three Guild contracts, one road, and two days limping after a thorncat clawed through my trouser leg.

The wound itself had been shallow.

The scar looked impressive because bodies enjoyed poor proportionality.

I had also earned another blade note from North Edge lead:

`FAST. DOES NOT FREEZE. NEEDS TO STOP CHASING AFTER FIRST SAFE LINE.`

I objected to the last part because I had chased exactly nine steps.

Sella asked how many steps the lead ordered.

Zero.

The objection died.

My new boots had stopped feeling new.

The heel caps showed road scratches. Leather had darkened from rain. I no longer noticed them every time I looked down.

That might have been the clearest sign of elapsed time.

Objects stopped announcing themselves.

Today I was not at Meln's yard to admire Jorren's furniture.

I was waiting for a Venn freight train from South Latch.

Tavin's letter had arrived D033.

Eight days ago.

It began:

**Your memory letter was terrible. Better.**

Then told me I had not missed his send-off.

I had been late.

Drunk later.

Apparently cried after three beers and attempted to challenge someone twice my size because nineteen-year-old Greg had been committed to making every social situation expensive.

No memory returned when I read it.

The fact still settled into me.

I had come.

Late was different from absent.

Then Tavin wrote that a South Latch freight line might put him in Carrow around D041 if weather and axle gods allowed.

I had checked the date seven times.

Not obsessively.

Calendar discipline.

Jorren had heard enough.

"He'll come."

"Maybe."

"You say maybe when you want yes."

"You own a sock drawer. Your judgment is compromised."

A yard bell rang.

Three wagons entered East Row.

Blue-green Venn canvas.

My body stood before I decided to.

Jorren smiled.

"Go."

"You're coming."

"Working."

There.

"Later?"

"Later."

I left him at his desk.

The Venn wagons rolled into receiving lane amid ordinary noise. Drivers shouting. Clerks waving lane flags. A man on barrel cart complaining that Venn blocked his turn.

I scanned faces.

Nothing.

First wagon crew unfamiliar.

Second.

Then someone climbed down from the third.

Lean.

Brown coat too large at shoulder.

Red scarf stained at one end with seal wax.

Hair shorter than the version my mind had failed to invent.

He landed, slipped in grain dust, windmilled once, recovered, and immediately looked around to see whether anyone noticed.

I noticed.

He saw me.

Recognition did not arrive like memory.

It arrived like my body had been waiting for a shape.

Broken nose slightly left.

Scar through eyebrow.

Eyes that knew me before I knew what to do with them.

Tavin stared.

Then said, "You got tall."

I laughed.

"You didn't."

His face broke into a grin.

He crossed the lane.

I expected a joke.

He punched my shoulder.

"Ow."

"Good."

Then hugged me.

Hard.

No warning.

My arms came up automatically.

For a moment the yard disappeared behind wool and smoke and road smell.

No missing memory filled in.

I did not need one.

Tavin released first.

Looked me over.

"You look terrible."

"I own new boots."

"Worse."

"You smell like wax."

"Fuck wax."

Good.

A woman shouted from wagon.

"Tavin! South manifest!"

He closed his eyes.

"Work."

"Go."

He pointed at me.

"Don't leave."

The words hit harder than he intended.

"I'm not."

"Good."

He ran back.

I stayed because I had no job here and because leaving would have been insane.

Then the yard gave me one.

Receiving clerk recognized me from Meln's factor jobs and shoved a chalk board into my hands.

"You free?"

"Technically."

"Count Venn eleven through seventeen. Rami's sick."

"Pay?"

"Half day."

"Amount?"

He named six copper.

I took chalk.

Tavin watched from wagon and laughed.

"Of course you're working."

"Apparently."

For the next hour we occupied the same yard separately.

That was stranger than reunion.

Tavin knew freight.

Not in vague future-memory terms.

Current hands.

He checked seal numbers, lifted canvas corners, found a cracked strap, argued with a driver about water damage, and moved around his crew without waiting for me to validate any of it.

I counted packages.

Eleven.

Twelve.

Thirteen.

At fifteen, one crate mark did not match board.

I called receiving clerk.

Tavin heard.

"South copy wrong," he said from cart bed.

"How know?"

"Because I wrote it wrong."

"Excellent system."

"I crossed it out."

He showed manifest.

Correction existed.

Our copy had been made before.

Current logistics defeated blame with timing.

Then wagon two moved.

Nobody meant it to.

Rear wheel chock cracked under load.

A sharp wood snap.

Wheel rolled.

Driver shouted.

The wagon sat on slight yard slope.

Heavy barrels inside.

It moved six inches.

Then a foot.

One loader was between rear wheel and stacking platform.

Tavin jumped from cart bed.

I dropped chalk.

No need for magic first.

There was a spare chock three steps away.

I kicked it toward wheel.

Missed by inches.

Tavin caught it with boot, shoved down.

Wheel struck wood.

Chock skidded.

Not enough.

"Brake!" someone yelled.

Driver hauled lever.

Mechanism caught late.

Wagon jolted.

Top barrel shifted against side rail.

Rope snapped.

Barrel rolled off.

Heavy.

Tavin was under its line.

Now magic.

I threw a force plane without thinking about whether Stonehook had taught it or memory had.

Angled, not stop.

Barrel hit.

Mana buckled sideways.

My wrists held better than D004.

Not strong enough to arrest it.

Enough to change direction.

The barrel slammed into empty pallet instead of Tavin's knees.

Wood broke.

Barrel did not.

Tavin froze half a heartbeat.

Then jumped back.

The wagon stopped.

People moved.

Receiving lead shouted for secondary chocks.

Crew secured barrels.

Tavin came toward me.

I expected thanks.

He hit my shoulder again.

Same one.

"Stop doing that."

"What the fuck was that?"

"Force."

"You can do that?"

"Some."

He looked at broken pallet.

Then my Bronze tab.

Then me.

"Your letter omitted useful information."

"Your letter omitted eyebrow scar."

His fingers touched it reflexively.

"You don't remember?"

"No."

Expression changed.

Not pity.

Something quieter.

"Fence," he said.

"What?"

"You always said fence when you didn't want to explain scars. That one's actually fence. Fell drunk climbing Sorn's yard wall."

I laughed.

The tension broke.

"You are a terrible historian."

"Accurate historian."

"Why were you climbing?"

"You bet me."

Of course.

Freight lead shouted Tavin's name again.

He turned.

"Later."

"Later."

This time word felt less like uncertainty.

The wagon incident cost us two hours.

Broken chock replaced. Barrel inspected. Pallet charged to Venn or yard pending argument. Receiving clerk recovered chalk board from mud.

I finished counting.

Tavin finished unloading.

Work after adrenaline.

At third bell Venn crew released him until morning.

We walked toward Lower Rook carrying his small travel bag between us because the strap had torn and he refused to pay a porter.

"Two chairs?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Your letter was real?"

"Most of it."

"Mara?"

"West."

"Still?"

"Halden."

"You and her?"

"Seeing each other."

Tavin stopped in middle of street.

A cart driver cursed us.

We moved.

"Again?" Tavin said.

My stomach tightened.

"Again?"

His face changed.

"Shit."

"Tavin."

"You really don't remember that part."

"No."

We stood under awning while spring shower began suddenly.

People flowed around us.

"What happened?" I asked.

He rubbed face.

"You liked her. She liked you. You fought. You tried to decide something for her. She decided something without you. Both complained to me separately until I considered murder."

"Specific."

"That's all I know."

"Did we sleep together?"

Tavin's eyes widened.

Then he laughed so hard he had to lean against wall.

"What?"

"Finally."

"Finally what?"

"You asked a normal fucking question."

"Answer."

"No idea."

I shoved his shoulder.

He almost dropped bag.

"Careful. Freight professional."

"Fraud."

At Lower Rook, Maud looked at Tavin and said, "No."

He stopped.

"I haven't done anything."

"You look like trouble."

"Greg lives here."

"Exactly."

I laughed.

Noll came through side door in blue apron, saw Tavin, and stopped.

This mattered more than whether I remembered.

"Tavin."

"Noll."

Tavin grinned.

Noll punched him in chest.

Then they hugged.

Current relationships existed where I had holes.

The pain was real.

So was the relief.

I stood there holding half Tavin's bag like an idiot.

Noll looked at me.

"Food."

Of course.

We ate upstairs.

Three people, two chairs, one bed.

Tavin stole from my bowl.

Noll stole from his.

Justice.

Later Jorren arrived after factor shift because Noll sent a runner without asking me.

He and Tavin insulted each other's coats for five minutes before cards appeared.

Nobody asked me to mediate their history.

At midnight Maud hit ceiling with broom.

We lowered voices.

Tavin slept on floor using coat as pillow.

I lay awake listening to his breathing.

Forty-one days into the year, the Black Stair felt farther than it had on D001.

Not because I was failing.

Because current life was getting heavier.

I had not expected progress to make leaving harder.
