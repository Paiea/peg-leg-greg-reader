# Chapter 49: The Empty Board

The temporary Guild had too much wall and not enough work on it.

That was not literally true.

East Annex had vegetables on the other side of the canvas, two clerks arguing over reconstructed contract numbers, three clients waiting to be told whether their paperwork still existed, and enough damp coats to qualify as weather.

What it did not have was a job I wanted.

I read the board twice anyway.

One warehouse rat contract.

One two-day drain clearing with a note that said **strong stomach preferred**.

One north-gate cart escort already claimed.

Two roof-inspection jobs marked Silver.

A laundry boiler that had developed opinions.

I considered the boiler.

It had apparently boiled a copper fitting out of the wall yesterday.

I stopped considering the boiler.

Sella looked up from the intake table.

"You planning to stare one into existence?"

"I was hoping the board felt guilty."

"It doesn't."

"Bad institution."

"You came here voluntarily."

This remained one of her strongest arguments.

I checked the rat contract again.

Not because I had developed a passion for rats.

Because money still mattered and an empty morning remained an empty morning.

The rate was ordinary.

The client wanted it done before fourth bell.

The warehouse sat south of the dye lane.

I had done worse.

Then a man behind me said, "Taken."

I turned.

Bronze token. Broad shoulders. Missing front tooth.

He pointed at the chalk mark I had missed beside the contract number.

Claimed ten minutes ago.

"Congratulations," I said.

"Thanks."

He took the slip down.

There went the rats.

I felt almost abandoned.

Lera came through the east door carrying six practice sticks under one arm.

Her coat had charcoal dust on the shoulder.

Not soot.

Different problem.

"You free?" she asked.

Dangerous phrasing.

"Currently."

"Good. Carry these."

Of course.

I took three.

"Where?"

"Cooper Street drill court."

That got my attention.

"You won?"

"Nobody won. Watch gets mornings. Guild gets two afternoons and Sixthday after midday if the cooper association isn't using the yard for cart inspection."

Temporary space.

Shared schedule.

No miracle rebuilding.

"How bad?"

"Surface is level. Fence is intact. One corner smells like glue."

"Luxury."

"Exactly."

We walked.

The court occupied a long rectangle behind three cooper shops, half packed dirt, half old paving. The watch had painted lane lines on one side. A stack of barrel hoops leaned against the other wall.

Lera pointed with her chin.

"There."

A chalk board listed times.

**GUILD OPEN DRILL**

**PAID INSTRUCTION BY BOOKING**

Below that, somebody had written:

**DO NOT HIT COOPERS.**

Different hand.

Probably useful.

"When?" I asked.

Lera set down her sticks.

"For you?"

"Yes."

"Next Thirdday. Second bell after midday."

I counted money automatically.

One silver still hurt.

Less than it used to.

Different kind of hurt.

"Book it."

Lera looked at me.

Not surprised.

Not proud.

Just checking.

"Second sword block?"

"Second sword block."

She wrote my name.

No work contract had appeared.

No emergency had needed me.

I had come to the Guild intending to earn money and instead arranged to spend some.

Progress had remained financially offensive.

Lera handed me one practice stick.

"Since you're here."

I stared at it.

"Free?"

"Five minutes."

"That is not the same thing."

"Guard high."

I guarded high.

She struck low.

I blocked badly.

Five minutes became seven because Lera's relationship with time was tactical.

Then she stopped.

"Thirdday."

"I remember."

"You remember many things."

Fair.

I left the court without a job.

At East Market I bought boot grease, onions, and a strip of dried pear that cost more than it deserved to.

Back at Lower Rook, Noll was carrying coal upstairs.

"Work?" he asked.

"No."

He looked at the onions.

"Tragic."

"I found training."

"Worse."

Also fair.

I greased my boots by the window.

The second window still made the room feel excessive sometimes.

Two places for weather to enter.

Two places for light.

Enough floor to practice.

Enough money to pay for instruction even after a morning failed to become wages.

The board had been empty for me.

The day had not...
