# Chapter 51: The Court

Lera hit me in the shoulder because I blocked correctly.

This felt unfair.

"Again," she said.

We stood in Cooper Street drill court under a sky the color of old tin.

The watch had used the yard that morning. Their chalk lane lines still crossed the dirt. Two recruits were stacking weighted shields against the far wall while pretending not to watch us.

My one silver had purchased an audience.

Excellent.

Lera raised the padded stick.

"Same entry."

I cut.

She caught the blade line on her practice sword.

I arrived behind the cut.

Recovered.

Correct.

Then she stepped into me.

Not with the weapon.

Body.

Shoulder to chest.

My feet were still organizing the previous success.

I moved late.

Her padded stick tapped my ribs.

"Dead."

"You hit me after the block."

"People continue existing after blocks."

Annoying world.

"Again."

We repeated it.

Cut.

Contact.

Recover.

Lera entered.

This time I gave ground too early.

She followed.

Tap to thigh.

"Dead differently."

"Variety."

"Again."

Old Greg knew this problem.

Not the drill.

The problem.

A young fighter thinks recovery means returning the weapon to useful position.

A professional understands recovery includes where the rest of the body has ended up and what the other person can now reach.

I had known that for decades.

Current hips had not.

Current ankles were learning.

Current lungs were much better than they had been when I first started paying Lera to hit me.

That was useful.

Lera raised an eyebrow.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Good. Again."

Cut.

Block.

Arrive.

This time I recovered the sword and moved my rear foot first.

Not back.

Outside.

Small angle.

Lera entered and found less chest.

Her padded stick touched my sleeve instead of ribs.

"Better."

One word.

Worth at least three copper.

We kept working.

The lesson did not become faster because I had found one correct foot.

Lera changed the entry.

Sometimes she gave ground.

Sometimes she crowded.

Sometimes she bound my practice sword and made me decide whether to fight the bind or abandon the line.

Once I tried something clever with my wrist.

She hit me in the hand.

"That was old," she said.

I flexed my fingers.

"It worked later."

"You are not later."

There.

Clean.

No insult.

No mysticism.

Just current anatomy.

At the far wall, one watch recruit dropped a shield.

The other laughed.

Lera did not look.

I did.

She hit me in the forearm.

"Also dead."

"Public training is educational."

"For me."

We went another half hour.

By the end, my shirt stuck to my back despite the cold.

The shoulder she had hit first had become a map of future bruise.

Lera finally lowered her sword.

"Enough."

I breathed.

"I can continue."

"I know."

That stopped me.

She pointed at my feet.

"Your recovery is better. Your legs are stronger. You are also starting to trust them enough to create new mistakes."

Progress.

"Which mistakes?"

"You assume because you can leave a bad position now, you should. Sometimes the bad position is still better than the place you're escaping to."

I looked at the scuffed dirt.

Two lives and I was still paying silver to be told not to run from problems badly.

Efficient universe.

Lera picked up her coat.

"Practice the outside step. Not every recovery. One in three."

"Why?"

"Because if you practice it every time, you'll make it a religion."

Fair.

The watch recruits started their afternoon block as I left.

One of them nodded at me.

"You Guild?"

"Bronze."

He looked at Lera.

"She always hit that hard?"

"No."

His face brightened.

"Really?"

"Sometimes harder."

I walked away before Lera could correct my recruiting strategy.

At Lower Rook I ate too much stew and practiced exactly three recoveries.

On the fourth, I stopped.

Not because the movement was solved.

Because the instruction had been to stop making everything one answer...
