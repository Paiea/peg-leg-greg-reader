# Year Chapter 09: The Field Guard

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D082 / SPRING`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 13`

`LOCATION: North pumping road`

`RANK: Bronze`

Thirteen days taught me that the Blackglass Anchor disliked being treated like a shield and loved being bolted to things.

It also taught me that Harl could complain for forty minutes while building exactly what I asked for.

"Bad idea," he said.

He drilled the third hole anyway.

We stood behind Bell Tile in a work shed Dena had temporarily surrendered after I promised not to set anything on fire.

The Anchor sat inside a new bronze mounting plate with three hinged eyes spaced around its black oval body. Harl had replaced two corroded frame arms with clean brackets. A leather harness crossed my chest and back, but the Anchor itself did not sit against me. It clipped at my left hip to a rigid side plate with three short connection rings.

"Why three?" Harl asked.

"Because it had three."

"Excellent engineering."

"You asked."

He tightened the last screw.

During the last thirteen days I had learned more by failing than succeeding.

If I pushed force directly into the glass and expected it to amplify, nothing useful happened.

If I connected one force plane to one eye and another stable surface to a second, the Anchor spread load between them.

If I tried to redirect too much at once, the silver threads brightened, the bronze heated, and my own mana control went sloppy.

If I had only one anchor point, the object was almost useless.

Not power source.

A relationship machine.

That sounded too philosophical, so I kept calling it expensive rock around Noll.

My wrists had adapted too.

Not dramatically.

I could hold a static plane against sideways pressure for fifteen breaths instead of seven before form softened. I had started practicing with Callen at Guild yard once every four days. He hit the plane with padded staff from different angles while I tried not to swear.

He enjoyed this too much.

Mara's visit date remained D128.

Forty-six days.

She sent one letter complaining that my last packet contained more information about force geometry than feelings.

I replied:

**The force geometry is going well. I miss you. Better?**

Her answer had not arrived yet.

Noll had started training a new kitchen runner at Brass Spoon.

Jorren now covered Meln's afternoon receiving desk twice a week.

Tavin was still south but sent a note containing only:

**If your expensive black rock explodes, I want it written that I predicted this.**

Nobody respected research.

The Guild job on D082 came from Vaska.

North Pumping Road had an abandoned water station where city surveyors wanted measurements before summer repairs. Recent workers found hoof damage around intake wall and refused to return after a glasshorn charged their cart.

Glasshorns I knew better than channel hounds.

Not because current Greg had fought them.

Old Greg had.

Deer-like animals with mineral antlers that stored trace charge from certain soils and wards. Herds usually migrated around human routes. Solitary males could become territorial in spring.

The posting said `ONE LARGE BUCK CONFIRMED`.

Team was Vaska, me, Evin, Lessa, and a survey mason named Tor.

I liked that South Four crew had reassembled accidentally.

Lessa looked at my hip harness.

"Rock."

"Artifact."

"Noll told me rock."

"Why are you talking to Noll?"

"Brass Spoon has food."

Network betrayal.

Evin tapped bronze mount.

"Does what?"

"Shares force between anchors."

"Useful in fight?"

"Unclear."

"Good."

I stared.

He smiled.

Everyone had learned.

North Pumping Station sat one hour beyond city wall on low rise above irrigation channel. Stone building, half roof, two old iron pipes disappearing into hill.

Spring grass had grown high around it.

Glasshorn tracks cut through mud.

Large.

Fresh.

Vaska established rules before we entered.

"Survey first. Animal second. If it leaves, let it. Tor owns structure. I own route. Evin front reach. Greg blade. Lessa rear."

"Why me blade and not weird rock?" I asked.

"Because sword has more endorsements."

Fair.

We approached slowly.

The station door hung open.

Inside, floor was dry except one corner. Old pump frame gone. Survey marks on walls from prior crew.

Tor began measuring foundation cracks.

I opened mana sense.

The old intake pipe carried low residual charge from water flow.

Enough that the Anchor at hip warmed faintly.

Interesting.

Not active by itself.

Responsive.

I clipped one short line from Anchor ring to iron pipe bracket.

Lessa watched.

"Why?"

"Testing."

"Now?"

I looked at Vaska.

She shrugged.

"Doesn't change survey?"

"No."

"Then small."

Permission.

I fed a tiny force pulse into second Anchor eye.

Silver threads brightened under black glass.

The pulse spread partly into pipe bracket.

Water inside pipe shivered audibly.

Tor looked up.

"Don't."

I stopped.

"Structure?"

"Old pipe. You make it sing, I don't know what else moves."

Good boundary.

I unclipped.

Five minutes later something struck outer wall.

Hard.

Dust fell from ceiling.

Everyone froze.

Second impact.

Hoof scrape.

Glasshorn.

Vaska pointed to rear exit.

"Out south."

We moved.

Not because fleeing automatically.

Because open field gave better visibility than broken stone room.

The buck stood thirty yards north.

Beautiful.

Terrible.

Larger than horse through chest, dark gray coat, antlers branching wide with translucent mineral ridges catching sun. One tine cracked. Pale sparks crawled along the break.

It had hit station wall once with shoulder, maybe rubbing mineral plate.

Then saw us.

Head lowered.

Evin swore softly.

"Lane?"

West open.

South us.

East irrigation channel.

North road.

Vaska said, "Back south. Give west."

We moved.

The buck did not.

Its antlers brightened.

Old Greg memory said charge was not lightning exactly. Mana discharge through contact, enough to stun muscle and crack weak wards.

Current body had never taken it.

I did not want proof.

The animal stamped.

Tor backed too fast and caught heel on stone.

He fell.

The buck charged.

Not because he fell probably.

Movement.

Territory.

Doesn't matter.

Action.

Evin braced spear.

"No," I said.

He looked.

A glasshorn this size could drive spear through him before stopping.

Need deflection.

Station had two iron mooring eyes from old maintenance sleds set into south wall.

Two anchors.

My hand moved to hip.

Blackglass.

No time for elaborate.

I clipped left harness line to one wall eye, right line to second.

Vaska saw.

"What are you doing?"

"Making it expensive to hit me."

Poor explanation.

Buck ten yards.

I stepped forward and set a broad force plane angled west.

Not stop.

Redirect.

Fed plane into center ring.

The Anchor woke hard.

Silver threads flashed.

My force spread backward through both wall anchors.

For first time I felt what the object could do under real load.

It made me less singular.

The wall took some.

The ground took some.

I still took plenty.

Glasshorn hit plane with shoulder and antler.

Impact slammed through harness.

I left ground.

Not far.

One foot maybe.

The force plane slid west instead of collapsing completely.

Because the Anchor shared shear across two physical points.

The buck's charge changed by perhaps fifteen degrees.

Enough.

It passed Tor instead of trampling him.

It also passed me close enough antler cut coat sleeve.

One spark hit bronze mount.

Mana discharge flooded Anchor.

Black glass turned silver-white for half a heartbeat.

My left arm went numb.

I hit ground.

The buck skidded, turned.

Still aggressive.

Now we had a problem.

Evin got Tor up.

Lessa dragged him south.

Vaska shouted, "Can it run west?"

"It can!"

But animal turned toward us again.

No flight.

Second charge.

My arm still half numb.

Anchor hot.

I unclipped one line because if it overloaded through same points I did not know what happened.

Sword out.

This time Evin and I split.

He went right with spear to draw line.

I went left.

Buck chose him.

Evin waited longer than I liked.

Then dove behind low stone trough.

Antlers smashed trough edge.

Stone exploded.

The buck's head stuck for one instant in broken masonry.

Current opportunity.

I ran.

Sword two-handed because left arm had returned enough.

Mineral ridge protected neck top.

Gap behind front shoulder.

Same principle as tusker, different body.

I drove blade upward behind foreleg.

Deep.

The animal kicked sideways.

Hoof caught my thigh, not edge.

Pain.

I lost grip.

Sword remained in.

Bad pattern.

Glasshorn tore free of trough and spun.

Evin thrust into flank to keep antlers away from me.

Lessa shouted, "Greg, down!"

She had thrown something? No.

The cracked tine snapped.

Tor flew backward.

Glasshorn screamed.

Its head jerked.

The buck stumbled.

Hands shaking from shock.

No burn where gloves covered.

Right shoulder maybe sprained from fall.

Not cut.

A hoof bruise forming.

Black glass itself intact.

"Yes."

Vaska sent Tor back with Lessa after she wrapped shoulder. A replacement mason came from city by afternoon.

Evin and I dressed glasshorn enough to transport. Antlers required careful handling until residual charge bled away. We grounded them with wet rope to iron stake, waited two hours, then cut at base.

The broken tine Tor knocked free was mine by field salvage split after Guild fees.

It was worth seventeen copper raw.

The rest shared contract.

More importantly, the station survey showed intake wall unsafe. The glasshorn's repeated rubbing had worsened an old crack.
