# Chapter 52: The Packet

Mara's packet had a coffee ring on it.

Not mine.

Different city.

Different table.

Different bad decision involving a cup near paper.

I stood in Lower Rook's downstairs hall with the packet in one hand and stared at the brown circle near my name.

Noll passed behind me carrying onions.

"Love letter?"

"Freight administration."

"Hot."

"You have no standards."

"Correct."

I went upstairs.

The packet was thicker than her last one.

Three sheets.

One Vey Shipping route notice folded around the outside because Mara had apparently decided company scrap paper belonged to romance now.

I approved.

She was still in Bracken.

Mostly.

The first page began with weather.

Rain west of Halden.

Ice on the north grade.

A driver named Sen who refused to believe mud had depth until his rear axle demonstrated it.

Then work.

Mara had been moved from temporary route-clerk duty into account reconciliation for three lines running through Bracken.

Not promoted, exactly.

Trusted with something more expensive.

She hated it.

Then she liked it.

Then she made a mistake.

I read that part twice.

She had posted a fuel surcharge against the wrong outbound ledger and spent half a day proving to herself that the numbers were impossible before realizing she had made them impossible personally.

Her supervisor had not fired her.

This offended her sense of consequence.

She fixed it.

Stayed late.

Then wrote:

**I thought bigger work would feel like knowing what I was doing. It mostly feels like getting more expensive ways to notice when I don't.**

I laughed.

Current Mara.

Not the later woman memory had compressed into competence.

The second page asked about the Guild fire.

She had heard from a Vey driver before my packet reached her.

Of course.

Trade routes moved gossip faster than affection.

**Was it the one you remembered?**

Below that:

**Do you know why it happened?**

There.

Good questions.

Bad answers.

I kept reading.

She had not asked whether I was safe until halfway down the page.

Not because she did not care.

Because she already knew from the driver that nobody had died and because people who work in logistics develop a disturbing respect for sequence.

Then:

**I am glad you were there. I am also glad you did not go into the roof. Please tell me you did not go into the roof.**

I had not.

Sella would have killed me if fire failed.

The last sheet contained Bracken nonsense.

A baker near the Vey office sold sweet buns shaped like ships.

Mara claimed they looked like dead fish.

She bought three.

Her room had a ceiling stain shaped like the coast.

A woman downstairs sang loudly during laundry and knew only one chorus.

She missed Halden's soup.

Not me.

Then, two lines later:

**Also you. Obviously.**

Correct relationship language.

I sat at the desk.

My room's second window rattled once in the wind.

The Guild fire had burned paper about young Greg.

Mara's packet had crossed half the current map to arrive anyway.

I took out a clean sheet.

First answer.

Yes.

It was the fire I remembered.

I described the red roof, the later dark slate, and the unpleasant experience of recognizing history while carrying buckets.

Second answer.

No.

I did not know the cause.

The investigators did not know yet either.

I wrote that twice in different ways because future memory had trained both of us to treat my confidence as evidence sometimes.

Then I told her about East Annex smelling like cabbage.

About Sella reconstructing a twelve-silver closeout.

About the shared drill court.

About Lera hitting me because I blocked correctly.

I did not tell her the shoulder still hurt.

Then I did.

Honesty had annoying momentum.

I asked about the supervisor who had not fired her.

Asked whether Bracken was temporary still.

Asked whether the route-account work meant she would travel more or sit at a desk more.

Not because I needed the answer to make a plan.

Because I wanted to know what her days were becoming.

At the bottom I wrote:

**You made the numbers wrong and then made them right. That sounds suspiciously like work.**

I considered adding something wiser.

Did not.

Mara had enough supervisors.

I folded the letter.

The next packet run west left in two days.

Plenty of time.

No deadline beyond ordinary mail.

I put her packet on the shelf beside Tavin's.

Different roads.

Different people.

Both still moving when I was not looking...
