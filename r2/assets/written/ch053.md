# Year Chapter 11: The Second Guard

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D097 / SUMMER`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 6`

`LOCATION: East road out of Carrow`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

Summer arrived while I was oiling boots.

Not ceremonially.

The morning simply stayed warm before sunrise and Maud opened both front windows without complaining about drafts.

Six days had passed since the Westreach review.

In those six days I had done two things that mattered and several things that did not.

I paid twelve copper into a separate cloth purse marked `WEST`, which made the money feel less spendable without actually changing its nature.

I also asked the Guild for a multi-day road contract under a Silver field lead.

Sella looked pleased enough to hide it.

"Finally asking for the thing you need."

"I ask for things constantly."

"Usually after choosing them yourself."

She handed me a seven-day Kelm freight token.

Driver: Beren Olt.

Security lead: Siva Renn, Silver.

Second guard: Greg.

The word `second` bothered me for exactly three seconds.

Then I remembered the sponsor requirement.

Someone had to see me work.

Siva would.

Mara's D128 visit was thirty-one days away. The Kelm route should return me with a week to spare.

Should.

Roads enjoyed that word.

Noll had started leaving for Brass Spoon before I woke on opening mornings. Jorren's factor work had expanded into two late shifts. Tavin's last letter said Venn was sending him farther south for most of summer.

Everyone had schedules.

I put the West purse beneath the burned seven and left it there.

At East Gate, Beren had two mules and no patience.

He was broad through shoulders, broken nose, right hand scarred across palm. Jorren had mentioned him often enough that the face arrived with a category.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

He looked at Blackglass harness.

"That safe?"

"Sometimes."

"Bad answer."

"It is safe when I use it inside tested limits."

"Better."

Apparently the whole road profession had one school.

Siva stood beside front wheel checking harness straps.

Silver tab plain at collar.

No dramatic armor.

She was maybe thirty, dark skin browned further by road, hair braided tight, spear strapped beside driver's board and short bow across pack.

She looked at my sword.

Then hands.

Then boots.

Then Anchor.

"You blade endorsement?"

"Yes."

"Fauna?"

"Several."

"Human violence?"

I paused.

"Not much current."

"Good answer."

Beren groaned.

"Everyone stop saying good."

I liked him immediately.

The load was practical: brass fittings, two dye barrels, wrapped tool steel, four sacks dried herbs, and three sealed packets for Kelm factors.

Nothing worth a ballad.

Enough value to attract theft.

We left at first bell.

The road felt different because I had been on one before.

That surprised me.

First road, every mile had been evidence that I was traveling.

Now my body knew where pack pulled, how long before shoulders settled, which boot lace loosened after an hour, how often to drink before thirst became stupid.

Mundane competence had arrived quietly.

Siva noticed less because there was less to notice.

That felt like progress too.

By midday Carrow smoke disappeared behind low hills.

We ate beside drainage stone.

Beren gave each mule grain while we ate bread and hard cheese.

Siva studied road map.

"White Fork bridge open?"

Beren shrugged.

"Yesterday maybe."

"Useful."

"Road office posts at Fenner."

"We reach tomorrow."

"Yes."

I listened.

Old Greg remembered a stone bridge at White Fork.

I did not volunteer.

Current road office knew current bridge.

An hour after lunch, two riders appeared behind us.

Not threatening.

At first.

Young men in plain coats, no visible Guild tags, light horses.

They passed without speaking.

Siva watched them go.

"Know?" I asked.

"No."

"Problem?"

"Maybe."

She did not invent certainty.

We continued.

At next bend the riders were stopped beside road pretending one horse needed girth adjusted.

Siva moved bow from pack to shoulder.

No arrow drawn.

Message.

They watched our cart pass.

One smiled.

"Kelm?"

Beren said, "Maybe."

"Road bad north."

"Road always bad."

We went.

Siva waited until bend hid them.

"Scouts or idiots."

"Difference?"

"Scouts come back."

They did.

Near dusk.

We had reached a low pine stretch with no houses visible. Beren wanted another mile to marked camp.

Hoofbeats came fast behind.

Same two riders plus one more.

No pretending now.

One drew short club.

Another had knife.

Not bandit army.

Three men deciding one cart looked easier than work.

Siva said, "Beren keep moving. Greg rear. Don't chase."

Clear.

I moved to cart back.

Sword came out.

The first rider approached right side, reaching for rear canvas line.

I slapped his hand with blade flat.

Hard.

He shouted and veered.

Second came left with knife.

I shifted across cart bed, but he stayed outside sword reach.

Cut one tie line.

Canvas flapped.

Siva's arrow appeared in his coat sleeve.

Not body.

Pinned fabric to saddle blanket for half a second before it tore free.

He panicked, horse swerved.

Third rider came straight rear.

Club raised.

I could wait.

Instead I stepped onto rear rail and jumped.

This was questionable.

Current body landed on road badly but upright.

The rider expected me on cart.

He passed close.

I struck club wrist with sword guard, grabbed coat, and used his speed to pull him off horse.

We hit dirt together.

He rolled.

I rolled faster.

Sword point at throat.

Clean.

He froze.

His friends had kept riding.

Beren's cart was thirty yards ahead.

Siva shouted, "Greg!"

Do not chase.

I had not.

Technically.

But cart was leaving.

The man beneath sword looked young.

Maybe twenty.

Blood at lip from fall.

"Knife?"

He shook head.

"Drop purse."

"What?"

"Yours."

He stared.

"Road tax."

I heard myself.

This was absurd.

"No. Give me club and go."

He threw club away.

I backed.

He scrambled up and ran after loose horse.

I retrieved club.

Then sprinted toward cart.

Siva had stopped at next rise.

She looked furious.

"I said rear."

"I stayed rear. Then rear moved."

"You jumped off moving cart."

"Slow cart."

"He had friends."

"They ran."

"After."

There was correction available.

Also result.

I had taken one man down cleanly.

No injury.

No lost cargo.

"I could have stayed on," I said.

"Yes."

"Would have been safer."

"Yes."

"Still worked."

Siva looked at me for a long moment.

Then at club.

"Yes."

No lecture erased it.

She took club and tossed it into cart.

"Evidence."

"Of what?"

"Bad judgment or useful aggression. I have six days to decide."

I laughed.

Beren said, "Both. Save time."

We reached marked camp before full dark.

Two other freight crews already there.

News traveled quickly. One driver recognized description of riders.

"Sorn boys maybe. Been cutting canvas around east road."

"Report?" Siva asked.

"Road office has names."

We added ours next morning.

Current banditry became paperwork.

At camp I inspected myself.

One scraped palm from road fall.

New trousers dirty.

No serious damage.

Siva inspected Anchor harness because jump had twisted one side.

"Strap fraying."

I had missed it.

She pointed.

Near buckle, leather fibers white.

"Replace tomorrow."

"Can sew."

"Replace."

"Why?"

"Because you hang force from it."

Fair.

Beren cooked beans.

They were terrible.

Siva took over salt after first bite.

We ate around fire with two other crews.

Road talk became bridge rumors, tolls, market price, a dead horse near Fenner, and whether summer moths had appeared early.

I listened more than spoke.

This was second road.

Not proof anymore.

A place with recurring problems.

Before sleep I took out D001 paper.

D274 was 177 days away.

Siva sat across fire sharpening broadhead.

Silver.

A possible sponsor if I did not jump off too many carts.

I put paper away.

She saw.

"Westreach?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Black Stair."

"That's where it goes."

"I know."

"No. Why you?"

Same rude question as Noll.

I still had no better answer.

"I want to become someone they would take."

Siva looked at me over whetstone.

"That's not why."

Then kept sharpening.

I lay down irritated.

The road was warm enough that I did not need all blankets.

Summer had begun.
