# Chapter 53: The Permit

Harl Fen found me because bureaucracy had finally become interested in a rock.

He arrived at East Annex carrying three rolled sheets and leading Pate through a vegetable market.

This was not technically allowed.

Sella saw the mule.

Harl saw Sella seeing the mule.

"Five minutes," he said.

Sella pointed outside.

"Zero minutes."

Pate looked betrayed.

We moved to the loading lane.

"West Cut?" I asked.

Harl handed me the top sheet.

City seal.

Road office.

**LIMITED THAW-PREP REPAIR SURVEY. STONE CROSSING ONLY.**

Not the whole Cut.

Not widening.

Not reopening to carts.

One crossing.

"They finally authorized it."

"They authorized me to tell them what it costs," Harl said. "Different activity."

Right.

"When?"

"Tomorrow. One night out if weather holds. Two if Sive argues with the stones."

"Sive?"

"Mason."

Pate tried to eat the edge of the permit.

Harl moved it.

"Escort?"

"One Bronze."

"Edda?"

"Booked. Orchard cold-store inventory escort west of North Lane."

Independent life.

"Jorren?"

"Didn't ask. You already walked the crossing, you know the glasshorn route, and I know you don't wander off when I say we're done."

Professional romance.

"Rate?"

He told me.

Field day plus overnight.

Ordinary.

I looked at the permit again.

The same crossing from our five-day survey.

One of the places Harl had already judged not viable for loaded animals.

Now somebody had enough money, weather, and administrative attention to ask whether changing that one fact was affordable.

The world had moved a little.

Not because I needed it to.

"I can go," I said.

Harl nodded.

"Meet road office at first bell."

"Pate allowed there?"

"Barely."

Pate bit my sleeve.

"He remains political."

"He has principles."

We separated.

I did not rush home to find the brace ring.

That surprised me only after I noticed it.

The crossing was a masonry survey.

Not every hard object needed my hard object.

I packed food, dry socks, field gloves, and the waxed thread I already owned.

Sword.

No special gear.

At dinner Noll asked where I was going.

"West Cut."

He looked up.

"Again?"

"Different question."

"Same mud?"

"Probably."

"Then same socks."

Wisdom.

The next morning Harl waited beside Pate with a woman in a short gray coat and boots crusted white with old mortar.

"Sive Arden," he said.

Sive looked at me.

"Escort?"

"Greg."

"You know stone?"

"Enough to let you know it."

She considered.

"Useful."

Then climbed onto Pate's off-side pack step without asking permission.

Pate tolerated her.

Specialist recognized specialist.

We left Carrow through a North Gate carrying traffic again.

The repaired lane held under a flour cart while a city worker watched the support markers.

Current repairs doing current work.

I looked once at the stone overhead.

Later collapse remained later.

Harl did not ask what I remembered.

Good.

We turned toward West Cut.

One permit.

One crossing.

One question the city had finally decided was worth paying to ask...
