# Chapter 54: The Stone

Sive Arden distrusted every stone personally.

This seemed healthy for a mason.

We reached the crossing before midday.

Deepwinter had stripped the West Cut into cleaner shapes than I remembered from the rain survey.

Less leaf cover.

More exposed root.

Ice in the shaded pockets.

The crossing itself looked exactly as inconvenient as before.

Three broad slabs over a narrow wash.

The eastern slab had sunk toward the downstream edge.

The middle one rocked under loaded pressure.

The western approach had no room for a cart to correct once committed.

Sive walked around it twice.

Then lay flat on her stomach and looked underneath.

Harl took notes.

Pate ate dead grass.

I watched the trees.

No monsters.

No men.

No reason for the sword.

"Load line's wrong," Sive said from the ground.

Harl crouched.

"Stone?"

"Base. They set this on loose fill. Water took the fines."

She shoved a gloved hand beneath the edge.

"See daylight?"

I did.

"Can you reset it?" Harl asked.

"Yes."

"For pack animals?"

"Yes."

"Light carts?"

Sive stood.

"Maybe."

Harl's pencil stopped.

"Maybe is expensive."

"Then don't ask a mason for cheap certainty."

Professional affection everywhere.

She had brought two iron bars and a small line level.

We spent the next hour measuring approach grade, slab movement, wash depth, and the amount of room required to build a proper shoulder.

My job was mostly to stand where I could see the Cut in both directions.

This was real work.

It was also boring.

Both could coexist.

At one point Sive asked me to put weight on the middle slab.

I did.

"More."

I shifted forward.

The stone moved under my boot.

Tiny.

Enough.

"There," she said.

Harl marked it.

I stepped off.

No heroic intervention.

I had become calibrated luggage.

Around second bell I found old glasshorn sign twenty yards uphill.

Not fresh.

Two split prints in frozen mud beneath the leaf crust.

A scrape on bark at knee height.

I called Harl over.

He looked.

"Same seasonal line?"

"Probably. Older than this freeze."

Sive glanced up from the stone.

"Animals use the crossing?"

"Not usually the slab. They cut above it."

"If we widen shoulder?"

Good question.

I walked the line.

The planned shoulder would push closer to the animal path but not onto it.

Current evidence.

Later memory had glasshorns farther north by the time I knew this road well.

Why?

No idea.

"Mark a caution," I said. "Not a stop. If they build during movement season, someone should watch the uphill line."

Harl wrote it.

Sive did not ask me to explain glasshorn ecology in full.

Good specialist boundaries.

We camped at the old shelf near dusk.

No fire because wind was wrong and Harl hated sparks near dry root mats.

Cold meal.

Pate objected to everything.

Sive took off one boot and inspected a heel blister.

"Tomorrow I want the upstream bank," she said.

Harl nodded.

"Drainage cut?"

"Maybe."

"Again with maybe."

"You keep asking future questions."

I looked at both of them.

"I support this argument."

They ignored me.

We slept badly.

In the morning Sive found the actual expensive part.

Not the slab.

The bank.

Any repair strong enough for light carts would need water taken away before it reached the eastern footing.

Otherwise the next hard rain would start the same failure again.

"Stone is easy," she said.

Harl looked at the frozen wash.

"Water isn't."

"Correct."

Current world summarized.

We measured until noon.

Then Harl rolled the permit back into its tube.

"Enough."

Done meant done.

I looked once at the crossing.

Future widening still wasn't here.

Neither was the reason it eventually would be.

For now, three stones and a bad bank had earned two days of attention...
