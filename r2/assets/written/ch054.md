# Year Chapter 12: The Second Guard

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D105 / SUMMER`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 8`

`LOCATION: White Fork district`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

Eight days later, Siva still had not decided whether jumping off the cart counted as useful aggression.

She had, however, written `DOES NOT FREEZE` on the interim road sheet.

I knew because Beren left it face up.

"You read private?" he asked.

"It was on the table."

"Still."

"Then turn it over."

He did.

My replacement Anchor strap cost five copper at Fenner and had thicker stitching than the old one. The scrape on my palm from the road fall was already a dry line. Summer heat had turned camps warmer, dustier, and louder with insects. Beren's mules drank more. We stopped earlier around noon and traveled later into evening.

The road itself had shifted from spring mud to hard-packed ruts.

Old Greg remembered roads as distances.

Current Greg was learning them as seasons too.

White Fork bridge was open.

The ford below it was not.

A storm upriver had pushed enough debris through that road wardens closed lower crossing for repairs.

That should have simplified our route.

Instead it created a queue of carts at bridge.

We waited two hours.

Siva used the time to make me practice with the Anchor.

Not magic demonstration.

Load work.

Beren tied a fifty-pound grain sack to one cart rail and another to opposite rail, then Siva made me use three connection points to keep a suspended plank level while she shifted sacks.

"Why now?"

"Because you're bored."

"Cruel."

"Because if you only use it when someone is about to die, you stay bad."

Better reason.

The Anchor had become easier to feel over the last week. Not stronger. More legible.

Pressure entered one connection and silver threads inside black glass told me where it wanted to leave. If I fought that path, my shoulders paid. If I guided it, structure did work.

I kept plank level for twenty breaths.

Siva moved second sack suddenly.

Left edge dropped.

I corrected too hard.

Right rose.

"Again."

"Bridge line is moving."

"Again."

Beren laughed from mule shade.

We did again.

By time road warden waved us forward, sweat ran down my back and Anchor mount was warm.

No adventure.

Practice inside waiting.

That mattered later.

White Fork district opened into low river country east of bridge. Reeds, wet fields, willow patches. The road followed raised stone causeway for several miles.

Near sunset we heard a bell.

Three fast rings.

Pause.

Three again.

Every road crew around us changed.

Beren stopped talking.

Siva stood on driver's board.

"Call ahead."

I knew road bells existed from stories, not current experience.

Emergency.

Another three rings came from around bend.

Siva looked at me.

"Stay with cart until we see."

We rounded slowly.

A passenger coach had gone partly off causeway.

Rear wheel hung over reed ditch. One horse down in traces. Driver stood in road ringing hand bell. Four passengers clustered twenty yards away, one holding child.

No obvious attack.

Then reeds moved.

Low gray shapes.

Siva swore.

"Mirewolves."

Not wolves exactly.

Lean amphibious predators with long forelimbs and dark wet fur, native to reed channels. Usually hunted fish, birds, small livestock.

Blood from downed horse had changed priorities.

I counted three shapes.

Then five.

One passenger screamed.

The mirewolves stopped stalking horse and looked at people.

"Beren, block road with cart," Siva said. "Greg with me. Driver, get passengers behind freight."

No hesitation.

Beren swung our cart across causeway enough to create barrier while keeping mule team clear.

Siva drew bow.

I drew sword.

The first mirewolf came out of reeds.

Longer than a normal wolf, shoulders low, paws broad for mud. Its mouth opened too wide.

Siva shot.

Arrow entered chest.

Animal rolled once and vanished over causeway edge.

The others scattered.

Not gone.

Smart enough.

"Coach first," she said.

The downed horse was alive, one front leg tangled under broken shaft.

Coach rear axle sat half over ditch. If it rolled farther, horse could be dragged in.

Driver said, "Need pull coach back."

Siva looked at wheel position.

"Anchor?"

She meant mine.

I crouched.

Causeway had two old iron rail eyes set into stones, probably for winter chains.

Three connection points possible if we used our cart too.

"Maybe."

"What do you need?"

Not `can you solve it`.

Better.

"Rope to rear coach frame. One line to both causeway eyes. Third to our cart axle if Beren agrees. I can spread pull, not lift."

Beren heard.

"Use rear axle only."

Ownership.

We rigged fast.

Passengers moved behind our wagon. Driver cut one horse trace to reduce tension. Siva kept watching reeds while helping with rope between glances.

I clipped Blackglass center to coach line, left to causeway eyes through bridle, right to our rear axle.

The geometry was ugly.

Enough.

"Beren, slow forward when I say. Driver, push coach uphill side."

"You lead?" Siva asked.

For this part.

"Yes."

She nodded.

I liked that too much.

"Pull."

Beren moved mules.

Rope tightened.

Coach creaked.

The Anchor came alive at hip.

Force split between stone eyes and our axle. I did not create most of it. I kept one line from taking everything.

The rear wheel climbed inches away from ditch.

Driver pushed.

"Again."

Mules pulled.

Then mirewolves attacked.

Two at once from reeds on passenger side.

Siva shot one.

Missed because it changed line.

She dropped bow and drew spear.

I could not leave Anchor immediately without losing load balance.

There.

Role conflict.

One mirewolf cleared low stone edge.

Beren shouted.

A passenger man grabbed coach pole and swung wildly.

Bad.

Siva met animal with spear point.

It twisted around shaft.

Second came behind.

Toward passengers.

I released force.

The Anchor snapped load to physical ropes but both held.

Coach slid one inch back toward ditch.

Acceptable for seconds.

I ran.

Sword low.

Second mirewolf jumped at passenger with child.

I intercepted sideways.

Cut through foreleg and shoulder.

Animal hit me anyway.

Weight slammed chest.

I went down.

Teeth snapped near face.

I jammed left forearm against throat, bracer between jaws.

Wet breath.

Rot smell.

Current body suddenly understood that a monster on top of you weighed more than descriptions suggested.

I drove sword under ribs one-handed.

Once.

Again.

The mirewolf went slack.

I shoved it off.

Siva had killed hers with spear through neck.

Third shape stayed in reeds.

Then another came for downed horse from opposite side.

Beren threw a wheel chock at it.

Missed.

Driver shouted.

Evin was not here.

No extra fighter waiting.

I ran around coach.

The mirewolf had jaws on horse flank.

Horse screamed and kicked.

One kick caught animal ribs.

It rolled.

I arrived before it regained footing.

Sword down behind skull.

Clean.

It spasmed.

Then still.

Silence except horse.

Siva stood on causeway scanning reeds.

"Two more out there."

"Let them go?"

"If they go."

We waited.

Reeds moved away.

Good.

Now coach.

The rear wheel had slid another two inches but remained on stone edge.

Passenger man still held child now because woman had taken coach pole.

Roles changed without permission.

We returned to ropes.

My chest hurt where animal hit.

No bite.

Bracer gouged.

Blackglass harness twisted but intact.

"Can you?" Siva asked.

"Yes."

Actually yes.

We pulled again.

This time I used the morning practice.

When coach load shifted toward left causeway eye, I guided some into Beren's axle before correcting. Less fighting.

The Anchor stayed cooler.

Coach wheel climbed onto stone.

Everyone shouted once when it settled.

Then the downed horse.

Leg was not broken. Harness had pinned it awkwardly and shoulder was cut. We freed traces, rolled broken shaft away, and got animal standing after three attempts.

Passenger coach could not continue safely with one shaft broken.

So road problem became logistics.

One nearby freight crew took two passengers.

We took mother and child plus one trunk.

Driver stayed with coach until repair wagon from White Fork arrived.

Siva wrote incident note.

Beren checked our axle after Anchor load.

"Fine."

Then looked at my bracer.

Mirewolf teeth had punched two holes through outer leather but not inner plate.

"Not fine."

"Arm is."

"Gear isn't."

Right.

We camped at White Fork station instead of continuing.

The child, Arri, would not stop staring at me.

At dinner she asked, "Are you Silver?"

I laughed.

"No."

"You killed two."

"Three with Siva."

"You killed two."

"Yes."

"Then why Bronze?"

Siva nearly choked on soup.

I loved this question from everyone except paperwork.

"Because monsters don't issue promotions."

Arri considered.

"They should."

Power-fantasy governance.

After she left with mother to guest room, Siva handed me road sheet.

She had written:

`D105. COACH RECOVERY UNDER MIREWOLF PRESSURE. GREG OWNED LOAD RIGGING, RELEASED IT TO FIGHT WHEN PRIORITY CHANGED, RETURNED AND FINISHED RECOVERY. TWO KILLS. DID NOT CHASE.`

I read final words.

"You remembered."

"I was watching."

"Sponsor?"

Too direct.

Siva took sheet back.

"Not after nine days."

"Eight."

"Worse."

"What would make you?"

She looked at me.

"I need to know what you do when tired, bored, angry, wrong, and nobody is bleeding. Anyone can look useful for thirty seconds."

Sella's endurance point again.

"How long?"

"Work."

Road answer.

I leaned back.

Chest bruise hurt.

My sword needed cleaning.

My bracer needed replacement.

The Anchor had held a coach.

The morning plank practice had mattered before sunset.

Outside, mirewolf carcasses hung under station shed for Guild collector because glands and jaw plates had market value.

Tomorrow somebody would skin them.

Tonight I was tired enough not to care.

That might have been the first evidence Siva wanted.
