# Chapter 55: The Estimate

The city did not repair West Cut.

It produced an estimate.

These were different things despite sharing paper.

Harl asked me to come to the road office because Sive wanted the field notes confirmed before she submitted cost.

I went mostly because I had been paid for the survey and partly because watching professionals disagree over numbers was becoming a hobby.

Sive's estimate occupied one sheet, Harl's conditions occupied two, and the road office clerk had added a third containing reasons nobody should spend any money until thaw.

Healthy government.

"Stone reset is cheap," Sive said.

"Define cheap," the clerk said.

Sive gave the number.

The clerk stopped asking definitions.

"Drainage shoulder?"

Higher.

"Cart approach?"

Higher again.

Then came labor contingency because Deepwinter ground might hide damage they could not see until thaw, traffic control because a work crew could not simply occupy the whole crossing while pack traffic improvised around them, and a seasonal glasshorn watch.

The clerk looked at me.

"Required?"

"Caution," I said. "Not permanent guard. If work overlaps movement season, somebody should watch the uphill line."

She wrote exactly that, without inflating it into a standing guard requirement.

Harl pointed at the bottom total.

"This gets pack animals a reliable crossing and maybe light carts in dry conditions."

"Maybe?" the clerk asked.

Sive looked offended on behalf of reality.

"Yes."

"The North Gate is open."

"Conditionally," Harl said.

"Which is why this isn't emergency work anymore."

The reopened gate had changed the economics of the Cut. It had not erased the crossing or the problem. It had changed their priority.

The clerk leaned back.

"My recommendation is defer physical work until thaw, authorize drainage marking now, preserve the estimate, revisit if North Gate closes or spring traffic supports it."

Harl nodded slowly.

Sive shrugged.

"Stone will still be there."

Probably.

I had no vote, which suited me.

The clerk looked at me anyway.

"Anything from field side?"

I thought about later West Cut: wider shoulder, different traffic, some future authority eventually deciding the money was worth spending.

That did not make current spending correct.

"No," I said. "Current crossing is what the report says."

Harl glanced at me with the smallest approval.

We signed witness lines. The contract closed and I was paid.

No repair date. No next survey. No promise that West Cut would matter next month.

Outside the road office, Pate waited tied to a post.

Harl untied him.

"You free next week?"

Dangerous question.

"For what?"

"Nothing yet."

"Then maybe."

"Good answer."

He led Pate away.

The survey had become paper, and the paper had become deferred work.

For now, the Cut went back to being somewhere I did not have to stand.
