# Year Chapter 13: The Second Guard

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D116 / SUMMER`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 11`

`LOCATION: Kelm`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

Eleven days after the road bell, my chest bruise had faded to green and my replacement bracer smelled like new leather and dead mirewolf.

The second part was not metaphor.

A Kelm leatherworker had reinforced the wrist plate with a thin strip cut from one of the creatures' mineral jaw ridges. Siva said it was unnecessary.

I bought it anyway.

Eight copper after material credit.

It looked good.

That was a valid reason sometimes.

Beren's freight run should have reached Kelm, unloaded, collected return tools, and left within two days.

We had been in Kelm four.

The brass-shim order was late.

A river shipment carrying two casks of precision pins had gone to the wrong warehouse.

The mill office possessed three copies of the same manifest and none agreed about whether we were entitled to delay pay.

Beren had entered a spiritual relationship with rage.

"They owe eight per day."

Kelm clerk said, "Contract says after cargo ready."

"Cargo was supposed ready."

"Wasn't."

"Exactly."

"So not after."

I watched from loading dock eating an onion.

Road fantasy contained accounting battles I had no desire to master.

Siva sat beside me cleaning bowstring.

"How long?" I asked.

"Argument?"

"Kelm."

"Maybe tomorrow."

Mara's D128 date was twelve days away.

If we left tomorrow and road behaved, I could be back in Carrow around D122 or D123, wash clothes, take packet west, arrive Halden close to plan.

If we stayed two more days, I could still make it by faster river route.

If anything actually went wrong, warning date became lie.

I had sent Mara a packet from White Fork:

**Road is trying to eat calendar. Still aiming D128.**

No answer yet.

Letters moved slower than anxiety.

Jorren had written too.

His note reached Kelm through Meln's freight pouch.

**Noll says you killed swamp dogs. Maud says you owe two copper because your chair scratched floor. I do not know why this is my problem.**

Then:

**Meln made me train new counter. She is better than me. I dislike her.**

Good.

Current Jorren had professional jealousy unrelated to my road.

Tavin's packet took longer south route and had not reached.

The road did not keep everyone's story synchronized.

That afternoon, Beren won six copper per delay day after a senior factor found another clause.

He considered this defeat because he wanted eight.

Siva considered it free money because we were not moving.

I considered it time.

Then Kelm Guild bell rang twice from market square.

Short-work call.

Siva looked at me.

"You want."

"We are waiting."

"Same."

We went.

The posting was exactly the kind of contract Old Greg would have forgotten completely:

**BLUE CELLAR SLIME / BREWER DISTRICT / CONTAINMENT / TWO TO FOUR BODIES / FIRE PROHIBITED / TEN COPPER**

I stared.

"Slime."

Siva nodded.

"Slime."

"Actual slime."

"You were expecting dragons every week?"

"No."

Maybe a little.

We signed with a local Bronze mage named Fenn who had spectacles thick enough to distort eyes and carried three bags of salt instead of weapon.

"First slime?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Don't cut."

I looked at sword.

"Why?"

"Makes two."

Classic.

I was delighted.

The brewery cellar smelled like sour grain, yeast, wet stone, and something faintly metallic.

Owner led us downstairs and pointed.

Blue translucent mass covered one corner floor around cracked mash vat.

It moved slowly.

Not one blob.

Several overlapping shapes, each size of bucket, glistening in lamp light.

One extended pseudopod up vat side and dissolved a drip of sweet mash.

"Danger?" I asked.

Fenn said, "Skin irritation. Eats leather. If mana-fed, shock."

The Anchor at my hip warmed faintly.

Mana-fed.

Of course.

The cracked vat had an old warming rune under its iron band. Charge leaked through spilled mash into slimes.

"How many?"

Fenn counted edges.

"Nine maybe."

Siva said, "Maybe again."

Everyone had joined cult.

Plan was simple.

Shut warming rune.

Salt barrier around stairs.

Ash and drying powder to force slimes away from vat.

Scoop into ceramic containment pots.

No swords unless something went creatively wrong.

Something did.

The warming rune would not shut.

Valve lever turned but charge stayed.

Old wire beneath vat had fused to band.

Fenn crouched.

"Need break circuit."

"How?"

"Lift vat enough to reach lower clamp."

The vat was full.

Hundreds of pounds.

Owner said, "Drain first."

Fenn pointed at slime-covered floor drain.

Of course.

I looked at Anchor.

Siva looked at me looking.

"Can?"

"Not lift vat. Maybe unload one leg. Enough for clamp."

"Test."

Good.

We cleared dry side.

Brewery had iron wall rings used to secure barrels. Three points.

I clipped Anchor to two wall rings and one force handle under vat band.

Fed slowly.

Blackglass silver threads brightened.

The object spread upward pressure into wall.

One vat foot lightened.

Not lifted visibly.

Fenn slid thin bar under.

"More."

I pushed.

Shoulders loaded.

Vat rose quarter inch.

Enough.

Fenn reached clamp with insulated hook.

Then a slime touched my boot.

Cold first.

Then burning.

"Fuck."

I held force because dropping vat on Fenn would be poor prioritization.

Siva threw salt at my foot.

The slime recoiled, leaving leather smoking slightly.

"Boot!"

"Later."

Fenn snapped fused wire.

Warm rune went dark.

I released.

Vat settled.

Now slimes changed.

Blue glow faded.

Most shrank slightly as charge drained.

One did not.

The largest, tucked beneath vat, pulsed brighter.

"Mana core," Fenn said.

"Danger?"

"More."

Useful.

It surged out.

Fast enough to stop being comedic.

A blue pseudopod struck Siva's boot.

She jumped back.

Second reached for Fenn.

He threw salt.

The slime absorbed half before recoiling.

"Too fed!"

I could not cut.

Fire prohibited because alcohol stores.

Force?

A plane could push it without dividing body physically maybe.

I set broad barrier low to floor.

The slime hit.

It spread across plane like water against glass.

My mana tingled.

Then the creature began pulling charge from the force construct.

Bad.

"It eats mana!"

Fenn looked offended.

"Yes!"

Would have been useful earlier.

I released immediately.

The slime slopped forward.

Siva had grabbed an empty grain scoop.

She slammed it over main mass like giant bowl.

Blue edges leaked around.

"Pot!"

We moved.

Fenn poured drying ash around scoop rim.

I dragged ceramic containment jar closer.

Siva tilted scoop one inch.

Slime tried escape.

Fenn shoved ash.

We herded glowing mass into jar over three ugly minutes.

No sword.

No magic.

Mostly kitchen tools.

When lid sealed, everyone breathed.

Then we still had eight ordinary slimes.

They took two hours.

One dissolved part of my boot lace.

One climbed Fenn's trouser leg and made him scream high enough that Siva laughed for ten minutes.

The owner complained about spilled salt until Siva told him he could collect his own monsters next time.

At finish, Fenn tapped the big containment pot.

"Core slime sells."

"To who?"

"Alchemists."

There.

Monster materials.

Guild contract gave us a small material share because brewery wanted them gone, not owned.

My portion after sale: four copper plus base ten.

Not tusker money.

Enough to replace lace and pay dinner.

I also bought a small vial of dried slime membrane for two copper because Fenn said it insulated low mana transfer when lacquered.

"Why?" Siva asked.

I tapped Anchor harness.

"Maybe line insulation."

"You buy trash constantly."

"Research."

"Expensive word for trash."

Back at freight yard, return cargo still not ready.

Beren looked at my slightly smoking boot edge.

"What?"

"Slime."

He stared.

"You left for three hours."

"Productive city."

That night Siva and I ate at Copper Wheel while Beren attended another manifest argument.

She put road sheet on table.

"You want sponsor."

"Yes."

"I won't sign Westreach now."

"I know."

"I will sign your Silver review if this route closes clean."

My spoon stopped.

"Meaning?"

"Meaning I have seen you tired, bored, under animals, under thieves, under rescue load, and in a cellar where your magic made problem worse for half a second. You listen enough."

I smiled.

Could not stop.

"Don't."

"What?"

"That face."

"This is my face."

"Unfortunate."

She drank.

"But you jump off fewer carts now."

"One sample."

"Exactly."

I looked around Kelm tavern.

Travelers. Mill clerks. Guild tabs. A woman in green armor arguing about wyvern leather at next table. Two men rolling dice. Beren entering doorway furious with paper.

Normal fantasy world.

Normal current life.

Mara was twelve days away if road allowed.

Silver review no longer theoretical.

And my new boots had a slime burn on toe.

Progression remained expensive.
