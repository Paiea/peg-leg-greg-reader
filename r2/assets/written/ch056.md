# Chapter 56: The Draft

The larger room had a larger draft.

This was mathematically offensive.

I discovered it at three in the morning when wind came through the lower edge of the east window and found the only uncovered part of my back.

I woke angry at architecture.

By breakfast I had stuffed a spare rag into the gap.

Noll looked at it while eating oats.

"Elegant."

"Temporary."

"So was East Annex."

Everything became philosophy before tea.

Maud Pell collected rent that afternoon.

I pointed upstairs.

"Window leaks."

"Which?"

"East."

"Frame or sash?"

I stared.

"Cold part."

She sighed and climbed the stairs herself.

Maud ran one finger along the frame.

"Sash pulled."

"Can it be fixed?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"Carpenter comes Sixthday for the second-floor latch."

"So Sixthday."

"If he finishes the latch."

Landlord certainty.

I expected to be annoyed.

I was.

Then Maud pulled my rag out, folded it into a tighter strip, and wedged it properly along the bottom. The draft mostly stopped.

"Until then," she said.

"You do this often?"

"I own windows."

Another profession explained.

She looked around the room: field pack by the door, sword rack, letters on the shelf, practice line clear across the floor, and two chairs now because Noll had found one downstairs and declared it mine after I hosted dinner.

"Settled?" she asked.

The word was too large for rent collection.

"Mostly."

"Good."

She held out her hand.

I paid the week, three copper more than the old room.

Still irritating. Still worth it.

After she left, I moved the bed six inches away from the window anyway.

The carpenter came Sixthday.

His name was Pell too.

I looked at Maud.

"Family?"

"Brother."

"Efficient."

"Cheap."

He heard that.

"I charge you full."

"You invoice full."

Different domestic institution.

The repair took less than an hour. He shaved the swollen lower edge, reset one hinge pin, and added a thin felt strip.

No magical seal or future material. Just wood adjusted by someone who knew wood.

When he finished, the window closed cleanly.

I reached for my purse out of habit.

Maud looked at me.

"Rent."

Right.

I put the purse away.

Current Greg had enough money to solve some things. Apparently that did not make every repair in his room his bill.

That evening the wind came back.

I stood by the east window and waited.

No whistle. No cold line across the floor.

I practiced three cuts while the room stayed warm.

Then I sat beside the repaired window and read Mara's packet again, with no rag stuffed under the sash.
