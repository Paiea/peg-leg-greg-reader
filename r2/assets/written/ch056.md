# Year Chapter 14: The Visitor

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D128 / SUMMER`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 12`

`LOCATION: Halden`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

I reached Halden on the day I said I would.

This felt suspiciously competent.

The twelve days between Kelm and the river stair had been less elegant.

Beren's return cargo cleared on D117. We left Kelm before sunrise D118. The road gave us two hot days, one axle repair, and a bridge queue long enough that Siva taught me three ways to tie the same load line just to remain sane.

We reached Carrow D122.

Siva signed my Silver review sheet before taking payment.

`MULTI-DAY ROAD. GREG REMAINS USEFUL AFTER NOVELTY. LOAD SPECIALTY REAL. BLADE STRONG. JUDGMENT IMPROVING. WOULD FIELD AGAIN.`

I read `WOULD FIELD AGAIN` four times.

She saw.

"Don't make weird."

"Too late."

The signature mattered more than praise because it came from someone who had watched me be bored.

I spent D123 repairing gear. D124 at Bell Tile because Dena had a filter line issue and because returning to Carrow only to leave again felt rude even when nobody asked. D125 with Noll and Jorren for dinner.

Noll had a new kitchen runner named Peto who apparently cut onions badly and cried when criticized.

"You criticize?" I asked.

"Yes."

"Cruelly?"

"Correctly."

Noll had become somebody's difficult senior.

Jorren's factor rival, the new counter he disliked, had become his friend. Her name was Bessa. He still disliked that she counted faster.

"Then practice," I said.

"I do."

People changed while I traveled.

D126 I packed for Halden. D127 I took river packet west.

Now D128.

Summer Halden looked nothing like the city in my letters.

Louder. Hotter.

The river had dropped enough to expose dark stone along the lower steps. Market awnings stretched over streets. Vey barges crowded the quay. Someone had painted half a building yellow and stopped midway.

No one waited for my arrival.

Mara had warned me.

Her last note:

**Boat gets in around first bell. I work until second. Green Finch knows your name. Do not become tragic.**

I had considered arriving at Vey anyway.

Instead I went to Finch.

The blue sign was still blue.

The innkeeper did not know me. Mara had only left my name.

"Room?"

"Greg. Mara may have left name."

She checked the slate.

"Tall Greg."

I closed my eyes.

"Apparently."

The room was small, clean enough, one bed, one chair, washstand, window toward roof instead of river.

I set my pack down. The Blackglass Anchor made a dull knock against the bedframe.

I unpacked slowly.

New boots. Better belt. Replacement bracer. Glasshorn shard wrapped in cloth.

Four months of objects.

Then I bathed because road and river travel had become a social crime.

At second bell I walked to Vey.

Not early.

Mostly.

Halden streets were steeper than Carrow and apparently designed to punish people carrying luggage. I had none. Still complained internally.

Vey river yard announced itself through yellow marks and shouting.

A gate clerk stopped me.

"Business?"

"Mara Vale. Freight office."

He checked the board.

"Working."

"I know."

"Then wait."

I waited.

Ten minutes.

Fifteen.

At twenty, a man with yellow cord came out.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

"Mara says if you are going to stand there looking abandoned, come carry boxes."

I looked at the gate clerk. He was trying not to smile.

"I was standing normally."

"I'm Emmet."

"You are immediately suspect."

He led me into the yard.

Mara was not waiting at romantic river stairs. She was on a loading platform arguing with a woman holding three ledgers.

Her work-coat sleeves were rolled to the elbows. Hair tied badly. Red ink on two fingers.

Four months of letters collapsed into one current body.

My chest tightened.

She saw me. Her expression changed, but not enough to stop work.

"You're on time."

"I know. Disturbing."

"Give me twenty minutes."

"Your letter promised second bell."

"It is second bell."

"This is contract fraud."

The ledger woman looked at me.

"This him?"

Mara closed her eyes.

"Sera, Greg. Greg, Sera. Do not enjoy this."

Sera smiled.

I had imagined her incorrectly from the letters. Current Sera was broad, maybe forty, one side of her head shaved, red ink across her fingers, expression professionally hostile.

"Carrow problem."

"I have been branded."

"Mara complains efficiently."

"I do not complain about him," Mara said.

Sera laughed.

Jealousy I had not admitted existing disappeared partly and was replaced by embarrassment.

Mara pointed at a stack of waxed boxes.

"Those to blue platform. Emmet knows which blue."

There were five blue platforms.

I looked at Emmet.

He smiled too pleasantly.

"This way."

So my Halden reunion became freight labor.

Better than ceremony.

The boxes held ledger copies first. Then brass buckles. Then straps. The strap boxes were heavier.

I carried two until Emmet said, "One."

"I can carry two."

"I know. Stack count is by trip."

I carried one.

Mara moved around the yard without centering me. She checked marks, answered Sera, sent a runner, crossed to the river clerk, came back with a different board.

I watched between trips.

Emmet noticed.

"You know she comes back."

"I am not worried."

"Didn't say."

I disliked him.

Fifty-three minutes later, Mara handed her last board to Sera.

"Leaving."

Sera looked at me.

"Return him?"

"Probably."

"Paper?"

"No claim."

Terrible people.

Mara walked toward the gate beside me.

We made it halfway.

Then a cart rail snapped.

Three long crates leaned outward under canvas. Workers shouted. One brace pole went up.

Mara stopped. Her whole body changed into work.

I stopped too.

Sera ran past us.

"Hold rear!"

A loader caught the second brace.

The crates contained dyed cloth, lighter than they looked but stacked high. The bottom box had slid off its chock.

Mara looked at me.

"Can lift?"

"Yes."

"No magic unless asked."

"Understood."

Emmet and I took the top crate. Two other workers took the second.

We moved them to ground.

The bottom shifted again. The brace creaked.

Sera said, "Stop."

Everyone stopped.

One worker had his hand between crate and rail. He pulled it free slowly.

Then we removed the load from the rear instead of the side.

Ten minutes.

No collapse. No heroics. Just work.

When it was safe, Sera looked at me.

"Useful."

"Mara says that like insult."

"I can too."

Mara was laughing.

I loved that she looked different laughing at work than she did in letters.

Then we finally left.

Outside Vey gate, she turned toward me.

For one second neither of us spoke.

Four months of distance. Packets. Missed dates. Work.

I had imagined this enough that every possible line sounded rehearsed.

Mara solved it by grabbing my shirt and kissing me.

Hard.

I forgot language.

Current body understood.

When she stopped, her forehead rested against my chest because height remained inconvenient.

"Hi," she said.

"Hi."

She stepped back and looked at my face, then the chest harness.

"What is that?"

"Expensive rock."

"You bought an artifact."

"Technically salvage regulator."

"How much?"

"Twenty-four net."

Mara stared.

"You spent boot money on a rock."

"I already own boots."

"That is not defense."

I smiled.

She touched the leather cover.

"Safe?"

"Mostly."

"Greg."

"Tested within limits."

"Worse."

I liked that everyone shared vocabulary.

We walked toward dinner.

Halden moved around us.

Mara knew which street to avoid because the summer market blocked it. Which noodle stall had changed owner. Which stair had one loose stone.

At dinner she asked about Westreach.

I showed her the D001 paper. The fold lines were soft now.

She read the front, then the deadlines on the back.

"D274 path."

"Yes."

"Silver review?"

"Late summer. Siva will sign."

"Who is Siva?"

Jealousy reversed gently.

I noticed.

Mara noticed me noticing.

"Don't."

"I said nothing."

"Your face."

I laughed.

"Road guard. Silver. Better with bow than me."

"At bow?"

"Yes."

"Useful clarification."

We ate noodles.

Mara told me about Vey. Sera. Emmet. A machine-repair woman named Pell who stole keys. Her landlord raising rent two copper in summer. The blue cup she broke and replaced with a green one she hated.

Current facts.

I wanted all of them.

Later, at Green Finch, she stood in my doorway.

"I work first bell."

"I know."

"I am not giving you my entire schedule."

"I didn't ask."

"You were about to."

I had been.

"When can I see you?"

Mara's expression softened.

"Tomorrow after fifth. Then maybe D130 night. After that ask me like a person."

"Needy."

"Yes."

I hated the word.

Liked the answer.

She kissed me again.

Then left.

I stood in the room with the door open after she had gone.

Tomorrow I still needed a job. Mara still worked first bell. And if I wanted to see her after D130, apparently I had to ask like a person...
