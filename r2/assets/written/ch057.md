# Chapter 57: The Return

Jorren came back with flour in his hair.

Not metaphorical flour.

Actual flour.

"You look baked," I said.

He dropped his pack beside my door.

"Mill hopper split."

"On the grain escort?"

"After. We arrived. They asked for hands."

"Paid?"

"Two copper and dinner."

"Then you look employed."

He pushed past me.

Independent life had acquired dust.

Jorren had been gone six days.

One more than planned because the mill owner convinced half the escort crew to help move grain before snow.

He had accepted because the rate was decent and because, according to him, the owner's daughter had looked at him like declining would reveal a moral failure.

"Did it?" I asked.

"Probably."

He sat in one of my two chairs.

The second chair justified itself immediately.

Noll arrived twenty minutes later with stew because food had become our main scheduling system.

Jorren told the mill story.

Noll told him flour in hair improved him.

Jorren threatened violence.

Nobody followed through because the stew had dumplings.

After dinner, Jorren rolled his right shoulder twice.

I noticed.

"Hurt?"

"Sore."

"From hopper?"

"From a sack I tried to catch because I was stupid."

Clean diagnosis.

"You working tomorrow?"

"No."

"Guild?"

"No."

"Good."

He looked at me.

"You my mother now?"

"Your mother would tell you to catch sacks better."

"True."

We played cards instead of sparring.

That was my suggestion.

Jorren objected until I reminded him he owed me three copper from the last game.

Then he objected financially.

The burned seven remained disputed.

Current Carrow rules favored me when Noll dealt and Jorren when Jorren dealt.

This was corruption at household scale.

I won one copper.

Lost two.

Learned Jorren's old tell had changed.

He no longer touched his lip when holding a high pair.

Now his left foot stopped moving.

"You noticed," he said after I called him.

"Eventually."

"Damn."

"You fixed the other one."

"Because you kept stealing from me."

Current friendship producing adaptive behavior.

Good.

Near midnight he stood.

"Grain factor asked if I'm free next week."

I looked up.

"Escort?"

"Maybe. Millstone going north-east. Heavy cart. Wants two Bronze if it moves."

"You say yes?"

"Said I'd hear terms."

He put on his coat carefully around the sore shoulder.

"If it's decent, I'll ask you."

Not the Guild.

Not Sella.

Not me hovering near a board.

His contact.

His choice.

"Ask when you know," I said.

Jorren nodded.

Then left for his own room.

No sleepover.

No party meeting.

Just a man going home after losing two games and possibly bringing me work later...
