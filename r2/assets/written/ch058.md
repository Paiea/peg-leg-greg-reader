# Year Chapter 16: The Ward Specialist

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D153 / SUMMER`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 13`

`LOCATION: Halden west ruins`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

The ruin contract spent thirteen days becoming real.

That was different from a notice appearing on a Guild board and turning immediately into destiny.

Ves mentioned it first on D141 while signing the marsh-runner report.

"West aqueduct survey. Three days minimum. Maybe five."

"Danger?"

"Old stone, collapsed channels, heat pockets, possible nest."

"Possible what nest?"

"Unknown."

"Excellent."

"That's not enthusiasm I trust."

I trusted it enough for both of us.

The survey itself belonged to Halden municipal works. The Guild supplied field bodies because the aqueduct ran through old defensive cuts outside city wall where maintenance crews had stopped going after two men disappeared the previous summer.

Disappeared sounded dramatic.

Records were less helpful.

One man failed to return after checking flow gate.

Second went looking and also failed to return.

No bodies.

No confirmed creature.

No magical event.

Just absence.

Ves would lead.

He needed four people.

One mapper.

One healer.

One combat-capable second.

One load/ward specialist because old aqueduct used charge-balancing glass in the surviving drawings.

That last line made my stomach tighten.

"Blackglass?"

"Could be."

"You chose me because of that?"

"I chose you because you worked twelve days in Halden without making Sera ban your name."

"High standard."

"Also the rock."

Good.

Over the next twelve days, I did not sit around waiting.

That mattered.

I worked four Guild shifts, including one ugly cellar job killing blue-backed rats the size of cats beneath a spice warehouse. They were not magical enough to impress anyone and bit through one leather glove anyway.

I trained twice with Kes from Halden yard. He managed to sweep my leg once and mentioned it every time we met afterward.

I spent three evenings with Mara.

One was good.

One became an argument because I told her the aqueduct sounded manageable before admitting two workers had vanished.

One was quiet enough that we ate peaches on her floor while she did Vey sums and I repaired a harness clip.

That one might have been best.

The Blackglass Anchor changed too.

Not dramatically.

Pell, Mara's machine-repair friend, helped me build a proper carrying plate from spring steel and leather spacers. She charged seven copper and insulted the original mounting board until I stopped defending it.

"You bolted four-pound regulator to furniture wood."

"Scrap board."

"Furniture died before you found it."

The new plate mounted beneath my left ribs, three short connection eyes accessible without removing harness.

Better.

Less likely to knock spoons over.

D153 arrived hot and cloudless.

We met west gate before first bell.

Party:

Ves, Silver lead and spear.

Mara? No. Important. Her world should not automatically join Greg's adventure. She was at Vey.

Mapper was Lorn, a compact older man with one glass eye and enough rolled paper to start fire for a week.

Healer was Tessa, Bronze by Guild rank but municipal-certified, short black hair, heavy medical satchel, no visible weapon beyond knife.

Fourth was me.

Four-person party.

Not permanent.

Still enough that I felt something old inside me wake.

Formation memory.

Spacing.

Who carried what.

I wanted to optimize immediately.

Ves prevented.

"We walk two hours before you reorganize anyone."

"I haven't spoken."

"Your eyes did."

I hated everyone.

The west aqueduct began outside Halden vineyards where old stone channel crossed hillside on low arches. Most remained in use nearer city. The abandoned section disappeared into a limestone ridge through a maintenance gate sealed with municipal iron.

Mara had packed me food.

Not romantically.

She had seen my provisions and called them punishment.

Two cheese rolls, dried apples, salted chicken, something wrapped in leaf that smelled aggressively of garlic.

At west gate she had sent runner with note:

**Bring all your legs back.**

I had laughed.

Then felt strange about the sentence for reasons I could not explain.

Future peg-leg knowledge belonged to author, not Greg. Current Greg simply folded note into inside pocket because it was Mara.

We reached aqueduct gate at third bell.

Lorn checked municipal seal.

Unbroken since last survey attempt eleven months ago.

Ves opened.

Cold air came out.

Not dungeon air.

Wet stone and old mineral.

We lit lamps.

The maintenance tunnel ran beside a dry water channel six feet wide and four deep. Ceiling arched. Old blue tiles marked distance every hundred paces.

Black seams appeared in mortar after second marker.

I stopped.

"Related."

Lorn looked.

"To your regulator?"

"Yes."

"Can you prove?"

I held Anchor near but did not activate.

Silver threads brightened faintly anyway.

"Enough for me."

Ves said, "Not enough for report."

Correct.

We continued.

First hour boring.

Good.

Lorn mapped side shafts.

Tessa checked old bones found near wall and identified goat, not missing men.

I inspected three load arches where blackglass seams crossed supports. Each seemed to distribute stress and residual charge through aqueduct structure.

The Anchor increasingly looked like removable component from a much larger civil system.

That made it more interesting, not less.

At marker nine, water returned.

A thin stream entered channel from crack in wall.

Warm.

Not hot.

Ves crouched.

"Expected?"

Lorn checked map.

"No spring marked."

Tessa smelled.

"Iron."

I sensed low charge.

"Same network."

We marked.

At marker twelve, tunnel split.

Main aqueduct continued straight behind collapse.

Maintenance stair descended right into old settling chamber.

The two missing men had been assigned to inspect lower gate.

Right.

Ves looked at us.

"We camp before descent."

It was still daylight outside somewhere.

My body wanted to continue.

Old Greg wanted answers.

Current Greg had learned enough to eat first.

We established camp in dry alcove.

No tents underground.

Bedrolls on raised stone. Lamps hung high. One trip line with bells across rear approach. Ves made me help set it instead of wandering to blackglass seam.

Tessa boiled water.

Lorn complained about garlic smell.

I ate Mara's leaf-wrapped thing.

It contained rice, meat, and enough garlic to make creatures evacuate voluntarily.

"Partner?" Tessa asked.

I looked up.

"What?"

"Food. Nobody packs that for themselves."

"Mara."

"Halden?"

"Yes."

"Then why are you smiling like idiot?"

"I'm not."

Ves said, "He is."

Traitors.

After food, we descended.

The maintenance stair spiraled thirty feet down into circular settling chamber half full of dark water.

A narrow stone walkway hugged wall.

At far side, iron gate assembly stood beneath arch.

Blackglass everywhere.

Not decorative.

Three thick bands in wall converged at gate housing.

My Anchor warmed against ribs.

I stopped.

"It's active."

Ves said, "How active?"

"More than quay. More than salvage site."

Lorn looked at water.

"Why?"

"Don't know."

We moved clockwise along walkway.

Water below remained still.

Too still maybe.

At halfway point, Tessa stopped.

"Smell."

Garlic? No.

Rot.

Faint.

Lorn's lamp swept lower.

Something pale beneath water.

Bone.

Human arm.

There.

Missing person maybe.

Ves did not let us descend.

He marked position.

"Body recovery later unless safe."

We continued toward gate.

The iron wheel had been turned halfway open.

Rust around spokes disturbed.

Recent within year.

One missing worker probably opened it.

Beyond gate, water tunnel disappeared black.

I sensed motion.

Not physical.

Charge moving through wall bands in slow pulse.

Then lamp light caught scratches on stone.

Long parallel marks.

Not tools.

Claws.

Ves saw too.

Spear came off shoulder.

Tessa stepped behind Lorn, hand on satchel knife.

I drew sword.

No shame.

We listened.

Water clicked softly against gate.

Then something exhaled from tunnel beyond.

Low.

Wet.

Close.

My pulse rose.

This was more like the fantasy story I had been trying to reach.

The thought itself felt absurdly cheerful given human bones below.

Ves whispered, "Back two."

We backed.

Something struck gate.

Iron boomed.

Water surged through opening.

A head emerged.

Not creature I knew.

Broad jaw, smooth pale scales, no visible eyes, six black sensing pits around snout. Neck thick as my thigh.

It pushed through half-open gate until shoulders jammed.

Long forelimb scraped stone.

Claws matched marks.

"Tunnel drake?" Lorn whispered.

Old Greg memory said no.

Similar family maybe.

Current no classification.

Creature opened mouth.

Teeth.

Many.

"Back," Ves said.

We moved.

The drake lunged against gate.

Blackglass bands flashed.

Gate wheel spun quarter turn by itself under distributed pressure.

Opening widened.

Oh.

"Run."

Ves didn't need shout twice.

We ran around walkway.

The creature forced shoulders through.

Stone cracked.

Water exploded onto walkway.

Lorn slipped.

I caught his pack strap.

Map tubes scattered.

He screamed about papers while I hauled him upright.

"Fuck papers!"

"Municipal originals!"

Of course.

Drake came fully into chamber.

Eight feet body, long tail still in tunnel. Pale scales armored along spine, softer belly gray.

No eyes.

It tracked vibration.

Ves slapped spear butt against stone left.

Creature turned toward sound.

There.

Sense behavior.

"Stair!"

We moved.

Tessa first with Lorn.

I followed.

Ves last.

Creature charged along walkway.

Fast.

Too fast.

Ves struck stone right, redirected half second.

Then jumped toward stairs.

Tail hit wall.

Walkway cracked.

Section between us and creature broke into water.

Good separation.

Maybe.

Drake stopped at gap.

Head lowered.

Could it swim?

Almost certainly.

We climbed.

At stair top, Ves turned.

"Camp moves now."

No heroic stand.

We grabbed bedrolls and packs.

Trip bells rang behind us.

The drake had found upper path somehow.

Different access.

Bad.

Ves pointed main tunnel east.

"Gate."

We ran toward entrance.

Half mile of old aqueduct became sprint in armor.

My lungs burned.

Anchor bounced at ribs.

Behind us claws struck stone irregularly.

Not constant pursuit.

Maybe creature navigated by sound.

Ves signaled stop at marker nine.

I almost crashed into him.

"Why?"

He pointed warm spring crack.

"Water noise."

Ah.

We moved silently past.

Creature behind slowed, attracted to running water.

Useful.

We gained distance.

At marker four, ceiling trembled.

No creature.

Old masonry.

Lorn said, "Don't run here."

We fast-walked.

Discipline under fear felt harder than fighting.

Reached outer gate near dusk.

Everyone exited.

Ves slammed iron door and municipal lock.

Then added chain.

We stood in vineyard air breathing like idiots.

Lorn looked at empty map tube.

"I lost sheet twelve."

"You're alive," Tessa said.

"Both can be true."

He was right enough to be annoying.

We camped outside rather than return city in dark.

No one wanted tunnel behind unlocked? It was locked. Still.

At fire, Ves reviewed.

"We confirmed remains. Confirmed large unknown predator. Gate mechanism responds to load through blackglass. Lower access unsafe."

I said, "We can kill it."

Silence.

There.

Not curiosity only.

I wanted to go back and fight.

Ves looked at me.

"Maybe."

"You don't think?"

"I think four of us might kill it. I don't know whether there is one. I don't know what opening the gate does to water. I don't know if missing second worker is elsewhere. Survey contract is information, not extermination."

"Then next contract."

He smiled slightly.

"Now you're thinking like Guild."

Power fantasy through paperwork.

I hated how believable it was.

Tessa checked Lorn's scraped knee.

Then mine.

I had cut forearm on wall during sprint without noticing.

She cleaned.

"Tomorrow?"

Ves said, "Back to city. Report. Then municipality decides."

I stared at dark ridge.

The drake remained inside.

Human remains remained inside.

Blackglass network remained unexplained.

No closure.

But the year had just changed shape.

Westreach was far west.

The first real ruin was two hours from Mara's apartment and had almost eaten us before dinner.

I slept badly and loved that I had a reason to go back.
