# Chapter 58: The Ring

Pell Orst sent the note because he was tired of owning my metal.

**DONE. COME GET IT.**

Reasonable.

I had left the useful brace ring with him long enough that it had become part of the shop furniture in my mind.

The ugly spare remained on my shelf.

I wrapped it in cloth and brought it with me for comparison.

Pell's repair shop smelled like oil, old ash, and confidence in small hammers.

He set the repaired ring on the counter.

Three brass tabs around a dark iron body.

One tab slightly newer than the others.

Not pretty.

Good.

"Replacement matches?" I asked.

"Matches enough."

"Load?"

"Same warning as before. Modest. Static better than shock. Don't hang a horse from it."

"Wasn't planning to."

"People say that before horses."

I put the ugly spare beside it.

The repaired ring sat flatter.

One inner groove had been cleaned and re-cut.

Pell pointed with a pick.

"Old latch face was rounded. Would've walked under repeated pull. I squared it."

Later me remembered why the pattern survived in field kits long after better-rated hardware existed.

Not strength.

Tolerance.

Three ugly anchor points could become one useful geometry.

Current ring did not know any of that.

It was metal.

"Charge?" I asked.

Pell snorted.

"It doesn't make power."

"I know."

"Good. Half the people buying old support hardware think brass means magic lives inside."

"Sometimes magic lives inside brass."

"Then it charges more."

Market theory.

He had already received the agreed repair share from the scrap lot.

No new payment.

I liked him more.

"Can I test the latch?"

He jerked his head toward the back wall.

A timber post held six iron eyes at bad angles.

Of course it did.

I threaded three short cords through the ring tabs.

Different heights.

Different directions.

Pulled.

The ring rotated until load equalized across two, then settled against the third.

No magic.

Just geometry.

I shifted one cord lower.

The ring accepted the ugliness.

There.

Later field memory confirmed itself in the smallest possible way.

Pell watched.

"You use these before?"

Dangerous question.

"Similar."

"Where?"

"Later."

He stared.

"I regret asking."

Correct.

I packed the repaired ring.

"You want the spare fixed too?"

I looked at it.

One cracked tab.

Bent latch groove.

Repairable.

Not necessary.

"No."

Pell nodded as if I had passed a private test.

"Good."

"Why good?"

"Because you already own one."

Retail hostility.

I took both home.

The repaired ring went on the equipment shelf beside waxed cord and spare buckles.

Not in the field pack.

Not yet.

The ugly spare went back into its cloth.

Noll leaned into the doorway.

"Magic?"

"Metal."

"Useful?"

"Probably."

"Expensive?"

"Less than it could have been."

He nodded.

All necessary appraisal complete.

The ring stayed on the shelf for the rest of the week.

Nothing in Carrow rearranged itself to justify my purchase...
