# D426: Scope

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The Guild wanted me to fall down.

This was the first useful thing anyone had asked all morning.

"Controlled," Sella said.

"Still falling."

"Yes."

"Excellent."

Edda stood ten feet away with her spear tucked under one arm.

She had been assigned as field assessor because apparently once you became Silver, other Silvers got paid to watch you embarrass yourself.

Good institution.

The Guild yard was dry.

I had asked for wet ground.

Sella refused.

I had asked for gravel.

Edda refused.

"First reassessment," she said. "Not execution."

Reasonable people everywhere.

Annoying.

I wore the training leg Bera had built twenty-four days earlier.

Not the first socket.

Third.

First rubbed the outer stump raw after forty minutes.

Second solved that by creating pressure behind the knee.

Third used a slightly higher leather wall and a thinner inner pad.

Ugly.

Reliable for nearly three hours on good ground.

The wooden foot remained simple.

No ankle.

I had stopped insulting it where Bera could hear.

My right hand held the good sword.

Not drawn.

Left held one short walking staff rather than two crutches.

That change alone had taken more work than I wanted to admit.

"Show me," Edda said.

I dropped.

Not collapsed.

Right knee bent.

Staff planted.

Left prosthetic moved backward enough to clear.

Right hand released the sword sheath and caught ground.

Hip turned.

I landed on the right side instead of directly on the stump.

Ugly.

Controlled.

Pain two of ten.

No socket shift.

I stayed down.

Edda walked around me.

"Can you get up?"

"Yes."

"Then don't yet."

I almost laughed.

She crouched.

Checked where the wooden foot had landed.

"You planned that."

"Obviously."

"Most people plan not to fall."

"Most people have two ankles."

She looked at me.

"Fair."

Then:

"Up."

Staff.

Right foot.

Knee.

Hip.

Stand.

Seven breaths.

Bera wanted ten.

I had gotten it to seven without using magic.

That mattered because field magic would not always be available when I fell.

Edda marked something on the sheet.

Sella watched from the rail.

No Tovan.

He had cleared me for the reassessment and refused to attend because, in his words, he did not need to watch me negotiate with gravity professionally.

Coward.

Next test.

Twenty paces carrying light field gear.

Easy now.

Not easy three weeks ago.

I did not let the distinction disappear merely because current me was bored.

Walking still required attention.

Wooden foot forward.

Load through socket.

Hip over it.

Right leg through.

Staff only when terrain or turn demanded it.

No hidden force-plane.

Twenty paces.

Turn.

Back.

Edda marked again.

"Static shaping."

Three target boards waited against the wall at different heights.

I formed the first plane chest-high.

Five inches.

Stable.

Second low and angled.

Third overhead.

The overhead one pulled my balance left because my torso still compensated differently when both arms rose.

I corrected without dropping it.

Edda noticed.

"Again."

I repeated.

Cleaner.

Then weapon draw.

That was where things became interesting.

A sword draw assumed feet.

Not philosophically.

Mechanically.

Old draw stepped slightly with the left, hips opening as the blade cleared.

My first attempts with the prosthetic had caught the wooden foot behind me and turned a simple draw into a lesson in furniture.

So I changed it.

Right foot half-step back.

Left wooden foot planted forward as static post.

Hip rotation smaller.

Draw closer to the body.

Less pretty.

Faster than trying to imitate the old motion.

I drew.

Blade clear.

Guard up.

Stable.

Edda's expression changed.

"Again."

I did.

Then she moved.

No warning.

Spear butt toward my right shoulder.

I formed a plane automatically.

Small.

Hard.

The spear struck and slid.

I pivoted on the right foot, let the wooden side act as post, and put the sword edge against the spear shaft before my brain finished being offended.

Edda stopped.

So did I.

Sella said, "That wasn't on the sheet."

Edda smiled.

"Now it is."

My heart was beating too fast.

Not from effort.

Pleasure.

There it was.

For half a second the body had not been a medical problem.

It had been geometry.

Different geometry.

Still mine.

"Again," I said.

Edda lowered the spear.

"No."

Of course.

She looked at the sheet.

"You pass mobility for controlled sites. You pass static shaping. You pass basic weapon control."

"Road combat."

"No."

The pleasure vanished.

"Why?"

"Because you cannot retreat fast enough on broken ground."

"You didn't test broken ground."

"I watched you enter the yard."

"Dry cobble."

"And you caught the staff twice."

I hated her.

"Pursuit isn't required for every road contract."

"Retreat is."

"Then scope the job."

Edda paused.

Sella's eyes narrowed.

There.

They knew the tone now.

I continued.

"You are evaluating whether I can return to all prior Silver work. Answer is no. Fine. Wrong question. What field work exists where my failure to run does not create unacceptable risk?"

Sella said, "Greg."

"Static structure support. Controlled warehouse response. Prepared site work. Caravan load specialist where I'm riding, not perimeter. Ruin support if movement route is established before contact."

Edda stared.

I kept going.

"No pursuit. No scout. No open-water platform. No unknown-terrain front line. No job where another worker must stay behind to move me if the site fails."

Silence.

That last one mattered.

I knew it.

They knew it.

"You're writing your own restriction," Edda said.

"I'm writing a role."

"Convenient."

"Specific."

Sella came off the rail.

"Guild doesn't normally split Silver field scope that finely."

"Why?"

"Because normally we don't need to."

"Now you do."

She looked annoyed.

Good.

Annoyance meant the argument had reached administration.

Edda leaned on her spear.

"I'd take him on a prepared structure job."

Sella looked at her.

"You would?"

"Yes. Not road guard. Not chase work. But if I know the site and his exit route before we start, yes."

There.

Not sympathy.

Professional testimony.

Sella looked at me.

"Temporary limited field clearance. Prepared-site support only. Job lead has to accept the restriction. Healer keeps authority over socket wear duration. No solo response."

I wanted more.

Obviously.

I also recognized a breach when I saw one.

"Write it."

Sella sighed.

"You could say thank you."

"After you write it."

Edda laughed.

Sella wrote.

I read every line.

Changed one phrase from `mobility-impaired support` to `prepared-site support with restricted rapid withdrawal.`

Sella stared at me.

"Those mean the same thing."

"Then use the useful one."

She changed it.

I signed.

Silver tab stayed.

Field scope returned in a narrower shape.

Not what I had before.

Enough to work.

Outside the Guild, Jorren waited with two cups from a tea stall.

"Pass?"

"Partially."

He handed me one.

"That's pass."

"Limited prepared-site clearance."

"That's work."

"Yes."

He smiled.

I did too.

Then Jorren said, "Drink tonight?"

"Can't."

His smile faded slightly.

"Why?"

"Bera wants socket off by seventh bell. I need skin check. Arlo has a brass collar prototype after."

"Tomorrow?"

"Warehouse job with Kessa."

"Next day?"

"Training."

Jorren looked at me.

Not angry.

Something worse.

Tired.

"You booked the whole week."

"Yes."

"And next?"

I knew the answer.

He knew I knew.

"Mostly."

Jorren drank his tea.

"Right."

There was the cost.

Small.

Not enough to stop anything.

I could solve it later.

That thought arrived so smoothly I nearly missed it.

Later.

People became later very easily when the work had edges.

"Two nights," I said.

Jorren looked at me.

"What?"

"Two nights from now. No training after sixth bell."

"You scheduling friendship?"

"Yes."

He stared.

Then laughed despite himself.

"Fucking terrible."

"Will you be there?"

"Yes."

"Then system works."

"I hate you."

Good.

I drank the tea.

It had gone cold while we argued.

Still useful.
