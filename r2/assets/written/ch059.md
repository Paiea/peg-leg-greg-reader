# Chapter 59: The Ask

Jorren asked me before the contract existed at the Guild.

That mattered more than the rate.

He found me outside Bell Tile, where I had just finished watching Arlo argue about firing temperature with a man who owned three kilns and therefore had superior lungs.

Jorren held a folded paper.

"Terms came."

"Millstone?"

"Yes."

He handed it over.

Client: Vessa Grain Factors.

Origin: Carrow east yard.

Destination: Gannet Mill, two days north-east in winter conditions if roads held.

Load: one dressed millstone half, repair hardware, two replacement bearings, one crank assembly.

Crew: driver, millwright, two Bronze escorts.

Return by hired coach if available, otherwise paid walking day.

The rate was decent rather than spectacular, with a heavy-load premium and a winter delay clause.

"Why two Bronze?" I asked.

"Driver asked. Last stone cart got stuck outside Hesk's Run and the client lost a day finding hands."

"Threat?"

"Ordinary road. Weight is the problem."

"Who offered you?"

"Vessa. I did grain work for her cousin. He told her I don't complain when mills get stupid."

Reputation networked through complaint tolerance.

"And me?"

Jorren shrugged.

"I need a second."

"Guild has several."

"I know."

He had several options. He was asking me.

"You want me?"

"Yes."

Direct enough to become embarrassing if examined.

I looked back at the paper.

Four days likely, maybe five. No support specialist and no Silver lead. Two Bronze because load and winter made small problems expensive.

"When?"

"Secondday."

I checked my own schedule. No booked instruction, no business meeting, Mara's packet already sent, window repair done.

I could go.

"Yes."

Jorren took the paper back.

"Good."

"That's it?"

"What else?"

Apparently no ceremony.

He turned to leave.

"Jorren."

"What?"

"Shoulder?"

He rolled it once.

"Fine."

"Actually?"

"Actually."

I believed him because he had spent the last week not proving otherwise.

"Secondday," he said, and left.

Arlo came out of Bell Tile behind me.

"Did you buy a millstone?"

"No."

"Good."

"I'm guarding one."

He considered.

"Worse."

Maybe.

I went home and packed.

The repaired brace ring remained on the shelf.

I looked at it once. Heavy cart, awkward load, exactly the kind of thing the old pattern might someday help with.

I left it there.

The ring was not useless. I simply had no field method for it with current support, no practiced calls, and no reason to introduce unfamiliar equipment into a job whose main problem was weight and road judgment.

I packed another pair of socks instead.
