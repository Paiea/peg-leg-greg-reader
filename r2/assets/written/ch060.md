Ten days after killing the white drake, nobody cared anymore.

This was healthy.

I did.

A little.

The scale still sat on my Green Finch table because carrying it around would have crossed from trophy into illness.

The bandage was off my ankle by D172.

The cut had closed cleanly.

The Blackglass Anchor had developed a thin heat stain along one bronze eye from the aqueduct fight, which Pell said was cosmetic after testing and I did not entirely believe.

That number looked romantic until broken down.

One full night.

Two dinners.

One argument in a stairwell because I had agreed to a Silver review date before telling her I might leave Halden immediately after.

Two twenty-minute meetings near Vey because work won.

Enough to test.

Good.

East retaining works sat outside Halden lower wall where river had eaten soil beneath a freight ramp. Municipal crews were rebuilding stone face before autumn rain.

The test was simple to explain.

Work one real shift under Silver lead.

Reviewers would observe.

No staged monsters promised.

No guarantee nothing happened.

Three candidates.

Me.

A Halden Bronze archer named Senn.

A broad woman named Kala who had spent two years doing caravan guard and looked bored by advancement rituals.

One was Calla, field captain, short white hair, old burn across jaw.

Other was Oren, logistics specialist, heavy book at belt instead of weapon.

Our shift lead was not reviewer.

That mattered.

Ves.

"I am always special."

Kala laughed.

Good start.

Worksite problem was actual work.

Retaining stones arrived by cart above ramp, lowered by paired hoists to river workers below. Temporary ward plates monitored lateral pressure because bank remained unstable.

Our job as Guild field hands:

Keep work lane clear.

Support riggers.

Watch bank.

Handle magical or creature interruption if it happened.

Mostly stand, carry, listen.

Silver evaluation began with boredom.

Perfect.

Fair.

Kala chatted with stone crew and learned two names before I learned one.

Good test discipline.
