# Chapter 60: The Load

The millstone made the cart look embarrassed.

Half a wheel of gray stone lay flat in a timber cradle over the rear axle, strapped until the straps themselves looked nervous.

The driver was a woman named Orra Dane.

The millwright was her brother Pell Dane, which created immediate confusion with Pell Orst in my head and none in anyone else's.

Current people were inconsiderate about names.

Jorren climbed onto the rear step and inspected the cradle.

Not pretending.

He had spent enough time around grain and mills that he knew what he was looking at now.

"Rear left strap twists," he said.

Pell Dane checked.

"It holds."

"Didn't say it doesn't."

Pell retied it anyway.

Good start.

My job was front and road.

Jorren's was rear and load.

Nobody assigned that formally.

We did it because it made sense.

The first day was slow.

Not dangerous.

Slow.

Every hill became a negotiation between horse, brake, stone, mud, and whether Orra wanted to hear suggestions.

Usually she did not.

Correct.

At midday the right wheel dropped into a rut deep enough that the axle beam kissed mud.

Orra swore.

Pell swore differently.

Jorren looked at me.

I looked at the road.

No attackers.

No monster.

Just a thousand pounds of expensive circle refusing to continue north-east.

"Unload?" I asked.

Pell looked horrified.

"No."

"Then lift wheel."

Orra already had the jack bar out.

Good.

We dug around the rut.

Pell cut two short crib blocks from carried timber.

Jorren found flat roadside stone.

I took the jack bar with Orra.

No Barrier.

No brace ring.

Four bodies and leverage.

"Now," Orra said.

We lifted.

Jorren shoved stone under the wheel.

Pell packed behind it.

Lower.

Reset.

Again.

Twenty minutes.

The wheel climbed out.

No applause.

Orra checked the axle.

Pell checked the cradle.

Jorren checked the straps.

I checked the road ahead because everyone else was checking the thing they actually understood.

We moved.

Near dusk we reached a roadside inn with a stable big enough for the cart.

The innkeeper charged extra because the millstone occupied half a covered bay.

Pell argued.

Orra paid.

"Client reimburses," she said.

"Still theft," Pell said.

"Documented theft."

Institutional progress.

Jorren and I shared a room with two beds and one blanket too few.

He won the extra blanket because he had asked first.

I hated fair systems.

"You bring the ring?" he asked while unlacing boots.

I looked at him.

"How do you know about the ring?"

"Noll."

Of course.

"No."

"Would it help?"

"Maybe."

"Why leave it?"

"Because maybe isn't a field method."

Jorren nodded.

No challenge.

No demand I prove future expertise.

He rubbed his feet.

"Good socks?"

"New enough."

"Mine are dying."

"Buy better."

"You rich now?"

"Two pairs."

He threw a sock at me.

Friendship.

The next morning the cart rolled before sunrise.

Heavy work had made us slower.

It had also made the job feel less like escorting a client and more like moving one stubborn fact across the map...
