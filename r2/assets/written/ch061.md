# Year Chapter 19: Five Days

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D181 / SUMMER`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 5`

`LOCATION: Halden river packet / Carrow approach`

`RANK: Bronze`

Five days was long enough for the retaining-worker's ankle to become somebody else's story.

He had not lost the leg.

That mattered more than whether the Guild liked my review.

Bren, the site medic, sent word through Ves that the break was clean and ugly but ordinary. Six weeks minimum before load work. Longer before carts.

I slept better after that.

The Silver result still had not posted.

D183.

Two days after I returned to Carrow.

The timing felt designed specifically to punish me.

Mara thought it was funny.

"You could stay."

We were standing in Green Finch room on D180 while I packed.

She sat on the single chair with one boot on and one off, watching me fold clothes badly.

"For two days?"

"Yes."

"Then if I pass, I celebrate in Halden and immediately leave."

"Could."

"If I fail, I become tragic in your room."

"Already have practice."

"Cruel."

"Accurate."

I put drake scale between shirts so it would not crack.

The Blackglass Anchor went into padded harness case Pell had made from leftover machine leather. She had charged me another four copper on D178 and told me the artifact was now better dressed than I was.

True.

Halden had changed my inventory.

Not just gear.

Receipts.

Guild marks.

A little carved river bird Mara bought from festival stall because she claimed my room needed one object with no professional use.

I had objected until she threatened to keep it.

Now it was wrapped in sock.

"You're really going," she said.

There.

The sentence changed room.

"Yes."

"I know."

She looked at half-packed bag.

"Good."

I waited.

No explanation.

"Good?"

"You have a life there."

"I have one here too."

Mara's expression softened in a way I distrusted because it made leaving harder.

"Some."

"That's rude."

"You have me here. You have work you can get. You have people who know you now. But you still say 'my room' and mean Lower Rook."

I looked around Finch.

Temporary bed.

Temporary chair.

Drake scale.

Her boot.

"Yes."

"So go home."

No relationship cliff.

No breakup.

No promise to move cities.

Just distance returning.

I hated how adult that felt.

"When next?"

Mara looked toward ceiling as if dates lived there.

"Autumn maybe. Vey has east loads. I might come Carrow."

My chest lifted.

"Might?"

"Do not schedule my freight."

"I wasn't."

"Face."

I threw a sock at her.

She caught it.

"This is mine."

It was.

Of course.

We laughed too hard.

That night we stayed together without trying to make it memorable.

This seemed important enough that I almost ruined it by noticing.

Breakfast next morning was bread and peaches.

Mara walked me to river packet before Vey.

She carried nothing because I refused to let her carry my bag and she called me stupid for making principle out of luggage.

Halden summer morning already hot.

River low.

Boats crowded at stone steps.

Packet master checked my ticket.

"Carrow."

"Yes."

"Two days if current. Three if storm."

Mara said, "Two."

Packet master looked at sky.

"Tell river."

I liked him.

Boarding bell rang.

Mara kissed me.

Not long.

People waiting behind us.

"Write," she said.

"You too."

"I do."

"Sometimes nine days."

"Count less."

Impossible request.

I stepped aboard.

This time she left before boat did.

Vey shift.

I watched her climb stairs, disappear into market, and felt no need to turn it into abandonment.

That was new.

The packet moved east.

Halden shrank.

Then became river bend.

Then gone.

I expected grief.

Got hunger.

Bought meat pie.

Human body remained disrespectful.

The first day passed mostly without story.

I talked with a wool merchant named Fenn who thought Black Stair expedition was a waste of good animals.

"People go west to find old rocks," he said. "Then come east and buy roofs."

"You don't like old rocks?"

"I like roofs."

Fair.

A family traveling to Carrow had three children who discovered my drake scale when I took it out to check wrapping.

The youngest asked whether I killed dragon.

"Drake."

"Dragon?"

"Smaller."

"Baby dragon?"

"No."

"Then dragon."

Language lost.

I let him touch scale.

His mother apologized.

"He's been asking everyone with sword."

"Good strategy eventually."

At dusk we tied at river post.

I slept in packet bunk badly.

No Mara.

No Lower Rook yet.

Between places.

The second day river changed.

Clouds built before noon.

Wind against current.

Packet captain ordered canvas lowered.

Passengers moved inside except stubborn people who liked weather until weather became personal.

I stayed under awning because air felt good.

Then mooring line snapped.

Not while docked.

A tow line securing cargo punt behind packet.

Sound like whip.

Punt swung sideways in current, loaded with six crates and one deckhand.

He fell.

"Line!"

Crew moved instantly.

I moved because apparently river had trained me.

Deckhand caught punt rail before entering water fully, one arm hanging.

The punt itself began rotating, threatening to strike packet stern.

Captain shouted for pole.

Two crew pushed.

Need new tow line across gap.

A sailor threw coil.

Wind took it short.

I grabbed second.

"Can you throw?" he asked.

"Yes."

No caveat.

The Halden rescue came back in body.

I timed swing, threw ahead of punt's bow.

Line landed across rail.

Deckhand caught with free hand but could not tie while holding himself.

"Loop it!"

He wrapped once around cleat.

Not secure.

Enough for tension.

Crew hauled.

Punt swung toward packet.

Too fast.

Crates shifted.

One tore loose.

I saw geometry.

Could use Anchor.

Not connected.

Need physical points.

I clipped one short line to packet stern ring, one to spare tow line, third to harness control.

"What are you doing?" sailor shouted.

"Taking shock."

"Can it?"

"Some."

Captain heard.

"Do it!"

Good enough authority.

When punt line went taut, load hit.

Anchor silver threads flared beneath leather cover.

Instead of full shock snapping rope or jerking cleat, force split partly into stern ring and my controlled plane.

My shoulders slammed back.

Not pleasant.

The punt slowed.

Crew gained rope.

Deckhand got both arms over rail.

Another sailor hooked his coat and hauled him onto packet deck as punt came alongside.

Crate went into river.

One lost.

Five saved.

Nobody died.

Captain ordered me release Anchor.

I did.

No extra test.

The artifact had become something I reached for during work, not a special scene.

That felt like progression.

Storm hit twenty minutes later.

Rain hard enough river disappeared beyond gray wall.

We anchored at nearest post instead of reaching Carrow that night.

Packet master had been right.

Three days if storm.

My D183 Silver result suddenly aligned with arrival.

Of course.

I spent night in crowded river inn with wet passengers and one angry wool merchant whose lost crate had contained dyed thread.

Not his roof money, apparently.

The deckhand had dislocated shoulder, reset by healer at post.

He bought me beer with his good hand.

"You Guild?"

"Bronze."

He looked at Anchor.

"That thing Guild?"

"Mine."

"Useful."

I smiled.

"Yes."

No need explain cost.

Next morning storm cleared.

We made Carrow by late afternoon D182.

City walls appeared under clean sky.

My body relaxed before mind named why.

Home.

Again.

"Rent arrived by packet."
