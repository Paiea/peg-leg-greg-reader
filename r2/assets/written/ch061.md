# Chapter 61: The Toll

Hesk's Run bridge refused the millstone without seeing it.

A red board stood across the road before the tollhouse.

**HEAVY LOADS STOP. ICE DAMAGE.**

Orra pulled the team up.

Pell Dane stared at the sign as if literacy had betrayed him.

"We crossed here last month."

The toll keeper came out wearing a patched blue coat.

"Then cross last month."

I liked him immediately.

Orra climbed down.

"How heavy?"

"Anything over four hundred cart load beyond normal wagon."

Pell pointed at the bridge.

"Looks fine."

The keeper looked at him.

"You a bridge?"

"Millwright."

"Then it looks excellent for milling."

Jorren coughed into his glove.

Probably hiding a laugh.

The keeper explained.

One upstream footing had lifted after freeze.

Bridge still open to ordinary carts.

Heavy concentrated rear loads prohibited until inspection.

Our millstone sat directly over the rear axle.

Worst possible shape.

"Can we pay premium?" Pell asked.

"No."

Beautiful institution.

No bribe lane.

No heroic crossing.

No special Bronze exception.

Orra unfolded the route sheet.

"Detour?"

The keeper pointed east.

"Old quarry road to Fenner Ford, then north. Six miles. Ford shallow but muddy."

"Time?"

"Half day if your horses like you."

Orra looked at the horses.

The horses did not comment.

Pell swore again.

Jorren asked the useful question.

"Any heavy cart through the ford this week?"

"Stone wagon yesterday."

"Got through?"

"With two teams."

We had one.

Orra looked at Pell.

"Client decision. We wait for bridge inspection or detour and hire pull if needed."

Pell's jaw worked.

Money.

Time.

Mill waiting for replacement stone.

None of us could decide for him.

"Detour," he said.

Good.

We turned.

The quarry road was worse than promised and better than feared.

Mud under frozen crust.

One steep descent.

At Fenner Ford the water came only to wheel hub, but the far bank had been churned into brown paste.

A farm team waited nearby because apparently economics anticipated weakness.

The farmer named a price.

Pell objected.

Orra looked at the bank.

"Pay him."

Client decision already made by reality.

The second team hooked ahead.

Jorren walked rear left.

I took rear right.

Not pushing unless wheel slipped.

The cart entered.

Water.

Stone.

Mud.

The rear wheel spun once.

"Hold!" Orra called.

Both teams stopped.

Pell wanted more pull.

Jorren pointed at the wheel.

"Digging. Need angle."

We backed six inches.

Orra shifted the lead team left.

Try again.

This time the wheel climbed.

No magic.

No sword.

A job solved by listening to the person standing where the problem was.

We reached the far bank.

Pell paid the farmer.

His expression suggested personal betrayal by geography.

The detour cost us daylight.

We camped at a hamlet three miles short of planned lodging.

Cold barn loft.

Cheap stew.

Jorren checked the stone straps before eating.

I checked the road markers for tomorrow.

Nobody said we had worked well together.

The cart still existed.

That was enough evidence...
