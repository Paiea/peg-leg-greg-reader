# Chapter 62: The Mill

Gannet Mill had already removed the broken stone.

That was the first thing I liked about it.

The second was the smell.

Warm grain, wood dust, river water, old oil.

A working place.

The mill wheel turned slowly under winter flow while six people waited in the yard with ropes, blocks, timber skids, and the focused impatience of people whose work had been stopped by one broken circle.

Nobody cheered when our cart arrived late.

The mill owner looked at Pell Dane.

"Bridge?"

"Closed heavy."

"You pay pull team?"

"Yes."

"Good. Bring it."

Current accountability.

The unloading took longer than the escorting had.

A timber A-frame already stood over the yard.

Two chain blocks hung from it.

Jorren immediately joined the grain hands on the left line.

I took the right because someone pointed there.

Pell Dane directed rigging.

Orra kept the cart brakes locked and the horses well away.

Nobody asked the Bronze escorts to become millwrights.

"Tension," Pell called.

We pulled.

The sling tightened around the stone.

"Up one."

Chain blocks clicked.

The stone lifted a finger width.

Cart settled slightly as weight left the axle.

"Hold."

Everybody held.

Pell crawled under far enough to check sling position.

Not heroic.

Probably unsafe by some later standard.

Current professionals had their own thresholds.

"Again."

Up.

The millstone cleared the cradle.

Orra rolled the cart forward.

Then the whole yard turned the stone through ninety degrees using guide lines and curses.

I understood several better methods from later life.

None existed here with this equipment.

I kept my mouth shut unless the line in my hand mattered.

Useful restraint.

When the stone reached the skid bed, the mill owner touched its edge like checking a horse.

"Good."

Pell's shoulders dropped.

Contract result.

We ate in the mill kitchen afterward.

Bread made from flour ground on the remaining stone.

Dense cheese.

Hot onion soup.

Jorren had earned an extra half bowl by knowing one of the mill hands from his grain run.

He shared none.

Friendship had limits.

The client settled our escort pay before dusk.

Heavy-load premium included.

Delay clause documented.

The bridge closure was nobody's breach.

We could have taken a coach back the next morning, but the only one had already filled with two merchants and a woman transporting six chickens in baskets.

Jorren looked at the chickens.

"Walk?"

"Walk."

Paid return day.

Our packs were lighter without responsibility for the stone.

The road felt different immediately.

At Hesk's Run the red board still stood across the heavy lane.

Ordinary carts crossed one at a time.

The toll keeper recognized us.

"Mill survive?"

"Barely," Jorren said.

"Tragic."

We crossed on foot.

Back in Carrow two days later, Vessa Grain Factors had already filed the closeout because Orra returned faster by cart after unloading.

Jorren signed his line.

I signed mine.

Sella's temporary desk had acquired a proper inkstand.

East Annex smelled less like cabbage now.

Not none.

Less.

Outside, Jorren adjusted his pack.

"Same next time?"

I looked at him.

Not a party declaration.

Not forever.

A question about the next job he might actually get.

"If you're asking."

"I'm asking."

"Then yes."

He nodded once.

We split at East Market because he lived east and I didn't.

Two Bronze workers with separate rooms, separate money, separate schedules, and a growing habit of choosing each other when the work fit.

That was enough for now...
