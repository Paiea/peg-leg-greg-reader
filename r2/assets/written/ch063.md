# Chapter 63: The Helper

Dena wanted a helper because being competent had become inconvenient.

She brought the problem to the owners with a person attached.

The person was sixteen, narrow as a broom handle, and carrying a clean chamber without touching the collar.

"Cessa," Dena said.

Cessa nodded at us.

"She has mornings free until spring market starts," Dena continued. "Can wash, carry, chalk returns, and ride the short route if I go with her twice first."

Ivena looked at Cessa.

"Why this work?"

"Pays."

Arlo asked, "Clay?"

"No."

"Brass?"

"No."

"Filters?"

"We have one at the dye house."

"Do you know what it does?"

Cessa thought.

"Makes the ditch less blue."

Arlo looked wounded.

Dena looked delighted.

"Hire her," I said.

Ivena ignored me because we had not reached numbers.

Dena proposed three mornings per week plus route-day pay if used outside the shop. A helper, not a full employee or apprentice.

The service sheet supported it. Dena had spent enough time washing and hauling that she was losing route hours to tasks somebody cheaper could learn.

Later businesses would call this leverage and produce a chart.

Current Dena called it, "I am tired of smelling like tannery water before breakfast."

We approved a four-week trial.

Cessa started immediately because apparently the interview had occurred after Dena had already taught her where the brushes were.

I respected that cautiously.

By midday Cessa had mislabeled one return rack.

Dena caught it before the wrong chamber moved.

"Customer mark first," Dena said.

"I did."

"You read it backward."

"Still read it."

"Do it forward."

At lunch Cessa ate twice what I did and asked if owners always stood around this much.

"Yes," Dena said.

"No," Ivena said.

Arlo said, "We're working."

Cessa looked at the clean racks.

"Okay."

After lunch she carried another chamber back to the shelf without touching the collar.
