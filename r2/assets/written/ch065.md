# Year Chapter 23: The Builder

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D214 / AUTUMN`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 13`

`LOCATION: Carrow workshop / training yard`

`RANK: Silver`

---

Thirteen days turned a dead glassmaw into three invoices and one very good idea.

The invoices came first.

Pell charged five copper for cutting one narrow strip from the shoulder plate without shattering the mana-sink crystal.

Bell Tile charged two for kiln heat even though I owned part of the business and Arlo argued ownership should make it free.

Ivena said ownership was why I should pay.

"Otherwise every owner becomes theft department."

"That sentence is hostile to entrepreneurship."

"Good."

Everyone.

The good idea took longer.

The Blackglass Anchor had one weakness I could feel every time I used it hard.

Not capacity exactly.

Feedback.

When load shifted fast, the Anchor distributed force between its connected points, but some of that relationship traveled through my control line and into my body because I was still using myself as the easiest adjustable anchor.

This was why shoulders hurt.

Why wrists cramped.

Why one glasshorn hit had numbed my arm.

Pell hated this design.

"You are part of machine because lazy."

"Adaptable."

"Meat is bad bearing."

"Memorable sentence."

"Write."

I did not.

The glassmaw plate scattered mana instead of carrying it cleanly.

A narrow strip between Anchor control eye and harness might bleed violent feedback into harmless distortion while preserving enough signal for me to steer.

Maybe.

Pell made me say maybe before every test.

D202 through D213 became iteration rather than adventure.

That itself made time feel better.

I still worked.

Two Silver day contracts.

One warehouse ward check.

One escort where nothing happened except rain and a drunk merchant who tried to sing until everyone threatened him.

I trained with Callen and now beat him more often than not.

He stopped commenting on it.

That was better evidence than praise.

Jorren got a pay raise at Meln's factor yard and spent part of it on a proper desk coat he immediately stained with ink.

Noll fired Peto.

That surprised me.

"Why?"

"Late six."

"Times?"

"Days."

"Oh."

"And steals onions."

Tavin would have respected.

Noll had become someone who could decide another person no longer worked there.

He did not enjoy it.

That mattered too.

Mara's packet on D210 contained one sentence I reread too much:

**East load confirmed. D248 arrival if river behaves. D249 if it acts like river.**

So Mara might be in Carrow in thirty-five days.

I did not write it on wall.

Only calendar.

Progress.

On D214 Pell had prototype ready.

We tested at Bell Tile loading frame because no one wanted me experimenting in Lower Rook after the spoon incident became company folklore.

The new assembly looked disappointing.

Good gear often did.

Blackglass Anchor in its spring-steel cradle.

Three primary load eyes.

A fourth smaller control eye connected through a two-inch glassmaw crystal strip housed between leather washers and copper mesh.

Pell called it a **dampener**.

I called it **feedback brake**.

She said mine sounded like children's toy.

Hers won provisionally.

Dena loaded test frame with two hundred pounds stone.

"First static."

I clipped left and right Anchor eyes to frame.

Control through dampener to harness.

Opened mana.

The difference was immediate and terrible.

I could feel less.

"I hate it."

Pell smiled.

"Good."

"Stop."

"Why hate?"

"Everything is dull."

"Meaning it works."

Maybe.

The Anchor still distributed load.

I could sense direction but not every sharp edge of tension.

Like wearing gloves.

Safer.

Less precise.

We tested lateral shove.

Dena used lever on hanging load.

Anchor shifted force.

Normally my shoulder would twitch at sudden change.

Now only pressure, softened.

"Better," I admitted.

Pell looked smug.

"Again."

We increased.

Three hundred pounds.

Four.

At five hundred, dampener strip began to glow faint gray and warmed.

Not blackglass silver light.

Different.

Pell stopped.

"Heat limit."

"Can larger strip?"

"Yes, but then less feel."

Tradeoff.

Good.

Magic gear should not only become better.

It should become choices.

"Can bypass?"

She pointed at latch.

"Direct control here."

I could switch dampened or raw connection manually.

That felt important.

Normal work safer through dampener.

Fine control emergency maybe direct.

Dangerous but chosen.

"I like."

"You paid for it."

"How much?"

She named twelve copper materials and seven labor.

"Nineteen?"

"Silver makes more."

Everyone had become Maud.

I paid.

Then Callen arrived with padded sword because I had asked him.

Pell looked at us.

"No."

"Need movement test."

"Not in workshop."

So training yard.

The second half of D214 became ridiculous enough to work.

We mounted Anchor harness under practice coat.

Two short retractable lines clipped to belt-side rings connected to fixed yard posts? That would tether me, useless for combat. Instead we tested self-contained use with one Anchor line to shield-sized weighted sled and another to waist control, simulating dynamic support.

Callen attacked while I had to move a sixty-pound sled along marked lane without letting it cross boundary.

"Why?" he asked.

"Because fights do not pause so I can become engineer."

"Fair."

First run was awful.

I watched sled too much.

Callen hit shoulder three times.

Second run I ignored sled too much.

It crossed line.

Third I used Anchor to feel gross direction through dampener, not precise load.

That worked better.

Callen came high.

I parried.

Sled drifted left.

Small force correction through Anchor.

No shoulder spike.

Callen feinted low.

I stepped around, cut ribs, and shifted sled simultaneously.

Something clicked.

Not power increase.

Integration.

Magic stopped being separate demonstration.

It became another hand occupied by part of brain.

Old Greg had lived that way eventually.

Current Greg was finally beginning.

We ran until sweat soaked shirt.

Then Pell, watching from fence, threw a small sandbag at me without warning.

I caught.

"What?"

"Dynamic load."

"You could say."

"Then not dynamic."

She threw second.

I blocked with force plane reflexively.

Anchor not connected to external anchors, so no distribution benefit.

The bag bounced into Callen's face.

He swore.

Pell laughed so hard she had to sit.

No one learned anything except friendship maybe.

Later, Lera saw the glassmaw strip and asked what it did.

I explained.

She made me practice sword without Anchor for thirty minutes.

"Why?"

"Because gear breaks."

Simple.

That mattered more now that build was becoming specialized.

I did not want to become man whose competence disappeared when one artifact cracked.

So we trained raw.

My sword remained strong.

My force without Anchor remained smaller but usable.

Good baseline.

At dusk I walked home with new harness under coat.

The city felt colder than two weeks ago.

Autumn proper.

Leaves collected in drains.

Market sold squash instead of peaches.

I passed a gear shop displaying winter crampons in window.

Westreach.

D274 in sixty days.

D326 departure in one hundred twelve.

I had Silver now.

I had a growing magical build.

I had money enough to buy upgrades.

And suddenly the remaining requirements looked less like impossible wall and more like expensive list.

That was dangerous because achievable goals invite overconfidence.

At Lower Rook, Noll held a letter.

"Tavin."

Finally.

I opened before taking boots off.

**Alive. Sorry. South route ate month. Lost one wagon to axle, no people. Heard Silver. Hate this for everyone.**

Then:

**Carrow maybe D244. If work holds. Don't make dinner dramatic.**

Mara maybe D248 or D249.

Tavin maybe D244.

Four days apart.

My current people might occupy same city again after months.

I smiled.

Noll looked.

"What?"

"Autumn getting crowded."

He shrugged.

"Buy food."

Practical planning.
