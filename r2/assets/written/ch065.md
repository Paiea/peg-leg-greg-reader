# Chapter 65: The Anchor

Neris did not like my brace ring.

This made me like the test more.

We were in a borrowed storage yard behind East Annex with one sandbag, three iron eyes, two ropes, and no reason to impress anyone.

I had asked for twenty minutes, not a lesson. She had charged me four copper anyway.

Professional boundaries.

"Old pattern," she said, turning the ring over.

"Yes."

"Bad latch."

"Repaired."

"Still old pattern."

"That's why I bought it."

She looked at me.

"Later?"

"Later."

Neris sighed.

We set three anchors at ugly angles around the sandbag and joined the cords through the ring.

The hardware was not magical. It gave me a physical geometry to work against.

I formed a five-inch plane against the ring's open face, somewhere stable that was not aligned with any one anchor.

Neris pulled one rope.

The load shifted and the ring rotated. My plane held position relative to the face without me rebuilding the anchor relationship in my head.

That was the useful part. Not more power, just less mental housekeeping.

"Again," Neris said.

The second pull was harder.

The plane failed and pressure bloomed behind my eyes.

Neris dropped the rope immediately.

"Why?"

I released.

"Load."

"More specifically."

"I treated stable geometry as stable demand."

"Good."

Annoying word.

The ring had made one variable easier. It had not removed the others.

We tested six times.

By the end, I knew the old hardware could reduce setup complexity under awkward anchors. It could not create reserve, save a bad objective, or tell me when to stop.

Neris handed it back.

"Useful."

High praise.

"Worth eight copper?"

"Ask after it saves more than eight copper."

Accounting magic.

I put the ring away and went home with exactly the same number of field assignments as before.
