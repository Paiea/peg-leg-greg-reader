# Chapter 65: The Anchor

Neris did not like my brace ring.

This made me like the test more.

We were in a borrowed storage yard behind East Annex with one sandbag, three iron eyes, two ropes, and no reason to impress anyone.

I had asked for twenty minutes, not a lesson.

She had charged me four copper anyway.

Professional boundaries.

"Old pattern," she said, turning the ring over.

"Yes."

"Bad latch."

"Repaired."

"Still old pattern."

"That's why I bought it."

She looked at me.

"Later?"

"Later."

Neris sighed.

We set three anchors at ugly angles around the sandbag.

The ring joined the cords.

Not magical by itself.

Physical geometry.

I formed a plane against the ring's open face.

Small.

Five inches.

The ring gave me somewhere stable to put it that was not aligned with any one anchor.

Interesting.

Neris pulled one rope.

Load shifted.

The ring rotated.

My plane held position relative to the face without me rebuilding the anchor relationship in my head.

There.

Not more power.

Less mental housekeeping.

Useful.

"Again," Neris said.

Second pull harder.

The plane failed.

Head pressure bloomed behind my eyes.

Neris dropped the rope immediately.

"Why?"

I released.

"Load."

"More specifically."

"I treated stable geometry as stable demand."

"Good."

Annoying word.

The ring had made one variable easier.

That did not remove the others.

We tested six times.

By the end I knew one thing.

The old hardware could reduce setup complexity under awkward anchors.

It could not create reserve.

It could not save a bad objective.

It could not tell me when to stop.

Neris handed it back.

"Useful."

High praise.

"Worth eight copper?"

"Ask after it saves more than eight copper."

Accounting magic.

I put it away.

No field assignment arrived to justify it...
