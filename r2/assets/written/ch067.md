# Chapter 67: The Audit

Mara arrived in Carrow before I knew she was coming.

This was how I learned Vey Shipping had become more important than my correspondence schedule.

A boy from the Vey office found me at East Annex.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

He handed me a folded note.

Mara's handwriting.

**Here through tomorrow. Audit. Working until late. If you are free after sixth bell, east office. Do not make this weird.**

Excellent opening.

I read it twice anyway.

No packet warning.

No romantic journey.

Her company had sent her here because route accounts touched Carrow and someone had decided current Mara could inspect them.

The world moved correctly.

I worked a short Guild closeout that afternoon and reached Vey's east office after sixth bell.

Mara was still working.

Of course.

She sat at a borrowed desk with three ledgers open and ink on one finger.

She looked up.

For a second neither of us moved.

Letters had dimensions badly.

Then she stood.

I hugged her.

She hugged back hard enough to make the pause embarrassing in retrospect.

"You got taller," she said.

"No."

"Then I forgot."

"Rude."

She touched my coat sleeve where an old practice bruise had yellowed beneath cloth she could not see.

"You look different."

"That one might be true."

Mara looked tired.

Better clothes than before.

Not expensive.

Office practical.

A Vey badge pinned at the collar.

Current person accumulating.

"How long?" I asked.

"Tomorrow afternoon if these numbers stop lying."

"Numbers usually need help."

"I know. I taught them."

We ate noodles from a stall because she refused to leave the ledgers long enough for real dinner.

I sat on a crate beside her desk.

Not glamorous.

Good.

She asked about the fire.

In person the questions were shorter.

"Cause?"

"Still unknown."

"Roof?"

"Destroyed west. Operations fine."

"You?"

"Fine."

She looked at me until I added, "Mostly."

Better.

Then she went back to work.

I watched current Mara argue with a freight number for twenty minutes.

She had not come to Carrow for me.

I was glad she was here anyway...
