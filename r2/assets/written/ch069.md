# Chapter 69: The Departure

Mara left Carrow before lunch.

Vey had a cart north-west and no interest in our emotional pacing.

We walked to the depot together.

Her bag was heavier because audit copies had multiplied overnight.

"Your company hates trees," I said.

"Trees started it."

She adjusted the strap.

At the depot three drivers were already arguing over wheel grease.

Mara became work-shaped as soon as she crossed the yard. People asked her questions; she answered two, redirected one, and admitted she did not know another.

Before boarding she said, "I can ask about field freight work if you ever want west."

It was not quite an offer. More like a door she could open for me.

"Don't," I said.

Mara blinked.

I kept going.

"Not because no. Because I don't want you turning your job into my route."

Something in her face eased, maybe relief.

"Okay."

"If Vey posts something I fit, tell me."

"That's different."

"Yes."

She nodded.

The driver called her.

Mara hugged me once.

"Write."

"You too."

Then she climbed into the cart.

I watched until the yard gate swallowed them.

Afterward I went to the Guild and took a one-day storeroom escort because rent did not care that Mara had left.
