# Year Chapter 27: Gold Work

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D260 / AUTUMN`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 11`

`LOCATION: East floodworks / river district`

`RANK: Silver`

---

Mara stayed five days.

Not eleven.

That distinction mattered.

She left Carrow on D254 with Vey's east load, two new jokes stolen from Noll, one argument unresolved about whether I should sell the six-eye coupler, and a promise to write after reaching Halden.

I did not ask when she would visit again.

Not because I stopped wanting.

Because there was no answer yet.

Tavin remained east. Jorren's new room acquired a second shelf. Noll promoted Sia from runner to prep assistant after she corrected his onion cut in front of the head cook and survived.

My own autumn became colder.

The first frost arrived D257. I bought a heavier cloak D258.

Thirty-four copper.

Silver earnings disappeared into clothing at offensive speed.

D260 began with city sirens from the east floodworks.

Not bells.

Three brass horns along the river wall, low enough to vibrate windows.

I was at Bell Tile with Pell, trying to fit the six-eye coupler into a test frame without connecting it to anything live.

Dena looked toward the street.

"Flood."

Pell said, "Not our flood."

Then a runner came through the door.

"Guild wants Greg. East works. Specialist call."

Pell looked at me.

"Apparently your flood."

I grabbed the Anchor harness.

East floodworks protected the lower warehouse district where an old river channel met the modern stone gate system. Autumn rain had raised the water for four days. One sluice gate had failed halfway closed that morning.

That alone was manageable.

Then old blackglass reinforcement beneath the gate started carrying unexpected load into the adjacent wall.

City engineers recognized the material family and requested me by name.

That part landed harder than the horns.

At East Works, chaos looked organized from far away.

Workers stacked sandbags. Teams hauled rope. Two pumps ran. One crane had been repositioned over the sluice channel.

Vaska stood on the wall shouting at three people. Harl pointed at a crack spreading through the lower gatehouse.

And four Gold tabs stood beside them.

Not at dinner. Not training.

Working.

The Gold team had arrived the previous night from a south contract and been redirected because the floodworks carried city-scale risk.

Lead was **Aren Doss**, narrow, dark reinforced coat, short polearm folded across her back.

Heavy was **Bram**, broad, shaved head, two rectangular shields with copper sacrificial rims.

Mage was **Iri**, older, six glass rods in a fan case at his hip.

Fourth was **Kesa**, scout-rigger, small and quick, coil harness across her chest, hooked knife at her side.

They did not look legendary.

They looked busy.

Aren saw my Silver tab.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

"Blackglass specialist?"

"Specialist is generous."

Vaska shouted from behind, "He owns one and asks too many questions."

Aren nodded.

"Enough today."

She pointed at the cracked gatehouse plan.

"Local authority is Vaska. Structural is Harl. Water operations is Nema. I own Guild hazard response only. If any of them says stop, we stop."

I liked them immediately.

The problem was simple enough to say and ugly enough to matter.

Gate Three was stuck forty percent closed. River pressure was rising. One fixed blackglass regulator beneath the gate had failed, and the network was pushing extra load into the Gate Two wall.

Gate Two still worked.

If the wall cracked fully, both could fail.

Warehouses downstream would flood.

Hundreds of workers were already evacuating.

Harl showed me the exposed maintenance pit.

The blackglass node was larger than my Anchor and cracked through one arm.

"Can your piece bypass?"

I crouched.

Maybe.

The six-eye coupler would have been better, but it was at Bell Tile and untested. My three-eye Anchor could bridge enough of the failed arm to unload it temporarily, if the external lines held.

Aren asked, "For how long?"

"Don't know."

"Estimate."

"Minutes under full gate load. Longer if we reduce water pressure first."

Nema, the water operator, said, "Gate One can open another twelve percent without downstream spill."

Aren nodded. "Do that first."

Teams moved.

Up close, Gold did not look like silent perfection.

It sounded like fast questions.

Aren asked Bram whether his shield copper could ground stray discharge.

"Once. Maybe twice."

Iri disagreed. Blackglass was not electrical enough for the shield ground to matter.

Bram said he'd rather waste a strip than an arm.

Aren told him to keep one shield ungrounded.

Fifteen seconds.

Decision made.

Kesa inspected the maintenance pit and refused the first anchor ring because of corrosion.

Harl agreed.

They used his wall bolt instead.

Gate One opened. Pressure on Three eased slightly.

The crack stopped growing.

Temporary window.

My job was narrow: bridge the failed node long enough for the crane and gate crew to raise Gate Three six inches and clear whatever was jammed under the track.

Six inches.

Enough.

We rigged the Anchor.

One line to the sound node arm. Second to the maintenance frame Harl approved. Third through the glassmaw dampener to my harness control.

Aren looked at the connection.

"You need body control?"

"For steering."

"Can someone else take it?"

"Not yet."

"Then Bram behind him."

I almost objected.

Did not.

Bram stood one step back, not touching.

If I collapsed or the harness failed, he owned the part where my body hit stone.

Unpleasant and smart.

I opened the Anchor.

The gate load arrived all at once.

Huge compared with previous jobs.

The blackglass network beneath the wall felt like a waking animal.

Not alive.

Just scale.

Pressure across multiple gates. Water against stone. One failing regulator forcing everything sideways.

My Anchor caught a fraction and spread it.

The dampener strip heated immediately.

"Hot," I said.

Iri held a rod near it without touching.

"Stable rise."

Aren said, "Crane now."

The crane team took the gate weight.

Winch turned.

One inch.

Two.

Water roared through the larger gap.

Something under the track shifted.

Then the node cracked louder.

My Anchor line snapped tight. Shoulder pressure climbed.

Bram said, "You're leaning."

"I know."

"Then stop."

He put one shield edge against my back, not to carry magic, just body.

Humiliatingly useful.

Three inches.

Four.

Iri said, "Dampener near limit."

I could not feel the rise cleanly through the dampener.

So I trusted him.

Five.

Then the debris came free.

A broken timber beam shot through the gate opening and slammed the downstream wall.

The gate jumped.

Load spiked.

The glassmaw strip flashed white and split.

Suddenly the Anchor was direct.

Force hit my body hard enough to narrow vision.

Aren shouted, "Greg?"

"Still!"

Seconds.

Not minutes.

"Six!" Nema yelled.

Aren did not ask for seven.

"Release!"

I did.

The Anchor went dark.

Bram caught me under the arms because my knees had stopped participating.

The crane held the gate mechanically. Workers shoved a replacement track wedge into place. Gate Three lowered three inches, then raised again.

No blackglass assist needed once the debris was gone.

The Gold team did not congratulate me.

They moved to the wall crack.

I sat against stone until my hands stopped shaking.

Iri crouched beside the Anchor.

"Dampener sacrificed."

The glassmaw strip had split through the center.

"Fuck."

Nine-silver monster plate reduced to two cracked inches.

Iri said, "It did its job."

"Expensive job."

"Cheaper than spine."

I nodded.

Aren returned.

"Can you work without dampener?"

"Yes. Smaller."

"Today?"

I moved my shoulders.

Painful.

"Not smart."

"Then don't."

I spent the next two hours doing nonmagical work.

Carrying tools. Holding plans dry. Running messages between Vaska and Harl.

At one point I caught myself embarrassed because the Golds had seen me reduced to clipboard.

Then Kesa handed me a rope coil.

"If you can walk, you're useful."

That helped.

The second crisis came at fourth bell.

The gatehouse crack spread despite the cleared track because the failed blackglass node was still loaded by Gate Two.

The city needed Gate Two shut temporarily.

That would raise upstream water and might flood the mill channel.

Nema objected. Harl insisted on the wall first. Vaska sided with Harl.

Aren asked timing.

Three minutes, maybe.

Kesa pointed out downstream workers still inside the gatehouse.

Evacuate first.

They argued fast and out loud.

Bram wanted shields at the worker exit. Iri wanted rods on the crack before closure. Kesa wanted rope across the maintenance pit.

Aren took all three, assigned the order, and moved.

Gate Two closed.

Water rose.

The wall crack groaned.

Iri's rods lit blue. Bram braced one shield under a falling slab, not because he could hold a building, but because that slab threatened the exit. Kesa got the last worker across. Harl drove a temporary steel dog across the crack.

Nema reopened Gate Two at two minutes twenty.

No collapse.

I watched from outside because my magic was benched.

That gave me a better view than being useful would have.

At close, Aren signed my city sheet.

`SILVER SPECIALIST. BLACKGLASS BYPASS HELD GATE LOAD LONG ENOUGH FOR MECHANICAL CLEARANCE. SELF-LIMIT RECOGNIZED AFTER FEEDBACK FAILURE.`

Then she asked, "Westreach?"

I stared.

"How know?"

"Ressa Holt asked if we'd see you."

Sponsor had talked.

"Yes."

"Role?"

"Load and ward specialist, maybe."

"Maybe dies D274. Pick a noun."

Fourteen days.

"Field load specialist."

"Better."

"Would you take me?"

I asked before pride stopped me.

Aren thought.

"Today, yes, under lead. Not as sole structural expert. Your gear is clever and immature. Your sword is better than your magic. You know enough to stop when told, usually."

"Usually."

"That's what I said."

A Gold leader had just said *useful now* without saying those words.

I walked home with the cracked dampener in my pocket.

At Lower Rook, Mara's packet waited.

**Back Halden. Vey alive. I miss your stupid room.**

Then:

**Do not get eaten before winter.**

I touched the Silver tab once, then the cracked glassmaw strip.

Fourteen days to candidacy.

At least now I knew what noun to put on the form...
