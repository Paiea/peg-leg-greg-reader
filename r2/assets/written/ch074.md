# Chapter 74: The Old Road

The east road remembered more versions of itself than I did.

We left Carrow under clear weather and reached the first old marker before noon.

It stood twenty yards from the current road in a thicket of winter-dead thorn.

Lin Varo climbed through with a measuring line. Harl followed. I stayed where I could see both road and brush.

Pate refused the thorns entirely, making him the smartest member of the party.

"Marker says East District," Lin called.

Harl checked his sheet.

"Current ledger says Carrow."

"Stone older."

"Stone can be wrong longer."

Administrative geology.

Later memory told me this whole stretch would eventually become a straight raised road.

The current road wandered around wet ground and one farmhouse that did not exist later.

I looked at it too long.

An old man split kindling beside the door. Whatever happened to the road later, the house was still current enough for him to need firewood.

Harl noticed.

"Remember something?"

"Different road later."

"Useful?"

I checked the actual marker.

"Not yet."

We walked.

The survey became repetition with changing mud: marker, distance, drain, bridge plate, ownership note.

At dusk we stayed in a six-room inn where the owner charged Pate more than me because Pate required hay and had worse manners.

I considered appealing.

Did not.

The road east was not adventure yet.

It was the expensive act of finding out whose road it was.
