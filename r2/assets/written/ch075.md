# Chapter 75: The Orchard

The orchard path closed because the pond was awake.

That was Lin's wording.

I assumed metaphor until we reached it.

A long irrigation pond sat below bare pear trees, thin ice covering most of the surface.

The road crossed its outlet on a low stone causeway.

A woman in green wool had chained the causeway shut.

Harl stopped.

"Road office."

"I know," she said.

"Why closed?"

"Pool shifted last night."

I looked at the water.

Nothing moved.

Then the reflection did.

Not the surface.

The reflection.

A line of pale sky bent sideways across still water and returned to normal.

Magic.

Not Barrier.

Not anything Old Greg had used.

The woman saw me looking.

"Don't stare at the middle."

I stopped.

Useful local instruction.

Her name was Esta Mar.

Orchard keeper, water-right holder, and apparently the person whose job included knowing when the pond's place-effect made the causeway unsafe.

"How long?" Harl asked.

"Could settle by evening. Could take two days."

"Alternate?"

"North ridge. Adds three miles. Pate can do it. Cart can't."

Pate looked capable of opinions but remained silent.

Harl did not ask her to prove the pond was dangerous.

He asked what evidence meant reopened.

Good professional.

"Reflection holds to true horizon for one full glass," Esta said. "Then I walk the first ten stones."

No theory.

No school.

Procedure.

Harl looked at our load.

Survey gear.

No cart.

"North ridge," he decided.

Esta unlocked a side gate.

"Stay above the white posts."

We did.

I never learned what the pond was.

That felt correct...
