# Year Chapter 36: Snow Line

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D336 / WINTER`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 10`

`LOCATION: Western road / snow line`

`RANK: Silver`

---

Ten days on Westreach made Carrow feel like something I had imagined.

Not forgotten.

Far.

The expedition changed shape every day.

At D327 we still looked like a city procession. Twenty-eight wagons in careful order. People clean enough to identify coat colors. Animals impatient.

By D330, mud made everyone brown from the knee down.

By D332, one supply wagon had broken a wheel and spread its load across three others.

By D333, two people had turned back with fever under healer escort.

By D334, nobody asked when the next halt was. We stopped when the road allowed.

D335 we reached Harth, the last large settlement before the snow road.

I sent Mara one letter because packet existed.

Not long.

**Harth. Alive. Field Support Three hates oats. Miri hates them professionally. Ten days in and I understand why expedition needs this many socks.**

Then:

**Carrow already feels farther than ten days. I miss you.**

Direct.

I wrote Noll and Jorren on one sheet because paper was expensive west. Tavin would get news through Carrow eventually.

At Harth, Field Support Three resupplied rope, lamp oil, and a crate of bridge spikes.

My personal pack lost four pounds of food because common ration became regular, then gained five pounds of winter line.

Net failure.

The Blackglass Anchor had become ordinary enough that Bera called it `Greg's buckle`.

This offended me deeply.

It had already solved a wagon lean, a snapped tent ridge, and a mule stuck in mud where we rigged a three-point pull and spread the shock across two trees instead of yanking the harness.

The Anchor had not changed much since spring.

I had.

D336 we left Harth before sunrise and crossed the snow line by second bell.

There was no official line. Rain became snow, then the road stopped showing mud and turned white entirely.

Pines thickened. Mountain walls appeared ahead like weather made stone.

I wore the green neck wrap.

Miri said it still ruined camouflage.

"We are twenty-eight wagons."

"Standards."

Our engineering train traveled near the front because road problems needed solving before the food train discovered them.

Field Support Three rotated positions.

That morning Miri scouted ahead with two route riders. Maren stayed at the tool cart. Leth beside the ward wagon. Bera and Kell on the flanks. Senn center. Me rear right.

The first ice bridge came near noon.

Not an ice bridge. An old stone span across a shallow ravine, half buried in snow, one side reinforced with timber from prior expeditions.

Miri signaled stop.

We walked forward.

The stone arch had cracked since the last Westreach. One support face was missing a chunk the size of a chair.

Maren crouched.

"New."

The road captain asked, "Can we cross?"

Maren looked at the wagons behind us.

"People, yes. Loaded carts, maybe not until braced."

So the expedition stopped.

Hundreds of people learned patience at once.

Engineering crews moved.

Field Support One took the upstream bank. Two brought timbers. Three got under the arch because Maren hated us.

We descended the ravine by rope.

Snow sat waist deep in drifts. Water still moved beneath the ice.

The crack ran through the outer arch stone, but the inner core held.

Maren planned two timber braces from ravine bed to outer span, plus a load-sharing line around the parapet while the first light wagons crossed.

My Anchor could help equalize brace load.

Not hold the bridge.

Assist.

We were rigging the first timber when Miri shouted from above.

"Movement north!"

Everyone froze.

Then the horn.

Two short.

Beast alert.

I climbed high enough to see the road.

Something large moved through the uphill pines.

White-gray.

Upright.

Troll.

Actual fucking troll.

Old Greg memory supplied **snow troll**. High-country omnivore. Too smart to treat like an animal. Not civilized enough to trust when hungry.

Current one carried a tree branch as a club.

Eight feet, maybe. Long arms. Thick pale fur crusted with ice.

It looked at the expedition and saw a moving pantry.

Then a second shape appeared behind it.

Two.

The road captain shouted defensive halt. Wagons tightened. Guards moved outward.

Our problem was that Field Support Three was still under the bridge.

Maren said, "Up. Now."

We abandoned the half-rigged timber and climbed.

I was last with Bera because my Anchor lines had to come free.

The first troll came down the slope faster than its body suggested.

Expedition archers loosed.

Three arrows hit fur. One sank into the shoulder. The others glanced from thick hide and bone-heavy ridge.

The troll roared and charged the nearest wagon.

Flour.

Of course.

A Gold field team from the forward guard intercepted the first one. Different team from Aren's, led by a man with a long glaive and a woman casting blue fire.

They moved cleanly.

The second troll went wide toward the engineering carts.

Our problem.

Maren shouted, "Three!"

Field Support Three formed around the tool wagon.

Bera front shield. Kell spear right. Miri bow from the cart. Leth ward screen. Senn behind. Maren left with hammer and short sword.

Me where needed.

The troll hit Leth's first ward with the branch club.

Blue light shattered.

Bera caught the next swing on her shield and went to one knee.

Kell stabbed the thigh.

The troll kicked him aside.

I drew my sword.

Then heard the bridge crack behind us.

One of the panicked mule teams had backed onto the unbraced parapet.

A small supply cart sat half on the bridge, rear wheel close to the crack. The driver screamed for the mule to move forward.

If the span went, the ravine cut the expedition road.

Monster in front.

Bridge behind.

"Maren! Bridge!"

He looked once.

"Greg, bridge! We have troll!"

I hated leaving the fight.

Went anyway.

The rear wheel had loaded the cracked outer span. The stone line widened visibly.

The driver tried forward.

The mule slipped.

"Stop the animal!"

He did.

The cart carried medical crates. Light individually. Maybe eight hundred pounds together.

No time to unload by hand.

Behind me, the troll roared. Metal rang.

I clipped one Anchor line to the sound inner parapet ring, one to a bridge spike the engineering crew had already driven, and kept the third on direct control because the dampener would blur the crack response.

This was bigger than spring work by an order.

The structure carried the load, not my body alone.

I opened.

The bridge became pressure.

Too much on the cracked outer edge.

I redirected a fraction into the inner ring.

Not a fix.

Enough to ease the wheel.

"Forward now!"

The driver pulled the mule.

It slipped again.

I could push the cart while holding the load shift.

Two operations at once.

Spring me could not have done it.

Current me tried.

Small moving plane against the rear axle.

Push.

The Anchor kept the cracked side from taking the full change.

Six inches.

One foot.

The mule found footing.

Pulled.

Rear wheel crossed the crack.

The bridge groaned.

The cart reached sound stone.

I released.

My shoulders hurt but remained mine.

Then the troll screamed differently.

I turned.

Bera had opened its hand. Kell's spear was stuck in its side. Maren was down on one knee. Leth's ward flickered.

I ran back.

The troll raised the club over Maren.

Too far for sword.

I put a raw force plane under the club head.

Could not stop it.

Could move it.

The club hit the plane and shifted left just enough to smash snow instead of Maren's skull.

He rolled.

I reached them.

My sword cut the troll's wrist where Bera had opened the fur.

Deep.

It dropped the club.

The creature swung bare-handed.

I ducked and stabbed under the ribs.

Thick hide. Four inches, maybe.

Not enough.

Its left hand closed around my shoulder.

Huge fingers.

Then my feet left the snow.

Bad.

I stabbed its forearm.

The troll roared but did not release.

Bera shield-bashed the knee. Kell, back on his feet, drove the spear through its calf.

The leg buckled.

I fell with the troll.

Snow saved some dignity.

Miri's arrow hit its eye.

The troll went wild.

I rolled clear.

Maren shouted, "Finish!"

The opening was there.

I stood, took the sword in both hands, and stepped behind the creature while it clawed at the arrow.

First cut at the neck below the skull.

Deep.

The troll turned.

Bera's axe bit into the same seam.

Kell's spear held the body off balance.

I cut again.

Blood went dark across the snow.

The troll collapsed.

Still moved.

Maren drove his short sword into the base of the skull.

Done.

We stood panting.

No time to enjoy it.

The first troll was still fighting the forward guard. The Gold team had it contained, blue fire burning one arm. Within another minute it went down under combined guards.

Two trolls dead.

Kell had bruised ribs. Bera's shield strap had torn and her wrist was sprained. Maren had opened his scalp on ice. One forward guard had a broken arm. One mule had been injured in the panic.

No deaths.

Then we went back to the bridge.

Still cracked.

We spent the next three hours bracing the span.

My shoulders were already tired from the fight and the cart support, so Maren limited Anchor cycles.

"Five minutes, then off."

I obeyed.

Timber braces went in. Field Support One drove a second spike. Two rigged the safety line.

The expedition crossed wagons one at a time with loads redistributed.

Twenty-eight wagons turned the afternoon into a bridge schedule.

Snow kept falling.

Westreach hunters took teeth and tendon samples from the trolls and marked the carcasses for Harth crews to recover later. Too much hide and meat to butcher efficiently in a storm-road column.

I received no loot.

Fine.

The thing I kept was Maren's sentence while we coiled rope.

"You made the right choice."

"Bridge?"

"Yes."

"I wanted the troll."

"I know."

"Then why?"

"Because you went where the crew needed you."

At camp that night, Bera's wrist was wrapped. Kell complained about his ribs. Leth added too much pepper to the stew and got banned from seasoning anything.

Miri told everyone the troll had picked me up like a child.

This became more important than the kill.

"Feet fully off the ground," she said.

"Briefly."

"Dangling."

"I stabbed him."

"Still dangling."

The crew laughed.

I did too.

Outside, snow covered the blood.

Ten expedition days behind us.

Twenty-nine to the Black Stair.

The road was white now, and climbing.

Spring me would have thought the bridge, the Anchor, the cart, and the troll were four different problems.

Tonight I was mostly angry that Miri kept reenacting my feet...
