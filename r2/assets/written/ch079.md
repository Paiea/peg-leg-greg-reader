# Chapter 79: The Return

Eight days became nine because rain found us on the return.

Not dramatic rain.

Persistent rain.

The kind that did not threaten your life and still managed to make every sock feel personally betrayed.

By the time Carrow's North Gate appeared through the dark, my coat had gained three different kinds of wet and Pate looked cleaner than I did.

Mud respected hierarchy.

A gate worker held up a lantern.

"Road office?"

Harl raised the document tube without taking it out from under his coat.

"Returning survey."

The worker looked at Lin, then me, then Pate.

Pate had a strip of dead grass hanging from his mouth.

"Anything to declare?"

Harl looked at the mule.

"Several opinions."

The worker waved us through.

Carrow after nine days felt both familiar and slightly rude.

The North Gate lane had changed again while I was gone. One support marker had moved closer to the curb. A flour cart waited under the arch. Two city laborers were packing something around the base of a post I remembered as already finished.

Apparently finished remained temporary if you waited long enough.

Lin peeled away toward the district records office with the document case hugged under her coat.

"Second bell tomorrow," Harl called after her.

She lifted one hand without turning.

I looked at him.

"Tomorrow?"

"You want your pay?"

"Less than I want dry feet."

"Second bell."

Professional cruelty.

Pate's stable was west of my route, so I left Harl to negotiate whatever mule debrief existed and walked toward Lower Rook.

The market was mostly shuttered. Rain ticked off awnings. Somebody was still frying onions behind one half-closed stall, and the smell followed me for half a street.

Lower Rook's front room was quiet when I came in.

Noll looked up from a table near the stove.

He stared at me for two seconds.

"You smell regional."

"Nine days of professional geography."

"Successful?"

"We found a government marker under pigs."

He nodded slowly.

"Worth leaving for."

"Absolutely."

He pushed a heel of bread toward me.

I ate it standing up.

"Anything happen?" I asked.

Noll thought.

"Maud yelled at a chimney man. Bakery burned a batch of rolls. Jorren came by once. Somebody left you letters."

"Guild?"

"Still there."

"Workshop?"

"Dena came for tea yesterday. Didn't mention fire."

Good enough.

I climbed upstairs.

My room smelled like dust, old wood, and air that had been waiting for me to come back and open a window.

Two letters lay under the door.

One was from Mara.

The other had Guild ink on the fold.

Neither carried an urgent mark.

I put both on the desk unopened.

The bed looked excellent.

So did the floor.

The floor required less effort.

I sat on it to pull off my boots and discovered the left sock had fused emotionally to my foot.

Noll called from the hall.

"Bath water's still warm if you don't care about dignity."

"I stopped caring near Mill Fen."

"Good."

I washed mud out of places that had not participated in the survey.

Then I hung my coat, spread the damp field gear where it could dry without becoming biology, and opened the east window for exactly thirty seconds before the cold convinced me the room's stale air could remain a local tradition.

I slept eleven hours.

At second bell the next morning, Harl looked offended by my face.

"You slept."

"Aggressively."

Lin already had the survey sheets spread across a road-office table.

The work looked different indoors.

Nine days of roads, mud, bad inns, orchard water, pigs, arguments, rain, and Pate's theft had become lines of ink.

Lin tapped the district sketch.

"Three bridge assignments corrected. This one stays East. These two move Carrow maintenance after the old marker position is accepted."

Harl pointed at the farm note.

"Legal review. Access claim predates current road line. We don't decide it."

"Good," I said.

Neither looked at me.

Lin moved to the orchard route sheet.

"Causeway closure procedure added."

I read it.

**CLOSED ON WATER-RIGHT HOLDER NOTICE DURING POOL SHIFT. REOPEN AFTER TRUE HORIZON HOLDS ONE FULL GLASS AND HOLDER CLEARS FIRST TEN STONES.**

No theory.

No attempt to explain what the pond was.

Just the procedure that had gotten us across without anyone discovering whether the road existed where it looked.

That was enough.

Harl signed his section. Lin signed hers.

I signed the field witness line.

The clerk counted nine days of pay instead of eight.

Rain had finally produced something useful.

Outside the office, Harl tucked his copy into the tube.

"East again if they approve the follow-up."

"When?"

"Don't know."

"Good."

He looked at me.

"You learned that answer."

"Expensive training."

We separated at the road office steps.

I went back to Lower Rook with nine days of wages, two unopened letters, and a bag full of laundry that had become more urgent than either.

The map was wider.

The first thing I did with that achievement was wash socks.
