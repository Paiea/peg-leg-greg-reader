# Chapter 80: The Commission

Dena wanted commission, not ownership.

I had been prepared for the wrong argument.

She put three customer names on the bench.

"I brought these in."

Ivena checked.

She had.

One dye shop Dena had met on a service route.

One tannery referred by an existing customer.

One soap maker who had apparently spent ten minutes complaining about ditch fines before Dena realized complaint could become sales.

"You want a raise?" Arlo asked.

"No."

"More route pay?"

"No."

"Then what?"

"If I sign a new service customer, I want one-time commission after first paid month."

There.

Later businesses would build entire compensation systems around this sentence.

Current Dena had built it because she noticed she was making money for owners in conversations nobody assigned.

Ivena asked the important questions.

How much.

What counted as Dena's lead.

What if customer failed after one month.

What if Arlo did the technical sale.

What if existing customer referred them.

Dena had answers to most.

Not all.

We argued for an hour.

Not about whether she deserved money.

About how to define the event.

Eventually we agreed on a small flat commission after first full paid service cycle for customers Dena sourced and logged before owner contact.

No lifetime percentage.

No ownership grant.

No vague promise.

Dena signed the page.

"Good."

Then went back to work.

The business had developed a new way to pay someone because that someone asked before we had invented the policy ourselves...
