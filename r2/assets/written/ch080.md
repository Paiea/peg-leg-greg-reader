# Chapter 80: The Commission

Dena wanted commission, not ownership.

I had been prepared for the wrong argument.

She waited until the morning route was unloaded, then put three customer names on the workbench between Ivena's ledger and Arlo's tea.

"I brought these in."

Ivena turned the page toward herself.

Dena had written the names, first service date, and how each had reached us.

One dye shop she had met while swapping a chamber on the south route.

One tannery referred by an existing customer.

One soap maker who had apparently spent ten minutes complaining about ditch fines before Dena realized complaint could become sales.

Arlo picked up the list.

"You want a raise?"

"No."

"More route pay?"

"No."

He looked at me.

I lifted both hands.

"She brought the problem."

Dena took the paper back from him.

"If I sign a new service customer, I want a one-time commission after their first paid month."

Arlo blinked.

Ivena did not.

That should have warned us which conversation we were actually in.

"How much?" Ivena asked.

Dena named a number.

Arlo made a noise.

"That's almost a route day."

"Yes."

"For talking?"

Dena looked at him.

"You can do the talking."

I looked down so nobody saw me enjoy that.

Ivena tapped the first customer.

"This dye shop. You met them on paid service time."

"Yes."

"Existing route put you in the room. Why is that your lead instead of ours?"

Dena considered instead of answering quickly.

"Because I asked what they were dumping. They weren't on our list. Nobody sent me to sell them anything."

"Fine. Tannery?"

"Rusk dye house gave them my name."

"Existing customer referral."

"To me."

"Because you service Rusk."

"Yes."

Ivena made a small mark beside the name.

"Not clean."

Dena leaned one hip against the wash trough.

"Then make a rule."

There was the actual request.

Not money first.

Definition first.

Cessa carried a rinsed chamber past us and stopped just long enough to realize adults were arguing about pay.

Dena pointed toward the rack.

"Forward labels."

Cessa kept walking.

Arlo had reached the soap maker.

"This one isn't even active yet."

"Starts next week if you stop changing the mounting bracket."

"I improved it."

"You changed it."

"Those can be the same thing."

Ivena said, "Not for commission accounting."

Arlo sat down.

We stayed there longer than I expected.

The easy version would have been to give Dena something and congratulate ourselves for being generous.

The harder version was deciding what event we were paying for.

If Dena heard a complaint on a service route, did that count?

If an existing customer handed her a cousin's name, did that count?

What if Dena opened the conversation and Arlo spent two hours doing the technical sale?

What if the new customer paid one month and vanished?

What if they signed a service agreement but never paid at all?

"No payment before payment," Ivena said.

Dena nodded.

"Fine."

"One-time only."

"Yes."

"No percentage of later service."

"I didn't ask for one."

Arlo looked relieved by something nobody had proposed.

I said, "What proves the lead was hers?"

Dena tapped the service sheet.

"I write it before one of you contacts them."

Ivena looked at the sheet.

Then at Dena.

"Name, source, date."

"Fine."

"If it's already in our customer book, no commission."

"Fine."

"If an existing customer directly asks us for service and happens to speak to you first, no commission."

Dena frowned.

"Depends."

Good.

We had reached the part where everyone understood enough to disagree properly.

The tannery took another fifteen minutes.

Ivena argued that Rusk's referral belonged partly to the existing business relationship.

Dena argued that relationships did not walk themselves across town, inspect a ditch, explain the swap cycle, and get a first service date.

Arlo proposed half commission for referrals.

Ivena asked how we would distinguish a referral from a recommendation from a casual mention.

Arlo stopped proposing things.

Eventually Dena solved it by making the rule smaller.

"If I bring the name in before the customer contacts the shop, it counts. If they contact the shop first, it doesn't."

Ivena sat back.

"That I can record."

So that became the line.

A small flat commission after the first full paid service cycle, for a customer Dena sourced and logged before owner contact.

No lifetime percentage.

No ownership grant.

No promise that every useful conversation qualified.

Just a defined payment for a defined result.

Ivena wrote it cleanly on a fresh sheet.

Dena read every line.

Arlo read half and claimed the rest looked right.

I read it because apparently I owned part of a business now and this was what ownership did to a morning.

Then Dena signed.

"Good," she said.

She picked up the next dirty chamber and went back to the wash trough.

Cessa looked over from the return rack.

"Do I get commission?"

Dena pointed at the label in her hand.

"Read that forward first."

Cessa turned it around.

The rest of us went back to work.
