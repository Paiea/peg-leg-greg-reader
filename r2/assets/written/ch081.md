# Chapter 81: The Moving Plane

I bought another support block from Neris after the first lesson had spent enough time becoming habit.

That was how she phrased it.

"You stopped trying to improve it every morning?"

"Mostly."

"Good. Now ruin that comfort."

We were back in a borrowed yard behind a cooper's storehouse with a hanging sandbag, a timber frame, and enough chalk lines to make the wall look accused of something.

Neris pointed at the center mark.

"Five-inch plane."

I formed it.

Stable.

The edge sat exactly on the chalk.

"Right one inch."

I looked at her.

"Move it?"

"That is what I said."

This was terrible already.

I reached for the construct the way I would have later in life.

The plane vanished.

A new one appeared an inch to the right.

Neris tapped the frame with one finger.

"That was a new plane."

"It ended in the correct place."

"So does walking out of a room and coming back through another door. I said move."

"I noticed."

"Again."

I released and reset.

Five inches. Center line.

I tried to keep the construct active while changing where I meant it to be.

Nothing happened.

I pushed harder with attention.

The plane flickered and disappeared.

Neris did not react.

"Again."

Old Greg could alter support geometry under load. I remembered sliding a surface under a falling beam, changing an angle while someone moved, letting one edge travel while the other stayed fixed.

Memory made the idea feel easy.

Current magic disagreed.

Current Greg liked decisions with edges.

Form.

Hold.

Release.

On or off.

Moving something that already existed required a continuous decision instead of a replacement one.

That sounded simple until I tried to do it with my forehead.

Attempt three collapsed immediately.

Attempt four moved nothing and gave me the strong impression I was about to sneeze through my eyes.

On five, the left edge shifted and the right did not.

The plane twisted.

Neris slapped the sandbag aside before it touched.

"What changed?"

"One edge listened."

"That is not technical language."

"You started it."

She almost smiled.

"Again."

I drank water first.

The yard had gone colder while I was concentrating. Or I had stopped noticing it. My fingers were stiff around the cup.

Neris waited.

That was one of the things I paid her for.

She did not fill silence because I was failing.

Attempt six moved the plane perhaps a quarter inch before I lost it.

Not enough to matter.

Enough to prove there was a difference between impossible and bad.

"Again," Neris said.

"You have other words."

"They cost extra."

Attempt seven vanished while I was still deciding whether it had moved.

On the eighth, I stopped trying to move the whole plane.

Not because Neris told me to.

Because I finally noticed what my attention was doing.

I was holding the plane as one finished object and then asking that finished object to exist somewhere else.

Instead I held the relationship between the plane and the chalk line, then changed the relationship.

Very slightly.

My forehead tightened.

The plane trembled.

One chalk edge blurred.

Neris leaned closer.

"Hold."

I held.

The construct stayed.

She measured with her thumb.

"Half inch."

I released and breathed out.

"That counts."

"It counts as half an inch."

No celebration.

I appreciated her consistency less while tired.

We kept going.

Once I understood the shape of the attention, progress became possible and remained irritatingly small.

Half inch.

Three quarters.

Collapse.

Nothing.

One inch, but the plane tilted enough that Neris refused to count it.

"The object moved."

"The useful object did not."

Fair.

Near the end she hung the smallest training weight from the frame.

"Now hold that and move."

I formed the plane under load.

Stable.

Tried to shift it.

Gone.

Again.

Gone faster.

Third attempt gave me a pulse of pressure behind both eyes before the plane even moved.

Neris raised one hand.

"Stop."

I stopped.

She unhooked the weight.

"Unloaded only."

"I can almost move it loaded."

"No. You can almost fail it loaded in a new way."

That distinction was becoming expensive.

She redrew the center line where my failures had smeared it.

"Practice one inch. No load. Five clean attempts, then stop."

"How long?"

Neris considered me.

"Until it stops feeling like holding your breath with your forehead."

I packed my things.

At the gate I looked back at the chalk frame.

Most of my progress was visible as smears.

One of them was almost an inch wide.
