# Chapter 82: The Handrail

The moving plane first mattered on a staircase.

Not combat.

Better.

Meren had called me to a hotel two blocks west of Market Hall where the second-floor handrail ward had started pulsing whenever somebody leaned on the upper turn.

By the time I arrived, half the brass channel was open and the staircase had been closed with a rope, two chairs, and a handwritten sign large enough to insult literacy.

**STAIRS CLOSED. USE REAR SERVICE STAIR.**

The hotel clerk pointed toward the back hall every time somebody entered.

Meren was crouched at the landing with both hands inside the rail housing.

"You know the objective?"

"Support anything you remove if you call it. Keep people out of the lane."

"Good."

He pointed at the rope.

"That second part should be easy."

This was our first mistake.

The guest appeared above us while Meren had one temporary support unclamped.

She was old enough that the cane looked less like an accessory than a long-term business partner.

She put one foot on the top step.

The hotel clerk looked up.

"Ma'am, the stairs are closed. Rear stair is at the end of the hall."

"I know where the rear stair is."

She took another step.

Meren did not look away from the open ward.

"Stairs closed."

"My room is upstairs."

"Then remain in it."

She kept coming.

The clerk made the helpless face of a person whose authority had been formally presented and personally rejected.

I moved toward the bottom of the stairs.

"The rail is not fully supported."

"I have a cane."

This was not an answer to the problem I had described.

It was apparently the answer she had available.

Meren's left hand held the replacement ceramic link in place. His right was inside the lower clamp.

One temporary support point had already been removed.

The loose section of rail looked normal until someone put weight on it.

The woman reached for it.

"RAIL," Meren said.

I formed a five-inch plane beneath the loose segment.

The construct settled cleanly against the underside.

She descended another step.

I expected her hand to land near the center of my support.

It did not.

She reached higher.

The rail rolled a fraction under her grip.

Load shifted toward the open side.

My plane held, but wrong.

An inch wrong.

Yesterday that would have meant release and rebuild.

There was no time for that without letting the rail move under her hand.

I held the construct and changed its relationship to the wood.

My forehead tightened.

The plane slid.

Not smoothly.

Not far.

One inch.

The rail settled back into her grip.

The woman paused.

I thought she had finally understood what was happening.

Then she said, "Thank you," and continued down.

Of course.

I kept the support under her hand until she reached the section Meren had not opened.

"GONE," he said.

I released.

The woman stepped off the final stair and adjusted her coat.

The hotel clerk stared at her.

"The rear stair is safer."

"It is farther."

She walked toward the lobby.

Nobody had an answer to that strong enough to bring her back.

Meren waited until she was out of earshot.

Then he looked at me.

"That new?"

"One inch."

"You moved the same plane?"

"Yes."

He looked at the rail.

"Useful inch."

I enjoyed that for almost two seconds.

"Release. I need the support lane clear."

Right.

The actual job remained unfinished.

I stepped back while Meren seated the new ceramic link, rebuilt the temporary support, and tested the rail twice under his own weight before reopening the stairs.

The hotel clerk removed the rope.

"Think she'll use the rear stair next time?" I asked.

He looked toward the lobby.

"No."

Probably not.

I left with one inch of magic that had finally mattered to someone who had no idea it was new.
