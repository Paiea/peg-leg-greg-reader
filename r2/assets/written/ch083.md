# Chapter 83: The Returner

Status: **SELECTED R2 WRITTEN PROSE**

---

Forty-seven days after I touched the Black Stair, Carrow's west gate charged us two copper to come home.

I objected.

Not successfully.

"Road maintenance," the clerk said.

"I left on this road."

"Then you've used it twice."

Miri laughed behind me.

I paid.

The Westreach Expedition did not return as one expedition.

That would have been too neat.

Three wagons stayed at the Black Stair with survey crews. Two went south from Harth. One had become parts. Half the freight workers took other contracts before Carrow. A dozen people returned early with injuries or family messages. Gold teams detached wherever someone paid them to become somebody else's emergency.

Field Support Three came back together because Maren said the Guild wanted our equipment ledger intact, and because none of us trusted anyone else to explain why one bridge pin was bent into a shape normally associated with cooking hooks.

Twenty-one wagons entered Carrow that morning.

We had left with twenty-eight.

Still enough to annoy traffic.

People noticed us.

Not everyone.

A vegetable seller barely looked up. A boy ran beside the lead wagon for half a block asking whether we had seen dragons.

We had not.

He was disappointed by the entire western frontier.

One woman pointed at my Blackglass Anchor and asked if it was treasure.

"Expensive rock," I said.

Miri, walking beside the tool cart, said, "He paid too much."

"You weren't there."

"I know you."

The Anchor looked worse than when I bought it.

Better too.

The bronze cage had a fresh plate from Storm Keep, two mismatched bolts from Nine Teeth, a repaired dampener sleeve, one bent eye, and a black scratch across the glass where the Stair's service mount had bitten the housing during wagon twelve.

It worked.

Mostly within limits I now understood.

That was worth more than looking expensive.

My Silver tab was scratched too.

Miri's green scarf had become my green scarf because she kept insisting I wore it wrong and eventually stopped trying to take it back.

My boots were on their second set of soles.

My body knew what forty days of road did after breakfast.

Carrow smelled like river smoke, bread, horse piss, wet stone, and somebody frying onions too early.

Home.

I had expected the word to feel larger.

It felt specific.

Lower Rook was six streets away.

Noll would be at the Brass Spoon until afternoon unless his schedule had changed. Jorren would probably be at Meln's yard, unless his schedule had changed. Maud would charge me for dust, absence, emotional damage, or some category she invented while I was gone.

All three possibilities made my chest hurt pleasantly.

Maren stopped the tool cart outside the Guild annex.

"Unload first."

Of course.

Nobody got to become sentimental while carrying municipal property.

We spent two hours returning ropes, bridge pins, spare ward frames, a cracked hand winch, and three boxes that had left Carrow full of equipment and returned full of equipment that was technically different.

The quartermaster objected to this distinction.

Kell objected to the quartermaster's objection.

Bera found the original list and proved one missing hammer had already been missing before departure.

Leth turned in six pages of blackglass notes nobody had asked for.

Senn stole two apples from the desk bowl while the clerk watched.

Miri returned one route compass and kept the broken brass bell because Maren had stopped pretending it belonged to inventory.

Then, abruptly, Field Support Three stopped being my day.

Bera went to her sister's house. Kell went wherever a man with thirty-nine days of road and no clean laundry went first. Leth disappeared toward Bell Tile with his notes. Senn had grandchildren waiting.

Miri stood beside me outside the Guild.

"Now what?"

I looked west without meaning to.

The road disappeared between buildings. The Black Stair was somewhere beyond mountains, weather, bridges, camps, and one ridiculous amount of oats.

"Home."

"Good."

She punched my shoulder lightly.

"Don't become domestic."

"Impossible."

"You own two chairs."

"Three, technically. One belongs to whoever steals it first."

"Domestic."

She left before I could defend myself.

I walked to Lower Rook alone.

For a year I had wanted roads because they moved.

For forty-seven days I had wanted Carrow because it stayed somewhere while I moved.

Both seemed unfairly reasonable.

Maud was in the front room arguing with a lamp.

Not a person holding a lamp.

The lamp itself.

She had its brass base open on the table and one tiny spring between two fingers.

She looked up when I entered.

My face. Coat. Pack. Scratched Anchor harness.

Then back at the lamp.

"Rent starts today."

My throat tightened.

"Good to see you too."

"You paid through last month."

"I was gone."

"Room wasn't."

She stood after another second and hugged me hard enough that the Anchor cage pressed into my ribs.

Then she pushed me away.

"Dirty."

"Road."

"Bath."

"Eventually."

"Now."

My room had survived.

The second chair sat near the window where Noll always moved it. The old chair still leaned slightly left. The burned seven waited beneath the brass key. Mara's carved bird sat beside the empty place where the Anchor usually lived.

Three letters were stacked on the table.

One from Tavin. One from Mara. One from the Guild, because apparently returning physically did not satisfy paperwork.

I touched none of them for almost a minute.

Instead I set the Anchor on the table.

Four pounds.

The wood creaked in the familiar place.

That was when I knew I was home.

Then Noll came in without knocking.

He wore the blue apron under his coat and carried a paper sack.

He stopped in the doorway.

I stood.

Neither of us said anything useful.

Noll crossed the room and hugged me once.

Short.

Hard.

Then he opened the sack.

"Food."

"I just crossed half the continent."

"You look thin."

"I gained two pounds."

"Bad pounds."

There was no defense against categories he invented himself.

He had changed while I was gone.

Not dramatically.

Hair shorter. A stitched Brass Spoon mark on the apron. A small ring of keys hanging from his belt.

"Keys?"

Noll looked down.

"Close three nights."

"You close the restaurant now?"

"Kitchen. Vessa closes money."

Noll had acquired responsibility while I was watching a mountain become infrastructure.

"Congratulations."

"More work."

"More pay?"

"Yes."

"Then congratulations."

He considered.

"Okay."

We ate pork buns at the table.

I told him about the Black Stair.

Not all of it.

Wagon twelve. The socket. The black steps. The fact that nothing happened when I touched one.

Noll nodded at the correct parts.

"Rock useful?"

"Very."

"Still rock."

Stable relationship.

Jorren arrived before second bell because Noll had sent a runner from the Spoon.

He had a better coat.

I told him immediately.

"Better coat."

"Factor pay."

"Unbearable."

"Silver expedition."

"Fair."

He had been made permanent assistant factor while I was gone.

Not promotion exactly, he explained for nine minutes.

Then he described the increased pay.

Promotion.

We argued.

By third bell my room contained three people, two chairs, one stool borrowed from downstairs, road gear, restaurant food, factor complaints, and enough noise that Maud hit the ceiling with a broom from below.

I had missed this so badly I became angry about it.

Then Jorren pointed at the letters.

"Mara."

"Yes."

"Open."

"You and Noll have same disease."

I opened Mara's first.

It was eight days old.

**Westreach apparently failed to kill you, according to three separate Guild packets and one cook who has never met you. Good.**

Then work.

Sera had moved desks. Emmet had broken another chair. Vey was considering a permanent mixed road-river account desk in Halden because apparently nobody could agree whose numbers were wrong quickly enough.

Mara had applied.

Last line:

**I am in Carrow next Tenday for route books. If your heroic expedition returns after I leave, I will be furious at geography.**

I checked the date.

Today.

Jorren watched my face.

"What?"

I stood.

Noll said, "Bath."

"Later."

Both of them stared.

Right.

I smelled like forty-seven days of road, even after one wash at Harth.

Current romance had standards.

"Bath first," I said.

Noll nodded.

"Growth."

Everyone had stolen Sella's word.

Mara found me before I found her.

I came downstairs with wet hair and a clean shirt and nearly walked into her in the front hall.

She held the same blue cup she had once broken and apparently replaced again.

For one second she looked exactly like a memory I had never had.

Then current.

Tired from work. Ink on one thumb. Different coat than last autumn. Hair pinned with a cheap brass clip.

She stared at me.

Not the Silver tab. Not the Anchor.

Me.

"Hi," she said.

"Hi."

Mara touched the scratch on my cheek from Nine Teeth.

"You look older."

"I am almost one year older."

"Annoying answer."

"Accurate."

Then she hugged me.

No hesitation.

I held her.

A full year had ended at the Black Stair.

The next thing was apparently Maud's hallway, Mara against my chest, and Noll deliberately washing the same bowl behind us three times.

Mara pulled back.

"I leave in two days."

"Of course."

"River stairs. First bell."

"I'll be there."

She raised an eyebrow.

"Not early."

"No promises."

"Greg."

I smiled.

The old D001 paper was still folded inside my pack.

One year from today.

Done.

I did not know yet that Mara was about to hand me another date...
