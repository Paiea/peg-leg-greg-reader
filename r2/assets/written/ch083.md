# Chapter 83: The Scabbard

The sword was fine.

The scabbard was not.

Lera noticed before I did.

We were ten minutes into a paid block when she held out one hand.

"Sword."

I gave it to her.

She drew the blade halfway, stopped, and drew it again more slowly.

The sound was wrong once she made me listen for it.

Not a scrape.

A soft drag near the mouth.

Lera turned the scabbard toward the gray morning light and pressed one thumb inside the throat.

"Replace."

My stomach dropped before my brain caught up.

"Blade?"

"Scabbard."

Relief arrived embarrassingly fast.

She pulled the sword free and wiped the near side with an oiled cloth.

One strip of steel caught the light differently.

"See that?"

I did after she angled it twice.

"Polish?"

"From the lining. Leather's rough here."

She tapped the inside of the scabbard.

"Not blade damage yet. Keep drawing through it and you'll make wear where you don't want wear."

I had spent enough attention on owning a good sword that I had started treating the thing holding it as scenery.

That felt stupid in a more expensive way than usual.

"Can we train?"

Lera looked at the scabbard.

"With practice wood."

"Of course."

She handed my sword back hilt-first.

"And don't put it back in there ten times because you're curious."

I had not planned to.

I had planned to do it once.

Apparently she knew me.

After the lesson I carried the sword wrapped separately and took the scabbard to a leatherworker near East Market.

The shop had belts hanging from ceiling hooks, harness pieces on one wall, and three different smells that all claimed to be leather.

The leatherworker looked at my sword first.

"Blade stays with you."

"Gladly."

Then she examined the scabbard mouth with a narrow bone tool.

"Lining's worn. Throat's loose on one side too."

"Repair or replace?"

She held the scabbard up to the light.

"Body is fine. New inner liner. Reset throat."

"Cost?"

She told me.

More than socks.

Less than sword.

Still irritating.

"How long?"

"Two days. Three if the hide delivery comes late."

A decorative scabbard hung behind her with bright fittings and stamped leather.

I looked at it for half a second too long.

She noticed.

"I can build you a new one."

"Does it work better?"

"It looks better."

"That wasn't the question."

"Then no."

Useful seller.

I ordered the new inner liner and throat repair.

No silver fittings. No new body. No upgraded sword hiding inside a maintenance bill.

She wrote my name on a tag and tied it through the suspension ring.

"Don't store the blade bare."

"I know."

"People who know still do it."

Pell Orst had said almost the same thing about horses and support rings.

Professional pessimism was becoming reassuring.

I bought a cheap temporary blade sleeve from her because knowledge was not a container.

At Lower Rook I set the wrapped sword on the rack and looked at the empty space where the scabbard usually hung.

Noll stopped in the doorway.

"Broken?"

"Almost."

"Expensive?"

"Less than broken."

He nodded.

That was enough sympathy.

Two days later I would have a scabbard again.

For now, the sword was fine because somebody else had noticed the thing around it wearing out first.
