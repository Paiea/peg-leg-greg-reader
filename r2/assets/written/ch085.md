# D014: The Schedule

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Two weeks after Mara left, Noll stopped asking whether he worked at The Brass Spoon.

This was how employment apparently became real.

Not with a contract.

Not with a uniform.

Not even with money, although money had helped.

He came downstairs on Fourday wearing kitchen trousers, found me at the cookroom table, and said, "Move your boots. I work tonight."

That was it.

Job.

I moved my boots.

"Congratulations."

"For what?"

"Employment."

Noll looked at me as if I had congratulated him for weather.

"Been employed."

"You were on trial."

"Got paid."

Strong legal theory.

He opened the cupboard.

Took out bread.

Opened the second cupboard.

Found no cheese.

Looked at me.

"You eat it?"

"No."

"Jorren?"

"Road."

"Then Maud."

"Why would Maud steal your cheese?"

"Rent."

I had no rebuttal.

Noll cut the bread with the good knife and put half on my plate without asking whether I wanted it.

The room had adjusted around his work over the last fourteen days.

His coat hung from the second hook now because the first was too close to the stove smoke.

A folded white cloth sat on the shelf beside his bowl.

He had bought two more pairs of socks.

One was drying over the chair because apparently prosperity remained damp.

Four nights at The Brass Spoon had become five for this week because Domm's sister had delivered a baby and Domm had discovered family obligations could defeat soup.

Noll complained about the extra shift twice.

He had also bought better onions.

Both facts mattered.

I ate the bread.

My right shoulder hurt when I reached for the cup.

Not injury.

Work.

Vell's Doss run had taken nine days instead of six because a wagon ahead of us broke an axle on the north-east rise and created a line of carts long enough to become a temporary village.

We had slept beside the road twice.

I had spent one afternoon helping move sacks from a leaning grain cart while Jorren shouted useful things like "other side" after I was already on the other side.

The pay sat in a cloth purse inside my room.

More than I would have had if I stayed home waiting for letters.

Less than I wanted now that I had it.

Money had learned to create ambition.

"Shoulder," Noll said.

I looked at him.

"What?"

He pointed with the knife.

"You're moving weird."

I moved my arm once.

"Sacks."

"Lose fight?"

"Draw."

"Sacks don't bruise."

"Their friends do."

Noll accepted this because he had lived with cargo before I remembered how.

Someone knocked at the front door downstairs.

Maud shouted from the hall.

Then shouted again, louder, as if the door had refused the first instruction.

A moment later footsteps came toward the cookroom.

Maud appeared holding a folded packet.

"Greg."

My body stood before I did.

Almost.

I caught myself halfway.

Maud noticed.

"You expecting debt?"

"Possibly."

She handed me the packet.

Vey seal.

Halden mark.

Mara's handwriting across the outside.

Noll looked at my face.

Then at the packet.

Then went back to cutting bread with exaggerated disinterest.

Terrible actor.

Maud stayed.

"Anything else?" I asked.

"No."

She stayed.

"Maud."

"Rent tomorrow."

"I know."

"Good."

She left.

Noll waited until her steps disappeared.

"Open it."

"I was going to."

"You held last one six weeks."

There were too many historians in this building.

I broke the Vey seal.

Three sheets.

The first was Mara.

The second was Mara too.

The third was a copy of something official with a small note written sideways in the margin.

I read the note first.

**They gave me two keys. One opens the records room. The other opens nothing anyone can identify. Thought you'd appreciate professional continuity.**

I laughed.

Noll immediately leaned over.

"What?"

"No."

"You laughed."

"Private correspondence."

"In cookroom."

"Still private."

"Bad room for it."

Fair.

I took the letter upstairs.

Noll called after me, "If she says you're stupid, that's public information."

I closed my door.

The room had changed too.

Not dramatically.

The field pack was unpacked again.

My road cloak hung over the chair because Doss mud had resisted one washing and I had lost patience before it lost color.

Two copper coins sat on the table beside Tavin's burned seven.

I had finally put them there on purpose.

Debt preparation.

The Vey slip from departure morning lived beneath my room key.

I pulled it out.

Mara's writing.

HALDEN EAST OFFICE.

STONE STEPS.

TERM CLOSE.

FIRST BELL.

TWO YEARS. DO NOT BE EARLY.

Fourteen days had done nothing to the ink.

Reasonable.

I put it back and read her letter.

She had reached Halden in three days.

The goat had eaten part of a passenger's hat on the second afternoon.

This occupied six lines and one drawing that did not flatter the goat.

Her new room was above a cooper's yard two streets from Vey. The housing allowance did not begin for another ten weeks, so she had taken the cheapest room whose ceiling did not leak directly over the bed.

The east desk had five people.

Mara liked two.

Disliked one.

Had not decided about the remaining two because, in her words, **judgment before payroll is how idiots create enemies.**

I read that twice.

Not because it was profound.

Because I could hear her saying it.

The official sheet was her term appointment.

Not the whole contract.

A copy of the first page because Mara apparently understood I would check the date.

She had circled it.

Two years.

Same morning.

The ridiculous promise remained attached to bureaucracy.

Good.

Her second page was mostly ordinary.

Cold rain.

Bad coffee.

A warehouse clerk named Len who hummed while adding and stopped only when the numbers became difficult.

Her mother had written before Mara reached Halden because mothers had methods faster than commercial freight.

Tavin was due north again by month's end.

Then, near the bottom:

**You asked why I wanted the job. I gave you the work answer. There is another one. I like being somewhere no one remembers me at sixteen. Halden only knows what I do now. I didn't realize how much I wanted that until I came back to Carrow and everyone still knew which window I climbed out of.**

I sat on the bed.

Read it again.

That was not about me.

Some part of me tried to make it about me anyway.

I told that part to shut up.

It objected.

Poorly.

Mara continued:

**This does not mean I hate Carrow. It means I like finding out who I am somewhere else. You should try it without moving permanently or becoming unbearable.**

There.

Back to familiar ground.

The final line said:

**Fourteen days gone. You have 716 left. Don't start packing.**

I stared.

Then laughed hard enough that someone knocked on the wall.

Noll.

I knocked back once.

He knocked twice.

Hostile communication system.

I found paper.

Not good paper.

Good paper encouraged performance.

I wrote:

**Mara,**

Then stopped.

The road had given me nine days of things to say.

Jorren stepping into mud up to the same boot again and accusing the road of favoritism.

Vell discovering one grain sack had been packed wet and making the seller replace three because he distrusted the entire row after one betrayal.

The north-east rise.

Cold ground.

A wheel sinking so deep six of us had to unload the back half of the cart before two mules could pull it free.

My shoulder.

The little satisfaction of being useful without being the center of the job.

Noll's schedule.

Maud's cheese theft theory.

Two copper waiting for Tavin.

I wrote those.

Not all elegantly.

Good.

Then:

**I like that Halden does not know you at sixteen. I am beginning to suspect I don't either. That may be useful for both of us.**

I crossed out **useful**.

Wrote:

**That may be fairer for both of us.**

Worse.

Crossed that out too.

Eventually:

**I like that you get to be new there.**

Simple enough to survive.

At the bottom I wrote:

**Doss paid well enough that I am considering another road before my shoulder finishes complaining. This is either career development or evidence against me.**

Then:

**716.**

I looked at the number.

Too much.

I crossed it out.

Mara already knew.

I folded the letter.

Downstairs, Noll had found cheese.

"Maud?" I asked.

"Second shelf."

"So you accused our landlord falsely."

"Could've moved it."

"Why?"

"Rent."

Perfect system.

I sat.

Noll looked at the folded letter in my hand.

"She call you stupid?"

"Not directly."

"Saving ink."

I ate cheese.

The Brass Spoon clock down the street struck fifth bell faintly through the window.

Noll stood immediately.

Not because anyone told him.

Because he knew his schedule now.

He wrapped the remaining bread, checked the white cloth on the shelf, and reached for his coat.

"You going road again?" he asked.

"Maybe."

"With Jorren?"

"If he comes back before someone else hires him."

Noll nodded.

No concern speech.

No warning.

He had a shift.

I had a letter to send.

Jorren had a road somewhere east.

Mara had 716 days and five coworkers in Halden.

The city had managed two weeks without waiting for any of us.

I put on my coat and followed Noll downstairs.
