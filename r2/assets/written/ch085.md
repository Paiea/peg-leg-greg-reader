# D014: The Worker

Status: **SELECTED R2 WRITTEN PROSE**

---

Two weeks after Mara left, Noll acquired another key.

This was how responsibility announced itself in Lower Rook.

Not with a ceremony.

Not with a raise letter.

A brass key appeared on the ring at his belt beside the kitchen door key and the dry-store key.

I noticed at breakfast.

"New?"

Noll looked down.

"Back room."

"The Brass Spoon trusted you with inventory?"

"They trust lock."

"Less flattering."

"More accurate."

He cut bread with the good knife and pushed half toward me.

The Spoon had changed his schedule again.

Three closes became four. Two mornings he opened prep before Vessa arrived. Sia now took the onion station without Noll hovering over every cut, which he described as management and she described as finally being left alone.

Noll complained about every extra responsibility.

He also polished the keys.

Both facts mattered.

My own schedule had become irritatingly empty after Westreach.

For a year there had always been a next date.

D274.

D304.

D326.

Black Stair.

Then home.

Then Mara left with a new two-year term and gave me a date so far away it was useless for deciding what to do after breakfast.

Good.

Probably.

The Guild tried to solve this by offering me work.

A surprising institutional strength.

Sella slid three slips across her desk on D006.

One monster escort.

One road survey.

One municipal reinforcement inspection that would have paid best if my Silver file did not still contain the line:

**NO INDEPENDENT LEAD ON MUNICIPAL STRUCTURAL MAGIC PENDING FURTHER CERTIFICATION.**

I hated that line more after the Black Stair.

"I held a failing road plate under a twenty-five-wagon expedition."

Sella looked at the paper.

"Under Field Support Three and expedition engineering authority."

"I worked Storm Keep."

"Under Leth, Maren, and the ward chief."

"East floodworks."

"Also under local structural authority."

"This feels personal."

"It is literally a rule about independent authority."

Unfair use of language.

She pushed a fourth slip toward me.

**EAST QUAY / COMMERCIAL LOAD SUPPORT / MEREN VOSS**

Six-day call window.

Silver specialist accepted.

No independent municipal lead.

"Meren asked for Westreach load support," Sella said. "Apparently somebody told her you can stand near expensive things without becoming the most expensive problem."

"Who?"

"Didn't ask."

"Hostile desk."

"Take it or don't."

I took it.

Meren Voss was not impressed by Westreach.

This improved her immediately.

She met me in Warehouse Seven beside a suspended crate of ceramic ward plates and said, "Show me six inches."

No hello.

"Of what?"

"Plane. Flat. Load toward post. Six inches if you can hold clean. Four if you can't."

I could hold larger.

That was not the question.

I shaped six inches.

She walked around it.

Tapped the brass guide rail.

Put two fingers against my wrist.

"Edge is harder than center."

I looked at her.

"Yes."

"How much?"

"Enough that large planes waste reserve."

"Good. You know."

Then she dropped a two-pound sandbag against the outer edge without warning.

The plane flexed.

I held.

"Again," she said.

Westreach had taught me how to distribute force across a mountain system.

Meren wanted six inches that behaved exactly the same every time.

Annoyingly different skill.

I came back the next day.

And the next.

At D009 a Halden packet arrived.

I did not open it in the Guild doorway.

Growth remained narrow but real.

I opened it at lunch with my back against Warehouse Seven.

Mara's first page was mostly work.

The new east desk had inherited four months of unresolved road-river disputes because apparently promotion meant being given better-quality problems.

Sera had moved two desks but still found reasons to stand at Mara's.

Emmet had already borrowed one of the Carrow route books and failed to return it.

Then:

**716 days. I am not counting for romance. It is printed on my pay sheet and Vey has weaponized arithmetic.**

I smiled.

Underneath:

**Do not be weird about the number. Tell me what you're doing this week instead.**

So I did.

I wrote about Noll's new key.

Meren's six-inch plane.

Jorren spending three hours arguing that damaged wagon hardware belonged in a different factor ledger if it was repaired before resale.

The Blackglass Anchor sitting on my table because Meren refused to let me bring an artifact into a warehouse before she understood what my bare shaping could do.

I almost wrote that Carrow felt small after Westreach.

It didn't.

That surprised me.

The city felt dense.

More doors than I had opened.

More work than I had done.

More people continuing without waiting for the next expedition.

At dinner Noll put his keys on the table.

Jorren put a new ledger pencil beside them.

I put nothing down.

"No object?" Jorren asked.

"I contain multitudes."

Noll looked at me.

"You contain soup."

He filled my bowl.

Better.

Fourteen days after Mara left, the two-year promise was still in my coat drawer.

I had checked it three times.

Not every morning.

That counted.

Tomorrow I had Meren at first bell.

Noll closed the Spoon at seventh.

Jorren left for Doss on Fourday.

Mara worked Halden whether I thought about it or not.

The schedule had become life again.

Good.
