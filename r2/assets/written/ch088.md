# Chapter 88: The Returner

The return trip contained less danger than the outbound trip and more wet wool.

I preferred the danger.

Wool held water with a sense of duty. By the second morning our canvas smelled like an animal had learned bookkeeping.

Jorren walked beside the rear cart, checking tie-downs after every bad rut.

"Frame shifted."

I looked. One machine frame had moved half a handspan toward the wheel side.

"Stop?"

"Now."

The driver sighed.

We stopped and retied it. No attack appeared. No creature used the tactical opening. The world allowed maintenance without punishment.

The river crossing stayed ordinary on the way back. The ferryman still tested the water before loading us, found no silver lines, took the fee, and crossed us without repeating an explanation for my benefit.

By the fifth day the road had become familiar enough that I started recognizing bends before later memory supplied them.

A split oak near a drainage ditch. A farmhouse with three blue shutters. An inn where the stew had too much salt and one good bench near the stove.

Things I had learned this week rather than forty years from now.

I caught myself sorting the two kinds of memory again and stopped long enough to eat the stew while it was hot.

On the sixth morning one axle pin began walking out of the lead cart. I saw the bright line where metal had shifted.

"Hold."

The driver stopped before I finished pointing and climbed down.

"Damn."

Orrin Kesh had taught me to look at axle pins because he was a driver and liked arriving alive. No future memory required.

We reset the pin, wired the retainer, and continued.

Jorren saw me smiling.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Bad face for nothing."

"I learned that here."

He looked at the axle.

"Congratulations on the pin."

"Thank you."

Friendship supported achievement accurately.

Carrow appeared after midday, first as smoke, then outer fields, then the north road widening into carts and people who had somewhere specific to be.

North Gate remained open under the same restrictions: one lane, load check, work crews still inspecting stone. The repair had survived six more days without asking history for permission.

At East Annex the caravan closeout took longer than the entire final mile.

One damaged ceramic fitting. One ferry delay. One return machine-frame retie. No cargo loss. No injury. Six contracted days plus one recognized delay adjustment.

Sella read the client note.

"You were gone seven."

"River had fish."

She looked at me.

"I am not putting that in the ledger."

"Spawning lightfin altered ferry conditions."

She wrote that.

Civilization.

Jorren signed first because he had accepted the job first. We were paid, with no bonus for learning about fish.

Outside, Jorren stretched both arms above his head.

"Bath."

"Food."

"Bath first."

"You have become wealthy."

"I have become offensive."

Both.

He turned toward East Market. I turned toward Lower Rook.

Seven days together had not made our schedules one thing.

At home my room smelled closed: cold ashes, dry leather, a little dust on the shelf.

Two bowls. Still two.

Noll had left a folded scrap on the table.

**STEW BELOW. DON'T EAT THE POT.**

I put my pack down, racked the sword, pulled off my boots, and separated the field clothes before they contaminated everything else.

Then I sat on the bed and did nothing for almost a minute.

My hips hurt. My shoulders hurt. No wound, just seven days of cart vibration stored in the body.

I had gone farther north-east than Current Greg had ever gone.

Then I had come home.
