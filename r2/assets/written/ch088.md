# D126: The Friend

Status: **SELECTED R2 WRITTEN PROSE**

---

The old chair died under Noll.

This felt appropriate.

He sat down after a closing shift at the Brass Spoon, the repaired back gave one exhausted crack, and Noll descended six inches with the dignity of a collapsing shelf.

He remained seated in the wreckage for a moment.

I looked at him.

He looked at me.

"Trash," he said.

"History."

"Trash history."

The second chair by the window survived.

That one had been repaired twice already and had hosted Mara, Jorren, Tavin, Noll, Miri once, three Guild packets, the Blackglass Anchor during a table repair, and one wet cloak that should not have been placed there.

The dead chair had mostly hosted me.

I was offended by the symbolism.

We carried it downstairs.

Maud looked at the broken back.

"Finally."

"You knew?"

"Everyone knew."

"I sat in that."

"Yes."

Landlord care remained theoretical.

The replacement cost three copper and created a problem immediately.

It did not fit through the door while the window chair remained where Mara had last moved it.

Noll held one end.

I held the other.

"Turn," he said.

"Which way?"

"Other."

"You have not chosen a way."

"Then both wrong."

Strong engineering.

We moved the table six inches.

The new chair passed.

Nothing collapsed.

Current domestic triumph.

Noll sat in it immediately.

"Good."

"That's mine."

"You have one."

He pointed at the window chair.

"That one is for guests."

"I am guest."

"You live next door."

"Close guest."

There was no law against murder in my room specifically, but Maud would charge cleaning.

One hundred twenty-six days after Mara left, I could buy a chair without checking whether doing so endangered rent.

That was not new anymore.

What was new was how little ceremony money required now.

Three copper had become furniture instead of a decision tree.

Silver work paid.

Westreach had paid.

Commercial load support paid better now that people asked for me by name.

The room had accumulated around that fact.

Better field shirts.

A kettle that did not leak.

A box for spare clamps instead of clamps tearing holes in my pockets.

The Blackglass Anchor had its own padded shelf because the table had finally lost an argument with four pounds of metal and glass.

The Vey slip lived under my room key.

604 days.

I did not count every morning.

Mara occasionally counted for me.

When she wrote.

She had not written in twenty-three days.

That part I noticed.

The first week was ordinary.

Her packets had never followed a perfect schedule.

The second week produced irritation.

The third produced worry irritating enough that I visited the packet office twice in one day.

The clerk offered no emotional services.

Good boundary.

I wrote Mara on day seventeen.

Not a good letter.

Not terrible.

The dangerous middle.

**You are probably working. I know this. The packet office claims nothing is missing. I do not believe institutions automatically. Send one line if you have one line. More if you have more.**

Then I made myself write about something else.

Meren.

The municipal certification Sella still refused to waive.

Noll's new keys.

Jorren's factor job.

A north-road contract where the Blackglass Anchor saved a wagon axle and then required two hours of cleaning mud from its cage.

Life, not a distress flare.

D126, the new chair arrived.

Mara's packet arrived that afternoon.

Of course.

Noll saw the Vey seal in Maud's hand and immediately stood from my new chair.

"Open."

"I was going to."

"You held one six weeks once."

Too many historians.

Three sheets.

The first began:

**Alive. Sorry. Vey sent me north with Sera for an account merge that became three account merges, one warehouse fire that did not burn our warehouse, and a packet office that believes Halden mail is a philosophical category.**

I sat.

Window chair.

Noll kept mine.

Mara continued.

**I am fine. I was busy, then embarrassed that I had been quiet long enough to worry you, then being embarrassed made me take longer, which is stupid and apparently contagious.**

That sounded current enough to hurt.

Then work details.

Northbank had narrower streets.

Sera hated the inn.

Mara liked one bakery and refused to tell me the name because she claimed I would optimize the route around bread.

Correct.

Near the bottom:

**I have 604 days on the pay sheet today. You probably have 603 by the time this reaches you. Do not correct me in writing.**

I smiled.

Then:

**Also, if you bought another terrible chair while I was gone, I reserve inspection rights.**

I looked at the new chair.

Noll followed my eyes.

"Mara knows?"

"Apparently future sight."

"Good chair."

"You stole it."

Noll leaned back.

No creak.

"Very good."

I wrote Mara back that night.

First line:

**The chair is excellent and you are banned from reviewing it.**

Then:

**Glad you're alive. Next time one line is enough. I will attempt to believe it.**

I did not write that twenty-three days had made the room feel smaller.

Not because it was false.

Because she did not need to manage every true thing immediately.

Instead I described Noll falling through the old chair in enough detail to become malicious.

He read over my shoulder.

"Not like that."

"Exactly like that."

"Feet stayed floor."

"Irrelevant."

Current life restored itself through argument.

The new chair remained mine for almost six minutes after I finished the letter.

Then Jorren came in and sat down.

I gave up.
