# D218: The Correspondent

Status: **SELECTED R2 WRITTEN PROSE**

---

Mara came to Carrow while I was gone.

Again.

This time she had thirty-six hours and I spent all thirty-six under North Quarry arguing with a buried drainage system.

The world had developed timing as a weapon.

I came back after eleven days with slate dust inside one boot, a split thumbnail, and enough dried mud on my cloak that Maud made me remove it in the front hall.

"Floor," she said.

"The floor has survived worse."

"Not for this rent."

She took the cloak.

Then said, "Mara was here."

Everything in me stopped badly.

"Was?"

"Morning packet west."

There it was.

Past tense.

Hostile grammar.

"How long?"

"Day and half."

Thirty-six hours.

Maybe less.

I had been north dealing with stone that thought water belonged inside walls while Mara occupied my city and left again.

No one had done anything wrong.

This was somehow worse.

I climbed to my room.

The new chair had moved.

Not the window chair.

Mine.

Three feet toward the light.

Mara had opinions about furniture and considered absence insufficient protection.

A folded paper sat on the seat.

My name outside.

Under it:

**Your chair is acceptable. Placement remains criminal.**

I sat on the bed.

The room looked almost exactly like it had eleven days earlier except somebody I wanted had passed through it without me.

I opened the note.

**Greg,**

**I had thirty-six hours in Carrow. Before you decide Vey did this specifically to hurt you, they gave me forty-eight and the north packet lost twelve to rain. I am writing this first because I know you.**

I stared.

Rude accuracy.

She had arrived for a route-account correction involving three Carrow warehouses and one Vey subcontractor who apparently believed numbers became optional when freight changed boats.

She had worked most of the first day.

Noll fed her after sixth bell.

Jorren found her the second morning because Noll sent word.

Maud had charged her for tea.

Of course.

Mara had sat in my room for perhaps an hour before leaving.

Not waiting dramatically.

She had read one of Emmet's route books, inspected the chair, and written me this note.

**Your Anchor shelf is better than the old table. The blackglass looks worse. I assume this means better.**

Correct.

**Noll has become unbearable about soup. Jorren owns a coat he cannot afford morally. Maud says you still leave boots where people walk. Carrow appears functional without you. I assume the quarry is too.**

I laughed once.

Then the paragraph I knew was coming.

**I wanted to see you. I am disappointed. I am not angry at you for being at work. Please do not turn those into the same thing because it is easier.**

There.

Mara had learned my worst shortcuts.

Maybe always knew them.

I read it again.

Disappointed.

Not betrayed.

Not evidence the relationship was failing.

One person had thirty-six hours.

Another had an eleven-day contract.

The clocks missed.

That was all.

It still hurt.

Both could be true.

At the bottom:

**512 days. Still intend to be on the steps. Still expect you not to be early.**

Then:

**Next time maybe I will arrive while you are actually in your own city. Bold plan.**

I put the letter down.

Noll knocked once and entered without waiting.

He carried a bowl.

"You read?"

"Yes."

"Mad?"

"No."

He handed me soup.

"Sad?"

I looked at him.

"A little."

Noll nodded.

No repair attempt.

Good friend.

"She move chair," he said.

"I noticed."

"Better."

"Traitor."

We ate.

The quarry had paid well.

My left shoulder hurt from three days of bracing work.

The Blackglass Anchor needed one bolt replaced.

A Guild note beneath Mara's said Meren wanted me on East Quay in two days.

Life had not stopped because I missed thirty-six hours.

That was not comforting exactly.

It was useful.

I wrote Mara before bed.

**I am disappointed too. I am not angry at you for having work. I hate that you got to move my chair without supervision.**

Then I described the buried quarry door.

Not all of it.

Enough.

**Next time tell Vey to schedule around me. I understand this is how commerce works.**

I folded the letter.

The chair stayed by the window.

For now.
