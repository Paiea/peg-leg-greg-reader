# Chapter 90: The Instructor

Lera left Carrow because somebody else paid her more.

This happened four days after the yard reopened.

I respected it immediately and disliked it with equal efficiency.

She told me after a paid block.

Not before.

Important commercial ethics.

I had spent one silver learning that my outside recovery step had become too automatic in exactly the way she warned me it would.

"You made it a religion," Lera said.

"A small religion."

"Still collecting followers."

She hit my calf.

I corrected.

At the end she put the practice sword down and said, "I'm gone after Fifthday."

I thought she meant lunch.

"Where?"

"Dorrin."

That was farther east than my recent survey work and south of the north-east caravan road.

Current map surfaced before later memory did.

Progress.

"How long?"

"Six weeks. Maybe seven."

"Why?"

"Watch academy needs a temporary blade instructor. Their regular broke an ankle."

"And Carrow is allowing this?"

Lera looked at me.

Right.

"You have a profession."

"Excellent diagnosis."

"They pay better."

"Much."

There it was.

Not a calling.

Not a mysterious invitation.

Someone else had a staffing problem and money.

Lera had a skill.

Carrow was one place that skill happened.

"Your Guild blocks?"

"Transferred or refunded. Most people took transfer."

"Mine?"

"Completed."

Of course.

She had waited until after beating value into me before announcing supply disruption.

Professional.

"Anyone you'd recommend while you're gone?"

"For what?"

"Sword."

"That is a category, not a goal."

Everyone had become Sella.

I thought.

Current problems.

Recovery under pressure.

Not turning one answer into doctrine.

Body position after contact.

"I still have enough from you to practice badly for six weeks."

"Correct."

"That was not reassurance."

"It was instruction."

She sat on the edge of the weapon rack and started wiping down the padded blade.

I watched the yard around us.

Three Bronze members using the far lane.

One watch recruit borrowing a Guild shield because the Cooper Street arrangement had created friendships nobody intended.

A carpenter replacing the fence post.

Dark slate visible over the west roof line.

The institution had rebuilt enough that one instructor could leave without creating a crisis.

That felt important.

Maybe because I had once treated every useful person as if their continued proximity were part of my progression.

"You coming back?" I asked.

Lera looked up.

"Unless Dorrin gives me a reason not to."

Fair.

Not promise.

Not threat.

"Write?"

"No."

"Cruel."

"You don't need reports from my temporary employment."

She knew me too well.

I stood.

"Practice less?"

"Yes."

"Short sessions?"

"Yes."

"Stop before fatigue teaches lies?"

"You have been listening."

"Expensive habit."

Lera smiled once.

Then pointed at my rear foot.

"Also stop standing like that while talking."

I looked down.

Outside recovery position.

Religion.

I moved the foot.

She left Carrow two days later on a watch coach headed east.

I did not go to the gate.

She had work.

So did I.

The yard remained.

My sword remained.

Instruction had developed absence...
