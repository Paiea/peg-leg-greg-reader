# D305: The Candidate

Status: **SELECTED R2 WRITTEN PROSE**

---

My municipal certification assessment began with a dead sheep.

I had expected paperwork.

Sella had apparently decided livestock was more educational.

"South spillway," she said.

"Today?"

"That's how field assessment works."

"I already have Silver."

"Congratulations again."

She tapped the third line in my file.

**NO INDEPENDENT LEAD ON MUNICIPAL STRUCTURAL MAGIC PENDING FURTHER CERTIFICATION.**

The line had survived Westreach.

Storm Keep. The Black Stair. Gold hazard work. A year of commercial support since I first earned Silver.

I hated it with mature consistency.

"You know I held the Stair approach."

"Under expedition engineering authority."

"I am sensing a theme."

"Maybe you'll pass."

Olen Marr waited beside the door eating an apple.

Silver support. Twenty-three years Guild service. Gray at one temple. Plain sword. Two leather cases at his feet.

He looked at me.

Then at Sella.

"This him?"

"Unfortunately."

I had corrupted the Guild enough that strangers received advance warning.

The dead sheep was wedged against south spillway Gate Three with half a tree, two barrels, and enough river weed to establish property rights.

Winter rain had raised the tributary overnight. Gate Three had opened four inches and stopped. The winch chain was tight enough to make everyone nearby respectful.

Kessa was already on the walkway.

Of course.

She saw Olen.

"Certification?"

"Maybe," he said.

Kessa looked at me.

"He does that?"

"Disease."

Jorren was at the winch.

He made a small suffering noise.

Nobody introduced anyone because the water was rising.

Olen crouched beside the gate.

"Who owns what?"

Not magic first.

Authority.

Kessa owned rigging and the walkway structure. Tessa Bruhn, spillway keeper, owned water operations. The mill workers knew the gate's ordinary behavior. Jorren owned the winch because everyone trusted him with mechanical things capable of removing fingers. Olen owned my assessment and Guild field support.

I owned nothing automatically.

That answer used to annoy me more.

"If assigned, I can redirect side load on the gate or guide debris. I don't know the track."

Olen nodded.

"Ask what changed."

Tessa had been awake since before dawn and had no interest in my professional development.

Excellent witness.

"Opened normal first turn," she said. "Four inches. Then chain loaded hard. Knock from west track. Sheep arrived after."

"Repairs?"

"West shoe replaced summer."

Kessa swore from above.

"Replacement iron?"

"Yes."

Kessa pointed at the stone guide.

"Originals are bronze-faced. Bad iron alignment wears the guide instead of itself."

We could not see the shoe while the gate was loaded. We could not safely force the chain. Gate Two was already carrying extra water, and Tessa did not want Gate Four opened unless Three stayed stuck.

Current problem.

First we cleared debris.

I tied into the walkway post and shaped a narrow plane downstream of the branch.

Water hit harder than its depth suggested.

The sheep rotated.

Undignified even in death.

Mill workers pulled. The branch shifted, caught, shifted again.

I dropped my plane before its edge tore and reset.

Second pull cleared the branch.

The sheep followed.

Kessa saw the west shoe immediately.

A strip of dark iron protruded from the track at an angle.

Twisted. Not completely broken. Enough.

"Can we settle it?" I asked.

"Maybe if side load comes off," Kessa said.

Jorren made the noise again.

Nobody acknowledged him.

The plan was small.

I would push the gate east just enough to reduce the bite on the west shoe. Jorren would lower by clicks. Kessa would watch the track. Tessa owned stop if the water changed.

Olen did nothing visible.

Assessment profession.

I shaped.

The gate pressed against my plane.

Not all of it. A fraction. Enough to make my forearm shake.

Jorren eased one click.

Two.

The shoe moved.

My upper edge thinned.

Old instinct arrived immediately.

Hold one more.

Prove it.

Useful instinct sometimes.

Wrong here.

"Stop."

Jorren stopped instantly.

I dropped the plane.

Nothing dramatic happened.

Olen said, "Could you have held another click?"

"Probably."

"Why stop?"

"Upper edge was thinning. If it tore while Jorren moved the chain, the gate would twist west again."

Olen nodded once.

"Again when ready."

That was all.

I rested two minutes. Drank water. Shaped again.

This time the gate lowered three inches before the west shoe dropped into line with a metallic knock.

Kessa shouted, "There."

We lowered the gate fully and removed the twisted shoe.

One corner had worn nearly halfway through.

Tessa fitted a bronze-faced spare from storage.

Gate Three opened.

Water passed.

The mill pond level began dropping.

The dead sheep had already been dragged away by two workers who appeared angry at it personally.

Olen finished another apple.

"Guild," he said.

That was apparently the ceremony.

Back at the desk, Sella read his report while I pretended not to read upside down.

His handwriting remained criminal.

I caught **scope appropriate**.

**Local authority respected.**

**Stop threshold self-initiated.**

Then Sella took my file and drew one line through the sentence I had hated for more than a year.

Not erased.

History stayed visible.

Beneath it she wrote:

**MUNICIPAL STRUCTURAL SUPPORT: CLEARED UNDER SITE AUTHORITY. INDEPENDENT ENGINEERING DESIGN REMAINS OUTSIDE GUILD SCOPE.**

I read it twice.

"That's it?"

Sella looked up.

"You want another Silver tab?"

"No."

"Bell?"

"Maybe."

"No."

Olen took an apple from the bowl beside her ledger.

"What changes?" I asked.

Sella closed the file.

"You can accept municipal structural-support contracts that used to require a named senior support hand. You still don't become an engineer because you own a weird rock and survived Westreach."

"Cruel wording."

"Necessary wording."

"Rates?"

"Higher when the liability is higher."

Actual reward.

Jorren waited outside because witnessing professional bureaucracy entitled him to my tea.

"Promoted?" he asked.

"No."

"More pay?"

"Sometimes."

"Promotion."

"That is not how credentials work."

"More pay."

Noll would side with him.

I hated consensus.

A Halden letter waited beneath my door that evening.

Mara had written the remaining days under my name, as usual.

**425.**

Then:

**Heard Sella finally killed your third line. Congratulations. Do not become a bridge.**

I read that twice too.

Not because I needed the warning.

Because Mara had somehow received Guild gossip faster than I had reached home.

Packet networks remained terrifying.

I put the letter beside the Vey slip.

The third line was gone.

The work attached to it wasn't...
