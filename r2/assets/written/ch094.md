# Chapter 94: The Master

Arlo hired an apprentice before deciding whether he was a master.

Ivena decided for him.

"You are training him," she said.

"That does not make me a master."

"It makes you the person responsible when he ruins a chamber."

"That sounds worse."

Accurate.

The apprentice was seventeen and named Pellin Varo.

No relation to Lin Varo as far as anyone knew. Carrow had enough people for duplicate surnames to remain administratively irritating.

Pellin knew clay.

He centered a chamber body faster than I could follow and slower than Arlo. Then he tried to fit a brass collar by forcing the wet throat wider with both thumbs.

Arlo made a noise usually reserved for structural collapse.

"Stop."

Pellin stopped.

"Why?"

Better question than defense.

Arlo showed him the pressure tongue and explained how a throat widened under drying would pull unevenly and make the collar look correct until firing.

Pellin listened.

Second attempt, he reached for force again.

Arlo stopped him.

Third attempt, Pellin's thumbs started inward and stopped on their own.

"There," Arlo said.

"I didn't do anything."

"Exactly."

"Wage?" I asked Ivena quietly.

She gave the first-month number, then the higher second-month number if he remained useful.

"Can we afford him?"

"Can we afford Arlo shaping every body himself?"

The ledger answered.

Demand had not exploded. It had accumulated: more chambers in service, more matched spares, more cleaning returns. Bell Tile still handled firing peaks. Dena's routes were fuller. Arlo's hands had become the constraint.

Later language would call this scaling.

Current Arlo called it, "Pellin, stop touching that until I finish the sentence."

Dena arrived with a return rack and immediately put Pellin to work washing chambers.

He looked offended.

"I can throw bodies."

"You can also wash."

"I was hired for clay."

Dena pointed at the chambers.

"Clay."

She won.

By lunch Pellin had stopped performing expertise and started asking which rack meant customer-ready.

No one asked me to teach him.

I bought lunch anyway.

Pellin ate enough for two people.

"Business pays?" he asked.

Arlo and Ivena said "No" together.
