# D347: Closed

Status: **SELECTED R2 WRITTEN PROSE**

---

The Brell bridge was still closed when we came back.

This created two problems.

The first was geography.

The second was Kessa.

She saw the scaffolding before the rest of us saw the red flag.

"Stop."

Vell stopped the lead wagon.

Five days had passed since the ice release.

The mill shaft now lived at Rellin Cross, where twelve men had spent most of a day lowering it through a roof opening because the mill had been designed by people who apparently hated future repairs.

Our wagons were lighter coming back.

One carried old bearings for recasting.

One carried flour.

Two carried tool chests and the accumulated smell of animals.

The fifth carried six passengers who had paid Vell for the return road and were now learning why schedules were a form of fiction.

Timber scaffolding wrapped the south footing.

Two men worked on a platform above water level.

A third stood waist-deep on the bank with a shovel.

Kessa climbed off before Vell finished swearing.

"Wait," he said.

She did not.

Second problem.

I followed.

The repair camp had grown beside the crossing.

Canvas shelter.

Stone stacked along road.

Timber braces.

Rope.

A portable hand winch anchored to two driven posts.

The bridge keeper recognized Kessa.

His face improved.

"You're back."

"What moved?"

No hello.

Professional affection.

"South-east corner. Bed washed out behind the footing. Three feet under outer edge. Maybe more upstream."

There was the word.

Maybe.

A mason climbed from the work platform.

"Stone is sound. Ground isn't. Plan is rubble and lime bags in the void, two temporary piles upstream, brace until spring. Full rebuild when water drops."

Kessa inspected the current diagonal timber.

"Angle's too shallow."

"Bank won't take steeper."

"Move the anchor."

"Frozen ground."

"Thaw it."

"Takes a day."

"Bridge has been closed five."

Different priorities.

Neither man was stupid.

That helped.

The mason looked at my Silver tab, then at the blackglass harness under my coat.

"You're the support hand?"

"One of them."

Kessa answered for me.

"Short directional load. Existing design only. He does not make missing ground reappear."

I appreciated accurate marketing.

"Could he unload one connection while we reset the brace?"

"For breaths," Kessa said. "Not minutes."

I nodded.

That was exactly the kind of work my new certification allowed.

Under site authority.

Existing structure.

Clear stop.

No pretending magic converted geology into confidence.

Vell came over.

"Detour's still open. Adds a day."

He looked at Kessa.

"You coming?"

She looked at the scaffolding.

Then him.

"I'll stay."

No guilt.

Her hired movement work ended here. The repair crew needed a rigger more than our empty wagons did.

Vell nodded.

Then looked at me.

"You?"

I became annoyed before he spoke.

Return road.

Known work.

One extra day.

Or bridge repair.

Two days maybe three.

Higher support rate.

A problem I understood enough to be interested and not enough to be bored.

Kessa.

Also money.

Money remained spiritually persuasive.

"Repair rate?" I asked.

The mason told me.

Vell laughed.

"Traitor."

"Commerce."

"You learned."

Jorren came over when the negotiation ended.

"Staying?"

"Yes."

He handed me a small packet from his coat.

Halden mark.

"Was on Vell's mail bag. Forgot."

I stared.

"How long?"

"Four days."

"You carried Mara's letter for four days?"

"You were there."

"That is not better."

He smiled and left before I could throw him into the river.

Vell's wagons took the detour before noon.

Edda lifted one hand as they passed.

Jorren shouted, "Don't become a bridge!"

Kessa shouted back, "He won't!"

I hated that Mara and Jorren had independently selected the same warning.

Then the road quieted.

I opened Mara's letter at lunch on a timber pile.

First line:

**Sella says your third line is dead. You absolute asshole. Congratulations.**

I laughed hard enough that Kessa looked over.

"Good?"

"Mostly."

Mara wrote about work.

Len had transferred to route accounts for three months and now hummed in two buildings.

Sera had taken a promotion Mara wanted and Mara was angry about it while admitting Sera deserved it.

The chimney beside her room still smoked directly into her window on windless mornings.

Progress.

Near the bottom:

**384 days. I still intend to be on the steps.**

Then:

**I know structural support is not engineering. I am writing this because your face is probably already becoming unbearable.**

I folded the letter.

No plan to see me sooner.

Not sad.

Slightly sad.

Both.

I put it inside my coat and went back to work.

The bridge repair did not become dangerous that afternoon.

That mattered.

We unloaded stone.

Drove test stakes into frozen bank.

Built a charcoal trench around the new anchor position to thaw ground slowly.

Kessa rejected one timber because a split ran too near the end.

The mason complained about wasting wood.

She made him cut the bad section and use the shorter piece elsewhere.

Nothing failed.

No one got hurt.

At dusk, the river sat lower than morning.

The temporary brace held.

The footing had not moved according to Kessa's chalk marks.

We ate bad stew in the canvas shelter.

I criticized it until the mason told me I could cook tomorrow.

I withdrew the complaint.

I slept on a straw pallet beside three repair workers with my boots near the stove.

Mara's letter stayed inside my coat.

Outside, the Brell moved in the dark against a footing we intended to fix.

Everything was exactly as dangerous as people already knew it was.

That was why we were there.
