# Chapter 95: The Processor

Edda asked for me because she remembered the shardback job.

Not the Gold crew.

Not the fight.

The processor.

A south quarry had found six shed plates near an abandoned cut and wanted licensed retrieval before reopening the path.

No active den confirmed.

No creature hunt.

Edda owned field safety.

Rin Mesk owned material judgment.

I owned staying where they put me unless I saw something specific.

Good team.

The quarry cut smelled of wet stone and old dust.

Plate fragments lay under a collapsed shelf where something large had rubbed against rock months earlier.

Most were dull gray.

One held a faint blue line near the ridge.

Old Greg recognized the pattern before Current Greg understood why.

Cold-set tension.

Not enough to explain mechanics.

Enough to stop a hand.

Rin reached.

"That one."

She froze.

No argument.

Looked where I pointed.

Then changed angle.

"Still loaded."

Clean win.

Edda looked at me.

"Sure?"

"Enough not to touch it."

Correct evidence standard.

Rin took a forked copper tool from her case.

Not something I knew.

She pressed one end to stone beside the plate and tapped the other twice.

The blue line faded.

A sound like ice cracking far underground moved through the shelf.

I wanted to ask what the tool did.

I did not ask while her hands were working.

Growth.

She checked again.

"Safe."

Then I asked.

"What did you do?"

"Gave it somewhere cheaper to relax."

That was not a school name.

Good.

"Same as a tension sink?"

Rin glanced at me.

"Cousin. Not same."

More magic widened without organizing itself for my convenience.

We collected six safe plates.

Left two because the rock around them was unstable.

The quarry owner objected.

"That's money."

Rin handed him a liability form.

"Then retrieve it yourself after signing this."

He read two lines.

Stopped objecting.

Professional magic had paperwork.

We were paid ordinary escort/retrieval rates.

No artifact dropped into my pocket.

No punishment for being right.

Rin remembered my name this time.

That was probably worth more...
