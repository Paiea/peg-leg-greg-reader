# D350: Below the Knee

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

My foot died before I did.

Tovan checked three times.

By the third, I wanted him to stop touching it.

Not because it hurt.

Because it did not.

Fingers at the ankle.

Warm metal against the skin.

Ressa's hands around my calf while she listened to something under the blood and swelling.

Nothing changed.

My foot remained attached.

My foot remained dead.

"Say it," I said.

Tovan sat beside the bed.

"The foot isn't viable."

I looked at the blanket.

Knee.

Shin.

Foot.

The shape was still complete.

That offended me.

"How high?"

"Below the knee if the tissue stays good where it is now."

"Distance."

He touched the blanket a handspan below my kneecap.

"About there. Exact line depends on what I find."

"Knee survives."

"That is the plan."

"Hip intact."

Tovan frowned.

"Yes."

"Femur intact."

"Yes."

"Patellar tendon?"

Now he was looking at me differently.

"Intact."

"Hamstring attachment."

"Greg."

"Answer."

Ressa looked at Tovan.

Tovan answered.

"Intact."

Good.

Knee.

Hip.

Most of the leverage above the injury.

The list assembled itself before I asked it to.

Length remaining.

Pressure tolerance later.

Socket surface.

Rotation.

Lateral stability.

Uneven ground.

Stairs.

Running unlikely with anything cheap.

Fighting possible with redesign.

Possible.

Not now.

Later.

Later had become a problem with parts.

That was better than a problem called my leg.

"How long before you cut?"

"Earlier is better. Before midnight."

"How long before dead tissue threatens the knee?"

"Not a clean number."

"Range."

"Hours, not days."

"Good."

Tovan's expression tightened.

"Nothing about this is good."

I looked at him.

"The knee is good."

He stopped.

Ressa looked down.

Kessa sat by the wall in torn work clothes with dried mud on one knee and a bruise climbing under her sleeve.

She had not left since the bridge.

Everyone in the room seemed determined to make the situation large enough to drown in.

I did not have time for that.

Not yet.

"Pain control?"

Tovan answered.

"Sleep draught for the operation. Ressa controls bleeding with me. Pain medicine after. Healing for closure and infection risk."

"How much healing?"

"Enough to support the surgery. Not enough to force damaged tissue shut before we know what survives."

"Good."

He did not correct me this time.

"Prosthetics."

"Eventually."

I hated the word immediately.

"Don't use that."

Tovan blinked.

"What?"

"Eventually is not a unit. Earliest temporary weight-bearing device."

"You have not had the operation yet."

"Correct. So give me the sequence."

He stared at me for a moment.

Then something professional in him won over irritation.

"Closure first. Swelling has to come down. Wound has to tolerate pressure. Crutches before any socket. Temporary support after that. Final fit later, after the shape stabilizes."

"Weeks. Months."

"Yes."

"Which specialist in Carrow?"

"Several."

"Names."

"I don't know who is best for what you want."

"Then names anyway."

Ressa said quietly, "He's angry."

I looked at her.

"Correct."

No one moved.

There.

Apparently naming it changed the room more than pretending it was not there.

"I'm angry," I said again. "I would like to remain useful while angry."

Tovan rubbed one hand over his face.

"You need surgery."

"First item."

"Then you need to heal."

"Second item."

"You cannot schedule tissue."

"I can schedule everything around it."

Kessa made a sound from the wall.

Not a laugh.

Almost.

I pointed at her.

"Paper."

"What?"

"You have a notebook."

"For bridge measurements."

"Excellent. It has experience."

She stared at me.

Then pulled it from her coat.

Tovan said, "Greg."

"If I sleep through the operation and wake missing a leg, I would prefer not to also wake missing the conversation."

That shut him up.

Kessa came to the bedside.

I dictated.

Knee preserved if possible.

Closure.

Infection.

Swelling.

Crutches.

Temporary socket.

Carrow fitters.

Healer clearance.

Guild scope.

Work I could do seated.

Tovan interrupted at that one.

"No."

"No what?"

"No work planning today."

"Write it anyway."

Kessa did.

Tovan looked furious now.

Good.

Someone else could carry some.

"You are nineteen," he said.

That almost did it.

I looked at him.

Not loudly.

Not dramatically.

"Yes."

He waited.

I waited longer.

He had no idea why the number made me want to put my fist through the wall.

Nineteen.

Young enough that everyone would call this tragic.

Old enough inside to know exactly how quickly a body could become a problem other people discussed in past tense.

I had watched men lose limbs at thirty.

Forty.

Seventeen.

I had watched people recover badly because they thought survival was the end of the problem.

It was the start.

I was not doing that.

"Do the operation," I said.

Tovan leaned forward.

"I need you to understand what you're agreeing to."

"Remove the lower left leg below the knee because the foot and lower tissue are nonviable. Preserve as much healthy length as you can without gambling the closure. Preserve the knee. Do not chase length through questionable tissue."

Silence.

Tovan's eyes changed.

That had not sounded nineteen.

I knew.

I did not care.

"Yes," he said.

"Do it."

He asked whether I wanted a witness.

I pointed at Kessa.

"Unfortunately."

"Charging," she said.

Better.

Tovan asked who needed notification.

Noll.

Jorren.

Mara.

Her name made the machinery in my head hitch.

Not stop.

Hitch.

Halden.

Stone steps.

Three hundred eighty-one days after midnight.

I hated that I knew the number.

I hated that part of me had already calculated road time on crutches.

I hated more that another part had started correcting the calculation for a future fitted leg.

Not now.

"Mara," I said.

Kessa nodded.

"What do I tell her?"

"Facts."

"Only facts?"

I almost told her to add that I would still be there.

The sentence came up ready.

Then anger killed it.

I did not need to turn my missing leg into a performance for Mara before it was even gone.

I also did not need permission to keep the promise.

"Bridge repair accident. Lower left leg crushed. Surgery required below the knee. Alive."

"Anything else?"

"No."

Kessa looked at me for another second.

"Fine."

Tovan left to prepare.

Ressa gave me water.

Then medicine.

The edges of the room softened.

My leg did not.

Kessa stayed.

For a while neither of us spoke.

Then I said, "The brace was right."

She looked up too fast.

"We don't know that."

"Anchor held. Footing shoe held. Bank failed west and under."

"You remember all that?"

"Yes."

"You were under a beam."

"After."

I closed my eyes.

Saw it again.

Water cutting brown under the bank.

Brace angle.

Packing rubble.

The platform cleared.

Then the ground going where ground was not supposed to go.

"Thaw trench may have accelerated the crust failure," I said. "Packing changed seep. Pulling the old brace changed load. Winter scour already existed. We don't know which combination took the roof."

Kessa stared.

"Stop."

I opened my eyes.

"What?"

"Stop doing the report from the bed."

"I want to know."

"So do I."

"Then why stop?"

Her mouth tightened.

"Because I put you there."

There.

Wrong problem.

"No."

"Greg."

"You asked a Silver load hand to stay on a bridge repair after the bridge closed. I asked the pay. Pell approved the crew. We inspected. We cleared when movement showed. The bank failed outside the line."

"And your leg is gone."

Not yet.

Soon.

I felt rage rise so cleanly that it almost made me calm.

"Yes."

Kessa flinched.

I continued.

"That does not make the previous sentence false."

"I know."

"Then don't make me comfort you for hiring me."

The words landed hard.

Too hard.

Kessa looked away.

I knew immediately.

Current Greg would apologize.

Old Greg would decide whether the damage mattered to the objective.

The second thought arrived first.

That frightened me less than it should have.

"That was cruel," I said.

Kessa looked back.

"Yes."

"Still true."

"Also yes."

I nodded.

No repair beyond that.

Not now.

Tovan returned with two assistants.

The sleep draught smelled like bitter cherries and metal.

I drank it.

Before the room disappeared, I said to Kessa, "Keep the notebook."

"Why?"

"Because I will ask tomorrow."

"About what?"

"Everything."

The last thing I felt clearly was my left foot.

Cold toes beneath the blanket.

Still mine.

Then nothing.

When I woke, morning light was on the wall.

My mouth tasted like old copper.

My left foot was cold.

I tried to pull it beneath the other leg.

Nothing moved.

For several seconds I did not remember.

Then I did.

I looked down.

Right leg continued beneath the blanket.

Left stopped below the knee.

Bandage.

Then air.

My foot was gone.

My foot still felt cold.

I stared at the empty shape until Tovan entered.

He carried water.

"How do you feel?"

I looked at him.

Pain.

Nausea.

Cold toes that did not exist.

A pressure bandage where half a plan used to be.

Anger sat underneath all of it.

Not hot anymore.

Useful.

"How much length did you keep?"

Tovan stopped.

Then gave me the number.

I nodded.

"Closure?"

"Clean."

"Knee?"

"Good."

"Fever?"

"No."

"When can I sit up?"

"Now, slowly."

"When can I stand?"

"Not today."

"Tomorrow?"

"Maybe."

I hated maybe.

Fine.

A hole did not care whether I hated the measurements.

It only cared whether I climbed.

"Water," I said.

Tovan handed me the cup.

I drank.

Then I asked for Kessa's notebook.
