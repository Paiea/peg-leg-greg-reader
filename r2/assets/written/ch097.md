# Chapter 97: The Friend

Tavin insulted my coat before saying hello.

"You dress like a clerk with a sword."

"You smell like wet rope."

"River company."

"Excuse accepted."

Then we hugged.

Not because I remembered hugging him before.

Because current Tavin opened both arms and made the decision easy.

He was broader than the boy-shaped memory fragments I had finally recovered around his name.

Scar beside one eyebrow.

Callus across the base of his right thumb.

Different boots than the pair from Mara's descriptions.

Current work had happened to him while I was elsewhere.

Good.

He had one night.

Venn lodged the crew near North Gate.

I brought him to Lower Rook because letters had described the room badly and because owning two bowls remained a solvable embarrassment.

Noll arrived after his Brass Spoon shift carrying bread.

Jorren came because I had asked him three days earlier.

Not coincidence.

Planning was allowed when people participated.

Four people.

Two bowls.

We borrowed two from downstairs.

Maud Pell charged nothing but looked disappointed in my inventory.

Tavin stood in the middle of the room and turned once.

"You actually live like a person."

"Cruel."

"You have shelves."

"Recent innovation."

He picked up the old card deck.

Stopped at the burned seven.

"You kept this?"

"Your mother gave it to me."

"Mara's mother."

Right.

"Yes."

Tavin looked at me.

The correction landed gently.

He knew enough now not to make every missing fact a test.

"She'd like that you kept it."

"She'd like that I stopped losing it."

"Also true."

Dinner became bread, stew, Noll's stolen kitchen pickles, and a small cheese Jorren claimed was legal.

Tavin told river stories that contained no historical significance.

A captain who sang only while docking.

A mule that hated bells.

A clerk at Rell Ford who could detect forged wax by smell and used the ability mostly to insult merchants.

I remembered none of them from later history.

Good evening.

After dinner the cards came out.

Bad decision.

Tavin shuffled like someone who had done it on moving boats for years I had not lived with him.

"Burned seven," Noll said.

Tavin looked up.

"Red only."

Noll pointed at him.

"Fraud."

Jorren started laughing before the argument even formed.

I sat back.

Current people.
