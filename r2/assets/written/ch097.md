# D352: The Rebuilder

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Standing required four people and a chair.

I hated all five.

"Again," I said.

Tovan folded his arms.

"No."

"That was attempt three."

"Correct."

"Attempt two was better."

"Attempt two nearly put you on Ressa."

"Still useful data."

Ressa looked at me.

"I am not data."

"You are an expensive fall surface."

"More expensive now."

Good.

Nobody was treating me like glass.

I remained angry anyway.

The chair waited three feet from the bed.

Three feet.

I had crossed a battlefield under barrier fire farther than that with half my armor burning.

Different body.

Different life.

Different rules.

I knew that.

Knowing did nothing to the distance.

"Again," I said.

Tovan shook his head.

"Ten minutes."

"Five."

"Ten."

"Seven."

"This is not a market."

"Everything is a market."

Tovan looked at Ressa.

"Seven."

Excellent medicine.

I lay back.

Not because I wanted to.

Because seven minutes of rest bought another attempt.

That was the exchange.

The notebook Kessa left sat on the table beside my water.

I had already filled two pages.

Attempt one: pushed too far left because my body expected contact under the missing foot.

Attempt two: right hip compensated late.

Attempt three: better vertical line, failed during pivot.

Pain after standing: six of ten if Tovan's scale was useful, which it was not.

Phantom position: left foot felt flat and slightly turned inward.

Crutch height: one notch too tall on the first pair.

Grip padding inadequate.

Tovan had read the page and called it obsessive.

I had asked whether the observations were wrong.

He had not answered.

Seven minutes later, I stood again.

Push through hands.

Right foot.

Hip over foot before lifting either hand.

Do not chase balance with the missing side.

The phantom foot pressed against a floor that did not exist.

Ignore it.

I stood.

Right thigh shook.

I kept breathing.

"Chair," I said.

Tovan swore quietly.

Ressa moved close.

"One pivot."

"I know."

This time I did not rotate where the left heel said it was.

I moved the crutch first.

Small.

Then right foot.

Then the second crutch.

Ugly.

Slow.

Correct.

I sat in the chair.

Actually sat.

No one caught me.

For perhaps two seconds I felt something close to triumph.

Then Tovan said, "Done for the morning."

Rage returned immediately.

"I crossed three feet."

"Yes."

"Once."

"Yes."

"That is not training."

"Your incision is two days old."

"My right leg is not."

"Your right leg is currently doing two jobs. Your arms are doing jobs they have never done. Your balance is relearning where your body ends."

"Then they need repetitions."

"They need tissue."

I stared at him.

He stared back.

I knew this argument.

Not this exact one.

The category.

Officer wants movement.

Healer wants time.

Quartermaster says equipment does not exist because yelling has not invented it.

The old answer was to identify the limiting resource and stop arguing with the wrong person.

Fine.

"What fails if I overdo it?"

Tovan answered immediately.

"Closure can split. Swelling increases. Deep tissue gets angry. You fall. You injure the right leg. You destroy your hands. You turn three weeks into six."

There.

Useful.

"Then give me the maximum that doesn't do those things."

"There is no exact maximum."

"Give me your conservative one."

Tovan's jaw moved.

"Three standing sessions today. Four tomorrow if the wound behaves. No hopping drills. No stairs. No loaded carry. No pressure on the end of the limb."

I picked up Kessa's pencil.

Wrote it down.

Tovan looked offended.

"You are going to treat that like an order."

"Yes."

"Not a challenge."

"Correct."

That surprised him.

Good.

I had no interest in proving I could disobey a healer.

I wanted the shortest route out.

Sometimes shortest meant not being stupid.

The door opened.

Jorren came in carrying my coat.

Mud on one boot.

Of course.

He stopped.

Looked at the chair.

Looked at me in it.

Looked at the blanket ending early over my left side.

"You sitting?"

"Advanced technique."

"Looks bad."

"Jealous."

He put my coat on the bed.

Then stood there.

Not talking.

His eyes kept moving back to where the leg ended.

I noticed.

I resented noticing.

"Look properly," I said.

Jorren frowned.

"What?"

"You're doing the thing where you don't look because you think not looking is polite."

He stared at me.

Then at the bandaged leg.

Actually looked.

"Below knee."

"Yes."

"Knee good?"

"Yes."

"Hurts?"

"Yes."

"Foot?"

"Feels cold."

His face twisted.

"That's stupid."

"Extremely."

Better.

He sat in the other chair.

"Kessa said the bank went after everybody cleared."

"Correct."

"She blames herself."

"Incorrect."

"You blame her?"

"No."

"Pell?"

"Not yet."

"Yourself?"

I looked at him.

There it was.

The clean emotional question.

I could have answered honestly.

Instead my mind ran the event again.

Inspection.

Scour.

Repair.

Stop call.

Clear line.

Secondary bank failure.

Wrong collapse model.

"I blame the map of the ground," I said.

Jorren waited.

"Meaning?"

"We thought we knew where the void ended. We didn't."

"That's it?"

"No."

I looked at the missing part of the blanket.

"I'm also pissed off."

"At who?"

"Yes."

He laughed once.

Then his eyes went wet.

That made me angrier.

Not at him.

At the fact that my body had become something that could do that to his face.

"Don't," I said.

Jorren rubbed his eyes.

"Fuck you."

"Better."

"You lost your leg."

"I noticed."

"I am allowed to be upset."

There.

Current Greg would have apologized immediately.

The other part of me sorted priorities.

His emotion did not threaten the objective.

My irritation did not need solving.

"Fine," I said.

Jorren stared.

"Fine?"

"Be upset. Just don't make me manage it."

That hurt him.

I saw it.

I let it happen.

Then, because I was not completely gone:

"Not today."

Jorren's face changed.

Small.

Enough.

"All right."

Noll arrived with soup fifteen minutes later.

He looked at the chair.

"You win?"

"Temporarily."

He set the bowl down.

Chicken, rice, ginger.

No speech.

No pity.

I loved him for it and immediately converted that love into logistics.

"How far from Lower Rook front door to cookroom?"

Noll paused.

"What?"

"Steps. Approximately."

"Why?"

"Need ground-floor route."

Jorren looked at me.

"You're measuring home?"

"Yes."

"You haven't left hospital."

"Correct."

"Greg."

"What?"

"Eat the soup."

Noll handed me the spoon.

Strong coalition.

I ate.

After they left, I wrote Mara.

The first version was terrible.

**Kessa's packet is accurate. Surgery succeeded. Knee preserved. I stood today. Carrow has at least three prosthetic fitters worth investigating and one Guild workshop capable of static shaping while seated.**

I stopped.

Read it.

That was a field report.

Mara was not a commander.

I was not reporting survival percentage.

I crossed out the entire paragraph.

Second version.

**Mara,**

**Kessa's packet is accurate. Tovan took the lower left leg below the knee. The knee is good. I stood today and reached a chair three feet away. It took four attempts and made me furious enough that Tovan threatened to sedate me recreationally.**

Better.

Still controlled.

I continued.

**I am angry. I don't know at whom in a useful order yet. I do know I am not interested in spending the next year being congratulated for reaching furniture.**

I stared at that sentence.

Kept it.

**I want to see you. If you can come without blowing up your own work, come. If you cannot, write. I am not going to tell you what choice makes you a good person.**

Then I almost added:

**I will still be at the stone steps.**

No.

Not because I doubted it.

Because I was too angry to use the promise as reassurance.

I wrote instead:

**Tell me what Halden's Vey office uses for warehouse lift braces. Especially the foot joints if they're articulated. Ask Sera if she knows who maintains them. This is not romantic. Unfortunately it is useful.**

There.

Mara would understand that something had changed.

So did I.

I signed the letter.

Then opened Kessa's notebook.

Page three.

I wrote one line across the top.

**CURRENT PROBLEM: MOBILITY, INCOME, FIELD USEFULNESS.**

Under it:

**Do not solve all three with the same tool unless the tool earns it.**

Old habit.

Old handwriting pressure.

My hand remembered before I did.

For the first time since the bridge, I felt something besides pain and anger that I trusted.

Not hope.

A problem.
