# D358: Ground Floor

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Maud put a price on gravity.

Four copper a week.

"Three," I said.

"Four."

"Room is half the size."

"Closer to ground."

"That is not a feature."

Maud looked at the crutches.

"Today it is."

I wanted to tell her where she could put the room.

Instead I calculated.

Four copper weekly.

Upstairs room through Firstday because most of my things still lived there.

Hospital wagon already paid.

Tovan follow-up partially covered by Guild injury support.

Reduced work unknown.

Three weeks minimum before temporary socket discussion if closure behaved.

Paying anger was stupid.

"Three and a half," I said.

"Four."

"Window latch repaired."

"Works."

"Opens in wind."

"Fresh air."

I stared at her.

She stared back.

No pity.

No discount.

Useful.

"Four until stitches out. Three and a half after if I'm still here."

Maud considered.

"Four until Firstday. Three and three quarters after."

"Three and a half."

"Three and three quarters."

I hated her.

"Fine."

"Paid until Firstday."

Of course.

I gave her the money.

Then nearly fell turning around.

The left crutch tip slipped on the hall board.

My body threw weight toward a foot that did not exist.

Jorren reached for me.

"Don't."

Too sharp.

He stopped anyway.

I caught the wall.

No fall.

My stump pulsed hard enough to blur the edges of the room.

Nobody spoke.

I hated that more.

"What moved?" I asked.

Jorren blinked.

"What?"

"Crutch tip. Why?"

Noll looked at the board.

"Wax."

"Where?"

He pointed.

Cookroom traffic had polished the wood near the threshold.

There.

Not random.

Surface problem.

"Rug," I said.

Maud, already walking away, called back, "Two copper."

"Die criminally."

"Rent first."

Strong institution.

The ground room sat behind the cookroom.

One step down.

Six inches.

Tovan had made me practice one step before discharge.

Crutches down.

Right foot follows.

Do not hop tired.

Do not trust the phantom heel.

I executed badly but without falling.

Then I stopped inside and looked at the room like an enemy position.

Bed on back wall.

Table too low.

Chair angled wrong.

Window at cart-wheel height.

Washbasin impossible if left where Maud put it.

One narrow route between bed and table.

Crutch clearance poor beside the chair.

Nothing about the room had changed since yesterday.

I had.

That did not mean the room won.

"Move the table," I said.

Jorren put down my bag.

"Hello to you too."

"Table. Window wall. Turn it."

He looked at Noll.

Noll shrugged.

They moved it.

"Chair beside bed, not opposite."

"Why?" Jorren asked.

"Transfer angle."

"You've been here twelve seconds."

"Long enough."

Noll moved the chair.

I pointed at the washbasin.

"Table."

"Food table," he said.

"Not anymore."

"Bad system."

"Temporary."

"All bad systems temporary. Then stay."

I looked at him.

That was annoyingly good.

"Fine. Basin on stool. Stool by wall."

Noll complied while muttering something about military furniture.

I sat.

Too hard.

Pain flashed through the healing leg.

I breathed through it.

Then opened Kessa's notebook.

Ground room route.

Bed to door: seven right-foot movements with crutches.

Door to privy: twenty-two if not crowded.

Single threshold.

Polished hall section hazardous.

Cookroom often obstructed between fourth and sixth bell.

Water transport unsolved.

Bathing unsolved.

Stairs possible later, not worth cost daily.

Jorren watched me write.

"You making a map?"

"Yes."

"Of the inn."

"Yes."

"You've lived here months."

I looked at him.

"Not like this."

He stopped joking.

That annoyed me too.

"Keep joking," I said.

"You just made a floor plan because the hallway is shiny."

"Better."

Noll brought eggs.

"Eat."

"I'm working."

"Eat while."

I did.

He had improved dramatically at giving orders since the Brass Spoon.

The afternoon became a campaign against stupid things.

The privy door was too narrow to turn normally, so I learned to enter backward.

The water jug needed a lid and shoulder strap.

The bed was six inches too low for easy transfer, so Jorren put blocks under the frame until I told him to stop before Maud charged vertical rent.

My crutches needed hooks beside the bed and door.

Noll drove two nails into the wall.

Maud appeared instantly.

"Wall damage."

"Medical infrastructure."

"One copper."

"Take the nails when I leave."

"Half."

I paid.

I hated everyone.

The hatred was productive.

By third bell I could get from bed to privy and back without another person touching me.

It took too long.

I timed it.

Jorren caught me.

"Stop that."

"Why?"

"Because you're timing going to piss."

"Current route is eighty-seven breaths."

"You are insane."

"Current route is bad."

"You're six days out from surgery."

"Both statements can be true."

He stared.

"You sound different."

There.

I knew what he meant.

Not voice.

Shape.

Questions getting shorter.

Everything becoming function.

I had used that headspace before battles.

Before retreats.

Before winter marches with too few wagons.

Before meetings where five hundred people lived or died depending on whether somebody understood the road.

It was a good headspace.

It had kept me alive.

It had also made me unbearable.

"I am different," I said.

Jorren's face tightened.

"Because of the leg?"

"Because I am pissed off."

That answer satisfied neither of us.

Good.

A packet arrived near evening.

Halden.

Mara.

My whole body accelerated and the room punished me for it immediately.

I stood before setting the left crutch.

Table caught my hip.

Kettle fell.

Water hit the floor.

Noll appeared in the doorway.

"You fall?"

"No."

"Kettle dead."

"Collateral."

He frowned.

"You talk weird."

Apparently everyone had joined the committee.

He kicked the crutch toward me.

I positioned it properly.

Waited.

Then crossed the cookroom.

Slow.

Too slow.

The runner handed me Mara's letter and tried not to look at the missing leg.

Failed.

I let him.

People looked at damage.

I had too.

Inside the room, I opened the letter.

**I got Kessa's packet yesterday morning and yours this afternoon.**

Then:

**I am glad you are alive. I am furious you were hurt. I know those are not opposite statements.**

Good.

Anger I understood.

**Vey can release me for five days starting next Fourday. Travel eats most of it. Sera offered to cover if I take unpaid leave. I can afford some. I have not decided. Before you tell me not to come, don't. I want to come. Wanting and being able are different.**

I read that twice.

Then kept going.

**Your last letter sounded like somebody writing an operations report while bleeding. You asked me about warehouse lift braces in the same paragraph where you told me you were angry. I asked Sera anyway. Halden east warehouse uses a hinged ash-and-iron foot on two of the larger lift braces. Maintenance is Havel & Son, north quay. Sera says the son is better and the father charges like he invented wood.**

I stopped.

There.

Information.

Immediate usefulness.

My mind moved before emotion did.

Ash.

Iron hinge.

Load cycling.

Different scale, same problem family.

Mara continued.

**I know that look and I am not even in the room. Do not build yourself a warehouse crane and call it a leg.**

I laughed.

Hard enough to hurt.

Good.

**Also: Tovan gets to decide when tissue can take pressure. You get to decide what you do with that pressure once it can. Those are different jobs. You are bad at letting them stay different.**

That irritated me because it was correct.

Then:

**I am not canceling Halden. I am not asking about the stone steps. Not because I think you won't come. Because I refuse to make you answer that from a ground-floor room six days after surgery.**

My anger found a new target.

Not Mara.

The room.

The crutches.

The entire concept of being protected from my own promise.

I read the sentence again.

She was right.

Still hated it.

**Write me what you need from me. Not what you think you should be able to do. What you need.**

That was harder than any technical question.

I found paper.

First draft:

**Ash dimensions, hinge sketches, maintenance failures, contact name.**

I stared at it.

Mara had not asked what I needed from Halden's warehouse.

I crossed it out.

Second draft:

**Come if you can without burning your job down. I want you here. I also want the Havel & Son dimensions if Sera can get them without stealing anything.**

Better.

Then:

**Today I can get to the privy alone. The room is reorganized. Water has a strap. The hall needs a rug. Maud is extorting medical infrastructure. I am still angry. Jorren says I sound different. He is correct.**

I stopped.

That was as honest as I wanted to be.

At the bottom I added:

**I don't want to be good at this. I want out of the hole.**

I nearly crossed it out.

Did not.

Outside the low window, a barrel rolled past.

The world remained at wheel height.

I looked at Kessa's notebook.

Mobility.

Income.

Field usefulness.

Then added a fourth line.

**TIME.**

Three hundred seventy-two days to Halden.

I had said I would stop counting.

Apparently I had lied.

Fine.

A deadline was just terrain with a number on it.
