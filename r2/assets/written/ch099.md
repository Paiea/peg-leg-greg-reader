# Chapter 99: The Observer

Meral told me after the job that she had been evaluating me.

This was rude.

The job itself gave no warning.

Two-day ridge relay inspection.

Meral leading.

One road technician named Varo Pell, no relation to either Pell I already knew as far as administrative civilization could establish.

Me as Bronze field hand.

No Jorren.

No creature contract.

Mostly walking, carrying, and making sure an old line had not become a dangerous line while winter loosened its grip.

On the second morning a replacement post shifted while Varo was tensioning the upper wire.

"RIGHT!" he called.

Not my word.

His.

I formed small at the base where the post wanted to kick.

Five inches.

Plane angled with the soil line.

Enough to change the first movement.

"HOLD."

I held.

Meral reset the brace stake.

Varo changed the line.

"GONE."

I released.

No one congratulated me.

We continued.

At camp a feral dog pack came close enough that I drew my sword.

They decided three armed adults and one cooking pot were not worth it.

I sheathed without combat.

Current professional range included animals choosing correctly.

The closeout happened at East Annex the following afternoon.

Meral signed the field sheet.

Then kept the pen.

"Your judgment is better."

I waited.

"That's suspicious."

"Sella asked whether I'd sign a readiness note if I saw enough."

There.

Rank pressure arrived from a conversation I had not been part of.

"Silver?"

"Application readiness. Not promotion."

Important distinction.

"Did you sign?"

"Yes."

For one breath I felt very nineteen.

Not old Greg reviewing a familiar ladder.

Current Greg being told another professional had changed her opinion of him.

Unpleasantly good feeling.

"Why?"

Meral looked annoyed by the question.

"Because you stopped trying to own every problem. Because your sword does not frighten me anymore. Because people can understand your support calls. Because when you don't know, you have started saying so before touching things."

Specific.

Better than praise.

"Does that mean I should apply?"

"Means I won't object if you do."

She handed me the closeout copy.

"You still need other evidence."

"Good."

"Why good?"

"One opinion is dangerous."

Meral almost smiled.

"There he is."

I was paid for the relay job.

The readiness note paid nothing.

It sat somewhere in Guild paperwork anyway.

That evening I ate alone because Noll was working and Jorren was gone on a cart run.

Belonging had calendars.

So did rank...
