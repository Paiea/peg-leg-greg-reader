# D365: The Planner

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

One year after Mara left, I dropped a spoon.

It landed under the bed.

I stared at it.

The spoon had chosen the exact distance where bending from the chair was unsafe and standing was annoying.

Excellent placement.

My crutches leaned against the wall.

Six inches outside easy reach.

Another mistake.

Not tragedy.

Mistake.

I dragged the nearest crutch toward me by the leather wrap, stood, turned, braced against the bed, and retrieved the spoon.

Forty-two breaths.

Yesterday it would have taken fifty.

I put the spoon on the table.

Then moved the crutch hook three inches closer to the chair.

Noll called from the cookroom.

"Dead?"

"Improving."

"Worse."

Good household.

The ground-floor room no longer looked temporary.

That pissed me off.

Two hooks for crutches.

Washbasin raised.

Bed blocked higher.

Table rotated for transfer clearance.

Water jug with shoulder strap.

Chair with one arm removed because it caught the left side when I pivoted.

A folded rug across the polished hall section after Maud charged me one copper and called it communal safety.

Every improvement proved I had needed it long enough to improve.

I hated that logic.

I used all of it anyway.

Kessa's notebook sat open on the table.

The first pages had bridge measurements.

Then surgery notes.

Then standing attempts.

Then the room.

Then work.

A different handwriting had started appearing between mine.

Tovan's.

Noll's, once, because I made him write the cookroom traffic hours and he wrote **TOO MANY**.

Jorren's route distances.

Mara's copied warehouse brace dimensions from Halden.

Not a diary.

A campaign book.

That word was too dramatic.

I kept using it in my head anyway.

Tovan arrived after breakfast.

He unwrapped the stump.

Less swelling.

Closure clean.

No fever.

Pain still sharp on the outer side after standing.

Phantom heel now felt crushed instead of cold.

Apparently the missing foot had developed new hobbies.

"Stitches in four days," Tovan said.

"Temporary socket."

He sighed.

"You ask every visit."

"You answer differently sometimes."

"Three to five weeks if healing continues."

"Three."

"Three to five."

"What makes it five?"

There.

He stopped being annoyed.

"Swelling. Skin breakdown. Closure intolerance. Infection. You doing something stupid."

"Good."

"That word again."

"Those are variables."

"Some."

"Which aren't?"

Tovan looked at me for a long second.

"Your body gets a vote."

I hated that answer because it was not useless.

"Fine."

He checked my hands.

Two blisters hardening under the crutch grips.

"Gloves."

"Wearing them."

"Inside too?"

"Yes."

He looked disappointed that he could not catch me.

I liked that.

Then he noticed the notebook.

"What's Havel & Son?"

"Halden brace makers."

"Why?"

"Joint geometry."

"For a leg?"

"Maybe."

Tovan closed his eyes.

"No."

"You haven't heard the idea."

"No articulated joint until you can tolerate a basic socket."

"That is not the same as no."

"Greg."

"I'm not building it tomorrow."

"Good."

"I'm finding out what exists."

"Also good."

We stared at each other.

Apparently medicine and planning could coexist if both parties remained irritating.

After Tovan left, I went to the Guild.

Six streets.

I knew every sitting point now.

Baker bench.

Low wall on Copper.

Barrel rack outside the cooper if nobody was using it.

Guild lane still had nothing because adventurers apparently arrived with legs included.

I did not sit.

My arms burned by the fifth street.

I made the sixth anyway.

Not because suffering proved anything.

Because I needed the current limit.

At the Guild door, Sella came around the counter.

"Don't."

She stopped.

"I was going to open the door."

It was already open.

We both looked at it.

"Habit," she said.

"Bad one."

"You have several."

Fair.

My Silver tab was still on my coat.

I had thought about removing it.

Then got angry at myself for thinking about it.

Rank did not live below the knee.

Scope did.

Different problem.

Sella put the Brell pay, injury support, and temporary field restrictions on the counter.

No road combat.

No open-water structure work.

No unsupported load shaping.

No field response until healer clearance and reassessment.

"How do I clear reassessment?"

Sella looked at me.

"Healer first."

"Then?"

"Mobility demonstration. Static shaping. Emergency movement. Weapon control if you still want weapon clearance."

"Written criteria?"

"Not exactly."

"Make them exact."

"That's not how this works."

"Then how do I train for it?"

"Recover."

I felt anger rise.

Sella saw it.

Good.

"No," I said. "Recover is not a task. What do you need to see?"

She stared.

Then started listing.

Transfer from seated to standing without assistance.

Twenty paces with mobility aid while carrying light gear.

One controlled turn under load.

Static plane at three angles.

One emergency drop to seated or ground without uncontrolled fall.

Weapon draw without losing balance.

No requirement to run.

There.

I wrote it down.

Sella watched me.

"You look happy."

"I am not."

"You look something."

"Now it has edges."

She understood enough not to ask.

"What work can I do before clearance?"

Bench ward work.

Inspection records.

Static shaping seated.

Warehouse layout.

Training support.

Lower pay.

I hated the list.

"Two bench shifts next week."

"Tovan clears sitting?"

"Yes."

"Fatigue?"

"Within limit."

Sella raised one eyebrow.

"You have a limit now?"

"Several."

"Good."

"Temporary."

She smiled slightly.

I hated her less.

Outside, I sat on the Guild step because my hands were shaking.

That pissed me off too.

A Bronze adventurer stepped around me, saw the Silver tab, saw the missing lower leg, and kept moving.

No pity.

Good.

I opened the notebook.

Under CURRENT PROBLEM, I crossed out the original four lines.

Not because they were solved.

Because they were too broad.

I rewrote them.

**1. HEAL WITHOUT LOSING TIME TO EGO.**

I hated that line most.

**2. RESTORE INDEPENDENT DAILY MOVEMENT.**

**3. RETURN TO PAID USEFULNESS BEFORE FULL PROSTHETIC FIT.**

**4. DESIGN MOBILITY AROUND CURRENT BODY, NOT MEMORY.**

**5. REGAIN SILVER FIELD SCOPE.**

Then I stopped.

The sixth line was obvious.

**6. HALDEN, D730.**

I stared at it.

One year remained.

Not approximately.

Three hundred sixty-five days.

My old life had taught me something ugly about long timelines.

They looked generous until the first winter went wrong.

A year was plenty.

A year was nothing.

Both were true.

I closed the notebook.

On the walk home, I stopped at Arlo's shop.

Not because I needed anything yet.

Because his workbench had clamps, brass collars, leather scraps, and the kind of man who asked what a thing needed to do before asking what it was supposed to look like.

Arlo saw the crutches.

Then the leg.

Then my face.

"No," he said.

I stopped.

"I haven't asked."

"You have that face."

"What face?"

"The one before you make my afternoon expensive."

Good.

I put Kessa's notebook on his bench.

"I need to understand sockets."

Arlo looked at the page.

Then at me.

"For you."

"Unfortunately."

"Healer involved?"

"Yes."

"Good."

"Don't get comfortable."

He pulled the notebook closer.

"Show me what you know."

There.

Not encouragement.

Work.

I leaned on the crutches and started with the Havel warehouse hinge.

Arlo listened for perhaps thirty seconds.

Then interrupted.

"Wrong scale."

"I know."

"Wrong loading."

"I know."

"Then why start there?"

"Because the bearing problem rhymes."

Arlo looked at me.

Longer this time.

"Rhymes."

"Not matches."

He grunted.

Accepted provisionally.

We spent an hour proving most of my first ideas bad.

That was the best hour I had had since Brell.

When I reached Lower Rook, Mara's one-year-old Vey slip waited beneath her latest letter.

HALDEN EAST OFFICE.

STONE STEPS.

TERM CLOSE.

FIRST BELL.

TWO YEARS. DO NOT BE EARLY.

I looked at the empty space below my left knee.

Then at the notebook.

Then at the clock line I had written outside the Guild.

D730.

I was in a hole.

Fine.

Holes had walls.

Walls had measurements.

Measurements became routes.

I turned the page.

At the top I wrote:

**YEAR TWO: GET OUT.**
