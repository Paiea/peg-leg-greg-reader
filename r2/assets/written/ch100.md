# Chapter 100: The Applicant

Sella put the Silver application sheet in front of me and did not tell me to sign it.

The Guild intake desk had moved back into the repaired east offices three days earlier. Not all operations. Not the archive. Not every room.

Enough.

Dark slate covered most of the west roof now. The temporary vegetable-hall era was ending in pieces.

"Meral signed readiness," Sella said.

"She told me."

"Neris will sign current support competency if asked."

I looked at her.

"She volunteered?"

"No. I asked whether she would."

"Why?"

Sella tapped the sheet.

"Because this is my job."

I read the requirements.

Observed field assessment. Record review. Two current Silver endorsements or one Gold.

Fee.

Of course fee.

"How much?"

She pointed.

I winced.

"What changes if I pass?"

"Contract eligibility. Rate floor on some work. Permission to lead certain Bronze assignments. Higher responsibility expectation."

"What changes if I fail?"

"You remain Bronze."

"Publicly embarrassing?"

"Only if you make it public."

I looked at the blank signature line.

Later young Greg had reached Silver as a warrior. That fact had lived in my head for months like a marker beside a road.

Current body was stronger than when I woke. Sword work cleaner. Still not old Greg.

Support stranger and smaller than old memory wanted, but operational in ways it had not been early.

Work history real. People trusted me for specific things, not everything.

Meral trusted my field judgment enough to sign. Neris apparently trusted my support work enough not to refuse. Jorren trusted me enough to request me for longer work and enough not to request me for everything.

"Can I take this?" I asked.

Sella nodded.

"It isn't sacred."

"Paper in this building has had a difficult year."

She stared.

"Take it."

I folded the application and put it inside my coat.

Outside, a roofer was removing one of the last scaffold braces from the west side.

Dark roof above him.

I walked home with the application still unsigned.

Dinner happened first.
