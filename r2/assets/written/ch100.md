# Chapter 100: The Applicant

Sella put the Silver application sheet in front of me and did not tell me to sign it.

Good institution.

The Guild intake desk had moved back into the repaired east offices three days earlier.

Not all operations.

Not the archive.

Not every room.

Enough.

Dark slate covered most of the west roof now.

The temporary vegetable-hall era was ending in pieces.

"Meral signed readiness," Sella said.

"She told me."

"Neris will sign current support competency if asked."

I looked at her.

"She volunteered?"

"No. I asked whether she would."

There went destiny.

Good.

"Why?"

Sella tapped the sheet.

"Because this is my job."

Also good.

I read the requirements.

Observed field assessment.

Record review.

Two current Silver endorsements or one Gold.

Fee.

Of course fee.

"How much?"

She pointed.

I winced.

Progress remained monetized.

"What changes if I pass?"

"Contract eligibility. Rate floor on some work. Permission to lead certain Bronze assignments. Higher responsibility expectation."

"What changes if I fail?"

"You remain Bronze."

"Publicly embarrassing?"

"Only if you make it public."

Excellent.

I looked at the blank signature line.

Later young Greg had reached Silver as a warrior.

That fact had lived in my head for months like a marker beside a road.

I had treated it carefully because markers were not destinations.

Current body was stronger than when I woke.

Sword work cleaner.

Still not old Greg.

Support stranger and smaller than old memory wanted, but operational in ways it had not been early.

Work history real.

People trusted me for specific things.

Not everything.

Specific things.

Meral trusted my field judgment enough to sign.

Neris apparently trusted my support work enough not to refuse.

Jorren trusted me enough to request me for longer work and enough not to request me for everything.

That last one might have mattered most.

"Can I take this?" I asked.

Sella nodded.

"It isn't sacred."

"Paper in this building has had a difficult year."

She stared.

"Take it."

I folded the application.

Put it inside my coat.

Did not sign.

Outside, a roofer was removing one of the last scaffold braces from the west side.

Dark roof above him.

Not because history had arrived.

Because he had finished his part of the job.

I walked home with a Silver application in my pocket and no requirement to become anything before dinner...
