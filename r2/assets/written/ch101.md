# Chapter 101: The Route Board

West Cut finally got a survey because two merchants stopped agreeing about who was losing money.

Harl Fen explained this at East Annex while Pate tried to eat a posted notice.

"City didn't care about old alignment," Harl said. "Now North Gate traffic is up and West Cut carriers want rate relief."

"So?"

"So somebody has to know whether the cut can carry thaw carts before anyone funds repairs."

An old geography thread had moved because trade changed, not because I remembered it.

The survey was four days.

Harl. Pate. A city works clerk named Senna Vey. One Bronze field hand.

"Why me?"

Harl looked at me.

"You've walked it. You don't panic when old infrastructure disagrees with memory."

Specific enough.

"Rate?"

He gave it.

Better than ordinary escort. Not Silver money.

I accepted.

Jorren was already on a grain run south.

We left next morning under thaw rain.

West Cut looked worse without snow hiding it. Ditches full. One culvert half blocked. Ruts deep enough to become property.

Senna measured everything.

"People use this?" she asked.

Harl pointed at fresh wheel tracks.

By midday we reached the first retaining wall.

Later memory wanted it rebuilt in dressed stone. Current wall was stacked field rock, moss, and stubbornness.

Senna touched one shifted course.

"Mark."

Harl marked it.

The road did not need to matter later to be expensive now.
