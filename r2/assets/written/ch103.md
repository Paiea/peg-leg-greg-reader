# Chapter 103: The Spring

The water came from a spring nobody had mapped because it only existed during hard thaw.

Senna found the first sign above the road.

Not water. Dead grass flattened in a narrow line beneath last year's leaves.

We climbed.

Twenty yards uphill the ground became soft enough that Harl stopped us.

"Spread out."

We did.

Pate stayed below.

A shallow basin under exposed roots held clear water. No glow, no monster, no magical explanation required. Just thaw pressure finding an old path.

Senna checked her historic map copy.

Nothing.

Harl checked older maintenance notes.

One entry from eleven years ago:

**UPPER WET AFTER LATE FREEZE. CLEARED BY MIDSUMMER.**

One bored worker's sentence had survived long enough to matter.

"Drain alignment later?" Harl asked me.

I pointed farther east than before.

"I think."

"Think useful?"

"Useful enough to look."

We looked and found two buried stones under brush in a shallow line.

Maybe old drain edge. Maybe boundary stones.

Senna refused to classify them until excavation.

The survey changed from "repair retaining wall" to "investigate seasonal spring and old drainage before wall work."

Less satisfying. More correct.

That evening Harl wrote the recommendation while I dried socks beside the fire.

The first problem had been shaped wrong. Tomorrow someone else would have to pay to learn what shape it actually had.
