# D447: The Partner

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Mara saw the leg before she saw me.

Fair.

I was standing on a warehouse floor with my back half turned to the door, arguing with Kessa about six brace points and one stupid roof line.

The wooden foot showed beneath my shortened left trouser leg.

Dark ash.

Leather upper.

One iron band above the ankle that was not an ankle.

Bera had finally let Arlo reinforce the lower shaft after I split the first one against a loading curb.

Not in combat.

Carrying onions.

Nobody respected a narrative.

"No," Kessa said. "If we move the fourth brace, the roof load changes."

"The roof load already changes. That's why the third anchor keeps creeping."

"By a finger width."

"A finger is enough if it becomes two."

"Over six years."

"Excellent. We'll schedule collapse."

Kessa swore.

I smiled.

Then saw Tero looking past me.

His face had become interested in something that was not work.

I turned.

Mara stood in the warehouse doorway holding a travel bag.

Gray coat.

Hair tied badly behind her head.

Blue cup strapped to the side of the bag because apparently some objects survived everything.

For one second, neither of us moved.

Her eyes went to my face.

Then down.

Leg.

Back to my face.

She did not hide it.

Good.

My entire body reacted anyway.

Chest tight.

Mouth dry.

Anger somewhere stupid and immediate because she had looked.

Then relief because she was here.

Then desire, which ignored administrative order entirely.

"You're early," I said.

Mara stared.

"Hello to you too."

Right.

I put the chalk down.

Kessa looked between us.

"We can finish tomorrow."

"No," I said.

Too fast.

Mara's eyes changed.

Kessa's did too.

I heard myself.

"Ten minutes," I said. "Anchor three, four, and six. Then done."

Kessa folded her arms.

"Greg."

Mara said, "Finish."

I looked at her.

"You sure?"

"I took three days on a river boat. Ten minutes won't kill me."

Probably.

Bad phrase.

Nobody corrected it.

I turned back to the roof plan.

My concentration did not come back cleanly.

Mara existed behind me now.

That changed the room.

I hated that it changed the room.

I loved that it changed the room.

Kessa pointed at brace four.

"You were saying."

Right.

Work.

I finished the inspection.

Not well enough to impress myself.

Well enough.

Anchor three needed a wider plate.

Four stayed.

Six got a monitoring mark instead of replacement.

Kessa signed.

I signed.

Tero carried the tray away.

Then there was no work left between me and Mara.

Much harder problem.

I picked up the walking staff.

Not because I needed it for the flat floor.

Because the warehouse threshold was uneven and I had learned not to perform independence for audiences.

Mara noticed that too.

Of course.

I walked to her.

Left foot forward.

Load.

Right through.

The gait was better now.

Still visible.

Always would be, probably.

Mara waited until I stopped.

Then put down her bag and hugged me.

Hard.

No careful arms.

No avoiding the left side.

I nearly lost balance anyway because I had not planned for her weight.

My staff hit the floor once.

Mara pulled back immediately.

"Sorry."

There it was.

I hated the word.

"Don't."

Too sharp again.

Her face changed.

I closed my eyes for half a second.

"Not you. Just don't apologize for hugging me."

"You almost fell."

"Then I almost fell."

"Greg."

"Mara."

There.

Old rhythm.

She looked down at the leg again.

This time slower.

"Can I?"

I knew what she meant.

"Look?"

"Touch."

That surprised me.

"The wooden part?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because I've been reading about it for three months and it still feels imaginary."

Fair.

I held the staff steady.

Mara crouched.

Not reverently.

She put two fingers against the ash shaft just above the iron band.

Tapped it once.

"Wood."

"Advanced finding."

"Shut up."

She stood.

Her eyes were wet.

I looked away immediately.

Wrong choice.

Mara caught my chin with one hand and turned my face back.

"No."

"What?"

"You don't get to pretend I am not having a reaction because you don't like it."

My jaw tightened.

"I'm not pretending."

"You looked at the wall."

"Wall is stable."

"Asshole."

Good.

Better than pity.

I kissed her.

Not because it solved anything.

Because I had wanted to since the doorway.

Mara kissed me back hard enough that the staff became relevant again.

She felt it move.

Pulled back slightly.

"Still not apologizing."

"Learning."

"Dangerous claim."

Good.

We walked to Lower Rook.

Slowly.

Mara carried her own bag.

I refused once.

She ignored me once.

Healthy system.

At the ground-floor room, she stopped.

Looked inside.

Bed.

Table.

Chair with one arm removed.

Hooks for crutches.

Notebook.

Three pieces of wood on the windowsill from Arlo.

My sword on the wall now because Noll had installed hooks low enough for me to reach from the chair.

Mara stepped in.

"This is smaller."

"Maud charges extra."

"Of course."

She put the blue cup on the table.

I stared at it.

"You brought it."

"You sound surprised."

"I thought Halden had laws."

"Only against useful theft."

I sat.

Not because I was tired.

Because Bera's wear limit had twenty minutes left and I did not need Mara watching me get stupid about it.

Mara noticed the clock glance.

"Leg off when?"

"Twenty minutes."

"You schedule it."

"Skin doesn't negotiate."

She smiled slightly.

Then saw the notebook.

"That's Kessa's?"

"Was."

Mara picked it up.

I almost stopped her.

Did not.

She flipped pages.

Bridge.

Surgery.

Standing.

Room measurements.

Guild criteria.

Socket wear.

Work rates.

Havel brace sketches she had sent.

D730 written in the margin of four different pages.

Mara went quiet.

"What?" I asked.

"Nothing."

"Bad liar."

She closed the notebook.

"You sound like your letters."

"Good?"

"No."

There.

I leaned back.

"Explain."

Mara looked at me.

"That."

"What?"

"Explain."

I waited.

She sat on the bed.

"You ask questions now like every answer is for a report."

"That is vague."

"See?"

Annoying.

"Give me an example."

Mara laughed once without humor.

"I wrote you that Sera's mother was sick. You wrote back asking whether Havel used rivets or pins on the warehouse foot."

I remembered the letter.

Not the mother.

The hinge.

Fuck.

"I answered later."

"Four days later."

"I had work."

"So did she."

There was no technical answer to that.

I looked at the notebook.

Mara continued.

"I like that you're doing things. I like that you are working. I like the leg."

I looked at her.

"You like the leg."

"It's a good leg."

"Bera would charge you for that opinion."

Mara smiled despite herself.

Then the smile left.

"I don't like feeling like Halden is one of your supply lines."

That one landed.

Hard.

My first instinct was defense.

You sent useful information.

You asked what I needed.

You knew I was trying to get back to work.

All true.

All inadequate.

Old headspace supplied another answer.

Relationship degradation threatens support network.

Fix communication frequency.

Schedule non-task correspondence.

The thought disgusted me enough that I almost laughed.

Mara saw something in my face.

"What?"

"I just tried to optimize you."

Silence.

Then Mara laughed.

Actually laughed.

Hard enough to lean forward.

"Oh my God."

"Not funny."

"Extremely funny."

"I was going to schedule non-work letters."

That made it worse.

She put both hands over her face.

I waited.

Eventually Mara looked up.

"Don't do that."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Now."

She studied me.

"What happened?"

There it was.

Not what happened to the leg.

She knew that.

What happened to me.

I could have said pain.

Anger.

Fear.

All true.

The cleanest answer was uglier.

"I know how to be like this."

Mara went still.

"Like what?"

"Useful when things are bad."

"You were useful before."

"Not like this."

She waited.

I looked at the notebook.

"This is familiar."

"From the things you remember?"

"Yes."

"The old things."

"Yes."

Mara's expression changed.

Not fear exactly.

Recognition of a door she had not known was open.

"Do you like it?"

I answered too quickly.

"Yes."

There.

Honest.

I looked at her.

"I hate why it's here. I like that it works."

Mara nodded slowly.

"That I believe."

The room stayed quiet.

Then she pointed at my left trouser leg.

"Twenty minutes."

Right.

I had forgotten.

"Useful."

"Shut up."

Straps.

Socket.

"No. Bera says this is acceptable after two hours."

"Yes."

"Good."

No pity.

No lesson.

Awkward fit.

Warm shoulder against mine.

"Good."

"Her brother came from West Quay. He's awful."
