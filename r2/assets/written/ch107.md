# D568: No Chase

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Edda hit me with a stick because I moved correctly.

"Again," she said.

I looked at the padded practice blade against my ribs.

"I blocked."

"Then chased."

"One step."

"Bad step."

"It was forward."

"Exactly."

The Guild yard had become offensive in new ways.

Flat packed earth used to feel neutral.

Now I knew where rain collected near the west fence, where the south half sloped just enough to make the peg bite differently, and which practice circle had a shallow rut from years of people pivoting on the same foot.

I wore the field peg.

Good sword stayed on the rack.

Practice blade in hand.

Short force-plane work allowed.

Sella watched from the covered bench with a reassessment sheet on her lap.

I disliked the sheet personally.

Edda did not care.

"Again."

She came from my right this time.

Short spear padded at both ends.

Not fast by her standards.

Fast enough.

I set the peg.

Right foot back.

Sword caught the shaft near the middle.

Plane flashed low at her front foot.

Not enough to trip.

Enough to make the ground feel wrong.

Edda checked the step.

I had an opening.

Old training said take it.

Current body said opening belonged to somebody with two fast exits.

I stayed.

Edda recovered.

Looked at me.

"Better."

"Painful praise."

"Again."

We had been doing this for an hour.

Not fighting.

Testing choices.

Sella's requirements for expanded clearance were irritatingly specific.

Maintain balance after contact.

Withdraw twenty yards over uneven ground without assistance.

Recover from kneeling.

Manage one controlled surprise from either side.

Demonstrate that I would not turn a static support role into pursuit because my ego had acquired legs.

She had not written the last sentence exactly that way.

The meaning survived.

I wanted road work back.

Not all of it.

Known-route escort with prepared camps and wagon support.

More importantly, I wanted the Guild to stop treating any moving fight as categorically outside my body.

So I trained.

Edda came again.

Left this time.

I redirected the spear.

No chase.

She withdrew.

I held position.

"Good."

"I hate this."

"Why?"

"Because it works."

Edda smiled.

"That's most fighting."

No.

Most fighting was discovering what worked after already wanting something prettier.

Close enough.

We moved to the uneven strip behind the yard.

Broken stone embedded in dirt.

Three shallow steps.

One low beam.

Designed for recruits.

Currently designed by my enemies.

I put the practice sword away and took the short staff.

Sella called, "No time target."

"Then why are you holding a watch?"

She covered it with one hand.

Criminal institution.

I moved.

Peg placement mattered more than speed.

Right foot found the next stable surface.

Peg followed to a known point.

Staff only where needed.

The low beam was easiest sideways.

First step down was ugly.

Second clean.

At fourteen yards, Edda threw a padded sack at my head.

I ducked.

The peg slid on loose stone.

My body went left.

Plane hit the ground before my hand did.

Not a shield.

Three-inch force surface against dirt.

Enough to arrest the hip.

I caught myself.

Stood.

Kept moving.

Sella said nothing.

That was good.

Twenty yards.

Turn.

Back.

My socket burned at the rear edge by the time I reached the yard.

I stopped before anyone told me.

Also good.

Edda checked the liner.

"Red."

"I know."

"Done."

"One more."

"No."

I looked at Sella.

She did not look up from the sheet.

"Edda owns training safety."

Of course.

I sat.

Angry.

Not at them.

At skin.

Stupid tissue determining strategic range.

Bera had explained this seventeen times.

Pressure did not become less real because I found it boring.

Edda sat beside me.

"You know what changed?"

"Everything."

"No."

She pointed toward the practice ring.

"Before, you fought like every opening was an invitation."

"That's normal."

"For young men."

"Cruel."

"Now you decide which openings deserve movement."

I looked at her.

"Because moving costs more."

"Yes."

There was no sympathy in it.

Good.

Cost created selection.

Selection could become style.

I hated how useful that thought was.

Sella finally came over.

Paper in hand.

"Prepared-site combat restriction stays."

My jaw tightened.

Then she continued.

"Known-route escort eligible with wagon support, healer access, and declared no-pursuit role."

I looked at her.

"That's road clearance."

"Some road clearance."

"Moving site."

"Known route."

"Combat possible."

"Yes."

There.

Another wall became a door because we described it correctly.

"When?"

Sella sighed.

"You can look at the board now."

I stood too fast.

Edda caught my sleeve.

"Socket."

Right.

I sat again.

Ten minutes.

Skin first.

Campaign discipline.

I hated that it had become natural.

Jorren was waiting at Lower Rook that evening.

He sat at the cookroom table with two bowls and the expression of a man who had been waiting long enough to become furniture.

Mara had once done that outside my room.

Different life.

"Dinner," he said.

I stopped.

Right.

We had planned this.

Three days ago.

I had moved it once because of training.

Then told him tonight definitely.

"I forgot."

Jorren looked at me.

No joke.

Worse.

I put the field peg beside the wall and sat.

"Sorry."

"Training?"

"Yes."

"Good?"

"Road clearance."

His face warmed despite himself.

"Good."

Then flattened again.

"Still forgot."

"Yes."

Noll put bread on the table without being asked.

Dangerous neutrality.

Jorren ate one spoonful.

"You remember Halden every day?"

There it was.

I looked at him.

"Most."

"You remember work."

"Yes."

"You remember socket times."

"I write them."

"Write dinner."

Simple.

Insulting.

Correct.

My first response formed immediately.

Build a better schedule.

Color-code people.

Set fixed windows.

Reduce collisions.

I almost said it.

Then Mara's voice arrived from memory.

Supply line.

I closed my mouth.

Jorren noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing useful."

"That's new."

I laughed.

Good.

We ate cold stew.

Not because the problem was solved.

Because dinner had already waited long enough.
