# Chapter 108: The Distribution

The filtration venture paid me eighteen copper and immediately asked me not to become excited.

Ivena had learned management.

She put the coins in three neat stacks on the workbench while Arlo pretended not to look at them.

"Six, six, six?" I asked.

"No."

"Then why three stacks?"

"Because you keep touching money while I am still explaining it."

I moved my hand back.

Dena laughed from the wash trough.

Pellin did not. He was carrying two wet chamber bodies and had reached the stage of apprenticeship where other people's jokes were less important than not dropping payroll.

The morning meeting had started because Ivena wanted all three owners physically present before the first distribution since Pellin's hire and Dena's wage adjustment.

That sentence contained too many expensive nouns.

I liked it.

Probably dangerous.

Ivena opened the ledger.

"Reserve first."

Arlo made a small suffering noise.

"We know."

"You know emotionally. I want you to know numerically."

She read the balances.

Service reserve healthy after spring replacements.

Charcoal account covered.

Bell Tile firing paid through the next booked shelf.

Pellin's first month paid.

His second-month wage increase already included because he had survived long enough to become more useful and more expensive.

Dena's route-day allowance included.

Cleaning supplies included.

One expected collar order included.

Then the amount left.

Arlo leaned over the ledger.

"Kiln."

Dena said, "No."

Ivena said, "No."

I said nothing quickly enough to deserve credit.

Arlo pointed at me anyway.

"You thought it."

"Thoughts are private property."

"Not in this shop."

Pellin passed behind him with the two chamber bodies.

"What's wrong with Bell Tile?"

Arlo turned as if betrayed by youth.

"Nothing is wrong with Bell Tile."

"Then why buy a kiln?"

Ivena looked at me.

I looked at Dena.

Dena looked delighted.

Arlo closed his eyes.

"Because owning our own firing would give us schedule control."

Pellin nodded.

"Do we need schedule control?"

The question landed harder than mine would have.

Arlo opened his eyes.

"Sometimes."

"Enough to pay for a kiln?"

"Go put those on the green rack."

Pellin went.

Ivena waited until he was out of hearing range.

"I like him."

"You pay him," Arlo said.

"Exactly."

The numbers remained what they had been before Pellin accidentally joined ownership strategy.

Bell Tile overflow still cost less than buying and feeding capacity we did not need every day.

The kiln idea survived.

It simply failed to become today.

Ivena tapped the last line.

"Distribution. Eighteen copper each."

That got my attention back.

My first instinct was to ask whether we should leave more inside.

My second was to distrust the first instinct because businesses were allowed to eventually pay their owners.

"After everything?"

"After everything we currently know about."

"And if something breaks tomorrow?"

"Reserve."

"And if two things break?"

"Then we have a worse meeting."

Reasonable.

Dena wiped both hands before coming over.

"Route cart left wheel is squeaking again."

Ivena did not even blink.

"Maintenance line already includes it."

Dena looked offended.

"I wanted to cause fear."

"Too late."

The business had become harder to frighten with one wheel.

That felt like something.

I did not say what.

I took the eighteen copper.

The coins felt heavier than eighteen copper because I knew too much about where they had come from.

Dye houses.

Tanneries.

Soap makers.

Dena's routes.

Arlo's hands.

Pellin learning not to widen wet throats with his thumbs.

Ivena refusing to let optimism count as cash.

Bell Tile firing shelves somebody else owned.

No single brilliant sale.

No breakthrough week.

Work accumulating until the ledger permitted a small pile of coins to leave.

I put twelve copper into my personal reserve before I had time to invent a better use for it.

Three went to field socks because the old pair had developed a thin place under the heel.

One went to Pell Orst for brace-ring maintenance.

That left two.

That evening I spent them at The Brass Spoon.

Noll came out after his shift while I was finishing the last of a bowl he claimed he had not touched.

"Rich?" he asked.

"Eighteen copper distribution."

He sat across from me.

"That sounds like rich people words."

"It bought socks."

"Less impressive."

"And dinner."

"Whose dinner?"

I looked at the empty second bowl.

Noll looked at it too.

"Business expense?"

"Fraud."

He smiled.

The venture had paid me eighteen copper.

By bedtime, most of it had already become other things.

The twelve in reserve stayed coins.

For now.
