# D598: The Veteran

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The road looked smaller than I remembered.

That was insulting.

Same north-east run toward Doss Ferry.

Same drainage cuts.

Same ugly stand of black pine where Jorren had once insisted a wagon wheel sounded haunted.

Different body.

Different scale.

I rode more.

That changed distance.

Vell had given me the front-right seat on wagon three because it had the best step and the worst view.

"Compromise," he said.

"Hostile seating."

"You wanted known-route work."

"I wanted dignity too."

"Separate contract."

Good merchant.

Jorren walked beside the lead wagon.

Edda had taken second guard because apparently my first road return needed witnesses from every period of my life.

Two Bronze hires handled rear watch.

Young enough that they looked at my Silver tab before the wooden leg.

Then looked at both.

Normal.

I wore the walking leg for travel.

Field peg rode beneath the bench wrapped in cloth with the tool kit.

Bera had made me practice changing it on a wagon before agreeing this was not idiotic.

Twenty minutes under bad conditions.

Too long for emergency.

Fine for camp.

The first day contained nothing worthy of story.

Excellent.

Dust.

Lunch.

One axle squeak.

My socket got hot after six hours because late summer remained committed to skin damage.

I took the leg off at camp and found one red patch along the outer edge.

Not open.

Ilyra's salve remained in my pack because Gold healers apparently infected your inventory permanently.

Jorren sat across the fire.

"Road?"

"Still here."

"You?"

"Also."

"Good."

That was enough.

Second day, rain.

Of course.

Not heavy.

Persistent.

The road became slick rather than flooded.

I moved less on foot and hated every sensible choice.

At noon, one of the Bronze hires, Pell Orin, asked why I had brought two legs.

"One is for walking."

He waited.

"The other?"

"Work."

"What work?"

"Standing badly in one place."

Pell looked disappointed.

People wanted prosthetics to have secrets.

Mostly they had straps.

We reached the old quarry turn before dusk.

There had been nothing dangerous there the last three times Jorren traveled it.

That made it exactly as safe as evidence allowed.

Vell slowed the wagons anyway.

Narrow road.

Stone wall on one side.

Drop to scrub on the other.

Bad place to discover new information.

I was on foot because sitting through the turn limited sight lines.

Staff in left hand.

Sword right.

Walking leg, not peg.

No reason to change yet.

The first rock hit wagon one from above.

Not thrown hard enough to kill.

Hard enough to make the horses jump.

Jorren shouted before it landed.

"High left!"

Everyone moved.

Second rock.

Larger.

Not bandits.

Wrong rhythm.

Something crashed through brush above the stone wall.

Then another.

Two ridgebacks.

Current ridgebacks, not later ones.

Lean creatures about the size of large goats with plated shoulders and foreheads built for breaking roots, fence rails, and apparently caravan discipline.

One had blood along its flank.

Something had driven them downhill.

Important later.

Immediate problem first.

The lead ridgeback jumped the wall.

Bad landing.

Slid into the road between wagon one and two.

Horses panicked.

Pell moved toward it.

"Don't chase!" I said.

He stopped.

Immediately.

That feeling again.

Useful first.

Enjoy later.

"Open right lane. Let it leave."

Jorren already had the lead team pulled left against the wall.

Edda moved the rear horses back two lengths.

Space opened toward the scrub slope.

The ridgeback saw it.

Then wagon two's rear horse kicked at exactly the wrong time.

Animal turned.

Committed toward Pell.

He froze.

Too close for a lecture.

I planted the walking foot.

Bad anchor.

Enough.

Plane low and angled.

The ridgeback hit it with one foreleg.

Not stopped.

Turned.

Momentum passed Pell by a foot.

Edda struck the creature's armored shoulder with the flat of her spearhead and pushed the turn farther.

It went down the open lane and vanished into brush.

Second ridgeback never entered the road.

It scrambled along the wall, found a break thirty yards ahead, and fled downhill after the first.

Silence.

Horses breathing.

Pell staring at me.

"Move," I said.

He moved.

Good.

"Check wagon one team. Jorren, left trace. Edda, rear. Nobody goes uphill."

Everyone did exactly that.

Then I heard myself.

Not loud.

Not panicked.

Old.

The voice had belonged to roads with bodies on them.

Current road had none.

Good.

Keep it that way.

We found the thing that drove the ridgebacks ten minutes later without climbing toward it.

Tracks above the wall.

Deep.

Three-toed.

Bigger predator.

Probably hillcat.

Maybe something else.

Vell looked at the darkening sky.

"Camp here?"

"No," I said.

Too fast.

Everyone looked.

I pointed downroad.

"Open meadow two miles ahead. This turn compresses us against the wall. Whatever pushed those animals may follow scent downhill. We can make the meadow before dark if we move now."

Vell asked Jorren, "Road?"

"Clear enough."

Edda looked uphill.

"Agree."

Vell nodded.

"Move."

No argument.

Gold parties were not the only competent people in the world.

Good reminder.

We reached the meadow before full dark.

No predator followed.

Maybe my caution was unnecessary.

That was the best possible outcome.

At camp Pell brought me water without asking.

I almost resented it automatically.

Then noticed he had brought everyone water.

Different thing.

He sat nearby.

"You knew where it would go."

"No."

"The ridgeback."

"I knew where I wanted it to go."

He frowned.

"Difference?"

"Large."

Edda, across the fire, said, "Listen to that part."

I looked at her.

"Are you teaching through me?"

"Cheap instructor."

Pell asked about the plane.

I showed him with a flat hand.

Angle.

Momentum.

Not magic theory.

Just feet meeting surfaces.

He understood enough.

Later, when everyone else had settled, Jorren sat beside me.

The walking leg was off.

Liner drying near the fire.

My left knee ended under the blanket exactly where it had ended for eight months.

"You sounded old," he said.

There.

No joke.

I looked at the fire.

"I was."

Jorren waited.

"For maybe thirty seconds."

"Bad?"

I thought about Pell stopping when I told him.

The open lane.

No one hurt.

The way every person had become position, speed, role, risk for a few clean breaths.

"No," I said.

Then, because Mara would have hated the first answer alone:

"Not only."

Jorren nodded.

We sat there until the liner dried.

The road remained quiet.

I had missed that too.
