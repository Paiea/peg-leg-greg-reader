# D620: Three Offers

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Mara sent me three futures and told me not to solve them.

Cruel woman.

The letter arrived after breakfast while I was changing from the walking leg to the field peg for a Hearthline warehouse job.

Noll put it on the table.

"Halden."

"I can see that."

"You stop moving."

I looked down.

One strap half-threaded.

Right.

"Private observation."

"Kitchen."

Apparently privacy had never recovered.

I finished the strap before opening the letter.

Campaign discipline.

Small victory.

Mara began with weather.

Halden rain had returned sideways.

Sera had purchased a hat large enough to become local infrastructure.

Pell had finally been removed from the east desk because someone discovered he had been recording river delays under warehouse corrections for eleven months.

Mara claimed this was not vindication because vindication should happen before producing that much paper.

Then:

**Vey gave me my end-term offers.**

There.

I sat.

Not because of the leg.

Current body had fewer excuses for every action now.

**First: renew Halden for another two years. Same office, higher pay, more route authority.**

Expected.

**Second: Northbank. Regional reconciliation desk. Better title. Worse weather. Enough money that Mother would suddenly remember she always believed in shipping.**

Northbank.

Farther west.

Two extra travel days from Carrow in good season.

My mind calculated before permission existed.

I stopped reading long enough to hate myself.

Then continued.

**Third: Carrow. Vey wants someone who understands both offices and apparently I have complained about your city's records so consistently that this qualifies as expertise. East desk lead. Less pay than Northbank. More authority than Halden.**

My chest did something embarrassing.

Carrow.

Mara here.

Current room.

Current work.

No packet delay.

No three-day river calculations.

My brain built a life in under two seconds.

Then Mara stabbed it.

**Do not start.**

I laughed out loud.

Noll looked over.

"Bad?"

"Accurate."

I kept reading.

**I know exactly what your face did at Carrow. I am not choosing a job because you have finally learned how many legs you own.**

Rude.

Fair.

**I also am not refusing Carrow merely to prove independence. That would be equally stupid. I like the work here. I might like Northbank more. I might like coming home. I do not know yet.**

Home.

Which one?

Mara continued before I could assign jurisdiction.

**I have until five days after term close to answer. We still meet on the stone steps before first bell. I am not changing that. I want one morning where neither of us arrives already pretending the next two years are decided.**

There.

The promise had acquired teeth without changing words.

**You may tell me what you want. You may not give me a route analysis unless I specifically ask for one. These are different activities.**

I put the letter down.

Three offers.

My first instinct was obvious.

Carrow.

Best relational access.

Existing support network.

Her mother somewhere nearby enough for visits.

Vey authority increase.

Lower pay offset by lower travel cost between us.

No.

Stop.

I stood.

Field peg under me.

Walked to the window.

Outside, a fish cart blocked half the lane while two men argued about wheel clearance.

Current Carrow.

My city now, apparently.

Not necessarily hers.

I had my own offers too.

Hearthline wanted a standing retainer for prepared-site survey work.

Not full party membership.

Better.

They would send notice when a site needed load support and pay a small monthly reserve for priority availability.

Sella had called it "specialist status."

I had called it "money for occasionally doing nothing."

She had called me exhausting.

Kessa had three warehouse and civic jobs booked through autumn.

Bera wanted to build a better walking socket after another month of stable limb shape.

Vell had asked whether I wanted recurring known-route slots.

Carrow was no longer the place where I happened to wake up young.

That complicated everything.

Good.

Real things should.

I went to the Hearthline job.

Did not answer Mara immediately.

Also progress.

The warehouse had a sagging upper gallery and no monsters.

Venn apologized.

"We try to provide variety."

"Refund."

Daro said, "You are paid by day."

"Then add monster at fifth bell."

Nesha looked toward the roof.

"Could drop a beam."

"Too predictable."

Gold companionship remained terrible.

We worked until afternoon.

At one point Venn asked whether I could take a four-day ruin survey south next month.

I asked site conditions.

Known approach.

Camp established.

Broken terrain inside.

Prepared retreat lanes.

Possible.

Not automatically yes.

I liked that my life had reached the point where good work could conflict with other good work.

For months every decision had been about escaping incapacity.

Now there were too many roads again.

That night I answered Mara.

I started three times.

First version became analysis.

Burned it.

Second became noble nonsense about choosing whatever made her happy.

Burned that too.

The third survived.

**Mara,**

**I want Carrow.**

I stared at the sentence.

Too simple.

Good.

Continued.

**Not because it is objectively best. I have already constructed that argument and you were right to forbid it. I want you here because I want mornings that do not require packet schedules. I want you to know Noll's current soup before it changes again. I want to complain about your work while you are physically available to tell me I misunderstood it. I want your coat on the second chair often enough that it becomes annoying.**

There.

Want.

No model.

Harder than logistics.

**I also do not want you here if six months later you look at me and realize you took the smaller life because I made distance unpleasant. I know those two statements conflict. Keep both.**

Then I wrote about my work.

Not because it affected her decision.

Because it was my life.

Hearthline retainer.

Road clearance.

Second leg.

Edda hitting me with a stick.

I did not mention the artifact idea because no artifact existed.

Good.

At the bottom:

**See you at first bell. Do not be early.**

I almost added the days remaining.

Did not.

Folded the letter.

Noll appeared in the doorway carrying laundry.

"You done?"

"Yes."

"Good?"

I looked at the burned drafts in the basin.

"Expensive."

He nodded like that made sense.

Maybe it did.
