# Chapter 109: The Moving Load

Neris made me walk while holding the plane under a suspended crate.

This was rude in several dimensions.

The crate hung from a timber gantry behind East Annex, low enough that failure would bruise pride before property. Two porters held side ropes. Neris had packed the bottom with sand instead of anything somebody would invoice us for.

"You are not supporting the crate," she said.

"I know."

"Say it."

I looked at the near right corner.

"I am supporting one corner against downward drift while the porters own the load."

"Better."

One porter, a square-faced man named Hess, adjusted his grip.

"Do I get to complain if he drops it?"

"Yes," Neris said.

"Before or after?"

"Dealer's choice."

I liked him immediately.

Neris marked a chalk line on the paving.

Three steps.

That was all.

The plane itself was tiny.

One inch.

Enough surface under the corner to matter if it stayed where I wanted it.

The problem was the staying.

"Call first," Neris said.

"RIGHT CORNER."

Hess heard me and shifted his grip slightly outward.

I formed.

The familiar pressure settled behind my forehead.

Small.

Stable.

"Move."

We took one step.

The crate moved.

My plane did not.

The corner slid half off it before I reacted.

"GONE."

I released.

Hess caught the extra weight.

"That felt stupid," he said.

"Accurate."

Neris pointed back to the line.

"Again."

Second attempt, I tried to drag the plane after me.

It disappeared entirely.

Third, I moved it too fast and created a new plane ahead of the old one.

Neris tapped my shoulder.

"That is rebuilding."

"It moved."

"You cheated."

"Magic has rules about academic integrity now?"

"I do."

Hess coughed into his sleeve.

Probably laughing.

Fourth attempt got two steps before the support lagged.

Fifth got three steps and left me holding my breath hard enough that Neris stopped the exercise before the crate did.

"Release."

I released.

"I had it."

"You had blue lips."

"Exaggeration."

Hess looked at me.

"Not much."

Traitor.

We sat for five minutes.

Neris did not fill the silence with theory.

She made me drink water.

That was worse because theory could be argued with.

When the pressure behind my eyes eased, she crouched beside the chalk line.

"What are you actually moving?"

"The plane."

"Wrong answer."

I stared at the paving.

Old Greg could alter support geometry under a running fighter without thinking about one inch.

Current Greg had spent months learning to form small, release cleanly, and stop before reserve became injury.

Moving a support under load meant maintaining the relationship between the plane, the object, and my own changing position instead of treating the first successful shape as sacred.

"Relationship," I said.

Neris shrugged.

"Close enough to try again."

Sixth attempt failed on the first step.

Excellent teaching.

Seventh lasted two.

Eighth got three clean enough that Hess said nothing until we stopped.

"That one felt normal."

I looked at Neris.

She was already resetting the chalk.

"Again."

Ninth failed because I enjoyed the eighth.

Tenth worked.

Three steps.

Right corner supported.

No lag.

No sudden rebuild.

I called the release.

"GONE."

The porters settled the crate onto its blocks.

The pressure behind my forehead had tightened, but not crossed into pain.

Neris watched me for a moment.

"Stop."

I stopped.

No argument.

That earned a raised eyebrow.

"Learning," I said.

"Maybe."

Hess rubbed his hands together.

"Are we done being educational furniture?"

Neris handed him two copper from the lesson fee.

"Now you're professional educational furniture."

He considered the coins.

"Better."

I walked home without practicing the movement again.

That may have been the hardest repetition of the day.
