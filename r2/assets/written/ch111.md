# Chapter 111: The Pairing

A client asked for Jorren and me together before either of us knew the job existed.

Sella told us separately.

This was how I learned our informal pair had become client memory.

I arrived at the Guild ten minutes before Jorren and found a Hollen Brick foreman standing at Sella's desk with lime dust on both boots.

He looked at me once.

"Fire closeout."

"Greg."

"I know."

Apparently my name remained less memorable than administrative inconvenience.

Sella slid the work sheet toward me but kept one finger on it.

"Do not accept until the second requested Bronze is here. Client asked for both."

That was new.

"Both who?"

"You and Jorren."

The foreman nodded.

"Culvert man and fire closeout."

I considered objecting to the titles.

Then Jorren came through the door.

The foreman pointed.

"Culvert man."

Jorren looked at me.

"What did you do?"

"Apparently paperwork."

Sella tapped the contract.

"Hollen Brick. Three carts of bagged lime and covered barrels to the south kiln yard. Three-day expected run. Two Bronze escorts."

"Threat?" Jorren asked.

The foreman answered.

"Ditch thieves took part of a load last spring."

"Same route?"

"Mostly."

"Same season?"

"No."

"Current reports?"

"Nothing confirmed."

Jorren looked at Sella.

She gave a tiny nod.

The threat note was history, not prophecy.

I read the rest.

Cargo loss clause.

Delay clause.

Wheel and animal trouble remained client responsibility unless escort negligence caused it.

Meals at destination.

Bunk room if we arrived after dark.

Return by empty cart if space remained, otherwise paid walking day.

"Why us?" I asked.

The foreman looked surprised the question mattered.

"He worked our culvert line last winter."

He pointed at Jorren.

"Didn't pretend the road was better than it was."

Then at me.

"You handled that burned-contract closeout with Ressa Dorn's cousin."

I stared.

"I did?"

"Twelve-silver warehouse settlement. East Annex. You told them what was verified and what wasn't."

Right.

A conversation I barely considered work had apparently traveled through a family.

Reputation was becoming difficult to audit.

Jorren picked up the sheet.

"Rate?"

Sella pointed.

We both read it.

Ordinary Bronze field rate with a modest cargo-risk premium.

Jorren looked at me.

"I can."

I checked my own week.

No booked instruction. No filtration meeting that required me. Noll working two of the evenings anyway.

"I can."

"Then yes," Jorren said.

The foreman held out his hand to Sella as if the matter were complete.

She did not give him the form.

"They sign individually."

"Right."

He had the expression of a man discovering Guild procedure one inconvenience at a time.

Jorren signed first.

I signed second.

Nothing happened.

No partnership cord appeared.

No party registration.

Two separate Bronze workers had simply agreed to the same job after a client asked for both names.

Outside, Jorren folded his copy into his coat.

"Fire closeout?"

"Culvert man?"

"Better title."

"Debatable."

We walked toward East Market because he needed boot grease and I needed food for three days.

At a dry-goods stall, Jorren bought enough smoked sausage to suggest he had forgotten inns existed.

"Planning for siege?"

"Planning for your appetite after walking."

"That is slander."

"Evidence."

I bought dried apples and another pair of cheap sock liners.

The Hollen carts left at first bell next morning.

Three days, if road and wheels agreed.

The client had paired us on paper.

The road would decide whether that meant anything useful.
