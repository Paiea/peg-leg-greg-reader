# D661: Three Breaths

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Harrow let us test Faultglass because greed was stronger than suspicion.

Useful human constant.

The test happened in his warehouse courtyard with six witnesses and one chair I had not requested.

Venn.

Daro.

Nesha.

Harrow.

A private appraiser named Sel Vennick who smelled of cedar oil and distrusted everyone professionally.

Me.

Faultglass sat on folded cloth in the middle of the table.

Still ugly.

Smoke-gray oval.

Blackened copper rim.

No engraving visible until Daro cleaned the inside edge with alcohol and found three tiny grooves beneath the corrosion.

"Contact points," he said.

Harrow looked pleased.

"So it is magical."

Daro looked at him.

"A chamber pot can be magical."

Harrow became less pleased.

Good warder.

Vennick asked me, "You have used one before?"

"Seen this one used."

Later.

Different owner.

Different face behind it.

Iven Marr laughing while a gate tried to become firewood.

"How?"

I pointed at the inner rim.

"Mana through the frame. Low. Don't push until the glass responds."

Daro looked at me.

"You sure?"

No.

"Enough to start."

Vennick did not like that answer.

Neither did I.

Daro tested first.

Not looking through it.

Mana into one groove.

Nothing.

Second.

Nothing.

All three together.

The glass cleared.

Not transparent.

Clearer.

Smoke pulled toward the edge like breath leaving cold glass.

My stomach tightened.

There.

Faultglass.

Current.

Not memory.

Harrow whispered, "Oh."

Bad sound from an owner.

Daro released mana.

Smoke returned.

"Stable response," he said. "Low draw. No obvious external field."

Vennick wrote something.

I hated him.

Not personally.

Economically.

"Viewing test," Daro said.

Everyone looked at me.

Of course.

I sat.

Not because I needed to.

Because if vision went wrong, falling would be stupid.

Faultglass felt cold between my fingers.

Heavier than it looked.

I held it over my left eye.

Right eye closed.

Nothing.

Smoke-gray world.

Harrow's table distorted slightly at the edges.

I fed mana into the three grooves.

Low.

The glass cleared.

The courtyard disappeared.

No.

Stayed.

Changed.

The table became lines.

Not drawn lines.

Pressure.

Four legs carrying weight upward through joints.

A bright band where one crosspiece had warped and the joint compensated.

Stone beneath showed pale branching stress around an old crack I had not noticed.

The warehouse wall behind Venn glowed along the lintel over the loading door.

Not weak.

Loaded.

Important difference.

I moved the glass.

My own field peg lit from iron tip through shaft into socket.

A hard bright point along the rear edge where Bera had already told me pressure concentrated after long standing.

I knew that.

Faultglass did not.

It showed anyway.

My pulse accelerated.

One breath.

Daro raised one hand and formed the smallest diagnostic ward line he could manage.

The world exploded blue-white.

I flinched.

Not color exactly.

Intensity.

The ward line showed as a twisted strand with tension gathered at both ends and a softer middle.

I could see where Daro was holding it.

Not his mana reserve.

Not his spell formula.

Where the structure argued with itself.

Two breaths.

I looked at Venn.

Nothing unusual.

Then he put one hand on the table and shifted weight forward.

Shoulder line brightened.

Knee.

Wrist.

Physical load.

No hidden soul.

Good.

Three breaths.

I released mana.

The courtyard came back wrong.

Harrow's red coat looked brown.

The sky had almost no blue.

Daro's face carried a bright false line across one cheek where the ward tension had been.

I blinked.

Still there.

"Color?" Venn asked.

"Reduced."

"Afterimage?"

"Yes."

Harrow leaned closer.

"What did you see?"

I ignored him.

"Time," I said.

Nesha had a watch.

"Three breaths."

"Recovery."

She understood.

We waited.

Thirty breaths before the red coat became red again.

Longer for the false line on Daro's face to disappear.

Vennick wrote faster.

I hated him more.

Second test.

Five breaths.

Daro put a ward plate on the table.

Inactive first.

Faultglass showed physical stress around two rivets.

Then he fed charge into it.

The entire plate became a web.

One corner carried almost twice the strain of the others.

"Lower left anchor," I said.

Daro stopped.

Checked without the lens.

"Yes."

Vennick looked at me.

"You knew that before?"

"No."

Harrow made another bad sound.

I kept looking.

Six breaths.

Daro increased charge slightly.

The web sharpened.

I could see exactly where a force plane would reduce stress without fighting the whole structure.

Not guess.

See.

Seven breaths.

Pain began behind my left eye.

Not severe.

Useful threshold.

Eight.

A black spot appeared near the bottom of the glass.

I released immediately.

The black spot stayed.

Shit.

I closed both eyes.

Still there.

"Greg?" Venn said.

"Blind spot. Small. Left eye."

Ilyra was not present.

For once.

Daro crouched in front of me.

"Can you see my hand?"

"Yes."

"How many?"

"Three fingers."

"Two."

I opened my right eye.

Two.

The blind spot sat directly over one.

Harrow stopped looking pleased.

Good.

Nesha started timing.

The spot faded after perhaps two minutes.

Headache remained.

Color took longer this time.

Venn said, "Done."

Harrow said, "One more."

Venn looked at him.

No rank needed.

"Done."

Harrow shut up.

I wanted one more too.

That bothered me more.

Faultglass sat on the cloth.

I could already imagine work with it.

Ward plates.

Bridge anchors.

Armor.

Monster joints under load.

Force-plane placement precise enough to waste almost nothing.

A hundred problems became easier.

A hundred new ways to injure my eye appeared beside them.

Equation changed.

Exactly as remembered.

Vennick finally spoke.

"This is not survey glass."

Excellent contribution.

"No," Daro said.

"Military value. Engineering. Ward inspection. Perhaps medical if physical strain is visible."

Harrow's face changed with every word.

Price climbing behind the eyes.

My honesty had become expensive in real time.

Vennick asked Harrow for provenance records.

Harrow suddenly remembered where they were.

Of course.

I stood carefully.

Head still hurt.

Nesha handed me my staff.

"Want it more?" she asked quietly.

"Yes."

"Badly?"

"Worse."

She nodded.

"Also good to know."

Harrow came back twenty minutes later with a ledger and an entirely new personality.

Faultglass was no longer dead glass from a widow's survey box.

It was **an object requiring proper valuation**.

I asked again if he would sell.

He smiled.

"Not until I know what I own."

Reasonable.

I hated him for it anyway.

Outside the warehouse, Daro walked beside me.

"You changed the future price."

I stopped.

He did not know what the sentence meant to me.

Probably meant only that I had demonstrated value.

Still.

"Yes."

Iven Marr had paid fourteen copper.

That future was dead now.

Because I had recognized the lens.

Because I had been honest enough to test it.

Because knowledge changed markets merely by existing.

Daro asked, "Worth it?"

My left eye still ached.

I looked back at Harrow's warehouse.

"Ask me when I know who ends up owning it."

That answer frightened me slightly.

Not enough.
