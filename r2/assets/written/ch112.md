# D676: The Buyer

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Faultglass became unaffordable in twelve days.

Efficient market.

Harrow sent the valuation through Sella because apparently humiliating numbers needed institutional stationery.

I read it at the Guild counter.

Then read it again.

"No," I said.

Sella looked up from another contract.

"That is generally how prices work."

"This is not a price."

"It has a number."

"So do prison sentences."

She held out her hand.

I gave her the paper.

The appraiser's estimate was broad because no one knew Faultglass provenance, age, upper limits, or whether repeated use caused permanent damage.

That uncertainty had not made it cheap.

It had made rich people interested.

Engineering houses.

Two private ward firms.

A military procurement clerk from Northbank.

One collector in the capital who apparently purchased dangerous objects specifically because other people called them dangerous.

Harrow planned a closed auction in twenty-six days.

My entire current savings would not reach the minimum bid.

Not close.

Gold pay had taught me the wrong kind of optimism.

"Loan?" Sella asked.

"Against what?"

"Future contracts."

I did the arithmetic.

Three years of aggressive work if nothing went wrong.

Longer if something did.

My left knee supplied commentary by existing.

"No."

"Good."

I looked at her.

"You asked."

"Wanted to hear you say it."

Hostile finance.

I had already tried Harrow directly.

Cash plus future-use percentage.

Rejected.

Cash plus exclusive demonstrations for one year.

Rejected.

Hearthline partnership bid.

Venn had listened, then said they did not need Faultglass enough to buy it for me.

Correct.

Annoying.

Daro offered his entire current savings without prompting.

Not enough to matter.

I refused anyway.

Bera asked what the lens had to do with my leg.

"Nothing."

"Then good."

"Why good?"

"Means you want something besides fixing yourself."

I had no answer ready.

That irritated me for hours.

Now Sella handed the valuation back.

"Auction isn't today."

"Twenty-six days."

"You counted."

"Paper has date."

"Greg."

I folded it.

"I'm not doing anything."

"I didn't ask."

Worse.

Outside, Nesha sat on the Guild steps eating an apple.

Gold scouts developed terrible timing professionally.

"You heard," I said.

"Everyone heard. Harrow talks when money happens."

"Bad habit."

"Useful one."

I sat two steps below her.

Walking leg today.

No work.

Rest day according to Bera, which meant I had been to the Guild, fitting shop, market, and nowhere else officially.

Nesha bit the apple.

"You going to steal it?"

Direct woman.

"No."

She chewed.

"That answer arrived too fast."

"You asked a stupid question."

"I asked because you already mapped that warehouse with your face."

I looked at her.

She smiled slightly.

"Scout."

Right.

"I mapped exits because I map exits."

"You also asked which nights Harrow keeps private watch."

"Contract safety."

"You asked after contract ended."

Damn.

I looked toward the street.

Cart traffic.

Two Guild recruits carrying practice spears badly.

Woman selling pears.

Normal city.

"I know what it becomes," I said.

Nesha stopped eating.

There.

Not everything.

Enough.

"The glass?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"I knew the person who owned it later."

She waited.

No disbelief.

People around me had developed expensive tolerance.

"He bought it from Harrow's estate for fourteen copper."

Nesha looked at me.

Then laughed once.

Not happy.

"And you told Harrow it was valuable."

"Yes."

"That's funny."

"No."

"Very."

I hated her.

"So now," she said, "your secret future bargain is going to auction because you couldn't keep your mouth shut."

"Accurate."

"And you think that makes it yours somehow."

"I did not say that."

"Your face did."

Everyone had become Mara.

I looked at the valuation paper.

"If I had said nothing, Marr gets it later."

"Marr?"

"Future owner."

"Good person?"

I thought about Iven Marr.

Three children.

Wife who hated me.

A-ranker eventually.

Saved people.

Cheated at cards badly.

Once left me under a collapsed stair because he correctly judged I could free myself and incorrectly judged I would forgive him.

"Mostly."

"Does he need it?"

I almost answered yes.

Future history wanted yes.

But the question was wrong.

"It made him better at what he did."

"Same for you."

"Yes."

Nesha finished the apple.

"So either you take it from Harrow, or some rich bastard buys it, or maybe future Marr never gets it because you changed the sale."

"Yes."

"Messy."

"Yes."

She stood.

Dropped the apple core into a refuse bucket from six feet away.

Missed.

Gold scout.

She picked it up.

Better.

"If you steal it," she said, "don't tell yourself you rescued it from the wrong owner."

I looked at her.

"Harrow doesn't know what it is."

"He knows enough now."

"He can't use it."

"Not ownership."

"He acquired the whole box from a widow who probably didn't know either."

"Still not ownership."

Annoying moral architecture.

"I know."

Nesha studied me.

"Good."

She left.

I stayed on the Guild steps.

Twenty-six days.

The auction did not create a deadline for me.

It created a deadline for everyone.

That distinction mattered legally.

Not emotionally.

At home, I opened the campaign notebook.

Faultglass page.

Under **NOT MINE** and **YET?** I added four headings.

**BUY.**

Impossible at current valuation.

**BORROW.**

Harrow refused unsupervised use.

**PARTNER.**

No buyer had reason to give me control after purchase.

**TAKE.**

I stopped.

The word sat there.

No euphemism.

No recover.

No redirect.

Take.

My old life had contained operations where ownership was decided by whether the other side could stop you.

Wars made terrible teachers because sometimes the lesson worked.

I closed the notebook.

Twenty-six days.

I had time to become a better person.

I did not find that reassuring.
