# D695: The Thief

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I stole Faultglass on Thirdday.

The sentence remained ugly no matter how many times I tested alternatives.

Acquired.

Redirected.

Removed from auction.

Preserved.

Bullshit.

Stole.

Better.

The actual act was embarrassingly small compared with the planning.

Harrow's auction people asked Hearthline to verify the final object list because the bidders wanted the dangerous items separated from the merely expensive ones.

I was there because Faultglass was on the dangerous list and I was the only person in Carrow who had looked through it long enough to describe the side effects.

Professional access.

Legal reason to be near it.

Illegal intention.

No heroic obstacle appeared to save me from the choice.

At the end of the inspection, Faultglass should have gone back into Harrow's custody.

It did not.

When I left the building, it was mine in every practical sense and his in every legal one.

That was all.

No alarm. No chase. No guard catching my sleeve so I could tell myself the theft became exciting enough to justify itself.

I walked three streets with the walking leg, short staff, tool bag, and somebody else's artifact hidden among my ordinary work things.

My heart beat harder than it had during the ridgeback attack.

Stupid body.

At the fourth street, Nesha stepped out of a bakery carrying two rolls.

Of course.

"Greg."

I stopped.

She looked at my face.

Then the tool bag.

No supernatural scout power. Just pattern recognition and the fact that I apparently committed crimes with my eyebrows.

"You did it."

Not a question.

"Yes."

Nesha closed her eyes.

"Fuck."

"Accurate."

"Give it back."

Simple route.

Still available.

For perhaps ten seconds the world offered me an exit.

Return. Apologize badly. Lose Hearthline trust partially instead of completely. Maybe Harrow keeps the matter private to protect the auction. Maybe not.

Still.

Exit.

I did not take it.

"No."

Nesha stared.

"Why?"

I had answers.

Too many.

Harrow did not understand it. The auction buyer might lock it away. Northbank military might turn it into a classified tool. I could save lives with it. I could build better structures. I could become more useful. Future Marr would have owned it anyway.

All true enough to become dangerous.

"Because I want it," I said.

Nesha's face changed.

Not softer.

Cleaner.

"Better answer."

"Not good one."

"No."

She held out one roll.

I looked at it.

"What?"

"Bought two before learning you're an idiot. Still have two."

I took it.

Crime lunch.

We stood beside the bakery wall eating bread.

Normal street.

Nesha asked, "Anyone else know?"

"No."

"Daro will guess."

"Probably."

"Venn?"

"Eventually."

"Harrow will accuse workers."

I stopped chewing.

That route had been in my planning.

Abstractly.

Staff questioned. Auction handlers. Warehouse clerks. Professional access pool.

The phrase in my notebook had been **investigation surface**.

People had names when Nesha said it.

I swallowed.

"If he blames someone specific, I stop it."

"How?"

"I don't know yet."

"Return glass."

"Other method."

Nesha laughed without humor.

"There he is."

I hated that.

"I'm not letting someone else take punishment."

"But you're fine making them afraid until then."

That landed.

I looked at the bread.

"Yes."

Nesha studied me.

"You are very strange right now."

"Right now?"

"More."

Fair.

"Are you telling Venn?" I asked.

"I'm not lying to him."

"Not what I asked."

"I know."

Scout.

She finished the roll.

"You get one day from me."

"For what?"

"To decide whether you are going to make this everyone's problem."

"It already is."

"Good. You noticed."

Then she left.

I went home.

Noll was at Brass Spoon. Ground room empty.

I locked the door.

Put the tool bag on the table.

Opened it.

Faultglass lay wrapped in plain cloth beside a wrench.

Still ugly.

Still impossible.

Mine only because I had taken it.

I held it in my palm.

Nothing happened.

Artifacts did not care about morality apparently.

Convenient design.

I fed a thread of mana into the copper rim without looking through it.

Smoke pulled toward the edge.

Stopped.

Current.

Real.

I released.

Then put it down.

No testing alone.

Not because stealing created safety discipline.

Because blindness remained tactically stupid.

I opened Kessa's notebook.

The Faultglass page waited.

**NOT MINE.**

**YET?**

Below that:

**BUY.**

**BORROW.**

**PARTNER.**

**TAKE.**

I crossed out nothing.

Under **TAKE** I wrote:

**Done. D695. Harrow. No permission. Theft.**

No future historian required.

I sat back.

My left phantom heel started aching.

That happened when I was tired. Or stressed. Or for no reason because the nervous system was an amateur institution.

I rubbed the end of the limb through the liner.

Faultglass sat six inches away.

Iven Marr would not buy it for fourteen copper now.

Maybe he would never own it.

Maybe some future wall would fall because of me.

Maybe a different one would stay standing.

Future knowledge had always tempted me to treat history like inventory.

Take the useful piece. Prevent the expensive mistake. Move the right person. Avoid the wrong road.

The world had rarely cooperated.

This time I had stopped asking.

That was what scared me.

Not enough to return it.

I wrapped the glass again...
