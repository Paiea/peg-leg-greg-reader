# D706: The Student

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Faultglass taught me that my best plane was badly made.

Rude artifact.

I tested in the ground room because everyone qualified to supervise me was currently angry.

This was not ideal.

So I made it boring.

No active ward.

No unstable structure.

No load heavier than the table.

Door open.

Noll in the cookroom with instructions to interrupt if I stopped answering.

He accepted this responsibility by saying, "Don't die before dinner."

Professional support.

I put the field peg on because standing geometry mattered.

Faultglass in left hand.

Right hand free.

First test was the small four-inch plane Meral had once bullied into existence beneath a marked cord.

Old shape.

Old lesson.

Plane. Direction. Load.

I formed it against the table leg.

No lens.

Stable.

Familiar.

Then Faultglass.

Mana through the three grooves.

Smoke cleared.

The plane appeared.

I nearly dropped the lens.

Not because the plane looked weak.

Because it looked wrong.

The center carried almost nothing.

Stress burned along the outer edges where my shaping curled back toward itself.

I had spent months making something that felt flat and behaved like a frame.

Useful frame.

Still.

"Fuck."

Noll called, "Alive?"

"Annoyed."

"Good."

I released after three breaths.

Color washed slightly.

No blind spot.

Waited until normal.

Notebook.

Sketch.

Second test.

Same plane, lower pressure.

Same edge loading.

Third.

Wider plane.

Worse.

The larger I made it, the more mana I wasted maintaining edges that carried stress the center could have shared.

Meral had told me this in different language almost a year and a half ago.

I had understood enough to improve.

Not enough to see the mechanism.

Faultglass did not give answers.

It insulted assumptions with evidence.

Excellent tool.

I tried changing the plane.

Not flat.

Barely curved.

Pressure distributed inward.

Faultglass showed less edge flare.

More center load.

I laughed.

Noll appeared in the doorway holding a spoon.

"Bad?"

"Very good."

He looked at the lens.

"Stolen good?"

I froze.

Noll pointed at my face.

"Everyone knows."

Apparently Lower Rook had developed criminal intelligence.

"Not everyone."

"Maud knows."

Fuck.

"How?"

"You hide things under floor. Maud owns floor."

Strong landlord.

"Did she take it?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Said not her crime."

Institutional boundaries remained inspiring.

Noll looked through the open doorway at my setup.

"You returning?"

"Maybe."

He shrugged.

"Dinner soon."

Then left.

No moral lecture.

Worse somehow.

I went back to work.

Fourth test.

Curved plane.

Feathered edges.

That was harder.

My magic wanted borders.

Inside.

Outside.

Plane exists here, not there.

I tried thinning force gradually over the last finger-width.

First attempt collapsed.

Second folded.

Third held for one breath.

Faultglass showed the edge stress fall by almost half.

My mana draw dropped with it.

There.

Real leverage.

Not more reserve.

Better geometry.

I sat down because the room had started losing blue.

Six total viewing breaths across several tests.

Headache beginning.

No need to prove the artifact's downside again.

I waited twenty minutes.

Ate bread.

Drank water.

Did not look through Faultglass.

Then repeated the curved plane without it.

Failed.

Again.

Held.

Again.

Better.

The artifact was not doing the magic for me.

Good.

It had shown me a mistake I could now learn around.

That changed the ownership equation too.

Dangerously.

If I returned Faultglass tomorrow, the lesson stayed.

If I kept it, there would be more lessons.

How many?

Armor load.

Ward tension.

Bridge arches.

Creature movement.

Sword contact.

My own socket.

People.

No.

Not people generally.

That thought had arrived too quickly.

I picked up the glass again.

Did not activate it.

I remembered Iven Marr at Red Gate.

He had worn Faultglass over one eye and looked at soldiers the same way he looked at stone.

Not cruelly.

Structurally.

Where tension lived.

Where force would go.

Where something broke if pushed.

At fifty-nine I had thought Marr was eccentric.

At nineteen, with one leg and an old commander's brain returning piece by piece, I understood the attraction too well.

The artifact did not make people into systems.

It simply made systems visible.

I was the one who enjoyed that.

Bad distinction.

Important one.

I tested the field peg once.

No magic compensation.

Just standing.

Faultglass showed the iron tip bright against the floor, force climbing the shaft, spreading through the socket, concentrating exactly where Bera said it would.

My body became load paths.

For one breath that was fascinating.

For the second, horrifying.

I released.

Enough.

Notebook.

I wrote:

**Faultglass does not show weakness. It shows strain. Interpretation remains the weapon.**

Then:

**Plane edges waste force. Feathering works. Practice without glass.**

Then, after too long:

**Keeping it would make me better faster.**

Below that:

**Not the same as worth keeping.**

Harrow's ten days ended in three.

Halden waited twenty-four days beyond that.

I looked at the lens.

Then at the curved plane I could now form without it for almost four breaths.

The hole was much shallower than it had been.

That was the problem.

I could finally see things from the edge again.

Including myself.
