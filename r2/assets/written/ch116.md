# D709: The Thief

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I returned Faultglass because keeping it had become bad strategy.

This was not the same as becoming good.

Important distinction.

Harrow received me in the warehouse office at noon.

Nesha came because I asked her to witness the return and because she apparently enjoyed watching criminals become administrative.

Faultglass sat wrapped in the same plain cloth I had used at home.

I put it on Harrow's desk.

Did not slide it toward him dramatically.

Objects did not become more moral through table movement.

Harrow unwrapped it.

Checked the copper rim.

Held it to the window.

"You used it."

"Yes."

His jaw tightened.

"I said undamaged."

"It is."

"That is not your decision."

Fair.

A private appraiser checked next.

Not Vennick.

Different woman with a magnifying loupe and no interest in my existence.

She examined the glass, rim, grooves, surface, edge clouding.

"No visible change," she said.

Harrow looked disappointed.

Not because he wanted damage.

Because anger preferred evidence.

"Mana response?"

"Still present," I said.

The appraiser looked at Harrow.

He nodded.

She activated the rim without viewing through it.

Smoke cleared.

Returned.

"Responsive."

Harrow sat.

I remained standing because the walking leg was comfortable today and because sitting across from him felt like negotiation.

This was not.

"Why return it now?" he asked.

There were moral answers available.

Nesha waited.

I chose the true one.

"Keeping it costs more than I am willing to pay."

Harrow stared.

"My property costs you?"

"Yes."

"Interesting framing."

"Accurate framing."

Nesha made a small sound that meant stop helping yourself.

I ignored her badly.

Harrow folded the cloth over Faultglass.

"You cost me three private guards, a delayed auction, a second appraisal, and twelve days of people assuming my warehouse loses expensive objects."

There.

Money.

Current consequences liked invoices.

He named the amount.

I swore.

Nesha smiled.

Traitor.

"That's robbery."

Harrow looked at the lens.

Then at me.

"Educational sentence."

Fair.

We negotiated anyway.

Of course.

Not over guilt.

Over actual cost.

One guard had already been scheduled for auction week.

Second appraisal would have happened regardless after the first valuation changed.

Auction delay was four days, not twelve.

Warehouse reputation damage was imaginary unless Harrow could produce lost business.

He hated me more with every reduction.

We ended at a number that still hurt.

Three monthly payments.

No interest if paid on time.

Private settlement.

No city complaint.

No Guild theft filing.

Harrow retained the right to tell anyone he wanted that I was an asshole.

Not written.

Apparently common law.

I signed.

Then Harrow did something unexpected.

He asked, "What did you learn?"

I looked at him.

"From the glass."

Collector now.

Not victim only.

Curiosity had survived me.

I considered saying nothing.

He owned it.

He was going to auction it.

Information increased price.

My old brain suggested silence immediately.

Then I thought about the test.

Three breaths.

Eight breaths.

Blind spot.

Some future buyer might stare into an active ward until their eye cooked because Harrow advertised engineering miracle instead of cost.

"It shows strain, not weakness," I said.

Harrow listened.

"Physical and magical. Strongly loaded stable structures can look more intense than weak unloaded ones. Interpretation matters."

He nodded slowly.

"Side effects?"

"Color loss increases with exposure. Afterimages. Headache. Temporary blind spot at eight continuous breaths for me under moderate active ward viewing. Different users may vary."

Nesha looked at me.

No comment.

Harrow wrote it down.

"Anything else?"

There was.

Plane edges.

Feathering.

My own socket.

Marr looking at soldiers like walls.

"It changes what you notice," I said.

Harrow frowned.

"Meaning?"

"After you see strain directly, ordinary sight feels incomplete for a while."

He stopped writing.

That answer had not been in Vennick's appraisal.

Good.

"Dangerous?"

"Potentially."

"Magically?"

"Humanly."

He did not understand.

Neither did I completely.

Enough.

Harrow wrapped the lens again and locked it in the desk safe while I watched.

I did not map the lock.

This required effort.

Outside, Nesha walked with me toward the Guild.

"Feel better?" she asked.

"No."

"Good."

"Everyone enjoys that word incorrectly."

"You returned it."

"Yes."

"Because keeping it threatened work, Guild standing, Harrow complaint, and Halden travel."

"Correct."

"Not because stealing was wrong."

"That was on the list."

"Where?"

I considered lying.

"Fifth."

Nesha laughed so hard she stopped walking.

I waited.

"Fuck you," she said.

"Daro already filed that response."

Her laughter ended.

Daro.

Right.

"He know?" I asked.

"Venn told him you were returning it."

"Reaction?"

"He said good. Then said he still doesn't want to see you."

There.

Not fixed.

Good.

People were not contracts that reopened when terms changed.

We reached the Guild steps.

Sella stood in the doorway.

She looked at me.

Then Nesha.

"Returned?"

"Yes."

"Complaint?"

"Private settlement."

"Cost?"

"Private."

Nesha told her.

I stared.

"Scout," she said.

Apparently profession excused betrayal.

Sella winced at the amount.

Excellent.

"Hearthline?" I asked.

Nesha answered.

"Retainer stays canceled for now."

Expected.

Still hurt.

"Prepared contract work?"

"Venn decides case by case after Daro stops wanting to throw you into masonry."

"Fair."

Sella looked suspiciously pleased by the word.

I went home.

The loose floorboard beneath my table held nothing now.

Room felt larger.

Not better.

Just missing an object.

I opened the campaign notebook.

On the Faultglass page, beneath **Done. D695. Harrow. No permission. Theft.** I wrote:

**Returned D709. Private settlement. Hearthline retainer still lost. Daro still angry.**

Then:

**Feathered planes survive without lens.**

That mattered.

Last line:

**Would steal again?**

I stared at it for a long time.

Could not answer.

Left it blank.

Halden in twenty-one days.

That answer could wait too.
