# D721: The Traveler

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I left for Halden nine days early.

This was not romantic.

It was route planning.

Three days normal travel.

Five if river transfer failed.

Six if the west road washed.

Seven if the socket broke skin badly enough to require a rest day.

Nine gave me room to be unlucky without turning a two-year promise into a sprint.

S-class thinking had uses outside theft.

Noll packed food as if Halden had not invented eating.

Jorren checked my spare socket twice and pretended he had not.

Bera made me carry both lower legs.

Walking foot for road and city.

Field peg because, in her words, "You will find work by accident if I don't send it."

Cruel accuracy.

The good sword went on my belt.

Short staff strapped to the pack.

Faultglass did not come because Faultglass was not mine.

That still felt strange enough to notice.

Kessa met me at east gate before departure.

No job.

Just there.

"Daro?" I asked.

"Still mad."

"Useful update."

"He says the feathering idea is good."

I looked at her.

"He said that?"

"To me. Not you."

Progress had routing problems.

Kessa handed me a folded page.

Load sketches.

Three variations on tapered plane edges.

Daro's handwriting on one margin.

**center carries more if boundary stops pretending to be wall.**

I laughed.

"He is still mad?"

"Very."

Good.

People could contribute while angry.

Apparently relationships had more settings than solved and broken.

Jorren walked up behind me.

"Wagon's moving."

"I know."

"Then move."

"Goodbye, Kessa."

"Don't steal anything in Halden."

"Unhelpful specificity."

"Mara asked me to say it."

I stopped.

"She knows?"

Kessa smiled.

"Everyone knows."

Fuck.

The wagon left.

Road west.

Not the north-east route I knew better.

Different hills.

Different river branches.

Weather warm enough to make the socket liner unpleasant by noon.

I changed socks twice the first day.

No heroism.

Skin remained an empire with aggressive border policy.

Second day, one wagon wheel sank into a washed shoulder near a culvert.

Nothing dramatic.

Driver stopped before axle damage.

Cargo crew brought boards.

I watched the load shift while they prepared.

Then saw it.

Not with Faultglass.

The memory of Faultglass.

Outer wheel carried too much because the board angle was forcing the wagon weight toward the soft side.

I formed a feathered plane under the axle housing.

Not hard support.

Graduated pressure.

Enough to spread load inward while the team pulled.

Mana draw stayed low.

Edges did not fight me.

Wagon climbed free.

Driver looked at the empty air beneath his axle.

"New trick?"

"Stolen education."

He laughed because he thought I meant metaphor.

Good.

Third day, rain.

Fourth day, river delay.

Of course.

One ferry cable had snapped upstream and traffic backed half a day.

Nine-day buffer lost nothing important.

I sat under an awning eating Noll's last wrapped dumpling while merchants complained about time.

For once I did not.

Mara's term close remained five days away.

Current delay had room.

That felt almost luxurious.

At a roadside inn that night, a child asked whether the wooden foot hurt.

His mother apologized before I answered.

"Not that part," I said.

The child considered.

"Then what hurts?"

"Expensive parts."

He nodded like this explained adulthood.

Reasonable.

Sixth day I reached Halden's outer river road.

Too early by three days.

The city looked smaller than later memory and larger than Mara's letters.

Warehouses along the water.

Glass workshops farther uphill.

Vey marks on three buildings before I found the district map.

I knew future Halden.

Current Halden disagreed in useful ways.

One bridge missing.

One street still called Rope Lane instead of Saint Verren.

The west market roof wood instead of slate.

Present first.

I took a room two districts from Vey.

Not because I intended to hide from Mara.

Because arriving three days early at her office would make the promise stupid.

I sent a note.

**In Halden. Early in the city, not the steps. This distinction is legally important.**

Her answer came that evening.

**You are literally early.**

Then:

**Do not come to the office tomorrow unless you are on fire. I am closing two years of records and Sera has threatened murder. First bell still stands.**

Below that:

**I am glad you're here.**

There.

No optimization required.

I spent the next two days doing almost nothing useful.

This proved harder than losing the ferry delay.

Walked the river.

Had the socket adjusted by a Halden fitter Bera had recommended.

Bought Noll a knife I could not afford because the handle felt correct.

Did not inspect Vey route infrastructure.

Mostly.

At sunset on D729 I stood across the river from the east office.

Stone steps visible through warehouse gaps.

Same place Mara had named two years ago.

I could walk there in twelve minutes.

Maybe ten if stupid.

I went back to the inn.

No reason to spend the promise early.

Tomorrow had waited long enough.
