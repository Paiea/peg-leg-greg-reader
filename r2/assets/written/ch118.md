# D730: First Bell

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Mara was early.

I saw her before she saw me.

Stone steps outside the Vey east office.

Blue cup in one hand. Gray coat. Hair tied back badly enough that two years had apparently failed to improve engineering.

She stood on the third step looking toward the river.

Not first.

Not last.

Third.

I checked the street clock.

Still before first bell.

Unbelievable hypocrisy.

I crossed the last half block at normal pace.

Walking leg today. No staff. Good socket fit after the Halden adjustment.

Sword left at the inn because arriving armed for a relationship deadline felt theatrical even for me.

Mara turned when my wooden heel struck the first stone of the approach.

Her face changed.

I had seen that change before. Carrow packet office. D447 when she came after Brell.

Letters could contain facts.

Bodies remained different evidence.

She looked at my face first.

Then down.

Walking leg. Dark trouser cut normally over the socket now. Leather boot-shaped foot because Bera said city shoes mattered even when toes did not.

Then back up.

"You're late," Mara said.

I looked at the clock.

"You're early."

"I work here."

"Corruption."

"You were in Halden three days."

"Not these steps."

"Legal distinction?"

"Very important."

Mara tried to stay annoyed.

Failed.

I reached her.

For one second neither of us moved.

Then she put the blue cup on the stone wall and hugged me.

Not careful enough to become medical.

Careful enough to know where my balance lived.

I held her.

Her cheek against my chest. My left side taking load differently than it had two years ago.

No plane.

No correction.

Just body.

She pulled back first.

"You got taller."

"Impossible."

"Maybe I'm shorter."

"Vey compresses staff."

"Documented risk."

She kissed me.

Short.

Then again because apparently two-year promises qualified for revision.

The river moved behind her.

Someone inside the Vey office opened shutters.

Work remained rude.

Mara picked up the blue cup.

"Sit?"

I looked at the steps.

No rail. Wide enough. Dry.

"Yes."

We sat on the fourth step because the third belonged to history now.

I lowered myself with one hand on the wall.

Mara watched without pretending not to.

Fine.

People who loved you got some observation rights.

Not unlimited.

Some.

She handed me the cup.

Tea.

Too much sugar.

"Still blue," I said.

"Mother remains wrong."

I drank.

Mara looked at the wooden foot.

"This the normal one?"

"Walking."

"Kessa says you have another."

"Peg."

Mara smiled.

"Actual peg-leg Greg."

I stared at her.

"No."

"Absolutely."

"You traveled too far for this."

"You traveled here."

"Worse."

She laughed.

Then went quiet.

The actual morning arrived underneath the joke.

Two years.

Her Halden term ending behind us.

Three job offers somewhere inside that building.

Carrow waiting east. Northbank farther west.

My own work no longer resembling anything nineteen-year-old Greg had planned before waking with forty years of a dead man's memory.

Mara said, "You came."

Simple.

I looked at her.

"Yes."

No speech.

The promise did not require one.

She rubbed one thumb against the cup rim.

"Did you think you wouldn't?"

Brell.

Hospital ceiling. Tovan touching a blanket a handspan below my knee. Ground-floor room. Crutches. First socket. Guild restriction sheet tied in red. Faultglass under a loose board. Harrow's settlement. Nine-day route buffer.

"Yes," I said.

Mara looked at me.

"When?"

"Brell. Then less every month after."

She nodded.

Did not tell me she always knew I would.

That would have been a lie wearing romance.

"I thought you might decide the promise was stupid," I said.

"It was stupid."

"Cruel."

"Still came."

"Also stupid."

"Probably."

We sat with that.

First bell had not rung yet.

Carts moved on River Street.

A clerk unlocked the office side door behind us and saw Mara sitting outside.

"You coming in?"

"Eventually," she said.

The clerk looked at me.

"Greg?"

I stopped.

"Why does everyone know?"

Mara said, "Two years."

Terrifying institution.

The clerk went inside.

I looked at Mara.

"How much?"

"What?"

"How much does your office know?"

"Enough."

"Artifact?"

Her face changed.

Right.

Kessa had warned me.

Mara put the cup down.

"You stole an artifact."

"Returned."

"That is not the part I said."

"I know."

"From a client."

"Technically Hearthline's client."

Mara stared.

"Stop helping."

Fair.

"Yes."

"Why?"

Same question Nesha asked.

Different person.

Different answer now.

"Because I knew what it was going to become."

Mara waited.

I told her enough.

Iven Marr. The fourteen-copper estate story. Stress lines. Ward strain. Blind spot. Auction. Theft. Daro. Hearthline retainer. Return. Settlement. Feathered planes.

Mara listened without interrupting.

Then:

"Would you steal it again?"

The notebook question.

Still blank.

I looked at the river.

"Maybe."

Mara exhaled.

Not pleased.

Not surprised either.

"That's a terrible answer."

"It's the true one."

"I know."

She picked up the cup again.

"I think that's worse."

"Probably."

"Do you regret it?"

"Parts."

"Daro?"

"Yes."

"Harrow?"

I thought.

"Less."

Mara closed her eyes.

"Greg."

"You asked."

"I know."

She leaned her shoulder against mine.

Not forgiveness.

Contact.

"Kessa says you're scary at work now," she said.

"Kessa exaggerates."

"Nesha says you like telling people where to stand."

"Nesha is unemployed socially."

"Jorren says you sounded sixty on the road."

"Fifty-nine."

Mara looked at me sharply.

Right.

That number still belonged to a conversation we had not fully had.

Too late to retrieve.

"You know what I mean," I said.

"Unfortunately."

She studied my face.

"Do you like being him again?"

Old Greg.

S-class.

Command rooms. Fast decisions. People stopping when I spoke. Problems becoming geometry.

"Sometimes."

"Do you hate it?"

"Sometimes."

"Useful?"

"Very."

"Dangerous?"

"Also."

Mara nodded.

"Sounds like you."

I laughed.

Not because it was funny.

Because I had spent months asking whether the old headspace was a tool, wound, weapon, mistake, survival habit, or identity.

Mara had apparently filed it under Greg.

Terrible taxonomy.

Maybe correct.

The office clock inside began winding toward first bell.

Mara looked toward the door.

"I have to close the term ledger before Sera murders me."

"Important profession."

"Very."

I knew what came next.

Three offers.

Her decision.

I wanted to ask immediately.

Carrow?

Northbank?

Halden?

Probability had been chewing the question for months.

I waited.

Mara noticed.

Of course.

"You're not asking."

"You said after term close."

"I said I have five days."

"Worse."

"You hate this."

"Immensely."

She smiled.

Then turned serious.

"Do you still want Carrow?"

"Yes."

No analysis.

"Why?"

"Because you're there if you choose it. And because I want you there. Not because it wins a table."

Mara waited.

"What if Northbank?"

I hated the answer before speaking it.

"Then I will hate Northbank."

Mara laughed.

"Greg."

"You asked what I want."

"And after that?"

Actual question.

I looked at the wooden foot resting one step below my right boot.

Two years ago I would have answered with road times.

One year ago I would have built a campaign.

Six months ago I would have started redesigning my work around the route before Mara finished asking.

All of those instincts still existed.

"Then we figure out what Northbank actually costs," I said. "Not before you choose it. After."

Mara looked at me for a long moment.

"That's almost healthy."

"Take it back."

"No."

First bell rang.

One clear strike from inside the office.

Two years.

Nothing magical happened.

No rank. No artifact. No dead future correcting itself.

Mara stood.

I stood with her.

Hand on the wall. Right foot. Wooden left.

Balanced.

She did not help.

Did not pretend not to watch.

At the door she stopped.

"Breakfast after second bell?"

"I was promised one morning."

"This is one morning."

"Vey accounting."

"Exactly."

She kissed me again.

Then went inside.

The blue cup went with her.

I stayed on the stone steps.

Daro was still mad. Harrow still had two payments coming. Hearthline had not magically forgiven me. My walking leg fit well today, and the field peg waited at the inn.

Mara still had a decision to make.

I had fewer certainties than I wanted and more life than I knew what to do with.

Much better than a hole.

I sat back down to wait for second bell.

Not early this time...
