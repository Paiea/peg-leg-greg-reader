# Candidate Chapter 122: Quarry Bell

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

The quarry foreman had blood on his sleeve that was not his.

I noticed that before the hole.

Old habit.

People first when something had already gone wrong.

The hole came second.

Large enough for a wagon if the wagon had terrible judgment.

Fresh limestone had broken inward across the west face of the quarry. White dust still hung in the low evening air, thin enough now to see through but thick enough to taste.

Behind the break sat masonry.

Dark stone.

Worked stone.

Very old stone where the quarry map said there should have been another forty feet of limestone.

Lanterns had been set in a half-circle twenty feet back from the opening.

Nobody stood inside the line.

That was either a good sign or a very bad one.

The foreman pointed toward the dark.

"Three went in."

His name was Arven. I learned this because the Guild clerk beside him kept saying it every time he tried to walk closer to the opening.

"Why?" I asked.

Arven's jaw tightened.

Wrong question emotionally.

Correct question professionally.

"After the blast, we heard shouting," he said. "Thought somebody got caught behind the face. Fenn, Haro, Lis went in."

"Quarry crew?"

"All three."

"Did anyone see who was shouting?"

"No."

"Hear words?"

Arven shook his head.

"Sounded like men. Then stopped."

"And the three didn't come back."

"No."

He looked toward the opening.

The blood on his sleeve had dried brown at the edge.

"That from one of them?"

Arven looked down as if he had forgotten it was there.

"Mills. Cut his scalp when everyone ran after the second noise. He's fine."

Second noise.

I looked at the Guild clerk.

She was maybe thirty, gray coat, Bronze clasp, ink on both hands. A folding field table beside her held three quarry surveys under stones, two sealed note tubes, and a cup of tea nobody had touched recently.

"Second noise?"

"Tessa Mar," she said. "Halden works desk."

"Greg."

"I know."

Concerning trend.

Tessa pointed at the surveys.

"Blast at fourth bell. Hollow opens. Workers hear voices. Three enter before site control is established. About twelve minutes later we hear one low impact from inside. Not collapse. Not explosion. The ground here vibrates once."

"And everyone ran."

Arven said, "Wouldn't you?"

"Probably."

Honesty helped sometimes.

Tessa continued.

"Mills caught his head on a wagon brace. No other injuries. Nobody has entered since."

"Search team?"

"Waiting on Red Quay."

"How long?"

"Any minute."

That improved my opinion of Halden considerably.

Nobody had sent the convenient Silver stranger into the mysterious hole just because he arrived first.

"Why am I here?"

Tessa pushed the top survey toward me.

"Because I need to know whether the exposed face stays exposed long enough for the search team to work."

The quarry plan showed a solid west face.

Second survey, same.

Third, older by nine years, same.

"No void markers?"

"None."

"Old city map?"

"Requested."

"Ward survey?"

Tessa's mouth flattened.

"Reader came. Said there were dormant lines. Then said the blast damaged them. Then said perhaps there were no lines. Then asked whether the hazard supplement applied."

"Experienced."

Tessa almost smiled.

"We sent him home."

I looked at the opening again.

The fresh break was rough around the edges, but the dark masonry behind it was too regular.

Narrow vertical joints.

One curved rib rising into unbroken limestone.

Built first.

Buried later.

Or built inside an older cavity and then forgotten.

Neither option improved the evening.

"No more blasting," I said.

Arven laughed without humor.

"Hadn't planned it."

"No carts close to the face either. Twenty feet minimum."

Tessa made a note.

"Anything else before you inspect?"

I looked down at my walking leg.

I had stopped at the inn on the way.

Field peg hung from my bag.

Sword at my hip.

The fresh liner from the warehouse was still on.

Red skin had cooled but not vanished.

Broken quarry stone would be worse for the walking foot and probably better for the red spot if I stopped trying to correct every uneven step through the ankle.

"I need somewhere to change."

Arven pointed at a supply wagon.

"Behind that."

No reaction.

No question.

Good.

I changed standing with one hand on the wagon rail.

Walking leg off.

Residual limb checked again.

Still red.

No broken skin.

Field socket on.

Strap.

Peg seated.

The field leg made me shorter by a fraction and uglier by a lot.

More important, it turned loose stone into a problem I understood.

When I stepped out, Arven looked at the peg.

"Going in?"

"To the threshold."

"Guild said nobody in."

Tessa answered before I did.

"Threshold isn't in."

I liked her immediately.

The quarry slope down to the break had become powder over broken chips.

Right boot.

Peg.

Staff when the stone shifted.

Three points when I wanted them.

No hurry.

Everyone behind me seemed very interested in whether I would die six feet from the entrance.

I disappointed them.

The air coming from the hole was colder than the quarry.

Wet too.

Not cave wet.

Worked-room wet.

Water that had been touching stone nobody saw for a long time.

I stopped before crossing the old floor line.

There.

A shallow groove ran beneath the dust from one side of the entrance to the other.

Too straight for quarry work.

Part of the original structure.

"Nobody steps on this," I called.

Tessa repeated it immediately to the workers behind her.

No argument.

I crouched and brushed dust from the dark stone with my glove.

Fine cut groove.

Maybe drainage.

Maybe ward channel.

Maybe something else entirely.

I did not touch it with mana.

First the arch.

A cracked lip of limestone rested partly on the old masonry. The blast had removed whatever rock had packed against the outer face and left the old arch carrying load it might not have carried for centuries.

I set the staff against the quarry floor.

Formed a thin feathered plane under the cracked lip.

Minimal lift.

Just enough contact to ask the stone how rude it planned to become.

Pressure came back through the plane.

Then something else.

A second answer half a breath later.

I dropped the cast.

Tessa called, "Problem?"

"Maybe."

"Useful maybe or expensive maybe?"

"Usually the same category."

I changed angle.

Smaller plane against the old arch itself.

Pressure.

Stone.

Then the delayed return again.

Not a pulse from the material.

Load carried somewhere beyond what I was touching, changed, then came back.

A connected frame.

Large enough that the visible entrance was only one piece of it.

I stood.

Arven had come close to the lantern line.

"Well?"

"Face should hold tonight if nobody blasts, drives a loaded cart up to it, or decides curiosity is structural support."

"Tomorrow?"

"Ask tomorrow."

He hated that answer.

Reality had already chosen it.

I stepped left to look at the other side of the arch.

The field peg landed in the shallow floor groove.

The vibration came through the wood first.

One low movement.

Not sound yet.

Felt in the socket.

Staff hand.

Teeth.

I froze.

Someone behind me whispered, "Bell."

Then I heard it.

Low enough that the quarry swallowed most of the tone.

Metal somewhere deep under the hill.

One long note.

It faded slowly.

Nobody moved until it was gone.

Tessa said, very calmly, "That happened after the blast."

I turned my head.

"Once?"

"Once. This makes twice."

Right.

My body had registered the vibration and the air sound as two events.

Same event.

Different arrival.

I looked down at the old groove beneath the peg.

"Did the ground move the first time?"

Arven answered. "Felt it under the carts."

"Direction?"

He looked at me as if direction were an unreasonable demand from a vibration.

Fair.

"No idea."

I stepped off the groove.

Formed another tiny plane against the arch.

The pressure pattern had changed.

Not much.

Enough.

Load was shifting farther inside.

"Everyone back five more feet," I said.

Tessa did not ask why first.

She turned and moved the lantern line herself while two workers helped.

That told me more about her than the Bronze clasp had.

I retreated with them.

Arven stayed until Tessa looked at him.

Then he moved too.

We waited.

Nothing collapsed.

No third bell.

A woman near the upper road started crying quietly.

I had noticed her earlier and filed her under worker family.

Arven looked up.

"Fenn's wife."

There it was.

The three missing names stopped being three missing bodies.

Fenn.

Haro.

Lis.

Someone wanted each one back differently.

I hated rescue work for becoming personal after the plan was already difficult.

Lantern light appeared on the quarry road above us.

Five riders.

No banner.

No dramatic charge.

They came down at a pace that protected their horses on loose grade.

Two dismounted before the final turn and walked the animals in.

The others followed.

Nobody stared at the hole.

That was how I knew they were the team.

The first person went to Mills, the man with the bandaged scalp, and asked the healer beside him what had changed.

The second took Tessa's surveys without introducing himself and started comparing the blast face to the old contour marks.

A woman with two thin copper rods walked the lantern perimeter and watched the exposed arch from three different angles without crossing the line.

A broad man carrying a short hammer checked the sky, then the quarry walls above the new opening.

The last rider took a shield from her saddle before she came toward us.

Gold tab.

Red Quay mark beneath it.

Gray at the temples.

Dark padded coat with quarry dust already finding the knees.

She looked first at Tessa.

"Site authority?"

"Mine until Guild hazard transfer. Tessa Mar."

"Renn Saye. Transfer accepted when I sign it. Missing three?"

"Yes."

"Known alive?"

"None confirmed."

"Last contact?"

Tessa answered.

Renn listened without interruption.

Then she looked at Arven.

"Foreman?"

"Arven Soss."

"Anyone else entered?"

"No."

"Anyone planning to?"

Arven glanced toward the upper road where Haro's brother had begun arguing with one of the quarry guards.

Renn followed his eyes.

"Keep him out. Kindly first."

Arven nodded.

Only then did Renn look at me.

Field peg.

Staff.

Chalk marks near the entrance.

"Structural?"

"Yes."

"Guild?"

"Silver. Carrow file. Temporary Halden work."

"Name?"

"Greg."

Tessa said, "He checked the face."

Renn looked back at the opening.

"Result?"

"It should hold if we stop adding load outside. The buried structure is carrying through connections I can't see. Bell changed the pressure pattern."

Renn's eyes returned to me.

"Can you map the movement while we're inside?"

There were at least four reasons to say no.

Unknown structure.

Unknown bell.

Three missing people.

Old masonry buried under a working quarry after a blast.

Also one reason to ask a better question.

"What's the contract?"

Renn's mouth moved slightly.

Maybe almost a smile.

"There he is."

Tessa sighed.

"Of course that's his question."

I looked between them.

"I have debts."

Renn pointed toward the field table.

"Then let's make sure you understand exactly what you're being paid to survive..."
