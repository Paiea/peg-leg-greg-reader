# Candidate Chapter 123: Terms

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Renn Saye read contracts the way some people read enemy formations.

Fast.

Then again slower, looking for the part that would kill someone later.

Tessa put a fresh sheet on the field table.

Renn held it down with one hand while quarry wind tried to make Halden law optional.

"Site transfer begins on signature," Tessa said. "Halden Guild keeps property authority. Red Quay takes hazard lead below the lantern line."

"Quarry crew stays out," Renn said.

"Unless requested by you."

"No. Unless requested by me and cleared by Greg for structure."

I looked at her.

We had known each other for maybe four minutes.

She noticed.

"You object?"

"I'm deciding whether that's a compliment or liability."

"Both."

Reasonable.

Tessa continued.

"Rescue authorized through first light. Survey authorization through tomorrow noon if the site remains stable. Any worker property comes out with the worker. Historical material stays where found unless removal is necessary for rescue or safety."

"And if something historical is loose?" Renn asked.

"Mark it. Photograph sketch if possible. Guild crate only after rescue."

I said, "Salvage share?"

Tessa looked at me.

The quarry foreman looked at me.

Renn looked at me.

Somewhere behind us, Fenn's wife was waiting for news about whether her husband was alive.

Timing had perhaps not been ideal.

"After rescue," Tessa said.

"Obviously. Still part of the terms."

Renn watched me for another second.

Then nodded.

"He's right. Put it down now so nobody gets philosophical later."

Tessa's eyes narrowed.

"I hate contractors."

"Guild employee?" I asked.

"Yes."

"Then your hatred lacks standing."

"I can still remove you."

"Strong standing."

Renn signed first.

The salvage language was ordinary enough once Tessa stopped glaring at me. Licensed historical material, if any was legally released after Guild appraisal, would split according to Red Quay's standing contract. I was attached at one specialist share for this job only. No claim on Red Quay retainers. Expenses covered from site call until release.

"Food?" I asked.

Tessa tapped one line.

"Covered."

"Lodging if this runs past morning?"

Another tap.

"Covered."

"Replacement equipment damaged on site?"

"Reasonable replacement value subject to review."

"Define reasonable."

Tessa put the pen down.

Renn said, "Greg."

"Yes?"

"You can negotiate after we know whether the three workers are dead."

Fair.

"One specialist share is fine."

Tessa blinked.

"That's it?"

"I can start again."

"Please don't."

I signed.

Harrow remained funded if I lived long enough to pay him.

Excellent structure.

Renn folded the contract and tucked it into a waxed pouch.

"Now tell me what you do."

"You asked already."

"You said structural. That's a category."

She pointed at the shield on her back.

"I can call myself defense. That tells you almost nothing useful about what I can own in a room."

Behind her, Red Quay had spread through the site without needing to stand in a line for introductions.

The thin older man had Tessa's quarry surveys pinned under three stones and was copying the newly exposed entrance onto one in blue pencil.

A woman with a healer's red-thread cuff was checking Mills's scalp dressing and asking him the same orientation questions he had probably answered twice already.

The copper-rod woman stood outside the lantern line, turning one rod between two fingers while she watched the masonry.

The broad man with the hammer had gone uphill with Arven to inspect the rock shelf above the blast face.

Nobody looked idle.

I liked that before I knew whether I liked any of them.

"Field support," I said. "Sword. Some rigging. Some ward work. Load planes. Structural stabilization."

Renn shook her head.

"Resume."

"You dislike resumes."

"I dislike thinking I handed someone a problem because they said they could help, then finding out they meant they could stand nearby and have opinions."

"That is most support work."

"Not mine."

She leaned over the quarry map.

"What can I hand you down there and stop watching?"

That landed differently.

Field Support Three had wanted the opposite from me once.

Cover the missing piece.

Take the ugly handoff.

Be the person who knew enough across several jobs to stop a gap becoming an emergency.

It had fit.

Maybe still did.

But Renn already had five people who had been working together long enough to move before she spoke.

She did not need another person who could almost do everyone else's job.

The buried arch behind us carried load farther than I could see.

The floor groove had transmitted the bell through the peg.

If something changed while the others were busy with rescue, that change would become everyone's problem before anyone had time to argue ownership.

"Movement," I said.

Renn waited.

I tried again.

"If the structure changes under us, give it to me. Stone shifts. Footing changes. A wall starts taking load it didn't have. Ward pressure moves through something physical. Anything where the problem is not what the thing is, but where the force is going."

"Can you stop it?"

"Sometimes."

"Can you tell me when you can't?"

"Yes."

That answer came easier.

Renn nodded.

"That I can use."

The older man at the maps said, without looking up, "Until he argues with the stone."

"Name?" I asked.

He looked at me then.

Thin face. Gray beginning at the beard. Bronze clasp worn almost smooth.

"Mett."

"Survey?"

"Stone."

"That is less specific than she allowed me."

"I've worked with her longer."

Favoritism.

The healer joined us.

"Asha," she said. "If anyone has swallowed quarry dust, tell me now instead of coughing blood at midnight and acting surprised."

Nobody volunteered.

She pointed at the broad man's retreating back.

"Korr definitely swallowed some."

From uphill, Korr shouted, "I heard that."

"Then drink water."

He lifted one hand without turning.

Asha looked at me.

"Leg?"

"Attached."

"Skin."

Ah.

"Outer spot irritated. Checked before I came. Not broken."

"Pain?"

"Low."

"If it changes, tell me before it becomes my problem."

"That feels selfish."

"Medicine is mostly selfishness with licensing."

I liked her.

The woman with the copper rods finally came back from the lantern line.

She said to Renn, "Nothing active on the exposed arch. Old linework in the joints, maybe. Blast broke too much to call shape."

Renn nodded.

"Veya, Greg. Greg, Veya."

Veya looked at my staff, field peg, and hands.

Then at me.

"Structural?"

"Apparently."

"Don't touch my rods."

"I wasn't planning to."

"Everyone says that."

She walked away.

Introduction complete.

Korr returned with Arven and dust across one shoulder.

"Upper shelf holds," Korr said. "Foreman says no loose face above the opening. I say one block is ugly."

Arven said, "That block has been ugly thirty years."

"Then experienced ugly."

Korr looked at me.

"Greg?"

"Korr?"

"You the building man?"

"Tonight."

He lifted the short hammer.

"Tell me before I hit the building. Renn gets mean."

Renn said, "I get expensive."

"Same thing."

I looked at the hammer head.

Dense black metal. Short handle. No obvious rune work.

"How hard do you hit?"

Korr smiled.

"Enough."

Mett said from the table, "Ask how often."

Korr's smile disappeared.

Interesting.

I left the question alone.

For now.

Tessa brought the missing-worker sheet.

Three names.

Fenn Orris. Thirty-four. Married. Quarry setter, six years.

Haro Dev. Twenty-nine. Cutter. Brother on night crew.

Lis Tern. Twenty-two. General quarry hand. Bunkhouse resident. No local family listed.

Tessa gave the paper to Renn.

Renn read all three out loud once.

Not ceremony.

Memory.

Then she handed it to Asha.

"Order?" Renn asked.

Arven pointed at the opening.

"Fenn went first. Haro after. Lis last."

"Equipment?"

"Two lamps. One pry bar. Haro had a hand hammer."

"Rope?"

"No."

Korr muttered something about quarry workers.

Arven turned on him.

"They thought a man was trapped twenty feet in."

Korr lifted both hands.

"I was going to say fast response."

Nobody believed him.

Renn did not waste time fixing the mood.

She pointed at the map.

"We go six. Mett owns route and void calls. Greg owns moving structure. Asha stays central unless a patient changes that. Veya, no cutting old material until Mett or Greg clears what it is attached to and I clear the action. Korr, no impact into anything structural without Greg."

Korr looked at me.

"You fun?"

"Rarely."

"Shame."

Renn continued.

"Rescue before mapping. Mapping only what keeps us from getting lost or killed. If we find historical material, we mark it. If we find the bell, nobody touches it until we know why it rings."

"What if it rings while we're there?" Asha asked.

Renn looked at me.

I hated receiving authority before evidence.

"If the floor or walls move with it, we stop. I check. Mett checks. Then Renn decides whether we continue."

Mett nodded once.

Renn said, "Anybody can call out."

Korr asked, "Out as in retreat or outside?"

"Both. If anyone says out, we discuss the reason after we are outside."

I looked at her.

"Anybody?"

"Anybody."

Mett said, "Especially me."

"Why especially you?"

"Because stone kills people who vote."

Fair.

The next fifteen minutes were boring enough to save lives.

Rope checked.

Spare lanterns checked.

Water divided.

Chalk colors assigned so Mett's route marks did not look like my load marks.

Asha made Korr remove a loose shoulder buckle because she said she was tired of sewing avoidable holes in expensive idiots.

Veya sharpened one copper rod with a file.

Renn checked everyone's exit light, then checked her own last.

I retied the field-peg strap.

Then checked it.

Then checked it again because knowing something was probably fine had never stopped hands from wanting evidence.

Renn saw me.

"Problem?"

"No."

"Then stop touching it."

I disliked leadership when applied correctly.

A runner came down from the quarry road with a folded note.

"Greg?"

I took it.

Mara's handwriting.

Two lines.

**Got your note. Do not die before I choose because then the table becomes very offensive.**

Under that:

**Sixth can move. I cannot. Work first.**

I read it twice.

Then folded it and put it inside the notebook instead of answering.

Renn waited until I looked up.

"Ready?"

I looked at the entrance.

Five people I barely knew were already moving toward the lantern line.

Nobody asked whether I felt ready in a spiritual sense.

Professional adulthood had advantages.

"Ready."

We crossed the line.

The field peg struck old floor stone for the first time.

Behind us, Halden remained full of offices, bad clocks, expensive socks, three job offers, and people who expected us to come back.

Ahead, the corridor bent under the hill before the lantern light could reach its end...
