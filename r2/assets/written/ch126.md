# Candidate Chapter 126: Bell Room

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

We went back the next morning because responsible people apparently revisit places that tried to bury them.

Nobody was missing this time.

Major improvement.

The quarry had closed the west face completely. Two guards stood at the lantern line with a little board between them that read:

**NO ENTRY WITHOUT GUILD CLEARANCE**

Under that, in smaller writing:

**CURIOSITY IS NOT CLEARANCE.**

I pointed at the second line.

Tessa looked up from her table.

"Needed saying."

"Who?"

She looked toward the quarry crew.

Three men looked away at once.

Answer enough.

Haro was at the Guild infirmary. Crushed ankle, broken lower leg, circulation preserved. Asha said the foot would probably stay attached if Haro followed instructions, which meant the real risk was now Haro.

Fenn's arm had been set.

Lis's brother arrived from the south road before dawn.

Nobody made a joke about any of that.

Red Quay did make jokes about breakfast.

Korr had eaten six eggs.

Asha said this was not a medical emergency yet.

Mett wanted to know whether the eggs counted as reimbursable expedition expense.

Veya refused all conversation until tea.

Renn read the updated site authorization while they argued.

That might have been the most impressive skill in the group.

My residual limb looked better than it had after the warehouse.

Redness down.

No broken skin.

Field socket comfortable enough.

Hands and shoulders tired from yesterday's planes, but normal tired.

Asha made me tell her all of this anyway.

"Anything sharp?"

"No."

"Anything numb?"

"No."

"Any magic since the room?"

"No."

"Independent testing?"

I looked at her.

"You met my file too?"

"Renn summarized the entertaining parts."

Terrible woman.

"No independent testing."

Asha nodded and went to bother Korr about his knees.

Today's contract was smaller.

Survey only.

Map the accessible buried structure.

Identify what rang if possible.

Mark unstable sections.

No deep salvage.

No removal of old equipment unless leaving it in place created a current hazard.

Tessa tapped the last line twice.

"That includes artifacts."

Everyone looked at me.

I stared back.

"Why me?"

Renn said, "Your Guild note uses the word theft."

"Private administrative matter."

"Twice."

"Very private."

Korr said, "Did you steal two things?"

"No."

"Then why twice?"

"Legal enthusiasm."

Veya drank tea.

"I'm starting to understand him."

"Don't," Asha said. "It encourages him."

We descended.

Mett rerouted us around the damaged circular room instead of trusting yesterday's load shift. He found a narrow service passage behind a mineral crust that none of my force tests would have noticed because the wall carried almost nothing.

He tapped twice.

Pointed.

Then spent twenty minutes making us widen the opening by hand because he refused to let Korr hit it.

Korr complained the entire time.

"I have a hammer."

"You have a personality problem," Mett said.

"Separate equipment."

The passage dropped steeply.

Field peg territory.

I went sideways on two steps where the right boot could not land flat.

Veya waited below without offering a hand.

Then said, "Your bag is catching the wall."

I shifted it.

More useful.

At the bottom she stopped so quickly I nearly walked into her.

One copper rod pointed at the floor.

"Line."

A narrow brass strip ran through the stone from wall to wall.

Faint green light sat inside one hair-thin groove.

Active.

Or active enough.

"Ward?" Renn asked.

Veya crouched but did not touch it.

"Maybe signaling. Maybe trip. Maybe load sense. Don't step over until Mett checks both sides."

Korr looked at the corridor width.

"Can I step there?"

Veya pointed to a clean stone beside it.

"Yes."

"There?"

"Yes."

"There?"

"If you keep asking, no."

He moved.

The bell room announced itself before we saw it.

Sound changed.

Every footfall gained a faint metallic tail.

The staff tip rang a little against stone even when it struck softly.

Our breathing came back from somewhere ahead with a shape that was not ordinary echo.

Mett slowed.

No one needed the signal.

The corridor opened into a chamber taller than it was wide.

The bell was not hanging from the ceiling.

That was the first thing I understood.

The second was that calling it a bell was technically correct and structurally useless.

A bronze tongue taller than two men stood inside a vertical stone channel along the far wall.

It did not hang free.

Three blackened metal arms connected it to ribs crossing the room.

Each rib disappeared into a different wall or floor channel.

Three striker plates sat beside the tongue at slightly different angles.

Nothing moved.

The room still felt like it was waiting.

Mett whispered something appreciative and obscene.

Renn looked at him.

"Technical term?"

"Yes."

Veya had already moved to the nearest side station.

"Greg."

I saw it.

Brass cuff fixed around a stone armature.

Six nested bands.

Dark hinge.

Guide grooves inside.

Setter's cuff.

Not one.

Eight stations ringed the room.

Each station faced a different load rib.

Some cuffs had cracked open.

Two were missing entirely, leaving only old mounting pins.

One looked intact enough to wear.

I did not touch it.

My hand still remembered the flea-market cuff anyway.

Same weight family.

Same nested-band geometry.

The old vendor's dead inventory suddenly had a room.

Mett crouched near the first station.

"Floor wear."

Deep scuff marks sat where a worker's feet would have been.

Not one set.

Years of them.

Veya followed the cuff's mounting angle to the rib across the ceiling.

"Fixed alignment," she said.

"Looks like it," I said.

Renn stood in the center without touching anything.

"Explain it like I don't want to become an archaeologist."

That removed several paragraphs.

I pointed at the nearest rib.

"This station teaches one repeated brace. Same geometry every time. Fast. Cheap after enough repetition. Probably very reliable."

"Cost?"

"Depends on the cuff. Depends how long you use it. Old versions trained the caster into the pattern hard enough that other shapes got difficult."

Korr looked at the eight stations.

"So eight bad mages keep building standing?"

Veya said, "Eight very good mages at eight things."

I looked at her.

She shrugged.

"If it worked."

That was the question.

Mett tapped one floor channel and then another.

"Bell might tell them which thing."

We mapped without activating anything.

Three striker plates.

Three broad load states.

When pressure migrated through the buried network, the ribs flexed differently. Enough flex moved the bronze tongue until it touched one striker or another.

Different tone.

Different stations responding.

Maybe.

We had evidence for the movement.

Not for every operating rule.

Renn kept saying "maybe" back to us whenever explanation started becoming confidence.

I appreciated that less while it happened.

More afterward.

The chamber had been designed for people who repeated narrow actions inside a system that told them when those actions mattered.

I walked from station to station.

One cuff opening was narrow.

One wide enough for Korr's wrist.

One mounted left-handed.

Not identical workers.

Identical needs.

Something in my chest tightened.

Not desire exactly.

Recognition maybe.

The flea-market cuff had seemed like a trap because I remembered people after years of using one.

This room did not erase those people.

It did make one question harder.

What if the trade had been correct for the job?

Renn came beside me.

"You want it."

"No."

"Fast answer."

"I know where another one is."

Silence.

Korr turned.

Asha looked up from the wall inscription she was definitely pretending not to read.

Veya said, "Of course you do."

Mett asked, "Legally?"

"For sale."

"Disappointing."

Renn looked at the intact cuff.

Then at me.

"Do you need one?"

That was better than asking whether I wanted one.

Yesterday morning, the answer had been easy.

No.

Old Greg's answer had been easier.

Absolutely not.

I looked at the ribs.

At the stations.

At Veya's rods.

At Mett's little brass listening cup.

At Korr, who could do one terrifying thing and then had to breathe while everybody else protected the space around him.

"I don't know," I said.

Renn nodded.

"Then you don't touch this one today."

Reasonable people ruined dramatic mistakes constantly.

We kept mapping.

The bell rang while we were still inside.

Nobody panicked this time.

That felt like progress until the bronze tongue began to move.

"Back," Renn said.

We all took two steps from the center.

The tongue flexed toward the west striker.

Low note filled the chamber.

Not loud.

Huge anyway.

I felt the load move a heartbeat before the sound finished forming.

West network taking more.

North easing.

One floor channel warming under my right boot.

I formed a tiny feathered plane against the nearest rib.

Not to change it.

To feel.

The structure answered cleanly enough to make my forearms tighten.

Mett had his hand on the floor.

Veya watched the green brass line.

Korr did absolutely nothing, which remained a professional achievement.

Asha watched us because if the building did something new, her expertise would arrive later and messier.

The note faded.

Pressure settled.

No collapse.

No second tone.

Mett stood.

"That was west load."

"Probably," I said.

Renn looked at me.

"You just learned her word."

"Contagious."

We left the cuffs where they were.

Tessa was visibly relieved by this when we returned to the surface.

"Nothing removed?"

"Nothing," Renn said.

Tessa looked at me specifically.

"Nothing," I repeated.

"Excellent."

Everyone needed better vocabulary.

The Guild appraisal team would come later.

Quarry engineers would decide whether the old structure could be isolated, stabilized, or simply buried again. Historians would arrive and make normal work difficult. Someone would argue ownership. Someone else would argue jurisdiction. Arven would want his quarry back.

None of those problems belonged to me today.

Red Quay's hazard fee cleared before lunch.

My specialist share was smaller than I had imagined while negotiating and larger than the warehouse job by enough to forgive the difference.

Tessa added reimbursement for the emergency call and one meal.

"One?" I asked.

"You ate breakfast under Red Quay's contract."

"I had half a roll before."

"Personal choice."

Cruel institution.

Korr got a separate salvage bag from the two lime crawlers we had killed. Shell plate, teeth, tendon strips, nothing glamorous enough to become treasure in a song.

Asha held up one cleaned tendon strip.

"This stuff absorbs impact strangely."

I took it.

Flexible crosswise.

Nearly impossible lengthwise.

"Can I have one?"

Asha looked at Renn.

Renn looked at the salvage sheet.

"Comes out of your share."

"How much?"

Korr groaned.

"He's negotiating monster string."

"Everything has a price."

Veya said, "Apparently."

I bought one strip.

Not because I had a plan.

Mostly.

The setter's cuff remained six streets uphill in Halden.

The monster tendon went into my bag.

One dangerous old thing I did not own.

One cheap ugly thing I did.

That seemed like enough artifact acquisition for one day...
