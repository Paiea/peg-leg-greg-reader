# Chapter 127: The Key

Noll moved because sleep had become more valuable than Lower Rook.

He told me over breakfast after a Brass Spoon closing shift.

"Room above a baker. East Market."

"More rent?"

"Two copper a week."

"Worth it?"

"Forty minutes less walking after close."

Excellent accounting.

He had already spoken to Maud Pell.

Of course he had.

His life did not require my meeting attendance.

On Sixthday morning he carried two bags, one pot, and a knife roll downstairs.

That was most of it.

I helped with the pot.

Maud took his key.

"You keeping the dining shifts?" she asked.

"If they keep me."

"They'd be stupid not to."

Noll looked at me.

"See? Professional endorsement."

We walked his things east.

That evening I came home and his old door was dark.

The hallway had not changed.

My expectation had.
