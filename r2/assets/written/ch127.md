# Candidate Chapter 127: Not Monster Socks

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Mara looked at the strip of crawler tendon on the table between us.

Then at me.

"No."

I had not explained yet.

This was becoming a professional pattern.

"You don't know what it is."

"It was inside your bag with quarry dust and blood."

"No blood."

"Greg."

"Very little blood. None of it mine."

Mara pushed the tendon back toward me with one finger.

We were eating late lunch in a narrow soup place two streets from Vey. I had washed at the inn first because the quarry had entered my clothes in several geological layers and because Mara's note had not said **come smell like buried limestone**.

The field peg leaned against the wall beside my chair.

Walking leg back on.

Fresh sock.

Pera's widened socket still comfortable after a night underground, which felt like a better review than anything I could have written.

"It's crawler tendon," I said.

"I guessed monster."

"Material category matters."

"You bought monster tendon."

"One strip."

"Why?"

I flexed it across the grain.

Easy.

Then lengthwise.

Almost nothing.

"Impact behavior."

Mara stared.

"That explained less."

"Asha noticed it absorbs sharp force. Might work as a washer above the field-peg ferrule."

Mara looked under the table at the walking foot.

Then at the field peg by the wall.

"In the leg?"

"Not in the socket. Lower. Nowhere near skin."

"Pera agreed?"

"I haven't asked."

"Then Pera has not agreed."

"Correct."

Mara took a spoonful of soup.

"I am learning your systems."

"Terrible hobby."

She swallowed.

"How was the quarry?"

There it was.

I had given her the safe version at Vey after returning.

Alive.

Not injured.

Three workers found.

One dead.

Old structure.

More tomorrow maybe.

Facts small enough to fit in an office doorway while two clerks pretended not to listen.

Now she wanted the rest.

I put the tendon away.

"Fenn's arm is broken. Haro's lower leg and ankle are bad. Asha thinks he keeps the foot. Lis died before we reached her."

Mara's spoon stopped.

"Twenty-two?"

I looked at her.

"Tessa told the office."

Of course.

Vey and Guild shared freight information, route closures, work stoppages. A dead quarry worker changed shipments before she changed stories.

"Yes."

Mara looked down at the soup.

"Did you know her?"

"No."

"Still bad."

"Yes."

We sat with that for a while.

The soup place kept being a soup place.

Bowls clicked.

Someone at the counter complained about too much pepper.

A delivery woman came in with three covered baskets and left without ordering anything.

Ordinary life remained disrespectful around death.

I had always liked that eventually.

Not immediately.

Eventually.

Mara asked, "What was the bell?"

"Load alarm. Probably."

"Probably?"

"Old structure carried stress through several networks. Big bronze tongue flexed between striker plates depending on where load moved. Different tone, different response."

"People inside?"

"Eight fixed stations around the room."

"Like workers?"

"Structural casters. Very narrow jobs."

She watched my face.

Damn it.

"What?"

"You liked it."

"The engineering?"

"Greg."

I looked at my bowl.

"It was well designed."

"That's not what I said."

"Then ask the thing you mean."

Mara took another bite first.

Rude use of soup as leverage.

"Did it make you want the market cuff more?"

I looked up.

"How do you know about the cuff?"

"You told me last night."

I searched memory.

Quarry.

Fenn.

Haro.

Lis.

Contract.

Mara at the Vey doorway.

I had apparently included the cuff somewhere between **nobody touched artifacts** and **Korr ate six eggs**.

"I was tired."

"You were very informative."

"Dangerous."

Mara waited.

I answered.

"More curious."

"Want?"

"Not enough to buy it."

"That's not no."

"Correct."

She nodded.

No argument.

That made it harder.

"Old me would have said no before touching it," I said.

"Old you also had two legs and apparently stole fewer magical objects."

"Statistically true."

"Current you?"

I thought about the room.

Eight stations.

Eight repeated answers.

The bell telling them when.

Veya waiting inside one perfect line while I changed the crawler's route.

Korr spending five breaths useless after doing something none of us could do.

Mett hearing a room through stone I could not hear through magic.

"Current me wants to know the exact cost before deciding the cost is stupid."

Mara nodded again.

"That sounds dangerous."

"Probably."

"You say that like you're discussing rain."

"Rain is also dangerous in sufficient quantity."

"Greg."

"Yes. It is dangerous."

Better.

She went back to her soup.

The conversation moved on because not every dangerous thought deserved immediate resolution.

Mara had spent the morning interviewing one of the Northbank branch supervisors by packet mirror.

That was apparently normal for Vey and deeply unnatural for everyone else.

"How?" I asked.

"Silver glass in the conference room. Northbank pays the line fee."

"Clear image?"

"No. His left eye kept becoming his eyebrow."

"Strong leadership presence."

"He sounded better than he looked."

She told me about the route desk.

Four clerks, maybe five by winter.

Western transfers.

Some authority over weekly adjustments instead of only filing what someone else had decided.

No final choice yet.

Two more days, she said.

I did not mention the table.

This became difficult enough that Mara noticed.

"You're being quiet."

"Eating."

"Suspiciously."

"Soup requires concentration."

She smiled.

I survived without adding a column.

After lunch Mara went back to work and I went to Pera.

The tendon remained wrapped in paper.

I did not put it on her counter immediately.

Experience.

Pera had a customer standing between the benches, older man with a brace under one knee and a cane he kept leaning in the wrong place.

She saw me.

"Sit."

"I'm not here for the walking leg."

"Then wait."

I sat.

The older man looked at the field peg beside my chair.

"Road?"

"Sometimes."

He tapped his cane.

"Mine hates stairs."

"Most legs do."

"This one hates them professionally."

I liked him but kept it to myself.

Growth.

Pera adjusted the brace strap, made him walk twice, then sent him away with instructions he promised not to follow.

The door closed.

Pera looked at me.

"What?"

I put the wrapped tendon on the counter.

She unfolded the paper.

Silence.

Then:

"No."

"You don't know what I want."

"You brought dead animal into my shop."

"Small part."

"No."

"It might damp impact."

Pera lowered the strip.

Looked at me.

Looked at the strip.

"Maybe."

There.

Civilization.

She made me wash it again behind the shop.

"Asha cleaned it."

"Asha is a healer."

"Yes."

"I am not putting healer-clean monster into equipment."

"Different standards?"

"Different dirt."

Fair.

Once Pera accepted the strip as material instead of insult, the problem became ordinary.

No tendon near skin.

No tendon inside the socket.

No replacing the wood structure with something clever.

The field peg stayed simple because simple equipment failed in comprehensible ways.

We tested a piece between two scrap plates.

Pera hit the top with a mallet.

The lower plate jumped.

Then she used equal-thickness leather.

Hit again.

More jump.

She repeated both tests.

"Small," she said.

"Yes."

"Might matter."

"Yes."

"Might make ground feel worse."

"How?"

"Damping is information loss too."

That had been my next thought.

I hated when competent people arrived there first.

"Prototype," I said.

"That word does not protect you."

"It protects the final design from being blamed."

She almost smiled.

We made a washer.

Thin tendon piece sealed between waxed leather faces, trapped above the field-peg ferrule where I could remove it without touching the socket.

Ugly.

Replaceable.

Cheap compared with almost everything else in my life.

I changed legs and stood.

Pera pointed.

"Walk."

"You could vary the command."

"Walk."

Six steps.

Turn.

Six back.

The peg strike felt softer through the knee.

Not comfortable exactly.

Less sharp.

Also less precise.

I stepped from wood to threshold stone.

Then back.

"I feel less ground."

"Problem?"

"Maybe."

Pera's mouth twitched.

"Everyone uses that word badly."

This city was stealing my material.

I tried uneven alley cobble outside.

Better impact.

Slightly worse information.

Could matter on loose ground.

Could matter in a fight.

Could be wonderful on a road.

Could be stupid everywhere.

A real prototype.

Pera handed me the old ferrule setup.

"Carry it."

"Obviously."

"If tendon swells, change it. If it smells different, change it. If traction feels wrong, change it. If you bring me a second monster part because this one worked once, I charge double."

"Specific policy."

"I know customers."

I paid.

Not cheap.

Not cuff money.

The field peg went into my bag beside the old ferrule, spare liner, two socks, and the increasingly questionable definition of necessary equipment.

Outside, the Lantern Rows were uphill.

The cuff was still there unless someone had bought it.

I could have checked.

Instead I went toward Vey to meet Mara after sixth bell.

Not discipline.

I wanted dinner.

The distinction mattered more to me than it probably should have...
