# Candidate Chapter 129: Northbank

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Mara chose Northbank before the noodles arrived.

This felt disrespectful to noodles.

We were back at Tali's breakfast place because apparently major life decisions required the same bad bench, the same inaccurate clock, and some kind of food stain on official paperwork.

This time the folder beside Mara's hand was blue-gray.

No Carrow folder.

No Halden folder.

One.

I knew before she spoke.

Mara wrapped both hands around her tea.

"I accepted this morning."

I looked at the folder.

Northbank seal.

Very official.

Very rude.

"Okay."

Mara waited.

Tali put two bowls on the table between us.

Broth, noodles, egg, chopped green onion.

She looked from Mara to me, correctly identified a conversation happening, and left without asking whether anything else was needed.

Professional.

Mara kept waiting.

"That's all?" she asked.

"No."

"I know."

I hated Northbank.

This was not new.

I had hated it provisionally for several days.

Now the hatred had a seal.

"Why Northbank?" I asked.

The question came out better than I expected.

Mostly.

Mara turned the folder so the top page faced me.

"The desk is mine."

I stopped reaching for the noodles.

"Actually yours?"

"Route desk lead. Four clerks when I start. Two junior analysts attached to western transfer. I report to branch operations instead of another desk lead."

That was newer than the offer version she had described at breakfast five days ago.

"Who approves weekly changes?"

"I do, under the branch limits. Anything over the freight-loss threshold goes upstairs. Everything else is mine."

"Staff schedules?"

"Mine."

"Route substitutions?"

"Mine if the cost change stays inside band."

"Training?"

Mara's mouth started to curve.

"Also mine."

There it was.

Carrow offered home.

Halden offered familiar people, respectable pay, and another year under Sera's desk.

Northbank had apparently committed the unfair act of offering Mara more responsibility instead of merely more salary.

More Mara.

Difficult to argue against without becoming an asshole.

I tried anyway.

"Housing?"

Mara stared at me.

"Greg."

"Information."

"Included for six months. Then stipend if I keep the position."

"Good."

"You are doing the table in your head."

"The old table is obsolete."

"You made a new one."

I took a bite of noodles.

Silence remained useful law.

Mara laughed.

Not loudly.

Enough.

"How many columns now?"

"No comment."

"More than nine."

"Methodology has evolved."

"Terrifying."

I ate another bite.

The noodles were good.

This was inconvenient because I had wanted something else to blame.

Across the room, the potato man from our first breakfast was arguing with a new cook about how thin to slice onions. He had apparently diversified his authority.

Tali wound the wall clock and moved one hand ahead by several minutes without checking another clock.

I watched.

Mara saw me watching.

"Don't."

"She just corrected it by instinct."

"Greg."

"Hostile institution."

"When do I leave?" Mara asked.

I looked back at her.

There it was.

The actual question under the job.

"When?"

"Twelve days."

I knew the route before she continued.

River west to Mill Junction if packet space held. Road from there. Five days if everything connected cleanly. Six more likely. Seven if weather or freight priority became rude.

I did not say any of that.

Mara noticed the restraint.

"You know the route."

"Yes."

"Say it."

"Why?"

"Because pretending you aren't calculating is weirder than calculating."

Fair.

"Five days clean. Six normal. Seven if something breaks. River first. Road after Mill Junction."

"Correct."

Right.

Her profession literally involved routes.

I had somehow still experienced the urge to explain one to her.

Embarrassing.

"Start date fixed?" I asked.

"Yes. They want me there by the first workday after arrival. I have two buffer days if I leave in twelve."

"Reasonable."

"I negotiated one of them."

I looked at her.

"You negotiated buffer."

"I know you."

"That's unfair."

"Also weather."

"Less flattering."

Mara took a drink.

Then she said, "I didn't choose it to leave you."

I set the chopsticks down.

"I know."

"And I didn't choose it because I expect you to follow me."

That one hurt more.

Not because I wanted her to expect it.

Because some part of my head had already started building versions.

Northbank room cost.

Carrow work retained.

Guild reciprocity.

Monthly route.

Seasonal route.

Temporary contract there.

No permanent move.

Permanent move.

Mara had been hired for five days and I had apparently already colonized the city mathematically.

"I know," I said again.

Mara studied me.

"Do you?"

"Enough to not answer with travel frequency."

"That sounds painful."

"Extremely."

She smiled, then lost it again.

"What do you want?"

Carrow.

Easy answer.

Except it was not the whole answer anymore.

I wanted Lower Rook.

Noll's kitchen.

Jorren being irritatingly competent on roads.

Pell treating every material sample as an invitation to ruin a workbench.

I wanted Daro to someday hear my name without first remembering the thing I stole.

I wanted Mara close enough that breakfast did not require six travel days.

I also wanted Red Quay to ask me again.

I wanted to see whether Veya's fixed-point work got stranger with practice.

I wanted another job where somebody was much better than me at something and I got to be useful anyway.

I wanted the setter's cuff to remain for sale long enough that I could decide I did not need it.

Possibly.

Life had become offensively plural.

"I want you in Carrow," I said.

Mara nodded.

No apology.

Good.

"And?"

"I want you to take the better job if it's actually better."

"It is."

"Then I hate Northbank."

Mara laughed.

"There you are."

"Garbage city."

"You've been there."

"First life."

"Was it garbage?"

Unfortunately, no.

Northbank in my memory had broad river terraces, expensive bread, competent bridge crews, and a council that rebuilt one bridge twice because nobody wanted to admit the first redesign had been stupid.

Pleasant city.

Current Northbank would be younger.

Different shops.

Different bridge repairs.

Different people.

Mara's, before it was ever mine.

If it became mine at all.

"It was annoyingly decent," I said.

"Terrible."

"I know."

She ate noodles.

I did too.

For a few minutes the decision stopped requiring language.

That helped.

Then Mara said, "I don't want to do two years again."

"Neither do I."

"Letters were not enough."

"No."

"They were good."

"Some were excellent."

"Three pages about bridge pins."

"Important correspondence."

"Still not enough."

"Agreed."

Mara moved the Northbank folder aside so it no longer sat between our bowls.

"And I don't want every visit to become an expedition plan."

Targeted.

Fairly.

"Okay."

"You say okay like contract language."

"Would you prefer emotional screaming?"

Mara thought about it.

"Maybe once."

I put both hands on the table.

"Northbank is a garbage city. I hope your four clerks have terrible handwriting. I hope the housing has one window and the neighbor practices drums at dawn."

Mara started laughing before I finished.

"Better."

"I hope every route analyst says 'technically' before giving you bad news."

"Stop."

"I hope the bread is overpriced."

"You already said it was."

"Historical continuity."

She kicked my right shin under the table.

Same target.

Reliable relationship infrastructure.

I looked offended.

Mara looked happier.

That did not make Northbank less real.

It did make the next twelve days real too.

Not twelve days to solve the relationship.

Twelve days Mara still lived in Halden.

Twelve days of work, meals, packing, people saying goodbye, bad scheduling, probably one argument I could not prevent by spreadsheet.

Then she would leave.

After that we would learn the actual cost.

Not before.

Mara reached under the table and tapped the front of my field peg with one shoe.

"Is that the monster part?"

I looked down.

"Vibration washer."

"Made from monster."

"Everyone has become reductive."

"Does it work?"

"I forgot about it during the survey."

Mara smiled.

"So yes."

"Maybe."

"You like that word."

"I hate that you hear it differently now."

"What did the quarry people call it?"

I ate noodles.

Mara waited.

"Nothing."

"Greg."

"Monster leg."

She nearly choked.

This was not supportive.

When she recovered, Tali had come over because laughing hard enough to inhale noodles apparently qualified as service concern.

"Fine," Mara said, waving her away.

Tali looked at me.

"Your fault?"

"Usually."

"Good."

She left.

Everyone used that word badly.

Mara wiped her eyes.

"I leave in twelve days," she said again.

"I know."

"What are you doing tomorrow?"

I thought about it.

No Red Quay obligation yet.

Pera wanted the washer checked after more walking.

Harrow payment ready.

Guild board existed.

Carrow eventually.

"I don't know yet."

Mara's eyebrows rose.

"Again?"

"Don't make it weird."

"It's weird."

"I might work."

"Of course."

"But not Northbank."

Her face changed slightly.

"Thank you."

The words landed wrong at first.

Too much gratitude for me not interfering with her city.

Then I understood what she meant.

Not forever.

Today.

I nodded.

"You're welcome."

After breakfast Mara went to tell Sera officially, even though Sera apparently knew because offices were diseased with information.

I walked with her as far as Vey.

At the steps she took my hand.

No promise this time.

No two-year clock.

Just hand.

"After sixth?" she asked.

"Actual sixth?"

Mara stared.

"I had to."

"After sixth."

She went inside.

I stayed outside another minute.

Northbank remained six travel days away.

Carrow remained home.

Halden remained under my feet.

None of those facts had agreed to simplify themselves.

I started toward the Guild district anyway...
