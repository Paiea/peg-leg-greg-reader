# Candidate Chapter 132: Shape

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

The third job after the quarry was not with Red Quay.

That mattered more than I expected.

Halden Guild found me halfway through breakfast.

Not Mara breakfast.

Cheap inn breakfast.

Bread, eggs, tea, one piece of sausage with enough salt to preserve a horse.

The runner stopped in the doorway and shouted, "Greg?"

Three people looked at me.

This remained an unpleasant consequence of reputation.

"Depends."

The runner was older than the quarry boy and less impressed by evasive answers.

"Warehouse fire. River district. Structural call."

I put down the sausage.

"Still burning?"

"Yes."

Better question than interesting.

I stood.

Walking leg.

Wrong leg.

"How far?"

"Three streets past Copper Bridge."

Wet cobbles, hoses, debris.

Field peg.

I went upstairs, changed, grabbed sword and staff, then left the sword.

Fire did not care about my fencing confidence.

The runner watched me come down with field bag, short staff, field peg, and no weapon.

"No sword?"

"If the fire draws one, we're already fucked."

He laughed once.

Good enough.

We ran badly.

He ran.

I moved as fast as the peg allowed without turning haste into a second emergency.

The river district announced the fire before we reached it.

Smoke above the roofs.

Bell pattern from the watch towers.

Three short.

Pause.

Two.

Warehouse fire with adjoining risk.

I knew Halden's alarm code because Mara had explained it after complaining that Vey workers ignored practice drills.

Useful romance.

The street was already wet when we arrived.

Bucket crews passed water from two public cisterns.

A hand pump fed canvas hose toward the front doors.

Fire warders in pale leather coats stood at the alley mouth with brass-faced shields angled toward the building.

The warehouse itself had stopped pretending to be salvageable.

Upper windows burned orange.

Roof smoke pushed through broken slate.

One corner had sagged enough that the gutter line pointed visibly downhill.

A woman with a red cord tied around one sleeve intercepted me.

"Guild structural?"

"Greg."

"Captain Osa Venn. Fire watch."

No introduction beyond that.

Good.

She turned me toward the building.

"Front crews are out. Nobody listed inside. West wall shares an alley with a lamp-oil store. Roof is moving that direction. If it goes through the wall, we lose the block."

"Anyone in the oil store?"

"Cleared. Owner tried to go back for books. We improved his judgment."

A man across the street was shouting at two watchmen.

Probably improved.

"Water?"

"North cistern steady. South line weak."

"Warders?"

Osa pointed.

Two shield warders at the alley mouth.

One older man with gray beard.

One woman maybe my age.

Their shields did not block heat.

They shaped it.

Air shimmered between the brass faces and the warehouse wall, bending heat upward enough that the oil-store shutters had stopped smoking.

Narrow job.

Important job.

"How long can they hold?" I asked.

"Nerri can hold another ten minutes. Pellin maybe twenty. They alternate pressure every thirty breaths."

"Roof?"

Osa looked at me.

"That's why you're here."

Fair.

I crossed behind the bucket line.

Field peg on soaked stone.

Good grip.

Terrible visibility.

Smoke stayed mostly above street level, but every gust pushed a gray sheet into the alley and erased half the building.

The warehouse had three floors.

Brick side walls.

Timber floor beams.

Heavy slate roof.

The west wall leaned toward the oil store by maybe an inch at the top.

Maybe two.

Heat distorted distance.

I pressed the staff to the street and cast a thin plane against the wall.

Not hold.

Feel.

The answer came ugly.

Roof load had shifted west because the eastern rafters were burning through first.

Every failed timber pushed more weight toward the surviving west frame.

Eventually the west wall would not fail because fire weakened it.

It would fail because it was the last competent employee.

Relatable.

I moved closer.

Osa caught my coat.

"Line."

I looked down.

Red chalk across the street.

Fire perimeter.

I had stepped one foot past it.

"Need closer."

"Ask."

Right.

Her site.

"Can I go to the pump cart?"

She looked at the roof.

"Thirty seconds. Nerri covers."

Osa turned.

"Nerri! West lane. Structural moving."

The younger warder lifted her shield higher.

The air changed around me.

Heat did not disappear.

It bent.

Like stepping beside an oven instead of into it.

I crossed to the pump cart.

Twenty seconds.

The west wall had two loading arches on the ground floor.

Both open.

Bad.

The center pier between them carried too much now.

Above, a roof truss shifted.

Slate slid.

Someone shouted.

Osa shouted louder.

"Clear west!"

I got back behind the red line as a strip of roof dropped into the alley.

Not the whole thing.

Six feet of burning timber and slate.

Nerri rotated her shield.

Heat bent up.

Pellin stepped beside her and caught the second wave.

No one asked me to help.

Correct.

They were better at heat than I would become by panicking creatively.

I looked at Osa.

"You don't need the warehouse."

"No."

"You need the oil store."

"Yes."

"Then stop trying to keep the west wall standing."

Osa's eyes narrowed.

"Explain."

I pointed with the staff.

"Roof is loading west because east is gone. We brace west, we keep feeding it more roof. Eventually it fails toward the oil store anyway."

"Other option?"

"Make it fail inward."

Osa looked at the burning building.

Then at me.

"Can you?"

"Maybe."

She did not complain about the word.

Professional.

"What do you need?"

There.

I walked the shape in my head.

Not six commands.

Conditions.

"Clear the front alley completely. Keep both heat warders on the oil side. I need your hook crew on the east front corner."

"Hooks won't move that roof."

"They're not moving roof. I need the corner opened when I call."

Osa turned to a man carrying a long iron hook.

"Dren. East corner. Three people. Wait for Greg."

Dren did not ask why.

He took two people and moved.

Good crew.

"Water?" Osa asked.

"Keep current pattern. Do not cool the west wall."

"Why?"

"I want it weak in the middle before it fails at the top."

That answer sounded criminal.

Osa accepted it anyway.

I set the first plane.

Low across the west loading arch.

Not holding the wall.

Creating a temporary inward shoulder.

Second plane higher.

Feathered toward the warehouse interior.

Third along the roof edge.

Small.

I could not carry the roof.

Good.

That would have been the wrong job.

I only needed to make inward slightly easier than outward at the moment everything stopped negotiating.

The building creaked.

Deep timber sound.

Nerri said something behind her shield.

I could not hear.

Pellin answered.

Speaker clarity had become irrelevant to survival.

Osa stayed beside me.

"How close?"

"Soon."

"That is not time."

"Building doesn't own a clock."

The upper west window frame sagged.

There.

I felt load hit the higher plane.

Too much.

Released half.

Let the wall move inward one finger-width instead of fighting it.

The roof shifted.

Osa saw it.

"Now?"

"Not yet."

Slate burst through the second-floor windows.

One piece hit the street and shattered.

Bucket line backed up without being told.

Good city.

The east front corner burned brighter.

We needed the opening.

"Hooks!"

Dren's crew pulled.

Not roof.

A charred loading shutter and the loose frame around it.

The opening widened.

Air entered.

Flame surged outward at the east face.

Everyone swore.

So did I.

Expected did not mean pleasant.

The roof moved.

West.

Then caught my upper plane.

Not stopped.

Turned.

A few degrees.

Enough to put the falling line toward the center aisle instead of the oil-store wall.

"Osa!"

She already knew.

"Clear!"

The watch pulled back.

Nerri and Pellin stayed.

That bothered me until I remembered their job was exactly this.

Their choice.

Their training.

Their captain.

Not mine.

I held the low plane.

Released the high one.

The west wall folded.

Not outward.

In.

Brick first.

Then timber.

Then half the roof followed it with a sound too large for the street.

Air punched smoke and sparks upward.

Nerri went to one knee behind her shield.

Pellin braced beside her.

Heat curved over them like water over stone.

The oil-store shutters rattled.

Did not ignite.

My low plane took one lateral hit from the collapsing wall.

I let it slide inward instead of strengthening.

Then dropped it.

Done.

The warehouse became a burning pile contained mostly inside its own footprint.

Mostly.

One beam stuck through the west opening at shoulder height.

Dren's hook crew dragged it inward once the flames allowed.

The bucket line advanced again.

Nobody cheered.

Work remained.

Osa looked at the new shape of the building.

"Oil store?"

A watchman ran the alley.

Came back.

"Wall hot. No flame. Roof clear."

Osa exhaled.

Then pointed at me.

"Stay until I say the debris is done moving."

"I was planning to."

"I don't know you."

Fair.

We spent another hour watching failure finish.

Less glamorous.

More important.

Twice I put small planes beneath hanging sections while hook crews pulled from safe angles.

Once Nerri told me to move because my coat sleeve had started smoking.

I had not noticed.

She slapped it out with one gloved hand.

"Structural," she said.

"Heat warder."

"Your specialty is being on fire now?"

"Temporary expansion."

"Bad fit."

She went back to work.

I liked her immediately and did nothing about it.

When the fire dropped low enough for watch crews to enter the front shell, Osa finally dismissed me.

"Name again?"

"Greg."

"Carrow?"

"Originally."

"Halden staying?"

"No idea."

Osa nodded as if uncertainty was normal.

Unsettling woman.

"Guild will have your rate by morning."

"Hazard?"

"You went inside the heat line."

"With permission."

"Still hazard."

Excellent profession.

I walked back toward the inn smelling like smoke, wet leather, and the inside of a chimney.

Mara was waiting outside Vey when I passed the office.

She looked at me.

Then looked again.

"You said you weren't taking the Esven job."

"I didn't."

"What happened?"

"Local work."

She touched the black smear on my sleeve.

"Local work caught fire?"

"Technically work was already on fire when I arrived."

Mara closed her eyes.

"Are you hurt?"

"No."

"Actually?"

"Sleeve casualty."

She checked my face, hands, then the socket side of my trousers.

Observation rights.

Some.

"Dinner?" she asked.

"Bath first."

"Please."

Cruel but accurate.

I started toward the inn.

Mara walked beside me.

Red Quay was two days down the road by now.

The job had happened anyway.

Different people.

Different problem.

Same narrow question underneath it.

Not how much force I could make.

Where the building was allowed to go.

I almost turned that into a conclusion.

Then Mara said, "You smell terrible," and saved me the trouble.
