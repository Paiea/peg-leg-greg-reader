# Candidate Chapter 136: The Tenant

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Pera said the marsh had invalidated my data.

I considered this dramatic.

She did not.

"Mud," she said.

"I noticed."

"Washer didn't work most of the day."

"Ground worked better."

"Exactly."

She removed the crawler-tendon insert from the field peg and pressed it between two fingers.

Still intact.

Dirt at the edges.

No swelling.

No smell change.

"Road after?" she asked.

"Two miles stone."

"Difference?"

"Noticeable again."

Pera nodded.

I handed her Suri's grazer tendon wrapped in cloth.

She stared at it.

Then at me.

"No."

"You don't know what it is."

"More animal."

"Different animal."

"Still no."

"Compare fibers."

Pera looked at the bundle again.

"Maybe."

Civilization remained fragile but possible.

She unwrapped it on a scrap board instead of her workbench.

The grazer tendon was darker and flatter than the crawler strip, layered more visibly along the grain.

Pera pulled one edge.

Barely moved.

Twisted.

More give.

"Different," she said.

I said nothing.

She looked at me.

"You were going to say useful."

"I have been persecuted for vocabulary."

"Good."

I paid her nothing because she kept the sample.

Best appointment yet.

The bounty payment waited at Halden Guild.

Two verified jaw plates.

Hunter share highest.

Bo's proof-processing fee next.

Three equal armed shares after.

Suri had written the split herself before leaving.

No argument.

I liked hunters.

From a distance.

The clerk counted my coins.

I counted them again.

She did not comment.

Halden had finally developed standards.

"Packet for you," she said.

I stopped with one coin between my fingers.

"From?"

"Two."

She handed over a thin blue Vey envelope and a thicker brown packet tied with Carrow post cord.

The blue one first.

Obviously.

I took both outside anyway.

There was a bench beside the Guild wall where afternoon sun reached one end and pigeons owned the other.

I chose sun.

Opened Mara's note.

Short.

**Mill Junction. Transfer went clean. One passenger snores while awake. I am not explaining.**

I smiled.

Continued.

**Northbank sent forty-six pages of route-desk procedure ahead of me. I have read twelve. Seven contradict each other. Promising.**

More Mara.

Good.

Then:

**Temporary housing confirmed. Third floor. Stairs. Before you start, yes, I noticed. No, you are not measuring them from Carrow. I am fine. Do not come yet. I mean it.**

I looked at the street.

She had anticipated the exact objection and ruined it before delivery.

Unethical correspondence.

Last line:

**I miss you. This is not the same as an invitation. Love, Mara.**

I read that twice.

Then once more because I was allowed.

Folded it.

Put it inside the notebook.

Not in the route section.

Different crime.

The Carrow packet came from Noll.

His handwriting remained hostile to ink.

**Greg,**

**Your room roof leaked over the chair, not the bed. I moved the chair. If you are not coming back this month tell me because I can rent the room to someone who actually lives in it. Not threatening. Practical. Soup is worse without you because Jorren keeps buying the cheap onions.**

I stopped.

Read that sentence again.

Not threatening.

Definitely threatening.

There were two smaller notes inside.

Jorren's first.

Cleaner handwriting than Noll.

**Got route deputy on Leth road rotation. Mostly paperwork, some field. First week I told a cart crew to wait on bad shoulder stone and they listened. Felt strange. You would have overexplained it. I did not. Worked anyway.**

Rude.

**Gone six days next week. If you come back and I'm not there, do not decide I died.**

He signed only his name.

No question.

No request for advice.

Good.

Pell's note was shortest.

**Noll says your peg has monster tendon in it. Bring scrap if true. We have been testing laminated heel blocks with Kesta's resin and I want comparison. If false, bring yourself anyway. I need someone to tell me why the third jig is lying. Daro says it isn't lying. Daro is wrong.**

Below that, in smaller writing:

**Do not tell Daro I wrote that.**

Excellent secrecy system.

I sat on the bench with all three notes spread across my lap.

Northbank.

Carrow.

Halden behind me.

No emergency in any direction.

Mara did not need rescue.

Noll did not need rescue.

Jorren had gotten better at his own work without waiting for me.

Pell had other people testing materials.

Even the room had apparently survived a roof leak through independent intervention.

Terrible.

I looked at Noll's first sentence again.

**If you are not coming back this month tell me because I can rent the room to someone who actually lives in it.**

There was a practical question hiding inside it.

Did I still live there?

I had been calling Carrow home while spending months elsewhere.

Easy word when nobody required evidence.

The room had my desk.

Old notebooks.

Two shirts I had forgotten.

One chipped bowl Noll refused to throw away because it was "still bowl."

The window that stuck in wet weather.

Pell's workshop close enough to hear if the alley was quiet.

Hearthline farther uphill and substantially less welcoming than before I stole from a client.

Jorren in and out on roads now.

Noll buying bad onions.

I wanted to go back.

That was annoyingly simple.

Not because work waited.

Not because Mara was there.

Not because a promise required the route.

I wanted my room.

I wanted Noll's worse soup.

I wanted to see what Jorren looked like giving orders he did not overexplain.

I wanted Pell to show me the lying jig.

I even wanted the particular discomfort of walking past Hearthline and knowing Daro had reasons not to wave.

Home had accumulated enough imperfections to become specific.

I folded Noll's packet.

Went back inside the Guild.

The clerk looked up.

"Problem?"

"Route board. Carrow."

She pointed without looking.

Second wall.

Three options in the next four days.

Fast packet south tomorrow, expensive.

Freight wagon two mornings from now, cheap and slow.

Mixed river-road departure in three days, middle price, better walking breaks.

I studied all three.

Old reflex comfortable again.

This time the decision was mine.

No one else waiting at a dock.

No promise bell.

No rescue clock.

I chose the freight wagon.

Cheap.

Four days normal.

Five if weather.

Room remained mine whether I arrived tomorrow or next week.

Probably.

Noll was capable of renting it out during breakfast if sufficiently offended.

I paid the deposit.

Then wrote three replies at a standing desk near the Guild door.

Mara first.

**Mill Junction understood. Forty-six pages is proof Northbank fears competence. I am not coming yet. This is me stating compliance so you cannot invent tone. I miss you too.**

I added:

**Third-floor stairs are your problem until invited otherwise.**

Painful sentence.

Kept it.

Noll next.

**Do not rent the room. I leave Halden in two days. Buy better onions.**

Then Jorren.

**Congratulations. I would not have overexplained. This letter is not evidence.**

Pell got a line at the bottom of Noll's packet.

**Tendon true. Bringing scrap. Jigs do not lie, people measure badly. Tell Daro I said nothing.**

I sealed everything.

Paid postage.

Outside, Halden looked exactly the same as it had before the mail arrived.

The cuff stall still existed somewhere uphill.

Pera still had my second monster sample.

Suri was probably already in another marsh.

Red Quay somewhere near Esven.

Mara moving north.

I had two days left in Halden.

No need to fill both.

I went back to the inn and told the keeper I was checking out after second bell in two mornings.

She looked at me.

"Back?"

"Maybe."

"Leave the good room available?"

"You have a good room?"

"Not for you."

Fair.

Upstairs, I started packing.

Better than Mara.

Obviously.

The wooden tourist bird came out of my coat pocket and sat on the bed.

Blue wing.

Green wing.

I considered leaving it behind.

Then put it in the field bag beside Veya's copper rod.

One stupid prize.

One stolen-by-consent piece of someone else's equipment.

Two tendon samples if Pera returned the grazer strip.

Enough evidence that Halden had happened.

I closed the bag.

Carrow in two days.

For the first time in a long while, I was taking a route because I wanted the place at the other end.
