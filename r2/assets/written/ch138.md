# Candidate Chapter 138: The Landlord

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Noll opened the door before I knocked twice.

He looked at my face.

Then my bag.

Then the walking leg.

"Late."

I looked toward the street clock.

"By what?"

"Month."

Fair.

He stepped aside.

"Room not rented."

"Your letter sounded threatening."

"Practical."

"You wrote 'not threatening.'"

"So you know."

I entered.

Lower Rook smelled like boiled onions, old wood, lamp smoke, and somebody else's laundry drying too close to a stove.

Home, apparently.

The hallway had changed.

New hook beside the door.

Different rug.

One wall patched with plaster a shade too pale.

A pair of women's boots sat beneath the stair where Jorren's used to live.

I pointed.

"Who?"

"Mesi. Upstairs back."

"New boarder?"

"Three months."

I had not known there was an upstairs back room available.

Noll had acquired architecture while I was away.

A woman leaned over the upper rail at the sound of her name.

Late twenties maybe.

Hair wrapped in a yellow cloth.

Ink on two fingers.

"You're Greg?"

There it was again.

"Unfortunately."

"Jorren said you'd say something like that."

Betrayal traveled well.

"Mesi," she said. "Tax copying."

"Greg."

"I know."

Terrible house.

She disappeared again.

Noll took my smaller bag without asking.

Not the field bag.

He knew better.

Or simply valued his fingers.

"Soup," he said.

"Room first."

"Soup hot now. Room same cold."

Strong argument.

I followed him to the kitchen.

Two bowls already on the table.

He had expected me.

That was irritatingly kind and therefore unacceptable to discuss.

I sat.

Walking foot fit under this table without negotiation.

Small thing.

Body remembered before mind did.

Noll ladled soup.

I tasted it.

Paused.

"Jorren bought onions?"

Noll pointed at me with the ladle.

"See. Bad."

"These are barely onions."

"Cheap."

"That is not a defense."

"Tell him."

"Where is he?"

"Leth road. Back tomorrow maybe."

Maybe.

The house did not coordinate reunions around my arrival.

Good.

I ate more soup.

Still bad onions.

"Route deputy?" I asked.

Noll nodded.

"Paper. People. Wagons. Complains more."

"Healthy advancement."

"Has clean coat."

"Tragic."

Mesi came down with a stack of copied forms under one arm.

Noll looked at the forms.

"No work table."

"Two minutes."

"Eat table."

Apparently the kitchen had jurisdiction.

Mesi sat at the far end and moved her papers onto the chair beside her.

"You came from Halden?" she asked.

"Yes."

"Glass district taxes are stupid."

"Probably."

"You don't know?"

"I try not to learn tax law recreationally."

She looked disappointed.

Good.

We ate.

No one asked about Brell.

No one asked about the leg.

Mesi complained that one merchant had declared eleven crates of blue window glass as "ritual visual material" to avoid a local levy.

Noll said this sounded clever.

Mesi said it sounded illegal.

I said both could be true.

Normal dinner.

Afterward I carried my bags upstairs.

The room was still mine.

Mostly.

Noll had moved the chair from beside the bed to the window.

The roof leak had left a pale stain where it used to sit.

Someone had repaired the plaster near the washstand badly enough that I could see the trowel direction.

My shelf held fewer things because I had taken half of them east.

The desk had one new scratch across the corner.

"Who?" I asked.

Noll stood in the doorway.

"Roof man."

"He scratched the desk fixing roof?"

"Roof man also moved desk."

"Bad trade."

"Roof dry."

Fair.

I put the field bag down.

Sword on the old wall hooks.

Walking leg off.

Liner hung near the washstand.

The familiar sequence happened faster than thought.

No need to decide where anything lived.

Then the bag opened.

Veya's copper rod came out first.

Noll picked it up.

"This?"

"Not mine."

His face changed.

Not much.

Enough.

"Stole?"

I stared at him.

"No."

Too fast.

Noll stared back.

"No," I repeated. "She gave it to me. Temporarily."

"Who?"

"Veya. Red Quay cutter."

"Why?"

"Inventory dispute."

Noll put the rod down very carefully.

"No stealing."

"I know."

"Good."

There were entire categories of trust I had personally made ridiculous.

No use resenting them.

The wooden bird came next.

Blue glass wing.

Green glass wing.

Noll picked that up too.

"This yours?"

"Unfortunately."

"Stole?"

"No."

He waited.

"Won it."

"How?"

"Fish."

Noll looked at me for a long time.

"Halden bad for you."

"Deeply."

He put the bird on the desk.

Not center.

Near the window.

It looked stupid there.

Fine.

The old Faultglass hiding place sat under the loose floorboard three feet away.

Empty now.

I noticed.

Of course.

No reason to open it.

I did anyway.

Nothing inside except dust and one bent nail I had apparently stored for future civilization.

I closed the board.

Noll watched.

Said nothing.

Good landlord.

"Pell?" I asked.

"Workshop."

"Now?"

"Always."

"Daro?"

Noll shrugged.

"Works."

That was all.

No message.

No dramatic waiting confrontation.

Hearthline existed uphill whether I visited or not.

Fine.

"Tomorrow," I said.

Noll looked at me.

"Pell?"

"Pell."

"Daro?"

"Not tomorrow."

He nodded like this was none of his business.

Which meant it probably was.

He left.

I unpacked slowly.

Clean shirts into drawer.

Road clothes into wash pile.

Tendon sample packet on desk.

Field peg beside the wall.

Pera's replacement washer remained fitted.

Mara's Mill Junction letter went into the desk drawer.

The comparison table was gone because Mara had stolen it lawfully enough to become precedent.

I found an older Carrow notebook under the desk.

Opened to a page from before Halden.

Work list.

Hearthline.

Pell.

Jorren.

Mara packet.

Guild.

All arranged like the world had once been stable enough to fit on five lines.

I closed it.

Outside the window, Lower Rook sounded slightly different.

Mesi walking overhead-back, which was apparently a place now.

Someone arguing in the alley about lamp oil.

A cart wheel hitting the same broken stone near the corner.

Farther away, Bell Tile metalwork after dark.

One hammer pattern I recognized as Pell's shop.

Three fast.

Pause.

Two.

Pause.

Again.

Could have gone.

He would be awake.

I was tired.

This was allowed.

I lay down.

The mattress felt softer than the Halden inn.

Too soft.

I moved once.

Twice.

Turned onto my back.

Home had apparently changed firmness while I was away.

Or I had.

I refused to investigate.

Morning solved enough things.

It solved this one by making me hungry.

Noll had bread and bad onions fried with eggs.

Mesi left before second bell carrying tax forms.

Jorren's bed remained empty.

No reunion yet.

After breakfast I fitted the walking leg, packed the grazer tendon scrap, and put Veya's rod into the field bag because leaving someone else's magical tool unattended in a boarding room felt like exactly the sort of sentence people would later use against me.

At the front door Noll said, "Pell first?"

"Yes."

"Hearthline after?"

"No."

"Good."

I stopped.

"Why good?"

Noll shrugged.

"Soup before dessert."

"That metaphor makes no sense."

"Go."

I went.

Bell Tile was waking up.

Workshop shutters opening.

Coal smoke.

Metal carts.

Pell's side lamp was already on.

Of course.

I crossed the street toward the shop.

Hearthline could wait another day.

Nothing broke because I let it.
