# Candidate Chapter 139: The Maker

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Pell called it a monster leg before I finished opening the bag.

I considered leaving.

"Vibration washer."

"Monster."

"Material."

"From monster."

"This argument has already damaged two cities."

Pell looked delighted.

His workshop had changed less than Lower Rook and more than I wanted.

Same long bench beneath the front windows.

Same scorch mark near the vise from an experiment nobody had successfully blamed on anyone else.

Same wall rack of files, awls, punches, saws, and three hammers that all looked identical until Pell explained why one was for idiots.

But there were new things too.

A resin warming box in the corner.

Two laminated heel blocks clamped beneath weights.

A narrow jig mounted to the test frame where an older wooden guide used to sit.

Someone else's handwriting on three chalk labels.

The shop had continued.

Good.

Pell reached for the field peg.

I caught his wrist.

"Ask."

He looked at me.

Then at my hand.

"Can I?"

"Yes."

"You weird now."

"Apparently."

He took the peg.

Turned it upside down.

Tapped the ferrule.

Pressed the waxed washer housing with one thumbnail.

"Who made?"

"Pera. Halden fitter."

"Good sleeve."

"She threatens customers."

"Good fitter."

Professional consensus remained violent.

I unwrapped the small grazer-tendon strip Suri had given me.

Pell smelled it.

"Why?"

"I don't know."

"You smelled it."

"Material tells things."

"What?"

"Mostly if bad."

Fair.

He put the grazer tendon beside the crawler sample packet.

Different immediately.

Crawler ribbon pale, tighter grain, almost no stretch lengthwise.

Grazer darker, wider fibers, more twist under load.

Pell pulled both with pliers.

"Crawler stiff. Grazer moves."

"Pera said similar."

"Pera smart."

"Dangerous praise."

Pell ignored me and clamped the two samples between scrap plates.

Then hit them.

Of course.

The crawler strip reduced the sharp rebound.

The grazer strip deformed more and returned slower.

Pell hit both again.

Then changed the angle.

Again.

"Grazer better sideways," he said.

"Maybe."

"Crawler better down."

"Maybe."

Pell looked at me.

"You learn Pera word."

"She infected me."

He smiled.

"Good."

I chose not to fight every battle.

Pell fitted the field peg into his floor stand and made me walk.

Six steps.

Turn.

Six back.

I stopped at the wall.

"Why does every person who touches this leg own the same ritual?"

"Walk again."

"Cult."

"Again."

I walked.

He watched the peg base, then my knee, then hips.

Not the socket.

Pera had done the same.

"Less shock," he said.

"Yes."

"Less ground."

"Also yes."

"Trade."

"Yes."

Pell nodded like I had finally learned something obvious.

"Keep?"

"Field days. Not always."

"Good."

There it was again.

I sat and removed the peg.

"You're enjoying this."

"You used to hate gear that not only better."

"I still hate it."

"But use."

"Sometimes."

"Better."

"Do not turn this into character commentary."

"What character?"

Excellent defense.

He went back to the bench.

The copper rod sticking from my field bag caught his eye.

Veya's.

Temporary custody.

Probably.

Pell pointed.

"This?"

"Not mine."

His face changed exactly like Noll's had.

"Stole?"

I closed my eyes.

"No."

"Fast answer."

"She handed it to me."

"Who?"

"Veya. Red Quay cutter."

"Cutter what?"

I pulled the rod free.

Simple copper.

Fine longitudinal groove near the tip.

Small maker stamp near the base.

"Her technique makes a line between two established points and cuts along it. Stone, metal, active magic if the edge is clean enough."

Pell took the rod.

"Straight?"

"Only straight."

"Moving?"

"Badly."

"Why you have?"

"She decided I count as a spare fixed point."

Pell looked at me.

Then the rod.

Then toward the test frame.

I knew that face.

"No."

"Yes."

"This is her equipment."

"We don't break."

"Your definition of break is historical."

Pell had already crossed the room.

Of course.

We clamped the rod against a scrap board mounted on the frame.

No Veya.

No cutting magic.

Just the physical problem her line had exposed.

A fixed point on something that wanted to move.

Pell shoved the board sideways.

I put a small feathered plane around the rod's contact patch.

Not against the board itself.

Around the place the rod touched.

The board moved.

The contact patch moved less.

For half a second.

Then the plane tore.

Pell stopped.

"Again."

Cult confirmed.

Second attempt smaller.

Failed faster.

Third broader.

Held longer but dragged the whole board enough to defeat the point.

Fourth angled.

Better sideways.

Worse on rotation.

Pell changed the frame before I asked.

Hung the board from two straps so it could swing and twist independently.

He pushed diagonal.

My plane failed immediately.

"Bad," he said.

"Excellent diagnostic."

"You hold wrong thing."

I looked at the board.

"I'm holding the point."

"No. Holding point to room."

There.

I stared at him.

Pell tapped the board.

"Veya wants point fixed to this, yes?"

"Relative to the surface."

"Then move with surface."

Obvious after he said it.

Infuriating.

I formed the next plane differently.

Not a brace into the world.

A shallow pocket following the board's local face.

Pell pushed.

The whole board swung.

Rod point stayed truer relative to wood.

Twist.

Plane distorted.

Adjusted.

Held.

Not strong.

Not remotely.

But that was not the job.

Pell smiled.

"Again."

We spent almost an hour making tiny failures.

No dramatic breakthrough.

No named technique.

Just smaller questions.

How much translation before the pocket tore?

How much twist?

Could two tiny planes share the point?

Could one release instead of fail?

Could the board move toward the rod without changing the relation Veya cared about?

My mana use stayed embarrassingly low.

My concentration did not.

By the eighth useful attempt, sweat had started under my collar.

Pell noticed.

"Hard?"

"Annoying."

"Hard."

"Fine."

He leaned against the frame.

"Old Anchor bigger."

Blackglass Anchor.

History arriving through a shop test instead of a speech.

"Much bigger."

"Strong."

"Yes."

"This weak."

"Yes."

Pell smiled wider.

"Better."

I frowned.

"Explain before I object."

"Better for this."

He tapped the copper rod.

"Worse big hold. Better small move."

Cleaner than I had been saying it to myself.

Worse big hold.

Better small move.

I looked at my hands.

No revelation occurred.

Good.

Pell released the board and started unclamping the rod.

"You trying smaller now."

"Seems so."

"Why?"

I thought of Red Quay.

Veya's line.

The warehouse fire.

Suri's marsh where none of this mattered.

"Because smaller is enough more often than I expected."

Pell nodded.

"Cheaper."

"Sometimes."

"Faster."

"Sometimes."

"Then good."

"You've become very philosophical."

"No. Material cheap."

Of course.

The workshop door opened.

A woman I did not know came in carrying a resin tin.

Pell looked up.

"Kesta."

She held the tin out.

"Third batch. Less brittle."

Pell took it.

"Greg. Kesta. Resin."

Kesta looked at me.

"The leg?"

I looked at Pell.

"Everyone talks."

He shrugged.

Kesta had been working with Pell on laminated heel blocks while I was gone.

Not my project.

Not waiting for my return.

She opened the resin tin and they immediately began arguing about cure time.

I stood there holding my field peg while the workshop stopped centering me.

Good.

I put the peg back on.

Packed the crawler sample.

Left the grazer strip with Pell and Kesta after both promised not to put it near human skin.

Veya's rod went back into my bag intact.

At the door Pell called, "Tomorrow?"

"For what?"

"Third jig. Lies."

"Jigs don't lie."

"Then you tell why wrong."

I looked toward the street.

Jorren should be back sometime today.

Hearthline still uphill.

No one had sent for me.

"Maybe afternoon."

Pell nodded.

No claim on the morning.

Good maker.

I stepped outside.

Bell Tile noise hit immediately.

Hammers.

Carts.

Someone swearing at sheet metal.

Behind me, Pell and Kesta had already resumed arguing.

I had been home less than a day.

The shop had needed me for exactly one question.

That felt about right.
