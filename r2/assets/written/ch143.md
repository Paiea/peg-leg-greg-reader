# Candidate Chapter 143: The Candidates

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Lysa's ward frame had eight hinges.

I counted because she was late.

Not late enough to matter.

Late enough that counting became available.

"Seven," Tomel said beside me.

"Eight."

"Bottom corner isn't hinge."

"It rotates."

"Everything rotating is not hinge."

"Dangerous philosophy."

Tomel held up the third rope coil.

"This is why you lost spar."

"Unrelated."

The ward court occupied a square of packed dirt between four removable posts.

Lysa stood inside with her copper frame open across both forearms.

New design.

Lighter than the one from Westreach.

Narrower joints.

More engraved channels near the corners.

Expensive enough that she had complained about it twice before the demonstration started.

The evaluator gave her a standard shelter problem.

Three posts.

Protect the marked interior.

Hold against weighted impacts from two directions.

Then adapt when one support became unavailable.

Simple on paper.

Paper remained fictional.

Lysa opened the ward.

Copper frame unfolded once.

Twice.

Set.

Pale amber light connected post to post.

Not thick.

No dramatic dome.

Three clean faces meeting at angles that looked almost architectural until the first weighted bag hit.

The ward flexed.

Recovered.

Second bag from side.

Recovered again.

Stronger than anything I could make in that exact geometry.

Not close.

Good.

I had stopped needing every comparison to hurt.

Mostly.

The evaluator walked behind the third post.

Pulled a release.

Post dropped sideways.

The ward face attached to it collapsed immediately.

Lysa did not try to rebuild the triangle.

She folded one side of the copper frame inward.

The remaining two ward faces narrowed.

Protected space shrank by almost half.

One weighted bag struck during the transition.

Ward buckled.

Held.

Lysa shifted her feet.

Moved the frame another inch.

The failed side became an angled spill surface instead of a wall.

Second impact glanced away.

Evaluator raised a hand.

"Clear."

Lysa released.

Applause.

Not huge.

Enough.

She came out of the court sweating slightly and looked directly at me.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You have a face."

"Everyone has become very interested in my face."

Tomel said, "Planning."

Traitors everywhere.

"You didn't replace the third face," I said.

"No."

"Why?"

Lysa looked back at the collapsed post.

"Because I didn't have a third post."

Simple answer.

I waited.

She sighed.

"If I rebuild the same shape after losing support, I spend more mana making a worse ward. Smaller shelter keeps the two good anchors and changes what counts as protected."

"When did you start doing that?"

"Westreach."

I looked at her.

"During the trial?"

"After."

Of course.

Life continued after the scene I remembered.

Lysa folded the frame shut.

"Maren kept making me train partial failures because I was too attached to full geometry."

"Cruel woman."

"Correct woman."

Tomel nodded.

"Still cruel."

Lysa pointed the closed frame at him.

"You dropped a wagon into a creek."

"Recovered wagon."

"After dropping."

"Complete workflow."

I had missed them.

Not dramatically.

Enough to feel it now.

Tomel's rigging demonstration happened an hour later near the lifting bay.

He had been assigned a practical recovery problem instead of a speech because someone at the Guild still valued mercy.

Loaded cart.

Rear wheel removed.

One corner sitting on a low skid.

Three anchor options painted around the yard.

Only one actually useful.

Tomel walked the setup once.

Did not touch rope.

Then asked the evaluator, "Do I own the cart or only recovery?"

The evaluator looked surprised.

"Recovery."

"Driver?"

A volunteer raised one hand.

"You own brake and horse."

"Fine."

Tomel chose two coils.

Not three.

I looked at him.

He looked back.

"Shut up."

No words spoken.

Beautiful.

He rigged a high line from the freight post, a redirect through the cart's frame, and a low tension line to keep the empty wheel corner from swinging.

Nothing magical.

At least not obviously.

His hands were faster than I remembered.

Not frantic.

No wasted loops.

He made the driver set brake before tensioning anything.

Made one assistant move three feet because their stance put them inside the failure line.

Then lifted the damaged corner just high enough to slide a replacement block underneath.

No dramatic suspension.

No cheering until the evaluator deliberately released one secondary anchor.

The cart shifted.

Tomel let one line run.

Caught another.

Load rotated toward the open yard instead of the volunteer crew.

Then stopped.

Evaluator marked the sheet.

"Why let it run?"

Tomel pointed at the released anchor.

"Because it already ran."

I stared.

Lysa, beside me, noticed.

"Don't."

"What?"

"Make it about you."

"I was not."

"You were about to."

Fair.

Tomel finished the recovery.

Afterward I helped him carry two coils back.

Two.

Progress.

"Route leadership?" I asked.

Tomel frowned.

"What?"

"You asked ownership before touching anything."

"Yes."

"New?"

"No."

"I don't remember."

"You remember yourself too much."

Rude.

He dropped the coils into a crate.

"Recovery gets stupid when nobody knows who owns brake. Learned that before Westreach."

There.

Not every useful behavior had begun in a chapter with me present.

I filed the thought and refused to narrate it further.

Lunch was crowded enough that we sat on overturned equipment boxes behind the ward court.

Lysa had rice wrapped in leaf.

Tomel bought two meat pies and regretted the second halfway through.

I had fried potatoes because the cheese stall from yesterday had lost my trust.

We traded actual updates.

Not complete life summaries.

Tomel had moved into recovery work around quarry and road freight because he liked jobs where the problem was already honest about being broken.

Not all of it.

Enough.

Red Quay.

Not hidden.

Just not lunch material.

"Yes."

"Idiot."

"Fair."

No moral tribunal.

Good.

"And you still got useful magic out of it."

More dangerous observation.

"Yes."

Of course.

"No."

His face changed.

**TEAM COURSE — SECOND GROUP — THIRD MORNING**

"We were competitors."

"Barely."

"Paper remembers differently."

No details beyond:

**MOVEMENT / LOAD / PROTECTION / ADAPTATION**

Excellent categories.

"Planning."
