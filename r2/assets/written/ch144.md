# Candidate Chapter 144: The Rigger

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Tomel became our leader by refusing the job.

The team-course judge pointed at the three of us and asked, "Caller?"

I looked at Tomel.

Lysa looked at Tomel.

Tomel looked behind himself.

"No."

The judge waited.

"That wasn't a nomination," Tomel said.

"I need one person for start, stop, and safety calls. You can change caller between stations if you announce it."

Tomel looked at me.

"You do it."

"Why?"

"You like telling people what to do."

Lysa coughed into one hand.

Traitor.

I looked at the course.

Then at Tomel.

"You recover loads for a living."

"Yes."

"The objective is a load."

Tomel frowned.

The judge said, "Clock starts after caller is declared."

Tomel sighed.

"Fine. Me."

Leadership achieved through administrative exhaustion.

The course occupied the old west loading yard behind South Yard.

One objective.

Move a rescue sled from the marked start box to the safe bay at the far end.

The sled held a two-hundred-pound training dummy strapped under canvas and a wooden medicine case painted white.

The case contained six glass vials filled with blue water.

Break them and lose points.

Drop the dummy and lose more.

Leave recoverable equipment behind and apparently the Guild became emotionally disappointed.

The route looked simple enough from the start line.

That was suspicious.

First, a broken loading ramp sloped down from the freight shed into the yard.

Second, an open drainage cut crossed the middle. A narrow plank platform hung over it from two old posts and one high guide anchor.

Third, a low masonry gate led into the final safe bay. A heavy practice beam hung partly loose across the top.

Movement.

Load.

Protection.

Adaptation.

The four words from the course notice had acquired physical form.

Other teams waited behind a rope line with score sheets, food, and the healthy desire to watch coworkers fail safely.

Jorren stood near the freight demonstration table in his clean coat.

He saw us.

Raised one hand.

I did not wave.

He smiled anyway.

The judge checked our gear.

Tomel had two rope coils.

Only two.

Character development.

Lysa's folding ward frame sat across both forearms.

I had changed to the field peg before entering because the drainage section included loose gravel and one wet run.

Veya's copper rod stayed in my field bag outside the course.

Not relevant.

Good.

"Three minutes planning," the judge said. "Do not touch course equipment until bell."

Tomel walked the start box.

I started talking.

"Ramp first. I can take part of the sled load, Lysa can keep the case stable, then you rig the drainage crossing and I handle the gate."

Tomel looked at me.

Lysa did too.

"What?"

Tomel said, "You lasted eight seconds."

"Planning is allowed."

"Planning the course, yes. You assigned both of us before looking at the sled."

I looked at the sled.

"I can see it."

"You can see from here."

Lysa pointed at the medicine case.

"Also, why am I carrying that separately?"

"Protection."

"It is already strapped to the sled."

"Badly."

"Maybe."

She walked around the start box without crossing the line.

From the other side, the strap arrangement was visible.

One broad leather strap across the case.

Two loops through sled rails.

The case sat too high.

Lysa said, "I'm not carrying it. We lower it between the dummy's knees."

I opened my mouth.

Closed it.

Fine.

Tomel crouched at the ramp edge.

"Sled runners?"

The judge said nothing.

Course questions apparently required discovery.

Tomel leaned farther without crossing.

One runner had a metal shoe.

The other had a cracked wooden cap wrapped in iron.

Not equal friction.

"We don't pull down the ramp," Tomel said.

"Obviously," I said.

He ignored me.

"We brake. Move the white case lower. One rope rear. Greg controls runner drift if needed. Lysa handles side strike only if it starts."

Lysa nodded.

No objection.

I said, "And the drainage?"

Tomel looked at the suspended platform.

"Later."

"Planning time."

"I can see later better when we're closer."

Infuriating.

The bell rang.

Tomel moved first.

"Lysa, case. Greg, rear left runner."

We crossed the line.

Lysa opened the sled strap and shifted the medicine case down between the dummy's padded legs.

Not carrying it.

Not warding yet.

She rethreaded the strap through a lower rail and cinched it herself.

I checked the cracked runner.

The iron wrap had one raised edge.

Would catch on broken ramp boards.

"Tomel. Left runner catches."

He looked once.

"Can you float the edge, not the sled?"

There.

"Yes."

"Do that."

He ran one rope from the sled rear through a freight-ring beside the top of the ramp.

Brake line.

No fancy rig.

No hauling system.

We turned the sled around so the dummy went down feet first.

Tomel took the rope.

Lysa walked downhill on the open side.

I stayed at the left runner.

"Down," Tomel said.

The sled moved.

Gravity owned most of the job.

Good.

I put a small plane under only the raised iron edge when it approached the first broken board.

The runner skimmed instead of catching.

Released.

Second board.

Same.

No need to carry two hundred pounds because one inch of bad metal was the actual problem.

Halfway down, the right runner hit damp wood and sped up.

Sled yawed.

Lysa raised one narrow ward face beside the medicine case.

Not touching it.

Just giving the case somewhere harmless to go if the strap stretched.

Tomel let rope run two hands instead of locking it.

The sled straightened.

At the bottom he stopped it with the last yard of line.

Time board changed.

Not great.

Not bad.

"Take part of load."

"Different."

Not deep enough to kill anyone.

Maybe eight feet down.

Stone channel dry except a narrow line of water.

Not much.

Enough.

Different expertise.

"Sled fits. Barely."

"I can hold center drift."

"Later."

Then the overhead guide ring.

"One high line from sled front through there. Rear control stays with me."

"Ward on which side?"

"That side if it swings. Upstream is open yard."

"If it swings upstream, medicine case hits rail."

"And downstream?"

Just sharing the problem.

Upstream had none.

Lysa nodded.

"Upstream ward."

Her choice.

Not fast.

Correct.

Not now.

"Fine."
