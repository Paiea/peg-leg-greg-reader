Lysa found me because she wanted to fail once.

Her words.

Not mine.

"Go."

"He does that."

Everyone had apparently formed a committee.

I followed Lysa toward the back practice lane.

The main exchange had thinned after the team course.

Some people were packing equipment.

Others had discovered the final afternoon could be used to repeat demonstrations without scores attached, which immediately made everyone more experimental and less polite.

A pair of Bronze casters were trying to move a warded barrel down stairs without either admitting whose idea had failed first.

Someone in the spar yard was teaching three children how to fall safely.

Jorren's load-sheet demonstration had ended and left six apprentices arguing over whether axle width was ever morally flexible.

Progress.

Tomel was gone.

Actual work.

A recovery crew had needed another rigger at the north yard, and he had left before lunch without asking whether our new temporary-trio mythology required attendance.

Good.

Lysa carried both pieces of her frame now.

Full folding frame across her back.

Small detachable rectangle strapped to one forearm.

The small piece had protected the medicine case yesterday while moving.

That did not mean it was a moving ward tool.

It meant it had moved once.

Important difference.

The practice lane had three waist-high carts on rails.

Guild used them for timing drills.

One person pushed.

One person cast.

The cart could move straight, stop, or be redirected through a switch into a side lane.

Simple.

Good.

Lysa looked at the nearest cart.

"I want the ward to stay with the marked square while the cart changes speed."

She pointed at a white square painted on the cart bed.

About the size of the medicine case.

"How fast?"

"Walking. Then faster if it works."

"Disturbance?"

"Later."

I nodded.

She looked at me.

"You are not designing the ward."

"I know."

"Your face does not."

I ignored this ongoing defamation.

We found a lane monitor named Senn who accepted our unscheduled drill because the scored events were over and nobody else wanted lane two.

"Ten minutes," Senn said. "Then a hauling class has it."

Lysa said, "Enough."

I said, "Maybe."

She looked at me.

I shut up.

First attempt.

Amber rectangle.

Clean.

About eighteen inches wide.

Lysa walked.

The ward moved with her.

Not the cart.

Difference appeared immediately.

The cart accelerated half a step.

White square moved ahead inside the ward.

Lysa corrected.

Too late.

The back edge of the square crossed out of protection.

"Stop," she said.

Senn stopped.

Lysa released.

"Again."

I almost smiled.

Cult expanded.

Second attempt.

Better at first.

Not by much.

Enough.

"Yes."

"I know that."

Right.

"My casting reference is body plus frame. I can move a ward while I move. That's not the same as attaching it to something moving differently."

Problem stated by owner.

Pell's suspended board from yesterday arrived in memory.

Local relation.

"What if the frame doesn't define the reference?"
