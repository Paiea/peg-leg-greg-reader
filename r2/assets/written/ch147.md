# Candidate Chapter 147: The Desk Lead

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Mara's first Northbank letter arrived with a bread complaint.

This proved two things.

She had arrived safely.

I had been right about Northbank bread.

Only one of these facts received appropriate respect.

The packet came to Lower Rook three mornings after the field exchange.

Noll handed it to me over eggs.

"Blue."

"I can see."

"Mara."

"Also yes."

"Open."

"I was planning to."

Mesi looked up from the tax appeal she had somehow brought to breakfast despite house law.

"You always argue with mail?"

"Only intrusive mail."

The envelope had my name in Mara's handwriting and a Northbank Vey stamp over the corner.

I opened it carefully.

Not because sentimental.

Packet paper tore badly if rushed.

Different concern.

Probably.

The first line read:

**The bread is expensive. You are not allowed to be pleased.**

I was pleased.

Noll saw my face.

"Good?"

"Bread."

He nodded as if this explained correspondence.

Maybe it did.

I kept reading.

Mara had reached Northbank a day later than the clean route because a wheel failed outside Mill Junction and the replacement driver had spent three hours insisting the original wheel was "mostly circular."

She had written that phrase twice.

I respected the wound.

Temporary housing was, in fact, on the third floor.

The stairs were narrow but dry.

The room had one good window, one terrible chair, a washstand that leaned left, and a neighbor who practiced flute badly between sixth and seventh bell.

Mara had already learned the neighbor's name.

Lina.

Twenty-one.

Worked at a map bindery.

Had apologized for the flute and then continued.

Northbank had become more specific in six lines than it had in all my route calculations.

The route desk had five people when the offer said six.

One junior analyst had quit two days before Mara arrived because his family moved south.

Nobody had replaced him.

Of the remaining staff:

**Elen** processed adjustments faster than anyone Mara had met and made enough small transcription errors to keep speed from becoming a virtue.

**Bato** maintained corrected road maps in a locked flat cabinet and behaved as if every revision were a family heirloom.

**Sena** was technically a junior analyst, twenty-three, painfully slow, and had already caught two river-weight discrepancies nobody else noticed.

**Orris** handled packet transfer windows and ate boiled eggs at his desk in quantities Mara described as "Korr-adjacent," which was an alarming sign that her social circles had begun contaminating one another.

The fifth person was Mara.

Desk lead.

Her desk was near the west window.

Not symbolic.

She complained about glare after third bell.

Good.

The first three days had mostly been procedure.

Forty-six pages had become fifty-three after branch operations gave her an updated supplement.

Nine pages now contradicted something older.

Elen had a private list of which rules "actually mattered."

Bato had a different list.

Sena refused to make a list until someone defined mattered.

Mara had written:

**I may like Sena most. This is unfortunate because conversation takes years.**

I laughed into my tea.

Mesi looked over.

"Still bread?"

"Analyst."

"Worse."

I kept reading.

On her second full workday, a western ferry line had missed three transfer windows in one morning because Northbank's published cutoff assumed unloaded road carts could cross a market street in twelve minutes.

They could not.

Apparently no one had changed the cutoff because branch operations had used the same schedule for four years.

Mara changed it by eight minutes.

Not huge.

Not revolutionary.

She told the desk first.

Elen said finally.

Bato asked which map note required revision.

Sena spent eleven minutes checking whether the later window broke the north-road handoff.

It did not.

Then Mara sent the change upstairs.

Branch operations wrote back:

**ACKNOWLEDGED. RUN ONE WEEK. REPORT EFFECT.**

That was all.

Mara had underlined it.

Then written beneath:

**I decided something and it stayed decided for at least seven days. I may have chosen correctly.**

There.

The job she wanted.

Not title.

Not salary.

The experience of making one bounded decision and having the institution move around it instead of absorbing it into someone else's desk.

I read that paragraph again.

Then moved on before making it about anything larger.

Northbank itself had stairs.

Too many.

A terraced city built along a bend where the river cut beneath pale cliffs.

Mara's office sat halfway up, housing higher, market lower.

She had already found a noodle stall cheaper than Halden and a bakery worse than both cities but somehow twice the price.

Bread complaint validated.

There was a public terrace near the old north gate where people played a tile game after work.

Mara did not know the rules yet.

She liked watching anyway.

That line bothered me pleasantly.

Something in Northbank I had not known first.

Good.

No.

Fine.

Her last page became more direct.

**I am tired. Not bad tired. New-job tired. I keep waking up and remembering I am the person people bring the question to. Sometimes I know. Sometimes I ask Sena and wait six years for her to answer. Sometimes Bato says a map cannot be changed because the cabinet is full, which I think is a personal belief disguised as storage.**

**Do not come next week. I know I said three to five weeks and you are probably turning that into a range with confidence intervals. Stop. Give me two more weeks before we even pick a date. I want to learn my route to work before you learn Northbank faster than I do.**

Cruel but fair.

Then:

**I miss you. The flute is bad. The window is good. I bought bread anyway. Love, Mara.**

I lowered the letter.

Noll had finished eating.

Mesi was still reading her tax appeal.

Nobody waited for a relationship briefing.

Excellent house.

I folded the pages along Mara's original crease.

Progress in document ethics.

Noll said, "Northbank good?"

"For her."

"Then good."

Allowed use.

"Bread bad," I added.

"Then Carrow better."

Simple ranking system.

I went upstairs and put Mara's letter in the desk drawer beside the Mill Junction note.

The wooden tourist bird stood near the window where Noll had placed it.

Blue wing.

Green wing.

Still stupid.

I left it.

My reply took longer than expected because the first version was mostly questions.

How many stairs?

What was the western ferry route number?

Did the office have a secondary exit?

How experienced was branch operations?

What did temporary housing cost after the first month?

Was Sena slow because cautious or slow because overwhelmed?

I crossed out most of them.

Not because questions were wrong.

Because Mara had just written five pages about her life and I could answer before interrogating it.

Second version:

**Northbank bread has been convicted. I accept no apology.**

Better.

I told her about the field exchange.

Not every event.

Broad barrier score somewhere in the middle.

Sera beating me.

Lysa and Tomel appearing afterward.

Fourth place in team course.

One medicine vial broken.

I wrote:

**We were good enough to become annoying and not good enough to become legends. Ideal result.**

Then about Lower Rook.

Mesi.

Noll's onions.

Jorren's official coat.

Pell and Kesta turning animal tendon into an excuse to argue.

I mentioned Lysa's wrist guide in one line because Mara had met enough work people through letters that names no longer required biographies.

Then:

**I will not come next week. Or the week after unless you change your mind. This is written evidence. Preserve it for court.**

I paused.

Added:

**Learn the tile game. I don't know it. This bothers me.**

Then:

**I miss you too. Love, Greg.**

Good enough.

I sealed it before a third version became a project.

At the post desk, the clerk weighed the packet.

"Northbank?"

"Yes."

"Five days normal."

I knew.

Did not say it.

Growth remained mostly private humiliation.

Afterward I walked without a job for almost an hour.

Field exchange mostly dismantled now.

South Yard returned to freight.

Spar ropes gone.

Food carts down to one.

The place where we had finished fourth held three ordinary wagons waiting for inspection.

Event over.

Good.

Near Hearthline's lower street I saw Venn coming the other direction.

He saw me too.

We both had enough time to pretend otherwise.

Neither did.

"Greg," he said.

"Venn."

He stopped.

I stopped.

No anger in his face.

No warmth either.

Professional middle ground.

"You working tomorrow?"

There it was.

Not invitation.

Question.

"Depends."

"Private counting house. Archive ward. Wall movement during charge cycle. Hearthline has ward people. Client's mason has stone people. Daro wants another load read."

Daro wants.

Small phrase.

I kept my face still enough to become work.

"Why me?"

"Because Daro says your current planes move differently than the last structural Silver we hired."

Not forgiveness.

Market differentiation.

Better.

Venn held out a folded sheet.

"Terms first."

I took it.

One-site consult.

Marked rooms only.

No unsupervised access.

No removal of client property.

Daro or Nesha present for ward tests.

Structural/load analysis only.

Faultglass written into negative space without needing the name.

Reasonable.

Ugly.

Earned.

"Rate?" I asked.

Venn named it.

I read the sheet again.

"Tomorrow?"

"First bell. Weaver Street."

I folded it.

"I'll come."

Venn nodded.

No handshake.

"Sign at site."

Then he kept walking.

I stood with the terms in one hand and Mara's postage receipt in the other.

Two different kinds of distance.

I refused to make more of that sentence.

Lunch existed.
