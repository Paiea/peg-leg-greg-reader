# Candidate Chapter 148: The Consultant

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Weaver Street counting houses had excellent stairs and terrible chairs.

This felt like a professional choice.

People who stored other people's money wanted clients to climb safely and wait uncomfortably.

I approved of half the system.

Venn met me at the front desk before first bell.

He had the contract in one hand and no coffee in the other.

Bad sign.

"Read it again," he said.

"I read it yesterday."

"Again."

I read it again.

One-site consult.

Marked rooms only.

Structural and load analysis only.

No unsupervised access.

No removal of client property.

Daro or Nesha present during ward tests.

Client records confidential.

I signed.

Venn signed beneath me.

The counting-house clerk watched both signatures and sanded the ink like we were entering a treaty.

Maybe we were.

"Bag," Venn said.

I looked at him.

"What about it?"

"Inventory."

Right.

I opened the field bag on the desk.

Short staff.

Marking chalk.

Two copper wedges.

String.

Small level.

Notebook.

Pell's borrowed measuring strip.

One Veya copper rod.

Venn picked it up.

"Client property?"

"No."

"Yours?"

I paused.

Venn looked at me.

"Complicated answer?"

"Loan with disputed social terms."

He put it back.

"Keep it in the bag unless the site lead approves."

Reasonable.

Painful.

Progress apparently included being professionally inventoried like a teenager near a sweet shop.

I closed the bag.

Venn did not apologize.

Good.

The archive sat below street level behind two iron doors and one ward line I could feel before I saw it.

Pressure along the teeth.

Not pain.

Static before a storm.

Daro stood inside with Nesha and three people I did not know.

He looked at me once.

Then at Venn.

"Signed?"

"Yes," Venn said.

Daro nodded.

No hello.

Fine.

Nesha gave me a smaller nod.

Also fine.

Introductions stayed professional.

**Rell Varn**, counting-house facilities manager. Thin man, gray beard, keys enough to become a musical instrument.

**Mira Senn**, mason contracted by the house. Broad hands, white dust in both cuffs.

**Tal Orre**, Hearthline ward technician working under Daro.

Daro did not need an introduction.

Neither did Nesha.

The problem occupied the west archive wall.

Twenty feet long.

Stone block over brick backing.

Three ward plates mounted at shoulder height, each copper-backed and bolted into the masonry.

Shelves stood six inches off the wall.

Ledger boxes filled them from floor to chest.

Red chalk marks crossed two mortar seams.

Mira pointed.

"Movement shows during charge. Returns partway after release."

"How much?" I asked.

"Outer seam, three lines at full cycle. Middle, one. Inner, none."

She held up a thin measuring gauge.

Not much.

Enough to matter if repeated every morning for ten years.

Daro said, "Ward response is stable."

I looked at him.

"You tested?"

His face did not change.

"Yes."

Right.

Bad question phrasing.

I tried again.

"What changes between idle and full charge?"

Daro pointed at the three plates.

"Field closes across west, north, and floor anchors. These three plates carry the west edge. Charge rises in four steps. Movement appears at step three."

"Mana draw?"

"Normal."

"Heat?"

"Normal."

"Plate movement?"

Tal answered.

"None visible."

Mira said, "Wall moves instead."

There.

Conflict already established before I arrived.

Useful.

No.

I had promised myself fewer evaluative words.

I walked the wall.

No magic.

First plate.

Bolts clean.

Mortar around the upper right bolt slightly newer.

Second plate.

Same.

Third plate.

Different backing washer.

"This replaced?" I asked.

Mira stepped closer.

"Two months ago. Original cracked."

"Before movement started?"

Rell shook his head.

"Movement is why we opened it. Washer cracked during inspection."

I crouched.

Walking leg today.

Archive floor flat enough.

The wall met the floor through a dark stone base course polished by age and cleaning.

No obvious settlement crack.

I put two fingers against the mortar seam.

Nothing.

Of course nothing.

Walls rarely confessed while idle.

"Charge one step," I said.

Daro looked at Venn.

Venn looked at the contract.

Then Daro said, "Tal, step one."

Tal moved to the control plate near the door.

Copper lines brightened faintly.

The pressure in my teeth increased.

I touched the seam again.

No movement.

"Step two."

Daro nodded.

Tal raised charge.

More pressure.

Still nothing visible.

Mira slid her gauge into the red-marked seam.

"Same."

"Stop there," I said.

Everyone stopped.

Not because I commanded the room.

Because I had asked during my part of the test.

Important difference.

I opened one tiny feathered plane beside the first plate.

Not touching copper.

Not touching wall.

Just close enough to feel local push when something moved into it.

Daro watched my hands.

"That's what changed?" he asked.

"Partly."

"Smaller."

"Yes."

He said nothing else.

I placed a second plane near the middle plate.

Third near the outer plate.

Three little catches.

No load yet.

"Step three."

Tal looked at Daro.

Daro nodded.

Charge rose.

The first plane touched something.

Not enough to see.

Pressure on the edge.

Middle next.

Outer strongest.

Mira's gauge clicked softly.

"One line."

Then two.

Then three.

I released all three planes.

"Down to two."

Daro did not move.

"Why?"

Fair question.

"I know the wall moves. I don't know what moves first. I want the return."

Daro looked at Tal.

"Two."

Charge lowered.

Mira watched the seam.

One line returned.

Then stopped.

Not all three.

Permanent little residue each cycle.

Bad.

I walked back to the first plate.

"Can I touch the mounting plate?"

Daro looked at me.

The question hung there longer than it should have.

Because once, apparently, I had decided asking was optional.

"Yes," he said.

I put my palm against the copper backing.

Cold.

Fixed.

Too fixed maybe.

"Mira. How old is this wall?"

"Base course old. Upper rebuild maybe sixty years. Archive refit twenty-eight. Ward plates from the refit."

"Bolts straight into stone?"

"Through facing into brick and iron strap."

That was more interesting.

I looked at Daro.

"Your field wants the plate to become part of the ward geometry."

"Yes."

"Does it pull west?"

"It tensions inward and down at full close."

"How much movement is acceptable at the plate?"

Daro frowned.

"None."

Mira said, "There."

Daro looked at her.

She pointed at the wall.

"That's what I told you."

"The plate is an anchor."

"And the wall isn't iron."

They had clearly had this conversation before.

Probably with worse vocabulary.

I crouched near the outer plate and looked at the bolt line.

Three plates tied through old masonry into a hidden iron strap.

Ward charges.

Plates tension inward and down.

Iron strap carries.

Facing stone resists.

Brick behind deforms a little.

Copper does not move.

Something else has to.

I said, "You're using the wall as the flexible piece."

Daro's face tightened.

"The wall should not be flexible."

"Everything is flexible."

Mira smiled.

Daro did not.

I kept going.

"Question is where. Your plate refuses movement. Hidden strap refuses most of it. Brick gives. Facing stone gives less. Mortar gets the difference."

Tal said, "Then reinforce the wall."

Mira shook her head immediately.

"Already tried at the outer seam. Made movement migrate inward."

There.

Excellent failure.

I did not say excellent.

Daro looked at me.

"You want the plate to move."

"Maybe."

"Ward anchor moves, ward geometry changes."

"How much before it matters?"

"Less than you think."

"How much?"

He looked annoyed now.

Better than angry.

Different category.

Tal answered after checking the control notes.

"Half a line at the plate can change edge stability under surge."

Small.

Very small.

Mira's seam moved three lines.

The building was already paying six times that somewhere else.

I took Pell's measuring strip from my bag.

Venn watched.

No objection.

I held it against the plate edge.

"What if the plate stays where the ward needs it, but the wall gets a controlled slip around the mount?"

Mira frowned.

"Sleeve?"

"Maybe."

Daro said, "No. If the plate floats, surge can walk it."

"Then don't float plate. Let backing move relative to facing."

Mira looked at the bolt.

Then at the mortar.

"Compression washer behind the face."

"Something like that."

Daro crossed his arms.

"And during charge?"

I looked at him.

"We test one temporary version before anybody drills your client's wall."

No one objected.

That felt earned.

Mira had scrap leather, thin copper shim, and two hard fiber washers in her site box because masons apparently carried the same kind of nonsense as prosthetic fitters.

Tal removed the outer plate only after Daro dropped the ward fully and Venn recorded the time.

I did not touch the screws.

This was not symbolism.

They were not my screws.

Behind the plate, the hidden mount was exactly where Mira said.

Iron strap.

Brick packing.

Stone face drilled tight around the bolt sleeve.

No room for anything to breathe.

Mira built a temporary sandwich around one test bolt.

Hard fiber against stone.

Thin copper slip layer.

Second fiber washer.

Plate reinstalled.

Ugly.

Temporary.

Measurable.

Daro inspected it himself.

"If this walks, I stop."

"Yes."

He looked at me.

"Not asking?"

"It's your ward."

Silence.

Then Daro nodded once.

Tal brought charge to step one.

Then two.

I placed one tiny plane beside the plate edge again.

Mira set her gauge in the mortar seam.

Daro watched the field, not me.

"Three," he said.

Charge rose.

The plate shifted.

Tiny.

Less than half a line.

My plane felt it.

Mira's gauge stayed nearly still.

Daro's hand came up immediately.

Tal held.

"Field stable," Tal said.

Daro watched the control plate.

Waited.

Five breaths.

Ten.

"Step four."

Full charge.

Pressure filled the archive.

Plate moved another fraction.

Not enough to reach half a line total.

Mortar seam moved one.

Not three.

Mira looked at me.

I pointed at Daro.

His system.

His call.

Daro stayed focused on the ward.

Tal said, "Edge stable. Draw normal."

Daro waited another ten breaths.

Then, "Down."

Charge fell.

Plate returned most of the movement.

Mortar seam returned too.

Mira checked twice.

"Less residue."

"How much?" Daro asked.

"Too small to trust from one cycle."

Good mason.

We ran six more cycles.

Boring on purpose.

By the fourth, Rell the facilities manager had stopped pretending to understand and gone upstairs to do counting-house things.

By the fifth, my left socket had started warming because standing still was its own kind of work.

I sat on a crate between cycles.

Nobody commented.

Sixth cycle.

Same result.

Outer seam movement reduced.

Plate movement remained below Daro's threshold.

Ward stable.

No surge test yet.

Daro called stop.

Tal powered down.

Mira removed the temporary stack and bagged every piece for measurement.

Nothing became permanent because one morning had gone well.

Civilization occasionally learned.

Venn asked, "Recommendation?"

Daro answered before me.

"Mira designs a proper mount with controlled backing slip. Tal and I define maximum anchor travel and surge test it off-wall first."

Then Daro looked at me.

"Load model?"

"I can write one."

"Today."

"Yes."

"No ward recommendations."

"I know."

"Structural only."

"Daro."

He stopped.

I almost said I heard you.

Would have sounded defensive.

He nodded.

Work resumed.

No apology.

No discussion of Faultglass.

No dramatic statement about trust.

Same items in.

Same items out.

"Two."

"I know."

"Writing two."

"Aggressive administration."

"Effective administration."

Fair.

Tal followed with the control notes.

Nesha had barely spoken all morning. She waited near the door while Daro signed Hearthline's site sheet.

He did not.

Then looked at me.

"Your broad planes got worse."

Not hello.

Apparently goodbye.

"Yes."

"The little ones are better."

"Yes."

Not approval.

Maybe recognition.

"Often."

"If Mira's mount passes surge, I want you on the install read. Same restrictions."

One more job.

Not retainer.

Not friend.

One more job.

Worse.
