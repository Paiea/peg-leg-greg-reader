# Candidate Chapter 149: The Recruiter

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

The first team I tried to build lasted nine minutes.

This was efficient.

The Guild board had changed since the field exchange.

Half the temporary notices were gone. Sparring schedules removed. Equipment demonstrations down. Team-course rankings still posted because Tomel had apparently threatened someone if third place disappeared before he could object to it again.

Ordinary contracts had reclaimed the wall.

Escort.

Drain clearing.

Two missing goats.

One missing apprentice marked **NOT GOATS** in different ink.

Then a fresh blue sheet near the center.

**FIRST SURVEY. THREE DAYS. MIXED FIELD.**

I stopped.

Blue sheets meant city-interest work without city-emergency rates.

Usually annoying.

Sometimes interesting.

The clerk behind the board was not Sella.

Different woman.

Round glasses, shaved left temple, four pencils tucked through her hair.

She saw me reading.

"You licensed Silver?"

"Unfortunately."

"Then read the bottom."

I did.

**WEST BRAMBLE FLOOD CUT. OLD RIVER COMPLEX EXPOSED AFTER BANK LOSS. FIRST ENTRY ONLY. NO DEEP EXCAVATION. SALVAGE TAGS ACTIVE. CITY ANTIQUARY OBSERVER ATTACHED.**

Better.

The river had eaten half a bank west of Carrow during the spring rise.

Not unusual.

Finding old stone beneath it was also not unusual.

Finding painted stone, glazed channels, and active magical residue apparently was.

The next lines listed requirements.

**1 PROTECTION / WARD.**

**1 RECOVERY / LINE.**

**2 LICENSED FIELD, AT LEAST ONE SILVER.**

**MEDICAL CERTIFIED PREFERRED.**

**NO OPEN FLAME.**

The last rule had been underlined twice.

"Why no flame?" I asked.

The clerk pointed at the sign.

"Because no flame."

Excellent institution.

"Who holds contract?"

"City antiquary office with Guild operations. Site lead is Fen Orl."

Name meant nothing.

Good start.

"What was exposed?"

"Courtyard complex. Maybe civic garden. Maybe shrine. Maybe bath. Antiquary office has changed language three times."

"Active residue?"

"Yes."

"What kind?"

"If we knew, first survey would be second survey."

Also fair.

I read the pay.

Base rate decent.

Three days minimum.

One day travel total because West Bramble sat close enough to Carrow to annoy everyone who liked proper expeditions.

Meals included.

Salvage share small but real.

Personal recovery rights restricted to tagged loose objects under hand-span size, unless antiquary claimed cultural hold.

There.

Fantasy bureaucracy.

I loved civilization.

The clerk said, "Interested?"

I looked at the requirements again.

Ward.

Recovery.

Field.

My mind built the answer before I stopped it.

Lysa.

Tomel.

Me.

One more fighter or medic.

Annoyingly obvious.

The clerk watched my face.

Not everybody needed to become an expert in my face.

"Can I hold the sheet?"

"No."

"Copy terms?"

"Yes."

She slid scrap paper across the counter.

I copied.

Not the roster I wanted.

The requirements.

Important distinction.

Probably.

"When?"

"Depart tomorrow after first bell."

That killed part of the plan immediately.

"Late posting."

"Bank opened wider yesterday. City wants people in before another rain."

"Weather?"

"Dry two days. Maybe three."

"Maybe from who?"

"Sky office."

Actual professional weather people.

Fine.

I folded my copy.

"I want in."

"Then put your name. Site lead chooses final roster before seventh bell today."

She handed me a short registration slip.

**GREG. SILVER. STRUCTURAL SUPPORT / FIELD. SWORD. LIMITED WARD.**

I added **PROSTHETIC, FIELD PEG AVAILABLE** because surprise terrain limitations benefited nobody.

The clerk read it.

"Good."

Allowed use.

I signed.

Then went looking for Tomel.

First attempt at team construction failed because Tomel had a job.

Rude.

I found him at North Recovery Yard with one boot on a wagon axle and half a rope coil in his hands.

Not packing.

Working.

A grain wagon leaned toward the left because one wheel hub had split and somebody had prevented full collapse with two stacked beams that were both beginning to regret participation.

Tomel saw me.

"No."

"I haven't asked."

"You have paper."

"Could be lunch."

"Paper lunch bad."

He went back to the rope.

Two yard workers held a temporary brace while Tomel threaded a line around the wagon frame.

I waited outside the marked work square.

Progress.

Tomel finished the loop.

Called one worker to tension.

The wagon settled half an inch onto the better brace.

Then he walked over.

"What?"

I handed him the copied terms.

He read.

His eyebrows lifted at **SALVAGE TAGS ACTIVE**.

Human.

Then he reached the departure time.

"No."

"Because current wagon?"

"Because current week."

"How long?"

"Whitebank road recovery. Four days minimum. Team leaves tonight."

"You already signed?"

"Yesterday."

There.

People had calendars.

Terrible design.

"Better rate?" I asked.

Tomel folded the paper once and gave it back.

"Much."

"Traitor."

"Pay me difference."

"No."

"Then no."

He turned back toward the wagon.

I said, "You don't even want to know what's in the ruin?"

Tomel looked over one shoulder.

"Yes."

"And?"

"Still have job."

Simple.

Annoying.

"Bring me something stupid," he said.

"Salvage restricted."

"Then describe something stupid."

"Possible."

He went back to work.

One ideal component removed.

Bad framing.

One person unavailable.

Better.

Lysa was harder to find because wardwrights apparently worked indoors when not performing for crowds.

Guild desk had her current contract registry.

Public enough to show availability.

Not public enough to show exact client.

**KERR, LYSA. TEMPORARY SITE WARD. SOUTH ROAD. DEPART 2D.**

Two days.

Our ruin departed tomorrow and claimed three days.

Overlap.

Still worth asking.

I left a note at her boarding house.

She found me before fifth bell at Pell's shop.

Of course Pell's shop had become the local communication nexus for people who disliked formal scheduling.

I was helping Kesta cut failed resin from a heel block.

Helping meant holding it while she did everything skilled.

Lysa walked in carrying the same ugly wrist guide from the exchange.

Pell saw it.

"You broke?"

"No."

"Then why here?"

"Greg."

Pell looked disappointed.

I put the heel block down.

"I have a ruin."

Lysa stared at me.

"You own one?"

"Contract."

"Less interesting."

I handed her the terms.

She read faster than Tomel.

Stopped at the ward requirement.

Then the departure.

"No."

Again.

I was developing expertise.

"South-road job?"

"Yes."

"Two days from now."

"I have setup tomorrow."

"Registry says depart two days."

"Registry does not carry ward frames."

Fair.

She kept reading.

"Who is site lead?"

"Fen Orl."

"Don't know him."

"Me neither."

"Rate is mediocre."

"Salvage."

"Antiquary hold means salvage is theoretical."

"Cynical."

"Experienced."

She handed the sheet back.

No regret.

No temptation strong enough to overturn existing work.

"You asking Tomel?"

"Already failed."

"He has Whitebank."

"Everyone knows except me."

"You were gone."

Right.

Carrow continued without my schedule.

Lysa adjusted the wrist guide.

It had padding now.

Not mine.

I pointed.

"Version two?"

"Version one and a half. Kesta gave me felt."

Kesta said, "Leather bites."

Pell said, "Still ugly."

Lysa ignored him.

"Works better?" I asked.

"Some."

"Keeping?"

"For convoy doors. Maybe wagon ward. Not career."

"Understood."

She studied me.

"You sound disappointed about the ruin."

"I had a clean composition."

"There."

I stopped.

"What?"

"Clean composition."

"For requirements."

"People aren't requirements."

I almost defended the phrase.

Then didn't.

Mostly because she was leaving anyway.

"Fine," I said.

"Who else?"

"Don't know yet."

"Then maybe meet them before fixing them."

"I wasn't fixing."

"Your face."

I pointed at her.

"Retired joke."

She smiled.

Then left with her ugly padded wrist guide and her existing job.

Nine minutes of total recruitment if counted generously.

Two refusals.

One because money and prior commitment.

One because schedule and prior commitment.

No emotional crisis.

This seemed healthy and inconvenient.

At seventh bell I went back to the Guild.

The blue-sheet clerk had moved three pencils from hair to mouth.

"Roster posted," she said around them.

"Where?"

She pointed.

Four names.

**FEN ORL — SILVER — FIELD LEAD / GUIDE**

**SERA NIN — BRONZE — BLADE / ESCORT**

**GREG — SILVER — FIELD / STRUCTURAL SUPPORT**

**NEMI VOSS — BRONZE — RESONANCE SURVEY**

I read the last line twice.

"What's resonance survey?"

The clerk removed one pencil.

"Ask Voss."

"Ward requirement?"

She pointed lower.

Attached fifth name.

**ORRA TEL — SILVER — WARD / MEDICAL BASIC**

Five.

Not four.

I looked at the clerk.

"Job sheet said four roles."

"Roles. Not people."

I hated competent clerks.

"Who chose them?"

"Fen and operations."

"Sera already applied?"

"Before you."

Of course.

Four days of pay apparently remained a powerful summoning ritual.

"Nemi?"

"City antiquary requested resonance survey after second site reading."

"What does she do?"

"Ask Voss."

Consistent system.

"Orra?"

"Ward contractor. Has basic field medical."

I looked at the roster again.

None of them were Tomel.

None were Lysa.

Sera had not been recruited by me.

Two people I had never met filled roles I would have assigned differently.

Fen, the site lead, apparently had opinions too.

Unreasonable structure.

The clerk handed me a departure card.

West gate.

After first bell.

Field rations provided.

Bring bedroll.

No open flame inside site boundary.

I read the last line again.

"Still no reason?"

"Still no flame."

Fine.

I went home to pack for the team I had failed to build.

Promising start.
