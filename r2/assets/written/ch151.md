# Candidate Chapter 151: The Antiquary

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Dessa Kelm arrived angry at a horse.

Not the horse she rode.

A different horse, somewhere behind her on the river road, had apparently blocked a bridge for twenty minutes and become responsible for several failures of local government.

She dismounted above the flood cut, handed the reins to the Guild keeper, and looked down at the exposed courtyard.

Then stopped being angry.

Useful transition.

Dessa was small, maybe fifty, with a gray braid wrapped twice around the back of her head and a leather document case strapped across her chest.

No field coat.

No weapon.

Fen noticed both.

"Stay above markers unless I say."

"I know."

"You asked to carry debris last survey."

"It was a tile."

"It was attached to wall."

"Loosely."

"Above markers."

Dessa looked at me.

"He's like this."

"Good."

Fen pointed at me.

"Don't encourage."

We had known each other less than three hours and boundaries were already forming.

Dessa took out a narrow viewing glass.

Not magical as far as I could tell.

Looked through it at the courtyard.

"The blue-green glaze is municipal."

Nemi said, "Meaning?"

"Public works, probably. Old Carrow river mark uses three-color glaze on water infrastructure and civic gardens. Private houses used cheaper green. Temples used white-blue."

Sera looked at the courtyard.

"So old rich fountain?"

Dessa frowned.

"That sentence has injured several professions."

"Still possible?"

"Yes."

Good answer.

Dessa pointed toward the leaf-bodied statues.

"Those carrying bowls appear on two other old sites. One garden wall east of Carrow. One broken bathhouse panel in city storage."

"Gods?" Orra asked.

"Maybe attendants. Maybe seasons. Maybe gardeners. Maybe civic personifications. Anyone who tells you confidently from three damaged examples is selling a lecture."

I liked her more.

Fen uncoiled marker rope.

"History later. Descent first."

The flood had left one usable route down the upper slope.

Not stairs.

A diagonal shelf of packed earth and exposed stone led toward the courtyard's highest corner.

Maybe thirty feet of descent.

Muddy in three places.

Root mat above.

Open drop toward the river below if someone slid too far.

Fen walked it alone first.

No rope.

Annoying confidence.

He tested each step with the heel before committing weight.

At the first muddy patch he drove one blue marker stake.

At the second he stopped, backed up, and chose a different line.

No performance.

No explanation until he returned.

"Upper route only," he said. "No one steps below the white root line. Bank undercuts there. Orra, one ward face on river side at middle mud. Not a catch-all. Just enough to stop a slip from becoming a fall."

Orra nodded.

"Sera second. Greg third. Nemi fourth. Kelm behind me."

Dessa said, "I can walk."

"Then prove it in position five."

She muttered something about field leads.

Fen ignored her.

Orra descended first after him to place the ward.

Two copper stakes into firm soil.

One waist-high gray plane between them.

No dome.

No glowing road.

Just a wall where the consequence was worst.

Sera went next.

Fast enough to become irritating.

I changed my grip on the short staff and followed with the field peg.

The crawler-tendon washer softened the sharpest impacts but the muddy slope made every placement a question.

Right boot.

Peg.

Staff.

Move.

At the first marker, the exposed stone beneath my peg shifted.

Tiny.

Enough.

I stopped.

"Loose," I called.

Fen looked up from below.

"Can you step over?"

"Yes."

"Then step over."

No need to repair geology.

I stepped over.

Nemi followed, carrying her little leather resonance case against her chest with both hands.

Dessa came last under Fen's direct supervision and complained exactly once when he told her where to place her foot.

"I have crossed excavation slopes for thirty years."

"Then you have thirty years of practice putting your foot where I tell you."

Sera covered her mouth.

I looked away professionally.

We reached the upper corner of the courtyard.

Closer, the glaze looked less decorative.

The channels were built into the stone floor, each about two fingers wide and shallow enough to step across.

Blue at center.

Green edges.

Some branches ended in small round cups cut into the paving.

Others vanished beneath fallen mud.

Water sat in most of them.

Clearer than the river.

I did not drink it.

Growth.

The leaf-bodied statue nearest us had no head.

Its stone bowl remained intact.

Dry.

Everything was fucking dry where it should not have been.

Dessa crouched beside the base without crossing Fen's marked line.

"Here."

She brushed mud away with a wooden spatula.

Three glazed marks appeared.

A wavy line.

Open circle.

Three short strokes above.

"Water mark," she said. "Public collection. The open circle is storage or distribution. The top marks might be season, district, or capacity."

"Might," Nemi said.

Dessa looked at her.

"Yes."

They seemed likely to get along through mutual refusal to invent certainty.

Fen said, "Voss. First read from here."

Nemi put down the leather case.

Opened it.

Inside sat six brass rods, each different length, wrapped separately in cloth.

Two small wood blocks.

One slate.

A little sand timer.

No glowing crystal.

Disappointing profession.

She chose the third-shortest rod.

Held one end lightly against the glazed channel.

Then struck the rod with a wood block.

Ting.

Small sound.

The channel answered.

Not echo exactly.

A faint ringing moved away from us through the stone.

One branch.

Then another.

Blue-green glaze caught sunlight as if the sound were moving color, though maybe my eyes invented that after the fact.

Nemi held still.

Everyone else did too.

The first return came almost immediately.

Thin.

Same pitch.

Nemi said, "Near branch open."

Second return arrived lower.

Not same note.

She frowned.

"Something changes material between here and center."

Dessa whispered, "Basin?"

Nemi did not answer.

Good.

Third return came late.

Very late.

Not from the direction of the basin.

From our left.

Toward the buried upper wall.

Softer.

Then again.

Two taps back.

Nemi lifted the rod away.

The sound stopped.

Sera had already turned left.

Hand on sword.

Fen said, "Report."

Nemi stared at the mud-covered wall.

"Three returns. One normal branch. One altered path toward center. One delayed response left."

"Active magic?" Orra asked.

"Could be. Could be cavity. Could be material that stores vibration."

"Two taps?" I asked.

Nemi looked at me.

"I struck once."

There.

Not explanation.

Just fact.

Fen moved the blue boundary rope five feet farther from the left wall.

"Nobody approaches that side yet."

Dessa looked physically pained.

"There may be an intact room behind it."

"Then it will remain intact while we don't die."

She muttered again.

We moved along the upper courtyard instead.

Fen first.

Sera watching mud and wall.

Orra watching channel crossings.

Nemi now holding the brass rod without striking it.

I watched footing and the relationship between exposed paving and the bank beneath.

The courtyard tilted toward the river maybe four degrees.

Enough that any free water should have run downhill.

It did not.

Water in the channels sat level inside each branch as if every little section had its own idea of down.

I crouched beside one.

No touch.

"Orra. You feel active field?"

She came over.

Held one hand above the glaze.

"Weak. Distributed."

"Directional?"

"Not enough to say."

Nemi said, "Tap would tell path."

Fen looked at the left wall.

Then at the channel.

"One more. Different source."

Nemi chose another rod.

Longer.

Lower tone.

Placed it against a dry cup in the paving instead of the channel.

Struck.

Tonnng.

The courtyard answered deeper.

I felt that one through the field peg.

Not loud.

Physical.

The blue-green channels shimmered.

This time I was sure.

Water inside them trembled outward from us.

Toward the center basin.

Every branch.

All at once.

The dry basin answered with a sound like somebody dragging one wet finger around the rim of a glass.

Long.

Rising.

Dessa inhaled sharply.

Nemi lifted her rod.

The sound continued.

That was worse.

Orra unfolded one ward stake.

Sera drew her sword.

Fen said, "Hold positions."

Nobody moved.

The basin kept singing.

Then the nearest leaf-bodied statue poured water from its dry bowl.

One thin stream.

Clear.

Impossible source.

It hit the glazed channel below.

The water already in the channel began moving.

Not downhill.

Toward the dry basin.

One branch after another joined it.

The courtyard woke quietly.

No explosion.

No light column.

Just water changing its mind.

Dessa whispered, "Collection court."

Nemi said, "Maybe."

Good antiquary.

Better listener.

Fen pointed toward the descent route.

"Exit remains open. Nobody advances. We watch one minute."

The first scream came from the mud near the far wall.

Not human.

High and wet.

Sera smiled.

"There."

I looked at her.

"You're enjoying this."

"Finally a job."

The mud moved.

Four-toed tracks had been information.

Whatever made them was still here.
