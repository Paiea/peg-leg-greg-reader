# Candidate Chapter 154: The Warder

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

By sunrise the white shoots had leaves.

Of course they did.

Small ones.

Translucent.

Almost colorless until light passed through them and turned the edges pale green.

They grew in lines beside the glazed channels, not randomly across the courtyard.

Every active branch had them.

Dry paving did not.

The central basin still held nothing except mist.

Dessa stood above the marker line with both hands pressed to her mouth.

For once, no notes.

Fen said, "Kelm."

She lowered her hands.

"I'm here."

"You look like you're about to ignore a rope."

"I am looking at an extinct garden."

"You are looking at a garden from above the rope."

"You have no poetry."

"Correct."

Orra knelt beside the first patch of shoots without crossing the marker.

She had wrapped a strip of white cloth over her nose and mouth.

Not dramatic.

Practical.

The rest of us wore the same now because the morning air smelled different.

Sweet.

Sharp underneath.

Pine resin mixed with flowers and something metallic.

The no-flame rule had become less bureaucratic.

Orra held a glass vial near the nearest leaves.

No magic.

Just open air.

After ten breaths she corked it.

Then held a second vial lower, near the glazed channel.

Fen asked, "What do you know?"

"Nothing yet."

Sera looked at the masks.

"Reassuring."

Orra ignored her.

She took one copper ward stake and planted it in bare soil above the marker.

Opened a gray face no bigger than a door.

Then held the first air vial on one side and a fresh empty vial on the other.

Waited.

"Filtering?" I asked.

"Testing movement."

Right.

I shut up.

After a minute she corked the third vial.

Labeled all three with chalk.

**OPEN AIR.**

**CHANNEL LOW.**

**BEHIND WARD.**

Dessa said, "Can you identify pollen here?"

"No."

"Then what are you doing?"

"Finding whether the ward changes what reaches us."

Dessa considered.

"Fair."

Orra looked offended by borrowed vocabulary.

Nemi sat several feet away with one brass rod against the ground.

No pulse.

Passive listening.

She had slept badly.

So had I.

Sera apparently slept through magical horticulture and woke angry that nobody had summoned her for plants.

"They don't have teeth," she said.

"Yet," I said.

Orra pointed at me.

"Do not help."

Fen walked the upper boundary while we waited.

The injured bank drake had not reappeared.

New tracks crossed the far mud during the night, but none came above the lower courtyard.

Maybe one animal.

Maybe more.

No one claimed certainty.

Orra finally opened the first vial again.

Smelled it carefully.

Then the channel vial.

Her eyebrows rose.

"Stronger low."

"Pollen?" Fen asked.

"Resin or oil vapor. Maybe pollen too. Sweet component is stronger near water."

She opened the ward-side vial.

Smelled.

"Less."

"Ward catches it?" Sera asked.

"Some."

"Enough?"

"For what?"

Sera gestured at the ruin.

"Going down."

Orra looked at her.

"That is not a concentration unit."

Sera sighed.

Medical people remained difficult.

Orra stood.

"We enter masked. Short exposure. Ward face between us and dense growth when possible. Nobody touches leaves bare-handed. If anyone gets dizzy, mouth numb, eyes burning, throat tight, or stupid, say it."

I looked at Sera.

"How do we distinguish last one?"

She kicked my field peg lightly.

"Baseline established."

Healthy team culture.

Fen approved a limited morning route.

Upper courtyard only.

From the first statue to the edge of the central basin approach.

No buried-wall opening.

No far-bank channel where the drake escaped.

We descended in yesterday's order except Dessa stayed at the observation line with Fen until the rest of us reached the first statue.

Orra went first this time.

Not because she led route.

Because the hazard was hers.

She carried one ward stake in each hand.

At the first dense patch of shoots she planted both stakes three feet apart and opened a narrow gray wall between us and the plants.

The leaves bent away from it.

Not from force.

From air movement redirected by the ward.

A dusting of pale gold lifted from their undersides.

There.

Pollen.

Enough that I saw it in sunlight.

Sera stepped back.

"Fine. Masks good."

Orra said, "Masks help. Ward better. Don't walk through that cloud."

We waited for it to settle.

Nemi listened through one brass rod.

"The plants are changing the channel tone."

Fen asked, "Danger?"

"No idea."

"Operationally?"

"Harder to hear movement through dense growth."

There.

Trade.

The waking garden made one tool better and another worse depending problem.

Nobody became obsolete.

We moved.

The first leaf-bodied statue had changed overnight too.

Water still poured from the stone bowl in a thread no thicker than string.

Around its feet, the white shoots were taller.

Knee-high now.

Dessa came close enough to inspect the statue base while staying behind Orra's ward line.

"There are planting sockets."

I looked.

Small round holes in the paving around the pedestal.

Too regular for cracks.

"So actual garden," Sera said.

Dessa gave her a look.

"Yes."

"I solved history."

"Please stop."

Sera looked pleased.

We reached the next channel intersection.

Three branches.

Water moving toward the basin through all of them.

The pale plants along one branch had opened tiny clear pods.

Inside each pod sat a black seed.

Or maybe bead.

Dessa crouched.

"Do not touch."

Nobody had moved.

She looked at me specifically.

Reputation had become cross-professional.

"I wasn't."

"Good."

Unallowed use.

Nemi put her rod against the paving outside the plant line.

No strike.

"Something under us is moving."

Fen stopped immediately.

"Direction?"

"Toward center. Slow. Repeating."

I felt nothing through the field peg.

"Mechanical?"

Nemi frowned.

"Maybe."

Dessa said, "Water wheel?"

"Underground?" Sera asked.

"Old gardens used lift screws, pumps, pressure cisterns."

"Would they still work?"

Dessa looked at the plants.

"Apparently some things do."

Fen looked at me.

"Ground?"

My turn.

I crouched away from the channel.

Put the short staff end against the paving.

No magic first.

Tiny vibration.

Regular.

Not enough to locate.

I formed one small feathered plane flat against the stone beside the staff tip.

Not holding anything.

Feeling local movement through contact.

There.

Pulse.

Pause.

Pulse.

Moving slowly toward the basin.

"Nemi's right."

Sera said, "Shocking."

"I am documenting independent verification."

"You put stick on floor."

"Advanced discipline."

Fen asked, "Does paving move?"

"Not meaningfully. Something below transfers vibration."

"Safe to cross?"

I looked at the seam pattern.

Old stone.

No fresh displacement.

No hollow sound under staff taps here.

"From structure alone, yes."

"Good enough."

Fen moved us another fifteen feet.

The central basin now filled half our view.

Closer, the impossible dryness looked worse.

Water from at least six channels reached its outer rim.

Then vanished through narrow slots cut beneath a raised stone lip.

Nothing pooled inside.

Mist rotated above the empty center.

The basin floor was carved with hundreds of shallow circles.

Different sizes.

Overlapping.

Like rain marks preserved in stone.

Dessa stopped breathing for a second.

Then remembered.

"Those marks appear on the bathhouse panel," she said.

Nemi said, "Meaning?"

Dessa smiled despite herself.

"I don't know."

Professional love story.

Orra raised one hand.

"Stop."

Everyone did.

She pointed at Sera.

"Eyes."

Sera blinked.

"What?"

"Red."

"Mud."

"Eyes."

Sera rubbed one.

Bad move.

Orra caught her wrist.

"Don't."

She opened the medical satchel.

Clean cloth.

Water flask.

Rinsed around both eyes while Sera complained about dignity.

"Burning?"

"A little."

"Throat?"

"No."

"Dizzy?"

"No."

Orra looked at the rest of us.

"We're done."

Dessa said, "We just reached the basin."

Orra did not look at her.

"Sera has exposure symptom."

"Minor."

"Still symptom. We do not know dose curve."

Fen said, "Out."

Immediate.

Sera looked at the basin.

Then Orra.

"I can see."

"Congratulations. Out."

Sera sheathed her sword harder than necessary.

Then obeyed.

We turned around.

No one got to inspect the rain-mark carving.

No one tested the basin.

No one opened the buried wall.

The ruin remained unsolved because someone's eyes were red.

I liked that more than I expected.

Halfway back to the first statue, Nemi stopped.

"Wait."

Fen turned.

"What?"

Nemi held up one hand.

The slow vibration beneath the paving had stopped.

I checked with the staff.

Nothing.

Then behind us, from the basin, came one low stone click.

A second.

A third.

Water stopped flowing.

All at once.

Every channel went still.

The white plants folded their translucent leaves inward.

Gold pollen vanished from the air as if a door had closed.

Orra watched the nearest leaves.

"Better. Move."

We moved.

At the top marker, Sera pulled off the mask and blinked hard in clean wind.

Orra checked her again.

Redness already fading.

"Mild exposure," she said. "Still counts."

Sera looked down at the suddenly quiet courtyard.

"Did it stop because we left?"

Nemi said, "Maybe."

Dessa said, "Or cycle ended."

Fen said, "Or something below finished moving."

I looked at the dry basin.

The mist was thinner now.

Almost gone.

At the buried left wall, one narrow line had appeared in the mud.

Vertical.

Too straight for a crack.

A seam.

Whatever had been moving under the court had done something.

Dessa saw it too.

Her entire body leaned forward without her feet crossing the marker.

Growth.

"Door," she whispered.

Nemi looked at the seam.

Then at her brass rods.

"Maybe."

Sera wiped water from her face.

"Tomorrow?"

Fen looked at Orra.

Orra looked at Sera's eyes.

Then the closed plants.

"Tomorrow if air stays clear."

Fen nodded.

Nobody asked me.

Correct decision.
