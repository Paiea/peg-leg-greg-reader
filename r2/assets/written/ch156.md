# Candidate Chapter 156: The Gardener

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

The gardener tried to prune Orra's ward.

This answered one question.

Not the useful one.

The old service cart rolled down the bronze rail toward us at walking speed.

Three jointed arms raised above the stone basket.

Hooked blade open.

Clamp clicking.

Bald brush turning slowly against nothing.

Orra's gray ward face stood across half the gallery behind us.

Delay surface.

Not barrier meant for mechanical gardening.

The hooked arm reached it.

Touched gray light.

Stopped.

Then closed like shears.

The ward snapped inward where the blade crossed.

Orra swore and released before the construct could load her frame harder.

The gardener rolled forward.

No hesitation.

No triumph.

Just work.

"It cuts magic," Sera said.

"It cut obstruction," Nemi said.

"Same result if obstruction is me."

Fen had us almost back to the shutter now.

"Wall side. Give rail clear."

We pressed into the service niches where possible.

Dessa held both cold-light beads against her chest.

I put my field peg behind one stone lip so the cart could not catch it if clearance went bad.

Sera stood nearest the rail with sword ready but down.

Good restraint.

The gardener reached us.

Its clamp arm opened.

Passed six inches from Sera's sleeve.

Did nothing.

Hook arm passed me.

Did nothing.

Brush scraped the bronze rail.

The construct rolled by.

Click.

Click.

Click.

Nobody died.

Sera looked disappointed.

"So it ignores people."

Nemi said, "It ignored us while we were not blocking rail."

Sera pointed at her.

"You make every sentence less comforting."

"Skill."

The gardener reached the open shutter.

Then stopped.

All three arms folded halfway.

Its stone basket tilted.

A little pile of ancient dirt slid from one corner.

The clamp reached toward the shutter track.

Pinched one clump of root.

Pulled.

Root tore free.

Dropped into basket.

The brush arm scrubbed the track.

The hook cut another root.

Maintenance.

Dessa made a sound behind me.

Happy this time.

"Caretaker."

Sera said, "We established gardener."

"Specific function matters."

"It has scissors."

"Pruning assembly."

"Scissors."

Fen said, "Nobody touches it."

Both stopped arguing.

The gardener finished clearing the shutter track.

Then rolled backward.

Not turned.

Backward.

Same speed.

Straight toward us again.

Sera sighed.

"Wall."

We moved into the niches a second time.

The machine passed.

No attack.

No interest.

At the first broken jar, the clamp arm stopped.

Reached down.

Picked up one ceramic shard from the floor.

Dropped it into the basket.

Brush swept black seed powder out of the niche.

Hook cut a dried root hanging through one grate.

Then it continued into darkness.

Dessa whispered, "It's still doing rounds."

"Badly," Sera said.

One wheel squealed every fourth rotation.

Nemi looked at her.

"I agree."

Small victory.

Fen let the construct move twenty feet away before speaking.

"We know its route?"

"No," Nemi said.

"Can you hear it on rail?"

"Yes."

"Good. Keep that."

No ceremony.

One useful job added.

We resumed exploration behind the gardener rather than ahead of it.

This felt like following an elderly janitor through a dangerous museum.

The gallery extended farther than the exposed wall suggested.

Forty feet.

Then turned right beneath the courtyard.

The bronze rails turned too.

At the corner, three side pockets opened in the wall, each wide enough for the gardener to leave the main track.

Two empty.

One held a second construct.

Inactive.

Worse condition.

One wheel missing.

Brush gone.

Hook arm bent inward.

Dessa immediately approached until Fen caught the back strap of her document case.

"Marker."

"It's inactive."

"You don't know that."

Nemi said, "I don't hear it."

Dessa looked vindicated.

Nemi added, "That means I don't hear it."

Vindication removed.

I crouched beside the track switch leading into the service pocket.

Simple bronze tongue in the floor.

The active gardener's rail passed over it earlier without diverting.

A small lever sat against the wall.

Old handle wrapped in something that had once been leather.

"Service stop," I said.

Fen looked over.

"Guess?"

"Yes."

Dessa said, "Likely."

Sera smiled.

"Two guesses. Strong evidence."

I left the lever alone.

Progress again.

Past the service pockets, the gallery narrowed.

More niches.

More ceramic tokens.

Most common marks repeated.

Wave.

Leaf.

Circle.

Bird.

Fish.

Snail.

Dessa tagged sample groups rather than individual pieces.

Sera found three tiny bronze coins caught behind a cracked shelf.

Not ancient royal treasure.

Small round civic pieces with a hole through the center.

Dessa cleaned one enough to read the stamp.

"Work issue. Garden labor token, maybe meal or tool deposit."

Sera looked at it.

"Worth?"

"To collector? A few copper if common. Maybe more if district mark is rare."

Sera tagged all three.

"Adventure."

"You killed two drakes."

"Coins."

Clear values.

I found a narrow bronze blade in a wall slot.

No handle left.

Curved inward like a pruning knife.

Edge still bright under dirt.

That was more interesting than the snail.

I did not touch it.

Dessa saw me looking.

"Attached?"

"Slotted."

She inspected.

Pulled one tag from her case.

"Loose tool in fitted rack. City hold until function assessed."

"Cruel."

"Legal."

"Often same family."

She tagged the blade.

I kept my snail.

Morally superior loot.

The active gardener's wheel squeak changed behind us.

Nemi lifted one hand.

"Coming back."

Fen pointed to the nearest service pocket.

"Can we use that?"

I looked at the track switch.

"Maybe if lever diverts it."

"Need to?"

Nemi listened.

"Same speed."

Fen looked at our narrow section.

Not enough niches for six people plus machine without crowding.

"We need it off main rail."

There.

Actual reason to touch mechanism.

Fen said, "Greg, lever only. Sera ready if machine changes behavior. Nemi call distance."

I moved to the switch.

No big magic.

No plan beyond one mechanical question.

Nemi listened through the rail.

"Thirty feet."

I tested the lever with two fingers.

Stuck.

Of course.

"Twenty-five."

I pulled harder.

Nothing.

Bronze tongue at the floor looked clear.

Handle shaft disappeared into wall grime.

Maybe corrosion.

Maybe load from switch plate.

"Twenty."

Sera said, "Want sword?"

"For lever?"

"For emotional support."

"No."

I put one tiny feathered plane behind the lever base.

Not to yank.

Take side load off the shaft while I pulled.

"Fifteen."

The lever moved.

Half inch.

Then another.

Floor tongue slid toward the service pocket.

"Ten."

I released the plane.

Lever seated with a dull click.

"Done."

We pressed to the far wall.

The gardener appeared around the corner.

Click.

Squeal.

Click.

Squeal.

It reached the switch.

Front wheels followed the diverted tongue.

Then rear wheels did not.

Of course.

The left rear wheel climbed the rail edge.

The whole construct tilted.

Hook arm swung outward.

Directly toward Sera.

She moved.

Fast.

The hook missed her coat by inches.

Gardener wheel dropped hard against stone.

Sera drew her sword.

"Your switch sucks."

"Old switch."

"Your lever."

"Shared historical failure."

The construct tried to keep moving.

One front wheel in pocket track.

Rear wheels on main.

Twisted across both.

Now actually blocking us.

And loading its own armatures badly.

Nemi said, "It's speeding up."

"Why?" Fen asked.

"I don't know."

Hook arm opened and closed twice.

Clamp struck wall.

Brush spun uselessly.

Not attacking.

Failing.

Dessa said, "Don't break it."

Sera said, "Stop saying that during dangerous things."

Fen looked at me.

"Can you put rear wheel into pocket?"

I looked.

Wrong question maybe.

Front wheel already captured.

Rear wheel needed maybe two inches sideways, not a stop.

"Yes. Sera, don't cut unless it leaves rail."

"If it leaves rail, I cut everything."

"Fair."

I crouched outside hook reach.

Small plane against rear wheel rim.

Not strong.

Angle toward service track.

The gardener drove itself forward.

Wheel hit plane.

Plane bent.

Two inches was a lot when bronze machinery disagreed.

I feathered edge.

Let wheel climb instead of forcing it sideways.

Half inch.

One.

Hook arm snapped toward empty air.

Nemi said, "Faster."

"I noticed."

"Reporting."

Wheel climbed the rail lip.

Dropped into pocket track.

The whole gardener straightened.

Immediately slowed.

Click.

Click.

No squeal.

It rolled into the service bay beside the broken second machine.

At the back, a bronze bumper touched the wall.

The gardener stopped.

Arms folded.

Completely still.

Everyone waited.

Five breaths.

Ten.

Nothing.

Sera sheathed her sword.

"We fixed gardener."

Dessa looked delighted.

"You parked it."

"Fixed enough."

Nemi crouched beside the main rail.

"Signal stopped when bumper touched."

Fen looked at the back of the service pocket.

"So actual stop."

I looked at the lever.

Then the bad rear-wheel alignment.

"Switch needs both tongues. One didn't move fully."

Sera said, "Your lever still sucks."

"Accepted."

No larger lesson required.

We left the gardener parked.

At the next corner, cold light from Dessa's bead caught blue-green glaze on a descending stair.

Not buried earth.

Built stair.

Five visible steps.

Then darkness.

Water sound below.

Not river.

Steady.

Contained.

Fen stopped us at the top.

First survey did not require proving courage to stairs.

"Mark and back," he said.

Nobody argued.

Dessa placed a numbered tag at the wall.

Nemi listened down the stair without pulsing.

Orra checked air.

Sera looked disappointed again.

I held the legally tagged blue-green snail in one hand while we waited.

The snail remained stupid.

The stairs remained dark.

Both could wait until tomorrow.
