# Candidate Chapter 157: The Surveyor

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Nothing attacked us overnight.

This felt suspiciously generous.

Sera complained anyway.

"Waste of watch rotation."

Fen handed her breakfast.

"You wanted attack?"

"No."

"Then eat."

She ate.

Simple leadership.

The garden stayed inactive through dawn.

No water movement.

No new pollen release.

White leaves folded tight beside the channels.

Orra sampled air before we crossed the marker and again at the service shutter.

Clear enough.

Masks stayed around our necks in case the cycle changed.

The gardener remained parked in the side bay.

Still.

Sera tapped the stone basket once with one knuckle.

"Don't," Dessa said.

"I barely."

"Don't barely either."

Progress everywhere.

At the descending stair, Fen stopped us.

Cold-light beads from Dessa's case went down first.

Not thrown.

Lowered on cord.

One bead illuminated six steps.

Then eight.

Then a landing.

No moving shadow.

No water across the stair.

Fen gave us stop conditions.

"Air changes, Orra calls out. Water rises onto walking surface, out. Nemi hears active machinery approaching, stop. Sera sees creature sign fresher than us, stop. Greg sees structural movement, stop. Kelm sees something fascinating, she says it instead of walking toward it."

Dessa said, "Unfair."

"Accurate."

We descended.

Fen first.

Sera second.

Nemi third today because the lower space belonged more to unknown machinery than stone.

I followed.

Orra behind me.

Dessa last.

The stair turned once beneath the courtyard.

Stone walls dry.

Blue-green glaze appeared in thin vertical strips every few steps.

Not channels now.

Markers maybe.

Nemi touched none.

At the landing, the water sound became obvious.

Not flowing river.

Continuous pressure.

Like rain against a window heard from another room.

Fen raised one hand.

We stopped.

The cold-light bead showed a corridor opening ahead.

Then the corridor wasn't a corridor.

It opened into a long chamber at least forty feet wide.

I forgot to move.

Water stood upright.

Not in a tank.

Not behind glass.

Three enormous vertical sheets of water stretched from floor to ceiling between bronze frames.

Each sheet maybe ten feet wide and a foot thick.

Clear enough to see through.

No wall holding either face.

Just water.

Standing.

The nearest one trembled slowly like fabric in wind.

Behind it, another.

Then a third farther into the chamber.

Service walkways ran between them.

Stone floor.

Bronze railings.

Drain grooves.

Old work benches built into the walls.

No temple.

No throne.

A water room designed by people who apparently found gravity negotiable.

Sera said, "Fuck."

Allowed use.

Dessa whispered something I could not hear.

Nemi did not speak at all.

Fen looked at Orra.

"Ward field?"

She held one hand forward.

"Strong. Stable from here. Different from upper court."

Then Nemi.

"Machinery?"

Nemi put one brass rod against the floor.

No pulse.

Listened.

"Three repeating sources. Slow. One per water frame maybe. Fourth rhythm deeper."

Fen looked at me.

"Structure?"

I stared at the nearest upright water sheet.

Then forced myself to look at what held it.

Bronze frame tied into floor and ceiling.

Stone around both anchor lines old but not cracked.

Water mass enormous.

Actual weight had to go somewhere even if magic changed direction.

Maybe.

"Frames carry something," I said. "I don't know if they carry the water's full weight. Anchors look stable. Walkway looks separate."

"Enough for first step?"

I checked the floor seam.

Tapped with staff.

Solid.

"Yes."

Fen advanced ten feet.

We followed.

The nearest water sheet changed as we approached.

Tiny droplets lifted from its surface and hung in air.

Orra stopped us.

"Masks."

We pulled them up.

She sampled the droplets with one cloth strip.

No color.

No smell through mask.

"Not pollen," she said. "Still unknown."

Fen waited.

No symptoms.

We moved again.

At the base of the first frame, a bronze plate carried six glazed symbols.

Dessa crouched.

"District marks."

Sera said, "Certain?"

Dessa looked at her.

"More than door."

Improvement.

She pointed.

Wave.

Open circle.

Three short strokes.

Then two marks I had not seen upstairs.

"This one appears on old north-field irrigation deeds. This one on drought allocation tablets."

Nemi asked, "So storage?"

Dessa smiled.

"Water allocation. Maybe storage too."

Better historian.

The dry basin above was not failing to fill.

It was sending water somewhere else.

Probably here.

Maybe.

I kept the word internal.

Nemi moved beside the first frame.

"I want one pulse."

Fen said, "Reason."

"Separate frame rhythm from deeper rhythm."

"Risk?"

"Could change cycle."

Sera said, "That's a risk."

Nemi nodded.

"Yes."

Fen looked at Orra.

"If cycle starts?"

"We already know upper pollen begins with active water. Down here unknown."

Fen looked at the stairs behind us.

Then at the chamber.

"One pulse from floor, not frame. Everyone stays behind first rail."

Nemi chose a medium rod.

Set it against the stone walkway.

Struck.

Tone traveled through floor.

The nearest water sheet rippled.

Not violently.

One vertical wave crossed left to right.

Second sheet answered after.

Third.

Then from deeper beneath us came a fourth return.

Low.

Regular.

Nemi lifted the rod.

The three water sheets settled.

The deeper rhythm continued.

"Separate," she said.

Fen asked, "Meaning?"

"Water frames respond to local network. Deeper source is already active."

"Pump?"

Dessa asked.

Nemi looked at her.

"Rhythm."

Dessa accepted the correction.

We followed the first walkway.

Not far.

Fen had set a hard first-survey limit at the second frame.

Enough to map geometry.

Enough to identify exits.

Not enough to turn discovery into conquest.

Between the first and second water sheets, the wall benches held maintenance equipment in better condition than upstairs.

Dry environment.

Less plant intrusion.

Bronze clamps.

Ceramic gauges.

Coiled tubing gone brittle with age.

One palm-sized device sat in a fitted recess.

Blue ceramic disk inside a bronze ring.

Three tiny fins around the edge.

Nemi stopped.

So did Dessa.

Sera looked at both.

"Expensive face."

Dessa said, "Do not touch."

No one had.

Nemi crouched beside it.

Not close enough for sleeves to brush.

"It's ringing."

I heard nothing.

"Without contact?"

"Very faint. Same family as frame."

Dessa opened her case.

Pulled a red city-hold tag instead of white salvage.

"Intact regulator, maybe."

Nemi said, "Maybe."

Dessa ignored her.

"Similar shape appears in one north-field drawing."

"What does drawing call it?" I asked.

Dessa hesitated.

"Translation is disputed."

Sera smiled.

"Say the stupid version."

Dessa glared.

"Rain tooth."

Silence.

I looked at the ceramic disk.

Sera looked at me.

Nemi covered her mouth.

Fen said, "Nobody say anything."

Too late.

"Rain tooth," Sera repeated.

Dessa tied the red tag to the recess.

"Provisional translation."

"Excellent artifact name."

"It is not necessarily artifact."

"Better artifact name."

I liked Sera more every day.

Nemi did not touch the rain tooth.

She wanted to.

That surprised me.

Not because she lacked curiosity.

Because it was the first object here that seemed aimed directly at her skill.

Fen noticed too.

"Need it for survey?"

Nemi thought.

Longer than I expected.

"No."

Simple.

"Would it help?"

"Probably."

"Still no?"

"Red tag. City object. We can learn without removing it today."

There.

Different temptation.

Not mine.

Good.

We continued.

At the second frame, the walkway widened around a round service station with four floor sockets and one empty chair mount.

No chair.

No operator.

One drainage channel ran from the base of the standing water into the floor.

Dry.

I crouched beside it.

The deep rhythm beneath us became easier to feel through the field peg.

Pulse.

Pause.

Pulse.

Not machine exactly.

Too slow.

Like a huge valve opening and closing somewhere below.

Nemi listened.

"Fourth source under this station."

Dessa pointed at a glazed label near the drain.

"Distribution below."

"Certain?" Sera asked.

Dessa read again.

"No."

Everyone improving badly.

Fen checked the time.

"Limit. Map and back."

Dessa looked toward the third frame.

"We can see another passage beyond."

"Yes."

"Ten more minutes."

"No."

"Five."

"No."

"You negotiate terribly."

"I win often."

He did.

We turned back.

Halfway to the stairs, the nearest water sheet moved.

Not ripple.

Lean.

The entire vertical surface bowed toward us by maybe six inches.

Orra shouted, "Stop."

We stopped.

The bronze frame hummed.

Nemi's head snapped toward the floor.

"Cycle."

The deep rhythm accelerated.

Pulse.

Pulse.

Pulse.

Upstairs, somewhere above tons of stone, the garden was waking again.

Fen pointed at the stairs.

"Out. Now."

No debate.

We moved.
