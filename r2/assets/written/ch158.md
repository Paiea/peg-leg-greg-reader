# Candidate Chapter 158: The Caller

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Fen did not shout faster when the room became dangerous.

I noticed because everyone else did.

"Stairs," he said.

Same volume as breakfast.

We moved.

The nearest standing water sheet bowed farther toward the walkway.

Six inches became eight.

Ten.

No glass wall.

No visible membrane.

Just tons of water apparently reconsidering verticality.

Nemi looked over one shoulder while walking.

"Frame tone rising."

Fen said, "Meaning?"

"Load changing."

"Failure?"

"Unknown."

"Keep moving."

Good.

Orra pulled her mask up before anyone else remembered.

"Masks."

We obeyed.

Dessa tried to look back at the rain tooth.

Sera put one hand between her shoulder blades and redirected her physically toward the stairs.

"History later."

Dessa stumbled one step.

"I can walk."

"Then do it forward."

Also good.

The second water sheet started moving as we passed it.

Not bowing the same direction.

It pulled inward toward its own bronze frame as if the center had become heavier than the edges.

The frame gave one low metallic groan.

I felt it through the floor.

Old anchor stone took load.

Held.

For now.

"Greg?" Fen asked.

"Frame two carrying more. Anchors still clean."

"Need action?"

"No."

One of my favorite new answers.

We reached the first frame.

The walkway beneath it was wet now.

Droplets had become spray.

Not enough to flood.

Enough to make old stone slippery.

Sera's boot slid half a hand.

She recovered.

"Wet," she said.

Useful report from the blade.

Fen pointed at the inner rail.

"Hands on rail. One line."

We did.

Nemi first.

Dessa behind her.

Orra.

Me.

Sera.

Fen last now.

He had changed order without declaring philosophy.

People who needed clean information went ahead.

People who could deal with something coming behind stayed back.

Simple.

We crossed the wet stretch.

The deep rhythm under the service station accelerated again.

Pulse-pulse.

Pause.

Pulse-pulse.

Nemi stopped.

Fen said, "Why?"

"Sequence changed."

"Can you walk and listen?"

"Yes."

"Then walk."

She walked.

No insult.

No argument.

At the stair landing, water struck stone behind us.

A heavy slap.

Everyone turned except Fen.

"Keep moving."

We kept moving.

I looked anyway.

The first vertical sheet had thrown one arm of water out beyond its frame.

Not collapsed.

A thick loop, maybe barrel-wide, curved through open air and entered a floor drain three feet away.

Designed.

Probably.

The service chamber was not failing.

It was changing modes.

That made it safer in one way and worse in several others.

"Not breach," I called. "Looks routed."

Fen said, "Doesn't change exit."

Correct.

We climbed.

Halfway up, the blue-green glaze strips in the stair walls brightened.

Not light exactly.

Color saturated until the blue looked almost black and the green almost gold.

Then the service gallery above answered with the gardener's wheel squeak.

Sera groaned.

"Of course."

Nemi said, "It's moving."

"Which way?" Fen asked.

She put one hand on the bronze rail at the top landing.

"Toward shutter."

We reached the gallery.

The gardener had left its service bay.

The old switch was still set toward the pocket.

Apparently the machine knew another route out or had forced the tongue back.

It rolled toward the open shutter, arms unfolding one by one.

Hook.

Clamp.

Brush.

Sera said, "We can walk behind."

Fen looked at Nemi.

"Speed?"

"Normal."

"Then yes. Behind it. Nobody passes."

We followed the gardener.

Ridiculous escort.

Outside the shutter, the upper courtyard had transformed again.

Water moved in every channel.

The pale plants stood open and waist-high now.

Clear leaves shook in air with no wind.

Gold pollen lifted in clouds above them.

Orra stopped us before the threshold.

"No."

Fen said, "Route?"

She looked at the open courtyard.

Our direct path to the descent slope crossed two dense plant lines.

Yesterday she had warded one.

Today both were producing enough pollen to turn sunlight yellow.

"I can make a corridor," she said. "Short. We move inside it. Nobody breaks ward edge."

"Do it."

Orra planted one stake at the shutter and one ten feet ahead.

Gray ward faces formed on both sides of a narrow path.

Not roof.

Air moved between them toward the open river.

Gold pollen struck the outer faces and curled away.

"Move to second stake. I reset from there," Orra said.

Fen pointed.

"Voss, Kelm, Greg, Sera. I'll follow Orra."

We entered.

The gardener rolled ahead without a mask because ancient machinery remained medically privileged.

Its hook arm began cutting the nearest pale stalks that leaned across the service route.

Each cut released more pollen.

Orra swore.

"Machine is making it worse."

Sera said, "Can I stop it?"

"Without breaking?" Dessa asked.

Sera looked at her.

"You are consistent."

Fen asked Nemi, "Rail control?"

"No rail outside."

The gardener had transferred onto a shallow bronze groove set into the courtyard paving.

I had not noticed it before the plants grew.

Fen looked at me.

"Can you redirect?"

The machine was twenty feet ahead, pruning happily.

Its groove split at the first statue.

One branch toward the basin.

One toward a side service alcove half-buried in mud.

There was a switch tongue.

No lever visible.

I could maybe shove the whole cart.

Wrong.

Could change wheel path at split.

Small.

"At the branch. If I can reach before it."

Sera said, "I'll reach."

Fen shook his head.

"Stay inside ward corridor."

Right.

Orra moved the forward stake another ten feet.

We advanced.

Pollen curled around gray light.

My eyes remained clear.

Breathing okay.

Field peg on wet stone.

Gardener approaching split.

Nemi said, "Five seconds."

Not magic.

Wheel rhythm.

I crouched inside the ward edge.

Too far for hand.

Not too far for one small plane.

The switch tongue sat flat between bronze grooves.

I did not need to move gardener.

Needed one front wheel to choose left.

I placed a thin angled plane over the right groove just past the split.

Not wall.

Bad surface.

Gardener front wheel hit it.

Climbed slightly.

Machine corrected toward lower resistance.

Left wheel followed the side groove.

Then right.

Whole cart diverted toward the service alcove.

Sera said, "Three feet again."

"Different object."

"Still three feet."

The gardener rolled into the muddy alcove and immediately began pruning a dense patch there instead.

Still pollen.

Farther from our route.

Enough.

Orra moved the corridor.

We crossed the courtyard in four short stages.

No one broke ward edge.

Dessa almost did once because a statue bowl had rotated ninety degrees.

Sera grabbed her document strap again.

"Forward."

"Stop handling scholars."

"Then stop becoming luggage."

I loved competent friendship developing against consent.

At the base of the descent slope, Orra released the final ward only after we reached clean wind.

Fen sent Dessa and Nemi up first.

Then me.

Sera.

Orra.

Fen last.

Halfway up, the courtyard tone changed.

One long rising note.

Nemi stopped at the top marker.

"Keep moving," Fen said.

She did.

We all crossed above the blue rope.

Orra checked eyes.

Throats.

Breathing.

No symptoms beyond sweat and Sera's general personality.

Below us, the garden ran at full cycle.

All five visible statues poured water now.

The dry basin's mist had returned, thicker than before.

Plants stood open.

The gardener pruned its side alcove.

Nothing chased us.

No wall collapsed.

The site had simply become a place we were not equipped to occupy safely during active phase.

Fen looked at the sun.

Then at Nemi.

"Cycle timing?"

She checked her notes.

"Yesterday active window was roughly three hours. Today start is earlier by twenty-two minutes."

"Why?"

"Don't know."

"Can we finish first survey without going back down active?"

Dessa said, "We have lower layout to frame two, intact regulator, service gallery, cultivation phase, likely distribution below."

Orra said, "And exposure limit."

Sera said, "And drakes."

I said, "And the lower frames change mode without failing."

Nemi said, "And a deeper rhythm we haven't located."

Fen nodded.

"Enough."

Dessa looked at him.

"Enough?"

"For first survey."

There it was.

The job had an end even though the ruin did not.

I understood this professionally and disliked it personally.

Fen looked at all of us.

"No more entry. We map from notes, collect tagged surface items after inactive if safe, and leave before dark."

Sera sighed.

"Adventure ending in paperwork."

Dessa said, "Civilization."

"Same complaint."

We went back to camp.

The vertical water sheets stayed underground.

The rain tooth stayed in its recess.

The stairs stayed open.

For once, leaving something interesting where it belonged did not feel impossible.

Annoying.

Possible.
