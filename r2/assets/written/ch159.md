# Candidate Chapter 159: The Salvager

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

The city let me keep the snail.

This was the first official result of the West Bramble survey.

I considered it a success.

Dessa disagreed with my ordering.

"We documented a functioning pre-city water allocation complex."

"And the snail."

"Three vertical reservoir fields."

"Still snail."

"Active cultivation cycle."

"Blue-green."

Sera held up two of her labor coins.

"I got money from old money."

Dessa closed her eyes.

We were back at camp with the garden inactive below us.

Fen had allowed one final retrieval from the service gallery after Orra cleared the air and Nemi confirmed the caretaker rail was quiet.

No lower descent.

No rain tooth.

No pruning blade.

No new exploration disguised as cleanup.

Only items already tagged during first entry.

That distinction required repeating twice for Sera and once for me.

Unfair distribution.

Dessa spread the salvage tags on a crate.

City hold in red.

Survey samples in yellow.

Potential personal release in white.

My snail had white string.

Sera's three work coins had white.

Her fish token also white.

One duplicate wave marker white.

Everything with inscription, fitted function, or uncertain system role stayed red or yellow.

Dessa picked up Sera's three coins.

"City keeps one as type sample. Two released."

Sera held out her palm.

Dessa dropped two in.

"Value?" Sera asked.

"Maybe four copper each to a collector. Maybe less."

"Adventure."

She pocketed them.

The fish token was released too.

Sera looked at it.

Then at the coins.

"Fish or money?"

"Both," I said.

"Correct."

Dessa looked at my snail.

"Duplicate icon. No inscription. Loose in broken jar. Released."

She cut the city tag and replaced it with a small paper receipt.

**COMMON GARDEN TOKEN, BLUE/GREEN SNAIL.**

Legal title.

Beautiful.

I put it in the same pouch as the stupid wooden tourist bird's repair wax.

Different cities could now compete in useless object production.

Noll would hate this.

Maybe love.

Hard to predict.

Nemi got nothing.

Not because she had found nothing.

Because everything she cared about was attached.

She sat on an overturned bucket reading her notes while Dessa processed tags.

Sera noticed.

"You want tooth."

Nemi did not look up.

"I want to test it."

"Same."

"No."

"Looks same."

Nemi finally looked at her.

"If I owned it, I would still want to test it. If I test it, I don't need to own it."

Sera considered.

"You would take if city sold?"

Nemi considered longer.

"Depends price."

Human after all.

Dessa said, "City isn't selling it."

"Then cheaper decision," Nemi said.

I liked that more than I should have.

Fen had spent most of the afternoon turning our field mess into a map.

Not artistic.

Useful.

Upper descent.

Courtyard boundary.

Channel runs we actually confirmed.

Service shutter.

Gallery.

Caretaker rail.

Stair.

Lower chamber to second reservoir frame only.

Everything beyond marked **UNSURVEYED**.

Far-bank channel marked **DRAKE ACCESS / UNKNOWN DEPTH**.

Buried lower rhythm marked **SOURCE UNKNOWN**.

No speculative arrows.

Dessa had a separate historical sheet full of arrows.

Professional diversity.

Fen called us over before packing camp.

"Report summary. One pass. Correct me if wrong."

We stood around the map.

He pointed to the upper court.

"Site cycles from inactive to active water and cultivation phase. Timing shifts. Trigger unknown."

Nemi said, "Agreed."

"Active phase increases pollen/resin exposure around cultivated channels."

Orra said, "Agreed. Masks plus warded airflow reduce exposure. Dose limits unknown."

Fen pointed to the far bank.

"Bank drakes use at least one channel opening. Two killed, one wounded and escaped. Number unknown."

Sera said, "Agreed."

"Service gallery contains functioning caretaker equipment, work tokens, tools, access to lower stairs."

Dessa said, "And labor indicators."

Fen added **LABOR / MAINTENANCE USE** beside the gallery.

Then lower chamber.

"Three upright water fields observed. Deeper distribution rhythm. One intact probable regulator under city hold."

Dessa said, "Provisional rain regulator."

Sera coughed.

Dessa glared.

Fen wrote **REGULATOR?** and nothing about teeth.

Coward.

Then he looked at me.

"Structure."

"Observed upper paving stable on surveyed route. Service gallery stable. Lower reservoir frame anchors stable during our entry. During active cycle, water-field geometry changes and frame loading changes. No failure observed. We did not stay long enough to characterize full load range."

Fen wrote.

"Anything else?"

I thought about the vertical water.

The dry basin.

The garden waking.

The rail switch failing half a move.

The rain tooth.

"Second survey should bring better non-slip footing," I said.

Sera laughed.

Fen wrote it.

Correct priority.

Orra added, "Eye wash in every field bag, not one medical kit."

Also written.

Nemi said, "Soft-handled utensils."

Fen looked at her.

"For survey?"

"Camp."

"No."

She looked betrayed.

Not every problem became policy.

Camp came down before fourth bell.

No drama.

Bedrolls rolled badly.

One tent peg vanished and was eventually found inside Orra's medical satchel for reasons nobody could explain.

The mule refused to stand near the fish token in Sera's pouch until she moved it.

This probably meant nothing.

Sera insisted it meant ancient fish magic.

Dessa told her to stop damaging scholarship.

We walked back through Bramble End.

Fen bought bread.

I bought bread too because the bakery had earned comparison rights.

Not for Mara.

Five days by post would convert bread into weapon.

For Noll.

Maybe Mesi if Noll failed to defend it.

Nemi bought a wooden spoon.

Problem solved privately.

Sera bought fried onions wrapped in paper and ate half before we reached the road.

Orra bought nothing.

Dessa found a local woman who remembered children playing near the flood bank thirty years ago and somehow conducted an oral history while walking beside the survey cart.

Fen let her because both feet stayed on road.

At the west gate, Guild operations met us with a payment clerk and two sealed document cases.

Base pay first.

Three days.

Full rate.

No hazard bonus because the contract had included first-entry uncertainty already.

Sera objected on principle.

Clerk did not care.

Salvage shares would settle later if city released anything with resale value beyond personal-tag items.

Probably small.

Maybe nothing.

Still possible.

Fen signed the field sheet.

Then the clerk asked, "Preferred roster for second survey?"

There it was.

Nobody looked at me.

Good.

Fen looked at the four of us.

Not asking permission to own future calendars.

"List current roster preferred if available," he said. "Not required."

Clerk wrote.

Sera said, "Rate needs higher if lower level."

The clerk looked up.

"Noted."

Nemi said, "I want antiquary access to regulator tests if I'm contracted."

Dessa said, "City decides."

Nemi looked at the clerk.

"Then put it in preference."

Clerk wrote.

Orra said, "Dedicated air sampling kit and eye wash per person."

Written.

Fen looked at me.

I considered what I wanted.

Not title.

Not party.

One practical term.

"If active-cycle entry is planned, I want the lower-frame load test done before people cross between water fields."

Clerk wrote.

Done.

No one said yes to a second expedition.

No one said no either.

Availability.

Rate.

Terms.

Actual adult fantasy party formation remained deeply unromantic.

I liked it.

Sera nudged my field peg with her boot.

"You trying to recruit us now?"

I looked at her.

"No."

"Your face."

"Retired joke."

"Still accurate."

Nemi held up her new wooden spoon.

"I require better camp equipment."

Orra said, "That's one spoon."

"Beginning of system."

Fen folded his copy of the roster.

"If second survey posts, Guild sends notice. Don't plan your lives around it."

Tomel would approve.

Lysa too.

We separated at the west-gate square.

Fen and Dessa went toward city offices with maps and red-tagged finds.

Orra turned south toward a clinic shift she had tomorrow morning.

Nemi headed for the east archive district with her wooden spoon sticking from her bag and no rain tooth.

Sera stood beside me for one moment longer.

"You going Guild?"

"Home."

"Good."

"You?"

"Paid escort tomorrow."

Of course.

"Try not to lose."

"Try not to steal snail."

"It is legally mine."

She laughed and walked away.

No promise.

No dramatic clasping of forearms.

No permanent roster.

I went to Lower Rook with bread under one arm, a blue-green ceramic snail in my pocket, and enough money to make the three days worth taking even if the city never called again.

Noll opened the door.

Looked at the bread.

Then at me.

"Good trip?"

I thought about vertical water.

Bank drakes.

Gold pollen.

Ancient garden carts.

Rain teeth.

"Yes."

Noll took the bread.

"Good."

Allowed use.

I went upstairs to unpack.
