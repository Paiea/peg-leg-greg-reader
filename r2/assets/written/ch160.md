# Candidate Chapter 160: The Cook

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Noll hated the snail.

This meant he loved it immediately.

I put the blue-green ceramic token on the kitchen table after dinner.

He stared.

Mesi looked up from a stack of tax copies.

"What is that?"

"Legal property."

Noll looked at me.

"Stole?"

"No."

"Fast answer."

"Because I keep getting asked."

Mesi turned the snail over with one finger.

Round blue shell.

Tiny green body.

One eye represented by a black dot that made the entire object look personally disappointed.

"It's terrible," she said.

"Thank you."

"Why do you own it?"

"Adventure."

Noll nodded as if that explained acquisition better than paperwork ever could.

"Mine?" he asked.

"No."

"Kitchen."

"Also no."

He picked it up.

"Kitchen."

This was how land rights developed.

The West Bramble bread sat beside the stove under a cloth.

Noll had already declared it better than Carrow bakery three and worse than Carrow bakery one, which was apparently enough geographic analysis for one evening.

My field bag lay open near the stairs.

Mud drying on the lower seam.

Field peg propped beside it.

Wet masks hanging from a chair back because Orra had made us wash them before leaving and apparently medical authority survived contracts.

Nothing in the room needed me to solve it.

Nice.

I ate soup.

Mesi copied numbers.

Noll argued with an onion.

The snail moved slowly toward the kitchen shelf every time I looked away.

"Stop stealing my legal property," I said.

Noll did not turn.

"Not stealing. Display."

"No permission."

He turned then.

One eyebrow up.

Right.

I deserved that.

"Ask," I said.

Noll held up the snail.

"Kitchen?"

I looked at the stupid thing.

Then at the empty corner of the shelf beside a chipped salt jar.

"For one week."

"Good."

Allowed use.

He put it there.

Mesi smiled into the tax copies.

"You two have strange governance."

"Strong institutions," I said.

"Bad institutions," Noll said.

House balance restored.

A packet waited upstairs on my desk.

Blue thread.

Northbank.

Shorter than Mara's first one.

Good.

No five-page city report every time.

I opened it after washing the West Bramble mud off my hands.

Three sheets.

First was almost entirely about work.

The eight-minute ferry cutoff had survived its one-week trial.

Missed transfers down.

One merchant complained because his usual late cart no longer received unofficial grace.

Mara had written:

**He has discovered that a published cutoff can be enforced. We are both growing.**

Elen made fewer transcription errors after Mara changed one double-entry check.

Bato still believed the flat-map cabinet represented divine law.

Sena had found another river-weight discrepancy and taken twenty-three minutes to explain it.

Orris had reduced boiled eggs at desk from an alarming number to merely cultural.

No emergency.

No promotion.

Work becoming work.

Second sheet contained Northbank things.

Mara had learned enough of the terrace tile game to lose intentionally slower.

Lina's flute remained bad but one song was now recognizable, which had somehow made it worse.

Temporary housing chair finally broke.

Mara bought another from a used-furniture seller who lied about one leg being shorter.

She had corrected it with folded paper.

I respected the engineering.

Third sheet was six lines.

**Do not come next week either. Before you complain, I am not avoiding you. I have branch review at the end of the week and I want one full report cycle before we start solving visits. After that, pick two possible dates and I will pick one. Two dates. Not six.**

Then:

**I miss you. I also like having a city you have not optimized yet. Love, Mara.**

Cruel woman.

I took out reply paper.

Wrote:

**Two dates is authoritarian. I will comply under protest.**

Then told her about West Bramble.

Not all eleven chapters worth.

Flood-cut garden.

Bank drakes.

Vertical water.

Gold pollen.

Ancient gardener cart.

I drew the snail badly.

Under it:

**Legally acquired. Noll has temporary display rights. Civilization survives.**

I did not mention the rain tooth first.

Then added it anyway because `rain tooth` was too stupid to withhold.

Mara deserved suffering.

I wrote:

**There is a city-held device whose provisional translation is Rain Tooth. No, I did not steal it. Please recognize growth.**

Then:

**I miss you too. Finish your report cycle. I will pick exactly two dates and hate both. Love, Greg.**

Sealed.

Done.

No route table.

Yet.

The next morning I woke late enough for Noll to accuse me of retirement.

Second bell.

No contract.

No camp watch.

No early departure.

My body had apparently noticed before I did.

Field peg stayed against the wall.

Walking leg today.

I checked the limb anyway.

No rub from West Bramble.

One tender spot near the outer edge from the wet descent.

Nothing broken.

I changed liner and went downstairs.

Noll had already moved the snail six inches toward the center of the shelf.

"Week," I reminded him.

"Six days."

"You counted?"

"Yes."

Dangerous tenant relations.

Breakfast was eggs, rice, and leftover bread.

Mesi had gone to work.

Jorren had left before first bell for transfer-yard inventory.

Lower Rook felt emptier when everyone had schedules.

Good.

I paid Noll rent through the end of the month.

He counted twice.

"Too much."

"Includes food last week."

"You gone three days."

"Food before."

He pushed two coins back.

"Too much."

We argued until the correct amount emerged.

No one learned anything.

Beautiful.

After breakfast I unpacked the rest of the field bag.

One wet chalk stick ruined.

Two good.

String tangled beyond moral repair.

Pell's measuring strip intact.

Veya's copper rod still there.

No ancient artifact hidden underneath.

I checked once anyway.

Habit.

Then laughed at myself.

The day remained open.

Hearthline had not sent for the install read.

West Bramble second survey had not posted.

Mara did not want me in Northbank yet.

Red Quay was somewhere else.

No one required me.

This should have been relaxing.

Instead I went to the Guild by third bell.

Not for adventure.

Ordinary work.

Something one or two days.

Preferably paid.

Preferably without ancient horticulture.

The job board had three options in my range.

Warehouse inventory escort.

Road-marker replacement with one night out.

Municipal stair inspection.

I stared at the last one.

Northbank had damaged my tolerance for stairs from a distance.

Road markers seemed fine.

I took the slip from the board.

Behind me, someone at the contract desk said, "I can read the route. Test me."

The voice did nothing.

The name did.

The clerk said it aloud while checking a file.

"Merek Sol, you've quit two route crews in six months."

My hand stopped on the road-marker slip.

Merek Sol.

Not a famous name yet.

Not here.

Not now.

In my first life, men had called him **Ash Road Sol** after the northern retreats.

Captain later.

Guide before that.

Contract breaker.

Raider, depending who told the story.

The man who left fourteen wounded behind at Narrows Gate when keeping them would have slowed his column.

I had met him once.

Years after that decision.

Older.

Scar from temple to jaw.

Gray in his beard.

Eyes that never seemed to stop calculating distance to the next defensible line.

The person standing at the contract desk now had no scar.

No beard.

Maybe twenty-two.

Thin enough that his coat hung wrong.

Dark hair cut with a knife or bad patience.

One patched boot.

He leaned over the counter with both hands flat.

"I quit one," Merek said.

The clerk looked at the file.

"Two."

"Second stopped paying. That's not quitting."

"They list abandonment."

"They list whatever keeps them from listing wage theft."

Not the voice I remembered.

Younger, obviously.

But not cold.

Angry.

Very specifically angry.

The clerk said, "You also missed a return filing on East Fen."

"Because my brother was in clinic."

There.

A fact old Greg had never known.

I stayed where I was.

Road-marker slip in one hand.

Merek Sol on the other side of the room, arguing about paperwork and a brother.

My memory of him had begun at Narrows Gate.

His life apparently had not.
