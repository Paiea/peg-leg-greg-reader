# Candidate Chapter 161: The Applicant

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

I should have taken the road-marker job and left.

This was obvious.

I did not leave.

Merek Sol remained at the contract desk arguing with Clerk Tessa, who had apparently inherited every difficult applicant while Sella was elsewhere enjoying peace.

Tessa tapped the file.

"East Fen return filing. Missing."

"Brother in clinic."

"I heard you. Still missing."

"I told Harven."

"Harven is not filing desk."

"He is crew lead."

"Crew lead does not close contract record."

Merek closed his eyes.

Not patience.

Containment.

I remembered the older face badly.

Scar down one side.

Short gray beard.

Same eyes maybe.

Hard to tell when forty years and one war sat between versions.

At Narrows Gate, Merek Sol had abandoned fourteen wounded.

That was the story.

Not rumor.

I had seen the casualty ledger later.

Fourteen names marked **LEFT DURING RETREAT**.

Three survived capture.

Eleven did not return.

Sol's column escaped.

Two hundred thirty-one people reached the south ridge before the road closed.

Old command had called it necessary.

Families had called it murder.

Sol had called it distance.

I remembered that exact word because I hated him for it.

"Distance decided before I did," he had told me years later over bad military tea. "You can be angry. Road still had length."

At fifty-nine, I had considered him a useful bastard.

At twenty-two, he was wearing one patched boot and fighting a filing clerk over his brother.

History remained rude about staging.

Tessa said, "No licensed crew will take you with two abandonment notes."

"One abandonment note. One pay dispute."

"The record does not agree."

"Record wasn't there."

That sentence sounded familiar in ways I did not like.

I looked at the road-marker slip in my hand.

One night out.

Southwest route.

Replace six markers after spring washout.

Two-person minimum because one section crossed unstable scrubland and Guild disliked solo work where ankles disappeared.

Crew not assigned yet.

Interesting.

Bad word.

I moved closer to the desk.

Not joining.

Listening better.

Merek noticed me immediately.

Eyes went to Silver clasp.

Then walking leg.

Then road-marker slip.

Quick.

Future route captain already looked at what mattered.

"You hiring?" he asked.

I almost laughed.

"No."

"Then stop standing like you are."

Current Sol had more mouth than future Sol.

Maybe scar removed humor.

Tessa looked at me.

"Greg. You need something?"

"Road markers."

"Sign and wait for pair."

Merek looked at the slip.

"Which route?"

"Southwest six."

He frowned.

"Six is bad after spring."

Tessa said, "Thank you, unemployed route expert."

He ignored her.

"Marker four won't be where map says. Bank slid."

I looked at him.

"When?"

"First big rain after melt."

"You were there?"

"Not this year."

"Then how know?"

Merek pointed at the wall map behind Tessa.

"Because creek bend moved last year and Guild never redrew the cut. Spring water hits outside bank now. Marker four is on outside bank."

Tessa turned toward the map.

I did too.

Southwest six ran along a narrow creek for maybe half a mile.

Old blue line curved gently.

One little black marker symbol on the outside of the second bend.

Merek was right about the geometry if the creek had shifted.

Tessa said, "Map revision is current."

Merek laughed once.

"Revision date?"

She checked the corner.

Her face changed.

Not much.

Enough.

Eight months.

Before spring.

"Could still be there," she said.

"Could."

He used uncertainty less politely than Nemi.

I asked, "How would you approach four if bank is gone?"

Merek looked at me properly now.

"Don't follow creek. Cut ridge before second bend, come down from west."

"Longer."

"Dry."

"How much longer?"

"Twenty minutes walking if you don't carry stupid things."

I looked at my field bag.

He followed the look.

"You carry stupid things?"

"Frequently."

"Then thirty."

Tessa said, "This is not an interview."

Merek turned back to her.

"Could be."

"No."

"You need route people."

"We need route people who finish paperwork."

There it was.

Current weakness much less dramatic than future legend.

Paper.

Reliability.

Quitting bad employers.

Maybe leaving people later.

Maybe not same problem.

I disliked how quickly my brain tried to draw one line through all of it.

Pattern wanted simplicity.

People supplied years.

Tessa pushed his file back.

"You can reapply after one clean sponsored contract or three months with one employer."

Merek stared.

"Sponsored by who?"

"Silver route lead, field lead, or registered crew head willing to put their name on your closeout."

His eyes moved to my clasp again.

No.

Not yet.

I looked at Tessa.

"That standard?"

"For reliability restriction, yes."

"Sponsor responsible for what?"

"Closeout filing and conduct note. Not debt. Not injury unless negligent supervision."

Merek said, "You asking because?"

"I like terms."

"You hiring?"

"Still no."

He looked annoyed.

Good.

No instant gratitude.

Tessa pointed at my road-marker slip.

"Sign or put back."

I signed.

One-night southwest six.

Silver field worker.

Pair pending.

Merek watched the form.

Then said, "Marker four is gone."

"You said could."

"It's gone."

"Confidence improved fast."

"You're going to check."

"Yes."

"Tell me tomorrow."

Not please.

Future captain energy in miniature.

I said, "Maybe."

He looked at me.

"You talk like an old man."

Everyone apparently had access to this observation.

"Occupational injury."

Merek took his file.

Started to leave.

Then stopped.

"If they pair you with Dellen, don't let him navigate."

"Who is Dellen?"

"Bronze on southwest work. Good climber. Gets lost on straight roads."

Tessa said, "Merek."

"What?"

"Leave."

"Leaving."

He left.

No dramatic glance back.

No destiny recognizing destiny.

Just a young man with bad paperwork and one patched boot.

I stayed at the desk.

Tessa stamped my contract.

"Do not sponsor him because he knows one creek."

I looked at her.

"I didn't say I would."

"You asked sponsor terms."

"I ask terms."

"You also collect problems."

Professional reputation broadening badly.

"Why is his file restricted?"

Tessa hesitated.

Not confidential exactly.

Guild work history was visible enough to licensed leads.

"First crew says he walked off after wage dispute on day four. Sol says they missed two pay intervals and were using unregistered night routes."

"Verified?"

"Night routes yes. Pay records incomplete."

"Second?"

"East Fen. Finished field work. Missed closeout because brother went into fever clinic. Crew lead filed abandonment because Sol didn't return to desk."

"That's not abandonment."

Tessa looked at me.

"Guild note says failure to complete contract closeout. Applicant hears abandonment because employers read fast."

There.

Different problem.

Paper had compressed two messy events into one reputation.

I knew something about compression.

"Brother?"

"Alive last I heard. Not Guild matter."

Clean boundary.

I nodded.

Tessa handed me the route packet.

"Departure first bell tomorrow. Pair posts by end of day."

"Who?"

"Not assigned."

"Dellen?"

"Maybe."

"Can he navigate?"

Tessa smiled slightly.

"Find out."

I left.

Merek Sol was gone from the front steps.

No convenient second conversation.

I walked home with the route packet under one arm.

At Lower Rook, the blue-green snail had moved another two inches toward the center of the kitchen shelf.

Noll denied involvement.

I believed him less than Tessa believed Merek.

Upstairs, I opened the route packet.

Southwest six.

Marker four on the creek bend.

I drew the ridge cut Merek described from memory.

Then stopped.

I had known Ash Road Sol as a man who could move hundreds of people through impossible terrain and leave fourteen behind.

Current Merek could read one creek from an outdated map.

Same skill family.

Maybe.

Not same man.

Not yet.

The dangerous part was how badly I wanted the sentence **not yet** to mean **therefore fixable**.

I closed the packet before that became a plan.
