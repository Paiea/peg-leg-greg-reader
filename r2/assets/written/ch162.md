# Candidate Chapter 162: The Climber

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Dellen got lost before we left Carrow.

Merek had undersold the problem.

"West gate," I said.

Dellen looked at the street sign.

"I know."

"You're walking east."

"Shortcut."

"To west?"

He stopped.

Looked at the morning sun.

Then at me.

"Testing you."

"Excellent. I failed to believe you."

Dellen Mora was Bronze, twenty-six, compact, cheerful, and carrying more climbing iron than southwest route markers reasonably required.

Two coils of light rope.

Six pitons.

Hammer.

Hook staff.

One canvas bag with replacement marker plates.

No map.

I noticed.

"Map?"

"You have one."

There it was.

Division of labor through negligence.

Tessa had paired us because Dellen knew marker maintenance and I had filed as Silver field.

Not because anyone wanted to test Merek Sol's opinion.

That part belonged to me.

We reached west gate eventually.

Mostly because I led.

Southwest six began as an ordinary farm road.

Low stone walls.

Spring grass.

Ditches still carrying meltwater.

Marker one stood exactly where the packet said.

Bent plate.

We replaced it.

Dellen climbed the post instead of using the little maintenance ladder attached to our cart.

"Why?"

"Faster."

He was right.

Annoying.

Marker two had lost one bolt.

Dellen fixed it while hanging sideways from the stone base for reasons nobody required.

Marker three stood at the edge of scrub where the road narrowed toward the creek.

Missing paint only.

We ate bread there.

Dellen asked, "You always this quiet?"

"No."

"Thinking?"

"Unfortunately."

"About route?"

"Partly."

He pointed at the map.

"Creek part easy. Follow creek."

Merek's voice arrived immediately.

**Don't follow creek. Cut ridge before second bend, come down west.**

I looked at the map.

Blue line.

Old bend.

Marker four drawn on outer bank.

Dellen already stood.

"Ready?"

"We're taking ridge."

He frowned.

"Why?"

"Spring slide likely took four."

"You been out here?"

"No."

"Then likely from?"

Good question.

"Someone who knows route."

Dellen looked toward the creek.

"Creek shorter."

"Ridge drier."

"Creek easier."

"Maybe."

He considered.

Then shrugged.

"You're Silver."

I disliked that reason.

"If ridge is stupid, say so after ten minutes."

"Deal."

We cut uphill.

The path did not exist.

Merek had said cut ridge, not follow trail.

Important distinction.

Dellen immediately became useful.

Scrub thickened near the first rise.

He found footing through exposed stone where I would have pushed through brush.

At one steep section he used the hook staff to pull a low branch down so I could step over instead of under with the field peg.

No speech.

No pity.

Just route.

Ten minutes.

Dellen looked back.

"Still stupid."

"You can say after twenty."

"Terms changed."

"Field authority."

He laughed.

At twenty-two minutes we reached the ridge shoulder.

The creek came back into view below.

The outside bank at the second bend was gone.

Not eroded.

Gone.

A thirty-foot bite of earth had collapsed into the channel.

Half the creek now ran through a new cut.

The old road line ended in open air.

Marker four did not exist.

Dellen stopped beside me.

"Oh."

Allowed use.

I looked at the map.

Then at the real creek.

Merek had been exactly right about the failure shape.

Not approximately.

Outer bank gone.

Ridge approach dry.

West descent possible.

Positive evidence arrived cleanly enough to become dangerous.

Dellen said, "Your someone was right."

"Yes."

"Guild know?"

"Not yet."

"Then we get paid for revision too?"

Excellent priorities.

"Probably."

"Good route."

We found a stable descent fifty yards upstream.

Dellen went first.

He was much better than me on steep ground.

Not close.

He placed one short line across the worst ten feet, anchored to a root cluster, and waited below while I came down.

Field peg bit dirt.

Right boot slipped once.

Line caught my hand before the slip became anything.

Dellen said nothing.

At the creek we found the old marker plate half-buried in silt.

Bent.

Post gone.

We documented position.

Installed new marker on the west approach instead of rebuilding a sign toward a road that no longer existed.

Dellen climbed a cedar to set the high visibility strip.

No ladder.

Of course.

I updated the route sketch.

New bend.

Bank loss.

Ridge bypass.

Temporary marker location.

Then stared at my own drawing.

This was what Merek Sol had seen from a wall map eight months out of date.

Not magic.

Terrain memory.

Water behavior.

Maybe experience from last year.

Strong.

Real.

Future Ash Road Sol had not become an extraordinary route captain by accident.

That part, at least, existed now.

Dellen dropped from the cedar the last four feet instead of climbing.

Landed clean.

"Five?"

"Other side of ridge."

"Which way?"

I looked at him.

He smiled.

"Testing."

"Northwest."

He turned southwest.

"Dellen."

"Right."

Not every weakness hid a tragedy.

We finished marker five before dusk.

Marker six sat near an old roadside shelter farther south, so the contract included one night out.

The shelter had three walls, a roof, and one bench that leaned toward whoever sat on the left side.

Dellen immediately took the left.

"Bad bench."

"Move."

"Already committed."

We made a small fire outside.

No ancient pollen.

No city antiquary.

No vertical water.

I appreciated normal flame.

Dinner was dried meat, rice cake, and onions Dellen had stolen from his own kitchen legally.

He asked about the field peg after eating.

Simple questions.

How far could I walk?

Mud worse than stone?

Did it hurt?

I answered enough.

Then asked, "You know Merek Sol?"

Dellen thought.

"Route kid?"

"Twenty-two. Dark hair."

"Yeah. Ash coat sometimes."

Ash.

My stomach tightened for stupid reasons.

"Worked with him?"

"One day. North wash last winter."

"How was he?"

Dellen shrugged.

"Annoying."

Promisingly human answer.

"Useful?"

"Yes."

"Reliable?"

Another shrug.

"He left early."

There.

"Why?"

"Don't know. Said clinic. Crew lead got mad."

Brother again.

Maybe.

Could have been excuse.

Current Merek still had agency to lie.

"Would you take job with him?" I asked.

Dellen looked at me.

"Depends job."

"Route."

"Yes."

"Recovery?"

"Maybe."

"Long expedition?"

"Don't know him."

Better answer than mine would have been.

Dellen leaned back on the bad bench.

It tipped farther.

He nearly fell.

Caught himself.

"Fucking bench."

No metaphor survived.

We slept.

Next morning marker six needed only a new faceplate.

Contract done before second bell.

Return route used the ridge bypass again.

At Carrow Guild, Tessa took our revised map.

Her eyebrows rose.

"Marker four gone."

"Yes."

"Bank?"

"Gone too."

Dellen said, "His weird guy was right."

Tessa looked at me.

"Merek?"

"Yes."

She read the route sketch again.

Then stamped **REVISION PAY** beside our contract.

Extra six copper each.

Dellen smiled.

"Good route."

I took the money.

Merek had been right.

That should have meant one thing.

He could read the route.

My brain wanted it to mean more.

That was my problem, not his.
