# Chapter 162: The Customer North

The mill north of Brenn Road wanted regular filtration service.

Regular changed the math.

One crate had been an experiment.

Six chambers every month meant handoffs, returns, breakage responsibility, and somebody remembering when the flour carrier was late.

Hesk wanted a higher rate.

Dena wanted him to disappear into a ditch.

Ivena wanted a written service schedule.

The customer wanted everything cheaper.

I sat through the meeting because ownership occasionally punished me.

We did not buy a cart.

We did raise the customer route fee.

They complained.

Then accepted because replacing local boiled-cloth filters cost more.

Hesk got one extra copper per round trip and a crate condition line he could point at whenever Dena accused him of crimes.

Dena still accused him of crimes.

The business acquired a route outside Carrow without acquiring a horse...
