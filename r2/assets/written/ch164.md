# Candidate Chapter 164: The Route Reader

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Merek was early.

This annoyed me because I had been preparing to test reliability.

He stood at west gate before first bell with one route pack, one coil of Guild rope, and no patched boot.

Different boots today.

Still cheap.

Both intact.

"New?" I asked.

He looked down.

"Borrowed."

"From?"

"Brother."

"Same size?"

"Mostly."

Dangerous footwear standard.

Tessa handed us the city verification packet.

Permanent reroute marker four.

Confirm ridge bypass.

Choose safe descent.

Flag any cart restrictions.

Recommend whether southwest six remained ordinary route or needed seasonal classification.

There.

Not just fastest line.

Actual users.

We left.

Merek walked faster than Dellen.

Not stronger.

More certain.

He knew which farm lane cut ten minutes without trespassing.

Knew one stone wall had a legal foot gap hidden behind willow.

Knew the first ridge shoulder stayed dry because wind stripped snow there before melt.

None of this required magic.

His head held routes the way mine held old battlefields.

Uncomfortable recognition.

At marker three, he did not follow yesterday's ridge cut exactly.

"Why left?" I asked.

"Your line climbs too early."

"Drier."

"Also stupid."

"Strong technical term."

"You went around brush that isn't there in summer."

He pointed.

Yesterday, dense spring scrub had pushed Dellen and me higher.

Merek found a narrow animal run through it.

Wet now.

Probably dry later.

"Seasonal route," I said.

"Exactly."

He moved on.

That was the problem with actual talent.

It kept providing evidence.

We reached the creek overlook before second bell.

Bank still gone.

No miraculous repair overnight.

Merek stared down.

"Worse than I thought."

"Thirty feet."

"More upstream too."

He walked the ridge edge.

Not dangerously close.

Professional distance.

Then pointed south.

"Permanent bypass should stay high until marker five."

I looked.

"That's long."

"Dry."

"Steep."

"Not bad."

For him.

I looked at the route packet.

**FLAG ANY CART RESTRICTIONS.**

Southwest six had never been major freight.

Still used by handcarts, pack animals, older travelers, injured workers, people carrying things they regretted.

I looked at Merek's line.

One section climbed exposed stone at maybe twenty degrees.

Not impossible.

Bad with wheels.

Bad with a bad knee.

Bad with one leg and a field peg if wet.

"For who?" I asked.

Merek looked at me.

"What?"

"Route is good for who?"

"People using route."

"Cart?"

He glanced at the slope.

"Small cart can make it."

"Loaded?"

"Depends load."

"Mule?"

"Yes."

"Old woman with two baskets?"

He looked at me.

"How old?"

"Annoyingly specific response."

"You asked."

I pointed at the slope.

"We need ordinary route, not best line for us."

Merek frowned.

"Lower line floods."

"Then maybe seasonal class."

"Seasonal means closure signs, extra markers, more paperwork."

There it was.

He disliked paperwork enough to let it shape terrain decisions.

Tiny.

Funny.

Potentially expensive if scaled.

"Paper is not a hill," I said.

"Feels heavier."

"We're verifying route. Include the problem."

Merek looked back uphill.

"High line still safest."

"For us."

"You made it yesterday."

"With Dellen rope on descent."

"You have field peg."

"Exactly."

He went quiet.

Not convinced.

Good.

I did not explain morality.

I handed him the spare marker post.

Heavy oak.

Maybe forty pounds with iron base.

"What?"

"Carry this up your line."

Merek stared.

"Why?"

"Route users carry stupid things."

"This is not a normal stupid thing."

"Still useful test."

"You carry half."

"No. You said line is fine."

He swore.

Then put the post across one shoulder.

Not because I commanded his future.

Because he wanted to prove me wrong.

Excellent motivation.

He climbed.

First thirty yards fine.

At the exposed stone, his pace slowed.

Post shifted.

He adjusted.

Another ten yards.

The narrow step I disliked forced him sideways.

One boot slipped.

Not dangerous enough to fall.

Enough that the oak post struck rock hard.

Merek stopped.

Looked at the step.

Then back down at me.

"Fine," he said.

"What?"

"Bad with load."

"Couldn't hear."

"Fuck you."

Healthy field communication.

He brought the post back down.

We tested a lower contour.

Wetter.

Longer.

But gentler.

One mud stretch near a spring seep.

Could be improved with gravel or corduroy boards if city cared.

Merek hated it initially.

Then found a narrow shelf twenty yards farther west that avoided the worst mud.

There.

His answer.

Not mine.

Lower route became reasonable.

Still not perfect.

We marked two options.

**DRY-SEASON HIGH FOOT ROUTE.**

**GENERAL BYPASS, LOWER CONTOUR, REQUIRES SURFACE WORK AT SEEP.**

City could decide cost.

Merek looked at both notes.

"They'll pick high."

"Maybe."

"Cheaper."

"Probably."

"Then why map lower?"

"Because decision belongs with actual options visible."

He gave me a strange look.

I almost heard Nesha threatening my good leg from months ago.

People were not load diagrams.

Roads were closer.

Still not permission to hide options.

We continued.

Merek found a better marker-five approach than yesterday.

I found one unstable stone shelf he had not noticed because he watched route line more than local load.

No one became best at everything.

At midday we ate beside the creek.

Merek checked the sun twice.

"Clinic?" I asked.

His face closed slightly.

"Visiting window fifth bell."

"We'll be back before."

"If not?"

There.

Contract test.

"We file partial and go back."

He looked surprised.

"Just like that?"

"This is one-day verification, not war."

Wrong word.

Narrows Gate moved in my head.

I kept my face still.

Merek said, "Crew lead on first job said personal shit waits until close."

"Crew lead wasn't me."

"No."

"Brother emergency is not permission to vanish. Tell me. Then we decide contract response."

"You already said that."

"Repetition helps."

"Old man."

"Eat."

We finished before fourth bell.

Back at Carrow, Tessa gave Merek the closeout form directly.

I expected trouble.

He read it.

Filled name.

Route number.

Hours.

Notes.

Missed one box.

Tessa tapped it.

"Sponsor conduct acknowledgment."

Merek looked at me.

"You."

"Apparently."

I signed after reading his field notes.

Accurate.

No invented heroics.

He had written **HIGH LINE UNSUITABLE FOR LOADED HANDCART / LIMITED MOBILITY IN WET CONDITIONS**.

His words.

Not mine.

I looked at him.

He noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You have a face."

Retired joke had spread to new populations.

"Sign your last box."

He signed.

Tessa stamped the closeout.

Then pulled his Guild file.

Added one line.

**SPONSORED CONTRACT COMPLETE. CLEAN CLOSEOUT.**

No ceremony.

Restriction removed from provisional block.

Old notes stayed.

Exactly as promised.

Merek read the line twice.

"That's it?"

Tessa said, "That's it."

"I can apply normal?"

"Yes. Employers still see history."

"Fine."

He tried to sound like it meant nothing.

Failed slightly.

Positive result.

Real.

Greg intervention: worked.

Dangerous evidence again.

Merek took his pay.

Counted.

Put most in one pocket and three coins in another.

"Clinic?" I asked.

He looked annoyed.

"Yes."

"You owe?"

"Yes."

Not my business.

I stopped there.

Merek headed for the door.

At the threshold he turned.

"If you get route work again, ask if you want."

Not gratitude.

Offer.

"Maybe."

He nodded.

Then left for the clinic.

Tessa watched him go.

"You look pleased."

"Contract closed."

"You look more pleased than paperwork deserves."

"Impossible."

She went back to her desk.

I stood with my sponsor copy in one hand.

One clean contract had changed Merek's current options.

Not his soul.

Not Narrows Gate.

Options.

That was enough to feel powerful if I was careless.
