# Chapter 166: The Second Carrier

The second outside customer lived east of Carrow.

Hesk lived north.

This became important immediately.

"No," he said.

Dena leaned across the workbench.

"You have a cart."

"A cart is not a direction."

"It moves in directions."

"Mine moves north on fourth-day freight and south on sixth-day returns. I am not taking six chambers east because you discovered geography."

Dena turned to me.

"He has a point," I said.

"You also have legs."

"Not freight legs."

The new customer was a dye house beside Old East Gate. Small operation. Reliable water demand. Bad local filter cloth. They wanted four chambers every five weeks and expected us to solve delivery because customers enjoyed tradition.

Ivena had already written three versions of a route schedule.

Arlo wanted to hire a second craft hand.

Dena wanted a second carrier.

Pellin wanted everyone to stop talking until the firing rack was empty.

Hesk wanted payment for having attended a meeting about a road he did not use.

He had also brought back the north mill's six empties without breaking one.

That improved his case.

We found another carrier through the flour exchange.

Marris Venn.

Two-wheel cart. East Gate twice a week. Carried flour in, soap and lamp oil out. She disliked fragile freight but liked recurring money.

Her price was higher than Hesk's.

Dena disliked this personally.

Marris disliked Dena within six minutes.

Ivena put a crate-condition line on the handoff sheet.

Marris added a weather exception.

Hesk added, from the other side of the table, "Mud exception."

"You are not even on this route," Dena said.

"Mud is universal."

Nobody bought a cart.

We acquired a second carrier and twice as many arguments about receipts...
