# Chapter 166: The Selector

Iria Dane had a face built for rejecting suggestions before they were finished.

Useful face.

She sat at the end of a long Guild table with the Storm Road packet open in front of her and six smaller packets arranged by name.

Mine was the thickest.

I disliked that immediately.

"Greg," she said.

Not a question.

"Iria."

She pointed to the empty chair across from her.

"Sit."

I sat.

Sera was already there with both boots on the bench beside her and a meat pie in one hand.

"You came," she said.

"Apparently."

"Thought you were becoming respectable."

"Temporary condition."

She took another bite.

Good reunion.

The man beside her was broad enough that the chair looked underfunded. Gray hair cut short. Thick fingers. No Guild metal on his coat except a road-service badge scratched nearly blank from use.

Kelm Orr.

He looked at my leg first.

Not rudely.

Professionally.

"Walking foot?"

"For town. Field peg packed."

"Bring both onto the road."

"Planned."

"Good."

Then he stopped caring about me.

Promising.

Across from Kelm sat a narrow man with dark curls tied badly behind his head. Evin Holt had two brass rods tucked through his coat straps and a flat slate on the table covered in little chalk arrows.

He was drawing new ones while Iria talked.

Wind specialist.

The last contractor had not arrived yet.

"Pavo's with the animals," Iria said. "He'll meet us at Halden freight."

She tapped the table.

"First rule. I own movement. We go, stop, cut cargo, shelter, retreat, or change route when I say. You can argue before the call. After the call, you execute unless the call is physically impossible."

Sera swallowed.

"What if it's stupid?"

"Then say stupid quickly."

"Good."

Iria looked at Evin.

"Weather."

Evin stopped drawing.

"I own charged air, crosswind windows, storm-vane interpretation, and whether a shelter is actually a shelter when the air is dirty. If I say metal down, put metal down. If I say don't touch a chain, don't touch the chain."

Kelm grunted.

"Some chains need touching."

"Then use a dry bar."

"You use a dry bar on a seized hanger, I'll watch."

They had met before.

Excellent.

Iria pointed at Kelm.

"Road hardware."

Kelm leaned back.

"If it was built to move, let it move. If it was built to stay still, don't make it move. If you don't know which, ask before you cast clever shit at it."

He looked directly at me for the last sentence.

"Strong briefing," I said.

"Heard about you."

"All accurate."

"Probably not."

Better.

Iria pointed at Sera.

"Immediate threat, animal panic, close defense. Pavo owns animal handling until something has teeth in the wrong place. Then Sera owns the teeth."

Sera raised the meat pie slightly.

"Finally, formal recognition."

Iria looked at me.

"Moving load. Short force corrections. Failure routing. Support geometry."

The words sounded more official from somebody else.

"Yes."

"Not broad warding?"

"Competent. Not why you hired me."

"Good."

There it was again.

The word was fine when other people used it.

Probably.

Iria opened the route map.

The Storm Road followed the western cliffs for fifty-three marked miles between the roadhead and the far freight gate.

Not one continuous cliff.

Stone shelf.

Cut tunnels.

High switchbacks.

Four major suspended spans.

Three wind channels where the old builders had mounted brass storm vanes taller than houses.

Shelter stations every six to nine miles.

Markers eighteen through thirty-one had lost most of their ward cover in the early storm.

The convoy could still pass.

That word carried a lot of employment.

I leaned over the map.

"Who surveyed after the storm?"

Kelm said, "Me."

I looked at him.

"All of it?"

"Eighteen to thirty-one."

"How long?"

"Day and a half."

"Alone?"

"No. I'm old, not stupid."

Sera said, "Debatable."

Kelm ignored her.

He pointed at two black circles.

"Span Twenty-One. Span Twenty-Seven. Both passable under controlled loading. Twenty-One lost one lateral keeper. Twenty-Seven has a cracked west hanger plate. Temporary reinforcement installed."

"By you?"

"By people I trust while I shouted."

"Better."

Iria turned another sheet.

Cargo distribution.

Four wagons.

Two merchant wagons carrying high-value sealed cargo.

One general freight wagon.

One maintenance wagon with spare hardware, food, tarps, and enough rope to make a rigger happy for several minutes.

Three mule strings.

Twelve freight workers including Pavo.

Six contracted specialists including Iria.

Twenty people total.

Not a party.

A moving village with wheels.

Good.

"Completion premium assumes all sealed merchant cargo?" I asked.

"Eighty percent by declared value," Iria said.

"So we can lose one chest and still make it."

One eyebrow moved.

"You started with loss math."

"You showed me loss math."

"Good."

Again.

Iria marked a second route in blue.

"Safe fallback is south interior road from marker seventeen. Adds six days. Not usable once we're beyond twenty-four without coming back."

"Weather window?" Evin asked.

"Current estimate gives us five days on the road, one margin day."

Evin made a face.

"Forecast gives five if the western band keeps speed. Four if it stalls over the shelf."

"Probability?"

"Of stall? Maybe one in four."

"Maybe?"

"Weather is not a clerk."

Tessa would have objected.

I liked him slightly more.

Iria looked around the table.

"Any roster objections, say them now."

I had one.

Several, technically.

I put a finger on the specialist sheet.

"I asked whether Lysa Kerr was available."

Iria's face became even more suited to rejection.

"She's not."

"Tomel Rusk?"

"Booked."

"Veya?"

"Red Quay left east two days ago."

"I know."

"Then why ask?"

"Hope is inefficient but persistent."

Sera snorted.

Iria said, "You requested three people after I had already built the crew."

"I requested information."

"You requested information with rates attached."

Fair.

"I like knowing how people work."

"So do I. That's why I hired people I know."

She pointed at Kelm.

"Eleven crossings with him."

Evin.

"Four."

Sera.

"Two escorts."

Then me.

"Zero."

"Promising distribution."

"I don't hire wishes, Greg. I hire dates. Kerr had a city commitment. Rusk had a recovery contract. Veya wasn't in town. You are."

That sentence did more useful damage than a lecture.

I looked at the map again.

My neat imagined crew had lasted less than ten minutes.

Good.

No.

Useful.

I stopped before the word became a habit.

"What about Kelm?" I asked.

Kelm looked up.

"What about me?"

"No combat rank."

"Correct."

"Storm Road after ward loss."

"Also correct."

"What do you do if something attacks?"

"Get behind Sera."

Sera raised her pie again.

"Finally, more formal recognition."

Kelm continued, "Then I keep the bridge from killing us while she handles the thing trying to."

I nodded.

"Reasonable."

"You expected a mage."

"I expected someone with more magical capacity."

"You have one."

He pointed at Evin.

Evin said, "Please don't spend me on bolts."

Kelm pointed at me.

"And him."

"Please don't spend me on bolts either."

"Then use a wrench."

Iria let the table settle.

Then she pushed a contract copy toward each of us.

"Read injury, abandonment, completion, and command clauses again. Nobody gets surprised on the cliff."

We read.

Sera finished her pie and wiped grease on a cloth instead of the contract.

Growth.

Probably hers, not mine.

The abandonment clause mattered most.

Iria could cut cargo if keeping it materially increased risk to people or draft animals.

Merchant seals did not override field command.

There was a dispute procedure afterward.

Of course there was.

You could survive the storm and then die in accounting.

I signed.

Kelm signed with a mark so practiced it might have been older than my current body.

Evin signed and immediately drew a wind arrow beside his name until Iria looked at him.

He erased it.

Sera signed last.

"When do we meet Pavo?"

"Halden freight yard tomorrow evening," Iria said. "Coach to Halden leaves before first bell. Bring food for the coach unless you enjoy station bread."

"I do not," Sera said.

"Nobody does."

Meeting adjourned.

Kelm rolled the route map carefully and put it into a leather tube.

I caught him at the door.

"You said if it was built to move, let it move."

He looked at me.

"Yes."

"How much do the suspended spans move?"

"Enough."

"Technical."

"You'll see."

"I prefer numbers."

"Then bring a ruler and lose it over the side."

He walked away.

Sera appeared beside me.

"You like him."

"I do not know him."

"You like him."

"He has information."

"Same thing for you."

Unfair.

We left the Guild together.

Carrow evening was cold enough that breath showed in the narrow streets.

Sera bought two skewers from a grill cart.

I bought one.

Then another because tomorrow's station bread had been mentioned and fear was rational.

"You trying to build a perfect team again?" she asked.

I looked at her.

"Again?"

"Field exchange. You watched everyone like pieces."

"I was observing."

"You were sorting."

"Different."

"Sure."

She handed the vendor a coin.

"I signed before I knew you were coming."

"Tessa said."

"Good."

There was that word again.

Sera bit into the skewer.

"Means if this goes bad you can't claim you assembled me."

"I wasn't planning to."

"Excellent."

We walked another block.

"What do you want out of the job?" I asked.

She stopped chewing long enough to stare at me.

I corrected quickly.

"Professionally. Not spiritually."

"Money."

"Of course."

"And I like coastal fights."

"Why?"

"Things have fewer places to run."

"We also have fewer places to run."

"Exactly."

This seemed like poor reasoning.

It was also probably why she was on the contract.

At the next corner she turned south.

"Before first bell," she said.

"I know."

"Don't make a table about us."

"No promises."

"Greg."

"Small table."

She raised one finger without turning around.

I went home and did not make a table.

I made a list.

Completely different.
