# Chapter 169: The Catcher

The first thing that went wrong after Span Twenty-One was not weather.

It was a spoon.

One of Pavo's workers dropped it while walking beside wagon two.

The spoon hit stone, bounced once, and disappeared over the cliff.

Everyone stopped.

Not because the spoon mattered.

Because the worker did.

He had leaned after it.

Only half a step.

Sera caught the back of his coat before half became whole.

"It's a spoon," she said.

The worker looked down at the sea.

Then at her.

"Good spoon."

"Was."

Pavo made him walk inside the wagon line for the next mile.

Professional consequence.

The road narrowed after marker twenty-three.

Cliff on our left.

Nothing on our right except air and occasional gulls that appeared to have stronger opinions about freight than most merchants.

Evin walked near the lead wagon now.

His brass rods stayed collapsed at his belt unless he needed a reading.

The sky was clear enough to make every warning sound insulting.

Blue overhead.

Gray sea.

Thin white clouds stretched west.

Iria still stopped us at the next shelter and ordered a full tie check.

"Why?" one merchant factor asked.

She looked at him.

He found another task.

I approved.

Pavo found a cracked buckle on wagon four.

Kelm found nothing and complained that inspections were less satisfying when people maintained equipment.

I checked the walking leg because the road between spans was smoother and my field peg had started annoying the back of my knee.

Change tool for terrain.

Obvious.

Still required remembering.

We ate standing beside the shelter wall.

Dried meat.

Hard cheese.

A flat onion cake one of Pavo's workers had made in Halden.

Sera traded half her cheese for my onion cake.

Bad trade for her.

I accepted before ethics became relevant.

Evin held a finger in the air.

"Wind's changing."

Kelm said, "Wind does that."

"Thank you. I trained eleven years for the confidence to tell you."

"Money well spent."

Evin ignored him.

"Turning more west. Charge rising a little. Not storm. Maybe shelf wash."

Iria asked, "Road effect?"

"More lateral gusts after Twenty-Four."

Kelm nodded toward the next bend.

"Road opens there."

We moved.

The next two miles were easy enough that I started distrusting them.

That was my mistake.

Not because I caused what happened.

Because I let easy become one problem instead of many small ones waiting to disagree.

The road rounded a headland.

Wind hit us from the right.

Hard enough that everyone leaned left without discussion.

Evin shouted, "Steady! Not gust!"

Good.

Steady wind was easier.

Then something metallic shrieked above us.

Not road hardware.

Animal.

Sera looked up first.

"Wirewings."

Three shapes dropped from the cliff face above.

Not birds exactly.

Too long through the body.

Leather-gray wings with dark metallic quills at the edges. Narrow heads. Hooked front claws. Each one maybe the weight of a large dog, but wings made size dishonest.

They were not diving at us.

They were diving at wagon two.

Lamp crystal.

Charged metal fittings.

Something attractive enough to make common sense optional.

"Animals center!" Pavo shouted.

Workers moved.

Sera already had her sword out.

The first wirewing hit the top rail of wagon two.

Not landed.

Hit.

Its claws scraped sparks off the iron corner.

The mule team panicked.

Pavo hauled left rein.

One mule went right anyway.

Wagon tongue twisted.

The right front wheel struck the cliff-side drainage ridge.

At the same moment, the second wirewing clipped a hanging cargo hook.

The hook swung free.

Toward a worker's head.

Three things.

Mule.

Wheel.

Hook.

I saw all of them.

That was not enough.

Plane at the hook first.

Small.

Fast.

I knocked its swing outward.

Worker ducked.

Good.

Then wheel.

The wagon was climbing the drainage ridge at a bad angle.

I put a force plane low against the wheel hub, not to stop it, just to bleed sideways travel.

The wheel came down.

By then the mule had pulled another half-step right.

Too late.

Pavo and Sera reached it before I did anything useful.

Pavo caught the bridle.

Sera got one hand on the harness ring and used her whole body to turn the animal's head back toward the road.

The wirewing on the rail launched again.

Third problem became fifth.

Evin's brass rod snapped open.

A crosswind pulse hit the creature from beneath and rolled it sideways.

Not enough to throw it off the cliff.

Enough for Sera to let go of the mule, step once, and cut across the wirewing's wing joint when it came down.

The creature screamed.

Loud.

Metal quills rattled against stone.

The other two answered.

One went for Evin.

He ducked.

I moved a plane into the creature's path.

Too slow.

Not wildly.

Half a second.

The wirewing passed the place where the plane was forming before the plane existed.

Evin rolled under the wagon instead.

Kelm swung an iron bar with both hands and hit the creature in the chest as it came low.

No rank.

Minimal magic.

Excellent bar.

The wirewing tumbled over the road edge.

For a second I thought it was gone.

Then wings caught air below us and it climbed again.

"Move the wagons!" Iria shouted.

Not kill everything.

Move.

Right.

The road widened thirty yards ahead near an old storm-vane base.

Pavo got wagon two rolling.

I took the outside rear corner.

If wheel drifted now I could correct sooner.

Sera ran beside the lead mule.

Evin came out from under the wagon covered in dust and fury.

"I hate those things."

"Useful information," I said.

"Later."

The wounded wirewing came again.

Sera met it.

The other two circled higher.

One screamed.

The mules tried to answer with panic.

Pavo shouted names.

Not commands.

Names.

"Toma! Toma! Easy. Hett, hold!"

The animals knew their own names better than they knew danger.

Good system.

Another gust hit.

Wagon four's rear tarp snapped loose.

Metal buckle whipped free.

I saw it.

I also saw wagon two's rear wheel start walking outward under the crosswind.

Both.

I chose wheel.

Plane.

Angle.

Short push inward.

Correct.

Tarp buckle struck the maintenance wagon behind us.

Not a person.

Fine.

Then a loose crate inside wagon four shifted because the tarp line had also been a secondary tie.

I heard it before I saw it.

Wood sliding.

Pavo did too.

"Four! Stop after the vane!"

We reached the wide base.

Iria moved wagons into the cliff shadow one at a time.

Sera killed the wounded wirewing there.

Clean thrust under the jaw when it committed low.

One of the others made a final pass, saw the bad economics, and left.

The third followed.

Quiet returned badly.

Everyone breathed too loud.

Mules stamped.

One worker had a cut across his cheek from something I had not seen happen.

Shallow.

A crate in wagon four had split one corner.

Dyed cloth inside.

Blue fabric pushed through the crack like a tongue.

No medicine lost.

No glass instruments broken.

No people over the cliff.

Good result.

Too many small failures.

Iria said, "Count."

Pavo counted animals.

Workers counted people.

Cargo factor counted seals.

Sera cleaned her sword on the dead wirewing's wing membrane.

Evin inspected his brass rod.

Kelm picked up one metallic quill.

I looked at the road where the fight had happened.

Hook.

Wheel.

Mule.

Creature.

Wind.

Tarp.

Crate.

None beyond me.

That was the irritating part.

I could have corrected the hook.

I did.

Could have kept the wheel centered.

I did.

Could have turned the mule's momentum slightly before Pavo and Sera reached it.

Didn't.

Could have intercepted the wirewing diving Evin.

Plane was late.

Could have caught the tarp buckle.

Not while holding the wheel.

Nothing impossible.

Just simultaneous.

Sera came over.

"You okay?"

"Yes."

"You have the face."

"Everyone needs to stop saying that."

"No."

She followed my eyes back down the road.

"You missed Evin."

"Yes."

"He didn't."

Evin was arguing with Kelm about whether the quill could hold charge after death.

Alive enough.

"I know."

"Then what's the problem?"

"I saw it."

Sera waited.

"I saw the dive before he moved," I said. "Plane was still late."

"By half a heartbeat."

"Yes."

"He rolled."

"Yes."

She looked at me.

"You want to be faster."

Not accusation.

Observation.

"Yes."

"Then train."

Simple answer.

Usually good answer.

I looked at the cracked cloth crate.

At the loosened tie.

At the wheel track near the road edge.

"Maybe."

Kelm walked over with the wirewing quill.

"Hold this."

I took it.

Blue spark snapped into my finger.

"Fuck."

Kelm smiled.

"Still charged."

Evin shouted, "I told you not to hand it to people!"

"Needed test."

Iria came over.

"Road status?"

Evin looked west.

His smile disappeared.

He opened both rods.

Waited.

The tips hummed.

"Charge climbed faster than expected."

"Storm?"

"Not yet. But the air's getting ready for one."

Iria looked at the road ahead.

Then back at the convoy.

"Repair the crate. Reset ties. Ten minutes."

She looked at me.

"Greg."

"Yes?"

"Whatever you were doing during the attack, keep doing it."

"Useful specificity."

"And don't try to catch everything."

There.

Professional insult.

Also correct.

We repaired the crate.

The blue cloth stayed inside.

Kelm kept the charged quill.

I kept thinking about half a heartbeat.

That felt less harmless.
