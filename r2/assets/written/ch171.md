# Chapter 171: The Halden Edge

We reached Halden's outer freight district without entering the city proper.

I recognized nothing for almost an hour.

Then I recognized a corner.

Not a building.

Not a sign.

A slope.

Two streets descending toward a long brick wall.

Old Greg had walked that slope hundreds of times later.

There had been a baker on the corner.

Current life had a rope yard.

Bundles of hemp hung from a rail where I remembered bread cooling in summer air.

The rope-yard woman watched me stare.

"You buying?"

"No."

"Then admire from somewhere less useful."

I moved.

Varin was measuring a drain outlet beside the freight wall.

Doss had found a cracked clay line under the shoulder.

Mira was arguing with a cart driver who believed orange flags meant decorative permission.

Halden remained busy without caring that I remembered a different version.

Our survey stopped three streets short of the central district.

No Mara.

No old addresses.

No pilgrimage.

At lunch Doss bought a meat pie and dropped half the filling down his sleeve.

"Professional," I said.

"Silver observation?"

"Extremely."

We spent the afternoon proving that the freight-wall drain belonged to Halden, the upstream ditch belonged to Carrow district maintenance, and the water itself recognized neither government...
