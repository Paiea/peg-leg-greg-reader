# Chapter 173: The Borrower

I carried the cuff for a full day without using it.

This was more difficult than I wanted to admit.

Not because it called to me.

It did not hum.

It did not warm through the box.

No ancient whisper explained that destiny had finally located my wrist.

It was a rented tool wrapped in oiled cloth between two pairs of socks.

The problem was that I knew exactly what it was for.

Every time a load shifted, my mind supplied the same thought.

Faster.

Not stronger.

Just faster.

We left Maelin's station before sunrise.

The western band had not arrived yet, but Evin said the air was getting "organized," which sounded worse than stormy.

"Clouds don't organize," Sera said.

"Air does."

"That's not better."

"It wasn't meant to be."

The road from marker twenty-five to twenty-seven climbed above the open sea again.

No trees.

No tall grass.

Mostly black stone, low gray scrub, and old road posts wrapped in copper wire.

Every few hundred yards a blue-white spark crawled between the wire turns and vanished.

Residual charge bleeding harmlessly into ground.

Mostly harmlessly.

Kelm stopped at the third post and kicked dirt over a melted patch beside it.

"That one bit someone last year."

"How?" I asked.

"Someone pissed on it."

Sera said, "Of course."

Evin closed his eyes.

"I hate people."

Pavo called from the lead wagon, "People buy freight."

Evin opened his eyes.

"Necessary evil."

The first problem came before breakfast.

Wagon one lost a brake shoe pin on a downhill grade.

Not catastrophic.

Pavo heard the scrape immediately and called stop.

Iria chocked the whole line.

Kelm crawled under the axle.

"Pin sheared," he said.

"Spare?" Iria asked.

"Yes."

Easy.

Then the wind shifted.

Not a gust.

A long side pressure from the sea.

The chocked wagon creaked against the grade.

Pavo added a second chock.

I watched the wagon tongue.

Moving reference.

Small movement.

Nothing dangerous.

I put one plane beside the rear bed, loose.

Kelm heard the cast.

"Don't make me fight you under here."

"I'm not holding."

"Good."

The wagon moved a fraction.

Plane followed.

No cuff.

Kelm replaced the pin.

We moved on.

Second problem came at a stone cut where the road narrowed around an old landslide repair.

A chunk of cliff had fallen overnight.

Not enough to close road.

Enough to leave one wagon width plus maybe six inches.

The drop on the outside was not infinite.

Only several hundred feet.

Comforting distinction.

Pavo got out of the lead wagon.

"I walk wheels."

Iria nodded.

"Kelm outside?"

Kelm looked at the edge.

"No."

"Sera?"

"I'll take outside."

I said, "I can."

Sera looked at me.

"You can what?"

"Walk outside line. Catch drift."

"You have one leg made for this road and one leg made by a carpenter with grudges."

"Field peg is stable."

"I am still better at not falling."

True.

Annoying.

Iria said, "Sera outside. Greg watch rear bed. Pavo wheels."

Done.

Wagon one passed.

Then two.

Wagon three's rear load made it want to swing outward where the road curved.

Harness ring moved.

Bed rail followed.

I caught the last inch.

No cuff.

Faster than yesterday because I was watching earlier.

Pavo saw.

"There."

I almost smiled.

Sera did.

"He likes when the wagon behaves."

"Wagon did not behave," I said. "We managed disagreement."

"Worse sentence."

Wagon three cleared.

Four followed.

No damage.

No applause.

Correct.

At midday we reached a little road shrine built into the cliff.

Not religious exactly.

Worker shrine.

Hooks for lost-route tags.

Copper plaques with names.

Old tools hung from nails.

Someone had left a cracked wheel pin and three dried flowers.

Kelm stopped.

So did Pavo.

Nobody explained.

We ate there.

Hard cheese again.

Dried pears.

Cold sausage.

Better than Lette's soup, which felt disloyal to say.

A courier came east while we were eating.

One rider.

Two packet bags.

He stopped for water.

Pavo knew him.

Of course Pavo knew everyone who moved anything.

"Carrow bag?" I asked.

Courier shook his head.

"Northbank packet transfer in the west bag. Names posted at station twenty-eight."

Mara's letter might already be ahead of me.

Strange system.

She could be west of me on paper while staying physically north.

I liked that more than I should.

After lunch, Evin made us wait twenty minutes while he checked a line of three storm posts.

The air around the third one smelled metallic.

Hair on my arms lifted.

"Charge pooling," he said.

"Fix?" Iria asked.

"Maybe."

He looked at the post.

Then at the road.

Then at the copper line disappearing uphill.

"Return bleed might be clogged."

Kelm said, "Drain box is twenty yards back."

They opened it.

Mud.

Leaves.

One dead lizard.

Road infrastructure.

Kelm cleared debris with a hook.

Evin checked charge again.

Lower.

Problem solved.

I had contributed nothing.

This was healthy.

The cuff stayed in my pack.

By fourth bell, the sky west had turned from blue to pale silver.

Not cloud cover.

Thin haze.

Evin kept looking at it.

"Band edge?" I asked.

"Forward moisture. Maybe."

"Maybe again."

"Would you prefer lies?"

"Only specific ones."

"Then yes, terrifying doom."

"Thank you."

He pointed at the cliff wall ahead.

"Road station twenty-eight is two miles. We should be there before dark."

We were not.

A rear wheel on the maintenance wagon developed a wobble one mile short.

Pavo called it before I saw it.

Kelm saw the cause before Pavo finished stopping.

"Rim wedge."

One wedge had backed out from the iron tire.

Repair took thirty minutes.

Then rain started.

Not storm rain.

Thin, sideways drops that found the back of my neck no matter which way I faced.

Walking foot went back in pack.

Field peg on.

We moved slowly.

At one point wagon four slid six inches toward the outside when both rear wheels crossed wet polished stone.

I saw harness movement.

Then bed.

Plane came.

Fast enough.

One clean correction.

No cuff.

We reached station twenty-eight after dark.

I was wet through.

Pavo was wet through.

Sera somehow looked merely damp, which was suspicious.

The station keeper handed Iria a packet of route notices.

Then looked at me.

"Greg?"

"Depends."

He held up a letter.

Northbank seal.

My name.

I took it before pretending indifference became embarrassing.

Mara's handwriting leaned slightly right when she wrote fast.

This one leaned enough to fall over.

**The west ferry office changed the tide table after I finalized weekly handoffs and nobody told us until two captains arrived at the same dock. I spent most of yesterday fixing someone else's improvement.**

I smiled.

She continued.

**Bess still hates me. Torren brought tea during the shouting. I have now learned that authority means people ask why you allowed a problem you were not informed existed. Very educational.**

There were two paragraphs about staffing.

One about rent.

One about a bakery bun she claimed would make Halden apologize.

Then:

**Your road letter arrived. I am glad you said you want the job. Be careful, but not in the useless way people say when they mean don't go. Go. Just come back with approximately the same number of parts.**

Reasonable standard.

At the bottom:

**Also I found your table. You left it in the back of the Northbank folder. Nine columns. Liar.**

I laughed loud enough that Sera looked over from the stove.

"What?"

"Private correspondence."

"Mara found the table?"

I stared.

"How do you know about table?"

"You told me."

I had not.

Probably.

Maybe.

Dangerous social leakage.

I wrote back before bed.

**Road is good. Wind is rude. Sera remains professionally violent. I rented a setter's cuff and have not used it. This sentence is intentionally placed before you hear about it later from someone else.**

I paused.

Mara knew about Faultglass.

Knew about my relationship with bad ideas.

I could hear her asking why.

So I answered before she could.

**I am half a heartbeat late when several moving failures happen at once. The cuff can make one family faster and may make other work slower afterward. I know the trade. I do not need it yet.**

Then:

**I bought nothing else. This is financial maturity.**

Technically rental.

Still.

I folded the letter.

Put it in the station's east packet bag.

Then checked the cuff box.

Still wrapped.

Still boring.

Good.

I put it back under the socks.

Sleep came quickly.

No dreams about artifacts.

Just wheels.
