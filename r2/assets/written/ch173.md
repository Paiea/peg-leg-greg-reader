# Chapter 173: The Bad Batch

The filtration shop smelled wrong when I got back.

Too much hot clay.

Too little talking.

Then Arlo started shouting.

Useful diagnostic.

Eleven chambers from the last firing had hairline cracks around the lower collar.

Not visible warm.

Visible after cooling.

Pellin had shortened the dry hold because the firing queue was backed up.

Arlo had approved moving the batch forward because the north mill shipment was due.

Dena had packed six before checking all collars because she assumed the cooling rack meant finished.

Nobody had committed the entire mistake alone.

This made everyone less happy.

"You changed the hold," Arlo said.

"You told me the kiln had to turn!" Pellin snapped.

"I said we needed the slot, not that you could guess at moisture."

"You built the schedule."

"You run the clay."

"And Dena packed them."

Dena turned around slowly.

"Finish that sentence."

Pellin did not.

Ivena stood between the ledger and the door with both palms flat on the worktable.

"Nobody ships the north order today."

Dena opened her mouth.

"Nobody," Ivena repeated.

That one belonged to her.

We lost eleven chambers.

Two more needed reinspection.

The north mill received a one-week service credit.

Thirty-one copper disappeared between clay, firing time, and the credit.

Arlo was still angry.

Pellin was still angry.

Dena was still Dena.

I owned part of a business large enough to have expensive ordinary mistakes now.

Nobody celebrated...
