# Chapter 174: The Reserve

Iria made us practice losing cargo before breakfast.

This was unpopular.

Merchant factors objected first because that was their professional role.

Pavo objected second because the porridge was still hot.

Sera did not object because she had already eaten.

I objected privately because I wanted coffee.

Nobody cared.

Wagon three carried the two sealed merchant chests on a rear cradle built to release from the main bed.

Not easily.

Nothing expensive released easily unless designers wanted theft to become exercise.

Two locking pins.

One safety chain.

One brake line that had to be clipped clear before the cradle dropped.

If everything went correctly, the cradle rolled backward on short rails and fell away without taking the whole wagon with it.

If everything went incorrectly, we learned why Iria had insisted on practicing.

"No live drop," she said. "Sequence only."

The merchant factor relaxed slightly.

"Pavo owns wagon. Kelm owns hardware. Sera owns outside clearance. Greg, watch the bed and tell me when release changes load. Evin watches charge if we ever do this under weather."

There.

Not Greg saves cargo.

Greg watches one part.

Good.

I almost laughed at myself.

Pavo pulled the wagon onto a shallow grade behind station twenty-eight.

Chocks in.

Team unhitched.

Kelm showed us the release pins.

"These are not decorative."

"Helpful," Sera said.

"People treat red paint as decoration."

Both pins had red heads.

One sat under a leather flap.

The safety chain ran through a release hook on the left rear corner.

Kelm made Pavo's youngest worker repeat the order twice.

"Brake line."

"Brake line."

"Left pin."

"Left pin."

"Right pin."

"Right pin."

"Chain last."

"Chain last."

Kelm pointed at him.

"Why chain last?"

"So cradle doesn't leave while we're touching pins."

"Good."

A normal good.

I allowed it.

Iria said, "Run sequence."

Pavo unclipped the secondary brake line and tied it forward.

Kelm pulled left pin.

Then right.

The rear cradle settled a fraction.

There.

Load change.

Wagon bed lifted slightly at the front.

Harness tongue would rise if team was attached.

I put a plane near the front bed.

Loose.

"Don't cast," Iria said.

I dropped it.

"You said watch."

"Habit."

"Break it."

Sharp.

Fair.

Sera cleared outside line.

Kelm put one hand on the safety release.

Stopped.

"At this point, if we pull chain, cradle goes."

Merchant factor asked, "How much cargo value?"

Iria said, "Enough."

"That is not—"

"Enough that I will not do it casually. Not enough that I will risk people to avoid doing it."

Conversation ended.

Kelm reset the pins.

Pavo reattached brake line.

We ran it again.

Then again with Pavo's worker calling sequence.

Third time took less than a minute.

"Under storm, double," Kelm said.

"Under panic, triple," Sera added.

"Under merchant commentary, quadruple," I said.

Factor looked at me.

Iria almost smiled.

Almost.

After breakfast, Evin called us to the weather board.

Station twenty-eight had one.

Actual board.

Wooden frame with sliding brass markers for wind direction, charge, cloud band, road closures, and last safe passage estimate.

Evin moved the western-band marker three slots east.

"It accelerated overnight."

Iria said, "How much margin?"

"If current speed holds, maybe eight hours after our planned far-gate arrival."

"If faster?"

"Less."

"Strong work."

"Weather remains rude."

Kelm pointed at the map.

"Span Twenty-Seven today."

This was the damaged hanger plate.

Temporary reinforcement.

The second place everyone had been paid extra to worry about.

Iria looked at Evin.

"Can we cross?"

"If we reach before fourth bell. Wind should still be below closure."

Kelm said, "Hardware can take normal controlled load."

Pavo said, "Animals can."

Sera said, "Unless more flying knives show up."

I looked at my pack.

The cuff remained inside.

Iria followed my eyes.

"No."

"I didn't ask."

"Good."

Again.

We left station twenty-eight before second bell.

Road climbed through a cut where old stormglass had melted into the cliff face in pale blue streaks.

Some lines ran six feet long.

Lightning scars.

Evin stopped us once to let charge bleed from a copper post.

Kelm stopped us twice for hardware.

Pavo stopped us once because one mule had a stone in its shoe.

Sera stopped us once because she wanted to piss.

Iria accepted all four categories of delay without ranking dignity.

Leadership.

Span Twenty-Seven appeared around noon.

Longer than Twenty-One.

Nearly a hundred feet between cliff cuts.

The road deck hung from two main chains and a web of vertical hangers.

West hanger plate at the far anchor had a temporary brace bolted across it.

Even from our side I could see new metal against old stone.

Kelm swore quietly.

"What?" Iria asked.

"Brace moved."

"How much?"

"Enough."

Iria looked at me.

"Do not."

"I said nothing."

"Your face did."

This had become oppressive.

Kelm crossed alone with a safety line.

Slow.

At midpoint the whole span shifted under a side gust.

Not violent.

Large.

The road moved several inches and stayed there before returning.

Kelm reached the west anchor.

Crouched.

Inspected.

Then waved Evin across.

Evin went.

The rest of us waited.

Twenty minutes.

Thirty.

Merchant factors started asking Iria questions.

She answered the first three.

Ignored the fourth.

Finally Kelm and Evin came back together.

Kelm's face was bad.

Not panic.

Work bad.

"Brace is fine," he said.

"Then?"

"Old plate cracked farther than survey."

"Passable?"

He looked at Evin.

Evin said, "Wind now twelve to fifteen. Gusts maybe twenty-two. Charge moderate."

Kelm looked at Iria.

"Wagons one and two, yes. Three is ugly. Four maybe."

Pavo said, "Three is heaviest."

"I know."

Iria asked, "Interior fallback from here?"

Kelm pointed east.

"Back to marker twenty-four. Then south."

"Cost?"

Pavo said, "Day and a half just to get back."

Evin said, "That probably loses weather margin entirely."

There.

Safe option.

Not easy.

Real.

Iria looked at wagon three.

Then at the span.

"Can we lighten three enough?"

Pavo said, "Move bearings to maintenance wagon. Merchant chests still heavy."

Merchant factor started, "Those cannot—"

Iria raised one finger.

He stopped.

Kelm said, "Drop rear cradle and three crosses fine."

We had practiced that before breakfast.

The factor went pale.

"Both chests?"

"Cradle holds both."

"Value—"

"I know value," Iria said.

I watched her.

She was not bluffing.

If Kelm said the bridge could not take the loaded cradle safely, she would cut it.

Contract allowed it.

Crew had practiced.

People lived.

Cargo did not get a vote.

I looked at the pack again.

The cuff box was inside.

Could it change anything here?

Maybe.

No.

Wrong problem.

Kelm did not need faster force routing.

He needed lower load on cracked hardware.

I said nothing.

Iria turned to Pavo.

"Shift bearings first. Reweigh by feel."

Pavo nodded.

We moved cargo.

Forty minutes.

No drama.

Bearings from three to four and maintenance.

Water barrel from four to one.

Tools redistributed.

Pavo walked wagon three twenty yards and back.

Kelm watched suspension.

Then both men went onto the bridge with Evin.

Another wait.

When they returned, Kelm said, "Cross three with cradle. One worker each side. No stops on deck. Greg at west half. I want late support only."

"How late?"

"Later than you want."

Of course.

Iria asked, "Safe?"

Kelm did not answer immediately.

That mattered.

"Within contract safe," he said.

Not perfectly safe.

Nothing was.

Iria made the call.

We crossed wagon one.

Then two.

Wagon three came last before four.

I stood on the west half near the cracked plate.

Field peg planted wide.

Cuff in pack on the far side.

Not on me.

Pavo drove.

Sera walked outside line.

Kelm watched plate.

Evin watched wind.

I watched movement.

The bridge shifted.

I let it.

Wagon three reached midpoint.

Crosswind rose.

Evin called, "Twenty!"

Deck traveled west.

Cracked plate took load.

Temporary brace tightened.

Kelm shouted, "Now."

One plane.

Small.

I caught last inch of deck travel and moved with it.

Not stopping.

Slowing the finish.

Plate held.

Wagon rolled.

Another gust.

Different direction.

I released.

Built again.

Late.

Correct.

Wagon three cleared.

Merchant factor exhaled loudly enough to count as weather.

Wagon four followed easier.

Then animals.

Then us.

On the far side, Iria waited until Kelm had checked the plate again.

"Status?"

"No new crack."

She nodded.

Then looked at the merchant factor.

"Your chests live another day."

He laughed weakly.

Not because funny.

Because alive cargo had become emotional.

We moved on.

I did not touch the cuff.

That mattered.

Not morally.

Mechanically.

We had faced a serious problem.

The safe option had existed.

Cargo could have been cut.

We had solved it without narrowing me further.

The cuff had not been necessary merely because it was available.

At the next shelter, I took the box out.

Set it on the table.

Did not open it.

Evin updated weather.

"Band gained another hour."

Iria stared at map.

"How many until far gate?"

Pavo said, "Two good days."

Evin said, "We have two if tomorrow stays below closure."

Kelm said nothing.

Sera looked at me.

Then at box.

"Still renting your bad idea?"

"Yes."

"Expensive decoration."

"So far."

Iria tapped the map.

"Tomorrow gets worse. Cuff or no cuff, we stop when I say stop."

"Agreed."

"If cargo needs cutting, we cut it."

"Agreed."

The merchant factor was not in room.

Helpful.

I put the cuff box back in pack.

Reserve.

That was all.

For now.
