# Chapter 174: The Clerk

Ivena hired the clerk before Arlo won the kiln argument.

Arlo considered this procedural betrayal.

"A clerk doesn't make chambers."

"Neither does writing the wrong customer on a return slip," Ivena said.

"We need firing capacity."

"We need to know which firing we're behind on."

The clerk was Senn Marr.

Half days.

Four mornings a week.

Former grain-counter assistant with handwriting so clean it made mine look criminal.

Senn spent the first morning asking why the north mill receipt, east dye-house receipt, and Hesk's crate-condition slip all used different date formats.

Dena blamed civilization.

Hesk blamed Dena.

Marris blamed Hesk despite having met him twice.

Senn made one form.

Then discovered Dena had been charging two different return fees depending on whether she liked the customer.

"That is not a policy," Senn said.

"It was working."

"It was emotional taxation."

I left the room because I wanted to remember the phrase without becoming involved.

By the end of the week we had one route ledger, two carrier sheets, a visible firing queue, and the same kiln.

Arlo stood beside it with his arms crossed.

"Still too small."

Ivena did not answer...
