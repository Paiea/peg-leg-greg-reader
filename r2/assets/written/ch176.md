# Chapter 176: The Escort

The fallen vane looked like a dead bird the size of a house.

One bronze fin lay across the inside half of the road.

The central arm had broken near its pivot and twisted downhill before the safety chain caught it.

Nothing blocked passage completely.

Everything made passage uglier.

Iria stopped us fifty yards back.

Evin went forward first.

Kelm with him.

Sera stood beside the lead mules, one hand on Toma's harness.

Pavo looked at the sky.

"They know."

"Who?" I asked.

He nodded at the animals.

All four lead mules had ears turned west.

Not at us.

At weather.

"Pressure?"

"Maybe. Smell. Sound. They don't write reports."

Unprofessional animals.

Evin and Kelm reached the vane.

Evin crouched near the broken pivot but did not touch it.

Kelm climbed over the bronze fin and checked the safety chain.

The air flashed blue along the fallen metal.

Small.

Fast.

I heard Evin swear from fifty yards.

Iria called, "Status?"

Evin walked back twenty yards before answering.

"Loaded with charge. Not enough to arc across road unless something gives it path."

Kelm shouted, "Chain holds."

"Can we pass?" Iria asked.

"One wagon at a time," Kelm said. "Inside wheels over stone lip. Outside wheels stay center. Nobody touches fin."

Pavo nodded.

"Animals?"

Evin looked at the mules.

"Keep metal tack away from vane."

Pavo swore.

"Harness has metal."

"Then keep them far inside."

"Road has cliff inside."

"I noticed."

Sera said, "I'll walk heads."

Iria looked at me.

"You watch wagon movement only."

"Yes."

"No clever contact with vane."

"I wasn't planning to."

"Good."

Oppressive word.

Wagon one went first.

Pavo drove.

Sera walked inside lead.

I took rear outside.

The broken fin filled half my vision as we approached.

Bronze ribs.

Stormglass inlays cracked dark.

One edge still carried tiny blue sparks that crawled against wind.

The mules pulled inward from it.

Good instinct.

Harness shoulder tightened.

I watched.

Tongue followed.

Rear bed drifted.

One small plane beside the outside rail.

Loose.

Moving.

Wagon passed.

No problem.

Wagon two did the same.

Then something screamed above us.

Not wirewing.

Bigger.

Sera looked up.

"Move!"

Shadow crossed the road.

A cliff wyvern dropped from the broken ridge above the vane.

Not enormous.

Large enough.

Long body.

Two rear legs.

Wing-arms with hooked joints.

Narrow head and a pale throat pouch bright with blue charge.

It did not dive at us.

It dove at the fallen vane.

Evin shouted, "It wants the charge!"

The wyvern hit bronze.

Every stormglass line on the broken fin lit at once.

Blue-white.

The animal convulsed.

Then the charge went somewhere else.

Through safety chain.

Into road anchor.

Across wet stone.

One of wagon two's mules screamed.

Pavo dropped reins rather than let current travel through him.

Good.

The team bolted.

Wagon two was halfway past the vane.

Worst place.

Sera ran with the animals.

No sword yet.

She grabbed the near lead by mane and harness leather, avoiding buckles.

Pavo yelled names from behind.

"Toma! Hett! Toma!"

The wyvern tore itself off the vane.

Smoke came from one wing membrane.

Now it was angry.

Different professional category.

It turned toward movement.

Toward the mules.

Sera drew.

"Greg! Wagon!"

Clear.

I wanted wyvern.

Not my job.

Wagon two's rear wheels slid outward as the team pulled inside.

Harness told me first.

Plane formed at rear rail.

Moved with bed.

Caught final travel.

Second plane at tongue.

Too much.

Dropped it.

One relationship, not two stops.

I shifted rear plane lower and angled.

Wagon mass corrected behind the team.

Pavo reached reins from the safe side and got control.

The wyvern lunged.

Sera stepped into it.

Not bravely.

Correctly.

Sword cut across the wing joint already burned by charge.

The animal snapped at her.

She ducked under the head and slashed the throat pouch.

Blue light spilled under skin.

Evin shouted, "Back!"

Sera moved before he finished.

The pouch burst.

No fire.

Air.

A concussive crack shoved everyone sideways.

My field peg skidded.

Wagon moved.

Wyvern moved.

Sera moved.

Three.

I caught wagon first.

Small plane under outside rear bed.

Half-step.

The wyvern's dead weight hit road and slid toward cliff.

Sera was still inside its wing.

I saw her boot catch membrane.

Plane toward her.

Late.

Again.

Half heartbeat.

She cut the membrane herself and rolled clear.

Wyvern went over the edge.

Not dead maybe.

Gone enough.

Sera came up on one knee.

"I am beginning to dislike flying things."

Evin shouted from behind the vane, "Beginning?"

Wagon two had stopped.

Pavo calmed the team.

Iria counted.

Everyone present.

One mule had a shallow burn where current jumped a buckle.

Pavo removed the tack and wrapped the skin.

Sera had a cut across one forearm from wing edge.

Not deep.

She cleaned it while complaining about animal architecture.

I stood beside the wagon breathing harder than work justified.

Again.

I had solved the wagon.

Sera had solved herself.

The plane toward her had been late.

Not because I picked wrong problem.

Because I could not arrive fast enough after finishing the first one.

The cuff sat in my pack on the maintenance wagon fifty yards behind us.

I did not look at it.

Iria came over.

"You good?"

"Yes."

"Sera?"

Sera said, "Fine."

"Pavo?"

"Mule walks. Tack needs replacing."

"Evin?"

"Vane is more charged now."

"Of course it is."

Kelm leaned over the safety chain.

"Wyvern impact bent secondary guide. Still holds."

Iria looked at road ahead.

"Can we pass remaining two?"

Kelm and Evin looked at each other.

Evin said, "No more than fifteen minutes here."

Kelm said, "Yes if we don't touch anything stupid."

"Move."

Wagon three crossed.

Then four.

No second wyvern.

Thank you, limited ecosystem.

Once the convoy was through, Kelm cut a small piece from the wyvern's torn wing membrane where it had remained caught on the vane.

He held it up.

"Charge sail."

Evin said, "Don't pocket that."

"Why?"

"Still live."

Kelm handed it to him.

Evin wrapped it in three layers of dry cloth.

Sera said, "If that's legal salvage, I claim first share for stabbing owner."

Iria said, "Road authority owns material attached to road hardware until assessed."

Sera stared at the cloth.

"I hate law."

"You like pay."

"Unfortunately."

We moved on.

The wind increased by degrees.

Not enough to stop us.

Enough that conversation got shorter.

At the exposed traverse, Pavo put two workers on each outside wagon line.

Kelm walked ahead checking every storm post.

Evin stopped using jokes.

That worried me more than sky.

Iria paced beside wagon one.

I stayed with wagon three.

Shoulder.

Harness.

Tongue.

Bed.

No cuff.

One correction.

Another.

Faster than before.

Still a sequence.

Still one after another.

The road turned around the cliff face.

Ahead, maybe half a mile, I could see the final suspended span.

Beyond it, stone walls of the far freight gate.

Close.

The storm wall over the sea was closer.

Evin stopped.

Held both rods out.

The tips lit blue.

Not sparks.

Steady light.

"Iria."

She came back.

"How bad?"

"Front edge changed."

"Meaning?"

"Faster. Maybe forty minutes. Maybe less."

Far gate looked insultingly near.

Iria looked at span.

Then at the little stone bay before it.

"Get everyone to bay. Now."

We moved.

Not running.

Faster than walking.

The first real gust hit before wagon four was under stone.

My plane caught rear sway.

Clean.

Then another gust arrived before the first correction fully released.

I built again.

Late by less than before.

Still late.

The cuff remained packed.

For another few minutes.
