# Chapter 177: The Adviser

The stone bay before the final span had been built for people who knew weather could win.

That made it better than most fortifications.

A low wall curved out from the cliff and created enough flat road for two wagons side by side.

Behind it, a black doorway led into the rock.

Storm refuge.

No bunks.

No kitchen.

Stone chamber wide enough for thirty people and maybe a dozen animals if everyone disliked each other efficiently.

Not enough room for wagons.

Iria put people inside first anyway.

Merchant factors included.

They did not object.

The sky had explained authority better than she could.

Pavo left two workers with each team.

Sera stayed outside.

So did I.

Evin and Kelm went onto final span.

The bridge was shorter than Twenty-Seven.

Wider.

Built heavier.

That was not comforting.

Heavy structures failed with confidence.

Wind hit the bay in pulses now.

Not steady.

One gust shoved me half a step toward wall.

The next pulled spray up from the sea and threw it over us like cold dust.

My field peg held.

Pavo's mules did not like any of it.

He had cloth over their eyes now.

"Helps?" I asked.

"Sometimes."

"Strong answer."

"They're mules."

Fair.

Kelm reached far anchor.

Evin stopped midpoint.

Raised both brass rods.

Blue light ran along them.

Then went out.

Then returned stronger.

Iria stood at bay wall watching.

Nobody spoke.

After five minutes, Kelm and Evin came back at a fast walk.

Kelm arrived first.

"Structure's sound."

Good.

Evin arrived second.

"Doesn't matter."

Less good.

Iria said, "Explain."

Evin pointed west.

The front edge was visible now.

Not as cloud.

As color change.

Sea under it had gone almost black.

Air between us and the band looked gray-blue, full of fine mist and charge.

"First front is maybe twenty minutes," he said. "Could be fifteen. Crosswind at span already gusting thirty. When front hits, fifty maybe more. Charged enough that exposed metal will start taking jumps."

"Crossing time?"

Pavo said, "All four wagons, controlled, twenty-five to thirty minutes."

Kelm said, "No stops on deck."

Evin said, "We don't have thirty."

There.

Iria looked at the span.

Then back toward road behind us.

"Retreat?"

Pavo said, "Back to Thirty-One is forty minutes minimum with wagons."

"Without?"

"People and animals twenty-five. Maybe."

Evin shook his head.

"Front catches us on traverse."

"Refuge holds?"

Kelm turned toward black doorway.

"People and animals, yes. Wagons stay outside. Chain them to bay rings."

Pavo said, "Wind may roll them."

"Then wagons roll."

Merchant factor came out of refuge.

"The cargo—"

Iria did not even look at him.

"Inside."

"But—"

"Inside."

He went.

Good survival instinct.

Iria said, "Safe plan. People and teams into refuge. Strip essential medicine and weather-sensitive glass if we can do it in ten minutes. Chain wagons. Cut wagon three rear cradle so it can't lever the whole bed if wind takes it."

Pavo nodded.

Kelm nodded.

Evin nodded.

Sera asked, "After front?"

"We wait. Recover what remains. Cross after weather clears. Completion premium gone. Merchant loss gets disputed later."

Sera shrugged.

"Sounds alive."

It did.

I looked at wagon three.

Rear cradle.

Two merchant chests.

Not medicine.

Not necessary food.

Money, basically.

Expensive objects somebody wanted farther west.

Cut them.

Shelter.

People live.

Correct call.

Iria looked at me.

"Problem?"

"No."

"Greg."

"Plan works."

"Then move."

We moved.

Pavo's workers started unhitching teams.

Sera led mules into refuge two at a time.

Kelm went to wagon three release pins.

I helped unload two medicine crates from wagon one because hands were hands when magic was not needed.

One crate weighed enough that my socket complained on second step.

I shifted grip.

Carried anyway.

Inside refuge, workers stacked essential cargo along back wall.

Evin kept calling time from doorway.

"Thirteen maybe."

Then, two minutes later:

"Twelve."

Weather arithmetic.

We pulled glass instruments from wagon four.

Copper wire stayed.

Dyed cloth stayed.

Machine bearings stayed.

Merchant chests definitely stayed.

Kelm had left pin out of rear cradle by time I came back.

Right pin next.

Safety chain last.

Exactly as practiced.

I stood beside wagon three.

Wind hit.

Bed moved against chocks.

Harness gone now.

No animals.

Different reference.

Bay ring.

Road wall.

I caught one small sway with plane.

Released.

Another gust.

Plane again.

Fast.

Not fast enough to imagine four wagons on bridge under worse.

The cuff box was in my pack inside refuge.

I knew where.

Of course I did.

Kelm reached safety chain.

"Ready to drop cradle when Iria says."

Iria looked at Evin.

"Time?"

He held rods out.

Blue light steady.

"Ten. Maybe twelve. Front slowed a little."

Something changed in my head.

Not inspiration.

Arithmetic.

Pavo had said twenty-five to thirty for normal controlled crossing.

Normal meant stopping each wagon at bridge mouth.

Checking sway.

Waiting for wind windows.

Greg support as one step in each sequence.

One correction.

Release.

Next correction.

What if release cost less thought?

Not new power.

Same planes.

Same moving references.

Faster family.

I looked at Kelm.

"How long per wagon if support timing isn't the bottleneck?"

He stared.

"What?"

"Assume I can manage last-travel correction without making Pavo pause between gusts. Structure sound. How long?"

Kelm looked at Pavo.

Pavo answered first.

"Four minutes each if teams walk clean."

"Sixteen."

"Plus spacing."

Evin said, "We don't have sixteen."

"Can you buy a wind window?"

He looked at me.

"What kind?"

"Not calm. Reduced crosswind at deck for maybe ten seconds at each wagon entry. Enough that Pavo gets team straight before full gust."

Evin frowned.

"I can bleed one entry gust through return wall. Maybe two. Not four if front is on us."

"What if we pair wagons?"

Pavo said, "No."

Immediate.

Correct.

"Fine."

Iria watched me.

"Say whole thought."

There it was.

Command wanted information.

Not theater.

"Cuff. Prime moving fixed-point family. I stop rebuilding every small plane from scratch. Pavo doesn't have to wait for me between movement calls. Kelm gets deck travel inside his hardware limits. Evin only needs to shape entries, not whole crossing."

Iria said, "And time?"

"Maybe four minutes each. Less if clean."

Pavo said, "Still sixteen."

"Three and a half if we don't stop."

"You don't get to make mules faster with math."

"No."

Kelm looked at span.

"Wagon one can cross three. Wagon two maybe three. Three with rear cradle is four minimum. Four three."

"Thirteen."

Evin said, "Front maybe ten to twelve."

Not enough.

I looked at wagon three cradle.

"Cut cradle anyway."

Merchant factor made a sound from refuge doorway.

Iria ignored him.

Kelm looked at me.

"Drop both chests?"

"Leave cradle chained in bay. Wagon three crosses light."

Pavo recalculated.

"Three minutes. Maybe less."

"Twelve total."

Evin looked west.

"Could have twelve."

"Could," Iria said.

Not yes.

I nodded.

"Safe plan still works. We shelter."

Important.

Sera leaned against refuge wall.

"You're not proposing this because people need it."

"No."

No point lying.

The people were already moving to safety.

"Then why?" she asked.

Wind hit bay wall hard enough to throw spray over top.

I looked at road.

At wagons.

At span.

At the far gate less than half a mile beyond.

Money mattered.

Cargo mattered to someone.

Reputation mattered.

The road mattered because we had spent four days learning how to move through it.

And yes.

I wanted to know if I could do it.

Not best reason.

Still true.

"Because I think we can cross without spending people," I said.

Iria's eyes narrowed.

"You're spending something."

"Yes."

"Your breadth."

"Maybe some."

"Documentation says persistence possible."

"Yes."

"And you still want to?"

"Yes."

No heroic wording.

Just yes.

Iria looked at each specialist in turn.

"Kelm. If Greg is as fast as he thinks, structure?"

"Holds unless gust exceeds forecast or plate changes."

"Evin."

"I can give entry windows. Once wagon is on deck, I manage charge, not wind comfort."

"Pavo."

"Teams can cross if we keep them moving. No stopping because Greg wants another try."

"Sera."

"I can keep heads forward. If animal panics, I solve animal."

Then Iria looked at me.

"Abort conditions."

I answered.

"Kelm calls structural change. Evin calls charge unsafe. Pavo loses team control. I miss two consecutive corrections or feel cuff heat beyond Maelin's normal range."

Iria added, "First wagon takes more than four minutes, we stop and shelter."

"Agreed."

"Front reaches span before wagon three clears, we stop where we are and use far bay if possible."

Evin said, "Far bay fits two wagons."

"Then one and two. Three and four stay."

Merchant factor looked sick.

Still alive.

Iria said, "This is not better than shelter. It is another option with worse personal cost to Greg and better cargo outcome if it works."

Everyone heard that.

Good.

She looked at me.

"Do you still want option?"

"Yes."

"Get cuff."

I went inside.

Pack against wall.

Socks.

Wooden box.

Oiled cloth.

The cuff looked exactly as boring as before.

I picked it up.

Safe plan still existed outside.

That was the point.

I walked back into the wind.
