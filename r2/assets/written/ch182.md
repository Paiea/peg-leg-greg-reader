# Chapter 182: The Specialist

Noll had moved my chair.

Not far.

Three inches left.

Maybe four.

Enough that I noticed before putting my bag down.

"Why?" I asked.

He looked up from the kitchen table.

"Why what?"

"Chair."

"Floorboard squeaks."

I stepped on the old position.

Squeak.

New position.

Nothing.

"Reasonable."

"Welcome home."

That too.

Carrow smelled like rain, chimney smoke, wet wool, and the cabbage Noll had apparently decided was dinner again.

I had been gone long enough that my room felt slightly staged when I opened it.

Bed made.

Window shut.

One book moved from floor to desk.

Blue-green ceramic snail still on shelf.

Good.

Continuity survived.

I put the stormglass beside it.

Three shards now.

All legal.

Excellent collection.

The western-road mud had dried white around the field peg tip.

I took the prosthetic apart before unpacking anything else.

Peg first.

Socket strap.

Lower pin.

Washed salt from metal.

Dried leather.

Checked wood for hairline splits.

None.

Walking leg next.

Pera would want to see the outer socket wear, but nothing had cracked.

One screw at the ankle had backed out maybe a quarter turn.

I set it on the table and reached for the little wrench.

The wrench rolled.

My hand moved.

A plane appeared beside it.

Instant.

The wrench tapped invisible resistance and stopped before falling off table.

I stared at it.

No cuff.

Three days since crossing.

Still instant.

I dropped the plane.

Wrench stayed.

Then I tried a broad hold under the walking foot while loosening the screw.

The shape took a breath.

Maybe less.

Long enough that I noticed.

Still strong once there.

I tightened screw.

Released.

Again.

Broad plane.

Delay.

Again.

Delay.

Slightly cleaner than at far gate.

Training already helping.

Good.

I let myself say it this time.

No one heard.

I wrote one line in the notebook.

**Broad access improving with repetition. Narrow family unchanged.**

Then another.

**Do not assume full return.**

That seemed fair to future me.

Downstairs, Noll shouted, "Food."

"One minute."

"Now."

"Aggressive."

"Cold cabbage is your problem then."

I went down.

Dinner was not cabbage stew.

It was cabbage fried with onion, sausage, and noodles.

Different crime.

I ate two bowls.

Noll asked about the road.

Not every detail.

He wanted to know if sea looked different from the cliff.

Whether the mules were smart.

Whether Sera still cursed during work.

Whether western coffee was as bad as Jorren claimed.

"Worse," I said.

"Impossible."

"I have evidence."

"You brought coffee?"

"No."

"Then no evidence."

Strong methodology.

I told him about the windrams.

He laughed until he had to put bowl down.

"They lick road?"

"Salt."

"And you're calling goat missing notice ridiculous."

"Different animal category."

"Sure."

I did not tell him about every half-heartbeat.

Not because secret.

Because dinner did not need all of me at once.

Afterward he made tea.

I washed bowls.

A plate slipped from wet fingers.

Small plane caught it before sink.

Instant.

Noll looked over.

"New?"

"Faster."

"Useful."

"Yes."

"Expensive?"

I looked at him.

"Why do you ask?"

"Everything you like is expensive."

Unfair.

Mostly accurate.

"Some."

"Money?"

"Also some."

He waited.

I did not explain whole cuff.

"Magic got narrower."

Noll nodded like I had said the soup was saltier.

"Can you work?"

"Yes."

"Can you walk?"

"Yes."

"Can you wash plate without cheating?"

I looked at plate.

"Probably."

"Then wash."

Ordinary standards.

I finished dishes by hand.

Next morning, Pell appeared before breakfast.

He did not knock long enough to count.

Noll let him in because apparently boundaries had categories.

Pell carried a small wooden box and one folded paper.

"Road leg."

"Good morning."

"Road leg."

I brought the field peg down.

He took it without asking and sat at table.

"Salt."

"Cleaned."

"Still salt."

"Road had ocean."

"Excuse."

He opened the box.

New metal sleeve.

Thin.

Vent slots along one side.

"For vibration washer?" I asked.

"For washer and wet. Current sleeve traps grit."

He slid old sleeve off.

Fitted new one.

Better drainage.

"How much?"

"Test first."

I stood.

Attached peg.

Walked kitchen.

Turned.

Walked again.

The sleeve clicked once on first step.

Pell frowned.

Removed it.

Bent one tab with pliers.

Put it back.

No click.

"Good," I said.

He glared.

"I say."

"Of course."

I walked again.

Pell nodded.

"Good."

There.

Official.

He noticed my left wrist when I reached for table.

Red band almost gone.

"New tool?"

"Setter cuff. Rental. Returned."

Pell's eyebrows moved.

"You use?"

"Yes."

"What cost?"

"Broad work slower. Narrow moving support faster."

He stared at me for several seconds.

Then said, "Show."

Of course.

I made one small moving plane around the new sleeve.

Instant.

Pell's eyes narrowed.

"Again."

Again.

Then broad plane over table.

Delay.

Pell noticed.

"Hnh."

"Technical."

"You trade hallway for one door."

I frowned.

"Not exactly."

"Door wide now. Hallway still there. Longer walk."

Better than my explanation.

I hated that.

"Maybe."

Pell packed tools.

"You want hallway, walk."

"That's the plan."

"You want door, use."

"Also plan."

He nodded.

"Then why face?"

Everyone needed to stop.

"No face."

Pell ignored me and left.

Noll said from stove, "You have a face."

I considered moving out.

At third bell, I went to Guild.

Not for work.

That was important.

I had to submit the western contract closeout copy for Carrow records and claim Pera's socket service reimbursement.

Tessa took both.

"Storm Road already filed."

"Fast."

"Iria."

That explained it.

Tessa stamped reimbursement form.

Then reached under desk.

One envelope.

Heavy paper.

Red corner mark.

I looked at it.

"No."

"I didn't say anything."

"You have the envelope face."

"That is not a thing."

She put it on counter.

**PRIORITY CONTRACT INQUIRY.**

Below that:

**MOVING LOAD / FAILURE ROUTING SPECIALIST REQUESTED.**

I stared.

Tessa said, "Consortium referral. Different client."

"Where?"

"South river works."

"When?"

"Three weeks."

Not urgent.

"Pay?"

"Inside."

I opened it.

Higher than Iria's suggested new rate.

Not dramatically.

Enough.

Three-day job.

Moving gate machinery under seasonal flood.

General wardwright already contracted.

Rigger already contracted.

They wanted me for one narrow line.

Not because no one else existed.

Because someone now knew exactly what kind of problem I was good at.

I read injury terms.

Travel.

Equipment.

Then caught myself calculating whether three weeks was enough time to rebuild broad access before the job.

Wrong question first.

Maybe.

I looked at Tessa.

"Need answer today?"

"No."

"Tomorrow?"

"No. Five days."

Good.

I folded contract.

Put it in bag.

Did not sign.

Tessa said, "You okay?"

"Yes."

"You look pleased."

"Pay is good."

"You look worried."

"Pay is very good."

She laughed.

I went home.

The contract sat on desk while I finished cleaning the walking leg.

One washer tried to roll away.

Tiny plane stopped it.

Instant.

I tightened ankle screw.

Tried broad support under heel.

A little faster today.

Still not what it had been.

Manageable.

Probably.

Downstairs, Noll called dinner.

I looked at contract.

Then at blue-green snail on shelf.

I set the contract under the snail so the window draft would not take it.

The snail was exactly heavy enough.

Legal salvage.

Excellent object.

I went downstairs before the food got cold.
