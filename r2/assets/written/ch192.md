# Chapter 192: The Three Owners

East Mill said yes.

The cooper said yes if the city paid for any damaged floorboards.

Hara Venn said no before Issen finished introducing herself.

Her house stood over the third parcel line with a narrow dye shop attached to the back.

Hara was sixty, square-shouldered, and wearing blue-stained gloves.

"No holes."

"We are requesting inspection access," Issen said.

"Inspection becomes measuring. Measuring becomes men with hammers. Men with hammers become invoices addressed to people who did not invite them."

Renn leaned toward me.

"I like her."

Hara heard him.

"I do not like you."

"Also fair."

Issen showed the old service plan.

Hara did not care.

Her grandfather had called the mill-side foundation stones bad winter stone and refused to store dye vats against them.

Renn asked why.

"Because he was not stupid."

"Technically useful answer."

"You are exactly why I said no."

I could have told her the exposed East Mill section stayed faintly warm without current water or heat.

That would have been true.

It also would have sounded exactly like the beginning of men with hammers.

Issen negotiated two hours in the dye-shop cellar with no cutting, no sampling, no moved shelving, and Hara present the entire time.

Hara added a copper deposit against broken jars.

The city clerk objected.

Hara closed the door.
