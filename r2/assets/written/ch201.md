# Chapter 201: The Oil Wall

I recognized the timber yard from the smell before I recognized the name.

Dry pine.

Lamp oil.

Hot resin.

Old Greg remembered a fire there.

Not a famous one.

Famous to the twelve men who had nearly died, probably.

To me it had been a winter delay and a story someone told badly at the Guild.

Current Greg was there because a warehouse-inspection team wanted one Silver support hand while they checked charged lamp fixtures beside dry stock.

The city inspector was a woman named Eda Marr.

She disliked timber merchants and expressed this as efficiency.

"Oil storage is moving."

The yard owner folded his arms.

"It has been there twenty years."

"Then you've been wrong for twenty years."

"No fire."

"Excellent. Let's continue that tradition."

The post-Guild-fire inspection rules required a masonry separation wall between bulk lamp oil and kiln-dry scrap.

The owner argued about floor space.

Eda gave him thirty days.

I remembered later oil barrels sitting against that scrap wall.

Maybe the fire had another cause.

Maybe the wall would be removed.

Maybe memory had simplified the yard.

I wrote the yard name under WATCH.

Eda saw me.

"Problem?"

"Not current one."

"Then don't make me one."
