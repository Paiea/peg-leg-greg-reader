# Chapter 238: The Seven Feet

The old marker did not make either family happy.

That was probably how we knew it was real evidence.

The survey line moved seven feet from one current fence.

Four from the other.

Both families had inherited boundaries built by people who had inherited other boundaries.

The city surveyor marked three points in red chalk.

One owner said the older fence proved use.

The other said the deed proved title.

The surveyor said both statements could become expensive if repeated to lawyers.

I was still standing beside the open shed wall because the charged marker needed to remain uncovered until measurements finished.

The farmer's son brought me a stool.

"Does Silver mean you know who wins?"

"No."

"Good job."

"Sometimes."

The surveyor finished.

The shed boards went back.

The marker remained underneath.

The city did not solve the property dispute that afternoon.

It did make the argument eleven feet more accurate...
