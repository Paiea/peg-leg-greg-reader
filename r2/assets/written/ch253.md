# Chapter 253: The Flour Room

The next East Mill appointment smelled better than the others.

That was the bakery.

Mila Doss owned the business and did not own the building, which had already made the property office unhappy before we arrived.

The old service sheet put a square mark beneath her flour room.

Renn called it a valve.

Issen called it a square mark.

Mila called it under my floor.

I liked her best.

The city had permission for visual inspection only.

No boards lifted.

No tapping.

No moving the mixing table.

Mila read the order herself.

"Forty minutes."

Issen nodded.

"If we need more, we ask."

"You ask before forty."

"Yes."

Renn had already crouched near the exterior vent.

Mila pointed at him.

"That one asks before breathing loudly."

"I have been profiled," he said.

The flour room floor looked ordinary.

Two dark oak boards had been replaced sometime in the last twenty years.

A narrow strip beside the north wall stayed dry where cold air from the vent had silvered everything else with faint frost.

I held my palm near it.

Not warm.

Less cold.

Different thing.

Renn put a gauge down without touching the floor.

Three degrees.

Then two and a half farther west.

Then ordinary cold beyond the mixing table.

"Route line fits," he said.

"Route line passes nearby," Issen corrected.

Mila folded her arms.

"Does nearby mean you want to open it?"

Issen looked at the order.

"Not today."

"Good answer."

At thirty-eight minutes Mila told us to leave because dough did not care about public infrastructure.

We left.

The bakery kept making bread above a square mark nobody had earned yet...
