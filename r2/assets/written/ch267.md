# Chapter 267: The Marker Cart

The winter road contract came with twenty-four marker stops and no promise that all twenty-four were reachable.

That clause made me like it immediately.

The district surveyor was Tamar Rusk.

She considered maps useful until the road contradicted them.

The driver, Soli, considered maps proof that office workers feared horses.

The Bronze assistant was Fenn, who had not yet learned that both opinions could become exhausting by noon.

My job was narrow.

Silver support if an old charged marker needed unloading, lifting, or isolation while a cap or tab was replaced.

At the first fork Tamar pointed east.

Soli turned north.

"East," she said.

"Bridge crew."

"Notice says open."

"Notice is yesterday."

Tamar looked at me.

I looked at Soli.

"He has the horse."

We went north.

Three miles later a road hand confirmed the east bridge was open to people and empty carts only.

Tamar crossed out one line on her sheet.

Soli did not say anything.

That was worse...
