# Chapter 315: The Five-Copper Morning

The filtration business solved a customer problem without me.

This was not new.

What was new was the bill.

A restaurant returned two chambers because both service dates had been copied one week late from Senn's route sheet.

The ceramics were fine.

The water was fine.

The paperwork was wrong.

Senn found the error.

Dena checked the customer copy.

Ivena approved a five-copper service credit before Arlo arrived.

Pellin drove the replacement pair over himself because his packing bench had spare capacity that morning.

By the time I heard about it, the customer had already accepted the correction.

"Five copper?" Arlo asked.

"Cheaper than losing them," Ivena said.

Senn looked miserable.

"My copy error."

"Yes," Ivena said.

He waited for rescue.

She did not provide it.

"Fix the handoff."

He moved the service-date field beside the customer confirmation line.

No new field.

Dena drew a tiny skull beside the old position before they retired the form.

I had invested in a business that now generated mistakes, credits, and corrective paperwork without consulting me.

This seemed suspiciously like success.

I took my share of the smaller distribution when it came.
