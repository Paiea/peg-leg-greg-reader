## Purpose

R2 is no longer an open-ended story-development run.

R2 has found enough of the book to finish the iteration. The remaining job is to give Run 2 a real ending, preserve what it taught us, and hand the useful discoveries to 3L without forcing R2 to become the definitive version of Peg-Leg Greg.

R2 ends the argument of this iteration. It does **not** need to finish Greg's entire life.

## R2's final question

Greg has learned that permanent loss does not necessarily end him.

He has also learned that he can rebuild around damage, extract value from bad decisions, make narrow specialists more effective together, and sometimes alter another person's trajectory for the better.

The dangerous belief is therefore not that Greg is invincible.

It is:

> **I can adapt to ugly outcomes better than most people, so ugly outcomes are cheaper for me.**

The final arc should test that belief at full fantasy scale.

The ending must **not** prove the belief false through cheap punishment. Greg's risky logic should substantially work. He should earn real value, solve a real problem, and remain recognizably competent.

The ending pressure is subtler:

> **What kind of life does Greg build if almost every cost can be made workable?**

A successful adaptation is not the same thing as an acceptable life.

## Final arc requirements

The final arc should feel like satisfying fantasy first, thematic closure second.

Required ingredients:

- a proper fantasy job / expedition / problem large enough to justify the ending;
- action, travel, danger, loot or salvage, strange magic, and at least one memorable fantasy environment;
- mundane texture: food, money, lodging, repairs, prosthetic maintenance, contracts, jokes, waiting, cleanup;
- sharply specialized people who retain independent competence, wants, schedules, and veto power;
- Greg's specialization expressed through conditions, movement, force, failure paths, and enabling other specialists rather than generic power inflation;
- the people-side mirror of that specialization: Greg can create conditions for growth, but he cannot own another person's direction;
- Mara continues to have an independent professional life and does not become an ending reward or plot accessory;
- at least one clean Greg win that feels good without hidden punishment;
- one consequential choice where a slower/safer path exists and Greg knowingly chooses an uglier, faster, or more irreversible option because he believes he can carry its cost;
- the choice should **work enough to reinforce his worldview**;
- the consequence should remain durable even after Greg adapts around it.

Possible durable costs include narrower magic, reputation, access, social trust, professional identity, relationship geometry, future options, or another permanent constraint. Do not default to another missing body part.

Artifacts may participate if the story earns them, but the final arc is not required to become an artifact plot. Blackglass/Faultglass history means artifact danger is already known territory.

## Non-goals

Do **not**:

- turn the final arc into a giant lore dump;
- explain 3L inside R2;
- force Greg to die;
- force another reincarnation;
- punish Greg because the author needs a moral lesson;
- create a chosen-one reveal;
- introduce a massive new magic system that requires another book to resolve;
- make every specialist a student of Greg;
- make Greg secretly best at everyone's job;
- convert the ending into a sequence of professional consulting contracts;
- keep adding arcs because a new idea is interesting.

Interesting unused ideas belong in 3L inheritance, not automatically in R2.

## Landing / epilogue

After the major arc resolves, spend 1–3 chapters letting consequences become ordinary.

Prefer small things:

- Greg eats.
- Someone gets paid.
- Gear gets repaired.
- The leg needs maintenance.
- Mara makes or reports an independent decision.
- A teammate chooses another job.
- A relationship has changed in a way no speech can fully repair.
- Someone offers Greg another opportunity.

The final image should show the reader who Greg has become without summarizing the theme.

A good R2 ending should leave the feeling:

> **This works. Greg can keep making it work. That may be the problem.**

## Freeze conditions

R2 is complete when:

1. the final written chapter is selected and published;
2. the final chapter has a stable Greg-role title;
3. navigation and project frontier point to the final chapter;
4. continuity / Story Truth state is updated through the ending;
5. `R2_TO_3L_HANDOFF.md` is completed with KEEP / REBUILD / KILL / SEEDS;
6. the repository records **R2 COMPLETE** and the final authority SHA;
7. a durable tag or equivalent completion marker is created if repository workflow supports it.

After freeze:

- no new R2 story arcs;
- prose changes are limited to critical continuity, readability, or production fixes;
- audio and art may continue selectively, but they do not reopen story authority;
- large-scale R2 audio/art completion is not required before 3L begins.

## Production policy after freeze

Do not let production become sunk-cost prison.

Preserve enough R2 audio and visual production to retain what was learned and to keep the public run useful. Representative audio runs, favorite arcs, and the ending may continue. Full 1–final audio coverage is optional unless explicitly re-authorized.

3L becomes the active story-development frontier after the R2 freeze.
