# R2 Postmortem

Status: **FINAL DRAFT FOR R2 FREEZE**

R2 finished by discovering what the story actually wanted to be, then writing that version rather than protecting every prior chapter-shaped experiment.

The final selected ending target is Chapter 182, `The Specialist`, pending publication/freeze verification.

## What R2 was for

Run 2 was not a failed attempt that happened to produce useful fragments. It was the iteration where the project learned how Peg-Leg Greg wants to work.

R2 proved that the story becomes strongest when Greg is allowed to be:

- experienced without being omnipotent;
- funny without becoming detached from consequence;
- physically and financially embodied;
- highly competent across many ordinary tasks while becoming truly exceptional in one increasingly narrow lane;
- capable of making bad-looking choices that genuinely work;
- surrounded by people who remain adults with their own jobs, schedules, wants, rates, failures, and veto power.

R2 also proved that story discovery and final prose do not need to be the same operation.

## The most important process discovery

The post-118 temporal run wrote roughly forty short speculative chapters at about one thousand words each. That run was not the book. It was a search surface.

It discovered:

- Greg's narrowing specialization;
- the asymmetric specialist ecology;
- the people-development mirror of Greg's magic;
- Mara's Northbank trajectory;
- the value of Red Quay as contrast rather than permanent party;
- the need for capability to remain separate from aspiration;
- the value of letting people train Greg;
- the danger of turning every fantasy problem into professional consulting.

After that search, the project fresh-performed the real post-118 book at listening-first chapter length rather than expanding the short drafts in place.

The lesson for 3L is simple:

> **Use cheap prose to find the book. Then write the book.**

Do not confuse rehearsal survival with canon entitlement.

## What R2 found about Greg

### High floor, narrow ceiling

Greg's decades of experience give him a high competence floor.

He can fight, read contracts, rig, reason about wards, travel, command, repair, negotiate, and survive ordinary professional situations without looking helpless.

That broad floor should not be confused with his ceiling.

Pure specialists repeatedly beat him in their lane.

R2 became much stronger once this stopped being a problem to solve.

Greg does not need to become the best wardwright, duelist, rigger, healer, hunter, route reader, or weather worker.

His exceptional lane became relational:

- what is moving;
- what is fixed;
- where force may resolve;
- where failure can safely go;
- how one specialist's action can become possible inside the available geometry and time.

By the end of R2, the smallest versions of this magic are his most frighteningly capable ones.

### Reconstruction

Greg's prodigy trait is not generic genius.

It is reconstruction.

He knows what systems are supposed to accomplish because he has decades of first-life experience. When a mechanism, body, resource, technique, or institution is missing, he can often rebuild around the absence.

This applies to:

- the prosthetic;
- Blackglass load work;
- sword/magic integration;
- post-Faultglass techniques;
- moving support geometry;
- team composition;
- his own damaged professional reputation.

### Adaptability as hubris

R2's strongest Greg flaw is not `I cannot lose.`

It is:

> **I can adapt to ugly outcomes better than most people, so ugly outcomes are cheaper for me.**

The story becomes more dangerous when this is true often enough to become evidence.

Greg loses a leg and rebuilds.

Greg loses mana options and rebuilds.

Greg steals Faultglass, pays reputation and money, returns it, and still keeps durable knowledge.

Greg successfully changes one small part of Merek Sol's trajectory.

Greg uses the setter's cuff on the Storm Road, wins, gets paid, becomes more valuable, and accepts a real narrowing cost.

The correct ending is not punishment.

It is reinforcement.

## What R2 found about people

### The party is a build, but people are not components

The initial party question was too conventional.

R2 got better when Greg stopped needing a balanced `tank / healer / mage / rogue` group and started working around people with sharp specialties and expensive weaknesses.

Greg's value can reduce the operational cost of another person's specialization.

That does not give him ownership over the person.

The strongest party/social discoveries were:

- someone can be individually awkward for normal teams and excellent in Greg's geometry;
- another specialist can be much better than Greg at one thing without diminishing him;
- availability and rates destroy ideal compositions;
- a temporary crew can become excellent without becoming `the party`;
- lose one person and tactics should materially change;
- people can refuse Greg's proposed adaptation;
- Greg can correctly see capability and still be wrong that someone wants that future;
- other people should identify Greg's hidden habits and train him too.

### Capability is not aspiration

Lysa and Tomel were the clearest proof.

A person can be capable of a new lane without wanting to build a life around it.

Greg's people-development instinct works best when he creates safe sideways pressure and lets the other person own what they discover.

The phrase to preserve conceptually is:

> **Greg creates conditions. He does not author another person's growth.**

### Some people just want the money

Sera's refusal to turn every contract into self-actualization was an important correction.

Not every recurring person should be an optimization project.

Some people are already good at what they do.

Some do not want another skill.

Some want four days of pay.

That is enough.

## What R2 found about Mara

Mara became stronger when she stopped functioning as Greg's emotional reward or narrative scheduler.

Northbank matters because she chooses a professionally stronger life even though Greg dislikes the geography.

By the end of R2 she has:

- her own route desk;
- staff;
- ferry and captain problems;
- local relationships and office habits;
- an apartment and routines that exist without Greg;
- actual authority that produces its own consequences.

She remains close to Greg without becoming his permission structure.

Her final R2 observation is one of the most useful lines for 3L:

> **You keep borrowing from yourself.**

She refuses the job of deciding which pieces of Greg he is allowed to spend.

That preserves adulthood on both sides.

## What R2 found about artifacts

### Blackglass Anchor

Blackglass proved artifact integration could be ordinary, practical, and tradeoff-driven.

Pell's old correction remains important:

`Meat is bad bearing.`

Greg's answer, `Adaptable`, became more important than it looked at the time.

### Faultglass

Faultglass is the strongest artifact seed R2 produced.

Greg steals it for knowledge.

The theft damages trust, work, reputation, and money.

He returns it.

The cost remains.

The useful knowledge also remains.

This teaches Greg a dangerous lesson:

> a bad decision can still leave him stronger.

The unresolved self-question `Would steal again?` should remain available to 3L as a seed, not as mandatory continuity.

### Setter's cuff

The cuff works because R2 waits.

It is not another spooky singular object.

It is professional equipment with a comprehensible vocational tradeoff.

Maelin has lived with that trade for decades and does not regret it.

Greg's final cuff choice is therefore not artifact roulette.

He understands the cost, has a safe alternative, chooses the narrower path anyway, and materially succeeds.

That is the right R2 ending.

## What R2 found about fantasy texture

Mundane fantasy is a core pleasure, but it cannot become the only plot machine.

Strong texture included:

- bad meals;
- good meals;
- socks;
- prosthetic liners;
- wheel pins;
- contracts;
- legal salvage;
- rooms;
- market junk;
- route forms;
- harness repairs;
- cheap charms;
- accounting after danger.

The story weakens when every chapter becomes `competent professionals solve infrastructure problem`.

The correction is not to remove mundane texture.

It is to mix it with:

- monsters;
- ruins;
- storms;
- fights;
- travel;
- festivals;
- rivals;
- strange objects;
- loot;
- visual environments;
- people who want Greg dead or simply do not care about his work logic.

The Storm Road ending is strong because it uses infrastructure as a fantasy environment rather than as an excuse for another consulting report.

## What R2 found about prose and audio

R2 is strongest when written for the ear first.

Preserve:

- mostly Greg POV;
- clear speaker ownership;
- referents that resolve once by listening;
- physical objects repeated when pronouns would become ambiguous;
- short fragments when they represent actual cognition;
- pauses that create listening space;
- written and Listen surfaces remaining close enough to feel like the same work.

Do not preserve as automatic style:

- repeated `Good. Different. Useful.` evaluator labels;
- repeated `Not X. Y.` reversals;
- neat thesis codas after the scene already proved the meaning;
- every chapter ending with a Greg principle;
- prose tricks that exist because the engine knows they once worked.

## Authority lessons

R2 accumulated many numbered files from old runs, future simulations, high-fidelity audits, and publication experiments.

This repeatedly created a dangerous illusion:

> `file exists` = `story is selected`.

False.

3L needs a simpler authority contract from the beginning.

At minimum distinguish:

1. story truth;
2. selected scene/chapter authority;
3. rehearsal/quarry;
4. production surfaces.

Never infer frontier from filenames alone.

## Production lessons

The site/audio/image pipeline became powerful enough that production itself could become sunk-cost pressure on story.

Do not let this happen in 3L.

Story authority must be able to change without `but we already generated the audio` becoming a deciding argument.

Production should follow confidence.

Representative audio and art are enough to prove a pipeline.

Full completion is optional until the story deserves it.

## Top 10 R2 scenes to self-plagiarize

These are seeds, not continuity obligations.

1. Greg wakes young and investigates the physical absence of old damage instead of celebrating regression.
2. Blackglass / Pell: `Meat is bad bearing.` Greg: `Adaptable.`
3. Field Support Three trial: stronger pure specialists exist; Greg wins the slot because he covers the gaps between.
4. Gold-team professionalism: competent specialists ask fast questions, use authority correctly, and bench Greg when continuing would be stupid.
5. Faultglass theft / return: real social cost plus durable learned value; `Would steal again?`
6. Lantern Rows: dangerous setter cuff seriously priced, then Greg buys socks.
7. Greg learns another specialist's narrow technique can become much stronger if he changes the surrounding geometry, while consent still matters.
8. Mara chooses Northbank because she wants her own work and authority, even though Greg wants Carrow.
9. Sera effectively answers a growth question with `nothing, I want the money`, then trains Greg instead.
10. Storm Road: real safe option, informed cuff choice, successful crossing, Mara's `You keep borrowing from yourself`, and the final specialist contract held down by the legal-salvage snail.

Strong alternates:

- West Bramble legal snail salvage;
- Maelin living happily inside an old specialization trade;
- Merek's sponsored route contract genuinely improving his current options;
- Greg declining a good Red Quay contract because ordinary remaining days with Mara matter.

## Top 10 R2 mistakes for 3L to avoid

1. Letting the story become Fantasy OSHA for too many consecutive chapters.
2. Reusing `competence → complication → neat Greg lesson` as default chapter grammar.
3. Treating every NPC as a lesson delivery system.
4. Treating every interesting mechanism as a new arc obligation.
5. Seeding Greg's eventual specialty too late.
6. Letting old numbered files masquerade as selected authority.
7. Naming chapters for the scene-owning NPC instead of Greg's embodied role.
8. Making artifact danger repetitive instead of stage-specific.
9. Letting production cost protect story decisions that should die.
10. Mistaking sophistication for avoidance of recognizable fantasy fun.

## Final R2 state

### Greg

- Home base: Carrow.
- Relationship: close to Mara, geographically inconvenient, adult, unresolved in productive ways.
- Prosthetic: walking leg plus field peg; ordinary maintenance continues; Pell has improved the field-peg wet/grit sleeve.
- Combat: competent sword user, not the story's best duelist.
- Magic: moving-reference / last-travel / small failure-routing family is now reflexively fast after cuff use. Broad static families remain accessible but slower; early retraining shows some improvement. Strength is not erased once a broad shape forms.
- Equipment: no setter cuff retained; rental returned. No new cursed object possession.
- Reputation/work: increasingly priced as a moving-load / failure-routing specialist at higher rates.
- Money: materially improved after Storm Road.
- People pressure: successful Merek intervention remains evidence that he can sometimes alter trajectories; this should not be read as proof he can own them.
- Hubris state: reinforced, not resolved.

### Mara

- Northbank route-desk lead.
- Own staff, captains, ferry problems, routines, apartment, and local life.
- Relationship with Greg continues without becoming permission structure.
- Understands the cuff choice and sees the pattern of Greg spending future versions of himself.
- Refuses responsibility for deciding what Greg is allowed to spend.

### Final Storm Road crew

Temporary crew, not declared permanent party.

- Iria: convoy command.
- Evin: wind / charged-air specialist.
- Kelm: road / span hardware specialist.
- Pavo: teamster / animals / freight rhythm.
- Sera: close violence / animal panic.
- Maelin: setter / vane specialist who embodies a chosen narrow life.

They return to their own work after the contract.

## What R2 intentionally leaves unresolved

- Whether Greg can fully retrain lost breadth.
- Whether he will accept the new high-paying specialist contract.
- How far the `borrowing from yourself` logic eventually goes.
- Whether Merek's life meaningfully diverges from Greg's first-life memory.
- Whether Greg ever forms a durable recurring party or remains part of a rotating ecology.
- Where the long-range antagonist / institutional pressure should live.
- What the definitive geography / endpoint of Peg-Leg Greg should be.
- Whether Greg's final life ends in success, death, compromise, family, organization, solitude, or something stranger.

Those questions are not unfinished R2 chores.

They are available design space for 3L.

## Final conclusion

R2 should freeze because it has answered the question it needed to answer.

Greg can make ugly costs workable.

He can become narrower and more valuable at the same time.

He can lose options without losing function.

He can improve people sometimes without owning them.

He can build a life around adaptation.

The ending does not prove that life wrong.

It simply makes the shape visible.

That is enough.
