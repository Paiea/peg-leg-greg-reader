# R2 → 3L Handoff

Status: **ACTIVE / COMPLETE AFTER R2 FREEZE**

3L working name: **Third Leg**.

3L is the third major iteration of Peg-Leg Greg. It is allowed to rebuild the story rather than merely continue R2.

A current structural hypothesis is that 3L may define a strong far-end state first and work backward toward the beginning, using endpoint truth to shape earlier causality. That is a **3L experiment hypothesis**, not yet locked architecture.

This document becomes the transfer layer between iterations.

## KEEP

Things R2 proved work well enough that 3L should begin by preserving or deliberately challenging them rather than accidentally rediscovering them.

### Greg

- Greg's practical, funny, embodied POV.
- Old experience creates a high competence floor without making him best at every current specialty.
- Greg's strongest progression lane is reconstruction: he knows what systems are trying to accomplish and can rebuild around missing mechanisms.
- Greg increasingly specializes around movement, force, fixed points, failure paths, and creating conditions where another specialist's narrow strength becomes usable.
- His dangerous hubris is **adaptability**, not invincibility.
- Permanent consequence does not equal game over for him.
- Greg can genuinely succeed with ugly choices, which makes those choices more tempting later.
- Greg should get clean wins and enjoy being good at things.

### People

- Supporting characters need independent work, money, schedules, preferences, failures, and reasons to say no.
- Mara works best as an independent adult whose professional life continues whether Greg is present or not.
- Party formation is strongest when people repeatedly choose each other through work rather than when role slots are filled.
- Sharply specialized people are more interesting than balanced RPG archetypes.
- Greg can help people discover a sideways capability, but capability is not aspiration and he does not own their growth.
- Other people should also improve, correct, frustrate, and train Greg.

### Story texture

- Mundane fantasy is a core pleasure: food, money, contracts, gear, prosthetic maintenance, rooms, travel, waiting, repairs, shopping, bad meals, good meals, and ordinary professional texture.
- Mundane texture works best when mixed with real fantasy pleasure: fights, monsters, ruins, loot, artifacts, rivals, festivals, travel, strange magic, and dangerous places.
- Ordinary life after action matters.
- Time must leave residue in body, money, work, gear, relationships, routes, reputation, and access.

### Production / form

- Audio/listening-first prose.
- Mostly Greg POV.
- Speaker ownership must be clear by ear without relying on visual rereading.
- Written and Listen surfaces should remain closely related rather than becoming separate stories.
- Greg-role / embodied-role chapter naming is useful when applied from the correct point of view.
- Story Truth above prose remains valuable.
- Cheap temporal / future rehearsal before expensive canon prose is valuable.
- Rehearsal prose is quarry, not automatically canon.

## REBUILD

Things R2 discovered late, inherited awkwardly, or proved conceptually without integrating cleanly enough from the beginning.

- Greg's long-range plot architecture.
- Early magic progression so Greg's eventual specialization feels seeded rather than discovered halfway through the run.
- Artifact progression and why specific artifacts tempt Greg at specific stages.
- Supporting-cast architecture so recurring people emerge with stronger long-term trajectories earlier.
- Party / work-circle evolution so Greg's relationship to groups compounds across the whole story.
- Regression knowledge: define what Greg remembers reliably, what has changed, and where old-life knowledge becomes misleading.
- The relationship between Greg's support magic and his people-development instinct.
- The role of permanent tradeoffs in progression.
- Major setting geography and travel so places accumulate identity and history instead of functioning as sequential work locations.
- Long-range antagonist / institutional pressure, if 3L wants one.
- Early chapter pacing and chapter-length strategy around the final audio-first target.

## KILL

Things 3L should not preserve merely because R2 once used them.

- Repetitive `competence → complication → neat Greg principle` chapter grammar.
- Overuse of `Good. Different. Useful.` evaluator cadence.
- Overuse of `Not X. Y.` reversals as a prose crutch.
- Endless professional consulting jobs as the default plot engine.
- The assumption that every interesting system deserves its own arc.
- NPCs whose primary function is to demonstrate one lesson for Greg.
- Greg being conveniently necessary to every competent team.
- Generic balanced-party construction.
- Automatic punishment immediately after Greg makes a risky choice.
- Artifact plots that repeat Faultglass under a new name.
- Worldbuilding mechanisms explained after the scene already made them clear.
- Keeping continuity baggage solely because deleting/rebuilding it feels expensive.
- Production sunk-cost logic as a reason to preserve story decisions.

## SEEDS

Specific discoveries, moments, dynamics, and images worth carrying into 3L as raw material. These are not promises to reproduce exact scenes.

Initial seed set:

- Greg checking the physical reality of his younger body rather than immediately celebrating regression.
- The leg / prosthetic becoming ordinary equipment and ordinary maintenance rather than a permanent tragedy scene.
- Faultglass: Greg steals for knowledge, loses reputation/access/money, returns the object, and still keeps durable learned value.
- The dangerous conclusion that a bad deal can still leave him stronger.
- `Would steal again?` as an unresolved self-question.
- Greg being selected for teams because stronger pure specialists already exist and he covers the seams between them.
- Greg learning that his job is sometimes to make another person's narrow specialty usable rather than cover their weakness himself.
- A specialist telling Greg he used their capability without giving them enough information/consent.
- Greg seeing unused capability in someone, being right about ability, and wrong about whether they want that future.
- Someone answering Greg's growth question with effectively: **nothing, I want the money**.
- Another specialist training Greg instead.
- Greg's magic becoming smaller, stranger, and more precise as he becomes better.
- Greg deliberately choosing people for a job and discovering availability, rates, and independent lives destroy his neat composition.
- A temporary crew becoming excellent without being declared a permanent party.
- Mara choosing the professionally stronger path even when Greg dislikes the geography.
- Greg declining a good adventure because ordinary remaining days with Mara matter.
- Hearthline allowing bounded paid access because Greg is valuable while distrust remains intact.
- A future-known dangerous person appearing first as an ordinary young worker with current reasons and current talent.
- Greg successfully rerouting one small part of that person's life and thereby gaining evidence that intervention works.
- Magical flea-market / dead-inventory texture.
- Greg seriously considering dangerous tools and then buying socks.
- Legal salvage being exciting.
- A magical environment that behaves like old infrastructure rather than a videogame dungeon.
- Greg's broad competence becoming merely respectable while his narrow specialty becomes exceptional.

Add final-arc seeds after R2 ends.

## 3L endpoint-first hypothesis

Before drafting 3L Chapter 1, consider defining several far-end truths first:

- What kind of man is Greg near the end?
- What has his magic/build become?
- Which costs did he knowingly accept?
- Which relationships remain close, changed, distant, or broken?
- What does Mara's life look like independent of Greg?
- Does Greg have a recurring party, rotating ecology, organization, family, or something stranger?
- What does he own?
- What does he owe?
- What does the world think he is?
- What does Greg think he is?
- Which old-life prediction was most wrong?
- What choice would ending-state Greg make that Chapter 1 Greg would reject?

Then work backward in causal layers rather than outlining forward from Chapter 1.

Possible method:

1. write one or more endpoint scenes / state packets;
2. identify the necessary prior state for each endpoint fact;
3. move backward by major irreversible transitions;
4. only after the causal spine holds, render Chapter 1 forward for the listener;
5. continue using cheap forward rehearsal to test whether the backward architecture survives lived scenes.

Do not lock this method until R2 is frozen and its postmortem is complete.
