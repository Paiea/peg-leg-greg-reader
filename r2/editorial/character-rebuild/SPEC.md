# R2 / Greg, Again Character-First Rebuild Spec

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL BRANCH-ONLY REBUILD**

Branch: `experiment/r2-character-rebuild`

Public/current R2 on `main` remains historical evidence and must not be destructively rewritten by this experiment.

## Goal

Restart from an earlier R2 story state, preserve what is actually alive, and rebuild forward using lessons from later R2 and Falling Off a Cliff.

The presumptive rebuild frontier is **Chapter 26** after an exact continuous read of Chapters 1-26. Chapters 27+ are **QUARRY / REHEARSAL / EVIDENCE**, not chronological authority.

The frontier may move slightly earlier if the exact read shows material structural repetition before Chapter 27. It must not move later merely to preserve existing prose cheaply.

## Core diagnosis to solve

Later R2 preserved strong practical reality but overlearned a repeatable chapter grammar:

`problem -> competent professional -> Greg identifies boundary -> narrow useful action -> sensible outcome -> Greg recognizes lesson -> tidy final observation`

The rebuild must preserve bounded competence without turning Greg into a nearly perfect restraint machine.

Greg may be petty, jealous, embarrassed, needy, frightened for the wrong reason, horny, lonely, proud, unfair, socially mistaken, emotionally late, overly attached, avoidant, or tempted to manipulate someone toward a future version he remembers. These flaws must emerge from character, scarcity, desire, old-life assumptions, and present relationships rather than artificial incompetence.

## Craft rules

### Reference before invention

Before a consequential scene with an established person, retrieve their existing desires, fears, ambitions, embarrassment patterns, humor, relationship to Greg, money, status, bodily habits, professional pride, contradictions, previous choices, and things Greg misunderstands about them.

Continuity beats generic escalation.

### Specificity before abstraction

When a moment matters, ground it in rooms, clothes, food, tools, sounds, posture, pauses, proximity, bad timing, interrupted sentences, money, fatigue, weather, embarrassment, gestures, mess, and what someone does after the conversation supposedly ended.

### Important scenes discover something

A consequential scene must reveal something that another generic work scene or ordinary conversation could not reveal as well. Revelation may be social, emotional, relational, economic, bodily, or practical. It does not need to be dramatic.

### POV is an attention hierarchy

Ask what Greg notices first, what he avoids noticing, and what he gets wrong because he is Greg.

Old Greg's memory may create nostalgia, grief, false familiarity, premature trust, impatience, dread, inappropriate affection, mourning for things that still exist, relief nobody else understands, assumptions about younger versions of people, or temptation to manipulate a person toward the future version he remembers.

Future evidence versus present evidence remains useful, but it cannot be the only dramatic use of memory.

### Anonymous challenger

Strip names and obvious biography. If a scene could belong to another competent reincarnated fantasy protagonist, or if recurring characters collapse into generic archetypes, the scene is under-characterized. Do not fix this with quirks alone. Change wants, attention, misunderstandings, refusals, risks, or choices.

### Unresolved pressure is allowed

Arguments may remain unresolved. Good decisions may hurt someone. Greg may recognize the right response late. Someone may misunderstand him without receiving an explanatory correction. Do not add artificial hooks, but do not sterilize residue.

### Do not turn every chapter into a thesis

Avoid endings that explain what the scene meant. Endings may land on another person's line, leaving, cleanup, food, bills, unanswered questions, embarrassment, silence, an object out of place, laughter, bad sleep, or the next ordinary task.

### Competent people remain messy people

Preserve anger, bluntness, superstition, vanity, impatience, bad humor, grudges, class resentment, territoriality, pride, generosity, inconsistency, and irrational attachment. Do not turn every adult into a sensible systems designer.

### Greg may want things

Track conflicting desires such as friendship, sex, affection, admiration, respect, money, comfort, adventure, impressiveness, solitude, usefulness, protection, control, belonging, escape, novelty, status, teaching, refusing to teach, being seen as Old Greg, or being allowed to become someone else.

### Relationships need distinct languages

Recurring relationships must differ in jokes, avoided subjects, initiation, affection, embarrassment, status, easy and difficult favors, and incorrect assumptions. Relationship movement may drive chapter selection more strongly than work.

### Mundane must change something

Protect distinctive mundane texture. Challenge repeated delivery of mundane texture. Ordinary scenes earn themselves when they change capability, belonging, intimacy, expectation, money, leverage, body, reputation, home, relationship, responsibility, or self-understanding.

## Quarry rule for Chapters 27+

Later R2 may supply people, scenes, jokes, objects, professions, places, relationship discoveries, magic evidence, economic consequences, body progression, strong premises, dialogue, and world facts.

Chronology is not protected. Material may move earlier, merge, change owner, or die. Existing prose receives no sunk-cost protection.

## Minimal rebuild cycle

1. Read the current connected run.
2. Ask what should actually happen next.
3. Write 3-5 chapters.
4. Read them as a reader.
5. Run character, anonymous, and repetition challengers.
6. Change only what clearly loses.
7. Continue only after the small run proves it has more life.

No 50-chapter conveyor, giant temporal window, permanent speculative backlog, or new doctrine hierarchy.

## First pass deliverables

1. Exact continuous read of Chapters 1-26.
2. Candid diagnosis of Greg, Mara, Jorren, Noll, filtration group, Sella/Guild relationships, current body progression, money, magic, social range, emotional range, chapter grammar, most-alive material, and onset of procedural flattening.
3. Selective read of later R2 producing 10-20 things worth saving and 10-20 habits not to reimport.
4. First reconstructed Chapters 27-31 in branch-only editorial experiment storage. Historical public R2 27+ remains untouched.
5. Connected reader pass and quality test. Revise only clear losers before continuing.

## Quality test for reconstructed 27-31

Ask:

- Did Greg want anything besides performing correctly?
- Did another character surprise us for reasons grounded in who they are?
- Did at least one scene expose an emotional misunderstanding?
- Did anybody make an independent choice Greg disliked?
- Did ordinary life feel physical and specific?
- Did Old Greg's previous life affect him emotionally, not only informationally?
- Did the chapters use meaningfully different scene grammars?
- Did we avoid explaining each chapter's lesson?
- Is there unresolved residue?
- Could these scenes only belong to these people?
- Would a reader care what happens next because of people, not merely because another interesting system exists?

If the run fails materially, fix it before generating faster.

## North star

> **A man who remembers history better than he remembers life is forced to live closely enough that life starts replacing history as his primary source of truth.**

Take **live closely enough** literally: people, sex, friendship, resentment, food, embarrassment, home, work, desire, boredom, loyalty, bodies, money, grief, stupid jokes, ugly decisions, affection, consequences, wanting the wrong thing, being wrong about someone, and changing one's mind.

The book is not ultimately about whether Greg's future map remains accurate. It is about whether the present becomes important enough that he stops living primarily inside that map.

## Standing command

**CHARACTER BEFORE SYSTEM.**

**SPECIFICITY BEFORE THESIS.**

**DISCOVERY BEFORE COMPETENCE DISPLAY.**

**UNRESOLVED PRESSURE BEFORE TIDY CLOSURE.**

**CURRENT PEOPLE BEFORE FUTURE KNOWLEDGE.**

**LATER R2 IS QUARRY, NOT RAILROAD.**

**WRITE SMALL CONNECTED RUNS.**

**PRESUME NOTHING SURVIVES BECAUSE WE ALREADY WROTE IT.**

**KEEP WHAT IS ALIVE. REWRITE WHAT IS MERELY GOOD.**
