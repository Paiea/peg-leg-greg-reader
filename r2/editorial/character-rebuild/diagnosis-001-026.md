# R2 Character-First Rebuild — Chapters 1–26 Diagnosis

Status: **EXACT-READ DIAGNOSIS / EXPERIMENTAL BRANCH ONLY**

Authority read: `r2/assets/written/ch001.md` through `ch026.md` continuously from `main` commit `63cb90d9eadbaccf4a825cd981d336f42e1147b0`, the exact commit used to create `experiment/r2-character-rebuild`.

This diagnosis is not new canon. It records what the existing first 26 chapters already make alive and what the rebuild must stop overlearning.

## Frontier verdict

**Keep Chapter 26 as the practical rebuild frontier.**

The structural problem does begin before Chapter 27, but not in a way that justifies reopening the whole first run now.

The repeated grammar becomes visible around Chapters 7–11:

`real problem -> Greg reaches too broadly or too late -> current expert / reality corrects him -> narrow action works -> lesson is named`

At that stage the pattern is still useful because the domains, consequences, and relationships vary enough to keep it fresh.

The pattern becomes materially costly around Chapters 19–20. `Who Cleans It` and `Ward Hand` are strong practical fantasy chapters, but they demonstrate how easily the novel can spend a full chapter proving another professional system works, then let Greg summarize the lesson afterward.

Chapters 23–25 prove the accumulation problem more clearly. The West Cut run contains good weather, animal behavior, road knowledge, Edda/Harl/Pate texture, field identity, and a strong glasshorn watch. But three consecutive chapters spend a great deal of page time proving route-survey procedure. Individually good. Collectively too similar.

Chapter 26 is still the right restart point because it ends on a genuinely useful changed state: Greg has enough money, gear, bodily knowledge, neighborhood belonging, and professional access that he can afford not to know what tomorrow is. That creates room for character choice rather than forcing the next chapter to be another survival job.

**Therefore:** do not rewrite Chapters 1–26 as part of this experiment. Treat their procedural drift as a warning about what Chapter 27 must not automatically do.

## Greg

### What is alive

Greg is best when his intelligence creates human trouble rather than perfect judgment.

Early Greg is vain enough to test the nineteen-year-old body immediately, nearly falls into furniture, uses a coat as a cleaning rag, and worries about the quality of a sword while still trying to understand whether he has actually returned to life.

He is competitive. Jorren can get an extra spar out of him simply by being good enough to annoy him.

He is financially irrational in specific ways. He buys the entire Kestrin shale cart when a bucket would have answered the question, then gambles after successfully closing the Vale debt because he wants to understand the system. The important flaw is not ignorance. It is that curiosity, pride, and model-building can override a solved practical problem.

He likes being impressive more than he admits. He notices rank, quality, professional skill, and other people's competence almost automatically. Competent people attract and irritate him.

He wants his old magic back emotionally, not only strategically. The tiny support plane matters because it reminds him what using magic felt like when it was part of his body rather than a historical fact.

He wants roads and adventure. By Chapter 23 he chooses the West Cut partly because he wants the days outside Carrow. That desire is not reducible to money or training.

He wants connection while being frightened of the obligations connection creates. He avoids Mara's letter, then hates what the missing memory costs when she arrives. He wants a kiss before she leaves, receives a hug, and is disappointed in a way he does not successfully optimize away.

He wants to be a decent man, but he can turn decency into another performance metric. Mara identifies this precisely: he treats truth like a constraint, supplies evidence that he behaved correctly, and confuses honesty with kindness.

He is vulnerable to loss of centrality. Jorren leaves. Arlo and Ivena renegotiate without him. Dena drives a filtration cart while he is doing something else. These events matter because part of Greg still wants the life around him to validate that he is important.

### What he notices first

Greg's attention is pulled toward:

- failure modes
- tools
- prices
- rank and professional competence
- physical exits and geometry
- what a system will become later
- small evidence that a person is better or worse at something than expected
- things that resemble a later version of the world
- places where a useful intervention might exist

That attention hierarchy is character. Keep it.

### What he avoids noticing

Greg often delays noticing:

- that he wants reassurance
- that he wants somebody to choose him
- that a practical answer can still be emotionally cruel
- that another person's absence bothers him for reasons unrelated to logistics
- that he is trying to turn a relationship into a solvable information problem
- that young Greg had a life and desires that are not merely missing data
- that being needed feels good to him
- that he may be jealous of people continuing without him

The rebuild should let avoidance survive longer than one explanatory paragraph.

### What he gets wrong because he is Greg

He mistakes future maturity for current infrastructure.

He assumes technical understanding grants social permission more often than he realizes.

He can interpret restraint as moral correctness and therefore miss the human cost of being careful.

He is prone to treating people as evidence about systems, then noticing late that he has flattened them.

He trusts his ability to classify himself. Mara is already evidence that his self-diagnosis can be cleaner than his actual behavior.

He may believe that because he is not lying, manipulating, or overreaching professionally, he is therefore behaving well. That is false and dramatically useful.

## Mara

Mara is the strongest proof in Chapters 1–26 that the book becomes better when another person refuses Greg's frame.

She is not a memory-delivery device. She owns a Halden job, a blue cup, too many books, ledger habits, impatience with addresses, a mother Greg has history with, an opinion about the burned seven, and a relationship to Greg that existed before the reset.

Her language with Greg is specific:

- she can be funny without becoming his comedy partner
- she notices when he is performing correctness
- she does not accept honesty as automatic kindness
- she hates being made into an uninformed fool for his comfort
- she can withhold a familiar gesture without turning it into punishment theater
- she asks for ordinary contact rather than reports
- she does not promise more correspondence than her actual work allows

The key early relationship discovery is not merely that Greg forgot two kisses. It is that missing ordinary intimacy hurts more than missing historical information, and Mara is allowed to be frightened by the person who knows facts about her without remembering how they were together.

Mara's independent ambition must stay real. Halden and Vey are not obstacles placed between Greg and romance. They are her current life.

Greg's likely mistakes with her:

- treating explanation as repair
- thinking transparency entitles him to forgiveness
- wanting signs of the old relationship that she is not obligated to reproduce
- confusing his grief about missing memory with her responsibility to soothe it
- reading her work choices as statements about him
- being tempted to use later knowledge of Vey or her future self to interpret a younger Mara prematurely

## Jorren

Jorren works because friendship is physical, competitive, practical, and lightly insulting rather than therapeutic.

Their language includes:

- sparring
- `Recovery`
- bad stories
- road choices
- dry threats
- noticing when the other has improved
- helping without ceremonial gratitude
- allowing the other person to have work that does not include Greg

Jorren is already on an independent clock. He takes contracts, gets boots, leaves north, acquires road experience, and returns on his own timetable.

The most important thing not to do is reduce Jorren to `reliable male coworker who confirms Greg is improving`.

He should be capable of:

- wanting work Greg does not want
- liking people Greg does not know
- changing while away
- becoming better at things Greg did not teach him
- asking Greg for help because Greg specifically fits, not because Greg is protagonist
- declining Greg
- finding Greg annoying in ways unrelated to safety or competence
- enjoying Noll, Mara, or other people independently of Greg

Greg should sometimes feel petty about Jorren's independent life. That is better than another clean celebration of distributed agency.

## Noll

Noll is the clearest example of the rebuild's north star already succeeding.

Greg does not recover Noll through lore. He learns him through a fire.

Noll:

- wakes Greg before smoke becomes flame
- knows the building's flue, pump, stair rail, and walls
- nearly gets hit by plaster because he acts quickly
- loses his mattress
- sleeps on Greg's floor
- borrows soap
- snores
- argues about laundry

`Neighbor` lands because enough current facts become a person without historical recovery.

Their relationship language is domestic irritation, resource borrowing, survival, cheap food, noise through walls, and jokes that do not require emotional explanation.

Noll should not become Greg's household sidekick. His later quarry potential is strongest when he acquires work, teachers, schedules, management problems, and relationships Greg enters as a visitor.

A useful future pressure is that Greg may continue mentally storing Noll as `neighbor` after Noll has already become a friend. That lag would be character-specific and socially awkward without requiring melodrama.

## Filtration group: Arlo, Ivena, Dena

The filtration material works when ownership is genuinely distributed.

### Arlo

Arlo owns craft, firing, tolerances, workshop process, and the right to reject Greg's factory-brain shortcuts.

He is strongest when he is not the engineer waiting for Greg's systems insight. His refusal to become an employee and his practical correction of interchangeability fantasies establish a real professional identity.

### Ivena

Ivena owns customer reality, commercial bluntness, negotiation, and the right to say `I am not your test bucket.`

Her best scenes are not explanations of business design. They are moments where she asks a different question because she values a different thing. `What if you don't clean it?` works because she solves Tuesday instead of forty years.

She should be allowed to resent, overcharge, misjudge, protect margin, prefer a customer Greg dislikes, or make a commercial choice Greg thinks is ugly but defensible.

### Dena

By Chapter 26 Dena is still lightly sketched, which is useful. Greg knows her name now and sees her carrying clean and dirty chambers while the venture functions without him.

Do not promote her immediately into a fully optimized logistics operator because later R2 eventually does useful work with her. Let current Dena earn definition through choices and repeated contact.

### Group failure to avoid

Do not turn the filtration group into four sensible systems designers who politely distribute expertise. Their venture should generate:

- money arguments
- capacity annoyance
- breakage
- pride
- bad timing
- somebody wanting equipment the business does not need yet
- labor resentment
- customers who improve the product while being irritating
- workers whose questions embarrass owners
- independent decisions made without Greg

## Sella / Guild relationships

Sella is valuable because she does not become Greg's mentor, therapist, or exposition kiosk.

Their relationship language is administrative intimacy:

- she reads his spending habits
- she knows when a warning has been added specifically for him
- she can be funny in five words
- she gives him work without framing it as destiny
- she does not reward every realization

The Guild feels strongest when specialization creates friction naturally rather than as a lesson about specialization. Meren can maintain commercial wards but cannot conduct the support intake. Lera can teach sword work. Orra owns river procedure. Edda owns field leadership. Sella owns administrative routing.

The failure mode is making every Guild professional another extremely reasonable adult who demonstrates a clean domain boundary Greg then admires.

Keep specific professionals. Give them incompatible preferences, bad moods, territorial habits, grudges, pricing opinions, and social mistakes.

## Current body progression

The body progression through Chapter 26 is strong because it accumulates through use rather than levels.

Current state:

- nineteen-year-old recovery is faster than Old Greg expects
- body still collects blisters, shoulder fatigue, forearm strain, hip soreness, scrapes, and cramps
- sword handling has become familiar but not old-Greg fluent
- tiny support planes are possible and increasingly useful in bounded moments
- Greg can hold several useful breaths under modest load
- road work teaches him feet, pacing, socks, wet boots, bedrolls, watch fatigue, and equipment maintenance
- field work leaves gear and body needing repair even when nothing dramatic attacks him

Do not convert this into a clean progression ladder. Let competence arrive asymmetrically. Greg may get faster at one support action while remaining awkward in another. A good body day can coexist with a stupid social day.

## Money

Money is one of the strongest stabilizers in the early run.

Greg begins with almost nothing, borrows badly, gambles, pays too much for curiosity, buys a good sword, pays an assessment deposit, takes warehouse/ward/field work, repairs boots, buys socks, buys food, and slowly earns enough slack to take a day off.

Money currently means:

- permission
- temptation
- status
- embarrassment
- bodily maintenance
- professional access
- time
- the difference between adventure and recovery

Do not keep Greg poor artificially. The important thing is that money remains connected to choices. More money should create new human problems: generosity, vanity, status purchases, who pays, who owes, whether to save, whether a business should distribute cash, whether Greg uses money to make himself feel useful or impressive.

## Magic

Magic is strongest when it is practical, bodily, bounded, and emotionally loaded.

Greg's current support magic is small enough that five inches matters.

The strongest magical beats so far are not `later spec differs from current spec`. They are:

- the first pale plane reminding Greg what he wants back
- using a tiny deflection to change a falling object's angle
- discovering that the current body wants to brace when the construct should carry load
- seeing later-famous competence embodied by current working people
- realizing that a small intervention can matter without making him the owner of the whole problem

Keep mysterious magic mysterious longer when possible. The later dry-wall discovery is promising quarry precisely because Old Greg does not immediately know it.

## Social range

By Chapter 26 Greg's life includes multiple circles that should not collapse into one cast:

- Mara and her mother / old Carrow intimacy
- Jorren / adventuring friendship
- Noll / Lower Rook household life
- Arlo, Ivena, Dena / filtration venture
- Sella / Guild administration
- Lera / sword instruction potential
- Meren / commercial ward work
- Orra / Vey river labor
- Edda, Harl, Pate / road-survey field life
- temporary customers, foremen, workers, clerks, cooks, drivers, and fire watch

The next run should increase intersection selectively rather than adding another professional circle immediately.

A major unused source of life is **people from different circles meeting and forming opinions without Greg controlling the relationship**.

## Chapter grammar audit

### Most alive

The most alive chapters are the ones where procedure and relationship collide rather than procedure becoming the whole experience.

Strong anchors:

- Chapter 3: Greg is smart enough to make a more sophisticated financial mistake.
- Chapter 5: solving the debt does not stop him gambling because curiosity wins.
- Chapter 6: Ivena and Arlo own the customer/material reality and Greg's old magic returns as desire.
- Chapter 12: memory failure damages intimacy on-page.
- Chapter 14: Mara rejects Greg's frame and sets a boundary he cannot optimize away.
- Chapter 15: ordinary departure, disappointment, hug, apple, cards.
- Chapter 17: Orra's domain language and Greg's wrong-rope/right-cut review stay concrete.
- Chapter 21: Mara exposes Greg's report-writing version of honesty; cold feet and bad stew become relationship material.
- Chapter 22: Noll becomes a person through a fire, a mattress, soap, and snoring.
- Chapter 24: the glasshorn watch works through waiting, animal behavior, uncertainty, and Edda/Harl/Pate rather than escalation.
- Chapter 26: maintenance, rest, money, lunch, Dena's passing cart, and the ability to afford uncertainty.

### Good but dangerous in accumulation

- repeated field/professional chapters where Greg spots a pattern, is corrected by a domain owner, then successfully performs the bounded action
- recurring `present evidence beats later memory` structures when the emotional meaning does not change
- explanations of why another person having independent agency is good after the scene already showed it
- repeated final Greg observations that turn a concrete chapter into a principle
- repeated professional adults who are witty, correct, and reasonable in the same register
- work chapters whose only lasting change is another small competence increment

## Immediate constraints for reconstructed Chapter 27

The next chapter should **not** be another paid lesson, ward-hand shift, road survey, or competence test simply because those are available.

At Chapter 26 Greg has enough stability that a person can interrupt the machinery.

The most promising live pressures are:

- Mara's correspondence remains active and emotionally unfinished.
- Jorren is away and can return changed by life Greg did not witness.
- Noll is now a current relationship, not just old-history residue.
- The filtration venture can function without Greg and may make choices he does not own.
- Kesra's assessment remains weeks away and therefore should not become the immediate plot magnet.
- Greg wants roads/adventure but is also beginning to possess a home worth returning to.
- Young Greg's erased ambitions and relationships are still capable of surprising Old Greg.

The first reconstructed run should exploit those pressures before opening another professional subsystem.

## Working rebuild proposition

The first five chapters should test a more human engine:

**Greg is beginning to build a present life, but people from young Greg's life and people from current Greg's life are starting to overlap. Greg wants continuity, admiration, freedom, and belonging at the same time. He is not emotionally skilled enough to get all four cleanly.**

That is a better immediate story question than `what useful job appears on the Guild board next?`
