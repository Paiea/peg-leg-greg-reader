# Chapter 29: The Seven

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Jorren left my floor before I woke and came back for dinner.

I found the first part rude and the second suspicious.

His blanket was folded beside the door.

Actually folded.

Noll's blanket.

I stared at it long enough to become aware I was judging another man's floor etiquette.

Then I went downstairs.

Jorren had spent the morning at the Guild closing the north contract and apparently most of the afternoon discovering that everyone he knew in Carrow had either moved, taken work, or become unavailable in ways designed specifically to inconvenience him.

He returned to Lower Rook because Noll had promised food.

Not because I had.

This was becoming a pattern after approximately one day.

"You invited him?" I asked.

Noll stood over the cookroom pan with the wooden spoon.

"Yes."

"Why?"

Noll looked at me.

"He eats."

Unassailable.

Jorren sat at the long table cleaning road grit from one boot with the point of a nail.

"You can leave," he said.

"I live here."

"Then stop complaining about dinner."

I sat.

Apparently current relationships had formed a coalition.

The meal was potatoes, cabbage, three pieces of sausage, and enough onion to establish that yesterday's purchase had not been wasted.

Noll served it in two bowls.

Then looked at the table.

Three people.

Two bowls.

He looked at me.

I looked at him.

"You live here," he said.

"So do you."

"My bowl is upstairs."

"Convenient."

Jorren picked up the pan.

"Solved."

Noll looked offended.

"That's the pan."

"Still round."

We ate.

Jorren from the pan.

Me from one bowl.

Noll from the other while watching Jorren scrape the bottom with a piece of bread and reconsidering the entire concept of hospitality.

The food was good.

Not professionally good.

Better.

No one had charged me for the chair.

After dinner we went upstairs because the cookroom filled with people whose relationship to cards looked expensive.

My room remained one chair short of civilization.

Noll took the chair anyway.

Jorren sat on the floor with his back against the wall.

I sat on the bed.

The deck was already on the table.

That had not been intentional.

Mostly.

Jorren pointed.

"Cards?"

"Old cards."

"You have new cards?"

"No."

"Then cards."

He reached.

I reached first.

Not aggressively.

Enough.

His hand stopped.

Mine had the deck.

For half a second neither of us said anything.

Then I felt stupid.

Good start.

I handed it over.

"Careful. Historical artifact."

Jorren opened the box.

The burned seven sat on top.

Of course.

Noll leaned forward.

"Debt card."

Jorren looked at him.

Then at me.

"What?"

I should not have told Noll anything before breakfast.

"Tavin says I owe him nothing. He owes me two copper."

Noll frowned.

"You said debt."

"His debt."

"You put your own money on it."

"Symbolically."

Jorren stared at me.

"You became strange."

"I woke up strange."

"Fair."

He picked up the seven.

One corner blackened.

The number still readable.

The little painted cups had survived except the last one, whose edge disappeared into the burn.

"Who burned it?"

"Me. Apparently."

"Why?"

"Losing."

Noll nodded.

"That sounds right."

"You did not know me then."

"Still sounds right."

Jorren smiled.

I did not.

Not yet.

I explained Tavin's version.

He won the hand.

I claimed the card was defective.

I burned it after losing.

Mara believed Tavin.

Nobody remembered the exact rule anymore.

Jorren turned the card over.

"Easy."

"What?"

"Burned seven takes the trick."

"That cannot have been the rule."

"Why not?"

"Because it rewards destruction."

"So does adventuring."

Noll held out his hand.

Jorren passed him the card.

Noll inspected it like he knew cards better from this angle.

"Red seven burns."

"There is one burned seven."

"Then easy to remember."

"You cannot both invent rules to an existing game."

Jorren looked at me.

"Can you prove the old one?"

There.

Small line.

Joke shape.

He meant the card.

My body did not care.

Something tightened before I had time to choose a better reaction.

"No."

Jorren smiled.

"Then excellent time to improve it."

"That doesn't mean everything I don't remember belongs to whoever speaks first."

The room changed.

Not dramatically.

Noll stopped turning the card between his fingers.

Jorren's smile disappeared.

My own voice was still hanging there, sharper than the sentence required.

I wanted it back.

No professional procedure available.

Jorren looked at the deck.

Then at me.

"I was talking about cards."

"I know."

That made it worse.

Noll put the seven on the table.

Flat.

"Red seven burns," he said.

I looked at him.

He shrugged.

"We need a rule."

The game continued.

Nobody apologized.

I dealt.

Jorren won the first hand under a rule he had not invented because Noll had stolen it first.

Noll won the second by claiming the burned seven could be played on any red card.

"You just added that."

"Existing law."

"Since when?"

"Now."

Jorren supported him.

Corruption had become organized.

I lost three copper.

This improved my mood enough to make me suspicious of myself.

By the fourth hand, the room sounded normal again.

Mostly.

Jorren had a new tell.

Before he left, his mouth had betrayed good cards. One corner tightened when he tried not to smile.

Now his face did nothing.

His left foot did.

It stopped moving.

He had been tapping his heel against the floor since the first deal.

Then, on a large bet, stillness.

I looked at his face.

Nothing.

Foot.

Nothing more.

I folded.

Jorren frowned.

Noll looked between us.

"What?"

"Nothing," Jorren said.

"He changed his tell."

Jorren looked at me.

"Damn."

There.

I smiled before I could decide whether the moment deserved protection.

"Road made you better."

"You made me change it."

"Same result."

"No."

He said it lightly.

I heard it anyway.

The road had made him change because I was not there.

Someone else had noticed.

Or nobody had.

Maybe he had simply become tired of losing money.

His life did not need my causal ownership.

I almost said something about that.

Mara would have thrown me into the river from three cities away.

I dealt the next hand.

Noll cheated badly.

I respected the effort.

The game ended when Noll had to go help downstairs with the evening pots.

He stood and collected both bowls.

Then looked at Jorren.

"You here tomorrow?"

Jorren hesitated.

Small.

Enough that I noticed.

"Morning."

"Eat before you go."

"If Greg buys onions."

They both looked at me.

"I regret introducing you."

Noll picked up the pan from beside the door.

"You didn't."

Then he left.

The door stayed open behind him.

I stared at it.

Jorren gathered the cards.

"He always like that?"

"Yes."

Too quick.

"Mostly."

Jorren put the deck back in the box.

The burned seven remained on the table.

"You want to tell me what that was?"

I knew what he meant.

"No."

He nodded.

No offense.

That should have made it easier.

It did not.

He stood.

"Guild found me a bed."

"Good."

He picked up Noll's folded blanket from beside the door.

"I'll give this back."

"He knows where I live."

"Apparently."

Jorren reached the hallway.

I heard myself say, "What are you doing tomorrow?"

He turned.

"Closing two things. Buying a strap. Seeing if anyone still owes me money."

"Busy."

"You?"

I looked around the room.

Tavin's unfinished reply under the two copper.

Mara's letter.

Field pack.

Good sword.

A business that had been moving without me.

"I'll show you something."

Jorren's eyebrows moved.

"Dangerous sentence."

"Mill Street. Afternoon."

"Why?"

There were several correct answers.

Filter venture.

Arlo.

Ivena.

Work.

Instead I heard myself say the one underneath them.

"I want you to see it."

Jorren looked at me for a second longer than necessary.

Then nodded.

"All right."

He went downstairs.

I stood in the doorway until I heard him and Noll arguing about blanket ownership one floor below.

Then I went back inside.

The burned seven sat by itself on the table.

I picked it up.

Turned it once between my fingers.

No memory.

Still mine enough to be annoying.

I put it back in the deck without deciding what that meant...
