# Chapter 32: Two Copper

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Noll asked me for two copper before breakfast.

Exactly two.

This made the request suspicious immediately.

He knocked once, opened the door before I answered, and stood there wearing yesterday's shirt with flour on one sleeve.

"Two copper."

No greeting.

No soap.

Progress, perhaps.

I looked at him from the bed.

"Good morning."

"Morning. Two copper."

"For?"

"Pork."

I waited.

Noll waited back.

There were people who became uncomfortable in silence.

Noll had apparently been raised correctly.

"You woke me up to finance pork."

"And vinegar."

"Ah. Diversified portfolio."

"Two copper."

I sat up.

The room was colder than yesterday.

Jorren's absence remained visible mostly because the floor had stopped containing another man.

Noll's folded blanket was gone too.

Returned to its legal owner.

The field pack sat beside the door.

Mara's envelope lay under the brass key.

Tavin's unfinished reply sat beneath the burned seven.

Two copper still rested on top of it.

I looked at them.

Then at Noll.

"Those?"

"Copper usually works."

"They're disputed."

"With who?"

"Tavin."

Noll considered the name.

"Letter?"

"Yes."

"He here?"

"No."

"Then copper works."

Disturbingly strong legal reasoning.

I picked up the coins.

They had spent several days pretending to be debt evidence.

Now they were cold against my fingers and worth exactly two copper again.

I held them out.

Noll took them.

Then stayed.

That was new.

"Anything else?"

"Need the knife."

"Which knife?"

He pointed at the small kitchen knife on my table.

I had bought it because cutting cheese with a field knife made every meal feel like a threat.

"Why?"

"Mine pulls."

"Sharpen it."

"I did."

"Then why does it pull?"

"Bent."

"How?"

"Bone."

I stared.

"You are conducting a full kitchen replacement through my room."

"Borrowing a knife."

"And money."

"Money doesn't come back the same."

"Finally, an economist."

Noll took the knife too.

He turned to leave.

"Noll."

He looked back.

"Why pork?"

His face did something small.

Not embarrassment exactly.

Closer to annoyance that the question had finally arrived.

"Brass Spoon."

I knew the place.

East Market.

Ten tables if later memory had not improved the furniture.

Prices high enough that people occasionally mistook linen for seasoning.

"What about it?"

"Man ate dumplings here."

"We have many men."

"From Brass Spoon."

"Still many."

"Cook."

Better category.

Noll leaned one shoulder against the frame.

Three nights ago, while I had been busy rediscovering Tavin and waiting for Jorren to stop being absent, Lower Rook had fed a group of carters in the cookroom.

One of them had brought a cousin.

The cousin worked evenings at The Brass Spoon.

He ate six of Noll's dumplings.

This was either praise or theft depending on whether he paid.

He had paid.

Praise, then.

"He said bring some today," Noll said.

"To the restaurant?"

"Kitchen."

"For who?"

"Vessa."

The name meant nothing current.

Later memory tried to offer me a woman with gray hair and a scarred spoon hand.

Wrong age.

Maybe wrong person.

I left it alone.

"And if she likes them?"

Noll shrugged.

"Maybe shift."

There.

An entire possible change in his life delivered with the same emotional ceremony as borrowing soap.

"What kind of shift?"

"Kitchen."

"Pay?"

"Don't know."

"Hours?"

"Don't know."

"What are you actually being offered?"

"Bring dumplings."

I opened my mouth again.

Noll pointed the borrowed knife at me.

Not threateningly.

Enough.

"Two copper."

Right.

Current stage of negotiations.

"Fine."

He left.

I stayed on the bed for several seconds.

Then got dressed faster than I had intended.

Not because the dumplings required supervision.

They absolutely did not.

Because Lower Rook's cookroom was downstairs and breakfast was also downstairs.

Coincidence remained available as a social technology.

The cookroom smelled like onion before I reached the last stair.

Noll had already taken over one end of the long table.

Pork in a paper wrap.

Small bottle of dark vinegar.

Cabbage.

Onion.

Flour.

A bowl of dough under a damp cloth.

The two copper had become ingredients so quickly that Tavin's debt felt financially irresponsible.

Noll looked up.

"Breakfast is there."

He pointed with his elbow toward a pot near the stove.

"You cooking for me?"

"No."

Comforting.

I found porridge.

Someone had added raisins in a quantity best described as theoretical.

I took a bowl and sat at the far end of Noll's table.

He ignored me.

This lasted almost a minute.

Then he pushed the vinegar bottle toward the light, frowned at the color, and poured a little into the filling.

"Too much," I said.

His hand stopped.

I regretted it immediately.

Noll looked at me.

"You make dumplings?"

"I eat them."

"Good job."

He poured the rest of what he had intended.

I ate porridge.

Silence restored jurisdiction.

His hands moved faster than I expected.

Dough pinch.

Roll.

Filling.

Fold.

Thumb press.

Set aside.

Not beautiful.

Consistent enough.

The fourth split along one edge.

Noll swore, ate it raw enough to concern me, then spat the dough back into his hand when he remembered the pork.

"Excellent kitchen standards."

"Shut up."

He threw the ruined wrapper into a scrap bowl.

The next one held.

I watched his fingers.

There were small burn marks near two knuckles.

One old cut along the side of his thumb.

I had seen those hands carrying buckets, stealing soap, holding a poker against a burning wall.

I had not watched them make anything more complicated than onions before.

Apparently I had not been paying attention to everything available through one wall.

Noll caught me looking.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"That's usually when you're thinking something annoying."

"Professional growth."

"See?"

Fair.

I finished the porridge.

Noll boiled one dumpling as a test.

It rose badly.

Not wrong.

Crooked.

He fished it out, put it in a chipped bowl, and cut it in half.

Steam escaped.

Pork, cabbage, onion.

Sharper smell from the vinegar.

He ate one half.

His face stayed neutral.

Then he pushed the other half across the table.

"Eat."

"Is this repayment?"

"No."

"Then I remain concerned about the debt."

"Eat."

I ate.

The wrapper was thicker on one side.

Filling good.

More vinegar than I would have chosen.

That did not mean too much.

It meant not mine.

"Well?"

I chewed.

There were several possible answers.

Salt.

Wrapper thickness.

Pork ratio.

Heat.

Ivena had spent enough time teaching me that useful criticism depended on what problem someone was actually asking about.

Mara had spent enough postage teaching me that sometimes the problem was asking whether criticism had been requested at all.

Noll was staring at me.

"Good," I said.

He frowned.

"That's it?"

I almost laughed.

"Very good."

"Better."

He took the bowl back.

Apparently emotional nuance remained measurable in dumplings.

I stood.

"Need anything else?"

Noll looked at the ingredients.

Then at me.

"Hold this."

He handed me the damp cloth.

I stood there holding a square of wet fabric while he moved the finished dumplings onto a shallow tray.

This was not the form of support work I had once imagined recovering.

Current scope remained humbling.

He covered the tray with the cloth.

Took my knife.

Stopped.

Looked at it.

Then handed it back.

"Done."

"You can keep it for the day."

"Why?"

"In case Vessa asks you to cut something."

Noll looked at me like I had suggested bringing a sword to a bath.

"They have knives."

Right.

Actual kitchen.

"Fine."

He wiped his hands.

Then reached for the tray.

I reached for my purse.

"Take another three copper."

Noll stopped.

"Why?"

"Food. Tram. Whatever East Market invents."

"I have money."

"You asked me for two."

"For pork."

"Which is normally purchased with money."

"I had money for me."

There it was.

Separate stack.

Not poverty.

Choice.

I left the purse closed.

Noll picked up the tray.

"If they keep me, I pay you back."

"You don't need to wait."

"I know."

"Then why say it?"

"Because I borrowed it."

He walked out before I improved the arrangement.

I stood in the cookroom doorway with the irritating awareness that being useful had ended two copper earlier than I wanted.

The morning continued anyway.

I went to the Guild.

Not for work.

Mostly.

I checked the board.

That counted as professional curiosity, not employment.

Two warehouse counts.

One river unload.

A night gate watch that paid badly enough to suggest the gate had offended someone.

No road I wanted.

Good.

No.

The word still had habits.

I left.

At Mill Street, Arlo was not there.

Ivena was not there either.

The workshop was locked.

Dena's cart tracks crossed the wet edge of the street and disappeared east.

Apparently nobody had arranged my day for me.

I bought bread.

Walked home.

By afternoon the building felt louder than usual because I was listening for one particular door.

That was embarrassing.

I tried reading.

The page won.

I tried cleaning the sword.

It did not need cleaning.

I tried writing Tavin.

The half-letter still said what it had said yesterday.

The two copper were gone from the burned seven.

That changed the table more than expected.

The card looked less like evidence now.

More like a card somebody had damaged because he was losing.

I did not enjoy the downgrade.

I put the unfinished letter in my coat pocket.

No reason.

Then took it out again.

Also no reason.

By fifth bell I had learned exactly how often Noll normally made noise through the wall.

A lot.

Enough that absence had texture.

No dropped pan.

No chair scrape.

No muttered argument with clothing.

No door opening because he wanted soap he already owned half of.

The room next door remained a room.

I disliked it.

This was excessive after several hours.

I went downstairs for dinner.

The cookroom had somebody else at the stove.

Older man.

Bad knees.

Good soup.

He charged me one copper and did not ask why I looked at the doorway every time footsteps crossed the courtyard.

Professionalism.

Noll arrived after dark.

Not dramatically.

He opened the rear door with his hip because both hands were occupied.

One carried the empty dumpling tray.

The other carried a paper packet.

I was at the long table.

I tried not to sit straighter.

Failed slightly.

Noll saw me.

"You still here?"

"I live here."

"Bad planning."

He put the tray down.

The paper packet followed.

"Well?"

I asked too quickly.

Noll looked at me.

That was enough punishment.

"Well what?"

"Did she eat them?"

"Yes."

"And?"

"Said wrapper's too thick."

My mouth opened.

Noll held up one finger.

"Don't."

I closed it.

"Said filling's good."

There.

"And?"

"Tomorrow."

"What tomorrow?"

"Shift."

I smiled.

Could not help it.

Noll saw.

His own mouth moved a little too.

Then he ruined the moment by opening the paper packet.

Inside was one dumpling.

Different fold.

Tighter.

More pleats.

"What's that?"

"Vessa made it."

"For you?"

"Told me mine opens when the pot crowds."

He picked it up.

Turned it once.

"She does this."

His fingers copied the fold in the air.

Fast pinch, turn, pinch.

Not mastered.

Already stored.

"You start tomorrow?"

"Trial. Four evenings."

"Pay?"

Noll told me.

Better than the random cookroom suppers when Lower Rook was busy.

Not enough to become impressive.

Two meals included.

That mattered.

"Hours?"

He told me those too.

Long.

Late.

The hallway would be quiet more often.

I noticed that before I noticed anything financially useful.

Interesting failure of priorities.

"You taking it?"

Noll looked confused.

"I went."

"The four nights."

"Yes."

Of course.

He picked up Vessa's dumpling and bit it in half.

Then remembered me.

He held out the remaining half.

I stared.

"You saved one dumpling and ate half before offering it."

"You had porridge."

"This morning."

"Still counts."

I took it.

The wrapper was thinner.

Fold held tighter.

Filling simpler.

Less vinegar.

I chewed.

Noll watched.

"Well?"

"Good."

He narrowed his eyes.

"Very good."

"Better."

We finished the dumpling between us.

Then Noll picked up the empty tray.

"Two copper tomorrow."

"Keep it."

"Borrowed."

"Buy pork again."

"They have pork."

Actual kitchen.

Right.

"Then buy soap."

Noll stared at me.

"I have soap."

"You have my soap."

"Shared custody."

Stable law.

He went upstairs.

A minute later his door opened.

Chair scraped.

Something hit the floor.

"Fuck."

There.

Home restored.

For tonight...
