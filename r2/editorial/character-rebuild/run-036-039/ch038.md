# Chapter 38: Two Keys

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Mara remembered the third stair squeaked.

She was wrong.

It was the fourth now.

We had reached the second landing when she stepped on the third board carefully, waited for the sound, and got nothing.

Then the fourth complained under my boot.

Mara stopped.

Looked down.

"They moved it."

"Fire."

"Still rude."

I smiled.

There were things Mara forgot too.

Not important things.

A stair.

Maybe she had remembered correctly and the building had changed.

Either answer improved my mood.

The rear rail wobbled when she put a hand on it.

"This is worse."

"Less flammable."

"Doesn't feel related."

"Modern safety is subtle."

She pushed the rail once.

It moved again.

"Your landlord should be arrested."

"Maud would collect rent from the cell."

That seemed likely enough to end the argument.

By the third floor, the hallway had gone quiet.

Noll's door was shut.

He had Brass Spoon tonight.

Four nights a week now.

The fact had existed for less than a day and already his absence felt scheduled.

Mara pointed at his frame.

"Hooks gone."

"What hooks?"

"Fish hooks."

I looked closer.

Three tiny holes near the hinge.

"Why did he keep fish hooks in the door?"

"Tavin stole them downstairs."

"Did Tavin fish?"

"No."

Of course.

I unlocked my room.

Two keys clicked together in my hand.

Mara heard it.

"Wait."

I knew which one before she touched it.

Small brass key.

Older than the room lock.

Her mother's back room.

I had put both on the same ring after spending several weeks finding one key in whichever pocket held the other.

Mara lifted the old one with her fingertip.

"You still have this."

"Your mother threatened me into it."

"She told you to keep it."

"With a spoon nearby. Context matters."

Mara turned the key once against the ring.

Then let it fall.

"I forgot."

I looked at her.

She noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Greg."

"You forgot something about me."

Mara stared for one second.

Then understood why I looked pleased.

"Don't enjoy that too much."

"Already did."

"Idiot."

Good.

I opened the door.

The room remained one room.

One bed.

One chair.

Table under the window.

Good sword on two wall hooks.

Washbasin.

Coat I had failed to hang.

Field pack beside the door with two pairs of socks visible at the top.

Mara's letters stacked beneath the current room key.

Burned seven beside them.

A bowl on the windowsill because domestic excellence had limits.

Mara stepped in and stopped.

I had wanted this.

Not merely Mara inside.

Her looking.

Seeing what had happened while she was in Halden.

That was embarrassingly close to the reason I had dragged Jorren to Mill Street.

At least I recognized the disease now.

Mara looked at the chair.

Then the bed.

"Still one."

"High-quality chair."

She pressed the back with one hand.

It creaked.

"Debatable."

"Bed is stronger."

Wrong argument.

Her eyebrows rose.

I became interested in the window.

Mara sat on the bed.

Not delicately.

Travel bag beside her feet.

The blue cup knocked against something inside.

I sat in the chair because surrendering immediately felt unwise.

She looked around slowly.

The sword got her first.

"That's the expensive one."

"Yes."

"How expensive?"

"Enough that I would prefer not to repeat the number near my purse."

Mara stood and crossed to it.

Her fingers touched the sheath, not the grip.

Darkened leather near the lower third where West Cut rain had dried badly.

"Used?"

"Not much."

"Good."

"It cut a laundry line during the fire."

Mara turned.

"That's what Noll meant."

I stared.

"He told you?"

"Letter from Mother. Noll told her."

There were too many packet networks.

"One shirt was on fire."

"Mother said three fell into mud."

"Different measurement."

"You bought a sword for monsters and killed laundry."

"The building survived."

"Hero."

She was laughing when she reached the table.

The burned seven ended it.

Not the laugh.

Changed it.

Mara picked up the card.

Black corner.

Red cups.

Young stupidity surviving better than memory.

"He really wrote you about this?" she asked.

"Tavin?"

"Yes."

"Two copper demanded with interest measured emotionally."

"He wrote me about it too."

"Of course he did."

"Last winter."

Mara turned the card.

"You were losing."

"Unproven."

"I was there."

I looked at her.

She tapped the table with the card.

"Back room. You said the seven had a printing flaw."

"Look at it. Clearly defective."

"Then you burned it."

"Allegedly."

"Greg."

"Fine. Probably."

She pointed the burned card at me.

"Banned."

I laughed.

Mara put the seven down on top of her own letter.

Not reverently.

That helped.

"Did you pay him?"

"No."

"You had two copper on it in your last letter."

I looked at her.

"I wrote that?"

"You wrote around it. I inferred."

Dangerous woman.

"Noll borrowed them."

Mara looked toward the wall.

"For soap?"

"Pork."

"That is worse."

"Dumplings."

"Better."

"He paid me back last night."

"So Tavin's money has returned."

"My money."

"Tavin's."

"Conspiracy."

Mara smiled and put the card back.

Then saw the field pack.

The socks betrayed me immediately.

Two folded pairs sitting in an otherwise mostly empty top compartment.

She crouched.

"Why are your socks packed?"

"Because they are independent thinkers."

Mara put one hand inside the bag.

Empty beneath them.

Her face changed.

"You packed for Doss."

Not question.

I leaned back in the chair.

There was no version of this that improved through cleverness.

"For about five minutes."

"Bedroll?"

"In. Then out."

"Food?"

"Never got that far."

She lifted one sock.

"And these survived."

"I lost momentum."

Mara put it back.

Not folded as neatly.

"You really wanted to go."

"Yes."

Dinner had already established that.

The bag made it less theoretical.

She stayed crouched for another second.

Then sat back on the bed.

"I would've been angry."

I looked at her.

She looked at the pack instead.

"If you went."

"You just spent dinner telling me not to make you responsible for me staying."

"I know."

"And you would have been angry."

"Yes."

There was no attempt to fix the contradiction.

Mara tucked one loose strand of hair behind her ear.

It fell back out.

"I would have understood," she said.

"That sounds convenient."

"I can understand something and still hate it."

"Poor design."

"Very."

I liked that answer more than I should have.

"I would've been angry too."

Mara looked at me now.

"If I went?"

"You did go."

"Greg."

Right.

"If you didn't come. After the letter."

"At me?"

I thought about the empty Vey desk yesterday.

The clerk saying no.

The road existing in the opposite direction.

"Eventually."

Mara almost smiled.

"Bad answer."

"At Vey first. Corren's axle. Weather. Maybe Jorren for leaving on schedule."

"Reasonable targets."

"Then you."

Her smile disappeared.

Not hurt.

Attention.

"Even if it wasn't my fault?"

"Yes."

"Good."

I stared.

"Excuse me?"

"Not good. Better than pretending you'd be noble about it."

"I can be very noble."

"You burned a playing card because you lost."

"Allegedly."

"Witness."

We looked at each other.

Then both laughed.

The room changed with it.

Less interview.

Still not easy.

Mara took off her coat.

Just because the room was warmer than the street.

Probably.

No.

I refused to use the word in my own head now.

She folded the coat beside her on the bed.

Gray blouse underneath.

Sleeves rolled once.

Ink at one cuff.

A pale red line at her wrist from the travel bag strap.

I noticed too much all at once.

Mara caught me.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Liar."

"You are in my room."

"I noticed."

"On my bed."

She looked down.

"Very observant."

"Professional training."

"Is that why you're still in the chair?"

No good answer.

I stood.

Not toward her.

Toward the kettle I did not own.

"Tea?"

Mara looked around.

"Where?"

"Downstairs."

"Sit down."

I stopped.

She pointed beside her.

Bed.

The chair had lost the vote.

I sat.

Enough space between us to pretend this was neutral.

Not enough to believe it.

Our knees touched when Mara turned slightly.

Neither moved.

I looked at her mouth.

Subtlety had left.

Mara saw.

"Still counting exits?" she asked.

"Door. Window. Rear stair if the hallway burns."

"Good."

"Why?"

"Wanted to know if some things survived."

I almost answered with something about how much had survived.

Instead I said, "You still bite the inside of your cheek when you're deciding whether to say something rude."

Mara stopped biting it.

"I do not."

"Current evidence."

Her eyes narrowed.

"That one was rude."

"You were deciding."

She moved first.

Not dramatically.

One hand caught the front of my shirt and pulled me the remaining distance.

Her mouth was warm from dinner.

Mine knew what to do before my mind did.

For one breath that was fine.

Then my right hand touched her shoulder and another room flashed through the movement.

Not Mara's.

Older body.

Different woman.

A bed with a carved headboard somewhere south of a city whose name did not arrive.

Gone immediately.

My hand stopped.

Mara felt it.

She pulled back enough to look at me.

"What?"

I could have hidden it.

Small pause.

Easy lie.

"Old reflex."

Her hand loosened on my shirt.

"From me?"

"No."

Specific answer.

Her face stayed still for a second.

I waited.

Mara touched the side of my jaw.

"All right."

That was all.

No demand for the other woman's name.

I did not have it anyway.

She leaned in again.

This kiss belonged to a room with one chair, two keys on a ring, a half-packed field bag, and Noll absent through the wall.

I let that be enough.

My hand went to her waist this time.

No interruption.

Mara's fingers slid behind my neck.

The next several minutes became difficult to count and therefore improved immediately.

At some point I moved the burned seven because Mara's elbow nearly crushed it.

"Historical artifact," I said.

"Bad card."

"Finally."

"Bad because you burned it."

"Propaganda."

She kissed me before the argument could develop.

Excellent censorship.

Later, both of us were sitting farther back on the bed.

My shoulder against the wall.

Mara's coat near our feet.

Her hand rested on my thigh as casually as if my entire body had not reorganized around the fact.

The room bell downstairs struck late.

Once.

Mara closed her eyes.

"Work."

I hated the word immediately.

"Emmet said second bell."

"I still need sleep."

"Overrated."

"You told me that before."

"Did I?"

"When we were sixteen. Then you slept through half a market day."

"Young Greg was unreliable."

"Current Greg checked a packet office twice."

"Once today."

"Twice yesterday."

Everyone kept records.

Mara did not stand.

That made it worse.

I looked at her coat.

Then the bed.

Then her.

She followed the sequence.

"Don't ask if you're going to be angry at the answer."

"I am capable of multiple emotions."

"Demonstrated."

Fine.

"Do you want to stay?"

Mara looked at me for a long moment.

"Yes."

Every useful thought disappeared.

Then she picked up her coat.

"I'm not staying."

They returned.

Poorly.

"Work."

"Work."

I heard my own voice become reasonable before I chose it.

"Makes sense."

Mara stopped with one arm in the coat.

"Don't."

"Don't what?"

"That."

She put the other arm through.

"You look disappointed, then you say something like you're approving my application."

I opened my mouth.

Closed it.

Caught accurately.

"I am disappointed."

"So am I."

That helped.

I hated that it helped because it felt like getting paid for sadness.

"Good."

Mara stared.

"Bad word."

"Yes. Sorry."

She laughed despite herself.

Then reached down for her travel bag.

The blue cup knocked gently inside.

I stood too.

At the table, Mara picked up the burned seven.

"What are you doing?"

"Fixing it."

"Impossible."

She slid it underneath her newest letter instead of leaving it on top.

"There."

"That changes nothing."

"Looks better."

"Authoritarian."

She opened the door.

Footsteps came up the rear stairs before either of us entered the hall.

Noll appeared carrying a paper parcel and wearing his Brass Spoon coat over kitchen clothes.

He saw Mara.

Stopped.

Looked at me.

Back to Mara.

"Late."

"Work," she said.

Noll lifted the parcel.

"Same."

No one looked embarrassed.

Unacceptable.

Noll opened the paper.

Three dumplings.

He held it toward Mara first.

She took one.

"You brought food home from work?"

"Vessa fold."

Mara bit into it.

Steam rose between them.

She chewed.

"Better than your old ones."

Noll smiled.

"Yes."

Not offended.

Proud.

He held the parcel toward me.

I reached.

Noll pulled it back.

"You paid already."

"That should increase my rights."

"No."

Mara laughed with her mouth full.

Current dignity had become impossible.

Noll unlocked his door.

Mara pointed at him.

"You still steal soap?"

He looked offended enough to become younger.

"No."

I said, "Mine."

"Shared custody."

Mara had to put one hand on the wall because she was laughing too hard to manage the dumpling and stairs simultaneously.

Noll went inside.

His door closed.

A chair scraped immediately.

Home.

I walked Mara back toward the river.

At the Lower Rook courtyard she stopped while I was half a step ahead.

I turned.

"What?"

Mara held out her hand.

Not dramatic.

Just there.

I took it.

We crossed the market that way until the Vey office lamps came into view...
