# Chapter 40: Not Early

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

I woke too early and stayed in bed out of spite.

Mara had said not to be early.

Specific instruction.

Reasonable.

I respected reasonable instructions whenever they aligned with behavior I had already been forced into.

Outside, Carrow was still mostly dark.

No first bell.

No wagon noise yet from the main street.

No argument from downstairs.

Noll's wall remained silent.

He had come home after I was already in bed. I knew because sometime in the night his door had opened, one boot had hit the floor, then the other, followed by a single exhausted swear.

Four nights.

The new schedule had already learned his vocabulary.

I looked at the ceiling.

First bell.

River stairs.

Not early.

There were worse problems.

This one was mine.

I got up anyway.

Then deliberately did everything slowly.

Washed.

Dressed.

Re-laced one boot after deciding the first attempt had looked personally offensive.

Checked my purse.

Did not check the time.

Checked the window instead.

Still dark enough that time remained deniable.

I put Mara's six-word note back beneath the room key after moving it for no reason.

The burned seven stayed beside it.

Two keys clicked together when I picked them up.

I put them in my coat.

The field pack beside the door still contained two pairs of socks and no plan.

I left it there.

Downstairs, the cookroom was mostly empty.

A night carter slept with his head on folded arms at one end of the long table.

Someone had left half a loaf under cloth.

I considered stealing breakfast.

Noll had affected my morals.

Instead I went outside.

A baker two streets toward the river had opened one shutter.

Warm bread smell reached the street before light did.

I bought two small rolls filled with egg and onion.

Then a third because the first two looked smaller after I paid.

The woman behind the counter watched me add it.

"Travel?"

"Not mine."

She nodded as if that explained arithmetic.

Maybe it did.

By the time I reached the river, first light had begun turning the water from black into gray.

The stairs were already crowded.

Not passenger crowded.

Work crowded.

Two Vey porters carried a document box between them while arguing about which end was heavier.

A deck hand rolled a barrel down a plank badly enough that everyone nearby discovered religion.

Three women with market baskets waited for a ferry farther downstream.

A man in a wool cap stood beside a stack of tied packet sacks and shouted city names as if the sacks might organize themselves through shame.

Halden.

Carrow west.

Rell Ford.

Mara stood halfway down the stairs with a ledger case under one arm and a Vey clerk holding a board in front of her.

Already working.

Of course.

Her travel coat was buttoned wrong by one button.

Hair tied back better than last night and worse than she probably intended.

Blue cup nowhere visible.

Packed safely, I assumed.

She signed the board.

The clerk pointed at something else.

Mara looked.

"That says three boxes."

"Three."

"We have four."

"Fourth is Carrow copy."

"Then why is it on the west boat?"

The clerk looked toward the porters.

One of them immediately looked away.

Current morning authority established.

I stopped at the top of the stairs.

Mara noticed me before I decided whether to interrupt.

Her mouth moved.

Small.

Then she looked toward the paling sky.

"Not early."

I went down two steps.

"You are."

"Work."

"Of course."

The clerk glanced between us.

"Greg?"

I stared at him.

"Why does Vey know me now?"

Mara took the board from his hands.

"Because I said someone named Greg might stand around looking suspicious."

"Professional description."

"Accurate."

She checked the fourth line herself.

Carrow copy apparently stayed in Carrow after all.

One porter carried it back up the stairs while muttering about ink people.

Mara handed the board back.

Then looked at the paper bag in my hand.

"Food?"

"Possibly."

"For me?"

"Depends how much Vey knows."

"Everything."

I gave her a roll.

She took it immediately.

No argument about half.

Travel rules differed from dinner rules.

She bit into it while standing on the stair.

Closed her eyes.

"Hot."

"That is usually how baking works."

Mara pointed the roll at me.

"You brought onion."

"Pell has damaged your standards."

"Pell would add more."

She took another bite.

I ate mine.

The third stayed in the bag between us.

For perhaps thirty seconds, that was the goodbye.

Egg.

Onion.

Cold river air.

Porters moving around us.

Mara chewing too quickly because a Vey deck hand had started calling for west freight.

It was better than standing still and trying to manufacture meaning.

I looked at the boat.

Different from her first Halden barge.

Larger.

Still ugly.

Canvas over the cargo hold.

Yellow Vey mark near the bow.

One patched section along the rail.

History had not improved transport fast enough for my convenience.

"How long back?" I asked.

"If the river behaves, two days."

"It won't."

"Then three."

"Work when you arrive?"

Mara looked at me.

"Greg."

Right.

Stupid question.

"Yes."

She finished the roll.

Wiped one thumb against the paper.

Ink still marked the side of the other hand from last night.

I wanted to touch it.

Did not need a reason.

I took her hand.

Mara let me.

No surprise now.

That changed something in my chest more than the kiss had expected permission to.

We stood like that while the Vey clerk argued with a deck hand about whether one sack was sealed.

Nobody gave us privacy.

Good.

Privacy had started becoming suspiciously expensive.

"When are you back?" I asked.

There.

The question I had planned not to ask.

Mara did not pretend she knew.

"Carrow?"

"Yes."

"No idea."

Annoying answer.

Her face told me she knew it was annoying.

"Useful."

"Vey sends people where paper catches fire."

"Metaphorically."

"Mostly."

I looked toward the boat.

West.

Halden.

Young Greg had once planned to follow her there.

Old Greg remembered roads beyond it better than he remembered the city itself.

Current Greg had never seen her desk.

That last fact bothered me most.

Mara squeezed my fingers once.

"You can come west."

I looked at her.

She immediately frowned.

"Don't make that face."

"What face?"

"The one where one sentence becomes legislation."

"I have no such face."

"You absolutely do."

I smiled despite myself.

"Is that an invitation?"

Mara thought about it.

Actually thought.

"Yes."

My chest tried something stupid again.

She continued before it gained authority.

"Not to move. Not to prove anything. Not because you missed Doss."

"Very restrictive invitation."

"Visit."

"I know what visiting is."

"Do you?"

"Historically, evidence mixed."

Mara smiled.

"If you come, tell me first."

"How much first?"

"Enough that I don't find you outside my office asking three clerks where I am."

"That happened once."

"You checked twice."

"Institutional propaganda."

"Enough warning, Greg."

"Fine."

I looked at her.

"I might."

Mara's expression softened in a way that made me want to say more.

So naturally the deck hand shouted her name.

"Vey Halden! Boarding now."

Mara closed her eyes for half a second.

"Excellent timing."

"Professional institution."

She let go of my hand.

I disliked that immediately.

Her ledger case shifted under one arm.

Travel bag sat two steps below beside the Vey board.

I picked it up.

"I can carry it."

"I know."

"Then why?"

"I also can carry it."

Mara stared.

"Your arguments are getting worse."

"Morning."

She let me take it.

The bag was heavier than expected.

"Books?"

"Two."

"Cup?"

"Obviously."

"Ledgers?"

"Three."

"You are traveling with more paper than clothing."

"Work."

There was the word again.

Still annoying.

Less enemy now.

We went down the last stairs together.

The loading plank bounced under two porters carrying a box ahead of us.

At the base, a Vey clerk checked Mara's travel paper.

Same name as yesterday.

Same Halden line.

Different morning.

I handed Mara the bag.

She took it.

Then neither of us moved.

There was room for a speech.

Dangerous architecture.

I said, "Your coat is buttoned wrong."

Mara looked down.

It was.

Her entire face changed.

"You waited until now?"

"I thought it might be fashion."

"You are useless."

She put the bag down and fixed the buttons.

I laughed.

Mara finished, looked up, and grabbed the front of my coat.

Kissed me.

Not long.

Long enough.

No hesitation about whose turn it was.

When she stepped back, I was still holding the third breakfast roll.

Mara took it from the bag.

"Payment."

"For what?"

"Professional services."

"I carried one bag."

"Badly."

"You kissed me."

"Separate account."

The deck hand shouted again.

Mara stepped onto the plank.

Then turned back.

"Greg."

"What?"

"Warning first."

Halden.

Right.

"I heard you."

"Dangerous."

"Go to work."

She did.

Not to the stern rail.

Not to some clean place where departures became illustrations.

Mara crossed the deck, handed her travel paper to a Vey man near the cargo cover, then immediately opened the ledger case.

I watched her argue with him about a page before the plank had even come up.

He pointed at the crate numbers.

She pointed harder.

Current Mara was leaving Carrow by correcting paperwork.

I smiled.

She looked up once.

Caught me still there.

Mara made a small shooing motion with two fingers.

I considered being offended.

Then a porter behind me said, "Move."

Apparently everyone agreed.

I moved.

Up the river stairs.

Past the packet sacks.

Past the ferry women.

Past a fish seller opening his stall while complaining about the cold.

The Vey boat was still tied when I reached the top.

That felt wrong.

I kept walking anyway.

By the time the first bell rang, I was halfway to Lower Rook.

The sound traveled over roofs behind me.

One bell.

Promise completed without ceremony.

I had not checked twice.

At Lower Rook, the cookroom had become awake in my absence.

The night carter was gone.

Maud stood near the stove counting spoons as if theft had become seasonal.

Noll was not downstairs.

Still asleep, probably.

I had one roll left.

No.

Mara had stolen the third.

I had nothing left.

This seemed unfair after breakfast had started with three.

I bought porridge from Maud for one copper.

"River?" she asked.

I looked at her.

"How do you know?"

"You look awake."

Apparently that was evidence now.

"Yes."

"Girl leave?"

"Woman."

Maud shrugged.

"Leave?"

"Yes."

She ladled more porridge into my bowl than one copper usually purchased.

I noticed.

Said nothing.

Maud would deny sentiment under oath.

I sat at the long table.

The building made morning noises around me.

Bucket downstairs.

Door upstairs.

Someone coughing in the courtyard.

A cart starting beyond the wall.

No river in sight.

No Mara.

No Jorren.

Noll asleep three floors above me.

Tavin somewhere south, maybe reading my letter, maybe not.

The Guild board would be open.

Mill Street would be awake.

A road could exist.

So could nothing important at all.

I ate the porridge before deciding what the morning was for...
