# Chapter 41: The Second Chair

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

I went to the Guild and bought a chair.

These events were connected less directly than the sentence suggested.

After Mara left, I finished Maud's extra porridge, went upstairs, changed my shirt because river air had made the first one smell like rope, then left again with the serious intention of checking the board.

Work.

Money.

Roads.

Responsible morning.

The Guild was six streets away.

I made it four.

A furniture seller had put three chairs on the sidewalk outside his shop.

This was aggressive marketing.

I stopped.

Not because Mara had said I owned one chair.

That would have been embarrassing.

Because I objectively owned one chair.

Different problem.

The distinction survived approximately five seconds.

The first chair had arms and a cracked cane seat.

Too wide for my room.

The second looked like it had been built for a child or a very disciplined adult.

The third was plain wood with a straight back, four intact legs, and one dark mark near the seat where somebody had probably set down something hot.

I pressed one hand against the back.

Solid.

The shopkeeper appeared in the doorway.

"Six copper."

I had not asked.

"Four."

"You don't know what it costs."

"You just told me."

"Then six."

Strong negotiation.

The man was perhaps fifty, with a leather apron and two fingers on his left hand stained black with varnish.

He looked at my coat.

Then sword.

Then the chair.

"Five."

"Why did it get cheaper?"

"You look annoying."

"Four."

"Five."

I tipped the chair backward slightly.

One rear leg made a tiny clicking sound against the stone.

The shopkeeper's eyes moved.

There.

"Loose joint."

"Not loose."

I rocked it again.

Click.

"Musical joint."

"Four and I'll tighten it."

"Four and I tighten it."

He stared at me.

"You know chairs?"

"No."

That slowed him down.

"Then why would you tighten it?"

"I own tools."

"Dangerous."

Apparently that word had escaped Mara too.

The shopkeeper crouched, looked at the joint, and pushed his thumb against it.

"Glue dried."

"So loose."

"Dry."

"Does dry make it quieter?"

He stood.

"Four and half."

"Copper does halves now?"

Dena's influence had become permanent.

The man pointed into the shop.

"I have cut bits."

Of course he did.

"Four."

He looked at the sword again.

"You paid for that?"

"Unfortunately."

"And you're fighting over one copper on a chair."

"Exactly why I can still afford chairs."

He laughed.

Not enough to give me four.

"Five, tightened."

I looked at the chair.

Five copper was not a financial event anymore.

That fact remained strange.

A few months ago I would have done meal arithmetic around the purchase.

Now the price mostly offended me because the man had started at six.

Money had become capable of being petty instead of frightening.

Useful development.

Probably dangerous.

"Five," I said.

The shopkeeper held out his hand.

I paid.

Then he picked up the chair.

"Where?"

"Lower Rook."

"Two copper delivery."

I took the chair from him.

"Absolutely not."

He looked at me.

Then toward Lower Rook, which was not visible from here because geography remained stubborn.

"Long carry."

"I have arms."

"One chair. Two copper."

"You already stole one."

"Five."

"Exactly."

I carried the chair.

This became less intelligent after the first street.

Not because it was heavy.

Awkward.

Different failure.

The back rose above one shoulder. The seat pressed against my forearm. One leg kept trying to catch pedestrians who had done nothing to deserve furniture violence.

I changed grip twice.

Then a third time.

A woman carrying flowers moved around me and said, "Door's easier."

"I am aware."

She looked at the open street around us.

Fair.

At the Guild corner I stopped.

The board entrance was thirty feet away.

I could check it.

Technically.

Walk inside carrying a chair.

Read contracts.

Possibly become known as a man preparing very comfortable guard work.

Sella would never let me recover.

I kept walking.

The Guild remained available.

The chair had acquired priority through physical inconvenience.

At East Market, one of Vessa's servers saw me.

I knew her face from Brass Spoon. Not her name.

She stared at the chair.

"Noll get fired?"

"Why would that require furniture?"

She shrugged.

"You people are strange."

Then continued toward the restaurant.

Apparently I had become `you people` through association.

Lower Rook appeared one street later.

Relief arrived earlier than dignity.

Maud was in the courtyard arguing with a delivery boy over turnips.

She saw me.

Then the chair.

The turnip argument paused.

"No."

I stopped.

"No what?"

"Don't need chairs."

"You don't."

"Building doesn't."

"It's mine."

Maud looked suspicious.

"Why?"

The question was becoming unreasonable through repetition.

"Sitting."

"Already got one."

Everyone knew too much about my room.

"Now I have two."

Maud looked over my shoulder as if expecting the second chair to explain itself.

"Don't leave it."

"Where?"

"When you go."

There.

Tiny phrase.

No intention behind it.

Probably.

Maud rented rooms. People left them.

Simple.

"Wasn't planning to."

She shrugged.

"Everyone says that."

Then returned to turnips.

I carried the chair inside.

The rear stairs immediately became an enemy.

Chair width plus stair width plus rail wobble created a problem the original carpenter had solved by assuming tenants would own less.

I rotated it sideways.

One leg hit the wall.

"Fuck."

A door opened above me.

Noll looked over the second-floor landing rail.

Hair flattened on one side.

Shirt half-buttoned.

Face still mostly asleep.

He stared at the chair.

Then at me.

"Why?"

"Good morning."

"Chair."

"Correct."

Noll came down three steps.

Looked at the angle.

"Wrong."

"I know."

"Turn."

"I am turning."

"Other way."

"There are only two ways."

"You picked bad one."

I almost told him to go back to bed.

Instead I rotated the chair the other way.

It fit immediately.

I disliked him.

Noll took the upper side without asking.

Together we carried it to the third floor.

At my door he looked at me again.

"Mara?"

There it was.

"What about her?"

"Chair."

"People need chairs."

"She left."

"I know. I was there."

"Then bad timing."

"This chair is not for Mara."

Noll's face did not move.

He had learned Vessa's management expression.

Terrible development.

"It's for people," I said.

"Mara people."

"Yes, Mara is technically a person."

"Jorren too?"

"Also technically."

"Me?"

I looked at him.

"Debatable."

Noll nodded.

"Chair mine."

"Absolutely not."

He opened my door.

I had apparently lost property law while carrying furniture.

The room was not designed for a second chair.

That became obvious immediately.

The existing chair sat near the table.

Bed took most of one wall.

Field pack beside the door.

Washbasin opposite.

Sword hooks.

Window.

Useful floor area had been surviving through negotiated neutrality.

The new chair broke the treaty.

We put it beside the old one.

Too crowded.

Moved it near the bed.

Blocked the pack.

Near the window.

Blocked the basin.

Noll stood in the center of the room watching me redesign five feet of civilization.

"Table move."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the table is correct."

"Chair says no."

Furniture had gained political representation.

We moved the table four inches toward the window.

The old chair shifted with it.

The second chair went across from it, slightly angled because the bed refused compromise.

I stepped back.

Better.

Not spacious.

Possible.

Noll sat in the new chair.

Immediately.

I stared.

"Get up."

He leaned back.

The loose rear joint clicked.

"Broken."

"Dry glue."

"Broken glue."

"Five copper."

Noll looked impressed for the wrong reason.

"Five?"

"It was six."

"Bad."

"I negotiated."

"Bad negotiation."

"You negotiated acceptable wrapper failures."

"Three."

"Exactly."

He rocked the chair again.

Click.

"Need glue."

"I know."

"Arlo has."

"I own glue."

Noll looked around.

"Where?"

I did not know.

Strong evidence.

I opened the table drawer.

Cord.

Wax.

Two spare buckles.

Small whetstone.

No glue.

Second drawer.

Paper.

Old support notes.

A spoon that had somehow become mine.

No glue.

Noll watched.

"Arlo has."

"Shut up."

I found a small bottle in the field-kit box under the bed.

Mostly dry.

Enough.

We turned the chair upside down.

Noll held the leg while I worked glue into the joint with a sliver of wood.

"Why chair?" he asked again.

I kept looking at the joint.

"One wasn't enough."

"For Mara."

"For everyone."

He waited.

Annoying skill.

"She said I still only had one."

Noll smiled.

"There."

"That does not make it hers."

"Mara chair."

"I will throw you out the window."

"Need chair first."

I laughed despite the legal weakness of his argument.

We tied the joint tight with cord while the glue set.

That meant nobody could sit in the new chair.

Noll took the old one.

Of course.

I sat on the bed.

We had successfully increased room seating from one chair to one chair.

Progress remained complicated.

Noll looked toward the table.

Mara's note sat beneath the key.

Burned seven beside it.

He did not pick either up.

"She gone west?"

"Yes."

"Halden."

"Yes."

"You going?"

Fast question.

No setup.

My eyes went to the field pack before I stopped them.

Noll saw.

Naturally.

"Maybe."

He made Mara's exact disapproving face badly enough that I almost threw the whetstone.

"Actual maybe," I said.

"Road?"

"Not yet. She said I can visit."

Noll nodded.

No grin.

No noise.

That was worse somehow.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You're terrible at nothing."

"Chair."

"What about it?"

"Need two if people come."

I stared at him.

That was almost exactly my defense.

I resented losing ownership of it.

Noll stood.

"Food downstairs."

"You just woke up."

"Yes."

"Brass Spoon tonight?"

"No."

Right.

Four nights did not mean every night.

Today was one of the spaces.

"Carter supper?"

"Tomorrow."

He went to the door.

Stopped.

Looked back at the new chair upside down with cord around one leg.

"Mara chair."

I threw the spoon from the drawer.

Noll caught it.

"Mine."

"That actually might be yours."

He inspected it.

"No."

Then put it in his pocket anyway.

The door closed behind him.

I stayed on the bed.

The room looked slightly wrong.

Table four inches farther toward the window.

One chair occupied by nobody.

One chair upside down drying.

Field pack moved closer to the wall so the new arrangement fit.

Small changes.

Expensive enough to notice.

I had missed the Guild board entirely.

That bothered me less than expected.

By midday the glue had set.

I cut the cord.

Turned the chair upright.

Pressed the back.

No click.

Good enough.

I sat in the old chair.

Looked at the new one across from me.

Empty.

Not sad.

Just available.

Then Noll opened the door without knocking, carrying two bowls, and sat in it before I could object.

Of course he did...
