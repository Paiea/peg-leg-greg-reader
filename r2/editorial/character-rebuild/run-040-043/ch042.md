# Chapter 42: Tax Clerk

Status: **CHARACTER REBUILD EXPERIMENT / TEMPORAL A / BRANCH ONLY / NOT PUBLIC R2 AUTHORITY**

---

Tavin called me a tax clerk before asking whether I was dying.

The letter arrived two mornings after Mara left.

Maud handed it to me in the courtyard while I was holding one end of a broken laundry pole for a woman from the second floor.

This was apparently what citizenship looked like now.

"South," Maud said.

I saw the Venn mark before the handwriting.

South Latch.

Freight yard.

Tavin.

My stomach tightened enough that the other woman noticed the pole move.

"Hold it."

"I am."

"Badly."

Everyone in Lower Rook had developed standards.

I tucked the letter inside my coat and held the pole properly until she tied the replacement cord.

Then I went upstairs.

Not quickly.

Fast enough.

The second chair was empty.

Noll had claimed it repeatedly since purchase, but he was at Brass Spoon for the afternoon prep and the room had recovered temporary property rights.

I sat in the new chair instead of the old one.

No reason.

The repaired joint held without clicking.

Five copper successfully converted into suspense furniture.

I opened the letter.

**Greg,**

**Did you hire a tax clerk?**

I stared.

Good start.

**If yes, fire him. If no, stop writing like one. `I am less sure what I mean by wanting roads` is not a sentence you say unless somebody is charging by the word.**

I read that line twice.

Tavin had not become delicate with distance.

Relief came first.

Then irritation because he was right.

I continued.

**The Rell Ford freight is gone. Obviously. I sent you that lead because you were supposed to be in Carrow, not because Venn planned to preserve it in amber until you discovered paper. Nobody died. You missed a job. This happens.**

Fair.

Annoyingly.

**You still owe me two copper. Four now because interest has been invented.**

Fraud.

I looked at the burned seven on the table.

The actual two copper were back in my purse after financing Noll's dumplings.

Tavin's financial theory had become detached from currency.

**I am doing seals now.**

That stopped me.

**Venn decided I can count wagon marks and apparently that makes me trustworthy enough to check merchant seals on return loads. It means I stand beside men who swear they packed twelve bolts when the paper says eleven and then act surprised when I can also count to eleven. Pay is three copper better each run. I hate wax.**

I smiled.

I pictured hands stained with seal wax.

Wrong, probably.

Maybe current Tavin wore gloves.

Maybe he had cut his hair.

Maybe he had become fat.

The letter provided none of this because people rarely described themselves for the benefit of a man who was supposed to know what they looked like.

That thought removed the smile.

I kept reading.

**South Latch is wet. Rell Ford smells the same. Venn hired a new driver named Harret who sings one song and knows half of it. I may kill him before winter. If this reaches a magistrate, that sentence is humor.**

Good legal awareness.

**Mira left the yard for a river company and took the good kettle. This is worse than Harret.**

Mira.

Nothing.

No tightening stomach.

No memory flicker.

Just a name Tavin expected to fit somewhere.

I put one thumb against the page.

Kept going.

The handwriting changed slightly near the bottom.

Still Tavin.

Less slanted.

**You missed my send-off. I thought you were angry about something and too stupid to say it. Then you disappeared into Carrow and sent me a letter so late it sounds like somebody explaining you to yourself.**

I read the next line.

**So what happened?**

Three words.

No careful frame.

No list of acceptable answers.

**Did you get sick? Hit your head? Join a temple? Are you trying to tell me you don't remember the road plan, or you don't remember me, or neither and you have simply become unbearable?**

I stopped reading.

The room did not help.

Two chairs.

One table.

Mara's note.

Burned seven.

Sword.

Two keys in my coat.

Current life everywhere.

Tavin somewhere south asking whether he existed inside it.

I looked at the letter again.

There were still three lines.

**Venn has a Carrow load next tenday. I asked for it. No promise. Sorn assigns crews and Sorn thinks personal joy is theft from the company. If I get it, I'll send word if the packet beats the wagons.**

Then:

**Four copper.**

And his name.

**Tavin**

I laughed.

One sound.

Then sat there with the letter open on my knee.

Carrow load.

Maybe.

No promise.

Good.

I had spent several days being trained against that word by everybody available and now Tavin had used the concept correctly without even writing it.

I read the letter again.

Not all of it.

The question.

**So what happened?**

I could answer.

I had answered Mara.

Badly at first.

Jorren knew enough to stop testing the edges every time memory failed.

Noll knew because living through a wall made privacy theoretical.

Tavin knew nothing except that I had missed him leaving and then written like an elderly clerk had seized my hand.

I found paper.

The second chair faced the first now because Noll had refused my original angle and moved it yesterday while eating soup.

I moved to the old chair at the table.

Tavin's letter stayed on the new one.

Like a person waiting.

Annoying furniture.

I dipped the pen.

**Tavin,**

Then nothing.

Several truthful openings arrived.

**Something happened to my memory.**

Too small.

**I don't remember your send-off.**

Cruel without context.

**I remember you badly.**

Worse.

**I have memories from the future and may technically be a fifty-nine-year-old S-class adventurer occupying the body of your nineteen-year-old friend.**

Accurate enough to require restraints.

I put the pen down.

The room was quiet.

Noll working.

Jorren still north-east.

Mara somewhere on the river or already west of Rell Ford depending on wind, current, and institutional incompetence.

Nobody available to make this easier.

I picked up the pen again.

**Something happened to my memory.**

I left the sentence.

Then:

**I remember things I should not remember and don't remember things I should. I did not remember your send-off. When Mara said your name, I knew it should hurt before I knew why.**

I stopped.

Read it.

Too formal.

Still mine.

I added:

**I know you taught me cards mostly because other people told me. I remember pieces now. Rain. The back room. Your hand hitting mine away from a pot. I don't know if that happened once or twenty times.**

Better.

More dangerous.

The next sentence took longer.

**I should have told you this in the first letter.**

There.

No qualification.

No excuse about not knowing how.

That was also true.

I stared at it long enough for the ink to dry.

Then wrote:

**I am not dying. I did not join a temple. I may still be unbearable.**

Tavin deserved useful information.

**The roads part was true. I have gone out. West Cut. Guard work. Glasshorns. Rain. One very stupid wagon. I wanted you to know that because apparently I still want you impressed by me. This is embarrassing, so if you repeat it I will deny the letter is authentic.**

I stopped again.

That sentence had not been planned.

Which was probably why it sounded less like tax policy.

I looked at Tavin's letter on the second chair.

Current Tavin did seals.

Hated wax.

Had a singer named Harret threatening his sanity.

Maybe coming to Carrow.

I wrote:

**Seals sounds terrible. Three copper better is almost enough to justify wax. Almost. Tell Mira she is a thief if you see her. I don't know who Mira is. That sentence feels insane to write, but pretending I do would be worse.**

I continued before courage organized an objection.

**If you get the Carrow load, come find me when Venn releases you. Lower Rook, third floor rear. There are two chairs now. This is apparently important.**

I looked toward the new chair.

Tavin's letter occupied it comfortably.

**You still get two copper. Interest is fictional.**

Then my name.

**Greg**

I read the whole thing once.

It was too long.

Too strange.

Not polished enough in several places.

Excellent.

I folded it before improvement became possible.

Then remembered I needed to send it.

The packet office was across the river market.

I had been there too often recently.

Institutional reputation risk.

I put on my coat.

Halfway to the door, it opened.

Noll walked in carrying my spoon.

I stared at it.

"You said not yours."

"Still not."

"Then why do you have it?"

"Kitchen has enough."

Apparently property could return through disinterest.

He put the spoon on the table.

Saw the folded letter.

"Mara?"

"Tavin."

Noll looked at the burned seven.

"Four copper?"

I stopped.

"How does everyone know about the interest?"

"He said before."

"When?"

Noll shrugged.

"Years."

Of course.

"It's fictional."

"You burned card."

"Conspiracy."

Noll went to the new chair.

Tavin's letter still sat on it.

He picked it up.

Not opening.

Just moving it to the table so he could sit.

"You going packet?"

"Yes."

"I go East Market."

Brass Spoon.

The packet office was mostly on his way.

I looked at the folded reply in my hand.

This was not a sword.

Noll could carry paper.

Probably.

Dangerous word.

"Will you post this?"

He held out his hand immediately.

I did not give it to him.

Noll waited.

Why was this harder than handing a clerk one copper?

Because the letter said things I had not said to Tavin before.

Because once Noll walked downstairs, I could still chase him.

I gave him the letter.

"South Latch. Venn yard. Tavin."

Noll looked at the front.

"Written there."

"I know."

"Then good."

He put it inside his coat.

I immediately wanted it back.

Not enough to ask.

Noll stood.

Then sat again.

"What?"

"Food first."

I stared.

"You are holding a major emotional event in your coat."

"Hungry."

Fair.

He opened the bowl he had brought in the other hand.

Potatoes.

Onions.

Something brown that might have once held a professional relationship with beef.

Noll ate.

My letter remained inside his coat.

I sat in the old chair and watched him chew.

This was unbearable.

Also stupid.

"Can you eat faster?"

"No."

"You work in a restaurant."

"Why would that help?"

I had no answer.

Noll took another bite.

The room had two chairs now.

One held me.

One held a man eating potatoes while carrying a letter telling my oldest friend I barely remembered him.

Apparently this was what I had bought furniture for.

Noll finished eventually.

He stood.

"Packet."

"Yes."

"Then work."

"Yes."

He left.

I did not follow.

That was the difficult part...
