# R2 Temporal 4×10 Rehearsal Override

Status: **ACTIVE FOR THIS EXPERIMENT / ZERO STORY AUTHORITY**

This file clarifies the user-approved meaning of `write` in the 4×10 experiment.

## Core correction

A/B/C/D do **not** draft forty candidate canon chapters.

They **rehearse** four ten-chapter future performances.

Each horizon still aims for roughly ten chapter-sized units at roughly 3,000 words each, approximately 30,000 words per horizon and 120,000 words total, because the experiment needs enough embodied prose to expose things that outlines cannot expose.

The word count is rehearsal depth, not authority.

## Rehearsal fidelity

Rehearsal should be high enough fidelity to test:

- Greg's actual prose voice and interiority
- thought/action contradiction
- dialogue and relationship language
- physical action and bodily behavior
- setting pressure and sensory specificity
- plot movement and causal pressure
- pacing at chapter scale
- scene endings and transitions
- jokes, objects, work, money, magic, danger, desire, embarrassment, boredom, and aftermath

A rehearsal may therefore read like a real chapter. That does not make it one.

## Authority rule

**REHEARSAL FIDELITY HIGH. AUTHORITY ZERO.**

No A/B/C/D event becomes true because it was rehearsed.

No chapter number is protected.

No wording is protected.

No relationship state, job, injury, trip, fight, purchase, promotion, kiss, argument, discovery, magic fact, or logistical consequence becomes canon merely by appearing in rehearsal.

After audit, surviving material must be freshly performed against the newest accepted R2 authority before any promotion.

## Simultaneous performance

All four rehearsal agents begin from accepted truth through Chapter 42 and durable pre-42/rebuild evidence.

They do not wait for one another.

They do not extend one another's invented future chronology.

A may rehearse 43–52 territory while B, C, and D simultaneously rehearse farther temporal pressures from the same Chapter-42 truth.

The provisional number ranges are coordinates for comparison and word-count accounting, not chronology promises.

## What can survive

The audit may preserve discoveries such as:

- a character choice
- a physical habit
- a contradiction in Greg
- a relationship pressure
- a setting that produces good action
- a useful danger shape
- a magic question
- a social collision
- a work or money consequence
- a joke
- an object
- a scene grammar
- an emotional turn
- a reason a proposed future fails

It may kill the surrounding 3,000 words completely.

## Audit interpretation

The final audit asks whether roughly 120,000 words of rehearsal created more leverage than a smaller speculative system would have created.

It must separately audit:

1. measured word count
2. engine behavior
3. prose
4. character
5. setting
6. plot
7. physical action

Do not grade rehearsal by publication cleanliness alone. Grade whether it discovered useful future story and whether the cost of producing it was justified.
