# Rehearsal A45: Doss Mud

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Jorren came back while I was washing rope oil out of my gloves.

This felt targeted.

I had a basin between my knees, soap under one thumbnail, and both gloves soaking in water the color of weak tea when somebody kicked my door twice.

Not knocked.

Kicked.

Noll did that when his hands were full.

"Open," I called.

The door opened.

Jorren stood there carrying a bedroll over one shoulder and enough dried mud to qualify as new clothing.

For one second I did not move.

He looked different.

Not dramatically.

That was worse.

Hair longer over one ear where he had apparently cut the rest badly or lost an argument with a knife. Left sleeve patched at the elbow in dark green cloth that did not match his coat. One boot newly resoled. Thin scrape along the right side of his jaw.

His shoulder looked fine.

I checked before I realized I was checking.

Jorren looked at the basin.

"Laundry?"

"Rope."

"You own rope now?"

"No."

"Good."

He stepped inside.

Then stopped because my room had two chairs.

There.

I had been waiting for someone to notice.

Not consciously.

Apparently my body had stored satisfaction in advance.

Jorren looked from the old chair to the new one.

"Rich."

"Five copper."

"Very rich."

"Noll says it is Mara's."

"Is it?"

"No."

Jorren put his bedroll in it.

Traitor.

"You look terrible," I said.

"You look clean."

"Insulting."

He dropped into the old chair.

I stood because there were now two chairs and neither available to me.

Material progress.

"Doss?"

"Doss."

"Kelm?"

"Kelm."

"Beren?"

"Alive."

"Vell?"

"Paid."

"Road?"

Jorren looked at me.

"Long."

I waited.

He did not elaborate.

This was unacceptable.

"What happened to your face?"

He touched the jaw scrape.

"Crate."

"Crate hit you?"

"I hit crate."

"Why?"

"Was dark."

"That explains nothing."

"Explains face."

Jorren leaned forward and took my drying cloth from the table.

He wiped mud off one hand with it.

"That is for gloves."

"Now hand."

"You returned from ten days away and immediately stole cleaning supplies."

"Nine."

"What?"

"Days."

Nine.

Inside the six-to-ten-day window.

I had counted without wanting to admit I counted.

He had left before Mara arrived.

Returned after she left.

Entire relationship movement had happened while he was somewhere north-east dealing with mud and crates.

His life had moved too.

"How was Kelm?" I asked.

"Dusty."

"It rained."

"Then muddy."

"Jorren."

He grinned.

There.

Same enough to hurt.

I threw the wet glove at him.

He caught it with his left hand.

New tell?

No. Wrong domain.

"Fine," he said. "Millstone got there. Vell's customer changed unloading yard after we arrived. Beren yelled. Yard man yelled. I got paid to move same stone twice."

"Good money?"

"Thirty-two."

More than the original job estimate.

"Why?"

"Second move."

"You negotiated?"

"Beren did."

No shame about that either.

"Return?"

"Brass scraps. Two sacks buckles. Empty cask."

"Danger?"

Jorren thought.

Actually thought.

"One washed road."

My attention sharpened.

"Where?"

"East of Doss."

"Bridge?"

"No bridge."

"Ford?"

"Ditch."

"How deep?"

"Deep enough Beren said no."

"Then?"

"Went north."

"How far?"

"Half day."

"Any glasshorn?"

Jorren looked at me.

"Why glasshorn?"

Old Greg memory.

Quarry future?

No. Accepted truth had glasshorn work on West Cut earlier.

Still current concern.

"Migration."

"Saw tracks."

"Fresh?"

"I didn't lick them."

"That would not establish age."

"Then good."

I realized I was smiling.

Not because the road sounded good.

Because he had returned and the questions could be asked badly in person.

I looked at the patched sleeve again.

"That?"

Jorren pinched the green cloth.

"Beren."

"Beren sews?"

"Badly."

"I can see."

The stitches were huge.

One diagonal enough to be artistic failure.

"Why green?"

"His spare."

"You accepted that?"

"Hole was cold."

Again.

No obsession with dignity where comfort worked.

I learned this about Jorren repeatedly and still found it strange because Old Greg memories of adventurers were full of men who would rather bleed than admit equipment failure.

Current people often had better priorities.

Sometimes.

"You hungry?"

"Yes."

"Noll?"

"Working?"

"Not tonight."

Jorren stood.

"Then food."

"You need bath."

He smelled like road.

Mud, leather, old smoke, and something sour from nine days of clothing rotation.

"Food first."

"Bath first."

"Why?"

"Because I own two chairs now."

He stared.

"This related?"

"I want them to survive."

Jorren picked up his bedroll.

"Fine."

We went to the bathhouse on South Lane.

Not because he needed supervision.

Because I wanted the road story and sitting in my room while he smelled like a wet mule had limits.

The bathhouse charged three copper for hot water and one extra for soap.

Jorren refused soap because he had his own sliver wrapped in cloth.

I paid anyway for myself.

"You bathing too?" he asked.

"I worked ropehouse."

"Two days?"

"Yes."

"Dirty city."

"You have mud inside your ear."

"Road."

He said it like explanation and status.

We stripped in the changing room.

The scrape on his jaw was not the only mark.

Bruise across left ribs, yellow at edges.

I stared.

Jorren saw.

"Crate."

"You said face."

"Same crate."

"How did you hit a crate with both face and ribs?"

"Fell."

"From?"

"Wagon."

My stomach tightened.

"Moving?"

"No."

I stared harder.

"Then how?"

Jorren climbed into the hot pool.

"Dark."

"You fell off a stationary wagon in the dark."

"Yes."

"This is the road danger?"

"Mostly."

I laughed.

Could not help it.

He splashed water at me.

I stepped in.

Heat hit the rope burn on my palm and turned it sharp.

I sucked air through teeth.

Jorren saw.

"Hand."

"Rope."

"You said."

"Crate dropped."

His expression changed.

Now his turn.

"Hit?"

"No."

"You?"

"Rope burn. Pulled someone."

"Why?"

"Crate was going to hit him."

Jorren waited.

"And I almost argued for testing full load before we knew rope was damaged."

He blinked.

"Why?"

"Because it would have given better evidence."

"And maybe dropped crate."

"Yes."

"Bad."

"Thank you."

He leaned back against the pool wall.

Hot water turned road dirt into a cloud around him.

I watched it move.

"You missed Mara."

"She here?"

"Was."

Jorren looked over.

"You okay?"

I hated the question because it was sincere.

"Yes."

Then, because he had just admitted falling off a stationary wagon:

"Mostly."

He nodded.

"Dinner?"

"Yes. Room. Kiss. She left for work. Then left west."

Jorren's eyebrows rose.

"Room?"

"Do not."

"I said one word."

"It was shaped badly."

He grinned.

I splashed him.

We stayed in the bath longer than necessary.

Not talking continuously.

That was new enough to notice.

Young Greg and Jorren, according to other people, had filled silence with cards, insults, or plans.

Current Jorren could sit in hot water with his eyes closed while I thought about rope failure and Mara's hand on my coat and a Vell posting hidden beneath the burned seven.

I almost told him about the posting.

Then did not.

Why?

Because I wanted to know if he would bring it up first.

That was manipulative in a small pathetic way.

I let it happen anyway.

After the bath, we went back to Lower Rook.

Noll was in the cookroom stirring something in a pot with both hands on the spoon.

He looked up.

Saw Jorren.

His entire face opened.

"Mud."

Jorren looked down at clean clothing.

"No."

"Before."

"Yes."

Noll abandoned the spoon to Maud without asking.

He crossed the room and hit Jorren once in the shoulder.

Not hug.

Hit.

Jorren hit him back.

Apparently affection had local dialects.

Noll looked at me.

"Food upstairs?"

"Your pot?"

"Maud."

Maud shouted from the stove, "Not mine!"

Noll went back for it.

Current life.

We carried the pot upstairs between us because it was too hot for one set of hands.

Physical logistics of friendship.

My room accepted three people badly.

Jorren took new chair.

Noll took old chair.

I sat on bed.

Again.

I had purchased myself lower status.

Noll had made dumplings.

Not Brass Spoon fold.

Rougher.

Larger.

He put them into broth with cabbage and a few strips of pork.

Jorren ate six before conversation became possible.

"Road?" Noll asked.

"Good," Jorren said.

I looked at him.

"You told me long."

"Both."

Noll nodded as if this was sufficient.

Annoying alliance.

"Beren?"

Jorren said, "Good. Loud."

"Mara?" Noll asked me.

"West."

"Chair?"

"Still not hers."

Jorren put one hand on the chair back.

"Mine now."

"Absolutely not."

He laughed.

After food, Jorren pulled cards from his coat.

Different deck.

Cheap paper-backed set with blue pattern.

"Where's yours?" I asked.

"Wet."

"Doss?"

"Road."

Everything was road.

He dealt.

Noll watched.

I looked at Jorren's hands.

Tell.

He used to flex the smallest finger when holding nothing.

Then he had changed it.

Current tell might be shoulder, mouth, breathing, card placement.

I lost two hands watching too carefully.

Jorren smiled.

"Still looking."

"You changed."

"Maybe."

"You did."

"Maybe you're bad."

Noll said, "He is."

Betrayal everywhere.

Halfway through the next deal, Jorren said, "Vell posted another Kelm."

My hand stopped over a card.

There.

He knew.

He had known before entering my room probably.

"I saw."

"You asking?"

"Maybe."

Jorren looked at me.

Noll looked between us.

"I'm not," Jorren said.

That surprised me enough to forget my card.

"Why?"

"Beren asked. I said no."

"Why?"

"City factor offered three weeks inventory and local runs."

I stared.

"You want that?"

"Pay steady."

"But road."

Jorren shrugged.

"Road later."

I had spent days imagining him as the person already leaving again.

He had returned and chosen city work before I could resent him for leaving.

Worse, he had not asked my opinion.

"Who factor?"

"Meln. East warehouses."

"What work?"

"Load counts. Cart routes. Maybe crews."

"That sounds like coordinator work."

"Maybe."

I felt a strange pulse of pride.

Then concern.

Then desire to explain how future road coordinators worked.

I swallowed it.

Mostly.

"Do you want crews?"

Jorren looked at his cards.

"Want money."

"That's not answer."

"Is for me."

There.

Current Jorren refusing my imagined ladder before I had even built it aloud.

Noll put one dumpling into his mouth whole.

Chewed.

"Greg go road."

Both of us looked at him.

He pointed at me with the spoon.

"He wants."

"I know," Jorren said.

I hated being discussed while present.

"I can decide."

Noll said, "Yes."

No argument.

That somehow made it worse.

I looked at the cards.

Vell posting beneath burned seven downstairs in my mind.

Ropehouse two days next week.

Jorren staying in Carrow for three weeks.

Noll four nights at Brass Spoon.

Mara west.

Tavin reply somewhere south.

Road available.

Home available too.

My hand moved to the wrong card.

Jorren slapped it down before I released it.

"You sure?"

I looked at him.

He smiled.

New tell.

Maybe.

"No," I said.

I chose another card.

Lost anyway.
