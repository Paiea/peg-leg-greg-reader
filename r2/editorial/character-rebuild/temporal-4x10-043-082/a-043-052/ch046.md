# Rehearsal A46: Vell

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I asked Vell about the road before breakfast because this seemed like the kind of decision improved by hunger.

It was not.

Vell worked out of East Warehouse Row, which meant finding him required walking past three buildings that all claimed to be Number Eight.

Carrow had adopted mathematical pluralism.

The first Number Eight stored grain.

The second stored wool.

The third stored people who believed they stored grain but mostly shouted at carts.

Jorren had said, "East Warehouse Eight. Vell. Ask Rami."

Then gone to his new factor job before I could demand better geography.

I found Rami by asking a woman rolling a barrel who looked like she wanted to hit me with it.

"Vell?"

She pointed with her chin toward the third Number Eight.

"Inside. Red scarf."

"Rami?"

She stopped rolling.

"Me."

Of course.

"Jorren said ask you."

Her expression changed by half a degree.

"Jorren back?"

"Yesterday."

"Meln got him?"

"Apparently."

"Good."

Another person knew more about Jorren's work than I did.

That no longer surprised me.

It still landed.

Rami resumed rolling the barrel.

I put one hand against the opposite rim automatically.

"I have it," she said.

"I was helping."

"You were touching."

I removed my hand.

Carrow remained consistent.

"Vell's inside," she said. "If you want Kelm, too late."

There.

I stopped.

"Filled?"

"Yesterday."

"Who?"

Rami looked at me.

"Why?"

Excellent question.

I wanted a name because names turned rejection into competition.

If the slot had gone to someone incompetent, I could be offended.

If it had gone to someone better, I could be offended differently.

"Curious."

"Siva."

I did not know Siva.

This was unsatisfying.

"Good?"

Rami smiled.

"Ask Vell."

She rolled the barrel away.

I went inside.

Warehouse Eight Number Three was taller than it looked from the street. Grain sacks stacked in bays along one wall. Cart wheels leaned against another. A raised office platform occupied the rear beneath windows filmed with dust.

Men and women moved through painted lanes between freight piles.

Nobody stood still long enough to become decorative.

A man in a red scarf leaned over a table near the loading doors with three sheets spread beneath both hands.

I waited.

He kept reading.

I waited longer.

Eventually he said, "If you're selling, no. If you're collecting, line's left. If you're Venn, Sorn can choke."

"Greg."

Vell looked up.

Early forties maybe. Crooked nose. Red scarf tied too tightly. One eye slightly paler than the other.

"Jorren's Greg?"

I immediately disliked ownership grammar.

"Greg's Greg."

Vell looked at me for another second.

"Yes, Jorren's Greg."

Fine.

"Kelm posting."

"Filled."

"Rami said."

"Then why ask?"

"Because Rami said ask you whether Siva is good."

Vell laughed.

"She said that?"

"Essentially."

"Siva's better than you."

I stared.

"You have never met me."

"Exactly. I know Siva."

Annoying logic.

"What does she do?"

"Road guard. Good with mules. Doesn't talk to ward marks unless they talk first."

Specific insult somehow.

"Why post still up?"

"Guild slow pulling it."

"Could have saved me a walk."

"Walking's free."

My heel felt fine, which made the statement unusually accurate and therefore suspicious.

Vell looked at the papers again.

I should have left.

The road was filled.

Answer obtained.

Instead I said, "Anything else?"

There.

Not `road`.

Anything.

Vell's eyes lifted.

"You want anything else or road?"

I hated him too quickly.

"Road."

"Then say road."

"Road."

He pushed one sheet aside.

"West Spur tomorrow. Overnight, maybe two. Empty mill cart out, iron pins back. Fourteen copper. Not guard rate. Driver wants second body because south ditch washed and axle's been slipping."

"Danger?"

"Cart."

"I mean attack."

"If somebody attacks an empty mill cart, ask what childhood did to them."

I considered.

One night.

Maybe two.

Not Kelm.

Not a six-to-ten-day road.

Small enough that saying yes felt like cheating.

Which was absurd.

Young Greg wanted roads, not minimum road length certification.

"Who driver?"

"Ossa."

"Good?"

Vell looked toward the loading doors.

A woman outside was backing a narrow two-axle cart between stacked barrels by hand signals to a boy holding the mule.

She was perhaps thirty, broad through shoulders, hair wrapped in yellow cloth.

The cart wheel missed a stone bollard by less than an inch.

"Ask her," Vell said.

I went outside.

Ossa finished backing the cart, set the brake, then crawled underneath before I introduced myself.

Only her boots remained visible.

"Ossa?"

"Depends."

Apparently Carrow had one joke too.

"Vell says West Spur tomorrow."

A hand came out from beneath the cart.

"Pass hammer."

There were four tools beside the wheel.

I picked the hammer.

Her hand took it.

Metal rang beneath the cart.

"Again," she said.

"Greg."

"Good for you. Wrench."

I passed wrench.

More metal.

Then Ossa slid out on her back.

Grease on one cheek.

She sat up.

"You the one Jorren mentioned?"

Again.

"What did he say?"

"Tall. Talks."

"Unfairly broad."

"Accurate so far."

She stood and wiped both hands on a rag.

"You drive?"

"No."

"Mules?"

"Not professionally."

"Cart repair?"

"No."

"Then why Vell send you?"

"Second body."

Ossa looked at my sword.

"You fight?"

"Yes."

"Lift?"

"Yes."

"Walk?"

"Usually."

"Good. West Spur is mostly walking."

She kicked the left rear wheel.

A small clunk answered.

"That axle?"

"Hub plate."

"Vell said axle slipping."

"Vell calls anything under cart axle."

"He seems confident."

"He's a factor. Confidence is cheaper than repair."

I liked her.

Danger increasing.

"Leaving when?"

"First light. East Gate."

"Pay fourteen?"

"You bargain with Vell. I get day rate."

"You don't know mine?"

"Not my money."

Ownership again.

Current adults persisted.

I said yes before I went back inside.

Not formally.

Just in my own body.

The pack beside my wall already existed in mind.

Two unused socks.

Rain wrap.

Food.

A one-night road did not require much.

I could still work Kerren's offered days next week.

No conflict.

That made the decision easier and therefore somehow less satisfying.

I wanted a road to cost something because then it would prove I wanted it.

That was idiotic.

I went back to Vell.

"I'll take it."

"Thirteen."

"You said fourteen."

"Ossa says you're useless with carts."

I looked outside.

She was tightening a strap and absolutely not looking at us.

"I am useful with other things."

"Thirteen."

"Fourteen."

"Thirteen and meal."

"Vell."

He smiled.

"Fourteen. Bring own breakfast."

"That was already the offer."

"Then negotiation successful."

I hated commerce.

He wrote my name on the sheet.

The physical act did something to me.

Ink.

Greg.

West Spur.

Tomorrow.

A road with a date.

Not future memory.

Not maybe.

Not Mara's west.

Mine.

My stomach lifted.

Then immediately I thought about telling Jorren.

Why?

Validation.

Of course.

I left Warehouse Row and went looking for him.

That was the first contradiction of the day.

I wanted an independent road so badly that the first thing I did was seek an audience.

Meln's factor house occupied two blocks east, according to Rami.

I found Jorren in the loading yard counting sacks aloud while a clerk wrote totals.

He wore the green-patched sleeve from Doss.

No road mud now.

Hair still uneven.

Current city Jorren.

He saw me at number thirty-eight.

"Thirty-nine," he said.

Then pointed at me.

"Wait."

I waited.

He counted through fifty-two before coming over.

"What?"

No greeting.

Work.

"West Spur tomorrow."

His face changed.

"Vell?"

"Yes. Ossa driver."

"Good."

There.

I wanted more.

"Overnight maybe two."

"West Spur road okay. South ditch bad after rain."

"You know Ossa?"

"One run."

Another fact.

"Good?"

"Loud."

"Everyone you know is loud."

"Maybe you hear loud people."

I considered that.

"Any advice?"

The question escaped before pride caught it.

Jorren looked toward the factor clerk.

"Bring dry socks."

I laughed.

"Already packed."

"Then you're expert."

"Apparently."

He slapped my shoulder once.

"Have fun."

Then turned back toward the sacks.

That was it.

No envy.

No joining.

No speech about the road he had just left.

I stood there while he resumed counting.

"Fifty-three," he said.

The clerk said, "You already said fifty-two."

"Yes. Next number."

"Just checking."

Jorren looked at me over his shoulder.

"You leaving?"

Apparently I had become obstruction.

"Yes."

I left.

At Lower Rook, the second chair was empty because Noll was at Brass Spoon.

I packed alone.

One shirt.

Rain wrap.

Two pairs socks.

Small field kit.

Salve.

Gloves.

Sword oil.

Three strips dried meat.

Bread bought downstairs.

I almost packed Mara's note.

My hand touched the folded paper under the room key.

Then stopped.

Why take it?

She was not going to West Spur.

No reason.

I left it.

Then picked up Tavin's burned seven card.

Also no reason.

Left that too.

Pack became lighter by refusing sentiment.

I felt proud for approximately twenty seconds.

Then I put the Lower Rook room key into the small inside pocket where it would not fall out.

Apparently sentiment could be metal.

I tightened the pack straps.

The movement pulled slightly at my healing left palm.

Rope burn mostly closed now.

I flexed.

Fine.

Tomorrow's road did not know about yesterday's crate.

My body did.

That felt more useful than philosophy.

Noll came home after dark smelling like broth, hot oil, and the particular steam of professional kitchens.

He opened my door without knocking.

Saw the pack.

Stopped.

"Road."

"Yes."

"Kelm?"

"West Spur."

"Small."

I stared.

"Road size is not measured morally."

"One night?"

"Maybe two."

Noll nodded.

Then saw my face.

"You wanted bigger."

"I wanted road."

"Got road."

"Yes."

He sat in the second chair.

Of course.

I remained standing beside the packed bag.

"Why are you making it sound simple?"

Noll looked at me.

"Because simple."

"It isn't."

"You go. You come back. Maybe."

"Maybe?"

"Road."

He shrugged.

I hated this conversation.

Then he pulled a wrapped parcel from inside his coat.

"Food."

"I packed food."

"This better."

He handed it over.

Two Brass Spoon dumplings wrapped in waxed paper.

Not leftovers exactly. One wrapper slightly torn, probably kitchen rejection.

The other perfect.

"Vessa know?"

Noll looked offended.

"Yes."

Probably.

I put them into the pack.

"Thanks."

Noll leaned back.

"Chair mine while gone."

"No."

"You not here."

"Property persists."

"We'll see."

I threw a sock at him.

He caught it.

"Road sock."

"Give that back."

He did.

I repacked it.

Then checked the pack once more before bed.

Not because I was nervous.

Because one-night roads required rigorous sock governance.

That was my position.

My hands checked the room key twice anyway.
