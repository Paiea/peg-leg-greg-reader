# Rehearsal A49: The Return Load

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I woke sore and happy, which was suspicious.

The alder camp smelled like damp ash and mule.

Ossa was already awake, because apparently drivers did not believe in vulnerable morning states.

She handed me a cup of boiled water with something green floating in it.

"Tea?"

"Leaf."

"Which leaf?"

"One that doesn't kill you."

"Comforting."

It tasted like bitter grass.

My right shoulder protested when I lifted the cup.

The moving support had taxed muscles that should not have mattered to magic.

Current body disagreed with theory.

Ossa noticed.

"Arm?"

"Shoulder."

"Can lift?"

I raised both arms overhead.

Painful but complete.

"Yes."

"Then load."

No bedside manner.

The iron pins were only forty-eight pounds total. We shifted them from the rear to a position farther forward because the detour road had rougher washboards and Ossa wanted less bounce behind the axle.

I learned this while kneeling in cold grass threading rope through floor rings.

Not through a lecture.

Better.

We left after sunrise.

The northern farm road climbed through pasture and low woods before rejoining Carrow road beyond the washed section.

No dramatic obstacles.

Three gates.

One angry goose.

A bridge narrow enough that Ossa made me walk ahead and stop opposing traffic.

I stood in road waving at a wagon driver who saw me, understood me, and came anyway.

"Stop!"

He kept coming.

I stepped into center.

He stopped ten feet away.

"Move."

"Bridge one cart."

"I'm loaded."

"So are we."

"I was here first."

He was not.

Road conflict had apparently reached childhood.

Ossa shouted from behind, "Harn! Back your ass up."

The driver leaned around me.

"Ossa?"

"Yes."

"Fuck."

He backed up.

Professional reputation more effective than sword.

I walked back as Ossa crossed.

"You know everyone?"

"No."

"Enough."

"Road repeats people."

That stayed with me for the next mile.

Not as wisdom.

As practical fact.

Roads were not empty lines between cities. They were recurring cooks, toll clerks, farmers, drivers, blacksmiths, bad dogs, washed ditches, people who remembered whether you paid.

Old Greg's map had distances.

Current road had Harn backing up because Ossa knew his name.

At midday we passed a roadside shrine made from three stacked stones and a strip of red cloth.

Ossa touched two fingers to the cart rail.

Small gesture.

I noticed.

"What shrine?"

"Not shrine."

"Then?"

"Marker."

"For?"

"Girl died there."

I looked back.

No name.

No inscription.

Just stones.

"You knew her?"

"No. Mother drove this route."

I waited for more.

None came.

Good.

We reached Carrow's outer farms by late afternoon.

The city's smoke became visible before walls.

My body recognized the approach in ways I had not expected.

The road widened.

More carts.

More voices.

Then East Gate towers above hedges.

I felt relief.

Immediately resented it.

I had been gone one night.

Two technically.

People crossed continents and did not become sentimental about walls.

Old Greg had slept under snow for weeks.

Current Greg saw Carrow and thought about Lower Rook soup.

There was no argument to win there.

At the gate, the clerk checked return paper.

"West Spur closed?"

"South shoulder," Ossa said.

"Runner came."

"Good."

"Marr charging five now."

Ossa swore so hard the clerk laughed.

We entered.

Warehouse Row at evening was louder than morning. Carts returning. Men rolling barrels. Grain dust hanging gold in light.

Vell stood outside Number Eight Three with red scarf loosened.

"Alive."

"Disappointed?" I asked.

"Thirteen next time."

"Fourteen plus shoulder surcharge."

"You don't own road."

"Neither does Marr apparently, but he charges."

Ossa handed Vell the closure note and access receipt.

"Three copper field lane yesterday. Detour today. Pins intact."

Vell read.

"Why didn't you take field today?"

"Mud."

"Marr would let you."

"Five."

Vell looked offended on principle.

"Robbery."

"You pay three yesterday."

"Route expense."

Their argument moved immediately into accounting territory I did not own.

I waited.

This was harder than it should have been because I had done things.

Seen crack.

Held wheel.

Grabbed brake.

Part of me wanted Vell to ask.

He did not.

He cared about pins, closure, cost, cart.

Correct priorities.

Annoying.

Ossa finally said, "He was useful."

I looked at her.

She did not look back.

Vell looked at me.

"Good. Fourteen."

He counted coins into my palm.

Fourteen copper.

Small enough to not change my life.

Large enough to feel earned in shoulder muscle.

"Any road tomorrow?" I asked.

Ossa laughed.

Vell said, "You're back ten minutes."

"I am asking, not leaving."

"No."

"No road or no asking?"

"Both."

Fine.

I went to Lower Rook.

The walk across Carrow felt different for perhaps three streets.

Then ordinary city reclaimed me.

Fish smell near East Market.

Children running between carts.

A man shouting about lamp oil.

Someone had chalked a crude penis on a tax notice.

Civilization.

At Lower Rook, Maud saw the pack.

"Two nights."

"Yes."

"Paid?"

"Yes."

"Mud upstairs, I charge."

"You charge for weather now?"

"If it enters building."

Marr had competition.

I washed boots in courtyard before going up.

My room key felt heavier when I took it from inside pocket.

I opened the door.

Second chair occupied.

Noll asleep in it.

Not sitting.

Asleep.

Head back. Mouth open. One boot off, one on. Brass Spoon apron folded badly across lap.

I stopped.

My first emotion was affection.

Second was property outrage.

"Get out of my chair."

Noll woke violently.

Hand grabbed the spoon beside him like weapon.

He stared.

"Greg."

"Apparently."

He looked at pack.

"Road."

"Returned."

"Good."

He stood.

Then hugged me.

Brief.

Unexpected.

One arm because spoon remained in other hand.

My body froze for half a second before catching up.

I hugged back.

Noll released.

Looked at my face.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You strange."

"You slept in my room."

"Chair."

"Not defense."

He pointed at wall.

"Maud let me."

"Maud does not own my room."

"She does."

Property law defeated again.

Noll had brought a bowl covered with cloth.

He lifted it from floor.

"Soup."

"How long has that been here?"

"Not long."

"Specific."

"Maybe hour."

I ate it anyway.

Potato, leek, small pieces chicken.

Warm enough.

Noll sat in new chair again while I sat on bed because apparently return did not restore hierarchy.

"Road?" he asked.

I told him.

Not everything.

Ditch. Marr. Dog. Mill. Rain. Shoulder collapse. Magic support.

He interrupted at dog.

"Bite?"

I showed bracer dents.

Noll touched them with one finger.

"Bad dog."

"Owner says good dog."

"Owner wrong."

Simple.

At collapse, his expression changed.

"Cart fall?"

"No."

"You?"

"No."

"Then okay."

"It was closer than okay."

"But now okay."

Present tense tyranny.

I ate more soup.

"Jorren?"

"Working."

"Still?"

"Late count."

Of course.

I wanted to see him too.

Wanted the same validation I had sought before leaving.

That embarrassed me less now.

Maybe because road had happened regardless.

"Tavin?"

Noll pointed at table.

A folded packet sat beneath burned seven.

My spoon stopped.

South mark.

Venn.

"When?"

"This morning."

My stomach tightened.

Noll watched.

"Open."

"I know how letters work."

"Then do."

I did not immediately.

Why?

Because road had ended five minutes ago.

Because Tavin could change the shape of evening.

Because I wanted one thing to finish before another began.

Current life did not care.

I finished the soup first.

Noll complained that this was insane.

I agreed.

Then opened the letter.

Tavin's first line read:

**Your letter is terrible. Better.**

I laughed so suddenly I spilled broth on my road trousers.

Maud was going to charge.
