# Rehearsal A50: Better

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

Tavin's letter was three pages long and only emotionally responsible by accident.

I read it twice.

The first time quickly because Noll sat across from me pretending not to watch.

The second time after he left because some sentences became different when nobody else occupied the room.

**Your letter is terrible. Better.**

Then:

**Memory thing sounds bad. I don't know what to do with that yet. If you expected wisdom, you wrote the wrong person.**

Good.

Not comfort disguised as expertise.

**I remember the send-off. You were late. Not missing. Late. You came after Sorn started shouting because I had packed the kettle wrong. You gave me two copper and said it was a loan so I couldn't refuse. I still owe you two. This is why interest remains fictional.**

I stopped there longer.

Late.

Not absent.

My memory had converted missing scene into moral failure because blankness prefers worst explanation.

Current Tavin supplied a different fact.

Young Greg had come.

Late.

With money.

No image arrived.

No sudden recovery.

Just paper.

It still changed something.

**You also cried after three beers and threatened to fight Ressa's brother. He was twice your size. Nobody let you. I am telling you this because your letter sounds like you think you were a mysterious damaged prince. You were mostly nineteen and stupid.**

I laughed.

Then felt embarrassed on behalf of a body I currently owned.

**I don't care if you remember every card hand. I care if you lie because you're scared I will notice. I noticed. Stop.**

There.

Tavin lacked Mara's precision but not force.

**Venn Carrow load is real. I got it. Six days maybe. We leave South Latch in three mornings, weather permitting. If packet beats us, congratulations, you know before I do.**

My chest tightened.

Not maybe anymore.

Tavin coming.

Current Tavin.

Wax-hating, Harret-threatening, kettle-losing Tavin.

Three mornings plus road.

Maybe six days.

Not tomorrow.

Enough time to become unreasonable.

I looked at the field pack still beside door.

West Spur mud drying on straps.

One road ended.

Another person moving toward me.

**Also four copper.**

Fraud persisted.

At bottom he added:

**If you have two chairs, one better not be shit.**

I looked at new chair.

Repair held.

Acceptable.

I slept badly.

Not from danger.

Anticipation.

Worse.

Next morning I went to Jorren's factor yard before deciding to.

Again.

Pattern becoming visible.

He was not counting sacks this time.

He stood on a loading platform with a chalk board while two cart crews argued below him.

"No," Jorren said.

One driver shouted, "Mine was here."

"Your paper was here. Cart wasn't."

"Same slot."

"No."

"Meln said noon."

"It is after noon."

"Bell just rang."

"Exactly."

I stopped outside the yard.

Jorren looked older from twenty yards away.

Not face.

Position.

People waited for his answer.

He did not seem surprised by that.

I felt proud.

Then jealous.

Not because I wanted the job.

Because he had a version of himself forming where I was spectator.

Ugly emotion.

I let it exist.

Jorren saw me after settling the carts.

"Road."

"Returned."

He came down from platform.

"How?"

"Wet."

"Good."

"You sound like Beren."

"Bad?"

"Unclear."

I showed him the bracer bite.

He laughed.

"Dog?"

"Pike."

"You know name."

"Unfortunately."

I told the ditch story.

He listened differently than Noll.

Asked where wheel sat. Asked whether Ossa unloaded. Asked how far shoulder collapsed. At magic support he frowned.

"You practiced moving?"

"Not enough."

"Then why use?"

There it was.

"Because static test held."

"Road not static."

"I noticed."

He looked at my shoulder.

"Hurt?"

"Sore."

"Lera?"

"Why would sword instructor care?"

"She knows shoulders."

Fair.

I almost dismissed it.

Then stopped.

"Maybe."

Jorren nodded.

"Tavin coming," I said.

His face changed immediately.

"When?"

"Maybe six days. Venn load."

He smiled.

"Good."

"You knew him better than I remember."

"Yes."

No softening.

"What should I expect?"

"Tavin."

"Useful."

"He talks more than you."

"Impossible."

"Worse jokes."

"Now you're inventing."

"Steals food."

"Noll already filled that role."

"They'll fight."

Then stopped because I was constructing people around myself again.

"Maybe."

Jorren looked back toward yard.

A clerk waved chalk board.

"Work."

"Go."

I meant it.

Mostly.

He left.

I stayed outside another moment watching him return to the platform.

One driver immediately started arguing again.

Jorren raised one hand.

Driver stopped.

There was authority in that motion I did not remember him having nine days ago.

Good.

Painful.

Both.

I went to Lera.

Not because Jorren told me to.

Because my shoulder hurt and pretending independence by ignoring useful advice was a stupid category.

The drill court smelled like wet sand and sweat.

She saw me wait.

Made me wait longer.

Then came over after the set.

"Road?"

"Yesterday."

"Shoulder."

"Pain?"

"There."

"Less."

"Weak?"

"No."

"Overuse."

I disliked this more than expected.

"Maybe two."

"It is barely injured."

Tavin coming.

Better.
