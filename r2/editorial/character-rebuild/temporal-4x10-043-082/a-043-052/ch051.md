# Rehearsal A51: Venn Yard

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Tavin arrived during a warehouse argument.

This suited him better than a river stair.

Six days after his letter, I was at East Warehouse Row because Vell had found me half a day of load counting while West Spur remained closed.

Not road.

Road adjacent.

Apparently enough to keep me near carts.

Rami had me checking chalk numbers against sacks being transferred from one grain bay to another. Boring, physical, dusty work.

I liked it more than I admitted.

At midday a Venn freight train entered the yard.

Three wagons.

Blue-green marks on canvas.

Drivers shouting for lane space.

One man already arguing with Vell before first wagon stopped.

"South seal says twelve!"

Vell shouted back, "Then bring twelve!"

"We brought twelve!"

"I see eleven!"

The man pointed at wagon two.

"One under canvas."

"Then uncover it before I learn counting wrong."

I smiled.

Tavin's letter had prepared me for seal people.

Then someone jumped from rear wagon.

Lean body.

Brown coat too large through shoulders.

Hair shorter than I expected from nothing.

Wax stain on left cuff.

He landed badly, one foot slipping in grain dust, windmilled once, recovered, and immediately looked around to see who noticed.

I noticed.

He saw me.

Everything stopped.

Not yard.

Yard kept shouting.

Me.

Tavin stared.

I knew him.

Not memory exactly.

Recognition lived somewhere lower than retrieval.

Face matched pieces that had no full scene attached.

Nose broken slightly left.

Dark eyes.

Scar through one eyebrow I had not known existed until seeing it and suddenly hated myself for not remembering how he got it.

Tavin looked at me for perhaps two seconds.

Then said, "You got tall."

I laughed.

Wrong response maybe.

Could not stop.

"You didn't."

His face broke.

Smile.

There.

He crossed the lane quickly.

I expected a joke.

Instead he hit me.

Not hard.

Fist into shoulder.

The sore one.

"Ow."

"Good."

Then hugged me.

Both arms.

No permission process.

Current Tavin chose it.

My body answered before thought.

I hugged back.

He smelled like road, wax, old wool, and smoke.

Something tightened in my throat.

I closed eyes.

No memory returned.

Didn't matter for three seconds.

Then Tavin stepped back.

"You look terrible."

"I bathed two days ago."

"Worse."

"You smell like seals."

"Fuck seals."

Good.

Vell shouted from across yard.

"Tavin! Twelve!"

Tavin closed eyes.

"Work."

"Go."

He turned.

Then looked back at me.

"Don't leave."

There.

My chest reacted stupidly.

"I'm working here."

"Of course you are."

He went back to wagon.

I returned to sacks.

For twenty minutes we existed in same yard doing separate jobs.

This was unexpectedly difficult.

I kept looking.

Tavin climbed wagon, cut seal, checked marks, argued with driver, crawled halfway beneath canvas, came out carrying a small crate while swearing about manifests.

Current work-self.

Not young Greg memory.

Not letter voice.

A man who knew where to put his hands on freight.

I wanted to watch all of it.

Rami caught me missing a chalk number.

"Greg."

"Sorry."

"Friend?"

"Yes."

"Then count friend after sacks."

Reasonable cruelty.

I counted.

At one point wagon two shifted because brake block slipped.

A loader shouted.

Wheel rolled six inches.

Tavin jumped down and kicked a chock beneath rim while another driver hauled brake lever.

Fast.

Ordinary.

I took one step toward them before stopping.

They had it.

Tavin looked at me afterward.

Saw the stopped step.

Raised eyebrow.

I shrugged.

He smiled.

Maybe he knew what that meant.

Maybe not.

By second bell, Venn transfer was done.

Twelve packages existed after all. One had been under canvas exactly where driver said.

Vell apologized by not apologizing.

Tavin collected travel paper from his crew lead, a square woman named Sorn who looked younger than I expected from letters and more hostile than imagination.

"Release until first bell tomorrow," she told him.

"Carrow yard?"

"Here. Don't be late."

"I am never late."

Sorn looked at me.

"Is that true?"

Tavin said, "He has no usable memory."

I choked.

Sorn stared.

Tavin smiled.

"Joke. Mostly."

"I dislike you," I said.

"Current evidence."

Sorn walked away.

I stared at him.

"You cannot say that to strangers."

"She isn't stranger. She hates me professionally."

"That doesn't improve it."

"Did she believe me?"

"I don't know."

"Then mystery."

Tavin picked up a small travel bag.

"Lower Rook?"

"Yes."

"Two chairs?"

"Yes."

"Good."

We walked.

Carrow had never felt more observed.

Tavin looked at everything.

Not tourist exactly.

Someone returning to city he knew but did not live in now.

"Fish seller still here?"

"Which?"

"Old woman with tooth."

"That narrows nothing."

"One tooth."

"Still nothing."

He looked disappointed in urban continuity.

At East Market he stopped at a bread stall and bought a sweet roll.

Then another.

Handed one to me.

"Four copper?"

"Interest."

"This cost one."

"Remaining three compound."

"Fraud."

We ate walking.

At Lower Rook, Maud looked at Tavin once and said, "No."

He stopped.

"I haven't done anything."

"You look like trouble."

"Greg lives here."

"Exactly."

I loved her.

"Guest one night," I said.

"Floor?"

"Chair maybe."

Tavin looked at me.

"You said two chairs."

"I didn't promise bed."

Maud charged one copper guest fee and made him write name.

Tavin wrote it badly on purpose.

She smacked his hand with charcoal stick.

Current relationship formed efficiently.

Upstairs, he entered my room and stopped.

Exactly like Jorren.

Two chairs.

Table.

Burned seven.

Mara's note beneath key.

Field pack.

Sword hooks.

A life.

Tavin looked longer than I wanted.

"You really did buy two."

"Five copper."

"Robbed."

"Everyone says that."

He put his bag in new chair.

Again.

Why did nobody respect seating?

Then he saw burned seven.

His hand stopped.

"You kept that?"

"Apparently."

"You remember?"

"Pieces."

Tavin picked it up.

The burned edge had darkened my table before.

He turned card between fingers.

"You cheated."

"Mara says you cheated."

"Mara lies when history benefits her."

"Noll says you cheated."

"Noll was twelve."

"Jorren?"

"Jorren cheats now."

Disputed history remained disputed.

Good.

Tavin put card down.

Then saw Mara's note.

He did not touch it.

"Mara?"

"Yes."

"How bad?"

"What?"

"You. About her."

I stared.

"That question assumes condition."

"So bad."

"We are seeing each other."

Tavin's eyebrows rose.

"Again?"

There.

Word landed.

"Again?"

His expression changed.

He realized.

Too late.

"Shit."

"Tavin."

"You really don't remember."

"No."

Silence.

Outside someone dragged a bucket down hall.

Tavin sat in old chair slowly.

"You and Mara were..."

He stopped.

"What?"

"Complicated."

"Everyone uses that word."

"Because you make simple things expensive."

"Specific."

He rubbed one hand over face.

"You liked her. She liked you. You fought. You stopped talking for a month. Then talked too much. Then she left Halden and you acted like it was fine."

"Was it?"

"No."

My chest tightened.

Old Greg supplied nothing useful.

"Why fight?"

"I don't know whole thing. She said you tried to decide for her. You said she already decided without you. Both complained to me separately. Worst week of my life."

That sounded plausible enough to hurt.

"Did we sleep together?"

Tavin's face became delighted.

"Finally useful question."

"Tavin."

"No idea."

"You just looked happy."

"Because you asked like human."

I threw cushion at him.

He caught it.

"There he is."

I hated how much that line affected me.

Door opened.

Noll walked in carrying pot.

He stopped.

Tavin stood.

They looked at each other.

Recognition happened before introduction.

"Noll," Tavin said.

"Tavin."

Then Noll punched him in chest.

Not hard.

Tavin laughed and grabbed back of Noll's neck.

They hugged badly.

Another relationship I did not own.

I stood beside bed watching two people from my life remember each other while I had to learn the room in real time.

It hurt.

It also felt good.

Both again.

Noll looked at me.

"Food."

Of course.

We ate stew from three bowls because Noll had brought his own from downstairs.

Inventory problem temporarily solved.

Tavin told Venn stories.

Noll told Brass Spoon stories.

They interrupted each other.

I learned Tavin had once worked two weeks in a fish yard with Noll's cousin.

I learned Noll remembered Tavin stealing onions.

I learned neither considered these facts important enough to mention before today.

At some point Jorren arrived after work.

No invitation from me.

Noll had apparently sent a runner.

Supporting characters forming logistics without protagonist approval.

Four people.

Two chairs.

Bed.

Floor.

Tavin laughed when he saw Jorren's green patch.

Jorren insulted his red scarf.

Cards appeared.

The burned seven stayed on table beside deck.

I lost three hands.

Tavin cheated at least once.

I could not prove it.

At midnight, Maud banged ceiling with broom and shouted for us to shut up.

Tavin whispered, "Still terrifying."

"You knew her?"

"Everybody knew Maud."

Of course.

I lay on bed later while Tavin slept on floor using his coat as pillow.

Jorren had gone home.

Noll next door.

Room crowded with breathing.

I stared at ceiling.

No recovered send-off.

No solved childhood.

Current Tavin snored softly enough to prove letters had not invented him.

That was enough for the night.
