# Rehearsal A52: First Bell

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Tavin left at first bell and nobody behaved correctly about it.

He woke before me.

This was offensive because he had spent half the night talking.

When I opened my eyes, he was already dressed except boots, sitting on the floor beside my table with one of Noll's leftover dumplings in his hand.

"That's mine."

"You were asleep."

"Property survives unconsciousness."

"Debatable."

He ate it.

Fraud expanded.

Outside, morning rain tapped window.

Not enough to delay Venn.

Probably.

I sat up.

My shoulder felt better. Road soreness remained in legs. Tavin's presence had made the room feel temporarily different, like furniture remembered another version of me.

"How long Carrow?"

"We unload today. Maybe load river cloth tomorrow. South after."

"So one more night?"

"Maybe."

I disliked maybe.

Training continued.

Tavin tied boots.

"You working?"

"Not until afternoon."

"Come yard."

I almost said yes before he finished.

Then remembered Bell Tile route check at midday.

Dena had asked yesterday.

Not urgent.

Still a promise.

"I can until second bell."

Tavin looked at me.

"You have schedule."

"Apparently."

"Terrible."

"You work freight."

"Different."

Of course.

We went downstairs.

Maud charged Tavin one copper for breakfast and another because he took two eggs.

"That is just buying food," he said.

"Yes," Maud said.

He stared at me as if I should intervene.

I did not.

At East Warehouse Row, Venn crew was already opening canvas.

Sorn saw Tavin.

Then me.

"You again."

"I live here."

"Unfortunate."

I liked her now.

Tavin worked.

I helped only where Vell or Sorn assigned me.

That distinction mattered because yesterday I had been Rami's counter. Today I was a spare body between two companies with different papers.

Sorn gave me a hook and told me to pull canvas loops from wagon pegs.

Simple task.

Physical.

Rain made rope slick.

One loop stuck.

I yanked harder.

Peg released suddenly and hook came toward my face.

I turned enough that iron grazed cheek instead of nose.

Tavin laughed from wagon bed.

"Legend."

"Work."

"I am."

I rubbed cheek.

No blood.

Current body continued accumulating small insults.

The first wagon carried sealed barrels of dye oil.

Second held machine parts wrapped in straw.

Third mostly empty except three crates from South Latch merchants.

One crate had cracked in transit.

Sorn found it before Vell.

She crouched, pressed wood edge, then pointed at stain on canvas below.

"Leak."

Everyone moved.

Not panic.

Fast organization.

Barrel? No.

Crate contained ceramic ink jars. One broken inside, black fluid seeping through slats.

Tavin climbed down with absorbent sand bag.

Vell shouted for receiving clerk.

I stood useless for two seconds.

Then saw black line moving toward wagon edge where rain could carry it into yard drain.

"Drain."

Sorn looked.

"Block it."

I grabbed a folded scrap canvas and jammed it around drain grate thirty feet away while another worker shoveled sawdust around leak path.

Rainwater pooled.

Black stain stopped short.

No magic.

No sword.

Just canvas and sawdust.

Tavin and Sorn opened crate carefully.

One jar shattered.

Two chipped.

The rest intact.

Receiving clerk immediately argued damage ownership.

Vell argued louder.

Tavin looked at me across wagon and rolled eyes.

Current friendship included shared hatred of accounting strangers.

We worked through second bell.

My Bell Tile commitment approached.

I kept checking sky and yard clock.

Tavin noticed.

"Go."

"They're not finished."

"You are."

"I can stay fifteen."

"Then be late somewhere else."

Annoying.

I wanted to stay because Tavin was here.

Because he might leave tomorrow.

Because six days of anticipation had produced less than twenty-four hours of actual time.

I also wanted to keep the thing I had told Dena I would do.

Old Greg would have optimized priority by consequence.

Bell Tile route check could happen without me.

Tavin unload could happen without me.

Both low consequence.

Current Greg had to choose based on what kind of person he wanted to be.

Too large a framing for one morning.

Dena asked first.

I put hook down.

"I'm going."

Tavin nodded like this was obvious.

That irritated me.

"You could pretend disappointed."

"Why?"

"For relationship texture."

"You're insane."

Better.

I left.

Walk to Bell Tile took twenty-five minutes fast.

Rain strengthened halfway.

I arrived wet from knee down, cheek bruised from hook, and three minutes before Dena.

She looked at me.

"Road?"

"Warehouse."

"Worse."

We checked filtration route with Pellin and new helper Cessa.

Not crisis.

Three customer stops.

First had a cracked seal from being overtightened.

Second complained about service time.

Third had no problem and tried to invent one to justify discount.

Cessa handled more than I did.

At second stop, a laundry owner argued with her about residue in basin.

I knew residue pattern was soap, not filtration.

Started to say so.

Cessa held up one hand without looking at me.

"Let me."

I stopped.

She scraped residue with fingernail, smelled it, asked owner what soap changed.

Same conclusion.

Owner accepted because Cessa asked before asserting.

I felt proud.

Then slightly useless.

Good combination maybe.

We returned to Bell Tile in late afternoon.

Arlo was shouting about kiln space.

Ivena was shouting less loudly and therefore winning.

Dena handed route notes to clerk.

Life everywhere.

Tavin did not own day merely by visiting.

That was probably healthy.

I hated health.

At Lower Rook, he was not in my room.

My stomach dropped before I checked table.

A note sat beneath burned seven.

**Venn yard until dark. Brass Spoon after. Noll said food. Don't become dramatic.**

I laughed.

Then became dramatic by going to Brass Spoon early.

I told myself I was hungry.

Technically true.

The restaurant at dinner rush was heat, steam, shouting, knives, bowls, servers moving through narrow gaps at speeds that should require licensing.

I sat at counter because no table.

Noll saw me from kitchen pass.

"Early."

"Hungry."

His face said liar.

I resented literacy.

Tavin arrived forty minutes later with wax on both sleeves and a shallow cut across one knuckle.

I saw cut immediately.

"What?"

"Crate nail."

"Clean?"

"Sorn poured spirits on it while insulting me."

"Good."

"You sound old."

That landed strangely.

I looked at him.

Tavin noticed.

"Sorry."

"No. Fine."

Was I old?

Yes.

No.

Both.

We ate dumplings.

Noll brought one extra plate without charging us, then disappeared back into kitchen before gratitude could become conversation.

Tavin watched him work.

"He's good."

"Yes."

"Was weird kid."

"Still weird."

"Different."

There.

Current Tavin encountering current Noll.

At some point Jorren arrived in work coat, not road gear.

He sat beside us and complained about a driver who tried to count broken sacks as delivered units.

Tavin argued freight law.

Jorren argued factor practice.

They disagreed for fifteen minutes while I ate.

Neither asked me to resolve.

Excellent.

Then a commotion near door.

Four people entered wearing Gold Guild tabs.

Not ceremony.

Just dinner.

I noticed before anyone else at our counter because old habits recognized equipment.

One woman had a staff with three modified grip bands and a collapsible head. A broad man carried paired short shields strapped flat against pack. Another wore a coat reinforced at one shoulder in a way designed for repeated magical recoil. Fourth had no visible weapon except a narrow case at hip and boots too expensive for mud.

Gold.

Actual Gold party.

They moved through crowded room without colliding once.

Not flashy.

The staff woman shifted left before a server turned. Shield man lifted one bowl from counter edge before someone bumped it, set it back without looking. Recoil coat chose wall seat automatically. Case-hip took chair with sightline to door.

Coordination lived in ordinary movement.

My attention locked.

Tavin saw.

"Know them?"

"No."

"Then why face?"

Jorren followed my gaze.

"Gold."

"Yes."

Noll came from kitchen carrying their water.

He did not care.

Or cared professionally.

Same thing here.

The Gold party ordered stew, rice, two vegetable plates, no alcohol.

One member took off glove revealing burn scars across fingers.

Another complained about wet socks.

Elite people still had feet.

I wanted to listen.

Not because fame.

Because their movement triggered Old Greg memory of formations I could not place.

Not these people.

Patterns.

Spacing.

Silent hand signals.

People trusting each other before danger arrived.

I felt longing so sharp it surprised me.

Tavin looked at me.

"You want that."

I could have denied.

"Yes."

Jorren said nothing.

That was worse.

"Not now," I added.

Tavin smiled.

"Didn't ask."

I hated him.

The Gold party finished dinner in twenty minutes.

They left.

No recruitment.

No destiny.

Just four people who knew how to move together.

Afterward Noll put their empty bowls in front of me.

"Wash."

"Why me?"

"You staring. Pay rent."

Apparently aspiration had consequences.

I washed Gold-ranked bowls in Brass Spoon kitchen while Tavin laughed from counter.

Better ending than prophecy.
