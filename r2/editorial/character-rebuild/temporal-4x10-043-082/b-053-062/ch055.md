# Rehearsal B55: The Orchard Road

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The orchard road smelled like wet leaves and animal shit.

Songs had omitted both.

We left the ridge camp after sunrise because everything we owned was damp enough to require negotiation.

Beren spread the canvas over three bushes while feeding the mules. Jorren held one corner when wind kept trying to return it to Carrow. I sat on a rock with my right boot off and inspected the heel blister like a man reviewing an enemy treaty.

Better than last night.

Worse than two days ago.

Salve had softened the skin around it. The raw center remained pink and ugly.

I could walk.

I wanted to walk.

I also wanted to cut the back out of my boot.

Current compromise involved two layers of cloth and a strip of leather Beren produced from somewhere beneath the driver's bench.

"You carry a pharmacy?" I asked.

"I carry things people forget until bleeding."

He looked at Jorren.

"Wrap."

Jorren wore Beren's spare oilcloth again.

"Didn't forget. Lost."

"Same result."

I tied the leather pad around my heel.

The boot fit tighter.

Not ideal.

Better than friction.

By the time we moved, sunlight had started breaking through low cloud.

The road remained mud.

Water ran from both ditches and collected in wheel ruts. We walked beside the cart because riding the driver's board while the mules worked uphill felt like an invitation to become morally irritating.

At least that was what Beren said.

I would have walked anyway.

Probably.

The orchard country began around second bell.

First scattered pear trees inside low stone walls.

Then entire hillsides planted in rows, trunks dark with rain, grass between them bright enough to hurt after two days of brown road.

Workers moved through the rows carrying hooked poles.

Too early for harvest.

They were cutting something from branches.

I looked closer.

Small gray bundles tied around limbs.

"What are those?"

Beren glanced over.

"Moth nests."

"Why tied?"

"Farmers tie traps."

"Magic?"

"Bugs."

Could be both.

Old Greg remembered orchard blights farther north. Mana moths that laid eggs in sapwood. Fruit that ripened hollow. Farmers burning whole rows to stop spread.

These traps looked familiar enough to trigger concern and wrong enough to deny it.

"What kind of moth?"

Beren shrugged.

"Gray."

Jorren said, "You ask color after he said gray?"

"Species."

"Gray species."

I hated both of them.

At midday we stopped at a roadside farm that sold feed, cider, and hot onion cakes from a window cut into the wall.

Beren knew the owner.

Of course.

Her name was Tal.

She had one arm to the elbow and used the remaining hand to flip onion cakes on an iron plate with more speed than I could manage with two.

"Beren," she said.

"Tal."

"Wheel still singing?"

"Priest dying slower."

"Should've bought hub from me."

"Yours cost seven."

"Mine works."

"Mine also works."

She looked toward the cart.

"Loud."

Beren accepted this.

Jorren ordered two cakes.

I ordered three.

Road appetite had become expensive.

Tal looked at me.

"New?"

"Greg."

"That answer yes?"

"Probably."

Beren said, "Carrow."

Tal nodded as if that explained my face.

She passed me a cake wrapped in cabbage leaf.

Hot oil soaked through immediately.

I shifted hands.

"Moth traps?" I asked.

Tal's expression changed.

Not alarm.

Annoyance.

"Early this year."

"Mana moth?"

"No."

Too quick.

I looked at the orchard across the road.

"How know?"

Tal flipped another cake.

"Because mana moth glows. These bite."

"They bite people?"

"Everything."

Beren stopped eating.

"Road bad?"

"Not yet."

"Yet?"

Tal pointed with the spatula toward the eastern hills.

"Night swarms near Low Perrin. Keep lamps low. Don't sleep under fruit trees."

I looked at the rows.

"Why lamps?"

"Attract."

"Heat or light?"

Tal stared at me.

"Lamp."

Fair.

Old Greg knew dozens of insects attracted to mana flame, heat, ordinary flame, blood, fear pheromones, stupidity, and one species attracted specifically to copper jewelry.

I wanted classification.

Tal wanted to sell cakes.

"Any deaths?"

"Moths?"

"Yes."

"No."

"Animals?"

"Two hens. One goat got eye swollen."

Beren resumed eating.

Road-danger scale recalibrated.

"We camp past Low Perrin?" Jorren asked.

"If road good."

Tal said, "Don't."

Beren looked at her.

"Why?"

"I said moths."

"Moths don't eat wagons."

"No, but Fenn's south ditch washed. Road narrows near orchard wall. Your load heavy. Sleep here or push to Wren Post."

"Wren adds two hours."

"Then sleep here."

Beren thought.

I watched.

This was his road.

He knew Tal.

He knew the wheel.

He knew what two hours meant for mules.

Old Greg memory wanted to contribute.

Low Perrin should have a broad grass verge before the stone wall.

Or had one decades later.

Could be enough space.

I almost said so.

Then remembered the repaired bridge.

The new toll hut.

The flood route.

I ate cake.

Beren said, "Wren."

Tal nodded.

Decision made.

We left with a small sack of feed and one bottle of cider Beren claimed was for camp morale.

The road narrowed after the farm.

Orchard walls came closer.

The sun returned fully, turning wet branches bright.

Workers had hung strips of dull metal between some trees. They flashed in wind.

Moth deterrent maybe.

Or birds.

Or aesthetics.

I forced myself not to ask every person we passed.

This became harder when a girl in a red scarf ran from an orchard row waving both arms.

"Cover lamps!"

We had no lamps lit.

Beren stopped the cart.

"What?"

She pointed uphill.

A gray cloud moved above the orchard.

Not weather.

Too low.

Too fast.

Thousands of small bodies changed shape together between tree rows.

Moths.

Maybe.

The swarm turned toward the road.

The mules noticed before we did.

Both ears went flat.

Left mule stamped.

Beren grabbed the lead.

"Eyes. Cover eyes."

Jorren moved to the right mule.

I pulled my rain wrap from the pack.

"Blanket better," Beren said.

I grabbed the wool blanket tied beneath the driver's board.

The swarm hit the nearest orchard row.

Leaves shook.

Workers shouted.

Metal strips flashed wildly.

Then gray insects crossed the stone wall.

Not large individually.

Thumb-length bodies.

Broad dusty wings.

Dark hooked mouthparts.

One struck my cheek.

Sharp sting.

"Fuck."

I slapped it away.

Another hit my coat.

The mules started pulling sideways.

Beren shouted, "Hold head!"

I threw the blanket over the left mule's face and neck.

Bad idea.

The animal panicked harder at sudden darkness.

It reared.

Lead line ripped through Beren's hand.

I caught the bridle near the cheekpiece.

The mule jerked its head.

My shoulder snapped forward.

Pain.

Not injury yet.

"Easy!"

Meaningless word.

The animal could not hear philosophy over wings.

Moths struck the blanket and crawled beneath edges.

One landed on my glove and bit through enough to pinch skin.

I crushed it against the bridle.

Dust smeared gray.

Jorren had the other mule backed against the cart pole, oilcloth over its eyes instead of blanket.

Smarter.

Beren took the left lead again.

"Don't pull straight! Side!"

I changed grip.

The mule dragged me two steps.

My bad heel hit a rut.

Pain shot up leg.

I kept hold.

Thought arrived late.

Magic.

Old Greg had animal-calming techniques.

Not spells exactly.

Mana pressure low, steady, broad enough to reduce startle in trained mounts.

These mules were not trained for me.

Still.

I opened sense.

The swarm became a thousand tiny mana disturbances.

Not magical creatures strongly.

But each wing carried trace residue.

Interesting.

Wrong time.

I pushed a low calming pulse through the hand on the bridle.

The mule flinched harder.

Beren shouted, "What did you do?"

"Nothing."

Lie.

The animal's eye rolled white beneath the blanket edge.

Mana pulse had added another unfamiliar sensation.

Idiot.

I stopped immediately.

Physical hold only.

Voice low.

Side pressure like Beren said.

The swarm thickened for perhaps thirty seconds.

Then moved on.

Just moved.

Across road.

Into the lower orchard.

Gray bodies left on mud.

Some crushed beneath hooves.

My face stung in three places.

Jorren had a bite near his ear.

Beren's right hand bled where the lead had burned skin.

The mules trembled beneath our hands.

No one died.

No goat eye.

Still enough.

The girl in the red scarf came over carrying a smoking bundle of green branches.

"Hold this near nose."

Beren took it.

Sharp herbal smoke rose around the mule.

The animal settled faster than it had under my mana.

I stared.

"What is that?"

"Wormleaf."

"Why?"

"Moths hate."

"Mules?"

"Also hate. Less."

Practical hierarchy.

She gave Jorren another smoking bundle.

I looked at the dead moth on my glove.

Trace mana residue.

Could be diet.

Could be orchard treatment.

Could be why my calming pulse felt wrong to the mule.

I had no idea.

The girl saw me inspecting it.

"Don't keep."

"Why?"

"Eggs."

I dropped it immediately.

She laughed.

Current expertise humiliated efficiently.

Beren looked at me.

"Nothing?"

I knew what he meant.

"I tried calming mana."

His face went flat.

"Without asking."

"Yes."

"On my mule."

"Yes."

"Don't."

"I know."

"Now."

There it was.

Not anger because the technique failed.

Anger because I used his animal as an experiment under pressure.

I wanted to explain that Old Greg had used the method hundreds of times.

That I stopped immediately.

That I was trying to prevent injury.

All true.

None answered ownership.

"Sorry," I said.

Beren looked at me for another second.

Then at his bleeding hand.

"Wrap that."

He meant my face or his hand?

Jorren handed him cloth.

His hand.

Fine.

We moved again twenty minutes later.

The mules remained jumpy.

The road narrowed exactly where Tal warned.

Fenn's south ditch had eaten half the verge beneath the orchard wall.

Beren drove with both hands despite the rope burn.

I walked farther from the left mule than before.

Not punishment.

Appropriate.

My cheek swelled around one bite.

Jorren looked at it.

"Pretty."

"Your ear is worse."

"Can't see."

"Trust me."

"No."

Fair.

We reached Wren Post after sunset.

Two extra hours became three because the mules refused speed whenever insects touched them.

The post was a roadside inn and stable built around a square yard.

Warm light.

Cook smoke.

Actual roof.

I wanted it with a depth that made road romance embarrassing.

We paid for stable space and one shared sleeping room.

Four beds.

The fourth occupied by a traveling cloth merchant who snored before we finished removing boots.

I washed moth dust from my face.

The bites looked worse in polished metal than they felt.

Beren's hand had a red line across palm similar to rope burn.

He cleaned it quietly.

I sat on the edge of my bed.

"I was wrong," I said.

He looked over.

"About?"

"Mana. Mule."

"Yes."

"I thought it would calm."

"Did not."

"I know."

Beren tied his bandage with his teeth.

"Road makes you know after sometimes."

That sentence irritated me because it sounded like a chapter ending.

Then the cloth merchant snored loud enough to rattle the wall.

Jorren threw a sock at him.

Missed.

I laughed until my swollen cheek hurt.
