# Rehearsal B57: Kelm Yard

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Kelm smelled like flour, wet stone, and horse piss.

I liked it immediately.

This was not endorsement of smell.

It was relief at arriving somewhere that did not resemble memory cleanly enough to become trap.

Old Greg knew Kelm later as a junction town with three paved lanes and a north guild annex.

Current Kelm had one paved square, no annex visible, and a west gate leaning slightly inward as if embarrassed by engineering.

Beren drove us through after second bell.

Jorren sat beside him. I walked because blister had finally stopped worsening and I distrusted success.

Town traffic thickened fast.

Grain carts. Stone wagons. Two drovers moving pigs through a market lane while everyone cursed them. A boy carrying six loaves on board above head. Women with laundry baskets stepping between wheels like mortality was administrative inconvenience.

Our customer was Kelm Mill Cooperative, south yard.

The millstone delivery itself had happened in Beren's prior trip in accepted Chapter 42 history? No, this horizon started from 42 and chose a fresh freight road, so current cargo here was hardware and return account materials, not old stone. Horizon facts remained local.

Beren had brought three crates of mill fittings plus one sealed packet from Carrow factor.

South yard foreman named Rell met us with chalk behind one ear and no patience.

"Late."

Beren said, "Road."

Rell looked at my face.

"Moths?"

"Apparently my face is route documentation."

"Gray orchard?"

"Yes."

"Then late acceptable."

Beren smiled like he had won arbitration.

Unloading began immediately.

No heroic arrival.

Crates onto low trolley. Trolley across cobbles. Each package opened and checked against paper.

I carried.

Jorren carried.

Beren handled signatures because hand injury made lifting worse and because he owned freight relationship.

Second crate had a bent hinge.

Rell blamed road.

Beren blamed old crate.

They argued while I held lid.

Inside fittings intact.

Argument continued anyway because principle had become self-propelled.

I looked at Jorren.

He shrugged.

"Road."

Disease indeed.

At noon Rell gave us mill meal free: thick bread, cheese, onions, weak beer.

We ate on loading dock while mill ran behind us.

Floor vibrated continuously.

I could feel it through boots.

Mana sense caught faint rhythm in stone.

Not magic.

Mechanical vibration with trace charge from turning mineral.

Old Greg had once used similar resonance to detect buried chambers.

My attention drifted.

Rell noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You staring floor."

"It vibrates."

"Mill."

"I know."

"Then why stare?"

No answer worth giving.

I ate onion.

After unload, Beren was supposed to collect two return crates of brass shims and one cask of damaged toolheads for Carrow rework.

Only one crate ready.

The rest delayed until tomorrow.

Beren swore at clerk.

Clerk said paperwork from north office missing.

Beren said paper had been sent.

Clerk said paper had not arrived.

This was dangerous because everyone was correct within own evidence.

One-night schedule became two.

Then three maybe.

I felt excitement.

Immediately felt guilty because delay cost Beren time.

Road desire and other people's economics rarely aligned cleanly.

"We get held pay?" Jorren asked.

Good question.

Beren looked at clerk.

"Yes."

Clerk frowned.

"Contract says delay after delivery."

"This is after delivery."

"Return optional."

"Return on sheet."

Paper appeared.

Argument moved inside office.

I was not invited.

Jorren and I waited beside cart.

"You like this," he said.

"What?"

"Delay."

I stared.

"No."

"Face."

Slander epidemic.

"I like being somewhere else."

"Same."

"Not same."

"Why?"

Because delay meant more road without choosing more road.

Because responsibility belonged to paperwork, not desire.

Because then I could want staying without admitting I wanted it.

Unattractive insight.

"Complicated."

Jorren nodded.

"Bad profession."

Vaska's phrase crossed horizons in writer memory, not character. I should kill that line here to avoid contamination.

Instead Jorren said, "You always make it hard."

Better.

Beren returned with six copper held-time approved if second crate not ready by evening.

He looked unhappy.

"Money," I said.

"Not enough."

"Still."

"Lost road day costs more later."

There.

Time priced differently depending on whose contract.

We took a cheap room above yard tavern.

Three beds, no fourth merchant.

I washed clothes in basin and hung them from window until owner shouted because water dripped onto sign below.

Current Kelm had regulations.

Afternoon free.

Dangerous.

Jorren wanted market.

Beren wanted sleep.

I wanted to walk town alone.

I did.

Not far.

South yard to paved square. Square to river. River north along warehouses.

I kept expecting remembered Kelm landmarks.

Most absent.

One stone arch existed where memory expected timber bridge.

Wrong direction perhaps.

One tavern name familiar, `Copper Wheel`, but current building smaller and sign painted green, not red.

I stood across street too long.

A woman sweeping steps said, "You coming in?"

"Maybe."

"Then decide before winter."

I went in.

No memory hit.

Good.

I bought cider.

At back table four adventurers played dice.

Bronze tabs.

One missing two fingers. One with healing band around neck. They talked about a rat job beneath tannery and how client lied about water depth.

No destiny.

Current low-rank life.

I listened without staring.

Mostly.

Then shouting outside.

Everyone looked.

A cart horse had slipped on wet paving near square.

Animal on side, harness tangled, cart angled across lane.

People ran.

I moved too.

Not because adventurer.

Because body moved.

Horse kicked wildly.

Driver on ground holding reins, shouting not to approach rear.

One shaft had splintered and pressed against animal flank.

A market woman tried to lift it alone.

"Stop," driver yelled.

I came from front.

"What do you need?"

"Head down. Cut right trace. Lift shaft after."

Clear ownership.

I knelt near horse's neck, not touching yet.

"Can I hold bridle?"

Driver nodded.

I took leather close to bit.

Horse's eye huge.

Breath hot.

Old calming mana offered itself.

No.

Not without permission.

I used voice only.

"Easy. Easy."

Useless word maybe.

Still something.

Driver cut trace with knife.

Two men lifted broken shaft.

Horse tried to rise too soon.

I kept head turned enough to prevent rolling into crowd.

"Wait!"

Driver pressed shoulder.

Animal settled.

Then on count, everyone moved back and horse struggled upright.

One hind leg shook.

No obvious fracture.

Driver checked.

Crowd dispersed almost immediately once danger became work.

I stood with mud on knees.

The Bronze adventurers from tavern watched from doorway.

Two-finger man lifted cup toward me.

Not applause.

Acknowledgment.

I went back in and finished cider.

No one asked rank.

Good.

At dusk I found Jorren in market carrying a new belt.

"Why?"

"Old cracked."

"How much?"

"Seven."

"Robbed."

"You own five-copper chair."

Argument ended.

Beren woke before dinner.

Second crate still not ready.

Six copper held pay triggered.

He became slightly less angry.

We ate stew in yard tavern.

Jorren showed belt.

Beren said robbed.

Thank you.

Before bed, north office runner finally arrived with missing paper.

Toolhead cask would be ready at first bell.

Departure tomorrow.

I felt disappointment.

There.

Clear.

I wanted another day.

Not because work.

Because I had barely learned Kelm smell.

I did not manufacture reason to stay.

That mattered enough without saying it.

I went upstairs and packed.
