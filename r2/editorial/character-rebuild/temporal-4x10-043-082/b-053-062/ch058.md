# Rehearsal B58: The Wash

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

We left Kelm with a cask that rattled.

This was not ideal cargo behavior.

Beren stopped before gate and made us unload it again.

Damaged toolheads had been packed loose enough to strike each other inside. Each bump sounded like a drawer full of knives being dropped down stairs.

"Who packed?" Beren asked.

Kelm clerk pointed at yard boy.

Yard boy pointed at cask.

Strong defense.

We opened lid.

Iron heads wrapped individually in straw but not wedged.

Beren swore.

Jorren said, "Can pack."

We did.

Twenty minutes stuffing old sackcloth between pieces. I used one of my spare cloths and immediately regretted the sacrifice.

Road had begun consuming possessions.

At gate, cask quiet.

Better.

South of Kelm road followed river for several miles before climbing west ridge.

Morning cold. Clear sky. Ground still saturated from previous rain.

My heel blister had formed new skin.

Moth bites itched viciously.

I did not scratch.

Mostly.

Jorren drove first while Beren rested hand.

He had improved.

Or mule had lowered expectations.

At river bend we found water across road.

Not flood exactly.

A side wash had jumped ditch and spread shallow sheet from hillside to river.

Three inches near upper edge.

Maybe eight at lower.

Current moved diagonally across packed surface.

Cart ahead crossed slowly.

We watched rear wheels drift one inch downstream before catching.

Beren said, "Walk."

We walked section first.

Water cold enough to numb boots.

Road surface beneath mostly stone.

One soft patch middle.

Beren poked with staff.

"Okay."

Jorren said, "Cart?"

"Yes."

I wanted to ask about unloading cask.

Weight low and modest.

No need.

I kept quiet.

Beren took driver board despite hand.

Jorren and I walked downstream side ready to push if wheel slipped.

"Don't stand behind wheel," Beren said.

"I know."

"Then stop drifting there."

I moved.

Apparently body still learning cart geometry.

The mule entered water reluctantly.

Front wheels crossed first stream line.

Cart shifted.

We pushed upstream side.

Cold water climbed to mid-calf.

At soft patch, right wheel sank.

Not deep.

Enough.

Mule strained.

Cart stopped.

"Hold!" Beren shouted.

He set brake.

Water pressed continuously.

I could feel wheel trying downhill.

Jorren put shoulder to cart rail.

"Push?"

"Not yet."

Beren climbed down.

His injured hand made him slower.

He inspected wheel.

"Stone under."

We needed raise enough to slide flat rock beneath.

Physical lift possible with lever.

Magic possible too.

I did not offer immediately.

Progress.

Beren looked around.

"Greg, find stone. Jorren, rail."

I waded upstream, hands searching under water.

Found flat slab perhaps twenty pounds.

Dragged back.

Jorren had pry rail under axle block.

Beren placed fulcrum rock.

"On three."

We lifted.

Axle rose.

Beren tried shove slab under wheel with boot.

Current pulled it away.

Again.

Second attempt stone caught.

Wheel settled on edge.

"Now."

Mule pulled.

We pushed.

Cart rolled free.

Water slapped knees.

No magic.

I felt ridiculous pride.

Then upstream shout.

Another wagon entered wash too fast.

Driver had seen us cross and decided evidence applied universally.

Two horses, heavier wagon loaded with timber.

Front left wheel hit soft patch harder and dropped nearly to hub.

Horse team jerked sideways.

One animal lost footing.

Chaos.

Beren shouted, "Move ours!"

Our cart still half in wash.

We got it onto dry road fast.

Then turned back.

Timber wagon driver was screaming at horses.

Bad.

One horse down on knee, harness twisted.

Timber load had shifted, three long beams pressing against side stakes.

If stake broke, beams would slide into water and possibly onto animal.

My body saw danger structure immediately.

Old Greg experience useful.

"Stop pulling!" I shouted.

Driver ignored.

Beren shouted louder, "Stop your fucking team!"

He listened to Beren.

Again reputation or volume.

We ran into water.

Jorren went horse heads with driver.

Beren inspected wheel.

I watched timber.

Top beam moved half inch.

Side stake bending.

"Load first!"

Beren looked.

"Yes. Rope."

Our rope.

I sprinted to cart, grabbed coil, returned.

Current around thighs now because I entered lower line.

Nearly fell.

Caught wagon side.

Moth bites irrelevant.

We ran rope over timber bundle and around opposite stake to bind load together.

Need tension.

My ropehouse days suddenly mattered.

I wrapped correct hitch under Beren's eye.

"Tighter."

I pulled.

Healing palm burned.

Ignored.

"Not hand wrap," Beren snapped.

I changed grip before old mistake repeated.

We cinched timber.

Stake still bending but bundle less likely to spill.

Horse on knee thrashed once.

Jorren shouted something low into its ear while driver loosened trace.

Not magic.

Hands.

The driver looked panicked enough to become dangerous.

"What do we do?" he asked Beren.

"Unload two beams."

"In water?"

"Or lose wagon."

He hesitated.

Cost calculation in crisis.

Beren did not wait.

"Greg, Jorren."

We untied top two beams individually and slid them backward.

Each heavy enough to require all three.

Water made footing terrible.

First beam nearly took my thumb against stake.

I yanked hand free.

Second landed in wash with splash and immediately tried float downstream.

Jorren caught end.

"Fuck!"

I grabbed other end.

We dragged it to bank.

Load lighter.

Wagon sat higher.

Horse regained feet after trace loosened.

Wheel still stuck.

Now lever.

Driver had jack screw under seat.

Useful equipment discovered late.

We placed broad wood foot beneath in water, turned screw.

Axle rose slowly.

Beren and driver packed rocks under wheel.

Then everyone pushed.

Two horses pulled.

Wheel climbed.

Wagon lurched forward.

Driver nearly fell from board.

Timber held.

We shouted until wagon reached dry.

Then silence except river.

My breathing loud.

Hands shaking from cold and effort.

Jorren had mud across face.

Beren's bandage soaked pink where palm reopened.

"Hand," I said.

"Later."

"Now."

He looked at me.

I had earned enough to be annoying.

We wrapped hand fresh.

Timber driver stood beside his wagon staring at wash.

"Thank you."

Beren said, "You entered too fast."

No politeness.

"I saw you."

"Different load."

There.

Road evidence local.

Driver looked ashamed.

Good maybe.

No need say.

We waited while water drained from boots.

The two unloaded beams had to be reloaded.

Of course.

Crisis aftermath was lifting same heavy wood you had just saved.

We used wagon's jack and rope to slide them up one at time.

Forty minutes later everyone moved again.

Timber driver offered two silver? No, scale likely copper, so he offered six copper to Beren for help.

Beren took four and divided: two himself, one Jorren, one me.

"Why not six?" I asked after driver left.

"Because road help not extortion."

"You charge."

"Because rope, time, hand."

"Four exactly?"

"Enough."

Economic morality had local shape.

We camped early because wet boots and reopened hand made speed stupid.

Found dry rise beneath fir trees.

I stripped socks and discovered feet wrinkled white.

Jorren laughed.

"Old man feet."

"You have same."

"Mine younger."

Impossible argument.

We hung clothes.

Beren cooked rice with dried meat.

His hand limited chopping, so I did.

Knife work mundane after danger.

I enjoyed that too.

At fire, Beren said, "You did good."

I looked at him.

No qualifier.

"Even magic criminal?"

"Today no magic."

"Exactly."

He understood joke maybe.

Then he added, "Next time don't run lower water."

"I know."

"You nearly fall."

"I caught."

"That is after."

Fair.

I thought about Gold parties again.

Elite teamwork looked like perfect coordination from outside.

Maybe inside it was just thousands of Beren corrections repeated until nobody needed them spoken.

Maybe magic integration came after knowing who owned mule, who owned wheel, who could say stop.

Old Greg remembered result.

Current Greg was learning grammar.

Too polished.

I handed Jorren burned rice from pot edge.

He complained.

Better.
