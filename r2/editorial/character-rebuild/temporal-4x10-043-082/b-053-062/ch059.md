# Rehearsal B59: Night Bells

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Someone screamed after midnight.

Not close.

Close enough.

I woke with sword in hand before I knew where I was.

Fir camp.

Wet boots near fire embers.

Jorren sitting up.

Beren already on feet.

Second scream from road south.

Human.

Then bell.

Small hand bell ringing fast.

Beren grabbed coat.

"Road call."

I did not know what that meant.

Followed anyway.

We left mules tied, cart blocked. Jorren took lantern. I carried sword but kept sheathed once brain caught up.

Bell rang again.

Through trees perhaps quarter mile.

We ran.

Current body complained immediately about cold start.

Heel mostly healed. Legs not.

Road opened ahead under moon broken by cloud.

A small passenger cart lay on side in ditch.

One horse loose, reins trailing.

Another trapped in harness against overturned shaft.

Three people.

Woman in night coat ringing bell.

Man kneeling beside cart.

Child crying somewhere under canvas.

"Here!"

Woman waved.

Beren reached first.

"How many?"

"Four. Driver, me, two boys."

"Driver?"

Man kneeling shouted, "Here."

He was driver, blood at scalp.

One boy visible beside him, maybe ten, holding arm.

Other inside overturned cart.

"Breathing?" I asked.

"Talking," woman said.

From inside came muffled, "I'm stuck!"

Good.

We moved.

Beren took horse problem.

Jorren went driver and visible boy.

I went cart.

No assignment spoken.

Maybe obvious.

Canvas side pinned against ditch bank. Rear opening upward, six feet off ground due cart angle.

I climbed wheel spokes carefully.

"Don't move cart," Beren called.

"Not planning."

Inside smelled like damp wool and spilled apples.

Lantern from Jorren gave enough light.

Boy wedged between bench and luggage trunk, maybe seven.

Face wet with tears.

"Hey."

"My leg."

"What's your name?"

"Perrin."

"Perrin, I'm Greg. Can you wiggle toes?"

"What?"

"Your foot. Move toes."

He did.

Good sign.

Bench edge trapped shin, not crushing fully.

Trunk had shifted against bench, preventing lift.

Could move trunk.

Maybe.

I climbed farther in.

Cart creaked.

Stopped.

Weight distribution dangerous.

"Beren."

"What?"

"Need stabilize before I shift trunk."

He looked from horse.

"Jorren, rope."

They ran line from upper axle to tree uphill.

Beren used cart rope, knot fast despite injured hand.

Jorren tensioned around trunk.

"Now."

I moved inside.

Perrin grabbed my sleeve.

"Don't leave."

"I'm not."

Words came easier because body had task.

I pushed trunk.

Heavy.

Current angle made it slide toward me.

I braced shoulder.

Old magic offered static plane.

Could support trunk independent.

This time no ownership issue. Human trapped. Need immediate.

I set mana under trunk edge.

Pressure small.

Enough to hold while I shifted grip.

Then pushed sideways.

Trunk moved two inches.

Bench lifted fraction.

"Pull leg."

Boy tried.

"Hurts!"

"I know. Again."

He pulled.

Shin came free.

I released plane.

Cart creaked again.

"Out."

I lifted boy under arms.

He screamed when leg moved.

Not broken maybe.

Could be.

I climbed backward with him.

At opening, Jorren reached up.

Took weight.

We lowered.

Woman grabbed child.

"Leg!"

"Don't let him stand."

Driver nodded despite blood.

Beren had freed trapped horse by cutting one trace. Animal stood shaking with scrape along flank.

Loose horse had vanished into dark.

"Why overturned?" Beren asked.

Driver wiped blood.

"Wheel struck wash."

"Which wash?"

He pointed back south.

"Road edge gone. Couldn't see."

Night travel.

No warning marker maybe.

I looked at bell.

"Road call?"

Beren said, "Freight and coach carry bells. Ring if trouble."

Simple mutual aid network.

No guild required.

We spent next hour making situation survivable.

Not fixing cart.

That required daylight, wheel crew.

We righted enough? No, dangerous. Left it secured. Moved passengers to our camp.

Driver could walk but dizzy. Older boy arm bruised. Perrin leg swelling but no deformity.

Woman named Seli kept thanking us until Beren told her to stop and hold lantern.

Useful redirection.

At camp, I made Perrin splint from two flat sticks and cloth, not because expert healer but because old field first aid remained.

I told Seli explicitly, "This is support until healer. He still needs one."

Good boundary without chapter applause.

Jorren found spare blanket.

Beren went back with driver to catch horse? No, too dark. They rang bell twice and waited.

Another freight crew arrived fifteen minutes later.

Then another.

Suddenly camp held twelve people.

Road network materialized from sound.

One new driver knew nearest healer at White Fork north, two hours if fast.

Another had light cart and agreed to take Seli and injured boys immediately.

Money argument occurred.

Seli offered twelve copper.

Driver said six.

Beren said eight because night and return.

They settled eight.

I watched emergency become commerce without shame.

Work cost.

Before departure Perrin grabbed my sleeve again.

"You coming?"

"No."

His face changed.

I almost said something comforting too large.

Instead, "You have your mother. Driver knows healer. We have our cart."

He nodded reluctantly.

Seli looked at me.

"Thank you."

This time I accepted.

Cart left with lanterns bouncing north.

Road quiet returned slowly.

We stood around dying fire.

Beren said, "Sleep."

"What about overturned cart?"

"Morning."

"Loose horse?"

"Morning."

"Driver?"

He sat wrapped in blanket.

"Morning."

Road had limits even after bell.

I lay down.

Could not sleep.

Adrenaline drained into thoughts.

The boy's hand on sleeve.

Trunk pressure.

Mana plane holding.

No argument about whether I should act.

No professional territoriality because problem obvious and ownership assigned by need.

This felt closer to Old Greg than most current work.

Not power.

Night call.

People hurt.

Fast decisions.

That scared me more than comforted.

Because part of me had felt good.

Not about injury.

About clarity.

Clean necessity.

Danger stripped ambiguity away.

Old Greg had lived too long inside that simplification.

Current Greg had been drowning in ambiguity and maybe wanted emergency because it made him easier to himself.

Ugly.

True enough.

Jorren whispered from bedroll, "You awake?"

"Yes."

"Me too."

Silence.

Then:

"Kid okay?"

"Probably."

"Probably bad word."

"Best available."

He accepted.

We slept eventually.

Morning revealed road failure thirty yards south of overturned cart.

A fresh wash had cut outer rut. Driver hit it in dark, wheel dropped, cart rolled.

Beren and other freight crew marked with branches and cloth before anything else.

Then we recovered loose horse from pasture half mile away.

Then righted passenger cart using two teams and ropes.

It took three hours.

No one sang.

Driver discovered axle bent.

Cart stayed roadside awaiting repair.

We left him with another crew heading Kelm.

Our return route south now had new warning.

My body felt exhausted before travel resumed.

Beren looked at me climbing beside cart.

"Still like road?"

I considered lying.

"Yes."

He nodded.

"Then you're stupid enough."

Possibly recruitment standard.
