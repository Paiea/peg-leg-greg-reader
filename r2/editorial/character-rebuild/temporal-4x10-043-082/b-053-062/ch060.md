# Rehearsal B60: Three Wagons

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

By the fifth road day, we had become part of a caravan by accident.

Not formal caravan.

Three wagons simply discovered traveling together was less stupid after the night overturn.

Our cart.

A salt wagon driven by Hessa and her nephew Brin.

A wool wagon driven by old couple Tarl and Mave.

No leader elected.

Beren still became one whenever road narrowed because he had strongest opinions and loudest voice.

Hessa ignored him whenever convenient.

This improved group health.

We moved south toward Carrow under clear weather.

Road drying.

Camp routine emerged.

Brin fetched water because fastest.

Jorren chopped wood badly.

Mave cooked because everyone learned after one Beren attempt.

I checked perimeter first two nights until Tarl asked whether I expected trees to attack.

I stopped performing vigilance and started helping tether.

Progress.

At midday a rider approached from south at speed.

Not messenger colors.

Young woman on foam-flecked horse.

She waved both arms.

"Glasshorn!"

Every wagon stopped.

There.

Actual threat word.

Old Greg memory sharpened.

Glasshorns known from earlier current work, herd behavior, reflective antler ridges, charge if startled or territory compressed.

Rider said herd crossed lower road ahead and scattered after timber crew noise.

"How many?" Beren asked.

"Eight maybe. Two calves. Big bull."

"Where now?"

"Fields both sides."

Bad.

Distributed animals could re-enter road unpredictably.

Hessa said, "Wait."

Beren said, "How long?"

"Until they settle."

Rider shook head.

"Timber crew still there. Trying drive them west."

Everyone swore.

Driving glasshorn herd under stress.

Old Greg knew this could become charge pattern.

I spoke.

"If they push from east, herd may cross road west. Don't approach."

Hessa looked at me.

"You know?"

"Worked glasshorn before."

Current evidence, not future memory.

Good.

Beren asked rider, "Any road wardens?"

"One. Yelling at timber."

"We wait here."

Decision.

I agreed.

My body did not.

I wanted to see.

Wanted data.

Wanted danger at manageable distance.

That urge felt disgusting after last night's child.

Still real.

We pulled wagons into field entrance and waited.

Ten minutes.

Fifteen.

Then distant shouting.

Birds lifted from south trees.

Hessa stood on wheel hub to look.

"Movement."

First glasshorn appeared around bend.

Not charging.

Trotting.

Large deer-like body, thick shoulders, pale mineral growth along antlers catching sunlight.

Then two more.

They crossed road fifty yards south and went into west field.

Calf followed.

Then shouting increased.

Big bull came around bend at run.

Not straight at us initially.

Behind it timber worker waving pole.

Idiot.

"Down!" Beren shouted.

We moved.

Wagons already off road but animals tied exposed.

Mules panicked.

Hessa's horses pulled at traces.

Bull saw motion.

Changed line.

Toward field entrance.

Toward us.

Everything compressed.

Old Greg gave options in instant.

Kill? Possible, terrible.

Mana barrier? Current capacity uncertain, bull mass high.

Deflect line with visual/noise? Glasshorn follows gaps, hates reflective flash maybe from current prior work.

Our earlier encounter with glasshorn migration established response? I remembered current truth enough: don't provoke, give lane.

We needed lane.

"Open field! Move wagons north edge!"

Beren already doing similar.

Too late for carts.

Hessa cut horse lead loose from wagon? No, traces. Tarl pulled wool wagon brake.

Bull twenty yards.

I saw west field gap between wagons.

But salt wagon blocked half.

I ran to rear wheel.

"Brake off!"

Hessa shouted, "What?"

"Roll it back! Give gap!"

Ground slight slope.

She understood instantly.

Released brake.

Three of us shoved salt wagon backward six feet while horses resisted sideways.

Gap widened.

Bull came.

Glasshorns were beautiful until close.

Mineral antlers bright. Breath foam. Hooves throwing dirt.

It could still choose us.

I drew sword but held low.

Not attack.

If line changed last second.

Beren grabbed a blue tarp and flattened it against our cart side, reducing visual motion.

Mave pulled Brin behind wool wagon.

Jorren stood beside me with spear from Beren's cart.

"Don't throw," I said.

"I know."

Bull reached entrance.

Slowed half step.

Saw gap.

Went through.

Ten feet from me.

Air moved with body.

I smelled animal heat and wet earth.

One antler tip passed above salt wagon rail.

Then bull crossed field after herd.

Nobody moved for three seconds.

Timber worker rounded bend still holding pole.

Hessa threw a stone at him.

Missed.

"You fucking idiot!"

Road warden came behind, also shouting.

The next ten minutes were social violence rather than physical.

Timber crew said animals blocked work.

Warden said wait.

Hessa said they nearly killed us.

Beren added route costs.

I stood breathing too fast with sword still in hand.

Jorren touched blade flat.

"Sheath."

I looked.

Right.

I sheathed.

My hands were steady.

That bothered me.

Not because skill bad.

Because danger clarity had returned and body liked knowing what to do.

Glasshorn herd disappeared west.

Warden closed road for one hour.

We waited.

No one complained.

At camp that night, Hessa asked what rank I was.

"Bronze."

She laughed.

"No."

"Yes."

"You moved wagon."

"Rank does not affect pushing."

"Sword looked practiced."

"It is."

"Magic?"

"Some."

She squinted.

"You're strange Bronze."

Jorren said, "Yes."

Betrayal.

Tarl told a story about a Gold party he once hauled for.

There.

Quarry.

"Four of them," he said. "Didn't talk much. One pointed, others moved. Took down cliff drake before my mule finished shitting."

Mave said, "You always add shit."

"Mule did."

"Story doesn't need."

"Truth does."

I listened.

Gold party had modified gear, coordinated spells, one member aborted pursuit when terrain changed.

Tarl thought abort was cowardice until later learned cliff edge collapsed where drake fled.

Good judgment looked boring after danger.

"Names?" I asked.

Tarl shrugged.

"Golds. Long ago."

He remembered event, not people.

That hit too close.

Old Greg disease mirrored by ordinary memory.

Maybe less monstrous than I thought.

Mave handed me bowl.

"Eat."

I did.
