# Rehearsal B62: East Gate

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Carrow appeared one day later than planned.

Nobody apologized.

The last leg was boring enough to feel earned.

Dry road. Two tolls. One broken strap on the salt wagon repaired before it became interesting. Lunch under an elm. Brin returned my oversized sock washed badly and still damp.

I accepted.

At the outer farms, the caravan began dissolving.

Hessa turned toward the salt yard by north gate.

Tarl and Mave toward the wool market.

No ceremony.

Hessa slapped Beren's cart rail once.

"Next time avoid timber idiots."

"You too."

Brin waved.

Mave gave Jorren a wrapped piece of cheese because she thought he was too thin.

She gave me none because apparently height looked fed.

Unfair.

Then three wagons became one.

I felt the absence immediately.

Accidental team gone.

Road quieter.

Jorren looked back at the diverging tracks.

"Good people."

"Yes."

No need to explain.

The East Gate queue was long.

While waiting, Beren calculated pay.

Base rate. Delay day. Held pay in Kelm. Extra four from the timber rescue split. Costs deducted from route purse, not our wages.

My share was larger than expected.

Thirty-nine copper.

I counted twice.

The road had become financially real.

"You coming again?" Beren asked.

I looked up.

There.

Invitation.

Not Gold.

Not rank.

A driver with an injured hand asking whether I would be useful again.

"Yes."

Too fast.

Beren smiled.

"Ask job first."

"Depends."

"Better."

Jorren laughed.

The gate clerk waved us through.

Carrow noise hit like a wall.

I had wanted it.

I had also wanted the road.

Both survived contact.

Warehouse closeout took an hour.

Beren returned the cask and crate to the factor.

Paper argued about one cracked brass shim that had been cracked before Kelm according to us and after Kelm according to the receiving clerk.

I stayed out.

Jorren didn't.

"Mark on outbound sheet," he said, pointing.

There was a note.

The clerk shut up.

Jorren had learned paperwork power on the road.

Beren looked at him differently.

"Factor work fits."

Jorren shrugged.

"Maybe."

No ladder claim.

At the pay table we received coins.

I held mine.

Thirty-nine.

Enough for meals, gear, seven more terrible chairs.

I thought about buying something road-specific.

Better boots.

Oilcloth.

Then I thought about Lower Rook. Two chairs had somehow created the expectation that a second bowl should exist too.

I wanted both gear and home object.

Contradiction becoming lifestyle.

Jorren shifted his pack.

"Bath."

"Lower Rook after?"

"Maybe."

"Noll?"

"Food."

"Obviously."

Beren left.

I stood alone in East Warehouse Row with my pack and road dirt.

Then movement at the far loading bay caught my attention.

A Gold-tab party was preparing for a contract departure.

A tall woman in segmented gray armor checked straps on two compact shields carried by another party member. A bald mage with copper rings along his staff tested each ring against a low pulse. The fourth, a small man with a heavy pack, walked around the group tightening everyone else's loose gear without asking.

They barely spoke.

One hand signal changed where the packs were stacked.

The mage stopped his pulse when a nearby mule flattened its ears.

The small man saw it before the handler did.

Gold coordination.

I watched.

One member noticed me watching.

The shield carrier.

He looked at my Bronze tab.

Then at the road mud.

"You need something?"

"No."

"Then stop inventorying us."

Direct.

"Sorry."

I looked away.

Embarrassing.

He called, "Bronze."

I turned.

He tossed me a small leather strap.

I caught it.

"Your left pack buckle is fraying."

I looked.

It was.

I had missed it.

"Thanks."

He had already turned away.

No recruitment.

No mysterious recognition.

Just a superior field eye.

I replaced the strap before leaving the yard.

That felt like a more useful Gold encounter than prophecy.

Lower Rook smelled like onions when I arrived.

Maud saw me.

"Mud."

"Dry."

"Still mud."

I cleaned my boots.

Third floor rear.

Key in the inside pocket.

I took it out.

The door opened before the key entered.

Noll stood there.

"Why are you in my room?"

"Waiting."

"How did you know today?"

"Didn't."

That stopped me.

He had been using the room again while I was gone.

Second chair.

Maybe eating.

Maybe resting between work.

My private space had become porous.

I should have been annoyed.

I was.

Also glad.

"You can't just live here."

"I don't."

"Evidence mixed."

He took my pack.

Not asked.

Set it beside the bed.

"Road?"

"Long."

"Good?"

I thought of moths, the washed road, the child under the cart, the glasshorn bull, Brin's boot, the Gold stranger's strap.

"Yes."

"Hungry?"

"Very."

He handed me a bowl.

I sat in the old chair.

Noll took the new chair.

The soup had pork this time.

I ate fast.

Halfway through, a knock.

An actual knock.

Jorren entered after his bath, hair wet.

He held the cheese from Mave.

"For dinner."

Noll took it like tribute.

We ate.

Road stories came out nonlinearly.

Jorren told Noll about my moth face.

I told Noll about Jorren losing the argument to Mave over wool height.

He denied it.

We argued.

No one asked about the future.

Later, alone after they left, I unpacked.

Wet strap.

Dirty socks.

One oversized borrowed sock returned.

Thirty-nine copper.

Frayed buckle replaced by a Gold stranger's spare.

I put the room key beneath Mara's note.

The field pack stayed open.

I did not immediately repack it.

That was probably healthy.

Then I checked the Guild board the next morning before breakfast.

Apparently health lasted eight hours.
