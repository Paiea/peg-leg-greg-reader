# Horizon C Rehearsal Brief — Coordinates 63–72

Status: **REHEARSAL ONLY / ZERO AUTHORITY**

## Pressure question

What happens if Greg eventually accepts Mara's current invitation and visits Halden as a guest in **her** lived world rather than as young Greg chasing an old plan or Old Greg navigating remembered geography?

This is a rehearsal possibility only. It does not obligate near-edge R2 to send Greg west.

## Rehearsal purpose

Test romance, desire, work clocks, unfamiliar city geography, and social displacement at chapter scale. Greg should have to live inside a place where Mara has routines, coworkers, obligations, preferred food, shortcuts, annoyances, and relationships that do not involve him.

The visit should contain substantial physical action and city movement. It cannot become ten chapters of Greg and Mara talking in rooms.

## Local assumptions allowed only inside C

C may rehearse a plausible future Halden visit after Chapter 42 with adequate warning having been sent. It may invent C-local Vey coworkers, lodging problems, commercial systems, and city incidents as needed.

No C-local event becomes true elsewhere.

## Action palette

- river travel into Halden
- carrying luggage, finding lodging, stairs, bad rooms, crowded streets
- Vey freight yards, ledgers physically moving with cargo, loading and inspection
- Greg walking the city alone while Mara works
- market or river infrastructure failure
- physical intimacy that competes with fatigue, work, privacy, money, and time
- Greg getting lost despite believing he knows the city
- helping in a dangerous or inconvenient situation without becoming Vey's savior
- magic infrastructure or urban ecology that differs from Carrow
- an event that leaves Greg dirty, wet, sore, embarrassed, or late

## Character pressure

Mara must remain a current adult with work ownership. She is not Greg's reward for traveling west.

Greg may arrive thinking the visit proves something, then discover Mara did not ask for proof. He may be jealous of people who know her daily self better. He may feel false familiarity with places he remembers differently. He may want more time or sex than the work clock allows. He may behave generously for selfish reasons.

Mara should also be inconsistent, tired, unfair, horny, funny, professionally territorial, or wrong sometimes. Do not make her the permanent emotional referee.

Supporting Halden people should know Mara independently and form opinions about Greg without receiving his preferred explanation.

## Setting pressure

Halden must become a place, not `Mara city`.

Use river approaches, freight yards, Vey offices, housing, food, streets, class differences, industrial noise, local magic, weather, smells, night movement, and the contrast between Greg's remembered map and current reality.

## Plot pressure

The ten chapters should contain a coherent visit with arrival friction, competing schedules, at least one non-romantic problem, a physical or social escalation, consequences, and an ending that changes what `visit` means without forcing a relationship contract.

## Prohibitions

- no endless date sequence
- no Mara available whenever Greg wants her
- no jealousy resolved through one mature conversation
- no sex used as automatic emotional closure
- no Greg magically becoming useful to Vey in every problem
- no Old Greg tourist-guide competence
- no tidy `home is wherever she is` conclusion

## Success

C succeeds if Halden gives Mara more independent life, gives Greg less control, widens the world, and tests whether R2 romance can coexist with sustained physical plot and setting.
