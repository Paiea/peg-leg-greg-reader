# Rehearsal C65: Two Hours

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Dinner lasted two hours because Mara kept standing up.

The first time, I thought she was leaving.

That reaction occurred before reason and was therefore difficult to hide.

Mara noticed.

Of course.

"Bathroom," she said.

"I didn't ask."

"Your face did."

"My face has been slandered across two cities."

She smiled and went toward the back of the restaurant.

I sat alone with two bowls, one plate of roasted peppers, and the knowledge that I had apparently become a man who interpreted standing as abandonment.

Excellent development.

The restaurant was called Lessa's after either the owner or an ancestor. I had not asked because Mara had ordered before I finished reading the wall board.

Garlic broth.

Fried noodles with pork.

Peppers blistered black at the edges.

A small plate of pickled greens she claimed Halden did better than Carrow.

This was false patriotism.

I ate them anyway.

Mara returned.

Sat.

"You okay?"

"Yes."

"You looked angry at the peppers."

"They started it."

She picked one up with chopsticks.

"You still don't use these right."

I looked at my own.

"I am holding food."

"Through force."

"Effective force."

She demonstrated with irritating ease.

Young Greg memory gave me almost nothing about chopsticks.

Old Greg had used them across several regions for decades.

Current fingers should have known.

They did, mostly.

Mara still found fault.

That felt comforting in an embarrassingly precise way.

We ate.

For ten minutes it was easy.

She asked about Lower Rook.

I told her Noll had started four Brass Spoon nights.

Her eyebrows rose.

"Vessa kept him?"

"You know Vessa?"

"Everybody knew Vessa's mother. Vessa scared me when we were fourteen."

Another edge I did not own.

"Why?"

"She threw a spoon at Tavin."

I nearly choked.

"Domm learned from family?"

"Who is Domm?"

There.

Finally, one thing I knew from Noll's current work that Mara did not.

Relief arrived far too strongly.

"Cook. Throws spoons. Sauces apparently excellent."

Mara laughed.

I told her about Noll's dumplings.

The four-night choice.

Keeping Lower Rook's fifth-night supper because it was where he lived.

Mara listened with her chin on one hand.

"That sounds like Noll."

"You knew him better than I knew you knew him."

"You keep saying that."

"Because it keeps happening."

She took another pepper.

"You were not the center of every friendship in Carrow."

"I understand the concept."

"Do you?"

"Intellectually."

Mara smiled.

I hated her.

Second time she stood, a Vey runner had appeared in the doorway.

Not Emmet.

Girl perhaps seventeen wearing a yellow cord around one sleeve.

She scanned tables, saw Mara, crossed quickly.

Mara closed her eyes.

"No."

The runner stopped.

"Sera says west tally came in."

"Tomorrow."

"She said red seal."

Mara's expression changed.

"Which company?"

"Torren Mill."

"Fuck."

There.

Current adult language.

Mara stood.

My stomach dropped again, but this time reality supported it.

"You have to go."

Not accusation.

Mostly.

Mara looked at me.

Then at the runner.

"How bad?"

"Sera said if you are eating, finish."

"That's not answer."

"She looked angry."

"Also not answer."

The runner shrugged.

Mara looked at our table.

Half the noodles remained.

"I can go," I said.

She looked back.

"To Vey?"

"No. Inn. Walk. Whatever."

I did not want to.

The words still came.

Generous behavior with selfish hope attached: maybe she would say no, stay.

She heard something.

Not all.

"Give me ten minutes."

The runner left.

Mara sat again.

Ten minutes.

I almost laughed.

"Your twenty became fifty-three."

"I know."

"Now ten."

"I know."

"Vey time differs from civilian."

"Apparently."

She ate noodles quickly.

I slowed down on purpose.

There.

Body betraying thought.

I had just offered to leave so she could work. Now I was physically stretching dinner by chewing like an elderly cow.

Mara watched me.

"Are you eating slower?"

"No."

"Greg."

"Peppers require respect."

She laughed despite being annoyed.

Good.

I wanted the laugh because it bought seconds.

Not noble.

Current.

"What is red seal?" I asked.

"Hold. Means somebody stopped the freight."

"Why?"

"Could be count. Damage. Payment. Wrong consignee. Broken mark."

"And Sera?"

"West tally. She catches things coming upriver before office books them."

"You like her?"

Mara paused with noodles halfway to mouth.

There it was again.

Jealous question wearing a coworker hat.

I knew it.

Asked anyway.

"Yes," she said.

Simple.

My stomach did something immature.

"How long you know her?"

"Two years."

"Longer than current me."

Mara put the noodles down.

"That sentence is unfair to both versions of you."

"Probably."

"What are you actually asking?"

I could have lied.

Asked if Sera was competent.

Asked if she had family.

Asked whether she and Mara shared lodging.

Instead:

"Do you have people here I don't fit around yet?"

Mara looked at me for a long second.

Restaurant noise continued.

Oil hissing behind the kitchen wall.

Someone at the next table arguing about a bootmaker.

Bowls knocking together.

"Yes," she said.

There.

No reassurance first.

"Okay."

"Pell. Sera. Emmet. Vey people. My landlord. Woman downstairs who keeps stealing my washing line."

"Enemy."

"Absolutely."

"You have friends outside Vey?"

"Some."

That also hurt for no defensible reason.

Mara watched it happen.

"Greg."

"I know."

"No, you don't get to `I know` this before I say anything."

I stopped.

Good correction.

"Fine."

"I built a life here because I lived here."

"I know."

She stared.

"Sorry. Continue."

"You are not late to it. You're visiting it."

Different.

Not comfort exactly.

"And I don't have to fit around everybody tonight," I said.

"Correct."

"But I want to."

Mara's expression softened.

"I know."

She got to use the phrase.

Unfair.

The third time Mara stood was after eleven minutes.

Not ten.

We had beaten Vey by one.

She paid half the bill before I could argue.

Then looked at me outside beneath the striped awning.

Halden had gone dark enough for street lamps.

Warm air smelled like garlic, rain-damp stone, horse, and river.

"I need maybe an hour," she said.

"Vey hour?"

"Asshole."

"Important distinction."

"Go to Finch. Or don't. I'll come find you after."

"At the inn?"

"Yes."

There.

My body heard that sentence before my mind finished parsing logistics.

Mara saw.

Her mouth tilted.

"Do not make that face either."

"I have no idea what you mean."

"Sure."

She stepped closer.

Kissed me once under the awning.

Quick.

Not enough.

My hand went to her waist automatically.

She leaned in for half a second longer.

Then stepped away.

Work.

The word did not need to be spoken.

I disliked it physically.

"One hour," she said.

"Approximately."

"Yes."

She turned and hurried downhill.

I watched until she reached the corner.

Then kept watching the empty corner for several seconds because apparently vision could negotiate absence.

I did not go directly to the Finch.

That would have felt like waiting.

I walked.

Halden at night changed shape.

Day shops closed shutters while food stalls appeared under lamps. Freight carts still moved near the river, bells tied to harness. Upper streets became quieter, windows lit behind curtains.

I passed the fish fountain again.

No knife sharpener.

A group of young men sat on the low wall drinking from a shared bottle.

One sang badly.

Different city once work stopped.

For some people.

Vey remained lit downhill.

I looked toward it from the square.

Then walked the opposite direction.

Good.

Deliberate.

Three blocks later I turned down a street that curved back toward Vey.

Not good.

I laughed at myself alone.

A woman selling roasted chestnuts looked at me.

"Problem?"

"Geography."

"Halden does that."

I bought chestnuts.

Apparently city disorientation required snacks.

I ended up at the river steps.

Boats moved under lanterns.

One Vey freight barge loading under yellow lamps.

Men carried sacks in pairs.

A crane turned slowly above them.

Physical city still working.

I sat on a stone step and cracked chestnut shells with my thumb.

Old Greg remembered Halden nights.

A tavern fight near north market.

A woman laughing on a roof.

A burned warehouse.

Mara older, perhaps.

Or not Mara.

Memory blurred people into atmosphere again.

Current Mara had eleven minutes of noodles before a red seal.

Current Mara had Sera.

Pell.

Emmet.

A washing-line thief.

I wanted all of it too quickly.

That was the problem.

No.

Not problem.

Want.

Problems could be solved.

Want had to be lived around.

That sounded suspiciously like a polished conclusion, so I ate another chestnut before it gained authority.

A bell rang from uphill.

Not Vey.

Church or clock.

I had no idea how long I had been sitting.

Good.

I returned to the Green Finch.

Blue sign visible under lantern.

The innkeeper looked up from a book.

"Visitor."

"Apparently."

"Your friend came by."

I stopped.

"Mara?"

"Dark hair. Fast. Looked angry."

"That is not narrowing."

The innkeeper pointed upstairs.

"Your room."

My body moved before manners.

I took the stairs two at a time.

The narrow hallway smelled like soap and old wood.

My door was closed.

Locked?

I had the key.

I opened it.

Mara sat in the one chair.

Boots off.

One stockinged foot tucked under the other leg.

My pack open beside the bed.

She had not touched it, probably.

Her head leaned against the wall.

Eyes closed.

For one second I simply watched her breathe.

Then:

"You broke in."

Her eyes opened.

"Innkeeper has another key."

"Institutional betrayal."

"You weren't here."

There was accusation in that.

Small.

Interesting.

"You said an hour."

"It was longer."

"I walked."

"I noticed."

"Were you waiting?"

Mara looked at me.

Now my turn.

"Maybe ten minutes."

I smiled.

"Vey ten?"

She threw one of my folded socks at me.

I caught it.

"You touched my pack."

"Sock was on top."

"Still."

"Arrest me."

I put the sock down.

Then sat on the bed because she had the chair.

One chair.

Again.

"Red seal?"

"Wrong consignee. Torren sent amended paper to river office but not us. Sera was right."

"You were wrong?"

"I hadn't guessed yet."

"So yes."

Mara narrowed her eyes.

I smiled.

She looked exhausted.

Ink on one hand.

Hair coming loose.

No work coat.

Current.

"Stay?" I asked.

The word came out before I could make it safer.

Mara looked at the bed.

Then at me.

"For a while."

Not night.

Not no.

I nodded.

My hand reached for hers.

She gave it.

We sat like that without talking for perhaps thirty seconds.

Then Mara said, "You have terrible lodging taste."

"You recommended it."

"I recommended location."

"One chair."

"Greg."

I kissed her before she could continue.

She laughed into it.

Then kissed me back harder.

That was better than a conclusion.
