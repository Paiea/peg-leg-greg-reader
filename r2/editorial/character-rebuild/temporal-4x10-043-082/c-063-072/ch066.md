# Rehearsal C66: Red Seal

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Mara left my room before sunrise and forgot one stocking.

I found it hanging from the chair back after the door closed.

Not erotic in the dramatic sense.

Gray wool. Heel darned twice. Slightly damp at toe from yesterday's rain.

It still affected me more than was reasonable.

I held it for perhaps five seconds.

Then realized I was standing alone in a rented room examining a woman's sock like evidence.

Current dignity remained fragile.

I folded it and put it on the chair.

Mara had work.

I had none in Halden.

That distinction felt larger at first bell.

The Green Finch served breakfast downstairs. Bread, soft cheese, boiled egg, weak coffee substitute made from roasted root. I ate slowly because no schedule existed.

People around me had schedules.

A mason left halfway through bread.

Two Vey clerks compared slips while standing.

A courier tied boot under table and ran out before finishing drink.

Visitorhood became visible through stillness.

I disliked it.

So naturally I went to Vey.

Not to interfere.

I had several excellent reasons.

Return stocking.

See Mara.

Learn whether red seal problem resolved.

Possibly find temporary work elsewhere nearby afterward.

Only one of those reasons required entering Vey office.

I used it anyway.

Halden Vey compound at morning was louder than yesterday evening. River carts lined loading lane. Clerks moved between brick office and covered yard. Yellow company marks everywhere.

I stopped at gate.

Good.

Boundary.

A guard looked at me.

"Business?"

"Mara."

"Which Mara?"

Excellent institutional defense against romance.

"Mara Vale. Carrow? Halden west tally?"

He found name on board.

"She working."

"I know."

"Then?"

I held up folded stocking.

His face did not change.

Professionalism admirable.

"Leave desk."

"Can I give her myself?"

He looked at stocking.

Then at me.

"No."

Correct.

I left it at gate desk inside paper wrap provided by clerk who definitely judged me.

Then walked away.

Three streets.

I was proud.

Then someone shouted my name.

I turned.

Emmet ran from Vey gate.

The runner from yesterday? No, Emmet was older, maybe early twenties, narrow face, yellow cord at sleeve.

"Greg?"

"Apparently."

"Mara says if you're not doing anything stupid, come back."

"That qualifier is hostile."

"Her words."

I returned.

Gate guard looked disappointed in policy.

Emmet led me around office toward river yard.

"Red seal?"

"Not same."

"Then why?"

"Need hands."

Hands I had.

Yard held three freight carts around a damaged crate stack. One cart had broken side rail. Crates leaned against each other under canvas. Workers were unloading fast before stack shifted.

Mara stood on rear platform with Sera beside her.

I knew Sera immediately because my jealousy had already invented three wrong faces.

Current Sera was forty maybe. Broad hips, shaved one side of head, red ink across fingers, expression of woman who had personally murdered patience years ago.

Relief came.

Then embarrassment that relief came.

Mara saw me.

"You can lift?"

"Usually."

"Good. No magic unless asked."

Specific enough that she knew me.

"Who owns load?"

Sera answered, "I do until paper says otherwise."

I liked her immediately.

Jealousy became less convenient.

We worked.

Crates contained dyed cloth bundles, not heavy individually. Problem was geometry. Broken rail let bottom stack slide outward.

Two workers held brace poles while others removed top crates.

I took upper crate with Emmet.

"Three," he said.

We lifted.

Wet canvas made grip poor.

Carried to covered platform.

Back.

Again.

Mara was not supervising me.

She was checking marks, redirecting crates by consignee, arguing with a clerk about which ones could legally break seal.

Work-self fully occupied.

I watched between carries until Emmet said, "Eyes here."

Right.

Third crate shifted while we lifted.

Stack groaned.

Brace worker shouted.

Bottom rail cracked further.

Everyone froze.

Sera said, "Nobody pull."

One man had hand on crate.

He stopped.

Mara climbed down from platform and crouched to see base.

"Weight left?"

Sera said, "Too much on broken side."

"Can unload from rear?"

"If canvas comes off."

Canvas tied beneath tilted crates.

Emmet looked at me.

I looked away because being tall did not make knots mine.

Sera pointed at small loader.

"Tomas, cut rear line. Only rear."

Tomas crawled under cart bed edge with knife.

Mara moved two people clear.

I stepped back too.

Good.

Line cut.

Canvas loosened.

Wind caught it immediately and slapped Sera in face.

She swore with impressive creativity.

Mara laughed once, then helped pull canvas over top.

Rear crates became accessible.

We unloaded from stable side.

Ten minutes later stack safe.

No collapse.

One worker cut finger on broken rail.

Sera wrapped it herself while insulting rail maker.

Mara's stocking remained irrelevant somewhere at gate.

This pleased me.

Afterward Sera looked at me.

"Visitor?"

"Yes."

"Guild?"

"Carrow Bronze."

"Mara says weird."

Mara, behind her, said, "I did not say weird."

Sera looked back.

"You said complicated. Same."

I liked Sera less now because accuracy.

"Need paid hand today?" Sera asked.

Mara looked at her.

Not at me.

Important.

"We can use one until second bell," Sera said. "Yard rate. No Vey authority. Carries, straps, count under loader."

There.

Temporary work inside Mara's world without Mara creating role for me.

I wanted to say yes immediately.

Then thought about why.

To stay near her.

Also money.

Also work.

Also curiosity.

No pure motive required.

"Rate?"

Sera named nine copper half day.

Fair enough.

I accepted.

Mara said, "I have office until noon."

"I heard."

"This is not date."

"Sera hired me."

Sera said, "Correct. Romance after shift."

I nearly dropped strap.

Mara looked murderous.

Sera walked away smiling.

Excellent current friend.

For three hours I worked under Tomas, who was seventeen and enjoyed ordering me because height reversed hierarchy.

"That stack."

"Which?"

"Blue."

There were six blue stacks.

"Useful."

"Dark blue."

Four.

"Tomas."

He laughed and pointed.

I learned Vey yard marks by doing.

Red chalk meant hold.

White slash meant counted.

Yellow cord meant internal transfer.

Two knots on canvas corner meant river exposure expected.

At one point I almost corrected a strap placement based on Carrow cart practice.

Tomas said, "Halden lift hook catches there."

I shut up.

Current infrastructure local.

At noon Mara came out of office carrying two ledgers and my folded stocking tucked under one arm.

She saw me see it.

"You left evidence."

"You did."

"Your inn chair stole it."

Sera called from platform, "Pay him before flirting."

Mara closed eyes.

I received nine copper from yard clerk.

Actual Halden money earned independently enough.

Then Mara and I walked toward lunch.

She handed stocking to me.

"Why am I carrying this now?"

"Because you brought it."

"It's yours."

"You found it."

"This is not property law."

We argued for one block before she took it back.

I smiled too much.

"Sera seems nice."

Mara looked at me.

"Now?"

"I was asking professionally."

"You were jealous of a hypothetical person."

"Allegedly."

"Her husband is sixty and makes terrible beer."

Relief tried again.

I hated myself.

Mara saw.

"Greg."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"No. Apparently not."

That made her laugh.

We ate standing at a river stall because she had thirty minutes.

Fried fish in paper, vinegar, onions.

My hands smelled like rope and dye cloth.

Mara's fingers had red ink.

Current Halden had entered both of us physically.

That felt better than being shown a landmark.
