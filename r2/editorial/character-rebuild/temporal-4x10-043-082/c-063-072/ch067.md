# Rehearsal C67: Crane Line

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The crane failed politely.

One click.

Then another.

Then a sound like a giant clearing its throat.

Everyone in Vey river yard looked up.

I was there because Sera had hired me again.

That sentence required explanation I refused to provide to myself.

Day two in Halden. Mara in office. I could have explored city. Could have checked Guild. Could have slept.

Instead I returned to Vey gate at first bell and asked whether yard needed hands.

Sera said yes before Mara knew.

Important technicality.

The river crane stood on stone quay, timber boom over water, iron capstan at base. Two workers walked bars to raise cargo. Rope ran through upper block to sling around crate net.

Old Greg had seen a thousand cranes.

This one differed enough to deserve respect.

Halden river current required loading boats slightly upstream of berth because hull drifted under suspended weight. Local riggers timed swing with water.

I carried tag line.

No authority.

Good place to learn.

The load when crane clicked was a long crate of machine rollers, maybe six hundred pounds, suspended four feet above quay.

Not huge.

Huge enough.

First click came from capstan pawl.

Second from timber brace.

Rigger shouted, "Hold bars!"

Workers froze.

Crane groaned.

Load swung six inches toward water.

Everyone moved out of fall zone except one dock boy who looked up instead of moving.

I grabbed back of his coat and pulled him two steps.

No heroism.

Obvious.

"Down!" rigger shouted.

Capstan crew tried controlled reverse.

Pawl jammed.

Could not lower.

Load continued small swing.

Boat beneath shifted with current.

Now crate over gap between quay and boat.

Worst place.

Sera came running from yard.

Mara appeared behind her with ledger still open.

Rigger, woman named Heli, shouted assignments.

"Secondary line! Boat off! Clear quay!"

I held tag line already.

"What do you need?"

Heli pointed to bollard upstream.

"Take that line around twice, no knot. Keep crate from river."

I ran.

Rope through gloves.

Around stone bollard.

One wrap.

Two.

Tension hit as crate swung.

My arms jerked.

Healing palm? Different horizon from A, no rope burn here unless previous C scenes didn't establish. Do not import. So hands intact.

I leaned weight.

Tag line was not load line, but six hundred pounds swinging made it feel ambitious.

Another worker joined me.

Boat crew shoved hull away using poles.

Crate now hanging over water only.

If dropped, expensive not fatal.

Good.

Heli climbed crane base to inspect pawl.

Mara stood clear with Sera, shouting crate marks to identify contents and value.

Interesting priority.

Not helping mechanically because not her job.

"Rollers!" she called. "Insured, river exposure not covered if rig fault!"

Sera shouted, "Thank you, comforting!"

Heli hit pawl housing with hammer.

Nothing.

"Need unload tension."

How?

Secondary hoist perhaps.

Another yard crane fifty feet away too far.

Block and tackle from warehouse could rig to quay beam.

Heli ordered it.

Workers ran.

I held line.

Shoulder began to burn.

Other worker beside me, Tomas, said, "Don't hero. Wrap more."

Right.

Third wrap around bollard took load from arms.

I had been holding unnecessarily because being useful felt like grip.

Greg problem transcended cities.

We waited four minutes while tackle arrived.

Longest part.

Crane creaked intermittently.

Every sound sharpened spine.

Heli rigged secondary line from crate sling to fixed warehouse ring through portable blocks. Crew tensioned by hand winch.

Once secondary carried enough, she freed jammed pawl with pry bar.

Capstan lowered slowly.

Crate touched water.

Everyone swore.

Not submerged, bottom edge dipped because boat drift.

"Boat under!" Heli shouted.

Boat crew shoved back beneath.

Crate landed on deck hard but intact.

Silence.

Then work resumed almost immediately because river did not care about adrenaline.

Heli closed crane.

Sera started damage notes.

Mara went aboard to inspect crate seal with receiving clerk.

I stood by bollard unwrapping line.

Hands shaking now that task ended.

Tomas saw.

"First crane?"

"First failed one here."

"Good answer."

He took line coil.

"You pulled boy."

"He was standing under."

"He does that."

"Maybe stop hiring him."

"Owner's nephew."

Of course.

The boy, whose name was Fen, appeared carrying water and looked embarrassed.

"Thanks."

"Look where everyone runs next time."

Too sharp.

He flinched.

I regretted tone immediately.

Old Greg's danger clarity had arrived and stayed too long.

"Sorry. You okay?"

"Yes."

"Good."

There it was again.

He left.

Sera came over.

"Nine copper became eleven. Hazard bonus."

"I didn't ask."

"That's why it's bonus."

I accepted.

Heli passed us carrying cracked pawl tooth in hand.

Metal had sheared at base.

"Old?" I asked.

"Three months."

That surprised me.

"Bad metal?"

"Maybe bad set. Maybe overload yesterday. Maybe maker's mother cursed me. We inspect."

No clean cause.

Good.

Mara returned from boat.

Ink roller crate had one wet lower board, no contents exposed.

Insurance argument scheduled.

Her face flushed from work and irritation.

"You okay?"

She asked me.

"Yes."

"Fen?"

"Yes."

"Sera?"

"Angry."

"Normal."

Sera shouted from behind, "I hear you."

Mara smiled.

Then looked at my hands.

"You're shaking."

I put them in pockets.

"Less now."

"Lunch."

"You're ordering?"

"Yes."

I went.

We ate on stone river steps, not restaurant.

Bread, cheese, pickled cucumber from yard stall.

Crane tower visible downstream, tagged closed with red cloth.

"You didn't use magic," Mara said.

"Wasn't asked."

"Would you have?"

I thought.

"Maybe if crate fell toward people."

"Could you stop six hundred pounds?"

"Not cleanly."

"Then?"

"Deflect maybe. Enough to miss someone."

Mara chewed.

"You want to know."

Not accusation.

"Yes."

"That scares me."

There.

I looked at river.

"Me too."

"Does it?"

Harder.

Because part of me wanted a real test.

Not for people to be endangered.

But if danger existed, I wanted to know current limit.

"Not enough," I said.

Mara nodded slowly.

No tidy reassurance.

"Old you?"

"Would know exactly."

"Current you?"

"Knows enough to not volunteer crane."

She smiled a little.

"Low bar."

"Important bar."

We sat.

A river tug passed pulling two barges. Water slapped steps.

My shaking stopped.

Mara leaned shoulder against mine briefly.

Then stood because lunch ended.

I did not interpret standing as abandonment this time.

Progress perhaps.

She said, "Tonight I have maybe three hours."

"Vey hours?"

"Do you want them or not?"

"Yes."

Immediate.

She laughed and went back to work.

I stayed on steps five more minutes.

Then went to Halden Guild because having a whole afternoon free while pretending Mara was not center required actual behavior.
