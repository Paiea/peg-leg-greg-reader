# Rehearsal C68: Halden Guild

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Halden Guild did not know me.

Wonderful.

The building stood uphill from river in old stone hall with newer wooden annex. Carrow Guild smelled like chalk, wet coats, and paper. Halden smelled like lamp oil, leather, and something fried from alley kitchen next door.

I showed Bronze tab at intake.

Clerk checked it against traveling registry.

"Carrow."

"Yes."

"Temporary?"

"Visiting."

"Looking for work?"

I hesitated.

"Maybe."

Clerk pointed at side board.

"Day and short only unless transfer filed. No city ward work without local signoff. No river contracts without river endorsement."

Different institution.

Current limits.

I liked the clarity.

Board had six short jobs.

Warehouse night watch.

Two-person furniture move.

Bakery cellar pest check.

Escort ledger from Guild to municipal office.

Training dummy repair.

One quarry sample carry requiring strong back and no sense.

I laughed at last wording.

Clerk said, "Quarry master writes his own."

"No sense?"

"Means no mana sensitivity. Samples interfere."

Interesting.

Unavailable to me.

I almost chose night watch because familiar.

Then furniture move because physical.

Then realized I had three hours with Mara tonight and night watch would consume.

There.

Schedule around her.

Not wrong automatically.

Still worth seeing.

I chose ledger escort because two hours estimated and city walk.

Pay six copper.

Boring.

Good.

A junior clerk named Halen handed me locked leather case and said, "Do not open."

"Wasn't planning."

"Everyone says."

"Who opens municipal ledgers for fun?"

"Adventurers."

Fair insult.

Destination was Records Hall across upper market. Halen came too because escort required two people, one carrying case, one witness.

I expected danger approximately zero.

Halden corrected with civic festival.

Upper market streets had been closed for Founders' Lantern Day, apparently today because no one had informed visitor. Hundreds of paper lanterns strung between buildings. Stalls occupied normal road. Children everywhere.

Halen stopped at barrier.

"Fuck."

"Alternative?"

"North stairs."

"How far?"

"Half hour."

We took north stairs.

Ledger escort became urban climbing.

Halden rose steeply away from river. Stone stairs cut between houses. Some covered. Some open. Laundry lines overhead. Water channels along edges carrying wash runoff.

Halen climbed faster because local legs.

I kept up through pride for first ten minutes.

Then stopped pretending breathing unaffected.

"You okay?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Carrow flat?"

"More reasonable."

He laughed.

At one landing crowd blocked path around fallen lantern frame.

Wooden arch had collapsed into stairs, no injuries, just obstruction.

People trying lift badly.

We could wait.

Halen looked at case, then clock tower visible through roofs.

"Records closes third bell."

"We can help."

He looked at locked case.

"Cannot set down."

"You hold."

He handed me case.

"No. You hold, I lift."

Right.

Authority of object.

He joined three residents lifting frame while I stood with ledger like useless nobility.

I wanted to help physically.

Could not without transferring chain of custody.

This was surprisingly irritating.

A woman saw sword and said, "You just watching?"

"Yes."

"Helpful."

"Legally."

She rolled eyes.

Halen laughed while lifting.

Frame moved enough to clear side.

We continued.

At top, Records Hall occupied square with carved stone doors and line of petitioners.

We delivered case.

Receiving clerk checked seal, Halen signature, my witness mark.

Six copper paid.

No attack.

But my thighs hurt.

On return we could use festival route because no deadline.

Halen bought fried dough.

I bought one too.

Lantern Day turned city unfamiliar again. Music from two corners. Children painting paper. A group of older women dancing badly. River workers off shift drinking at midday.

I wanted Mara here.

Then immediately corrected: Mara lived here. She had probably seen festival before.

I wanted to be with her here.

Different.

I asked Halen, "Vey gets day off?"

He laughed so hard powdered sugar left nose.

"No."

"Of course."

"River doesn't celebrate."

Institutional slogan maybe.

At one stall Gold tabs caught my eye.

Two adventurers, not full party, buying lanterns.

One had scarred scalp and enormous bow case. Other wore no visible weapon and carried three pastries.

They looked ordinary enough that nobody stared except children.

I did.

Bow case person noticed.

"Something?"

"No. Sorry."

I walked on.

Halen said, "Visitors."

"Them?"

"Yes. North contract."

"Gold?"

"Obviously."

Halden treated Golds as traffic.

Useful scale correction.

Back at Guild I did not take another job.

Three hours with Mara approached.

I went to Finch, bathed, changed shirt, then spent ten minutes deciding whether to wear sword to dinner.

Ridiculous.

Mara arrived at my room before I finished decision.

She knocked this time.

"Come in."

She entered wearing clean work clothes, hair damp from wash, no ledger case.

I stared at absence.

"No paper?"

"I left it."

"Voluntarily?"

"For three hours."

"Historic."

She saw sword on bed.

"Why is that there?"

"Wardrobe crisis."

"Wear it."

"Romantic."

"Halden night market. Pickpockets."

Practical romance.

We went.

Festival after dark was beautiful enough to irritate me.

Hundreds lanterns reflected in wet stone. Music. Smoke. Food. Crowds pressing close.

Mara moved through without thinking.

Knew where stairs narrowed. Which stall had good sweet buns. Which bridge got too crowded.

I followed.

Visitor.

At one point she grabbed my wrist and pulled me into side lane before parade cart blocked street.

Body contact casual.

I liked it more than planned kisses sometimes.

We ate grilled mushrooms from sticks.

Mara introduced me to a woman named Pell who repaired Vey account machines. Pell looked me over and said, "You're the Carrow problem."

"I have been branded."

"Mara complains efficiently."

Mara said, "I do not complain about him."

Pell laughed.

They had history.

Easy teasing.

I felt jealousy spark and let it burn without acting for almost thirty seconds.

Then asked, "How long you know each other?"

Mara stared.

Pell said, "Oh, this is fun."

I hated both.

Mara answered, "Four years."

"Good."

She raised eyebrow.

"Meaning?"

"Nothing."

"Greg."

"I am practicing being normal."

Pell said, "Badly."

Correct.

Later, near river, fireworks began from barge.

Not magical. Powder charges with colored salts.

First boom startled me enough hand touched sword.

Mara noticed.

She did not tease.

Old Greg memory had too many explosions attached to danger.

Current festival crowd cheered.

I lowered hand.

Second boom easier.

Mara slipped fingers into mine.

No speech.

We watched lantern smoke and colored fire above river.

Three hours became three and a half.

Vey did not send runner.

Miracle.

At Finch door Mara kissed me slowly.

Then stopped.

"I work first bell."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"I'm learning."

She smiled.

This time standing did not feel like leaving.

Mostly.
