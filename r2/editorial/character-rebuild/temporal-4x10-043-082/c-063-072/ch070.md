# Rehearsal C70: No Timetable

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I spent the next day proving I did not need Mara's timetable by thinking about her every hour.

Behavior improved before mind.

Perhaps acceptable.

I did not go to Vey.

Instead I took a Halden Guild furniture move.

Two rooms from upper market to river quarter. Eight copper. Three workers total.

The customer was a widow named Leri who owned too much furniture for a person moving into a smaller apartment.

She refused to admit this.

"Table fits."

The stairwell said otherwise.

We tried.

Table turned sideways.

Leg caught the banister.

The other mover, Koss, said, "No."

Leri said, "Previous men brought it up."

"Different stairs?"

"Same."

"Then table changed."

She did not appreciate that.

We removed the legs.

Physical problem solved by screws.

Not magic.

Then the wardrobe.

Heavier.

We carried it down three flights, stopping at every landing, fingers going numb under the edge.

Leri followed, giving instructions nobody could use.

"Careful corner."

"We know."

"Not the wall."

"Trying."

"My husband built that."

There.

The object's weight changed.

Not physically.

We became more careful.

At the second landing Koss slipped one step.

The wardrobe dropped two inches onto my thigh.

Pain.

I held.

The third mover swore.

We reset.

No injury beyond a bruise.

Leri cried silently at the bottom while touching one scratched panel that had existed before the move.

I almost told her.

Did not.

Sometimes accuracy was useless.

At the new apartment, the wardrobe barely fit the alcove.

Leri stood inside the empty room with her husband's furniture and looked lost.

She paid us.

No lesson needed.

I bought lunch afterward and walked Halden alone.

No Vey.

I found a public bath near the north wall.

Then a bookstall.

Then a small training yard attached to the Guild annex where Bronze and Silver members practiced.

I watched sparring.

One Silver woman used a staff style I did not know.

I wanted to join.

My arms still carried the soreness from crane lines and fire hose, but nothing felt injured enough to excuse me.

I paid two copper for the open-court fee.

Borrowed a padded sword.

Sparred a young man named Kes who assumed height meant slow.

First exchange I tagged his shoulder.

Second he kicked my thigh so hard the leg numbed.

Current body reminded me superiority was contextual.

Third exchange I almost used an Old Greg feint too advanced for friendly Bronze practice.

Stopped mid-motion.

Kes hit my ribs.

"Why stop?"

"Bad habit."

"Keep it."

"No."

He thought I meant an unfair technique.

Fine.

We sparred twenty minutes.

I won more than I lost.

No need to hide competence artificially.

At the end Kes said, "You're not Bronze with a sword."

"The tab disagrees."

"Rank test?"

"Later."

I liked being seen.

There.

Vanity simple.

I spent another hour practicing footwork alone until my arms and the furniture bruise finally objected.

Then stopped.

No one made me.

Also progress.

Dinner time approached.

Mara had said tomorrow dinner, no hour.

I had not asked.

This became a problem around fifth bell.

What did "dinner" mean?

Six? Seven?

Vey schedule variable.

Healthy communication required asking.

But going to the office for her schedule was the shape I was trying to stop making.

I sent a note through a runner.

**Dinner. Where and when? I promise I do not need the rest of your day.**

Then waited at the Finch.

Twenty minutes.

Thirty.

I considered walking.

Did not.

The reply came:

**Seven. Lessa's. Proud of you. Sera says this counts as rehabilitation.**

I laughed aloud.

At seven exactly Mara was late.

Nine minutes.

I noticed every one.

Did not become angry.

Mostly.

She arrived carrying no ledger, hair still damp from a hurried wash.

"Nine."

I said it before greeting her.

Mara stopped.

"Fuck you."

"I said nothing else."

"Your face."

"Systemic slander."

She kissed me anyway.

We ate.

I told the furniture story.

Mara knew Leri.

Of course.

"Her husband died last winter."

"I guessed something."

"Table?"

"Fits now."

I told the sparring story.

Mara looked interested.

"You liked him saying you weren't Bronze."

"No."

"Greg."

"Yes."

There.

Vanity admitted without correction.

"You are good with a sword," she said.

My chest warmed immediately.

Embarrassing.

"Thank you."

"You're also insufferable when you're good."

Balance restored.

Halfway through the meal, Sera appeared at the restaurant entrance.

My stomach tightened reflexively.

Mara saw it.

Sera approached the table.

"Not work," she said before anything else. "Pell stole my key."

Mara laughed.

"Again?"

"She says I left it. Lies."

Sera sat without invitation for three minutes, stole one pepper, complained about fire insurance, then left to find Pell.

No crisis.

I watched Mara smiling after her.

Jealousy flickered weaker.

Not gone.

I asked, "What do you like about her?"

Mara looked surprised.

"Sera?"

"Yes."

"She doesn't panic. She remembers everybody's mother's name. She is terrible with money outside work. She once slept under her desk because the landlord locked her out."

A specific person replaced the hypothetical rival.

That helped more than reassurance.

"Pell?"

"Mean. Funny. Better at machines than anyone Vey deserves."

"Emmet?"

"Too earnest. Will run himself sick if nobody stops him."

Mara's Halden people became people.

I listened.

Then told her about Jorren, Noll, current Carrow in return.

Not as a report.

Stories.

By dessert, the two cities felt less competitive.

After dinner we walked the river.

The burned warehouse stood black against the quay, roof open to the sky.

Workers had already covered salvage with canvas.

Mara stopped.

"Tomorrow they start inventory loss."

"You?"

"Part."

I almost asked how long.

Instead: "Do you want to see me tomorrow?"

Direct.

She looked at me.

"Yes. Late. Maybe after eighth."

"Specific enough."

"You can survive until then?"

"Unclear."

She laughed.

At the Finch she came upstairs.

We kissed longer than the previous night.

By the time clothes had migrated to the chair and floor, the bed frame had begun squeaking loudly enough that somebody in the next room knocked on the wall.

Mara buried her face in my shoulder to stop laughing.

I loved that moment more than I was prepared for.

Later she lay beside me tracing an old scar on my forearm I did not remember receiving.

"This one?"

"No idea."

"You used to say fence."

"Did you believe me?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because you said fence for everything."

Young Greg had a fallback lie.

Useful and sad.

"Did I lie to you a lot?"

Mara's finger stopped.

"Sometimes. Mostly about being fine."

Current me had continuity there.

"I still do."

"I know."

No speech.

She kissed the scar once, then told me to stop thinking because she could hear it.

Apparently everyone could.

I tried.
