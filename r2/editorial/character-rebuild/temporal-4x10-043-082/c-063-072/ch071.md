# Rehearsal C71: The East Barge

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Mara asked me to come to work.

This should have felt like victory.

Instead it felt dangerous because I had spent three days trying to prove I could exist without being invited into every part of her life.

"Why?" I asked.

We were eating breakfast at Finch. She had come early, which meant Vey had either collapsed or temporarily released her.

"Because east barge needs independent guard for one transfer and Sera says your temporary Guild record is clean."

"You checked?"

"Sera did."

"Professional."

"Yes."

Good.

Not romantic favor.

"Pay?"

"Guild rate. Twelve copper. Two hours maybe."

"Who hires?"

"River contractor, not Vey."

Even better.

I accepted.

East barge transfer happened from outer berth because vessel too deep for Vey quay at low water. Cargo moved by smaller flatboats between barge and shore.

Guard job was not bandits.

It was keeping curious people and opportunistic thieves away from staging crates while crews moved back and forth.

Boring with water.

I liked it.

Contract lead was Silver woman named Ora with scar along throat and no patience for adventurer theater.

She saw Bronze tab.

"Mara's?"

I immediately stiffened.

Mara, beside Sera, said, "No."

Ora looked at me.

"Good answer."

"Greg."

"Ora. You stay shore stack. Nobody opens. Nobody moves without yellow token. If fight, don't chase past quay."

Clear.

"Magic?"

"Only if needed. River wards active. Don't interfere."

Good.

I stood shore stack while flatboats shuttled.

Mara worked manifests under awning twenty yards away.

We barely spoke.

That itself felt healthy enough to distrust.

People came.

Cart drivers. Clerks. One drunk asking directions. Two children trying see foreign barge.

I redirected.

A man with yellow token collected three crates.

I checked number.

One wrong.

He insisted clerk said four-seven.

Token said four-one.

I held.

Ora verified.

Token wrong from clerk, man right.

I had followed system and still been obstacle.

Fine.

Then weather shifted.

Wind came upriver hard.

Flatboat crossing became rough.

One boat approached loaded with two long crates and four workers.

Wave from passing tug hit broadside.

Boat rolled.

One crate strap snapped.

Crate slid.

Workers shouted.

It hit gunwale, half over water.

Boat tilted dangerously.

Everything happened twenty yards offshore.

I could not reach physically.

Ora ran to edge.

"Cut it loose!"

Workers hesitated.

Cargo value.

"Cut!"

One drew knife.

Before cut, second strap tore.

Crate went into river.

Boat snapped upright violently.

One worker fell overboard opposite side.

Now action.

River current took him immediately downstream.

He surfaced, grabbed nothing.

Quay had rescue lines.

Ora threw one.

Short.

I grabbed second coil.

Old Greg could throw far.

Current me maybe.

I ran downstream along quay while man drifted.

Mara shouted his name? "Len!"

I reached lower stairs, threw rope ahead of him.

It landed six feet beyond.

Current carried toward him.

He missed first grab.

I paid line.

"Again!"

He caught.

Rope jerked.

I wrapped around stone post, not hand.

Good physical habit from other horizons shouldn't transfer, but current C Greg has worked crane tag lines and hose, enough to know use post. Fine.

Ora joined.

Together hauled him toward stairs.

Current pulled hard.

Len tried climb rope and wasted energy.

"Stop climbing! Hold!"

He did.

We drew line sideways until he hit lower step.

Two dockworkers grabbed arms.

Out.

He coughed river water and swore.

Alive.

Crate floated? No, sank partially, tether? Broken straps so gone.

Flatboat crew reached berth.

Everyone counted.

No other injuries.

Mara arrived at lower steps but stopped behind Ora until lead allowed.

Good.

Ora checked Len breathing, called yard healer.

Then looked at river where crate disappeared.

"Mark position."

Sera already doing.

Cargo salvage became next task.

I stood soaked to knees from lower stairs, rope burn? Hands gloved, no injury.

Adrenaline clean.

Ora looked at me.

"Good throw."

I liked praise.

No need pretend.

"Second throw would have been better."

"There wasn't second."

Exactly.

Mara said nothing until healer took Len.

Then she grabbed front of my coat and kissed me once in full public view.

Not long.

Sera made noise behind us.

Mara released.

"Sorry."

"For?"

"Work."

"I am not complaining."

Ora said, "Complain later. We still have cargo."

Everyone returned.

I smiled for next hour like idiot.

Danger aftermath included crate salvage contract. River crew marked drop with weighted line. Diver team would recover later if possible.

Lost crate contents: brass loom parts, insured.

Flatboat operation paused while wind assessed.

Contractor chose smaller loads, two crates max.

Schedule slowed.

Mara had to rewrite receiving times.

Sera argued with barge master about delay penalties.

Len returned from healer wrapped blanket and tried to work. Ora sent him home.

He objected.

She said, "You swallowed river. Go vomit privately."

Leadership style effective.

At closeout, I received twelve copper plus two hazard.

Mara and I did not get dinner until late.

We ate in Vey yard on overturned crate because both too tired to go anywhere.

She had bread and cold sausage from office kitchen.

"You kissed me in front of Sera," I said.

"I know."

"Ora."

"I know."

"Tomas probably."

"Greg."

"I am cataloging consequences."

"The consequence is Sera will be unbearable."

From office window Sera shouted, "Already am!"

We laughed.

Then quieter.

"I was scared," Mara said.

There.

"When he fell?"

"When you ran."

I looked at her.

"I was on quay."

"I know."

"Not much danger to me."

"Doesn't matter."

No logic.

Human.

I touched her knee.

"I was scared when crate shifted too."

"For Len?"

"For everybody. And I liked knowing what to do."

Mara understood enough from prior conversations to hear second part.

"You still like emergencies."

"I like clarity."

"That's worse."

"Probably."

We sat with that.

River lights moved.

A salvage boat positioned over lost crate marker.

Current city continued work.

Mara leaned against me.

"Tomorrow no work together."

"Reason?"

"I want one day where if you do something stupid I don't have to file it."

Fair.
