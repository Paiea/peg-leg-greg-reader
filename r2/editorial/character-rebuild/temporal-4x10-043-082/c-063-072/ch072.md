# Rehearsal C72: Visitor

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

My last full day in Halden contained no emergency.

This was harder to manage than expected.

I had booked return packet boat for next morning.

Not because visit was over emotionally.

Because money, work, room, Carrow existed.

Mara had not asked me to stay longer.

I had not asked whether she wanted me to.

That omission became louder as day progressed.

We agreed no work together.

So we became tourists in her city, which irritated Mara because she was not tourist.

"Where do visitors go?" I asked.

"Why would I know?"

"You live here."

"Exactly."

We started at hill garden because Pell claimed view good.

Climb terrible.

Stone stairs again.

Mara walked fast until I complained.

"You're six-three," she said.

"Height does not create lungs."

"Old Greg climbed mountains."

"Current Greg had fried noodles for breakfast."

She slowed.

At top, city opened below.

Halden roof terraces, river bends, Vey yellow marks tiny, burned warehouse black gap near quay, festival lantern strings still hanging.

I could identify places now.

Green Finch roof.

Lessa's street.

Guild hall.

River stairs where Len came out.

Sera's office window maybe.

City had begun accumulating personal geography.

Dangerous.

Mara watched me looking.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Liar."

"I know things now."

"Congratulations."

"That wasn't sentence."

"It was."

We sat on low wall.

Children chased birds nearby.

An old man sold tea from cart.

No one needed us.

I bought tea.

Mara stole mine after hers too sweet.

"You ordered sugar."

"Mistake."

"Then live with consequence."

She drank mine anyway.

We spent morning walking neighborhoods she liked, not landmarks.

A narrow street with cobbler who fixed her boots.

Public wash where women shouted at each other across basins.

Bookshop that let her keep account because she always forgot coin.

Pell's machine shop closed Sundays but window full of brass gears.

At one corner woman upstairs shouted, "Mara!"

Mara looked up.

"Give back line!"

Woman laughed.

Washing-line thief.

Real.

I pointed.

"Enemy."

"Nessa."

"I will remember."

"Do not start feud."

"Too late."

Nessa threw clothespin at us.

Missed.

I kept it.

Evidence.

Lunch with Sera and her husband became accidental because we met them at noodle stall.

Her husband Orin was indeed around sixty, barrel-chested, gentle, and carried bottle of beer Sera called criminal.

He offered me taste.

It was terrible.

I tried polite face.

Sera said, "Don't lie."

"It is... memorable."

Orin laughed.

"Bad then."

"Yes."

He seemed delighted.

Jealousy of Sera died completely and was replaced by pity for beer.

Useful.

In afternoon Mara took me to old west wall where freight road leaves city.

Not river route.

Road toward smaller towns, eventually Carrow via bends.

"You came boat," she said.

"Yes."

"Next time road?"

There.

Next time.

My chest reacted.

"Invitation?"

"Don't legislate."

"I remember rule."

"Maybe road. Different view."

"I would like that."

Direct.

Mara smiled.

We walked outside gate perhaps one mile.

No reason except road.

Dust. Low vineyards. Stone drainage channels. Halden behind us.

I wanted to keep walking west.

Not toward anything specific.

Body responded to road now.

Mara noticed.

"You really do want it."

"Yes."

"Not just Old Greg?"

"I don't think so."

"Good."

She caught herself.

I looked.

"Evaluator."

"Shut up."

We laughed.

On return, a wagon wheel lost iron tire near gate.

No danger.

Just inconvenience.

Driver swore. People pushed wagon aside. Blacksmith apprentice came with hammer.

We helped move one crate from tilted side because it blocked lane.

Action small.

Bodies still doing things.

By evening we were tired from walking.

Dinner at Mara's lodging instead of restaurant.

Her room was smaller than Finch but more lived.

Desk under window covered papers.

Two cups, one blue.

Three books stacked under wobbly table leg instead of read.

Work coat on peg.

Hair pins in bowl.

A sock drying near stove that was not mine.

I stood in doorway too long.

Mara said, "Do not inventory."

"I am appreciating."

"Same face."

She cooked eggs and potatoes badly.

I offered help.

She refused.

Then burned onions and accepted.

We ate from one plate because she had only one clean.

Domestic fantasy tried to inflate.

I punctured it by remembering I was leaving tomorrow.

Mara saw mood shift.

"What?"

I almost said nothing.

"I don't want to leave."

There.

No manipulation.

No implied ask.

Just want.

Mara looked down at fork.

"I don't want you to either."

My chest hurt.

"Should I stay?"

Question came anyway.

Mara thought.

Actually thought.

"No."

I hated answer.

She continued.

"Not because I want you gone. Because you have Carrow. I have work. Another week becomes waiting around my shifts unless you build your own reason here."

"I found Guild."

"Yes."

"Could work."

"Do you want to?"

Silence.

Not really.

I wanted Mara.

"No."

"Then go home. Come again."

Home.

Lower Rook entered sentence from her mouth without being named.

That mattered.

I looked around her room.

"This is home?"

"Currently."

"Halden?"

"Currently."

Temporary could still be real.

I stayed late.

We were intimate, slow, no need explicit detail. Important part was awkward stop when Mara laughed because my knee hit desk and knocked papers everywhere.

We spent ten minutes crawling floor collecting Vey manifests while half dressed.

Romance with paperwork.

One page stuck beneath bed.

I reached.

Mara grabbed wrist.

"Confidential."

"I can't read upside down."

"Still."

I handed without looking.

Boundary inside intimacy.

At midnight I walked back to Finch alone because Mara worked first bell.

She did not escort.

I knew route now.

At corner I looked back once.

Her window lit.

Then went dark before I reached river.

Next morning she met me at boat anyway.

Not early? Both early.

She brought two rolls.

I laughed.

"What?"

"Nothing. Carrow problem."

We ate on river stairs.

When boarding called, Mara kissed me, handed my bag, and said, "Write when you arrive."

"I will."

"Not tax clerk."

"Tavin already has trademark."

She smiled.

I boarded.

This time I watched from deck while she stayed on stairs.

She did not leave before boat.

Different grammar.

No meaning explanation.

River pulled us apart physically.

Halden became smaller.

I stood until Mara was one dark figure among many.

Then went below because boat breakfast smelled better than longing.
