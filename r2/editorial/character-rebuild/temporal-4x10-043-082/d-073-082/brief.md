# Horizon D Rehearsal Brief — Coordinates 73–82

Status: **REHEARSAL ONLY / ZERO AUTHORITY**

## Pressure question

Can R2 support a larger, more dangerous ensemble movement without collapsing back into competence fantasy, and what happens when Old Greg's confidence is actively wrong while current people have to act around him?

D is the widest search horizon. It is allowed to be bold and wrong.

## Rehearsal purpose

Stress-test higher physical stakes while preserving ordinary consequences, social ownership, distinct people, and the character-first north star.

The point is not to prove Greg can handle a big incident. The point is to see what a big incident reveals when Greg's body, memory, friendships, work connections, home, and current limitations all collide.

## Local assumptions allowed only inside D

D may rehearse a plausible farther future where several existing circles have accumulated enough proximity to overlap. It may place Greg, Noll, Jorren, Tavin, filtration people, Guild workers, or new local specialists in the same broad event if each has independent reasons to be there.

No D-local convergence is true elsewhere.

## Action palette

- severe weather, structural failure, route closure, drainage or river problem
- unknown or misidentified magic phenomenon
- nonhuman ecology behaving differently than Old Greg expects
- crowded evacuation, rescue, hauling, climbing, ropes, carts, broken flooring, smoke, water, heat, or darkness
- Greg using sword or magic under pressure and paying a bodily cost
- another professional ordering Greg to stop or change approach
- a rescue where Greg is useful but violates someone's control path
- injury, damaged gear, missed work, bills, cleanup, hunger, poor sleep, and social aftermath
- quieter chapters after action where consequences continue physically

## Character pressure

Old Greg should be tempted to act on remembered certainty. At least once, that certainty should be materially incomplete or wrong.

Greg may save something and still anger someone. He may be brave for mixed motives. He may think he is protecting a person who resents being managed. He may discover that somebody he mentally categorized as `supporting` has more authority in the present situation than he does.

Noll, Jorren, Tavin, Arlo, Ivena, Dena, Sella, or new specialists should retain distinct action ownership if they appear. Nobody waits for Greg to decide whether the world moves.

## Setting pressure

Use a physically complicated environment where place controls action: river edge, service tunnels, old masonry, freight structures, steep streets, workshop district, flooded lower levels, heat, smoke, magical residue, animal movement, or weather.

The setting should not merely look dangerous. It should constrain routes, visibility, communication, leverage, timing, and bodies.

## Plot pressure

D should test a multi-chapter escalation rather than ten independent incidents:

- ordinary life or work before pressure
- warning signs or conflicting interpretations
- escalation
- wrong assumption
- physical crisis
- consequence
- social disagreement about what happened
- material cleanup / cost
- relationship residue
- an ending that leaves future possibility without declaring a new quest

This is a rehearsal pattern, not a mandatory chapter-per-beat outline.

## Prohibitions

- no generic apocalypse
- no Greg as sole savior
- no professionals becoming exposition terminals
- no danger that leaves no bodily/material aftermath
- no Old Greg reveal that instantly explains the phenomenon
- no rank-up as payoff
- no `everyone respected him afterward` ending
- no using action to avoid relationship consequence

## Success

D succeeds if a larger action run still feels like R2: specific, bodily, social, funny in places, materially consequential, and centered on a man whose memories are not the same thing as a life.
