# Rehearsal D74: Three Explanations

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

By morning, the wall had cooled.

This made everyone less comfortable.

If it had stayed warm, we could have called it a warm wall and argued about why.

Instead, after a night of hard rain, the Bell Tile service passage returned to the temperature of every other damp stone corridor in Carrow.

The wax tab Dena had pressed to the wall remained unsoftened.

The second tab, six feet away, looked exactly the same.

Our measurement system had successfully proved that cheap wax was cheap.

"Excellent," I said.

Dena crouched beside the tabs.

"You sound angry at wax."

"It had one job."

"We don't know its melt point."

"Then it had no job."

Harl tapped the floor with his cane.

"Wall cold. Go home."

"You came here."

"To tell you that."

The old mason had arrived before me.

This weakened his argument.

Bell Tile's owner, Serit, stood at the top of the stair with his arms crossed. Ivena was beside him holding a paper that I suspected contained money. Arlo had one hand on the replacement filtration collar and the other on a small measuring rod he had built overnight from two metals riveted together.

Pellin held the lamp.

The city had sent a drainage inspector named Vaska.

She had introduced herself by looking at the warm-wall notes, saying, "Who wrote `pulses maybe three-two-one`?" and then staring at me when I raised my hand.

Vaska was perhaps forty. Short. Thick black braid. Waterproof coat still wet at the shoulders. She carried three iron keys on a ring large enough to suggest the city expected drainage infrastructure to attempt escape.

She touched the cold wall once.

Then the wet opposite wall.

Then looked at the ceiling.

"Three explanations," she said.

I liked her immediately.

This was usually dangerous.

"One," Vaska said, "old drain behind wall fills during rain, water friction or mineral reaction warms stone. Two, old ward or heat-work under foundation wakes when channels fill. Three, Bell Tile is secretly cooking something under the street and Serit is lying."

Serit said, "I am not cooking anything."

Vaska looked at him.

"Then we are down to two."

"There are more than two," I said.

Everyone looked at me.

I had counted at least six on the walk over.

Subsurface creature colony.

Mana pump.

Unmapped kiln vent.

Pressure-bearing ward.

Thermal spring.

Old mechanical system.

And those were categories, not explanations.

Vaska said, "Do any of yours come with keys?"

I looked at hers.

"No."

"Then mine first."

I hated how reasonable that was.

She went to the far end of the passage where a low iron door sat almost flush with the floor.

I had assumed storage.

Old Greg memory had not supplied anything useful.

Vaska used the smallest key.

The lock resisted.

She kicked the lower edge once.

Rust flakes fell.

Then the key turned.

The door opened inward onto darkness and colder air.

A drainage crawl.

Not a corridor.

Crawl was accurate.

Three feet high.

Stone channel along the center.

Narrow ledges on both sides.

Water moved through the channel at ankle depth, brown from yesterday's rain.

Vaska knelt.

Held the lamp low.

"This goes under the warm section?" Dena asked.

"Parallel first. Turns west after sixty feet."

"Plan?"

Vaska tapped her head.

"Useful plan," I said.

She looked at me.

I had done it again.

"Sorry."

"For what?"

"Nothing."

Good recovery.

Vaska handed me a second lamp.

"You coming?"

My body answered before judgment.

I was already crouching.

Harl's cane hooked the back of my belt.

I stopped.

"What?"

"You don't know where goes."

"Vaska does."

"Then let Vaska go."

Vaska said, "I need second if I open lower gate."

Harl considered.

Then pointed the cane at Dena.

"Her."

I looked at him.

"Why her?"

"She listens."

Dena made a small satisfied noise.

Traitor.

Vaska looked between us.

"Either. I need somebody who can haul gate if silted."

Dena held up both hands.

"I need to watch our line temperature. Greg can crawl."

Harl released my belt.

"Bad decision."

"Shared ownership," I said.

"Shut up."

I went in.

The first twenty feet were awful in a mundane way.

Stone ceiling low enough that I moved on hands and knees.

Lamp in one hand.

Cold water splashing the ledge when my boot slipped.

Old mortar rough beneath my palms.

The smell was wet earth, mineral, something stale from trapped air, and faint sulfur.

The same sulfur as the cracked collar.

Vaska moved ahead with practiced efficiency.

She wore knee pads sewn into her trousers.

I did not.

By thirty feet I had learned why drainage inspectors deserved pensions.

"How often do you do this?" I asked.

My voice sounded strange in the low tunnel.

"Enough."

"Useful unit."

"You talk when nervous?"

"No."

"Then you're always like this?"

"Apparently."

She laughed once.

The channel turned west.

Water became deeper.

Not much.

Ankle to mid-shin when we had to step into it around a collapsed ledge.

Cold entered my boots immediately.

I thought of dry road socks in another possible life I was not supposed to know here.

No.

Current accepted truth had road socks in a field pack.

Fine.

I had worn ordinary socks.

Bad decision independent of chronology.

At the turn, Vaska stopped.

She raised her lamp.

Blue marks covered the ceiling.

Harl had been right.

Not one ward.

A chain of hand-sized symbols painted or burned into stone every three feet.

Faded.

Some almost gone.

Old style.

My pulse increased.

I knew this family.

Maybe.

Force distribution.

Pressure transfer.

The geometry resembled retaining wards used in submerged structures farther south.

Except one stroke on every third mark bent inward instead of out.

"Don't touch," Vaska said.

I had not moved my hand.

I had moved my eyes in a touching way apparently.

"These could be pressure wards."

"Could."

"You know them?"

"I know they are blue."

Harl's scholarship spread.

I leaned closer without touching.

Mana residue faint.

Not dead.

Old Greg knew how to wake similar arrays.

A small pulse through the anchor line.

Test response.

I could do it with almost no power.

That thought came with hand position.

Two fingers at lower mark.

Mana thread.

Wait for echo.

Simple.

My hand lifted.

Vaska caught my wrist.

Fast.

"No."

I looked at her fingers around me.

"I was going to sense."

"You can sense from there."

"Closer is clearer."

"And wetter."

She pointed at the channel.

I looked.

Water moved beneath the lowest marks.

If the ward dumped force into water, current behavior unknown.

If it was a heat-transfer array, same problem.

If it was nothing, touching achieved nothing.

I knew all this.

My hand had still moved.

"Fine."

Vaska released me.

"You always know before touching?"

"Often."

"Bad answer."

We continued.

The lower gate was forty feet farther.

Iron grille across the channel.

Half-covered in branches, cloth, and a dead rat that had achieved civic significance.

Water pushed through clogged gaps.

Vaska swore.

"This should've been cleared spring."

"Why wasn't it?"

"Budget."

Magic met government.

There it was.

We set lamps on the ledge.

Vaska used a hooked bar to pull debris.

I held a sack open.

Branches first.

Cloth.

Leaves packed into black mats.

Rat last.

I refused to think about whether gloves were thick enough.

As the grille cleared, water speed increased.

A deep vibration moved through the floor.

I froze.

Pulse.

Three.

Pause.

Two.

Same as yesterday.

"Feel?"

Vaska nodded.

She put one hand on the stone.

Another pulse.

Not from wall.

From water pressure changing as debris came free.

"Gate chatter," she said.

"Gate isn't moving."

"Lower gate."

"Another?"

"Two levels down."

"How deep?"

"Enough."

"Is it supposed to chatter?"

"No."

That was more interesting.

We cleared the grille.

Water dropped half an inch within minutes.

The sulfur smell strengthened.

Vaska crouched beside the channel.

Touched water.

Pulled her fingers back.

"Warm."

I touched it too.

She glared.

"You just did that."

"You established safety."

"I established temperature."

"Also safety accidentally."

Water was warmer than the air.

Not hot.

Same warmth as yesterday's wall perhaps.

Flowing from deeper west.

"Thermal spring," I said.

"Maybe."

"Sulfur fits."

"Maybe."

"Warm water heats wall during high flow."

"Maybe."

I began to dislike her vocabulary.

Then I saw the blue marks again.

"Why wards?"

Vaska shrugged.

"Why did people build anything two hundred years ago?"

"Usually because something killed them."

"Comforting."

We returned to Bell Tile with wet knees, wet boots, a sack of civic debris, and less certainty than we entered with.

Dena had three temperature readings from Arlo's improvised metal strip.

Not precise numbers.

Relative bend.

The pipe support nearest the wall had warmed slightly as rainwater drainage increased overnight, then cooled through morning.

"Flow explains heat," I said.

Harl grunted.

"Told you water."

"You said water after pulses."

"Still water."

Vaska emptied the debris sack into a basin.

Serit saw the rat.

"Why is that in my tileworks?"

"City property," Vaska said.

"Take it."

"Gladly."

Ivena returned from upstairs with two cups of tea.

She handed one to Dena, none to me.

Favoritism.

"So?"

Vaska gave the simple version.

Clogged upper grille.

Warm drainage water.

Old ward marks.

Lower gate chatter.

Need deeper inspection after water level falls.

No digging.

Harl looked directly at me on the last part.

I ignored him.

"Could the warm water be causing ceramic cycling?" Arlo asked.

"Yes," Dena said.

I said, "Maybe."

Everyone looked at me.

Vaska smiled.

I hated her.

Arlo rubbed the cracked collar with one thumb.

"Then we isolate mounts from wall and see if it stops."

"Good first move," Dena said.

Serit said, "How long service down?"

Ivena answered, "Until tomorrow minimum."

"Customers waiting."

"So are cracked pipes."

Their argument moved away.

I stood near the cold wall.

Three explanations had become at least four.

Warm drainage could explain heat.

Heat could explain pipe expansion.

Blue wards remained unexplained.

Lower gate chatter remained unexplained.

Sulfur could be spring or something else.

The system was becoming less magical and more interesting.

I put my palm against the stone.

Cold.

No pulse.

Harl's cane tapped my boot.

"What now?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

"I want to see lower gate."

"No."

"Vaska is going."

Vaska was tying the debris sack shut.

She looked over.

"Tomorrow if level drops."

"I can come."

She considered me.

"Why?"

The correct answer was filtration because the heat might affect our installed line.

The honest answer arrived first.

"I want to know."

Vaska nodded.

"Better answer."

Harl swore softly.

I smiled.

Then the floor gave one small pulse beneath my boots.

Not three.

One.

Everyone felt it.

The room went quiet.

Vaska looked toward the crawl door.

Harl stopped smiling at nobody.

Dena said, "Water?"

Vaska did not answer immediately.

That was the first thing all morning that actually scared me.
