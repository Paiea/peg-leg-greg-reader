# Rehearsal D75: Lower Gate

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The city brought a rope.

This made me feel better until I saw the man carrying it.

Kerren.

Of course.

Carrow had enough people that unrelated problems should have remained unrelated longer than three days. Apparently infrastructure preferred social density.

He walked into Bell Tile's courtyard with a coil over one shoulder, Ressa behind him carrying a harness, and Vaska beside them holding the large ring of drainage keys.

I was already there.

This became a problem immediately.

Kerren looked at me.

Then at the stairwell to the service passage.

Then back.

"No."

"Good morning."

"Why are you here?"

"Drainage."

"You work drainage now?"

"No."

"Good. Leave."

Dena came out of the workshop door carrying Arlo's metal temperature strip.

"He's with us."

Kerren looked at her.

"Why?"

"He touched wall first."

This was terrible credentialing.

Ressa grinned.

"Gloves?"

I held up both hands.

"Yes."

"Learning."

Everyone had one joke.

Vaska ignored all of us.

"Lower gate is fourteen feet below upper crawl," she said. "Access shaft is behind sealed grating in the west cut. Yesterday's pulse means either gate shifted or something struck it. Water level dropped enough overnight to inspect."

"Why rigger?" I asked.

Kerren said, "Because city shaft ladder lost three rungs."

"That seems relevant."

"And because Vaska has more sense than you."

Vaska nodded.

"Correct."

Conspiracy expanding.

Bell Tile had suspended filtration service another day. Serit complained upstairs. Ivena had converted complaint into a written rate adjustment and seemed almost cheerful about the violence.

Arlo and Pellin were replacing wall-side pipe mounts with spacers that separated ceramic from masonry.

Dena planned to stay with them after the descent began.

Harl had refused to come.

His exact message, delivered by Pellin, was:

`If wall opens, run uphill.`

Useful.

We entered the service passage.

The warm wall remained cold.

Dry patch still obvious against damp opposite stone.

No pulses.

Vaska opened the upper crawl gate.

Water lower than yesterday.

I knelt.

Kerren caught my shoulder before I entered.

"Harness."

"For crawl?"

"For shaft."

"I can put it on there."

"Put it on here."

I almost asked why.

Then remembered he had spent years preventing falling objects from becoming workers.

I put it on.

Leather straps around thighs and waist. Chest loop. Iron ring between shoulder blades.

Old Greg had worn better harnesses.

Old Greg had also fallen from worse things.

Current Greg tightened the thigh strap until Kerren slapped my hand away and loosened it one notch.

"Blood is useful."

"I know."

"You say that a lot."

Ressa laughed.

Vaska went first into the crawl.

I followed.

Kerren and Ressa behind with rope and tools.

Four adults moving on hands and knees through a wet stone tube made authority less impressive.

At the blue marks, Kerren stopped.

"Ward?"

"Unknown," I said.

"You touch?"

"No."

Vaska said, "He tried."

"I sensed."

"With hand."

Kerren made a sound.

I kept moving.

The cleared upper grille let water run faster. Sulfur smell remained faint. The lower section beyond it descended enough that we had to walk in the channel itself, bent nearly double.

Cold water entered my boots again.

"I need drainage boots," I said.

Vaska said, "You need profession."

"I have one."

"Which?"

I opened my mouth.

Guild work.

Filtration owner.

Adventurer.

Ward-capable labor.

None answered neatly.

"Complicated."

"Bad profession."

The access shaft appeared as a square hole in the right wall behind an iron grating.

Vaska used the largest key.

This lock opened easier.

Behind it, a narrow stone platform dropped into darkness.

The ladder bolted to the wall had, as advertised, lost three rungs.

One near top.

Two in the middle.

Water could be heard below.

Not seen.

Kerren set a lamp at the edge.

"Depth?"

"Fourteen to gate platform," Vaska said. "Another six to channel bed."

"Anchor?"

She pointed at an iron eye set into ceiling stone.

Kerren tested it with a hammer.

Then another.

"Old."

"Everything here old."

He looked at the masonry around it.

"Need backup."

Ressa fed rope around a second structural bar outside the grating.

I watched.

Wanted to help.

Did not know their system.

This was becoming repetitive in a useful way.

Kerren clipped Vaska's harness to the main line.

"You first."

"I know."

"Say it back."

Vaska rolled her eyes.

"Main line tension. Ladder for balance, not weight. Stop at missing rung. Call before transferring."

Kerren nodded.

She descended.

Slow.

Boot on rung.

Hands on side rails.

Rope taking part of her weight.

At first gap, she leaned back in harness and stepped across.

No drama.

At second missing section, she called.

Kerren paid line.

Then she disappeared beneath the lamp edge.

"Platform," she called.

Ressa looked at me.

"Your turn."

My stomach tightened.

Not fear exactly.

Body recognizing drop.

Old Greg had rappelled cliffs taller than cities.

Current Greg's pulse increased at fourteen feet in a sewer.

I stepped to edge.

Kerren clipped me in.

"Main line tension. Ladder balance. Missing rung stop. Call."

"I heard."

His hand stayed on the clip.

I sighed.

"Main line tension. Ladder for balance, not weight. Stop at missing rung. Call before transferring."

He released me.

"See? Learning."

"I hate you."

"Down."

I descended.

First gap easy.

At middle gap, my left boot searched for a rung memory insisted should exist because feet prefer continuity.

Nothing.

My weight shifted.

Harness caught.

Rope tightened across chest.

I stopped.

"Gap," I called.

"Transfer," Kerren answered.

I leaned back farther.

Stepped past.

Boot found rung.

Fine.

No heroics.

At bottom, Vaska stood on a narrow stone platform beside a vertical iron wheel almost as tall as she was.

Gate control.

The channel beyond ran through an arch beneath us.

Water brown and fast.

Blue ward marks continued down the wall.

More visible here.

Not paint.

Inlaid mineral or glass.

They glowed faintly when water struck the lower gate.

I stared.

"There," I said.

Vaska followed my gaze.

"I see."

"Active."

"Yes."

"Yesterday you said blue."

"Yesterday water too high."

"You knew they could glow?"

"No."

Good.

Shared ignorance.

Ressa descended next.

Kerren stayed above to control line.

Three of us crowded the platform.

Vaska examined the wheel.

One spoke bent slightly.

Fresh scrape on iron.

"Gate shifted," she said.

"From pressure?"

"Maybe."

I smiled.

She saw.

"Do not."

"Nothing."

Water struck the gate below in repeating pulses.

Not random.

Three stronger surges.

Pause.

Two.

Then steady.

Same pattern.

I crouched at platform edge.

Ressa grabbed my harness strap.

"I am not falling."

"Good. Makes job easier."

I leaned enough to see.

The gate was a sliding iron panel set into channel walls. Mostly open, maybe three-quarters. Debris jammed along lower left edge.

Something pale moved behind it.

I froze.

"There."

Vaska crouched beside me.

Pale shape moved again.

Long.

Low.

Then vanished under brown water.

Ressa said, "Fish?"

"Too broad," I said.

Old Greg supplied limebacks.

Subterranean amphibious scavengers. Flat bodies, mineral plates, warm-water nesting. Usually harmless unless cornered. Found farther south in quarry drains.

My certainty rose immediately.

Too fast.

"Could be limeback."

Vaska looked at me.

"What's that?"

I described.

Ressa said, "Bite?"

"If cornered."

"Large?"

"Depends age."

"This Carrow thing?"

"I don't know."

There.

Important answer.

Water surged.

Three.

Pause.

Two.

The pale shape struck gate from behind on second pulse.

Iron shuddered.

Vibration traveled through platform.

"Not water," Vaska said.

My skin tightened.

"Something hitting."

Ressa called upward.

"Kerren! Animal or something behind gate."

His answer echoed down.

"Then come up."

Reasonable.

I wanted to stay.

Strongly.

Because if limeback, leaving it trapped could worsen pressure and injure animal. Because if not, we needed identification. Because the gate already shifted.

Because unknown thing behind stone had been scratching my mind for two days.

Vaska stood.

"Up."

"Wait."

She looked at me.

"If gate is jammed and animal is hitting it, pressure might be from both. We can clear debris from this side."

"Without knowing animal?"

"If limeback, they avoid open air."

"If not?"

I hated her.

"Then maybe not."

She pointed upward.

"Exactly."

I looked at water again.

Pale back surfaced behind the gate.

Not plated like limeback.

Smooth.

Almost translucent.

A ridge along center pulsed red once.

Old Greg memory went blank.

Completely.

That should have settled it.

Instead curiosity sharpened.

My hand went toward sword hilt.

Ressa said, "Why sword?"

I stopped.

What was I planning?

If creature came through gate, sword.

If sword in narrow water channel, terrible.

If unknown creature reacted to metal, worse.

I removed hand.

"Bad reflex."

Vaska looked at me.

Not judgment.

Assessment.

"Up," she said again.

This time I agreed.

Ressa climbed first.

Then me.

At the second missing rung, the thing struck gate hard enough that the shaft wall vibrated.

My boot slipped.

Rope snapped tight.

Chest strap caught me.

I swung six inches away from ladder.

Stone hit my right knee.

Pain flashed.

"Hold!" Kerren shouted.

I grabbed side rail.

My body wanted to climb fast.

Kerren's rope held me still.

"Don't climb! Find rung!"

"I have rail."

"Find rung."

I looked down.

Boot found lower rung.

Transferred.

Then climbed.

At top, Kerren grabbed the back of harness and hauled me onto crawl ledge.

"Knee?"

"Fine."

"Show."

"I can walk."

"Show."

Ressa was already checking it before I finished arguing.

Trousers scuffed.

Skin beneath bruised, no cut.

"Fine," she said.

I looked at Kerren.

"See?"

"You don't get points for correct accident prediction after accident."

"I didn't predict."

"Worse."

Vaska came up last.

We crawled back to Bell Tile.

Dena saw my wet clothing and knee limp.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

Three people said, "He slipped," at once.

I hated groups.

Vaska spread a drainage plan on Bell Tile's worktable upstairs.

Actual paper this time.

Old copy.

Lower gate marked as pressure division between two channels. One continued west under three buildings before turning toward river. Another descended south and vanished beyond plan edge.

"Creature behind gate," she said.

Serit stepped backward from the table as if paper could bite.

"What creature?"

"Unknown."

I said, "Not limeback."

Kerren looked at me.

"You guessed limeback?"

"Then updated."

"Professional."

"Shut up."

Dena traced the west channel.

"This runs under our wall?"

"Yes," Vaska said.

"Then animal hitting gate could create pulses. Warm water still separate?"

"Maybe not."

Pellin leaned over the table.

"What if animal makes warm water?"

Everyone looked at him.

He shrugged.

"You said wall warm when rain. Animal trapped when rain."

Simple.

Possible.

I had not considered it because I had separated thermal and animal explanations too early.

Arlo said, "Then pipe heat still same result."

Ivena said, "And city owns animal?"

Vaska looked at her.

"City does not own animals."

"City owns drain."

"Yes."

"Animal is in drain."

"That is not how ownership works."

"Good. Put it in writing."

Even unknown creatures met invoices eventually.

Kerren coiled rope.

"What next?"

Vaska looked at the plan.

"We need access west side of gate."

"From where?"

She pointed to a mark beneath a building labeled `OLD SALT STORE`.

Serit said, "That's gone."

"What's there?"

"Tenements."

Vaska sighed.

More people.

More current life above old infrastructure.

The unknown animal struck somewhere below again.

Faint through stone.

Three.

Pause.

Two.

Pellin counted under his breath.

I looked at the plan.

Old Greg knew nothing.

Current Carrow had keys, a rigger, a drainage inspector, a tile owner, three filtration owners, an apprentice, an old mason who refused to come, and some translucent thing trapped under tenements.

My knee hurt.

I wanted to go west immediately.

Vaska folded the plan.

"After meal."

"Why wait?"

She looked at my knee.

"Because people eat."

I opened my mouth.

Dena handed me bread before I could object.

I ate.
