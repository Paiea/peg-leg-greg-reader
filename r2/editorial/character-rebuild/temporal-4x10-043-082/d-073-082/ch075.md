# Rehearsal D75: Lower Gate

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The city brought a rope.

This made me feel better until I saw how much rope.

A broad-shouldered man in a gray work coat walked into Bell Tile's courtyard with a coil over one shoulder. A younger woman followed carrying two harnesses and an iron pulley block. Vaska came behind them with the large ring of drainage keys.

I had never seen either rope worker before.

Good.

Current Carrow still contained people who did not already know my failures.

Vaska pointed at the man.

"Ilan. City rigging list."

Then the woman.

"Mett. Works with him."

Ilan looked at the stairwell to the service passage.

"Shaft?"

"Fourteen feet to gate platform," Vaska said. "Ladder lost three rungs. Another six feet to channel bed. Water below."

Ilan shifted the coil off his shoulder.

"Anchor?"

"Old iron eye."

"Old how?"

"Older than my office."

"Bad unit."

I liked him immediately.

This was usually dangerous.

Dena came out of the workshop door carrying Arlo's metal temperature strip.

"Greg's with us," she said.

Ilan looked at me.

"Why?"

Dena considered.

"He touched the wall first."

This was terrible credentialing.

Mett gave me a quick once-over, then looked at Dena.

"He rig?"

"No."

"Drainage?"

"No."

"City?"

"No."

I said, "This introduction is going well."

Vaska ignored all of us.

"He senses magic and refuses to stay away from questions."

Ilan nodded.

"That I understand."

Bell Tile had suspended filtration service another day. Serit complained upstairs. Ivena had converted complaint into a written rate adjustment and seemed almost cheerful about the violence.

Arlo and Pellin were replacing wall-side pipe mounts with spacers that separated ceramic from masonry.

Dena planned to stay with them after the descent began.

Harl had refused to come.

His exact message, delivered by Pellin, was:

`If wall opens, run uphill.`

Comforting.

We entered the service passage.

The warm wall remained cold.

Dry patch still obvious against damp opposite stone.

No pulses.

Vaska opened the upper crawl gate.

Water lower than yesterday.

I knelt.

Mett caught the back of my coat.

"Harness first."

"For crawl?"

"For shaft. Easier here than kneeling in water."

Reasonable.

I put it on.

Leather straps around thighs and waist. Chest loop. Iron ring between shoulder blades.

Old Greg had worn better harnesses.

Old Greg had also trusted harnesses that later tried to kill him.

Current Greg tightened the thigh strap until Mett pushed two fingers beneath it.

"Loosen."

"It's secure."

"Your leg needs blood."

I loosened it one notch.

Ilan watched.

"First time in that harness?"

"This one."

"That answer worries me."

"It should not."

"Now more."

Vaska went first into the crawl.

I followed.

Ilan and Mett came behind with rope and tools.

Four adults moving on hands and knees through a wet stone tube made authority less impressive.

At the blue marks, Ilan stopped.

"Ward?"

"Unknown," I said.

"Live?"

"Faintly."

"Touch?"

"Not now."

Vaska looked back.

"He tried yesterday."

"I sensed."

"With hand."

Mett laughed.

I kept moving.

The cleared upper grille let water run faster. Sulfur smell remained faint. The lower section beyond it descended enough that we had to walk in the channel itself, bent nearly double.

Cold water entered my boots again.

"I need drainage boots."

Vaska said, "You need a profession."

"I have several."

"That's worse."

The access shaft appeared as a square hole in the right wall behind an iron grating.

Vaska used the largest key.

This lock opened easier.

Behind it, a narrow stone platform dropped into darkness.

The ladder bolted to the wall had, as advertised, lost three rungs.

One near top.

Two in the middle.

Water could be heard below.

Not seen.

Ilan set a lamp at the edge.

He knelt and hammered the ceiling eye twice.

Rust dust fell.

"No."

Vaska looked at him.

"No?"

"Not alone."

He crawled backward to a structural bar outside the grating and fed rope around it.

Mett passed him the pulley block.

They built a second anchor without discussion.

I watched closely.

Wanted to help.

Did not know their system.

Good enough reason not to.

Ilan clipped Vaska's harness to the main line.

"Say the transfer."

Vaska rolled her eyes.

"Main line takes weight. Ladder is balance only. Stop at missing rung. Call before transfer."

"Again if scared."

"I am not scared."

"Then remember while arrogant."

I liked him less now because he sounded applicable.

Vaska descended.

Slow.

Boot on rung.

Hands on side rails.

Rope taking part of her weight.

At the first gap, she leaned back in harness and stepped across.

At the second missing section, she called.

Ilan paid line.

Then she disappeared beneath the lamp edge.

"Platform," she called.

Mett looked at me.

"Your turn."

My stomach tightened.

Not fear exactly.

Body recognizing drop.

Old Greg had rappelled cliffs taller than cities.

Current Greg's pulse increased at fourteen feet in a sewer.

I stepped to the edge.

Ilan clipped me in.

"Tell me."

"Main line tension. Ladder for balance. Stop at missing rung. Call before transfer."

"Good."

"I can remember three instructions."

"People fall while remembering more."

He released me.

I descended.

First gap easy.

At the middle gap, my left boot searched for a rung memory insisted should exist because feet prefer continuity.

Nothing.

My weight shifted.

Harness caught.

Rope tightened across chest.

I stopped.

"Gap," I called.

"Transfer," Ilan answered.

I leaned back farther.

Stepped past.

Boot found rung.

Fine.

At bottom, Vaska stood on a narrow stone platform beside a vertical iron wheel almost as tall as she was.

Gate control.

The channel beyond ran through an arch beneath us.

Water brown and fast.

Blue ward marks continued down the wall.

More visible here.

Not paint.

Inlaid mineral or glass.

They glowed faintly when water struck the lower gate.

I stared.

"There."

Vaska followed my gaze.

"I see."

"Active."

"Yes."

"Yesterday they did not glow."

"Yesterday water was higher."

"You knew they could?"

"No."

Shared ignorance.

Mett descended next.

Ilan stayed above to control line.

Three of us crowded the platform.

Vaska examined the wheel.

One spoke bent slightly.

Fresh scrape on iron.

"Gate shifted."

"From pressure?"

"Maybe."

I smiled.

She saw.

"Do not."

"Nothing."

Water struck the gate below in repeating pulses.

Not random.

Three stronger surges.

Pause.

Two.

Then steady.

Same pattern.

I crouched at the platform edge.

Mett grabbed my harness strap.

"I am not falling."

"Then my hand changes nothing."

Hard to argue with that.

I leaned enough to see.

The gate was a sliding iron panel set into channel walls. Mostly open, maybe three-quarters. Debris jammed along the lower left edge.

Something pale moved behind it.

I froze.

"There."

Vaska crouched beside me.

Pale shape moved again.

Long.

Low.

Then vanished under brown water.

Mett said, "Fish?"

"Too broad."

Old Greg supplied limebacks.

Subterranean amphibious scavengers. Flat bodies, mineral plates, warm-water nesting. Usually harmless unless cornered. Found farther south in quarry drains.

My certainty rose immediately.

Too fast.

"Could be limeback."

Vaska looked at me.

"What's that?"

I described it.

Mett said, "Bite?"

"If cornered."

"Large?"

"Depends on age."

"Carrow animal?"

"I don't know."

Water surged.

Three.

Pause.

Two.

The pale shape struck the gate from behind on the second pulse.

Iron shuddered.

Vibration traveled through the platform.

"Not water," Vaska said.

My skin tightened.

"Something hitting."

Mett called upward.

"Ilan! Animal or something behind gate."

His answer echoed down.

"Then come up."

Reasonable.

I wanted to stay.

Strongly.

If limeback, leaving it trapped could worsen pressure and injure the animal. If not, we needed identification. The gate had already shifted.

Unknown thing behind stone had been scratching my mind for two days.

Vaska stood.

"Up."

"Wait."

She looked at me.

"If gate is jammed and animal is hitting it, pressure might be from both. We can clear debris from this side."

"Without knowing the animal?"

"If limeback, they avoid open air."

"If not?"

I hated her.

"Then maybe not."

She pointed upward.

"Exactly."

I looked at the water again.

The pale back surfaced behind the gate.

Not plated like limeback.

Smooth.

Almost translucent.

A ridge along the center pulsed red once.

Old Greg memory went blank.

Completely.

That should have settled it.

Instead curiosity sharpened.

My hand went toward my sword hilt.

Mett said, "Why sword?"

I stopped.

What was I planning?

If creature came through gate, sword.

If sword in narrow water channel, terrible.

If unknown creature reacted to metal, worse.

I removed my hand.

"Bad reflex."

Vaska looked at me.

Not judgment.

Assessment.

"Up," she said again.

This time I agreed.

Mett climbed first.

Then me.

At the second missing rung, the thing struck the gate hard enough that the shaft wall vibrated.

My boot slipped.

Rope snapped tight.

Chest strap caught me.

I swung six inches away from the ladder.

Stone hit my right knee.

Pain flashed.

"Hold!" Ilan shouted.

I grabbed the side rail.

My body wanted to climb fast.

Ilan held the rope still.

"Don't climb. Find the rung."

"I have the rail."

"Find the rung."

I looked down.

Boot found lower rung.

Transferred.

Then climbed.

At the top, Ilan grabbed the back of my harness and hauled me onto the crawl ledge.

"Knee?"

"Fine."

"Show."

"I can walk."

"Show."

Mett was already checking it before I finished arguing.

Trousers scuffed.

Skin beneath bruised, no cut.

"Fine," she said.

I looked at Ilan.

"See?"

"You slipped. Harness worked. Those are separate facts."

Annoying.

Vaska came up last.

We crawled back to Bell Tile.

Dena saw my wet clothing and knee limp.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

Three people said, "He slipped," at once.

I hated groups.

Vaska spread a drainage plan on Bell Tile's worktable upstairs.

Actual paper this time.

Old copy.

The lower gate was marked as a pressure division between two channels. One continued west under three buildings before turning toward the river. Another descended south and vanished beyond the plan edge.

"Creature behind gate," Vaska said.

Serit stepped backward from the table as if paper could bite.

"What creature?"

"Unknown."

I said, "Not limeback."

Ilan looked at me.

"You guessed?"

"Then updated."

"Good. Keep the second part."

I did not need another philosophy source.

Dena traced the west channel.

"This runs under our wall?"

"Yes," Vaska said.

"Then animal hitting the gate could create pulses. Warm water still separate?"

"Maybe not."

Pellin leaned over the table.

"What if the animal makes the warm water?"

Everyone looked at him.

He shrugged.

"You said wall warm when rain. Animal trapped when rain."

Simple.

Possible.

I had not considered it because I had separated thermal and animal explanations too early.

Arlo said, "Then pipe heat still same result."

Ivena said, "And city owns animal?"

Vaska looked at her.

"City does not own animals."

"City owns drain."

"Yes."

"Animal is in drain."

"That is not how ownership works."

"Good. Put it in writing."

Even unknown creatures met invoices eventually.

Ilan coiled rope.

"What next?"

Vaska looked at the plan.

"We need access west side of the gate."

"From where?"

She pointed to a mark beneath a building labeled `OLD SALT STORE`.

Serit said, "That's gone."

"What's there?"

"Tenements."

Vaska sighed.

More people.

More current life above old infrastructure.

The unknown animal struck somewhere below again.

Faint through stone.

Three.

Pause.

Two.

Pellin counted under his breath.

I looked at the plan.

Old Greg knew nothing.

Current Carrow had keys, two rope workers, a drainage inspector, a tile owner, three filtration owners, an apprentice, an old mason who refused to come, and some translucent thing trapped under tenements.

My knee hurt.

I wanted to go west immediately.

Vaska folded the plan.

"After meal."

"Why wait?"

She looked at my knee.

"Because people eat."

I opened my mouth.

Dena handed me bread before I could object.

I ate.
