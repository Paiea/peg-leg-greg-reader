# Rehearsal D77: Three, Two

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The creature stopped hitting the gate after we opened the bakery wall.

That was worse.

Silence arrived underground by afternoon.

Vaska disliked it enough to check twice.

Bell Tile wall cooled completely.

Warm water continued draining from Old Salt stair but no new pulses.

The city posted guards at bakery basement, which made Ema furious because customers assumed crime.

She put a sign outside:

**BREAD OPEN. SEWER CLOSED.**

Business improved.

Carrow remained Carrow.

I spent morning at Bell Tile helping Dena isolate the service pipe. Arlo wanted to reopen one filtration route because customer pressure. Ivena said no until water temperature normal for full day.

"We're losing money," Arlo said.

"We lose more if hot mystery enters product," Ivena said.

Pellin asked, "Can we boil with it?"

Everyone stared.

"What?"

"If warm water free."

Arlo started considering.

Ivena pointed at door.

"Both of you out."

They did not leave, but idea died.

At noon Vaska came with new plan copied from municipal archive.

Not drainage map.

Old heating-service plan from before salt store conversion.

There.

Blue lines labeled **RETURN WARMTH** in faded hand.

"District heating?" I asked.

"Maybe older bath system," Vaska said. "No city record after warehouse fire forty-three years ago."

"Forty-three?"

Old Greg memory twitched.

Nothing useful.

"Why wards?"

"Unknown."

"Creature?"

"Still unknown."

Dena traced line.

It ran beneath Bell Tile, Old Salt, then west toward river pumping house long demolished.

Three branches.

One ended at lower gate.

One at bakery stair.

Third continued south off surviving sheet.

"Three," Pellin said.

We looked.

He tapped branches.

"Then two?"

Three branches.

Two what?

He made impact rhythm with knuckles.

Tap tap tap.

Pause.

Tap tap.

Coincidence perhaps.

Maybe not.

I opened mouth.

Vaska said, "Do not turn rhythm into prophecy."

"I wasn't."

"Your face."

Institutional slander.

We went to old river pumping site in afternoon.

Current site was public washhouse and stable yard.

Property negotiation again.

No convenient dungeon entrances.

A city mason named Harl joined because he finally decided wall had not opened enough to justify running uphill.

"I said if wall opens," he reminded Pellin.

"It opened."

"Doorway. Building still there."

Technical courage.

At washhouse basement, blue mineral line emerged along stone floor beneath modern tile.

Warm.

Not hot.

Harl crouched.

"This isn't ward stone."

I looked.

"What?"

"Glass slag. Copper salt maybe. Old heating men mixed into mortar."

"It carries mana."

"Everything carries some."

He scraped tiny flake with chisel.

I felt charge fade immediately when detached.

Infrastructure, not spell inscription perhaps.

Interesting.

Then washhouse floor trembled.

Not pulse.

A rolling vibration moved south to north beneath us.

Water in basins upstairs sloshed.

People shouted.

Vaska looked at plan.

"Channel surge."

We ran outside.

Street gutter near river began steaming.

Then grate erupted.

Warm water blasted two feet upward carrying weeds, black mud, and something pale.

It landed in street.

Creature.

Smaller than I expected.

Six feet long including tail, body flat and translucent gray, four short limbs, red ridge pulsing along spine.

It thrashed once.

People screamed and scattered.

I drew sword.

Harl shouted, "Don't!"

I stopped before moving in.

Creature's mouth opened sideways under head.

No obvious teeth.

It scraped stone with forelimbs trying to move toward gutter.

Red ridge pulsed three times.

Pause.

Twice.

Street grate answered with vibration.

There.

Signal.

Not attack on gate.

Communication maybe.

Another pulse below.

Creature turned.

I lowered sword.

"It's calling."

Vaska said, "Or warning."

Fair.

Crowd formed at safe distance because humans incapable of not watching.

Washhouse owner yelled about street closure.

Harl stared at creature.

"Never seen."

Old Greg had not either.

Shared unknown.

Creature tried gutter again but body too wide for grate.

It panicked.

Red ridge brightened.

Heat rolled off skin.

Not fire.

Water around it steamed.

A boy stepped closer.

I caught shoulder and moved him back.

"Stay."

He resisted because children.

Creature struck tail.

Stone chip flew.

Now danger.

Vaska shouted, "Clear street!"

City guards pushed crowd back.

How to move animal without killing?

River fifty yards downhill.

Could open gutter grate? Too small.

Could guide with water.

Harl said, "Canvas."

Washhouse had heavy wet sheets.

We soaked two and held like barriers, not touching animal.

Ilan and Mett arrived from Old Salt after runner. They joined.

Four people made corridor downhill with canvas screens.

Creature reacted to movement, not people individually.

Red ridge flashed.

I sensed mana discharge through water film on street.

Tingling through boots.

"Don't touch bare water," I said.

Mett looked at me.

"We're standing in it."

"Too late."

Useful timing.

We moved screens.

Creature crawled downhill reluctantly.

Six feet became heavy when turning. Tail slapped Ilan's canvas and nearly tore from hands.

He let go instead of fighting.

Good abort at micro scale.

Creature moved.

At river steps it accelerated.

Everyone backed.

It slid into water.

Gone.

Then three red flashes appeared beneath surface.

Pause.

Two.

Farther out, another set answered.

Two creatures at least.

Maybe more.

No monster fight.

Still my heart hammered like one.

Vaska said, "Now we know where one wanted to go."

"River."

"Yes."

Harl looked at steaming gutter.

"Old return warmth may be habitat."

Pellin, who had arrived despite everyone telling him not to, said, "Then we trapped it by closing gate?"

Silence.

Possible.

The city had perhaps built over an ecosystem and then forgotten.

Or creatures had colonized later.

No answer.

Vaska pointed at him.

"Write that as question, not fact."

He did.

Good apprentice behavior.

That evening Bell Tile owners met with city in workshop.

Money became immediate.

Filtration route closure. Bakery damage. washhouse interruption. Drainage overtime. Property access. Unknown animals.

Ivena argued city owed business losses if old municipal infrastructure caused heat pulses.

City clerk argued system ownership uncertain because pre-charter transfer.

Arlo nearly exploded.

Dena wanted service pipe moved regardless of liability.

Pellin wanted to know whether creature heat could dry filters faster.

Ivena threatened him with unemployment.

I sat against wall with bruised knee and watched mystery become invoices.

This felt more real than revelation.

Then runner entered.

"Guild wants site summary."

Vaska frowned.

"Why Guild?"

"Because city marked magical fauna and unstable infrastructure. They may send Gold survey team."

There.

Gold.

My body reacted before pride allowed denial.

Interest.

Fear.

Want.

I looked at my wet boots.

Pellin saw face.

"You know Gold?"

"Some."

Old Greg did.

Current Greg did not.

Important difference.

"Are they good?"

I thought of memories without names.

"Usually enough to be expensive."

Ivena looked up.

"How expensive?"

Of course.
