# Rehearsal D79: Abort

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

We went under Old Salt at second bell.

Ema charged us for six loaves before allowing descent.

"City expense," she said.

Vaska signed.

Ivena watched with admiration.

The pump had lowered stair water to ankle depth. Broken iron door removed overnight. Beyond it, a brick service tunnel sloped west under tenements.

Blue copper-glass mortar ran along both lower walls.

Gold party order:

Nesk forward.

Veyra.

Orra.

Me.

Vaska.

Renn and Ilan rear with rope.

I disliked middle because it made me feel protected.

That was exactly why placement was correct.

We moved bent under pipes added decades after tunnel. Warm condensation dripped.

Every fifty feet, Nesk stopped and marked wall with chalk arrow.

Not because he could not remember.

Because smoke, darkness, panic change memory.

Old Greg knew that.

Current Greg had stopped doing it on easy routes.

I noticed without turning into vow.

First chamber held nothing interesting except broken valve wheel and rats.

Second had old ceramic channels along ceiling.

Orra tested.

"Still live low charge."

"Heating?"

"Transfer. Maybe heating."

Vaska copied location.

No creature.

Then tunnel widened into cistern.

Water black below catwalk.

Catwalk iron newer than walls, patched in three places.

Nesk knelt.

"Fresh scrape."

On rail.

Pale slime.

Creature contact.

Red glow flickered underwater.

Three.

Pause.

Two.

Another answered from far side.

Orra raised two rods.

Copper threads in gloves lit faint.

"Charge building."

Veyra said, "Across?"

Nesk looked at catwalk supports.

"First half. Can't see far anchor."

Renn clipped line to near rail and tested.

"Rail questionable."

"No line on rail," Veyra said.

Ilan found wall ring.

"This one sound."

They rigged.

Veyra crossed first despite being leader.

Not rank display. Test with best balance maybe.

Halfway catwalk flexed.

She stopped.

Looked down.

Red glow moved beneath.

One creature surfaced enough for translucent back, larger than street one.

Ten feet maybe.

No attack.

It pressed side against blue mortar at waterline.

Ridge brightened.

Wall answered.

Heat rose around cistern.

Orra whispered, "They couple into system."

Interesting.

Veyra continued two steps.

Then Nesk said, "Stop."

Everyone stopped.

He pointed far catwalk anchor.

Stone around it wet and cracked.

Not visible from entry until Veyra angle changed.

"Return," he said.

Veyra did not ask why twice.

She backed carefully.

I wanted someone to inspect closer.

Golds did not.

They withdrew from catwalk.

"Can send Nesk on line," I said.

Nesk looked at me.

Veyra said, "No."

"He has safety."

"To what anchor?"

Near wall only. If catwalk goes, pendulum into cistern.

I knew.

Still wanted.

"Could rig ceiling pipe."

Renn said, "Pipe unknown."

"Test."

Veyra looked at me.

"What do we gain today?"

"Far access. More system. Creature behavior."

"What do we need today?"

I paused.

City needed immediate hazard boundaries, not complete mystery.

"Know whether tunnel safe."

"It isn't."

Annoying answer.

Orra added, "Charge rising too."

Her copper rods now warm enough condensation steamed.

Veyra said, "Abort cistern. Mark. Back."

I felt actual resentment.

There.

Not intellectual disagreement only.

I had finally reached unknown underground with Gold party and they were leaving because infrastructure looked bad.

Old Greg had walked through worse.

Old Greg also remembered people trapped under rock.

That memory surfaced without names.

Two companions.

Mangled leg.

Horde approaching.

Someone protesting.

Someone saying Lucky.

Then blank.

My stomach turned.

I followed.

Ten steps from cistern, sound behind.

Metal scream.

Catwalk collapsed.

Not entire.

Far half tore from cracked anchor and dropped into water.

Creature thrashed beneath, red light flaring.

Wave hit chamber wall.

Warm water surged into tunnel ankle-high.

Nobody said I told you so.

Veyra simply increased pace.

"High ground."

We moved.

Water followed.

At second chamber, Orra stopped.

"Charge spike."

Blue mortar glowing now.

Nesk pointed to ceramic ceiling channels.

"Cracking."

Fine lines opening from heat.

"Run," Veyra said.

Now we ran.

Bent tunnel made running awful.

Renn behind shouted spacing.

"Three steps!"

I almost crowded Orra.

Backed off.

Ceramic channel burst behind us.

Steam filled tunnel.

Not scalding immediately but hot enough.

Visibility gone.

Nesk's chalk arrows mattered.

We followed marks through white vapor.

At stair, pump hose had shifted across path.

Veyra vaulted.

Orra stepped.

I caught boot, went down on hands.

Bruised knee struck stone again.

Pain.

Vaska nearly tripped over me.

Renn grabbed harness and lifted enough.

"Move."

I moved.

We emerged bakery basement coughing steam.

Ema stood with flour scoop.

"Again?"

Warm water began rising through doorway.

"Upstairs," Veyra said.

Ema did not argue this time.

Everyone evacuated.

City pump crew shut lower hose and sealed basement.

We stood in courtyard wet, breathing hard.

My knee throbbed.

Veyra looked at me.

I expected lecture.

"Still think Nesk should cross?"

Direct.

"No."

"Did you before collapse?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because I wanted answer."

"Good reason to notice. Bad reason to spend person."

There.

Not humiliation.

Cost language.

Nesk handed me bread.

"For surviving my hypothetical death."

I laughed despite myself.

"Sorry."

"You didn't send me."

"I suggested."

"People suggest stupid things. Party exists so stupid doesn't automatically happen."

That might be the most important Gold insight yet.

Not coordination as perfection.

Coordination as resistance to individual certainty.

I did not say it aloud because then it would become thesis.

I ate bread.

Veyra told Vaska city should close Old Salt access, isolate warm-water branches, and inspect from surface endpoints before any deeper entry.

No victory.

No creature identification.

A Gold party's first major action was to leave.

I hated how much I respected it.
