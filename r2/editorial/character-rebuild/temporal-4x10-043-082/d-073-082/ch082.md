# Rehearsal D82: The Invoice

Status: **TEMPORAL 4×10 REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The Gold party left Carrow arguing about breakfast money.

No recruitment.

No secret test.

No one told me I was destined for anything.

Renn owed Veyra two copper because he had eaten her fried bread twice.

This became the final dispute.

I met them at Guild because Vaska wanted one last joint summary before the city released the contract.

My shoulder was sore from the tail deflection. My knee was still bruised. My boots were finally dry.

Every piece of me had kept a receipt.

Veyra's team looked less impressive indoors.

Gear spread across a bench for maintenance.

Renn replacing a scorched copper shield strip.

Orra polishing corrosion from rods.

Nesk sewing a torn pocket.

Veyra oiling spear locks.

Gold competence included chores.

I liked that.

Vaska read the findings.

Old warm-return network exists beneath at least six blocks.

Copper-glass mortar transfers heat and low mana charge.

Unknown translucent river fauna uses or responds to the network.

At least three individuals observed, probably more.

No evidence of deliberate aggression except cornered response.

Closing old channels can trap animals and raise local pressure.

Reopening without survey can create occupied-building hazards.

City should map endpoints before permanent work.

Bell Tile service pipe should be physically separated from the old line.

Old Salt access closed pending structural repair.

Dye warehouse unauthorized valve sealed.

That was the revelation.

A maintenance report.

Not a species name.

Not an ancient civilization.

Enough to change city behavior.

I asked Orra, "What do you think the creatures are?"

She shrugged.

"River animals."

"No classification?"

"Eventually."

"You aren't curious?"

She looked offended.

"I'm very curious. That's why I don't want the first answer to become permanent."

There.

I wrote that mentally, then decided not to because borrowing a Gold woman's line as personal doctrine felt cheap.

Nesk asked Vaska whether the city wanted a downstream ecology survey.

"Different contract."

"We available?"

Veyra answered, "No. North job."

Their life moved elsewhere.

Not around Greg.

A Guild clerk brought the payment sheets.

The Gold contract amount made my eyes hurt.

Not absurd wealth. Enough to explain gear maintenance and why the city had hesitated before calling them.

Vaska signed the hazard extension.

I received local specialist pay for the two days where I had been formally requested, less than Gold by a large factor.

Correct.

Pellin would have asked the exact ratio.

I did not.

Almost.

Veyra caught me looking.

"Ask."

"How much per person after Guild?"

She told me.

I whistled.

"Still want Gold?"

The question was direct.

"Yes."

"For money?"

"Some."

"Good."

She laughed at herself.

"Sorry. Apparently Carrow says that too much."

I smiled.

"For what else?" she asked.

I considered.

"The way you move together."

Renn looked over from the shield.

"We argue constantly."

"I noticed the bread."

"Not bread. Everything."

Veyra said, "He means during work."

"Still argue. Faster."

Nesk pulled thread with his teeth.

"You saw the final version. We practiced ugly."

That mattered.

"How long?"

"Us four? Three years. Orra joined later."

Orra said, "Two and a half."

"Feels longer."

She threw a cleaning rag at him.

No mystique.

Practice, relationships, equipment, shared corrections, enough fights to stop needing some words.

Old Greg had remembered formations without people.

Current Greg wanted people enough that formations might eventually have names.

Dangerous sentimental thought.

I let it remain anyway.

Veyra packed her spear.

"Your force plane."

"What about it?"

"Useful. Don't make it carry what your shoulder can't correct."

"How do you know about the shoulder?"

"You keep touching it."

Body again revealing thought.

"Fair."

"Train with someone who can hit the plane sideways."

Specific recommendation.

No prophecy.

Renn finished the shield.

The team stood almost together without a cue.

Not magical.

Habits aligned.

They left for north gate.

I walked with them partway because my route to Bell Tile overlapped.

At East Market, Nesk bought apples.

Veyra paid because breakfast debt accounting had become too complicated.

Renn stole one.

She hit his arm.

The Gold party disappeared into the ordinary crowd before the gate.

I turned south.

Bell Tile had work.

Dena and Harl were rerouting the service pipe away from the warm wall. Arlo complained because the new path cost eleven feet of ceramic. Ivena said eleven feet was cheaper than another bakery flood.

Ema had sent bread as thank-you and an invoice for ruined flour.

Both in the same basket.

Pellin held the city's copy of the old return-warmth plan and had drawn a creature in the margin despite being told not to.

It looked like a fat lizard with a crown.

"That's terrible," I said.

"You draw."

I could not draw.

"Accurate enough."

We worked.

The pipe section came down.

The old wall behind it remained dry and cool.

Harl chiseled new mounts.

I held pipe while Dena measured.

My shoulder complained.

I switched sides instead of hiding it.

Dena noticed but said nothing.

At noon Ivena gathered everyone around the table for costs.

City reimbursement uncertain.

Bell Tile lost route days.

Bakery claim separate.

Dye warehouse likely fined.

Gold survey charged to the city.

Rigger time.

Drainage overtime.

Materials.

Unknown ecology had entered the accounting ledger.

I loved that detail more than classification.

After work I walked to the river.

Not because of the creature signal.

Maybe partly.

The water was ordinary brown-green.

Boats moved.

No red pulses visible.

The fish seller from the closure recognized me.

"Touch people often?"

"Only when they refuse evacuation."

"My knives were there."

"Survived?"

She opened her coat enough to show three handles.

"Of course."

She sold me fried fish at full price.

No forgiveness discount.

I sat on a river step eating.

Old Greg memory returned briefly.

Rock.

Two trapped companions.

Horde.

Lucky.

Still no names.

I could chase it.

Force details.

Build theory.

Instead I watched a child drop bread to river birds and get scolded by his mother.

Current Carrow.

Behind me, city workers hammered a new warning plate over the old drain outlet.

The mystery remained.

So did lunch.

I finished the fish before it got cold.
