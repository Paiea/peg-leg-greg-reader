### D323 `Seven Nights`

Greg encounters a large public mural of Far Lantern at Kett Ford claiming:

> SEVEN NIGHTS SHE MADE THE DAY.

Merel explains the actual Kestrel Pass mechanism to local children.

She did not literally create daylight for seven nights. She used shielded fires, force surfaces, reflected light, route control, repaired mirrors, logistics, and repeated nightly work to move more than two thousand evacuees through a dangerous winter track.

The title and song preserve the consequence while badly compressing the mechanism.

This gives Greg direct present-day evidence that public title stories can be broadly true and technically false at the same time.

That weakens his tendency to treat the future Red Bell song as precise biography without making the song meaningless.

### D329 `Why Me`

- support integration unusually strong for Silver;
- independent hazard margin still Silver.

He asks a more important question: why does Merel keep opening the door for him?

Her incentives are mixed:

- Greg is useful;
- his support instincts are rare;
- she likes teaching people who notice things;
- she believes he may become very good;
- she also wants access to his future memory because it may contain information about people, towns, campaigns, institutions, techniques, and preventable failures.

Merel is not disinterested.

When Greg pushes the question far enough, Merel asks whether she herself dies in his remembered future.

Known memory only:

- Greg remembers age forty-three or forty-four uncertainly;
- cause and place are unknown;
- her title survives strongly in later history.

Merel treats the information as useful rather than immediately dramatic.

Orris reacts more visibly.

This leaves Merel as trustworthy enough to answer direct questions honestly while still possessing her own strategic interest in Greg.
