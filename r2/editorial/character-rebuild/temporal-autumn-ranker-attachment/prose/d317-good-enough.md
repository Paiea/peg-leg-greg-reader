# D317: Good Enough

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

The reservoir did not need me.

This took most of a morning to accept.

Village name was Heddin.

Stone basin above town. Two feeder channels. One spill gate. Old ward lines around the east retaining wall.

Everything looked slightly wrong.

Not dangerous wrong.

Human wrong.

One channel had been widened by hand and never made symmetrical again. Spill gate chain had three different link sizes. The ward posts did not stand at equal distances because somebody had built a goat shed where the fourth one wanted to be.

I hated the goat shed immediately.

Merel noticed.

"No."

"I haven't said anything."

"Your face has."

Orris was eating an apple.

"He's fighting architecture."

"I'm observing."

"Violently."

The local engineer was named Fenn. Forty, maybe. Heavy coat. Missing two fingertips on right hand. Bronze badge pinned under scarf.

He walked us around reservoir and explained everything I had already noticed plus several things I had not.

The widened feeder channel happened after spring flood nine years ago.

Different chain links happened because original supplier closed.

Ward spacing was ugly because the fourth post used to stand where goat shed was.

"Why not move shed?" I asked.

Fenn looked at me.

"Because goats live there."

Reasonable.

"Move ward post?"

"We did."

"Geometry is worse."

"Yes."

I waited.

Fenn waited back.

"And it works?"

"Nine years."

Annoying evidence.

We reached east wall.

There was a hairline crack under one ward bracket.

I crouched.

Fenn crouched beside me.

"Seasonal," he said.

"How much movement?"

He showed me chalk marks from four years.

Tiny.

Consistent.

"You know load path?"

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

"Stone is one hundred twenty years old. Anybody who says entirely is selling something."

I liked him more.

Merel stood ten feet away doing nothing.

That became suspicious after a while.

"What are we here for?" I asked.

Fenn answered first.

"Annual inspection."

"I mean us."

Merel said, "Annual inspection."

"You are S-class."

"Still capable of looking at walls."

"This seems beneath market rate."

"Village pays regional contract. We rotate through six sites."

I looked at wall again.

No hidden calamity.

No buried ward network waiting to become interesting.

Crack moved less than fingernail width in four years.

I checked feeder flow.

Within margin.

Gate chain ugly but sound.

Goat shed offensive but apparently constitutional.

At lunch Fenn's wife brought bread and white cheese to gate house.

Goats contributed smell.

Orris fed one crust and immediately became popular.

I sat on low wall and looked at reservoir.

"You are disappointed," Merel said.

"No."

"Bad lie."

"Nothing is wrong."

"Correct."

"Then what am I learning?"

Merel tore bread.

"Nothing, if you're lucky."

I stared.

She ate.

Fenn argued with Orris about whether goats could digest apple seeds.

Nobody asked me to optimize them.

After lunch Fenn opened maintenance ledger.

Not for me.

For Merel.

They spent half an hour on replacement timing for spill gate chain.

Current chain probably had two years left.

Fenn wanted to replace next spring because winter supplier prices were bad and summer labor was already booked.

Merel asked three questions.

Then signed inspection line.

No correction.

No superior plan.

I looked at numbers over his shoulder.

Could probably save five or six percent by ordering two separate chain sizes from Carrow and splicing locally.

Maybe eight if Vessa touched procurement.

Thought arrived.

Stayed.

Did not become speech.

Fenn knew his town.

His labor.

His suppliers.

His winter.

His goats.

I knew a spreadsheet that did not exist.

Different authority.

On road out, Merel rode beside wagon.

"You were quiet."

"I suffered."

"Heroically."

"I could improve chain cost."

"Probably."

"Five percent."

"Maybe."

"Eight with Krail."

Merel laughed.

"Then you'd owe the village three new problems."

"Accurate."

Cold wind moved over fields.

Heddin disappeared behind hill.

Nothing had failed.

Nobody learned my name.

Fenn would replace ugly chain next spring and reservoir would probably keep holding water.

I had spent one full day near Far Lantern and done almost nothing useful.

Strangely tired.

Orris handed me an apple from wagon crate.

"You survived."

"Barely."

"First time?"

"What?"

"Watching someone else be fine."

I threw apple at him.

He caught it.

Platinum problem.
