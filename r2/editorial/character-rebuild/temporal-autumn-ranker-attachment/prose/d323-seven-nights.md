# D323: Seven Nights

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Kett Ford had a mural of Merel Dane.

This was unfortunate because Merel Dane had to walk past it.

We reached town near noon.

Bridge square. Bakery. Two inns. Small Guild office. School beside temple wall.

And thirty feet of painted Far Lantern.

Blue coat.

Hair blowing dramatically.

Both hands raised over mountains.

Golden sky behind her.

Tiny people below.

Words across bottom:

**SEVEN NIGHTS SHE MADE THE DAY.**

I stopped walking.

Merel did not.

Orris stopped beside me.

"Don't."

"I haven't said anything."

"Your face has."

Apparently contagious.

The mural Merel had two functioning knees, shoulders twice actual width, and enough hair to supply three current Merels.

"Accurate?" I asked.

Orris covered mouth.

Merel kept walking.

"Orris."

"He asked."

"You are thirty-eight."

"Forty."

"Worse."

School doors opened.

Children came out.

Of course.

Teacher saw Merel.

Then mural.

Then Merel again.

Her whole face changed.

"Far Lantern?"

Merel closed eyes briefly.

"Merel is fine."

It was not fine.

Within two minutes we had twenty-three children around us.

One asked if she really held sun over Kestrel Pass for seven nights.

Another said his brother said it was nine.

Third said thirteen.

Merel looked at teacher.

Teacher looked guilty.

"Seven," Merel said.

Children won.

I folded arms.

She saw me.

"Not a word."

"I am observing history."

"Poorly."

A boy maybe eight held up wooden toy lantern.

"Can you make daylight?"

Merel looked at sky.

Gray autumn cloud.

"No."

Collective disappointment.

"But mural says."

Merel turned and looked at mural for first time.

Long enough that I wondered if she had seen it before.

"Mural lies," she said.

Teacher inhaled sharply.

Children loved this.

"What happened?" girl asked.

Merel considered.

Then sat on low fountain edge.

Children gathered.

Orris leaned against bakery wall.

I stayed standing.

Merel said, "Kestrel Pass was winter. Bad winter. Army had road. Civilians had mountain track. Enemy scouts kept finding fires at night."

No dramatic voice.

Kids went quiet anyway.

"We needed people moving after dark without putting torches all along track. I could make force surfaces. Thin ones. Lots of them."

She held one hand flat.

A pale plane appeared above palm.

Small.

"Firelight hits this."

She tilted plane.

Light from bakery window shifted onto temple wall.

Children gasped.

"Then another."

Second plane.

Light moved again.

"And another."

Third.

A line of reflected gold crossed square.

Nothing like river barge.

Tiny.

Beautiful anyway.

"We put shielded fires behind rock where scouts could not see them. Then bounced light along safe parts of trail. Enough to see feet. Enough to see rope. Not enough to see from valley."

Boy frowned.

"That's not daylight."

"Correct."

"Song says daylight."

"Songwriter wasn't carrying fuel uphill."

Teacher laughed before stopping herself.

Merel smiled.

"It lasted seven nights because evacuation took seven nights. Not because I held spell for seven nights. We slept. We ate. We moved fires. Two mirrors broke. One mule kicked a captain."

Children immediately wanted mule story.

History lost again.

Merel told mule story.

It was good.

Apparently captain deserved it.

Afterward teacher apologized for mural.

Merel said, "Keep it."

I looked at her.

Teacher did too.

"Really?"

"Children like it."

"But it's wrong."

Merel shrugged.

"They know that now."

We ate lunch at bakery because school children followed us there and baker refused payment from Far Lantern.

Merel argued.

Baker won.

S-class result history defeated by meat pie.

I waited until road again.

Then asked.

"How much of title is wrong?"

Merel walked beside wagon.

"Depends which tavern."

"Kestrel Pass happened."

"Yes."

"Seven nights."

"Yes."

"Two thousand evacuees?"

"Two thousand one hundred twelve registered. Probably more."

"You built support lattice."

"Several."

"But daylight is wrong."

"Very."

I thought of song.

First bell broke gate.

Second broke guard.

Third broke Dunbarrow.

Merel saw it happen on my face.

"Renn?"

"Yes."

"Careful."

"I know."

"No. You know now. Different."

Annoying distinction.

I looked back at mural shrinking behind us.

Huge painted woman holding gold sky.

Real woman beside me had repaired one broken mirror herself because quartermaster was busy.

Both stories said she got people through pass.

One was easier to sing.

"Do you hate title?" I asked.

Merel took time.

"Sometimes."

"Like it?"

"Sometimes."

Mara's taxonomy.

Everybody stealing.

"Useful?"

"Very."

"Dangerous?"

Merel looked at me.

"Obviously."

I laughed.

She didn't.

Then did.

Road turned north.

Behind us children were probably already improving mule story.
