# D329: Why Me

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Merel's evaluation was three lines.

This offended me.

We sat at Westhook Guild on D329 waiting for east coach.

Two weeks done.

Rain outside.

Not enough to stop road.

Enough to make everybody smell like wet wool.

Merel pushed paper across table.

**Scope discipline: improved.**

**Support integration: unusually strong for Silver.**

**Independent hazard margin: still Silver.**

I read it twice.

"That's it?"

"Yes."

"Two weeks."

"Correct."

"Three lines."

"You want decorative border?"

Orris drank coffee beside window.

"I can draw flowers."

"No."

"Coward."

I looked at Merel.

"Still Silver."

"Yes."

No surprise.

Oddly little disappointment.

Current body agreed.

Unknown-ground retreat still slower than Gold should be.

Sustained output better, not enough.

Support judgment excellent in some rooms and dangerously eager in others.

Fair.

I folded paper.

Merel reached for her tea.

"Next attachment midwinter if you want it."

"I want it."

"Then come."

Simple.

Too simple.

I did not like simple anymore.

"Why me?"

Merel sighed.

"Again?"

"Different question."

"Sounds same."

"First time I asked why I got six weeks. Now I'm asking why you keep opening door."

Orris became very interested in coffee.

Merel noticed too.

"You can leave," she told him.

"No."

"Then stop pretending."

"Gladly."

He turned chair around.

Excellent privacy.

Merel looked back at me.

"Because you're useful."

"Not enough."

"Because your support instincts are rare."

"Still not enough."

Her mouth moved slightly.

Not smile.

Annoyance maybe.

"Because I like teaching people who notice things."

"Closer."

"Greg."

"Merel."

Orris whispered, "Fight."

We ignored him.

Merel put tea down.

"Because I want to know what you remember."

There.

Room changed.

Not dramatically.

Still wet wool.

Still coffee.

Still three-line evaluation between us.

But there.

"How much?" I asked.

"Not everything."

"Convenient."

"You asked."

"Now."

"Yes."

I sat back.

Merel waited.

Did not fill silence.

Good habit.

Or trained one.

"You knew I had future memory before first attachment."

"Enough to believe something strange was true."

"You still invited me."

"Yes."

"Because you wanted access to it."

"Partly."

"And didn't say."

"You did not ask what I wanted from you beyond work."

Krail answer.

Almost.

I felt that immediately.

Merel did too.

"Careful," she said.

"With what?"

"Making equivalence because emotion likes symmetry."

Annoying woman.

"Difference?"

"If you had asked whether my interest in you included your memories, I would have said yes. Krail answered a direct risk question with a true omission because she wanted your choice to go her way."

I hated that distinction because it was real.

Not complete.

But real.

"You still benefited from me not asking."

"Yes."

That helped.

No defense.

"What do you want to know?"

Merel looked toward rain.

Then back.

"Who survives."

I did not expect that.

"People?"

"People. Towns. Campaigns. Techniques. Institutions. Things that fail slowly enough somebody might change them."

"Sera."

"Sera."

"Renn."

"Renn if he consents."

Good.

"You?"

Merel went quiet.

There.

Old history.

Dead at forty-three.

Or forty-four.

I had known that before meeting her.

Had not told her.

Because no trigger yet.

Because uncertainty.

Because I liked her.

Because telling someone they die changes room.

All true.

Merel watched face.

Support Ranker.

S-class.

Not stupid.

"Do I die?" she asked.

Orris stopped moving.

Rain filled silence.

I could have lied.

Badly.

Could have deferred.

Could have said everybody dies.

Coward answers available in quantity.

"Yes," I said.

Merel's expression barely changed.

Orris looked at floor.

"When?"

"Before old me reaches S-class. I remember forty-three or forty-four. Records disagree in my head."

"Cause?"

"I don't know."

"Place?"

"I don't know."

"Title survives?"

"Very much."

Merel breathed out through nose.

Not relief.

Something adjacent.

"Useful," she said.

I stared.

"That is a terrible response."

"True one."

My own words used against me.

Rude.

Orris said, "Merel."

She looked at him.

Different face then.

Tiny.

Human.

"I know," she said.

Orris nodded once.

No speech.

Merel looked back at me.

"This is part of why I keep opening door."

"You want warning."

"I want information. Warning when information supports warning."

"Cold."

"You prefer lying?"

"No."

"Then don't demand warmth from taxonomy."

Mara would hate how much she sounded like Mara.

"Anything else?" I asked.

"Yes."

"What?"

"I think you might become very good."

There.

That one landed harder.

Stupidly.

I looked away.

Merel smiled.

"Embarrassing?"

"Shut up."

"Good."

"Stop using that word."

Orris laughed.

Coach bell rang outside.

We stood.

I tucked evaluation into coat.

Three lines.

Still Silver.

Far Lantern wanted my future memory.

Far Lantern also thought I might become very good.

Both could be true.

Apparently people were allowed incentives without becoming villains.

Very inconvenient discovery.

At coach door Merel stopped me.

"Greg."

"What?"

"Next time you want to know why someone keeps opening a door, ask earlier."

I thought of Vessa.

Mara.

Renn.

Daro.

Myself.

"Fair."

Merel nodded.

Then added, "Doesn't mean they'll answer."

"You ruin everything."

"S-class."

"Not how that works."

"Still useful."

We boarded.

Two weeks ended with no Gold badge, no master, and one more dead person currently alive beside me.

Autumn road east.

For once, enough.
