# D362: Green Coat

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I bought a coat I did not need.

This was Mara's fault.

Mostly.

Northbank market had winter stalls up three weeks too early because merchants believed cold became more profitable if announced in advance.

Mara had secondday afternoon free.

Actual free.

No ledger emergency.

No failed vote.

No office packet.

This made both of us suspicious.

"We could sit," I said.

"We've sat."

"Recently?"

"Breakfast."

"That was eating."

"You sat during it."

Difficult woman.

So we walked market.

Northbank wind came off river sideways.

My existing coat worked.

Brown wool.

Good lining.

Left side altered for socket access if I needed adjustment without removing half clothing in public.

Bera-approved.

Functional.

Ugly only in normal way.

Then I saw green one.

Dark green.

Almost black until light caught it.

Long enough to cover walking socket cleanly.

Split back.

High collar.

Brass closures instead of horn.

Stupid coat.

Mara kept walking three steps before noticing I had stopped.

She came back.

Looked at coat.

Then me.

"Oh no."

"What?"

"You like it."

"It's cloth."

"You like cloth."

"I own cloth."

Merchant smiled at us.

Predator.

"Try it," Mara said.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because then information becomes expensive."

Merchant's smile widened.

Mara laughed.

I tried coat.

Fit shoulders immediately.

Bad sign.

Sleeves correct.

Worse.

Left hem caught slightly against walking foot when I turned.

Good.

Flaw.

I could leave.

Merchant knelt, pinned hem half inch shorter on left, and ruined escape.

"Easy alteration," she said.

"How much?"

Mara said, "Don't ask if you're not buying."

"I need information."

Merchant named price.

I removed coat.

"No."

Mara looked at me.

"That's not that bad."

"For coat?"

"For good coat."

"My coat functions."

"Your coat looks like municipal blanket learned buttons."

"Bera chose it."

"Bera chose access."

True.

Cruel.

Merchant waited.

Professional hunter.

I calculated wool, tailoring, closures, alteration, Northbank margin.

Price was fair.

That made it worse.

"Why green?" Mara asked.

"What?"

"You've been staring at green one. There are brown and gray."

I looked.

There were.

Did not want those.

No useful reason.

"I like green," I said.

Mara stopped smiling.

Not serious.

Just surprised.

Apparently this was new intelligence.

"You do?"

"Apparently."

"Old Greg?"

"No idea."

"Current Greg?"

I looked at coat.

"Apparently."

Merchant said nothing.

Smart.

I bought it.

Full price.

No negotiation.

This hurt more than leg.

Not literally.

But spiritually.

Mara watched coins change hands.

"Are you ill?"

"I regret everything."

"You didn't bargain."

"Price was fair."

"Who are you?"

"Working on it."

Merchant marked alteration and told me collect tomorrow.

We left.

Five stalls later I looked back.

Green coat still hanging because merchant had apparently another one.

Mine existed somewhere behind curtain now.

Weird.

"Happy?" Mara asked.

"No."

"Liar."

"Financially wounded."

"You can afford it."

"Irrelevant."

She took my hand.

We kept walking.

At food row Mara bought fried river cakes with too much salt.

I bought honey nuts because wanted them.

No nutritional justification.

No route need.

No shared discount.

Terrible afternoon.

We sat on low wall near north lock and ate.

Mara told me about a clerk named Bessin who had accidentally sent reconciliation copy to dead warehouse owner because nobody removed name from distribution list.

I told her Renn broke my chair.

She laughed so hard honey nut went wrong way.

Nearly killed by furniture story.

Romance.

"You staying tomorrow night?" she asked after coughing stopped.

"Yes."

"No work?"

"Thirdday morning train."

"Train?"

"Road coach."

"You said train."

I froze.

Old word.

Wrong world.

"Future thing."

"Ah."

She did not ask.

That was maybe more intimate than asking.

Wind got colder.

Mara leaned into my shoulder.

I adjusted left leg on stone because socket angle bad.

She waited.

Then leaned again.

"Green," she said.

"What?"

"I didn't know."

"Neither did I."

"Anything else?"

I thought.

Honey nuts.

Good coats.

Teaching Bronze supports apparently.

Drake fights when no one died.

Mara against shoulder.

Not useful categories.

"Probably," I said.

"Terrifying."

"I know."

Next morning coat fit.

Merchant had shortened left hem exactly enough.

Mara made me wear it out of shop.

I complained entire way to breakfast.

Kept coat on.
