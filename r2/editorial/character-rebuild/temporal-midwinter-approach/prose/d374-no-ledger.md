# D374: No Ledger

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Merel asked for a list.

Of dead people.

Technically she asked for:

**CURRENTLY LIVING PERSONS OF SIGNIFICANT HISTORICAL IMPORTANCE WHOM YOU REMEMBER DYING BEFORE YOUR LATER ACTIVE PERIOD.**

Which was worse because capitalization made murder administrative.

Her letter arrived six days after Northbank.

Green coat hanging from chair because I did not want Renn touching it.

He had opinions about that.

I read line three times.

Then kept reading.

Merel did not pretend list was simple.

She wrote that Greg's memory was incomplete, contaminated by legend, and temporally uncertain.

She wrote that false warning could alter decisions and create harm.

She also wrote:

**Not warning is also a decision.**

Annoying woman.

Then:

**I am asking whether we can identify cases where uncertainty is still worth disclosing. Not whether you can save everyone.**

I put letter down.

Made tea.

Came back.

Letter remained unreasonable.

Renn came upstairs fifteen minutes later carrying replacement chair rung.

Finally.

"What's wrong?"

"Merel wants death ledger."

He stopped.

"Mine?"

"No."

"Good."

"That was fast."

"You told me song. I'm already contaminated."

Fair.

He sat on floor beside chair and began unwinding copper repair.

"Other people?"

"People I remember dying. Important ones."

"How many?"

I looked at wall.

Memory offered names in fragments.

Heroes.

Rankers.

Guildmasters.

Kings.

A baker whose death caused Noll to cry when we were thirty-two.

Was he historically important?

To Noll.

A road captain at Vey.

Three Platinum adventurers in winter breach.

Sera.

Merel.

People I only knew because songs.

People I had personally watched die.

People I heard were dead years later.

"Too many," I said.

Renn fitted new rung.

"Then don't."

Simple.

"People could live."

"People could also spend forty years scared of wrong death."

There.

I hated when Bronze quarry worker had point.

"You're biased."

"Obviously."

He tightened join.

"You told me town. Now every bell foundry looks like prophecy."

"I know."

"Do you?"

I looked at him.

He didn't sound angry.

Just current.

"Yes."

"Then list does that to everybody."

"Not if I only tell them when useful."

Renn snorted.

"Useful according to who?"

Worse.

I took Merel's letter to Guild anyway.

Not because Sella could solve ethics.

Because she had stamps.

Sometimes talking in room with paper helped.

Sella read letter.

She did not know full future-memory situation.

Enough now that pretending otherwise seemed stupid.

She finished.

"Absolutely not."

Relief came too fast.

"Why?"

"Because Guild is not storing secret death registry based on one Silver's memories from impossible previous life."

"When you say it like that."

"How else would I say it?"

"More respectfully."

"No."

She pushed letter back.

"If you know specific current hazard relevant to current contract, report hazard. If you know named person may face specific event, tell person. Maybe tell Guild if public safety. But no list."

"Merel isn't asking Guild to store it."

"Worse. Private notebook?"

"Maybe."

"No."

"You are very decisive for someone with no authority over notebook."

"Professional gift."

I went home.

Still no answer.

So I wrote Merel.

First draft became twelve pages.

Bad sign.

Second became six.

Still bad.

Third:

**Merel,**

**No broad list.**

I stared.

Too blunt.

Added:

**I think making one would turn every remembered person into a future incident and every current choice into contamination. Renn is already teaching me cost of that.**

Then:

**I will answer direct questions about specific people if I actually remember something. If I remember a specific future hazard with plausible current relevance, I will disclose uncertainty to person involved or someone responsible for public safety. I will not build a private death registry from songs and incomplete memory.**

I read.

Sounded reasonable.

Which meant Merel would find flaw.

She did.

Reply came eleven days later.

Three pages.

First line:

**You are using procedure to avoid deciding who deserves warning.**

I swore.

Renn, downstairs, shouted, "Merel?"

"Yes."

"Good."

"Stop."

I read rest.

Merel agreed no broad list.

Mostly.

She objected to my word *specific*.

History rarely preserved enough specificity.

Sera proved that.

Merel herself proved that.

If I waited until memory became precise enough for comfort, warning might always arrive too late.

Then she wrote:

**I am not asking you to control us. I am asking you to accept that withholding uncertain information is still agency. Yours.**

There.

That was harder.

Faultglass had been taking because I thought I knew enough.

Death ledger could become withholding because I was afraid to know too little.

Opposite errors.

Same Greg.

I sat with that until tea went cold.

Then wrote only one line back:

**Probably. Still no ledger.**

Her reply came four days later.

**Agreed. For now.**

For now.

Terrible phrase.

I folded both letters into notebook.

Not under WHO IS GREG.

Different section.

No title.

Just:

**PEOPLE ARE NOT FUTURE EVENTS.**

I stared at sentence.

Too neat.

Crossed it out.

Better.
