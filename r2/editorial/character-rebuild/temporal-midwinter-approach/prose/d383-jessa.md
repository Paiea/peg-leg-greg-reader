# D383: Jessa

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Jessa was better at Renn's trick than Renn.

Not overall.

One part.

Enough.

Carrow Guild quarry yard had given them old retaining block, three iron braces, one wet seam, and too many observers.

Jessa hated observers.

I liked her immediately.

Short.

Hair cut to jaw with quarry knife quality.

Hands gray at nails.

Dunbarrow boots still red with local clay despite two weeks in Carrow.

Renn stood beside her pretending he had not invited half world.

Actual observers:

Me.

Cedi.

Yori from Hearthline.

One Guild assessor.

Two quarry foremen.

Vessa, somehow.

"Why are you here?" I asked.

"Jessa needed room extension."

"That explains city."

"I like rocks."

"No you don't."

"I like contracts involving rocks."

Fair.

Jessa ignored us.

She put two fingers to stone.

Tiny pulse.

Nothing visible.

Second pulse.

Then she marked chalk line four inches left of iron brace.

"Water behind here."

Foreman checked old plan.

"No channel."

"Didn't say channel."

Renn grinned.

"Do it near brace."

Jessa looked at him.

"You already know."

"Show them."

There.

Pride.

Good kind.

Mostly.

She moved hand closer to iron.

First shallow pulse.

Metal threw it.

Second pulse came angled under return.

Her head tilted.

"Pocket. Small."

Renn's grin disappeared slightly.

I noticed because I knew his face now.

Nobody else did.

Assessor asked how she separated iron echo from void.

Jessa explained badly.

Not because she didn't understand.

Because explaining embodied sense sucked.

Renn interrupted twice.

Helpful first time.

Second time less.

Jessa stopped talking.

Looked at him.

"You want do it?"

"No."

"Then let me."

Silence.

Cedi looked at ground.

Vessa looked delighted.

I looked at Renn.

He flushed.

"Fine."

Jessa finished.

Assessor made notes.

Then asked Renn to repeat same read.

Bad luck.

He could.

Mostly.

But near iron his first return was dirtier.

He compensated with more mana.

Got same answer.

Assessor asked cost difference.

Jessa said hers.

Renn said his.

Jessa's was lower.

Not hugely.

Enough.

There it was.

Future Red Bell beaten at one tiny thing by quarry woman nobody had songs about.

I enjoyed this more than was kind.

Renn saw my face.

"Don't."

"I said nothing."

"Your face."

"Current face innocent."

"Old face worse?"

"Probably."

Assessor dismissed observers for lunch.

Renn walked off alone.

Jessa watched him.

"He's mad."

"Yes."

"At me?"

"Maybe."

"Idiot."

"Also yes."

Vessa said, "Useful idiot."

Jessa looked at her.

"Who are you?"

"Krail."

"That answered less."

"Good instinct."

I left before Vessa acquired another person.

Renn was behind tool shed throwing pebbles at barrel.

Poorly.

"You following me?"

"Lunch this direction."

"Lunch other direction."

"Then yes."

He threw pebble.

Missed.

"She's good."

"Yes."

"I taught her."

"Partly."

Wrong answer.

He looked at me.

"What?"

"She found shallow-double read herself."

"From thing I showed."

"Probably."

"Probably?"

"You hate that word now too?"

"Greg."

I leaned against wall.

"What do you want me to say?"

"Nothing."

"Excellent."

We stood.

He threw another pebble.

Hit barrel.

Victory.

"I don't like that she's better," he said.

There.

Simple.

Human.

"Okay."

"That's it?"

"You want optimization?"

"No."

"Then okay."

"Feels shitty."

"Probably."

"I want to be happy."

"You are."

"Also mad."

"Both allowed."

"You learned that from Mara?"

"Everyone teaches me basic things now."

Lunch bell rang.

Renn did not apologize immediately.

Good.

Would have felt rehearsed.

Barely spoke to Jessa.

Jessa barely cared.

Renn heard.

His jaw tightened.

Again.

He said congratulations anyway.

Sounded bad.

Jessa said, "Thanks," sounding worse.

We went home separately.

"I was being an ass."

"Yes."

"Sorry."

"Okay."

"Your iron read is better."

"Yes."

"You're enjoying this."

"Yes."

"Teach me."

Jessa made him pay for dinner.

Reasonable tuition.

I did not say anything.

Vessa, from next table, whispered:

"You're making face."

I whispered back:

"Shut up."
