# D401: Three Hollow Fords

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

There were three Hollow Fords.

Sera Vell informed me by postcard.

Not letter.

Postcard.

Front showed painted bridge and cheerful ducks.

Back:

**Greg,**

**There are three Hollow Fords. Possibly four if Eastmere counts the old crossing. Your future history is garbage.**

**I am alive. Wrist mostly works. Going north next. Not Hollow Ford. Stop looking like that.**

**Sera**

I was alone in Guild hall when I read it.

Still looked like that.

Sella noticed from desk.

"Death mail?"

"Alive mail."

"Worse?"

"Complicated."

I handed postcard over.

She read.

"Ducks nice."

"Focus."

"Three Hollow Fords."

"Yes."

"Common name."

"Apparently."

"Future historians incompetent."

"Apparently."

She handed back.

"Problem solved."

"No."

"Then problem improved."

I looked at ducks.

One painted duck had blue wing.

Artist had never seen duck or hated realism.

"What if death is different Hollow Ford?"

Sella shrugged.

"Then you still don't know."

"Helpful."

"You're welcome."

I put postcard in coat.

Not notebook.

Important.

Maybe.

Outside, first hard frost had stayed in alley shadows after noon.

Midwinter attachment in five days.

Gold review in forty-six.

Mara expected me after attachment, weather permitting.

Renn and Jessa were arguing about iron return at quarry.

Vessa had sent invoice for winter road lodging with referral commission disclosed in annoyingly large handwriting.

Merel had not asked for another death list.

Sera was alive.

At least today.

I walked to Brass Spoon.

Renn was already there.

"Why are you smiling?"

"Three Hollow Fords."

He stopped chewing.

"What?"

I gave postcard.

He read.

Then laughed hard enough to bend over table.

"Your future is stupid."

"History is stupid."

"Same thing."

"No."

"Three?"

"Possibly four."

He laughed again.

Jessa took postcard from him.

"Why is this funny?"

Renn pointed at me.

"He knows future. Badly."

Jessa looked at postcard.

Then me.

"That's the thing?"

I looked at Renn.

He froze.

"I told her some."

There.

New independent information decision.

My first instinct was annoyance.

Second was rules.

Third arrived late enough to be useful.

Renn had right to tell his own story.

"How much?" I asked.

Jessa answered before him.

"Enough that he has weird friend who thinks he knows songs about him. Not details."

"Good."

Renn pointed.

"You said good."

"Shut up."

Jessa looked between us.

"Is he always like this?"

"Worse."

Vessa arrived then because apparently timing had comedic intent.

She saw postcard in Jessa's hand.

"Private?"

Jessa looked at me.

I said, "No."

Vessa read front only.

"Bad ducks."

"Everyone hates artist."

"Correct."

She sat.

Did not ask back.

Small thing.

I noticed anyway.

Noll brought soup.

Winter soup now.

Thicker.

More root vegetables.

Time changed menu without announcement.

We ate.

Postcard stayed face-up near salt.

Three Hollow Fords.

Future had not become less useful.

Just less obedient.
