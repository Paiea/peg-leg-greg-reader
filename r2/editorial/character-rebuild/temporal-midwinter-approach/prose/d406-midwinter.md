# D406: Midwinter

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

Merel approved green coat.

This was almost worth money.

Almost.

"Good wool," she said.

"I know."

"Cut works with socket."

"I know."

"Color suits you."

I stopped.

"We're done."

Orris laughed from horse.

"He bought fashion."

"Shut up."

"How much?"

"Private."

"Too much."

"Orris."

Merel saved him by handing me route packet.

Midwinter attachment.

Two weeks.

Carrow north gate under gray sky.

Frost on stone.

No snow yet.

Packet listed:

Two municipal ward inspections.

One winter bridge load review.

One frozen aqueduct dispute.

Three days attached to a caravan support team crossing Rime Cut.

No Ranker hazard classification.

No giant secret.

Good.

Then second page:

**Possible joint work: Ash Crown, route dependent.**

I looked up.

Merel watched.

"Sera?"

"Maybe."

"Hollow Ford?"

"No."

"Certain?"

"As far as maps currently admit."

I handed her postcard.

She read.

One eyebrow rose.

"Three?"

"Possibly four."

Orris leaned from saddle.

"Ducks terrible."

"Universal consensus."

Merel turned card over.

"This is why your list was bad idea."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Rude."

She gave it back.

No lecture.

Good.

Vessa had not booked this trip.

Merel handled transport.

Bera had checked field peg yesterday.

Renn and Jessa were both in quarry yard and refused to wake before dawn to see me leave.

Reasonable.

Mara's last letter was in inner coat pocket.

Gold review sat forty-one days away.

Sella had written date in ink.

I had not moved it.

Yet.

Merel mounted.

"Ready?"

I looked at wagon.

Pack.

Field peg case.

Green coat.

No stolen artifact.

No deadline I had invented for myself.

No one waiting to call me Gold.

"Yes."

We left.

At gate watchman checked papers twice because winter route permits had changed.

Merel waited.

Did not use title.

Orris did not use Platinum badge.

I did not use Vessa.

Eventually papers passed anyway.

Took nine minutes.

Terrible.

Survivable.

Outside city road was hard with frost.

Horse breath smoked.

Merel rode beside wagon for first mile.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"I have one boundary for this attachment."

Of course.

"Which?"

"No future questions unless current event makes them relevant."

I looked at her.

"Your rule?"

"Mine."

Interesting.

After D329, she had spent weeks knowing she died young.

Apparently she had found limit too.

"Agreed."

"Good."

"You said good."

"I am S-class. Rules different."

"Corrupt system."

"Documented."

She rode ahead.

I smiled despite cold.

Midwinter road opened north.

For two weeks, future could wait unless present asked.

That seemed impossible.

We tried anyway.
