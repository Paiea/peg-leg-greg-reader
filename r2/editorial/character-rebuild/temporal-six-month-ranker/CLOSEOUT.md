### Ranker ceiling — corrected model

The initial rehearsal briefly described `Ranker` as though it were simply a capability category above Platinum. That was wrong and is superseded here.

Two systems coexist:

- Bronze / Silver / Gold / Platinum are Guild operating ranks based on practical current capability, power, judgment, contract scope, mobility, survivability, and team role.
- A **Ranker** is a person who has earned a durable public title from consequential verified results. No title, no Ranker.
- Titled Rankers are separately classified C -> B -> A -> S.
- The letter system is a tiny list, roughly 150 active people across the continent, and is not a second straight power ladder.
- A lower-metal adventurer can still become a C-class Ranker after one extraordinary result while remaining lower-metal for ordinary contracts.
- A Platinum can remain untitled forever and may be stronger in direct combat than some titled C or B Rankers.
- Letter advancement reflects accumulated result history, consequence, scale, repeatability, trust, and planning significance attached to the title.
- S-class is the pinnacle among titled Rankers, not shorthand for `stronger than every Platinum`.

Merel Dane, title **Far Lantern**, is both Platinum by operating rank and S-class among titled Rankers.

Her ceiling is established through action, not metal-color replacement:

- casually redistributes a runaway loaded river barge through a multi-plane moving force lattice;
- operates remote force surfaces hundreds of yards apart;
- identifies Greg's force-plane edge waste almost immediately;
- works alongside untitled Platinum Orris Hale, whose raw operating rank remains fully meaningful despite having no title.

Old Greg never personally knew Merel. He knows her from history and doctrine. She dies before his own final S-class era.

Merel spends seventeen days in Carrow and sees Greg on eleven of them. She does not immediately become his master. At D171 she offers a paid six-week **field attachment**, explicitly not apprenticeship. When Greg asks about `master`, she says no, then leaves the future question open if he returns without trying to become her.

### Renn Calder / future Red Bell

Renn Calder begins as Bronze under temporary work suspension after a quarry resonance incident.

Greg recognizes the name from future songs:

> First bell broke the gate.
> Second broke the guard.
> Third bell broke Dunbarrow.

Future public memory identifies Renn Calder as **Red Bell**, an eventual S-class titled Ranker associated with the destruction of most of Dunbarrow. Old Greg never personally knew him. Greg's information is songs, public history, and later military teaching, all mutually inconsistent in details.

Current Renn is not the song:

- young quarry worker;
- extraordinary low-cost structural resonance sensing and propagation;
- initially carrying buckets and doing ordinary labor;
- proud, curious, funny, negotiates money aggressively;
- from Dunbarrow, with a mother, sister Tali, cousins, and ordinary family history there;
- independently improves during a twenty-four-day period when he refuses to speak to Greg;
- explicitly writes rules for Greg after learning about the future song.

Renn's rules for Greg:

1. Do not tell people what Renn is supposed to become.
2. If Greg remembers something about Renn, disclose it before using it to make a decision about him.
3. Do not contact Dunbarrow.
4. If Renn says stop helping, stop.

They explicitly reject master/student labels at this stage.

Greg's initial motive is intentionally ugly and mixed: he sees a future S-class piece before it becomes the person in the song and wants to see whether getting there first can branch the outcome. After months of current contact, Greg also genuinely likes Renn. Those motives coexist.

At D161 Renn breaks a wagon axle in anger after a driver threatens/beats a stray dog and clashes with a child. Nobody dies. Renn admits he deliberately put charge through the wagon because he was angry and later admits that, for a second, he liked the power. This makes the future danger legible without declaring the future inevitable.

At D177 Renn receives an ordinary family invitation to his sister's wedding in Dunbarrow. He chooses to go home. Greg wants to follow because of the song but accepts Renn's refusal. Renn also declines a full dump of future Dunbarrow lore because Greg does not know timing, cause, or truth well enough and it would contaminate the wedding with paranoia.

Renn leaves for Dunbarrow D183.

He is not saved.

He is not claimed.

He is current.

### Greg

The run strengthens a possible support identity without resolving it.

Useful discoveries:

- Greg's instinct is still to optimize people and systems.
- Faultglass changed the price model: personal time is relatively cheap to Greg because he already lived a full life; reputation/trust is scarce and compounds socially.
- Trust does not return as a ledger balance. People make small choices while Greg is absent.
- Over the six months Harrow's settlement is paid in full.
- Harrow later vouches that Greg's technical strain notes are reliable.
- Daro eventually approves one case-by-case Hearthline prepared-site job and later another future-dated job. Retainer is not magically restored.
- Greg gets access to Far Lantern not by stealing an accelerator or maximizing personal training, but by being visibly useful, honest enough, and interesting as support.
- Greg's own advancement speeds up precisely while he stops treating Greg as the only progression target.

Merel provides a stronger support formulation:

> A support gets stronger and suddenly five people can go somewhere none of them could go yesterday.

This is useful rehearsal language, not mandatory future canon wording.

Identity remains open. Greg can name things he wants other people to do or become much more easily than he can answer what he wants to be.

At D180 he writes:

`WHO IS GREG?`

and

`WHAT DO I WANT?`

He deliberately does not decompose the questions into a campaign.
