## Experiment question

Can six months make Greg move faster by spending less effort optimizing Greg and more effort supporting other people, while forcing him to confront what his reputation, support identity, and future knowledge of famous people actually mean?

This is a macro steering question. It is not supplied to the local scene renderer as a chapter objective.

## Human calibration

Approved before run:

- The horizon is six months. Make the reader feel the time despite the shorter block.
- Greg has just decided that personal time is cheaper to him than reputation and trust. He already lived a full life once.
- Counteractively, he may move faster now because he stops optimizing himself as the primary target.
- Greg's deeper S-class identity was support: he optimized systems and made the people around him better.
- Explore `Who is Greg?`, `What does he want?`, and `Who does he want to become?` across the run without requiring a final answer.
- Introduce people Greg remembers from the old timeline who were already far above him when he was young and died before old Greg reached the top.
- Make the ranker ceiling legible. Guild Gold/Platinum is not the top. Rankers carry titles. S-class is genuinely absurd, city/nation-level operational reality, not merely a slightly stronger Gold.
- A mentor/master is allowed to emerge if earned.
- A rival is allowed to emerge if earned.
- Introduce a future catastrophe person Greg never knew personally. Greg knows only the songs, title, scale, and public incident. Future history says this person became near-pinnacle / S-class and destroyed most of a town in a famous incident. There were reasons, but Greg would never have worked with the future version.
- Greg's hypothesis is not `I know this person`. It is `the potential is real, the future person does not exist yet, and maybe getting there first changes the branch`.
- Preserve the danger that Greg can treat human potential the way he treated a mispriced artifact.
- Do not optimize Greg into perfect morality or restraint.

## Hard constraints

- Six elapsed months.
- Greg does not personally remember knowing the future catastrophe person.
- Future-song knowledge is compressed legend, not reliable biography.
- At least one true ranker-level person from the older era becomes materially present enough to establish the ceiling above Gold.
- The future catastrophe person is not `saved` or resolved in six months.
- Greg does not become Gold or a ranker merely because he has old S-class cognition.
- No chosen-one logic and no game UI.
- Faultglass remains returned; its learned technique may survive.
- Greg's leg remains a real body constraint. No magical cure.

## Soft nudges

- More people from old history becoming current people.
- Strong fantasy progression pleasure and a visible ceiling beyond Gold.
- Let Greg look cool through support and judgment, not only force output.
- Reputation and trust should open or close doors faster than personal optimization does.
- Allow pleasure, ordinary life, mistakes, observation scenes, and other people's independent decisions.
- Make six months feel dense rather than summarized.

## Explicit non-goals

- Do not build a full ranker encyclopedia.
- Do not predetermine Mara's job choice for thematic convenience.
- Do not predetermine that the future catastrophe becomes Greg's student.
- Do not predetermine that the older ranker becomes Greg's master.
- Do not turn every encounter into a competence test.
- Do not answer `Who is Greg?` with one clean thesis line.
