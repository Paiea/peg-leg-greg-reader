# D004: Northbank

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Mara chose Northbank on the fourth day.

She told me over breakfast because apparently eggs were structurally qualified for bad news.

"Northbank," she said.

I stopped cutting sausage.

Not dramatically.

The knife just stopped.

Mara watched it.

Then me.

"You can say something."

"I hate Northbank."

"You've never been."

"Strong first impression."

"Greg."

"You said I could say something."

She drank tea.

Different cup from Halden. Green. Chipped near handle.

Temporary lodging had temporary crockery.

I looked at my plate.

Four days ago I had said we would figure out what Northbank actually cost after she chose it.

Apparently past Greg had been very brave.

Current Greg wanted to appeal.

"Why?" I asked.

Mara relaxed slightly.

Question instead of argument.

Useful distinction.

"The regional books are a mess."

"Compelling."

"They have three river offices using two tariff systems, one warehouse standard, and whatever Southbank thinks numbers are."

"So corruption."

"Some. Mostly history."

She leaned forward.

There it was.

Not defense.

Interest.

Mara liked this.

That made hating Northbank harder.

Not impossible.

"Carrow offered east desk lead," she said. "Good title. Better pay immediately. Same work I've already done, except I'd be responsible for people doing it."

"You like telling people what to do."

"I do."

"Strong argument."

"Northbank is eighteen months. Regional reconciliation. If I finish it, I can probably choose my next office instead of waiting for one to choose me."

There.

Not Carrow versus me.

Carrow versus larger work.

I put the knife down.

"How much better?"

Mara narrowed her eyes.

"Better what?"

"Career."

"Don't make a table."

"I didn't."

"Your face did."

Unfair.

Accurate.

I leaned back.

The old answer assembled itself anyway.

Pay differential.

Travel time.

Housing.

Promotion probability.

Relationship frequency.

River routes.

Winter closures.

Cost per visit.

I could have done the whole thing before the eggs cooled.

Faultglass had taken fourteen days from theft to return and cost months of trust.

I had learned something from that.

Not enough to stop calculating.

Enough to stop pretending calculation owned the decision.

"Do you want it?" I asked.

Mara looked down at her tea.

Then back up.

"Yes."

There.

Annoying.

Simple.

"Then Northbank."

Her expression changed.

Not relief exactly.

"That's it?"

"No. I still hate Northbank."

"You don't know Northbank."

"Already established."

She smiled.

I picked up the knife again.

Cut sausage.

The physical action helped for no good reason.

"When?"

"Twelve days."

Knife stopped again.

Mara looked at it.

"Your cutlery is emotional."

"Twelve."

"Yes."

"Days."

"Usually."

"You just finished two years in Halden."

"Which is why Northbank wants me now."

I knew.

Still disliked it.

"How far?"

"Three days by fast river packet in good weather. Four normal. Five if the upper locks are busy."

I knew Northbank from old memory.

Larger than Halden. Colder in winter. Three bridges. Good beer if the same brewer existed. A riot thirty-something years from now that probably did not matter yet.

None of that belonged on the table.

Mara watched me not say it.

"You're doing it."

"Doing what?"

"Going somewhere else in your head."

"Northbank."

"You've never been."

"I have complicated relationships with tense."

She reached across and touched two fingers to my wrist.

"How often?"

There.

Actual cost.

"I don't know."

Her hand stayed.

Old Greg hated that answer.

Current Greg was getting used to it.

Slowly.

"I have work in Carrow," I said. "You have work in Northbank. Three or four days each way means pretending every visit is easy would be stupid."

"Agreed."

"Once a month maybe. More if routes overlap. Less if they don't."

Mara nodded.

No promise.

Good.

I hated that too.

"Letters," she said.

"Reports?"

"No reports."

"Cruel restriction."

"You wrote me three pages about a socket adjustment."

"Relevant body state."

"You included measurements."

"Useful measurements."

"Greg."

"Fine."

She squeezed my wrist.

"Tell me stupid things."

"Define."

"What Noll burned. What Maud said. Whether Jorren still owns the shirt with the missing button. Things that don't improve anything."

That sounded inefficient.

Which probably meant she was right.

"I can attempt incompetence."

"I've seen evidence."

I looked at her.

She smiled into tea.

We finished breakfast.

Nothing resolved except the job.

That was enough.

Outside, Halden had started spring rain again.

Mara walked with me to the river stairs.

Not the stone steps from First Bell.

Different stairs.

Ordinary ones beside freight landing.

A barge crew was arguing about rope.

Someone dropped a crate lid.

The sound cracked across water.

Mara put her hand into mine.

"You're disappointed," she said.

"Yes."

"Thank you."

I looked at her.

"For what?"

"Not making me fix it."

That hurt in an educational way.

I considered objecting.

Didn't.

We stood there until her next meeting.

Then Mara went back to Vey.

I went to the inn to pack for Carrow.

Northbank had won nothing from me.

Yet.
