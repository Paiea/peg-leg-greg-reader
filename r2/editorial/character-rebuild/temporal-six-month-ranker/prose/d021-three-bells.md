# D021: Three Bells

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn Calder's name was on a suspension notice.

I nearly walked past it.

That bothered me afterward.

The Guild side board held things people preferred not to read unless personally involved.

Late payment disputes.

License holds.

Missing equipment.

Temporary scope restrictions.

One complaint about a mule whose owner had apparently exhausted ordinary law.

I was looking for road work.

My eyes crossed **CALDER, RENN** and kept moving.

Then memory sang.

Not metaphorically.

A man's voice I had not heard in decades came back through a tavern I could not place.

Bad lute.

Everybody drunk enough to improve it.

Three lines.

**First bell broke the gate.**

**Second broke the guard.**

**Third bell broke Dunbarrow.**

I stopped breathing.

Looked back.

The notice remained ordinary paper.

**CALDER, RENN**

**TEMPORARY GUILD WORK HOLD**

**STONEWAKE QUARRY INCIDENT REVIEW**

**UNCONTROLLED STRUCTURAL RESONANCE / PROPERTY DAMAGE**

No deaths.

One minor injury.

Pending review.

Current rank:

Bronze.

I read that twice.

Bronze.

Renn Calder.

The Red Bell.

Not yet.

My skin went cold under my shirt.

Future Greg had never met him.

That certainty came cleanly.

No missing face trying to attach itself.

No old conversation.

No contract.

No battlefield.

Renn Calder belonged to songs, arguments, histories, and one ugly military lecture about why ranker incidents required evacuation radii instead of heroics.

S-class by the end.

Maybe before Dunbarrow.

Maybe after.

I did not know.

That should have embarrassed me more.

The song did not care about chronology.

Songs cared about rhyme.

The public story was that Red Bell erased most of Dunbarrow in one night.

Reasons depended on who was singing.

Revenge.

Rebellion.

A hostage execution.

A corrupt magistrate.

A cult under the old courthouse.

One northern version claimed the entire town had already been dead.

That was probably bullshit.

Probably.

I stared at `Bronze` again.

A future S-class Ranker was somewhere near Carrow breaking quarry property badly enough to get suspended.

My first thought was immediate.

Find him.

My second thought arrived half a breath later.

Why?

That was newer.

I took the notice number to Sella.

She was eating an apple behind the desk.

"No."

"I haven't asked."

"You have a face."

"Cruel institution."

She took another bite.

I put the notice number down.

"Renn Calder."

Sella stopped chewing.

Not much.

Enough.

"What about him?"

"Where is he?"

"No."

There.

Different no.

"Public notice."

"Public suspension. Not public home address."

"I didn't ask for his home."

"You asked where he is."

Fair.

"Who owns the review?"

"Quarry office and Guild safety."

"Public hearing?"

"No."

"Next review date?"

"Not yours."

I looked at her.

Sella looked back.

This was reputation.

Not abstract.

A year ago I could have asked strange questions and received mostly annoyance.

Now I had stolen an expensive artifact from a client-connected warehouse because future knowledge told me it mattered.

Sella knew that.

So when I appeared asking about a suspended Bronze stranger, she did not assume curiosity.

Good.

Painful.

Good.

"I'm not going to steal him," I said.

Sella closed her eyes.

"That sentence made everything worse."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Increasingly."

She opened her eyes.

"Why Calder?"

I could lie.

Useful resonance specialist.

Quarry work.

Interested in structural magic.

All technically plausible.

Instead I said, "I know something about what he might become."

Sella stared.

There were maybe six people in current life who knew enough about my memory problem for that sentence to have a category.

She was one.

"Something useful?"

"Maybe."

"Dangerous?"

"Very."

"To him?"

I stopped.

That was the correct question and I hated it.

"I don't know."

Sella put the apple down.

"Then you don't get his file."

No negotiation.

I nodded.

That surprised her.

Maybe me too.

"Public path?" I asked.

She considered.

Then pointed toward the contract board.

"Stonewake posts day labor. Quarry's east of River Six. Calder worked there under public Bronze contract before the hold. If he takes public work again after review, you'll see it when everyone else does."

"Review timing?"

Sella looked at me.

Right.

"Fine."

I picked up the notice number.

She put two fingers over it.

"Leave that."

I left it.

At the door she called my name.

I turned.

"Greg."

"Yes?"

"Whatever you think you know about him."

I waited.

"You knew Faultglass better than Harrow did."

That landed.

"Yes."

"Didn't make it yours."

There.

I looked through the open door at the side board.

Renn Calder.

Bronze.

Future S-class.

A song about a town.

Potential with a name on it.

"I know," I said.

Sella's face said she did not believe me yet.

Fair.

Neither did I.

I took an ordinary south-road contract instead.

Two days.

Mediocre pay.

Nothing historic happened.

That felt important enough not to explain.
