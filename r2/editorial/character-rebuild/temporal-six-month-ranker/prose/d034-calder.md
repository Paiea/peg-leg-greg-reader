# D034: Calder

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn Calder returned to public work thirteen days later.

I knew because his name appeared on a contract before I did anything clever.

This was disappointing.

Also healthier.

Stonewake Quarry wanted three Bronze laborers, one load ward assistant, and one independent safety observer for a controlled resonance retest.

The observer had to be Silver or better.

Sella put the token on the counter between us.

"You qualify."

"I noticed."

"Do you want it because you qualify?"

"No."

She waited.

"I want it because Calder is there."

"Better."

"Honesty?"

"Evidence."

I looked at her.

She drank tea.

Apparently she had replaced apples with moral superiority.

"I am not asking for his file," I said.

"Also better."

"I'm beginning to hate growth."

"Good."

Of course.

Stonewake sat in a cut east of River Six where half a hillside had been converted into blocks, dust, and men regretting lungs.

Renn Calder was carrying a bucket.

That was my first sight of the future Red Bell.

Bucket.

Gray shirt with one sleeve rolled higher than the other. Dark hair cut badly at the neck. Narrow shoulders that had not decided whether they were done growing. Two fingers wrapped in white cloth.

He looked nineteen.

Maybe twenty.

Songs had made him taller.

They usually did.

He dumped water into a trough beside the cutting line and looked at me.

Not recognition.

Assessment.

Walking leg.

Guild token.

Sword.

Then away.

Good.

I had spent two weeks remembering a monster.

Current Renn had a bucket.

The quarry foreman, Vessa Tor, explained the retest without drama.

"Calder rings stone."

Renn looked over from the trough.

"I don't call it that."

"Everybody else does."

"Everybody else is lazy."

Vessa ignored him with practiced skill.

"He puts charge in. Stone answers. Usually where he wants. Last time the answer traveled through a bedding seam into support cut three."

"Usually," Renn said.

Vessa pointed at him.

"That word is why you're on half pay."

Renn smiled.

Not pleasant.

Not cruel either.

Young-person smile when authority had correctly identified a weakness and therefore become annoying.

I liked him slightly less.

Useful.

The test block was a waist-high slab of dark quarry stone separated from the active wall by twenty feet.

No structure overhead.

No workers inside thirty feet except us.

Vessa had learned something from the incident.

Renn stepped to the block.

No staff.

No hammer.

He put two knuckles against stone.

That surprised me.

Future songs described bells.

I had assumed sound.

There was none.

Not at first.

Mana moved.

I felt that.

Thin.

Deep.

Not much volume.

Renn tapped once.

The stone clicked.

A line appeared from his knuckles to the far edge.

Perfectly straight for six inches.

Then it forked.

He tapped again.

The fork traveled down.

The block split into three pieces.

No explosion.

No heroic shock.

Just stone deciding it had always wanted to be separate.

I forgot to breathe.

Vessa looked at me.

"Problem?"

"No."

Renn glanced over.

There it was.

Future potential.

Not because the block broke.

Because I could feel how little mana he used.

He had not overpowered stone.

He had found where the stone wanted to fail and told it sooner.

Old Greg had known two resonance specialists at A-class.

Neither did that at nineteen.

Probably.

Memory was not a database.

I reminded myself before enthusiasm became certainty.

Second block had an iron rod cast through center.

Renn touched stone.

Paused.

Moved his hand three inches.

Tapped.

The crack curved around iron.

Vessa swore quietly.

I did not.

Mostly because my mouth had gone dry.

Third block was the real test.

Natural seam.

Unknown internal shape.

Renn crouched.

Put his palm flat.

Nothing happened for several breaths.

Then he frowned.

"There's a wet line."

Vessa said, "Where?"

Renn pointed.

"Back third. Slants left."

I sensed load.

Not water.

Could not confirm.

Vessa drilled a narrow probe.

Water darkened the dust exactly where Renn said.

Future Red Bell.

Bronze.

I understood the songs slightly more and trusted them slightly less.

Then Renn tapped.

The entire block jumped.

Not high.

Maybe half an inch.

Enough.

The crack did not stay in the block.

I felt it enter ground.

Fast.

"Stop."

Renn's hand came off immediately.

Good.

I dropped a feathered plane against the dirt between block and cutting line.

Not to stop force.

To feel it.

A pulse reached the plane.

Sharp.

Then another weaker one through stone beneath us.

The plane rang through my forearm.

Renn looked at it.

His face changed.

First real interest.

"What is that?"

"Boundary."

"You stopped it?"

"No. Felt it."

"Why?"

"Because you didn't."

He stared at me.

Vessa made a sound behind us.

Apparently my reputation for diplomacy remained safe.

Renn stood.

"I felt the block."

"Not the ground after."

"You know that how?"

"You looked surprised when I said stop."

His jaw tightened.

There.

Not villain.

Pride.

Normal amount.

Maybe extra.

"Again," he said.

Vessa said, "No."

Renn looked at her.

"We know what happened."

"Exactly. Test found what test was for."

He looked irritated.

I liked Vessa immediately.

The future S-class wanted another attempt.

Quarry foreman said no.

Future did not own present.

We spent the next hour marking the ground pulse and checking whether it had reached active support cut.

It had not.

Renn carried tools.

So did I.

He watched my planes twice.

I watched him not ask.

At lunch we sat under a canvas lean-to with eight quarry workers and food nobody would write songs about.

Renn ate boiled eggs like they had offended him.

I waited until he had one left.

Then stopped waiting because that had become weird.

"Calder."

He looked up.

"Greg."

"You know my name."

"Vessa said it six times."

"Less impressive."

"What do you want?"

Direct.

I had prepared three versions.

One professional.

One honest.

One that sounded less insane.

I accidentally chose a fourth.

"I think you're wasting about twenty years."

Renn stared at me.

A worker beside him stopped chewing.

Excellent.

"What?"

"Your sensing is ahead of your containment. If you learn where the impulse goes after the target, you can use more of what you already have without increasing output."

Renn's expression changed from confusion to suspicion.

"You saw three blocks."

"Yes."

"And now you know my life."

There.

Faultglass, but human.

I heard it too late.

"No."

"Sounded like it."

"You're right."

That stopped him.

Not forgiveness.

Unexpected input.

I tried again.

"I know load boundaries. You do something I can't do. I might be able to help you see where it goes."

"Why?"

Song.

Town.

S-class.

Potential.

Chess piece.

I hated the last phrase as soon as my own mind supplied it.

"Because I want to know if I can," I said.

True.

Incomplete.

Renn looked at me for a long time.

"No."

Then ate the last egg.

Fair.

I finished the contract.

Vessa signed my token.

Renn left before me.

No dramatic departure.

He had dust on both boots and carried the same bucket from morning.

The future Red Bell walked down quarry road with a bucket banging against one knee.

I watched until the bend.

Then Vessa said, "If you recruit my problem, do it after his review closes."

I looked at her.

"I wasn't recruiting."

"Good. You were bad at it."

Also fair.
