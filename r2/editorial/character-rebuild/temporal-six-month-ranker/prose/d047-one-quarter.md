# D047: One Quarter

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Noll bought one quarter of The Brass Spoon without telling me.

I found out because he charged me for soup.

"What?"

Noll pointed at the slate beside till.

**STAFF DISCOUNT: HALF**

**OWNERS: FREE MEAL DURING SHIFT**

"I'm not staff."

"Correct."

"I have eaten here free for years."

"Crime."

"Tradition."

Noll held out his hand.

I paid two coppers.

Then stared at him.

He stared back.

Something about his shirt bothered me.

Not shirt.

Key.

A brass key hung from cord under collar.

Kitchen back door used iron.

Store room used brass.

"Why do you have the store key?"

Noll looked down.

"Mine."

"I know it's yours. Why?"

"Owner."

I waited.

He ladled soup for a dockworker.

I kept waiting.

Noll put bread beside bowl.

Dockworker took it.

Noll wiped ladle against pot rim.

"Owner," he repeated.

"Of what?"

He looked around.

I followed.

Brass Spoon.

No.

"How much?"

Noll held up four fingers.

"Four?"

He turned hand and folded three down.

One.

Quarter.

I stared.

"You own one quarter of this place."

"Yes."

"Since when?"

"Nineteen days."

I counted backward automatically.

Halden.

I had been sitting on stone steps with Mara while Noll purchased part of a restaurant.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

Noll shrugged.

That answer irritated me more than a complicated one.

"Noll."

He pointed at soup.

"Cold."

"The soup can survive."

"Maybe."

I took the bowl to our usual table because apparently ownership did not include emotional availability.

Noll came over eight minutes later.

Not because I summoned him.

Because lunch rush thinned.

He sat across from me.

The second chair again.

Different building.

Same problem.

"How?" I asked.

"Tessa wanted out."

Tessa had owned half with her brother, Bren.

I knew that.

Probably.

"Why?"

"Mother sick. South."

There.

A whole life had happened while I was busy elsewhere.

"She sold you half of her half."

Noll nodded.

"Price?"

He told me.

I nearly dropped spoon.

"You had that?"

"Some."

"Some is not the number you just said."

"Bren carries rest."

"Debt."

"Yes."

"Terms?"

Noll looked tired suddenly.

Not physically.

Of me.

I stopped.

There it was.

The entire structure had appeared in my head.

Revenue.

Winter risk.

Supplier credit.

Ownership shares.

Whether he had overpaid.

Whether Bren could force sale.

What happened if Tessa's mother died and she came back.

Whether Noll had a written agreement.

I had not yet said congratulations.

"Sorry," I said.

Noll waited.

"Congratulations."

He smiled.

Small.

Real.

"Thank you."

I ate soup.

Three bites.

Hardest discipline of the month.

Then Noll said, "Ask."

"Everything?"

"One."

Cruel.

I chose carefully.

"Do you like owning it?"

Noll looked toward kitchen.

A young woman I did not know was cutting onions at prep table.

Another change.

"Yes."

"Good."

He looked back.

"Now two."

"Written agreement?"

"Yes."

Relief moved through me so hard I became offended.

Noll laughed.

"Jorren made me."

I stopped.

"Jorren knew?"

"Yes."

"How long?"

"Before."

"Before you bought?"

"Yes."

Betrayal.

Not real betrayal.

Worse category.

Friends having conversations without me.

"Why didn't he tell me?"

Noll shrugged.

"Mine."

There.

Not secret.

Not mine.

I looked at the kitchen again.

The new woman was arguing with Bren about onion thickness.

Bren lost.

Good hire probably.

I had not interviewed her.

The Spoon had not collapsed.

Noll had negotiated debt, ownership, paperwork, and a new employee while I was in Halden keeping a promise.

I had missed it.

Nothing had gone wrong because I missed it.

That should not have felt surprising.

It did.

"Who is she?" I asked.

"Pela."

"Good?"

Noll looked over.

"Fast. Bad eggs."

Pela shouted from kitchen, "I heard that."

Noll raised voice.

"Fix eggs."

"Fix your face."

Noll looked at me.

"Good."

I laughed.

Soup had gone lukewarm.

Still fine.

Before leaving I paid for another bowl to take to Maud.

Noll took the money.

No discount.

"Owner," he said.

"Monster."

"Two copper."

I paid.

Outside, I stood under the Brass Spoon sign for a minute.

The paint needed touching on left edge.

Noll probably knew.

I did not tell him.
