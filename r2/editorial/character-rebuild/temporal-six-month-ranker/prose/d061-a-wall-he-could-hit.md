# D061: A Wall He Could Hit

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn found me at The Brass Spoon.

Noll pointed him toward my table and charged him for tea.

Ownership had corrupted him completely.

Renn sat without asking.

"How much for a wall?"

I looked at him.

"Good morning."

"Morning. How much?"

"What wall?"

"The thing at Stonewake."

"Plane."

"Wall."

"Technically offensive."

"How much?"

There it was.

Not yes.

Not teacher.

A transaction.

Better.

"For what?"

Renn leaned both forearms on table.

The white wraps were gone from his fingers.

Small scars across two knuckles.

"I want to hit something harder."

Future song entered my head immediately.

Third bell broke Dunbarrow.

I pushed it out.

Current Renn wanted a test surface.

"No."

His face closed.

I held up one hand.

"Not no. Wrong question."

"Sounds like no."

"How much output can you produce before you stop knowing where it goes?"

Renn frowned.

"That's why I want the wall."

"Then you don't want a wall. You want somewhere failure can go without becoming somebody else's problem."

He stared at me.

"You talk like you're sixty."

"Rude."

"How old are you?"

"Enough."

"That's not an age."

"Neither is eventually."

He looked genuinely confused.

Good.

Some habits did not deserve context.

I named my day rate.

Renn laughed.

Not politely.

"Fuck off."

"Silver specialist."

"For a wall?"

"Plane."

"Half."

"No."

"Three quarters."

"Plus site cost."

"You're charging me dirt?"

"Someone owns the dirt."

He leaned back.

I waited.

This was familiar in a way I liked.

Not future knowledge.

Work.

Renn said, "I can do one day."

"One day is enough to learn whether this is stupid."

"And if it isn't?"

"Then we renegotiate."

He held out his hand.

I looked at it.

"What?"

"Agreement."

"You don't have to shake for day labor."

"You're weird."

"Established."

I shook.

We rented the old clay pit behind Stonewake because Vessa owed me a favor and because nothing structural remained within eighty yards except one collapsed shed everyone agreed could become more collapsed.

Kessa came because I wanted a second observer.

Renn objected.

"I'm paying you."

"Correct."

"Why is she here?"

"Because I can be wrong."

Kessa looked at him.

"Frequently."

"You're not helping."

"Free service."

Renn considered leaving.

Didn't.

Good start.

I made the first plane at twenty feet.

Waist high.

Six feet wide.

Feathered edges.

Not much force.

Renn looked at it.

"Hit that?"

"No."

"Then why is it there?"

"I want you to ring the stone. Plane is behind it."

We had dragged a quarry block into center.

Renn touched it.

Tapped.

Crack.

Pulse reached my plane.

I felt a sharp point near center, then a low fan spreading left.

"Again. Same output."

He did.

Different fan.

"Again."

Third.

Renn looked at me.

"You getting anything?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"Your second was cleaner."

"Why?"

"Don't know."

He smiled.

That was the first time I saw what songs probably liked about him.

Not menace.

Delight.

He liked not knowing when not knowing meant there was more.

"Again," he said.

"Rest."

Smile vanished.

"I'm fine."

"I am not."

That confused him enough to obey.

My right forearm tingled from catching pulses.

The walking leg was fine, but standing static had started pressure at rear socket.

I sat on a crate.

Renn remained standing.

Kessa handed me water.

"You look happy," she said.

"Professional."

"Happy."

"Unprovable."

Renn said, "He looks creepy."

"Thank you."

We changed geometry after lunch.

Two planes.

One behind target.

One under ground line at angle.

The second was barely a plane. More like a curved lip of force pressed into dirt.

Renn tapped.

The underground pulse hit it.

And came back.

Not much.

Enough that Renn jerked his hand away before I spoke.

"You felt that."

He looked at his fingers.

"Yes."

"Where?"

"Back of wrist."

"Again?"

He grinned.

"Thought you were the rest guy."

"I'm inconsistent."

Second tap.

Return pulse.

Renn's eyes widened.

Third.

He changed something.

I felt it before seeing block.

The pulse went down instead of out.

Stone cracked vertically.

Ground barely answered.

Renn laughed.

Actually laughed.

Loud enough that workers at active quarry looked over.

"Do that again," I said.

"No."

I stared.

"What?"

"You said one day."

He looked toward sun.

Fifth bell approaching.

Bastard.

"We have time."

"Renegotiate."

Kessa made a choking sound.

I had apparently created commerce.

Renn named a lower rate for his second day.

I told him he was the one paying me.

"Then I want discount."

"You learned too quickly."

"Your fault."

There was no song for this.

No title.

No town.

Just Renn Calder negotiating a day rate beside a broken stone while Kessa laughed at both of us.

I booked another day.
