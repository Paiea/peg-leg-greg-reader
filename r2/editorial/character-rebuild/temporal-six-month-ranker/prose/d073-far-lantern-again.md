# D073: Far Lantern, Again

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Merel Dane sent me a note with no greeting.

**Sella says you are wasting mana at your left edge. Third bell. East practice yard. Bring the leg you work in.**

I read it twice.

Then took it to Sella.

"Why did you tell an S-class Ranker about my left edge?"

Sella looked over the note.

"I didn't."

"It says Sella."

"I told her you were the Silver from river road. She asked what kind of idiot stares at force geometry during a runaway barge. I said your kind."

"Helpful."

"Apparently."

"How does she know the edge?"

Sella shrugged.

This was becoming a professional strategy.

I arrived at East practice yard with the field peg on and the walking leg strapped to my pack.

The yard was empty except for Merel and the Platinum man from river road.

He had a name now.

Orris Pell.

No relation to half the Pells I knew, probably. Carrow produced Pells the way damp produced mold.

Merel pointed at my field peg.

"Better."

"For standing."

"I know."

Of course she did.

Orris leaned against fence.

No weapon visible.

Merel wore the same blue coat.

No badge.

Rankers did not need one unless somebody was checking paperwork.

"Plane," she said.

I made one.

Small.

Horizontal at waist height.

Feathered edges.

Merel put three fingers on it.

Not physically.

Mana touched force.

My plane flexed.

Then collapsed.

I stared.

She had not pushed through.

She had found the weakest edge and let my own tension unzip it.

Three fingers.

"Again."

I made another.

Changed the edge.

She touched right side.

Collapse.

"Again."

Third lasted longer.

Not much.

"What are you doing?" I asked.

"Nothing important."

"Rude."

"Your center is honest. Your edges lie."

I looked at the plane.

"Meaning?"

"You shape a surface you can hold, then spend extra force pretending the boundary is cleaner than it is."

Faultglass had shown something similar.

Edge stress.

Feathering reduced it.

Merel saw it with fingers.

I hated her immediately.

Professionally.

"How much am I wasting?"

"Depends. Thirty percent on that one. More when you're tired."

I almost swore.

Didn't because number offended me into silence.

Merel smiled.

"There. That's why Sella remembered you."

"My face?"

"You get personally insulted by inefficiency."

Orris laughed from fence.

Apparently this was a recurring problem.

I tried again.

Merel stopped me.

"No."

"You said third bell."

"I didn't say training."

I looked at note.

She had not.

Bastard.

"Then why am I here?"

"Curiosity."

Same answer I had given Renn.

That felt dangerous.

Merel walked to bench and sat.

I remained standing until field peg reminded me pride had pressure limits.

Then sat across.

"How old are you?" she asked.

"Twenty-two."

I did the math after saying it.

Close enough.

Time had become strange through experiment labels, but body agreed.

Merel's eyebrows moved.

"You think older."

"People keep saying that."

"People usually mean tired. I don't."

Orris stopped smiling.

Merel continued.

"You watched the barge correctly. You looked at where load went, not what I held. Most Silver supports watch the bright part. Most Gold too. Platinum stops doing that if they live."

"And Ranker?"

"Ranker stops being useful as one category."

I waited.

She looked at me.

"You want the ladder."

"I know the ladder."

"Then why ask?"

Because I wanted to hear current version say it.

Future memory carried rank like weather reports from a country I had left.

"Calibration."

Merel accepted that.

"Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum are Guild operating classes. Contracts. Scope. Expected team capability. Ranker is when individual capability matters enough to classify separately. A-class can change a campaign. S-class can change whether there is a campaign."

Clean.

I felt old memory settle around it.

Yes.

Not exact words.

True shape.

"Titles?" I asked.

Merel's mouth tightened.

"Public convenience."

Orris said, "She hates hers."

"Far Lantern is stupid."

"People can see it from miles away."

"So can fire. Nobody calls every arsonist Near Sun."

I laughed.

Couldn't help it.

Merel looked pleased despite herself.

"Titles happen because people need short language for what a Ranker means. Sometimes Guild gives one. Sometimes soldiers. Sometimes songs. Sometimes the title is wrong and survives anyway."

Songs.

Renn Calder.

Red Bell.

My attention moved.

Merel noticed.

Of course.

"Who?"

"What?"

"You left."

"I am sitting here."

"Your face left. Who?"

I considered lying.

Then considered how badly that had worked recently.

"A Bronze resonance worker."

Orris laughed again.

Merel did not.

"Why does a Bronze make you think about Ranker titles?"

There was no safe answer.

"Potential."

"Everyone has potential."

"Not that kind."

Merel's eyes sharpened.

I had made the sentence worse.

"You know him?"

"No."

"Then you're guessing."

"Yes."

Not entirely.

Enough.

She waited for more.

I did not give it.

Eventually Merel stood.

"Tomorrow. Same time."

I looked up.

"Training?"

"Maybe."

I hated maybe.

She knew immediately.

"Good," she said.

Of course.

"How long are you in Carrow?"

"Seventeen days."

There.

A number.

My mind built schedule before I could stop it.

Seventeen days.

Fourteen useful sessions if travel and work allowed.

Maybe sixteen.

Merel watched my face.

"Don't."

"What?"

"Turn seventeen days into a campaign before you know if I like you."

I stared.

Orris covered his mouth.

Traitor.

"I wasn't."

Merel looked at me.

"Tomorrow, Greg."

She left.

Orris followed.

At gate he turned back.

"She likes you."

"Why?"

"She was rude twice."

"That seems low evidence."

"You're learning."

They went.

I sat alone in practice yard with my field peg planted in dirt and seventeen days trying very hard not to become a spreadsheet.

I lasted almost a minute.
