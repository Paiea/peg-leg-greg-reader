# D090: Seventeen Days

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Merel stayed seventeen days.

I saw her on eleven.

This was not failure.

I wrote that down on day six because apparently I needed evidence.

Day one she broke four planes with three fingers each.

Day two she made me walk.

Not train while walking.

Walk.

East wall to river market. Field peg first half. Walking leg second.

She asked questions about buildings.

Not magic.

Who owned the awning over baker lane.

Which stair I avoided.

Where carts backed up at fourth bell.

Which door at Guild opened inward and therefore became a problem during crowding.

I answered most.

Missed two.

"Support starts before mana," she said.

I hated the sentence because I already agreed.

Day three she did not show.

No note.

I waited twenty minutes before asking Orris.

"River office," he said.

"Emergency?"

"Paper."

S-class Ranker defeated by forms.

Excellent.

Day four I had paid work.

Merel told me to take it.

"I can move contract."

"Why?"

"You're here seventeen days."

"And?"

There it was again.

I took the contract.

Nothing important happened.

We replaced load wards on a dye warehouse roof. The owner complained about price. One apprentice stepped in blue pigment and became blue to ankle.

I came home tired and did not regret missing Merel until after dinner.

Then regretted it badly.

Both true.

Day five Renn and I rented the clay pit again.

Merel came because I told her I would not be at practice yard.

She watched from fence.

Renn disliked her immediately.

Good instinct maybe.

"Who's she?" he asked.

Merel stood thirty feet away in blue coat with Orris.

"Merel."

"Why is a Ranker watching me?"

"Because she's nosy."

Merel called, "Correct."

Renn stared at me.

"You didn't say Ranker."

"Would it change the stone?"

"Would change whether I came."

There.

My fault.

I had optimized for avoiding performance anxiety.

Forgot consent liked relevant information.

"Sorry."

Renn looked at Merel.

Then back.

"She staying?"

"Your call."

He thought.

"Fine."

Not forgiveness.

Permission.

We tested for an hour.

Merel said nothing until Renn hit a downward pulse cleaner than last time.

Then she said, "Again."

Renn looked at me.

"She's worse than you."

"Much."

Day six Merel asked why I used a field peg instead of walking leg for static work.

I explained.

She listened.

No advice.

Then asked, "What did you use before?"

"Two legs."

"Smartass."

"Accurate."

"Before the injury, what stance?"

I showed her.

She made me stop halfway.

"You're still casting from it."

I looked down.

Field peg planted wider.

Right knee bent.

Hip angle different.

But she was right.

My magic still expected pressure from a left foot that no longer existed.

I knew this in pieces.

Bera knew it physically.

Merel saw it in mana.

"How much does that cost?"

"I don't know."

"Good."

I swore.

She laughed.

Day seven she taught nothing.

We ate lunch.

Orris told a story about Merel falling through a roof in Tern because she had reinforced the wrong building.

Merel corrected him.

"Correct building. Wrong roof."

"You fell."

"Roof was already leaving."

"With you on it."

I laughed hard enough my socket shifted.

Had to stand and reset it.

Merel looked away while I did.

Not pretending not to see.

Giving privacy.

Different.

Day eight she was gone again.

Day nine I found a letter from Mara under my door.

Three pages.

Northbank had one bridge too many, all of them ugly.

Southbank numbers were apparently a personal attack.

She had bought a red blanket because the room Vey rented was cold at night.

At bottom:

**Noll says you met Far Lantern. You did not mention this. Your stupid-things correspondence policy is under review.**

I wrote back:

**She is rude. Noll owns soup now. Renn Calder exists. My left edge wastes mana. Maud has started feeding a gray cat she claims not to own.**

Then, because Mara had asked for stupid things:

**I dropped a spoon yesterday. It landed in my boot. Right boot. Important distinction.**

That letter felt harder than a report.

I sent it.

Day ten Merel took me to river embankment at dawn.

Fog.

No people except fishermen.

She made one plane over water.

Thin enough I could barely sense it.

Then another fifty yards away.

Then another beyond visibility.

I turned.

"How far?"

"Two hundred and twelve yards."

"You can hold that?"

"Yes."

"Why twelve?"

"Tree."

I looked through fog.

Could not see tree.

Of course.

"How many?"

"Planes?"

"At once."

Merel looked at me.

"Wrong question."

I hated her.

She smiled.

"How many that matter at once," she said. "That changes every field."

Then she released all three.

No demonstration beyond that.

Worse somehow.

Day eleven was last.

Not seventeenth day.

Last day I saw her.

Merel's river work finished early.

Orris brought her bags to Guild at second bell.

I found out because Sella sent Noll to find me.

Noll sent Pela.

Pela found me buying leather conditioner.

Carrow had become a nervous system against my consent.

I reached Guild before Merel left.

She stood beside a northbound coach.

"You're early," she said.

"Bad influence."

"Probably."

I held out the notebook page from seventeen-day campaign I had absolutely not made.

Then pulled it back before she could take it.

Merel laughed.

"Progress."

"When are you back?"

"Don't know."

I hated that answer on principle.

"Where next?"

"Morn coast. Then maybe Ullet."

"Work?"

"Yes."

Of course.

I wanted to ask for training.

Formal.

Master.

Whatever word made this continue instead of becoming eleven weird days inside a month.

I did not know whether I wanted Merel specifically or access to what Merel represented.

That distinction mattered more now.

"Can I write?" I asked.

Her expression changed slightly.

"Questions?"

"Maybe."

"No reports."

I stared.

Mara had infected the continent.

Merel took a small card from coat and wrote an address.

Not hers.

`Ranker Relay / Far Lantern` followed by route marks.

"Letters find me eventually."

"How eventually?"

"Not a unit."

I looked at her.

Orris laughed from coach.

Traitorous institution.

Merel climbed aboard.

Then leaned back out.

"Greg."

"Yes?"

"Stop trying to become the man who already knew all this."

My body went still.

She could not know what sentence meant.

Not fully.

Maybe she only meant I trained like someone recovering lost technique.

Maybe that was visible.

"What should I become?" I asked.

Merel considered.

Then shrugged.

"That's your problem."

Coach moved.

She sat back.

Orris waved.

I did not.

Seventeen days ended with six unused.

They remained unused anyway.
