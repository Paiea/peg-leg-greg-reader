# D103: Dunbarrow

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn was from Dunbarrow.

I learned this because Noll asked where he learned to hate onions.

Three months of future knowledge undone by lunch.

"Dunbarrow," Renn said.

My spoon hit bowl.

Not hard.

Enough.

Noll looked at spoon.

Then me.

Renn kept eating.

"Dunbarrow where?" I asked.

He looked up.

"How many do you know?"

"One."

"Then that one."

I knew three future songs immediately.

All wrong in different ways.

**Red Bell came north to Dunbarrow.**

No.

He came from it.

**No kin behind him, no grave before.**

Probably bullshit too.

Renn had mentioned an aunt once. Maybe joke. Maybe not.

The song I trusted least suddenly had better odds than the one taught in military halls.

Excellent historical method.

"Why?" Renn asked.

I realized I was staring.

"Nothing."

Bad answer.

Renn's face said so.

Noll took my empty water cup and replaced it without comment.

Not helping.

"You've done that before," Renn said.

"Done what?"

"Looked at me like I reminded you of a corpse."

My skin went cold.

Too close.

"Specific."

"You have a weird face."

"People keep reviewing it."

"Who is Dunbarrow to you?"

Not what.

Who.

Interesting.

I could tell him.

Future.

Song.

Town broken under his title.

I could destroy whatever current relationship existed by handing him an identity he had not earned.

Or I could lie.

Faultglass again.

Not object this time.

Information could be theft too.

I hated that thought.

"A place I know stories about," I said.

Renn waited.

"What stories?"

"Later."

His expression closed.

"That's not an answer."

"No."

"Then why ask me questions?"

Fair.

I looked at Noll.

He was wiping counter twenty feet away.

Not listening.

Absolutely listening.

"Because I know your name from one of them," I said.

Renn stopped moving.

There.

Not full truth.

Enough to be dangerous.

"My name."

"Yes."

"What story?"

"I don't know how true it is."

"Greg."

"I mean that. I know the song. I know what people said happened. I don't know you inside it."

He leaned back.

"Song?"

"Yes."

"There are songs about me?"

Future grammar.

I had fucked it already.

"Not now."

Renn stared.

One breath.

Two.

"You're insane."

"Possible."

"No. Don't do that."

I stopped.

"Do what?"

"Make a joke and act like that fixes the sentence."

There.

Current Renn was not stupid enough for my habits.

Good.

Bad.

Good.

"You're right."

He stood.

Noll looked over.

"Sit," I said.

Wrong word.

Renn's face changed.

Command voice.

Old Greg.

I hated myself immediately.

"Please," I added.

Worse maybe.

Renn remained standing.

"What happens in the song?"

There was no version of this conversation I wanted.

I had wanted to find him before the song.

Apparently finding someone before history also meant explaining why you had been waiting at all.

"A town gets destroyed," I said.

Renn looked toward door.

Then back.

"Dunbarrow."

I nodded.

His face emptied.

Not fear.

Not yet.

"By who?"

I could stop.

Maybe should.

The question belonged to him now.

"A Ranker called Red Bell."

Renn's eyes narrowed.

"And my name?"

"Renn Calder."

Silence.

Kitchen noise continued.

Knife on board.

Pot lid.

Pela swearing because something burned.

World refused to respect revelation.

Renn sat back down slowly.

"You think that's me."

"I think the name matches. Your magic matches enough to scare me. The timing could. That's all I actually know."

"You said Sella knows about your memory."

I looked at him.

He had been collecting information too.

Of course.

"She told you?"

"No. Vessa said you act like you've already read the report. Kessa said not to ask unless I wanted a bad answer."

Kessa.

Wonderful.

"I remember things that haven't happened yet," I said.

Renn laughed once.

No humor.

"Sure."

"Reasonable reaction."

"Shut up."

I did.

He looked down at bowl.

Half soup left.

"How much of Dunbarrow?"

"In the story?"

"Destroyed."

I told him.

Most.

Not every building.

Enough that future maps marked old and new town separately.

Renn swallowed.

"People?"

"Yes."

"How many?"

"Stories disagree."

"How many?"

I gave the lowest number I remembered.

His face changed.

There.

Current person.

Not future weapon.

"My mother lives there," he said.

The song line came back.

No kin behind him.

Bullshit.

"My sister. Two cousins. Half the quarry crew I grew up with."

He looked at me.

"You knew that?"

"No."

"But you came looking for me."

"Yes."

"Because you thought I was going to become strong."

Not a question.

I could soften it.

Didn't.

"Yes."

Renn's mouth twisted.

"Fuck you."

"Fair."

He stood again.

This time I did not tell him to sit.

"Were you trying to stop it?" he asked.

I thought.

Too long.

Renn noticed.

Of course.

"I don't know," I said.

"That's worse."

"Yes."

"Then what?"

Chess piece.

Potential.

Get there first.

All ugly when another person could hear them.

"I wanted to see what happened if someone helped you earlier."

Renn stared.

"Someone."

"Me."

"Because you're helpful."

"No."

That surprised both of us.

I took a breath.

"Because I was very good at making powerful people better in my old life. Maybe the best thing I ever did. And I saw your name and thought I could do it again before you became someone I would have hated."

There.

Ugly truth.

Renn's eyes stayed on mine.

"You don't know me."

"Correct."

"You know a song."

"Correct."

"And you decided what I was for."

That one hurt.

"Yes."

Renn nodded once.

Then left.

Noll waited until door closed.

"Bad?"

I looked at untouched half of Renn's soup.

"Very."

Noll picked it up.

"Waste."

"Also yes."

He carried bowl to kitchen.

I sat alone.

On the wall behind counter, ownership slate had been updated.

Noll's name remained one quarter.

Three months ago I had missed that because his life happened without me.

Today I had nearly reduced Renn's life to a future song because mine happened before his.

I paid for both bowls.

Then went home.
