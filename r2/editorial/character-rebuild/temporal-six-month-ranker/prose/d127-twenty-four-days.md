# D127: Twenty-Four Days

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn did not speak to me for twenty-four days.

I did not chase him.

This was harder than several monster contracts.

Day two after soup, I wrote an apology.

Then tore it up because paragraph three explained why I had done the thing I was apologizing for.

Day three I wrote another.

Shorter.

**I knew a future story and treated it like I knew you. I didn't. I'm sorry. You don't owe me another conversation.**

I sent that one.

No answer.

Day five I paid Harrow the second-to-last settlement installment.

He counted every coin.

"On time," he said.

"That was the agreement."

"You sound offended."

"Low bar."

"You put it underground and walked under it."

Fair.

He gave me a receipt.

No conversation about Faultglass.

No moral closure.

I left.

Day seven my walking socket started rubbing at rear edge.

Bera looked at it.

Then at me.

"More miles."

"Yes."

"More standing too."

"Yes."

"Different weight?"

"Maybe three pounds."

"Up or down?"

I thought.

"Down."

She adjusted liner and carved two millimeters from inner wall.

The leg fit better.

Bodies kept calendars whether I wanted them to or not.

Day nine Mara sent a letter from Northbank.

The red blanket remained useful.

Southbank numbers remained criminal.

She had found a bakery that sold pear cakes and described them for three sentences specifically to annoy me.

At bottom:

**You mentioned Renn once and then not again. I assume either nothing happened or everything did. Tell me which when you want.**

I wrote:

**Everything, then nothing. Currently nothing.**

Then told her about Bera insulting my weight and a pigeon stealing half Noll's bread.

Improvement.

Day eleven Daro walked past me in the Guild hall.

He nodded.

I nodded.

That was it.

It hurt less than last month.

More than I wanted.

Day twelve Venn Aric sent a prepared-site inquiry through Sella.

Not Hearthline retainer.

One job.

My name requested.

Daro's name was on approval line.

I stared at it.

Sella said, "Take it or don't."

"He approved this."

"Apparently."

"Why?"

"Ask him."

I didn't.

I took the job.

It went fine.

No special lesson.

Daro did his work.

I did mine.

At end he said, "Your left edge is less wasteful."

"Merel Dane."

He looked at me.

"Of course it was."

Then walked away.

Not forgiven.

Working.

Different.

Day fourteen I received my first reply from Merel.

Four lines.

**Stop making the plane prettier. Make the load path uglier and cheaper. Your peg stance is better. Your questions are too long. Who is Renn?**

I did not answer last one.

Not mine to explain.

Day sixteen Noll repainted Brass Spoon sign without telling me.

Wrong color.

I said so.

He charged me for soup.

Day seventeen I went two hours without thinking about Renn Calder.

Then noticed and ruined it.

Day nineteen Stonewake posted a resonance job.

Different worker took it.

I did not ask Sella whether Renn was still suspended.

This required visible effort.

She noticed.

Said nothing.

Worse.

Day twenty-one Harrow sent a note.

**Final payment due next month. Appraiser from South Gate asked whether your strain notes are reliable. I said technically yes. Do not make me regret this sentence.**

I read that three times.

Harrow had recommended information from me.

Not me.

Information.

Still.

Reputation did not return like money.

No balance updated.

People made tiny decisions when I was not present.

Those decisions accumulated.

Day twenty-three I took an escort south with no magic problem at all.

Three wagons.

Two merchants.

One bored Silver swordswoman named Cedi who beat me at cards four times and accused my field peg of cheating because it made my chair stable.

We saw one ridgeback at distance.

It left.

We let it.

At camp Cedi asked what I had done before losing the leg.

"Same things with worse planning."

She accepted that.

Day twenty-four I came home after dark.

A folded note sat under Lower Rook door.

No seal.

My name written badly.

Inside:

**Clay pit tomorrow. Second bell. No songs. Bring the wall.**

No signature.

Didn't need one.

I read it once.

Then put it on table.

Did not build a plan.

Made it almost all the way to bed.

Then went back and checked whether the field peg straps were dry.

Not a plan.

Maintenance.

Important distinction.
