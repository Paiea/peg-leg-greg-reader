# D128: No Songs

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn was already at the clay pit.

No bucket this time.

He stood on the broken shed foundation with both hands in coat pockets and looked like he regretted every decision that had brought him there.

Fair.

I carried two water skins, chalk, a coil of thin rope, and the field peg I was wearing.

No notebook.

That was deliberate.

Renn noticed anyway.

"You don't have paper."

"No."

"Why?"

"You said no songs."

"Paper isn't a song."

"I am adaptable."

He stared at me.

Then pointed toward the stone blocks Vessa had let us leave stacked against far wall.

"I want rules."

"For the test?"

"For you."

Better.

I put the water down.

"Okay."

Renn looked suspicious of how quickly I agreed.

"One. You don't tell people what I'm supposed to become."

"Agreed."

"Two. If you remember something about me, you tell me before you use it to make a decision about me."

That was harder.

Useful sign.

"Define use."

His face closed.

There.

I stopped myself.

"Sorry. Agreed."

"Three. You don't talk to Dunbarrow."

"I haven't."

"Don't."

"Agreed."

"Four."

He paused.

"If I tell you to stop helping, stop."

The sentence landed somewhere below ribs.

Mara had said versions.

Daro had said versions.

Renn had apparently reached same conclusion independently.

Excellent reputation.

"Agreed."

He waited.

"That's it?"

"You want argument?"

"Expected one."

"Growth."

"Don't make it weird."

"Already failed."

Renn almost smiled.

Almost.

"Your rules," he said.

I had not expected that.

Interesting.

"For you?"

"Yes."

I looked at blocks.

Old Greg built rules easily.

Current Greg was trying to notice why.

"If I say stop because I feel propagation outside target, stop."

"Fine."

"If your hands go numb, stop."

"They don't."

"If."

"Fine."

"If either of us doesn't know where the impulse went, we find out before increasing output."

Renn nodded.

"And?"

There was one I wanted.

Do not go to Dunbarrow without telling me.

Not mine.

Not yet.

"That's it."

Renn looked disappointed.

"Your rules are boring."

"Safety often is."

"Far Lantern say that?"

"No."

"Sounds like something old people say."

"I am being attacked for wisdom."

"You're twenty-two."

"Allegedly."

We started.

Not training.

Testing.

The distinction mattered less to Renn than me.

First hour we repeated ground-return pulse from last session.

Renn found it faster.

Second hour he did something new.

He touched block and said, "Move your wall left."

"Why?"

"I want to miss it."

"Concerning phrasing."

"Move it."

I shifted plane six feet left.

Renn tapped.

Pulse traveled right.

My plane felt almost nothing.

He smiled.

"Again."

Second pulse went left and hit center.

Clean.

"You can choose propagation now."

"Sometimes."

"Since when?"

"While I wasn't talking to you."

There.

Twenty-four days.

He had kept working.

Not with Guild.

Not with me.

"How?"

"Old quarry north of Dunbar road. Nobody uses it."

"Alone?"

Renn looked at me.

Rule four hovered.

I let it go.

"Show me again."

He did.

Third pulse curved.

Not literal curve.

Changed path through two different seams before reaching plane.

I laughed.

Couldn't help it.

Renn looked pleased.

"That good?"

"Very."

"How good?"

Future S-class good.

Song good.

Town-ending good.

Rule two.

I took a breath.

"Better than I was expecting at this point."

Renn's face tightened.

"Because future me?"

"Yes."

"Tell me."

I hated this.

"The songs make Red Bell sound like he can propagate through connected structures at town scale. Walls, streets, foundations. I don't know what's exaggerated."

Renn looked at block.

Not frightened this time.

Focused.

"Town scale."

"Eventually, if it's you."

"You still say if."

"I know a name and a song. Present proves the rest."

He nodded slowly.

My own rule coming back through somebody else's mouth without being spoken.

Strange.

"Can Far Lantern do that?"

"Different magic."

"Can she stop it?"

I thought about river barge.

Two hundred twelve yard plane.

Twenty moving surfaces.

S-class.

"Current you? Easily. Future Red Bell? I don't know."

Renn smiled.

There was something dangerous in it.

Not evil.

Competition with a person he had never met.

"Don't," I said.

His smile widened.

"Rule?"

"No. Advice."

"Then no."

Fair.

We worked until fifth bell.

At end Renn paid my reduced day rate.

I had not offered discount.

He had negotiated one last time and won because he now did half the setup himself.

Reasonable.

As we packed, he said, "I'm not your student."

"I know."

"And you're not my master."

"Strongly know."

"What are you then?"

I opened my mouth.

Support.

Specialist.

Future witness.

Old S-class.

Thief.

Friend seemed premature.

"Greg," I said.

Renn stared.

"That's terrible."

"Working answer."

He picked up rope.

"You owe me a better one."

"Apparently."

We left clay pit before dark.
