# D152: Northbank

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Northbank had too many bridges.

Mara had been correct.

I hated all four I used before noon.

The first was narrow.

The second had stairs.

The third smelled like fish.

The fourth was actually fine, which felt dishonest after the first three.

This was my second Northbank visit.

The first, seventy-something days earlier, had rained for three straight days and taught me that wet stone plus walking leg plus romantic optimism was a bad route-planning combination.

This time I brought the field peg in my pack, a better socket liner, and no expectations about weather.

Growth.

Probably.

Mara found me outside the regional Vey office at third bell.

Not because I was lost.

Because she was late.

Important distinction.

"You're early," she said.

"You're fourteen minutes late."

"Northbank time."

"Criminal city."

She kissed me before I could continue.

Effective governance.

Mara looked different.

Not dramatically.

Better coat. Dark green now. Hair tied back with an actual pin instead of whatever string had been losing arguments for years.

She had a leather folio under one arm with three colored tabs sticking out.

Professional evolution.

"You fixed your hair," I said.

"First thing you notice after five weeks?"

"It survived."

"I hate you."

"Northbank changed you."

She took my hand.

We walked.

Her route from office to lodging crossed one of the bad bridges.

Of course.

Mara slowed automatically at the first stair.

Not enough to become help.

Enough to match me.

She had learned my pace.

I had learned not to resent that every time.

Mostly.

Her room was on second floor.

Twelve stairs.

I counted because stairs remained stairs.

The red blanket from letters covered bed.

Ugly.

Warm-looking.

A stack of ledgers occupied chair.

"Romantic," I said.

"They live there now."

"I came all this way to lose chair to paper."

Mara moved the stack to floor.

"Happy?"

"Deeply."

We spent an hour doing nothing useful.

That is not euphemism.

Mostly.

I lay on bed with the walking leg off because socket needed air after travel.

Mara sat cross-legged near wall reading one of my letters aloud in an offensive voice.

"Maud's gray cat has accepted food but rejected legal ownership. Noll says this is contract law."

"Accurate."

"You wrote me four lines about a cat."

"You asked for stupid things."

"I did. I'm proud."

She put letter down.

"Now tell me about Renn."

There.

I had mentioned enough by letter that question had accumulated.

"Future S-class," I said.

"Maybe."

I looked at her.

"You read carefully."

"I know you."

"Dangerous claim."

"Very."

I told her more.

Not every song line.

Enough.

Dunbarrow.

Red Bell.

Suspension notice.

Clay pit.

The rules Renn wrote for me.

The lunch where I admitted why I had found him.

Twenty-four days of silence.

Glassback boars.

Mara listened.

At end she asked, "Do you like him?"

I stopped.

Not because question was hard.

Because I had not asked it.

"Yes."

Mara waited.

"He's annoying."

"So yes."

"Unfortunately."

"Would you still be doing this if he wasn't going to become Red Bell?"

There.

Different question.

I looked at ceiling.

Northbank ceiling had a water stain shaped like an island.

"At first? No."

"Now?"

"Yes."

Mara nodded.

No praise.

Good.

"Does he know that?"

"Probably not."

"You should tell him."

"Why?"

"Because apparently he has rules."

Cruel.

"Rule two is about future information."

"Greg."

"Fine."

She leaned against my shoulder.

The room got quiet.

Street below remained loud.

Northbank carts used iron wheel rims more than Carrow. Bad city.

"What does Merel want?" Mara asked.

"No idea."

"From you."

"Also no idea."

"You like her."

"Professionally."

"You say that about everyone you like."

"False."

"Kessa. Professionally. Daro, before theft. Professionally. Far Lantern. Professionally. That weird fitter."

"Bera is terrifying."

"Professionally."

I looked at her.

Mara smiled.

"What am I?"

"Administrative."

She hit me with pillow.

Fair.

Later we went to bakery with pear cakes.

They were good.

Mara had understated them to maintain leverage.

We ate outside beside canal.

One of Northbank's bridges reflected in water.

Still too many.

Mara said, "Do you know what you want yet?"

No context needed.

I hated that too.

"Several things."

"That's not what I asked."

"I want you closer."

She looked at me.

"Okay."

"I want Hearthline not to think I'm a thief every time my name comes up."

"Reasonable."

"I want my magic better."

"Of course."

"I want Renn not to become the song."

Mara waited.

"That's what you want other people to do," she said.

I looked at pear cake.

"You sound like Merel."

"She sounds smart."

"Rude."

"What do you want to be?"

There it was.

Who is Greg.

I could answer support.

S-class.

Useful.

Trusted.

Mara's boyfriend if terminology became unavoidable.

None felt complete.

"I don't know."

Mara took another bite.

"Good."

I stared.

"You people have ruined that word."

She laughed.

We stayed in Northbank three more days.

I did not solve myself.

I did learn the fourth bridge had a good dumpling stall underneath.

Partial redemption.
