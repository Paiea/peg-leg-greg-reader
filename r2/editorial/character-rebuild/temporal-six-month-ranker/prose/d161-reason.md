# D161: Reason

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn nearly killed a man over a dog.

That sentence is unfair.

Still true.

The dog belonged to nobody as far as I knew.

Brown. Thin. One torn ear. Lived behind cooper row and accepted food from six different businesses while preserving legal independence.

Maud's gray cat had competition.

I was walking back from Bera's with a new field-peg strap when shouting started ahead.

Not adventurer shouting.

Street shouting.

Different rhythm.

One voice angry.

One frightened.

Then dog yelped.

I moved faster.

Walking leg, not peg.

Bad for sprinting.

Still faster than last year.

Around corner, a cart driver had the dog by hind leg.

Not intentionally at first, I think.

The animal had gone under wagon after dropped meat. Driver tried pulling it out before horse moved. Dog bit him.

Then intention changed.

He had a short hitching stick raised in other hand.

A boy maybe twelve stood nearby yelling, "Stop!"

Renn was between them.

Of course.

I did not know he was there until I saw his shoulders.

Still narrow.

Not done growing maybe.

The driver shouted, "It bit me!"

Renn said, "Put it down."

Quiet.

Bad.

I knew that tone from people who had stopped deciding.

Driver swung stick at dog.

Renn caught his wrist.

The dog twisted free and ran.

Problem over.

Except people remained.

Driver shoved Renn.

Renn barely moved.

"Fuck off," the driver said.

Renn looked at his chest where hand had landed.

Then at him.

I felt mana.

Not much.

That was worse.

"Renn."

He did not look at me.

The driver did.

"Friend of yours?"

Wrong question.

Renn's left hand touched wagon side.

Just fingertips.

I saw wood grain near his hand tighten.

No visible crack yet.

Horse stamped.

"Renn," I said again.

He looked at me this time.

Angry.

Not gone.

Present.

"He was going to beat it."

"Dog ran."

"He hit the kid too."

I looked.

Boy had red mark across forearm.

Driver said, "Kid grabbed me."

Possible.

Not good.

Not town-ending.

Renn's fingers remained on wagon.

I felt resonance enter axle.

Fuck.

I made a plane under wagon bed.

Not because wagon mattered.

Because pulse did.

"Take your hand off."

Renn's eyes narrowed.

"Why?"

"Because horse is connected."

That reached him.

His gaze moved to traces.

Leather.

Wood shaft.

Harness hardware.

Animal.

Renn lifted hand.

The wagon gave one hard knock from inside itself.

Like someone struck frame with hammer.

Horse screamed and pulled.

Driver grabbed reins.

I caught side movement with plane while two people jumped away.

No collapse.

No death.

Axle split anyway.

A line ran from Renn's handprint down wagon side through wheel hub and ended in a clean fracture.

Silence.

Renn looked at it.

So did I.

The boy said, "Whoa."

Wrong audience.

Driver's face went white.

Anger vanished.

Fear replaced it.

There.

Reputation born in one second.

Not earned the way Renn wanted.

"Guild," driver said.

Renn's jaw tightened.

"Go ahead."

"Renn," I said.

"What?"

"Don't."

"Don't what?"

"Turn this into a duel because you're embarrassed."

His face changed.

I had guessed right.

Bad way.

"I'm not embarrassed."

"Then easier."

He hated me.

Fine.

I turned to driver.

"Your axle is broken. Nobody is hurt. Dog is gone. Kid needs someone to look at arm. You want watch?"

"He broke my wagon."

"Yes."

Renn said, "You hit a kid."

Driver spun.

"He grabbed me!"

"Stop," I said.

Both looked at me.

Old command voice.

I let it stay.

"Watch. Now. Everyone tells their version. Nobody touches anyone."

I looked at Renn.

"Including wagons."

He swore.

Good.

Still there.

Watch came.

Statements happened.

Boring.

Important.

Three witnesses said driver struck boy with stick after boy grabbed his sleeve.

Two said accidental.

One said not.

No one saw first contact clearly.

Driver admitted raising stick at dog.

Renn admitted breaking axle.

Not `accidentally`.

Not `it cracked`.

"I put charge through it because I was angry," he said.

Watchman stared.

That sentence probably saved him worse trouble later.

Maybe.

Renn paid damage deposit from glassback money.

All of it.

His face when coins left was worse than when Guild suspended him.

The boy's arm was bruised, not broken.

Dog returned behind cooper shop before sunset and accepted sausage.

I found Renn sitting on river wall after dark.

He knew I was there.

Did not look over.

I sat anyway.

Walking leg straight because knee wanted rest.

We listened to water.

Finally Renn said, "Was that it?"

"What?"

"The song."

I looked at him.

"No."

"Same kind."

There was no good answer.

"Maybe."

He laughed without humor.

"Guy hits a dog and suddenly I'm town murderer."

"That's not what I said."

"You thought it."

Yes.

"For a second."

He picked up a pebble.

Turned it between fingers.

Did not ring it.

"I was right."

"About what?"

"He shouldn't hit the dog. He shouldn't hit the kid."

"Yes."

Renn looked at me now.

"Then why does everybody act like being right means I wasn't allowed to be angry?"

There.

Future reasons.

Songs.

Dunbarrow.

I chose carefully.

"You're allowed to be angry."

"Generous."

"You're not allowed to pretend your anger knows where the force goes."

He looked back at river.

That landed badly.

Maybe correctly.

"You think that's what happens in Dunbarrow."

"I don't know."

"You always say that."

"Because I don't."

"But you're scared."

"Yes."

He waited.

I made myself say rest.

"Not because you were angry today. Because you were angry and your hand was on something connected to a horse and you didn't notice until I said it."

Renn's fingers closed around pebble.

"I noticed after."

"Yes."

"Too late."

"For axle. Not horse."

He nodded once.

We sat.

After a while he tossed pebble into river.

Normal throw.

Small splash.

"I liked it," he said.

"Breaking wagon?"

"For a second."

That was the sentence.

Not anger.

Not justice.

Pleasure.

I understood that too well.

Power solved distance between wanting and happening.

Sometimes faster than judgment could arrive.

"Yeah," I said.

Renn looked surprised.

"Yeah?"

"Yes."

"That's all?"

"You want me to lie?"

He thought.

"No."

"Then yeah."

We stayed until river lamps came on.

No training that night.
