# D171: Six Weeks

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Merel came back with two days' notice and offered me six weeks.

Not apprenticeship.

She corrected that twice.

"Field attachment," she said.

"That sounds like apprenticeship with worse branding."

"Then don't come."

I shut up.

Temporarily.

We sat in the Guild back room because Sella had decided putting an S-class Ranker in main hall destroyed productivity.

She was right.

People found reasons to walk past every thirty seconds.

Merel wore travel gray instead of blue coat.

Orris was not with her.

"Where's Platinum?" I asked.

"Has a name."

"I know."

"Then use it."

"Where's Orris?"

"Home."

I stopped.

For some reason I had assumed Ranker support staff lived permanently inside luggage.

"He has home?"

Merel stared.

"Greg."

"Bad question."

"Very. Wife. Two children. A garden he lies about enjoying."

Another person with off-page life.

Apparently world insisted.

Merel pushed paper across table.

Six-week route.

Carrow to Morn coast by inland road.

Three municipal ward inspections.

One old battlefield stabilization.

Two Ranker-support contracts with local teams.

Return uncertain because weather.

My name was not on paper.

"Where do I fit?"

"You don't. Yet."

"Excellent offer."

"I have room for one rotating field support observer. Paid. Silver scope respected. You do not enter unrestricted Ranker hazard because you are curious."

"Cruel."

"You do not turn every stop into proving you deserve the next one."

Worse.

"Why me?"

Merel leaned back.

I recognized wait.

She wanted me to answer.

"Because my planes are weird."

"Many people are weird."

"I see load well."

"Lots of engineers do."

"Because I think like support."

Merel's expression changed slightly.

Closer.

"What does that mean?"

There.

I had used phrase casually for decades in old life.

Support.

I held barriers.

Redirected force.

Kept people alive.

Managed fields.

But that was skill list.

Not answer.

"I make other people more effective."

Merel waited.

"That's optimization," she said.

"Yes."

"A quartermaster does that. A trainer does that. Good boots do that."

Rude to boots.

"I change what a team can attempt."

There.

Merel's eyes sharpened.

"Better."

I felt stupidly pleased.

Hated it.

"Support changes the feasible set," she said.

Old phrasing.

Not magic terminology.

Operational.

"A swordsman gets stronger and kills larger things. A support gets stronger and suddenly five people can go somewhere none of them could go yesterday. Different responsibility."

Renn entered my head.

Of course.

Merel saw it.

"The Bronze."

"Renn."

"Renn."

"Yes."

"How is he?"

I told her about cart.

Dog.

Boy.

Axle.

The fact Renn admitted anger.

The fact he liked it for a second.

Merel listened.

No visible alarm.

At end she asked, "And what did you do?"

"Damped propagation. Called watch. Sat with him."

"After?"

"Nothing."

"Nothing?"

"No training that night."

Merel nodded once.

That tiny approval felt worse than praise.

"You told me you know a future title," she said.

I looked toward closed door.

"Red Bell."

Merel's face did not react to title.

Of course not. It did not exist yet.

"Class?"

"S."

That changed room.

Not visibly much.

Enough.

Merel sat straighter.

"You are certain?"

"As certain as memory gets. Public history. Songs. Military lectures. I never knew him."

"What could he do?"

I described town-scale structural propagation as songs/history claimed.

Connected stone.

Foundations.

Walls.

Streets.

Potentially ward anchors if material connection existed.

Merel listened without interruption.

When I finished she said, "If even half is true, S fits."

There.

Validation I had not wanted as much as expected.

"Can you stop it?" I asked.

Merel laughed.

Actually laughed.

"Future hypothetical town-scale resonance from a man I've never met? Excellent question. No useful answer."

"Current Renn."

"Probably."

"Probably?"

"You hate that word."

"Yes."

"Then keep it."

I did.

Merel tapped route paper.

"Six weeks starts in twelve days."

My mind moved.

Northbank.

Work already booked.

Bera check.

Noll.

Renn.

Daro/Hearthline.

Money.

Mara.

I stopped halfway.

Not because optimization bad.

Because decision first.

"I want to go," I said.

Merel nodded.

"Then see what it costs."

There.

Not make it happen.

See.

"Why me?" I asked again.

Merel sighed.

"You are annoying."

"Documented."

"You notice systems. You do not have enough power to solve them by force, which is currently useful. Your body makes you plan exits. Your memory, whatever it is, makes you ask questions people your age don't ask. And you are still insecure enough to listen occasionally."

"Occasionally."

"Rare gift."

I looked at route paper.

"Master?"

Merel stared.

I regretted word instantly.

Not because I didn't want it.

Because I did.

Too much.

"No," she said.

Clean.

I nodded.

Hurt anyway.

Then Merel added, "Come back from six weeks without trying to become me. Then ask again."

My head came up.

She smiled.

Bastard.

"That's not a no."

"It was exactly a no. Future questions may receive different answers."

Future.

Rude word.

I folded route paper.

At door Merel said, "Greg."

I turned.

"Do not bring Renn because you think he is useful to me."

There.

She had found the chess piece without me saying it.

"I wasn't."

"Good."

"Would he be?"

Merel closed her eyes.

"Get out."

I did.

Six weeks.

Twelve days to decide what it cost.

I was moving faster than I had before Faultglass.

No theft required.

That thought arrived.

I let it stay.

Did not turn it into a rule.
