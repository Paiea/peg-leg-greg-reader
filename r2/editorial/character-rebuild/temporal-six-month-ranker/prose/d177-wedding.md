# D177: Wedding

Status: **SIX-MONTH TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn went home for a wedding.

Of course he did.

History had not mentioned the wedding.

History was becoming personally offensive.

He showed me the letter at Brass Spoon.

Not because I asked.

Because he dropped it beside my bowl and said, "Rule two."

I wiped soup from fingers before touching paper.

His sister's handwriting leaned hard right.

**Tali marries Enver on second Restday next month. Mam says if you miss it she will come to Carrow and kill you herself. Uncle Dav has room if you don't want the old house. Bring decent shirt. Do not bring quarry boots.**

Then three lines about weather.

A complaint about roof leak.

One sentence telling him Dunbarrow's east cut had reopened under a new stone buyer.

Nothing ominous.

Nothing about magistrates.

No garrison.

No cult.

No prophecy.

Wedding.

I put letter down.

Renn watched me.

"Well?"

I hated that question.

"Your mother sounds dangerous."

"She is."

"Tali your sister?"

"Yes."

"Enver?"

"Idiot. Fine otherwise."

"Strong recommendation."

Renn waited.

I knew what he wanted.

Not permission.

Future reaction.

"When?" I asked.

"Leave in six days. Wedding four after that."

D183.

My Far Lantern route began D183.

Of course.

I laughed once.

Renn's eyes narrowed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Greg."

"Merel leaves same day."

"So?"

There.

Not his problem.

Correct.

"So nothing."

Renn leaned back.

"You were going to come."

Not question.

"Thought about it."

"Because song."

"Yes."

"Wedding isn't in song."

"No."

"Maybe because nothing happens."

"Maybe."

He smiled without humor.

"You hate maybe too."

"Deeply."

Noll put bread on table.

Renn took one piece.

"I'm going alone."

There.

My chest tightened before reason arrived.

"Okay."

Renn stopped chewing.

Apparently he had expected argument.

So had I.

"Okay?"

"It's your sister's wedding."

"And Dunbarrow."

"Also your home."

"And the song."

"Also a song."

He stared.

I could feel my own thoughts trying to become roads.

Go with Renn.

Delay Merel.

Ask Merel to change departure.

Send Kessa.

Send nobody.

Contact Dunbarrow Guild under unrelated pretext.

Find future incident date.

Did I remember season?

No.

Maybe years from now.

Probably years.

Probably.

I put both hands flat on table.

"I want to say don't go."

Renn's face hardened.

"But?"

"But my reason is that I know a story about a future version of you destroying a place you currently love. That's not evidence your sister's wedding is dangerous."

"Good."

The word came from him now.

Unacceptable.

"I also want to go with you."

"No."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Increasingly."

He looked at bread.

"If you're there, every time I get angry you'll look for cracks."

That hurt because true.

"Probably."

"And everybody I know will think you're my weird older boyfriend."

I stared.

Noll made a choking sound behind counter.

"Absolutely not."

Renn smiled.

"Then don't come."

"Those are unrelated arguments."

"Both strong."

"One is criminal."

"Which?"

I hated him.

Useful feeling.

We ate.

After a while I asked, "Do you want me to tell you everything I remember about Dunbarrow before you go?"

Renn thought longer than expected.

"No."

That surprised me.

"Why?"

"Because you don't know when."

"Correct."

"You don't know why."

"Correct."

"You don't know if song's even right."

"Correct."

"Then I'll spend wedding looking at every angry person like maybe that's the one."

There.

Better reasoning than mine.

"If something feels wrong, I'll write."

"Good."

"Don't say good."

"Why?"

"You're making it creepy."

Fair.

Renn folded letter and put it away.

Then said, "You going with Far Lantern?"

"Yes."

First time I said it without internal negotiation.

"Six weeks?"

"Yes."

"That's insane."

"Why?"

"She's S-class."

I looked at him.

"So are you, apparently."

"Future me."

"Apparently."

Renn shook his head.

"You should go."

"I am."

"Good."

I pointed spoon.

"Stop."

He laughed.

Outside, spring had become something closer to summer while I wasn't paying attention.

Sun stayed later.

Road dust replaced mud.

Six months used to sound short.

It no longer did.
