# D194: Cheaper

Status: **SIX-WEEK TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Merel told me to make Orris cheaper.

I looked at Orris.

Orris looked at himself.

"Financially impossible," he said.

"Mana," Merel said.

"Also impossible. Premium product."

We were three days south of Morn coast on a road built into chalk hills.

No danger.

No contract emergency.

Merel had watched Orris run a barrier drill with local Golds and apparently decided I looked too comfortable.

"Ten percent," she said.

I stared.

"You want me to reduce a Platinum's mana use ten percent."

"Without reducing outcome."

"Using Silver magic."

"Preferably."

Orris smiled.

"I like her."

"You travel with her."

"Still."

I asked, "Consent?"

Merel pointed at Orris.

Good.

I looked at him.

"Do you want this?"

Orris thought longer than joke required.

"Yes. But no changing my stance without telling me first."

"Old injury?"

His face changed.

Merel's eyebrows rose.

Orris said, "Why?"

"You guard left rear before pressure reaches it."

He looked at Merel.

"Did you tell him?"

"No."

Back to me.

"Spear through left hip. Twelve years ago."

There.

Not weakness.

History.

"Still hurts?"

"No."

"Still expect it to?"

Orris stopped smiling.

"Annoying Silver."

"Documented."

We spent two days on it.

Not continuously.

Merel had actual work.

Orris had two route inspections.

I had a prepared-site retaining wall survey with local crew because field attachment still paid me to be useful occasionally.

Between those, I watched Orris move.

Platinum was unpleasant up close.

Gold people were fast enough that mistakes became expensive.

Orris operated like mistakes had to apply in writing.

His impact shell wrapped body from front right through left rear.

Not a sphere.

Offset oval.

He maintained extra thickness behind left hip almost constantly.

The old wound was gone.

The geometry remained.

I could not improve his shell.

I could barely understand half of it.

So I stopped looking at shell.

Looked at feet.

Then shoulders.

Then what happened half a breath before he committed forward.

Every hard entry contained same tiny hesitation.

Left heel planted.

Shell thickened behind hip.

Then strike.

Safety first.

Reasonable.

Expensive over hundreds of repetitions.

"I can give you something to push against," I said.

Orris frowned.

"Ground exists."

"Not ground. Decision."

"That sounds fake."

"Probably."

I placed one small feathered plane behind his left heel.

Not load-bearing enough to move him.

Just enough that when he committed, the heel met certainty immediately.

Orris ran entry.

Stopped.

"Again."

Second time.

Third.

On fourth he dropped extra shell thickness by accident.

I saw it.

He saw me see it.

"Don't talk."

"Wasn't."

"Face talked."

Merel sat on fence eating pear.

Excellent S-class labor.

We changed nothing else that day.

Next morning Orris asked for plane again.

Consent renewed.

Good.

I moved plane three inches.

Bad.

He nearly stepped through it because his body had already started expecting first position.

He swore.

I canceled immediately.

"No adaptive placement without call," he said.

"Agreed."

Merel threw pear core at ditch.

"Write that down."

I did.

Not because order.

Because she was right.

By end of second day Orris could run same barrier entry with the heel plane and reduce rear shell before commitment.

Merel measured draw with a Guild crystal.

Eight percent.

I hated eight.

Orris looked delighted.

"Cheaper."

"Eight."

"Eight is money."

"She said ten."

Merel said, "I said ten because you would chase ten."

I stared at her.

"Manipulative."

"Teaching."

"Not my master."

"Correct."

Orris ran another entry.

Harder this time.

The heel plane took load.

My mana draw barely moved.

His shell stayed thin until impact.

Then expanded exactly where needed.

He hit practice frame hard enough to move stakes.

Local Golds went quiet.

I felt something in chest I recognized too quickly.

Not envy.

Pleasure.

Orris was already stronger than me by an insulting amount.

And I had made him slightly better.

That felt better than adding eight percent to myself.

Dangerous information.

Merel watched me instead of Orris.

Of course.

"Say it," she said.

"No."

"Coward."

"Silver coward."

Orris laughed.

Merel waited.

I sighed.

"I like this."

"Making him cheaper?"

"Making him more capable without becoming him."

Orris said, "Romantic."

"You're ruining it."

"Good."

I pointed at him.

"Also ruined."

Merel got off fence.

"People are not projects, Greg."

There it was.

I bristled before meaning to.

"He asked."

Orris said, "I did."

Merel nodded.

"Then keep asking."

Simple.

Not don't optimize.

Not support is possession.

Consent.

Again later.

I could work with that.

Probably too well.

That night Orris bought first round at roadside inn because eight percent apparently qualified.

I had one beer.

Merel had two.

Orris had enough that his garden became subject.

He did, in fact, hate gardening.

His wife liked roses.

He liked wife.

Therefore roses.

Support logic everywhere.

At some point I realized we had spent twenty minutes discussing aphids.

No one learned magic.

Excellent field attachment.
