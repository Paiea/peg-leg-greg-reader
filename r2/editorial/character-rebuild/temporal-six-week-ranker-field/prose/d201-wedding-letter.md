# D201: Wedding Letter

Status: **SIX-WEEK TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Renn's first letter from Dunbarrow contained no catastrophe.

I read it twice to make sure.

No collapsed district.

No bells used as weapons.

No mysterious official named in a future song.

His sister got married.

That was apparently the event.

Tali cried before ceremony.

Her husband cried during.

Renn's mother cried at both and then denied all evidence.

One cousin got drunk before dinner and tried to dance on a bench that had not agreed to load.

Bench won.

Renn helped repair it next morning.

Normal family history.

I sat outside a Morn Coast Guild house with letter in one hand and bad coffee in other.

Sea wind kept trying to fold paper.

I used cup as weight.

Renn continued:

**Mam says tell the weird old Silver I got here alive. I did not tell her why you're old. You're welcome.**

Good.

Then:

**Wedding was good. Tali married Oran. He's a cooper. He is bad at cards and worse at dancing. I approve.**

Then, lower:

**I'm staying two extra weeks. Dunbarrow quarry office offered eight copper a day to check wet seams in north cut because old Bess thinks my trick is useful. This is not about your song. It is about eight copper. Do not send advice.**

I stared at eight.

Low.

Not criminally low.

Still low.

My hand reached for pencil.

Stopped.

Renn had specifically said no advice.

I hated written consent.

Very clear.

I wrote on back of route invoice instead.

**Eight is low.**

Looked at it.

Not sending.

Better.

A second letter waited under his.

Mara.

Northbank paper was heavier than Carrow stock and smelled faintly of warehouse dust.

Not romantic.

Accurate.

Her first line:

**We lost the south reconciliation vote. I am furious.**

I sat straighter.

The rest explained.

Three warehouse firms had agreed on revised river fees.

Fourth withdrew after two weeks because its owner wanted a separate exemption.

Vote failed four to three.

Mara had spent twelve days preparing numbers that now belonged to nothing.

Her supervisor told her this was normal.

She wrote:

**If this is normal I dislike normal.**

I understood completely.

My head began solving.

Which firm.

Owner leverage.

Alternative coalition.

Could Vey route around fourth warehouse.

Could Mara reframe fee structure.

Could I use Carrow contacts.

No.

Not no because optimization bad.

No because she had not asked.

I kept reading.

**Do not fix it. I know your face already.**

Terrible woman.

**I am allowed to be angry before becoming efficient again.**

Worse.

Then three paragraphs about a neighbor's laundry line, a Northbank bakery that sold lemon cakes too large for one person, and a clerk named Pera who had begun bringing a small dog to the office because nobody had successfully stopped her.

At bottom:

**I miss you. Also your last letter contained six sentences about Orris's left heel. I hope he appreciates what he has done to our romance.**

I looked at coffee.

No help.

Merel came out Guild door with three stamped packets.

Saw letters.

"Bad?"

"One wedding. One failed vote."

"Which is bad?"

"Vote. Wedding seems structurally sound."

She sat on railing.

Did not ask to read.

Another useful habit.

I opened fresh sheet.

For Renn:

**Congratulations to Tali and Oran. I will not send quarry advice. Your mother sounds reasonable.**

I considered adding **eight is low**.

Did not.

For Mara I wrote:

**That sucks.**

Stared.

Then:

**I am not going to fix it. This is taking effort and should count.**

Then:

**I miss you too. Orris's heel is eight percent cheaper now. You should respect science.**

I almost stopped there.

Instead I added:

**I wish I had been there when the vote failed. Not to help. Just there.**

That sentence felt more dangerous than any technical note I had written all week.

I left it.

Merel watched gull steal bread from somebody across yard.

"You answer your own letters?"

"Usually."

"Good."

I pointed pencil.

"Stop."

She smiled.

We mailed both before noon.

Then went to inspect a municipal ward cellar where nothing tried to kill us.

The ward was fine.

Inspector was disappointed.

I was not.

That evening sea wind got colder.

Summer still existed.

Just apparently not near ocean.
