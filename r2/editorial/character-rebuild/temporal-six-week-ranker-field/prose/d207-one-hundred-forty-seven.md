# D207: One Hundred Forty-Seven

Status: **SIX-WEEK TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

There were one hundred forty-seven active Rankers in the registry.

I knew because Morn Coast Guild had put the list on a wall.

Bad idea.

I lost twenty minutes immediately.

The board was not decorative.

Contracts used it.

Military offices used it.

Cities used it when deciding whether a request needed one famous person, six ordinary experts, or everyone staying home.

Names ran in four narrow columns.

C.

B.

A.

S.

Metal rank sat beside each name in smaller type.

Not hidden.

Not dominant either.

I found Tessa.

**TESSA BRIN — LAST DOOR — C — GOLD**

Merel:

**MEREL DANE — FAR LANTERN — S — PLATINUM**

Orris did not exist on board.

He stood beside me eating an apple.

"Still devastated?" I asked.

"Every morning."

I kept reading.

A line near bottom of C column stopped me.

**MIKO TAN — BLUE THREAD — C — SILVER**

I pointed.

"Silver."

Orris swallowed.

"Good color."

"C-class Ranker."

"Also good letter."

Merel came up behind us carrying stamped route permit.

"Blue Thread?"

"Yes."

I waited.

Merel sighed.

"Grey Fever cordon. Seven years ago."

I knew disease.

Not person.

"What did he do?"

"Carried treatment route through three closed districts after courier network failed. Twenty-two trips. Never broke quarantine line. Built a thread-mark system so people could leave medicine without crossing each other."

"Combat?"

"Mediocre."

Orris said, "Very mediocre."

"You fought him?"

"Sparred."

"And?"

"Silver."

Fair.

Merel tapped board.

"Metal rank did not change because plague happened. His current combat, magic capacity, field judgment, and contract scope remained Silver."

"But title stuck."

"People were already calling the route Blue Thread before registry finished arguing."

I counted columns.

C held most names.

B fewer.

A fewer again.

S was insultingly short.

Six.

Merel among them.

"How do you move letters?"

"Results."

"That's vague."

"Correct."

"Scale?"

"Sometimes."

"Repeatability?"

"Often."

"Trust?"

"Always matters."

"Power?"

Merel looked at Orris.

"Useful because power creates access to large problems. Not sufficient."

Orris held apple core up like evidence.

"I remain tragically unlettered."

I ignored him.

"So C can be one extraordinary result."

"Usually one result strong enough that a title becomes operationally useful, plus enough verification that we are not rewarding a lucky accident."

"B?"

"The name keeps solving things in more than one place."

"A?"

"Campaigns, regions, institutions. Results that change planning before person arrives."

I looked at S.

Six names.

"And S?"

Merel was quiet longer.

"When people plan around possibility that you might exist."

Not stronger.

Not exactly.

I understood anyway.

Kestrel Pass.

A support lattice no one else had reproduced.

Two thousand people moved through a battlefield because Far Lantern was there.

Old Greg's future name belonged on an S list too.

Different list.

Different decade.

Seven people then.

I remembered all seven.

I did not look for my own title on this wall.

It was not here.

Not yet.

That thought felt worse than wanting it.

"How many people chase this?" I asked.

Orris laughed.

Merel said, "Too many."

"Titles?"

"Yes. They do stupid things hoping story sticks."

"Does it work?"

"Sometimes."

That was worst answer.

Merel pointed to C column.

"Three of those people earned titles doing something they should not have survived. Two were disciplined afterward. One lost metal rank for the same event that got him a title."

I blinked.

"Possible?"

"Different systems."

Of course.

A person could do something extraordinary enough to become named and reckless enough to become less trusted for ordinary work.

That was annoyingly human.

I thought of Faultglass.

Could theft produce useful technique and worse reputation?

Already answered.

Great.

Orris tossed apple core into bin.

"You done staring at wall?"

"No."

"We leave in four minutes."

"Why didn't old Greg explain this better?"

Merel's head turned.

I froze.

Orris said, "Old Greg?"

Damn.

Information management remained weak.

"Long story."

Orris looked delighted.

Merel looked tired.

"Road," she said.

I followed.

At door I looked back once.

One hundred forty-seven names.

Most of continent would never enter board.

Some stronger people never would either.

A title was not what you could do.

It was what enough people decided your results meant.

Which made it power.

Just not mana.
