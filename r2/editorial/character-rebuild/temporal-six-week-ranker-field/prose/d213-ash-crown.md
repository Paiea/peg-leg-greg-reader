# D213: Ash Crown

Status: **SIX-WEEK TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I knew Sera Vell was dead.

This made breakfast awkward.

She sat across from me eating eggs.

Alive.

Very alive.

Complaining about salt.

**Ash Crown.**

A-class Ranker.

Platinum.

Old Greg remembered the title from campaign histories, officer lectures, and one terrible song that rhymed crown with down four times.

Sera Vell died at Hollow Ford before I reached Gold the first time.

Not today.

Today she stole Orris's bread.

"You weren't eating it."

"I was considering it."

"Too slow."

Orris looked at me.

"Ranker privilege."

Sera pointed fork.

"No such thing."

"Bread says otherwise."

Merel drank coffee and ignored them.

We were at an old battlefield station east of Salt Mere.

Joint contract.

Merel for structural support and civilian corridor.

Sera for containment if buried siege wards woke badly.

Local team for everything that actually knew local ground.

Me because field attachment had become an elaborate method of discovering how little context I possessed.

Sera noticed me staring.

"You know me?"

Bad question.

"By reputation."

"Good reputation?"

"Complicated."

She grinned.

"Best kind."

Merel looked at me over cup.

She knew exactly why answer was complicated.

I hated having one person in room who understood my lies before I finished them.

Sera was younger than memory made her.

Thirty, maybe thirty-two.

Black hair cut short on one side because old burn scar ran behind ear.

Three rings in left hand.

No crown.

Titles lacked quality control.

Her A plate sat under Platinum badge.

Not S.

I could feel more raw pressure from her than Merel.

That surprised me.

Not mana quantity exactly.

Heat wanted to happen around Sera.

She kept it from happening.

Merel's magic felt quieter.

Less like force stored in body.

More like room had already been measured.

Different people.

Different results.

Same metal.

Different letter.

Useful correction.

We reached battlefield by noon.

Nothing dramatic happened for three hours.

Excellent.

Local engineers mapped old trench lines.

Merel checked support pylons under refugee road built years after war.

Sera sat on broken wall telling story about being banned from two inns in one month.

I believed her.

Then somebody found a buried ward spindle.

Not by touching it.

Thank every god with administrative authority.

A mason's probe rang wrong against stone.

Local lead stopped work.

Merel went still.

Sera stopped smiling.

There.

Real people now.

The old siege ward had been designed to turn heat inward through fortification stone.

Bad system to find under civilian road.

Local warder read surface.

Merel read load paths.

Sera read what would happen if containment failed.

I stood behind marked line and did not invent relevance.

That took effort.

They spent twenty minutes deciding whether to disturb anything.

Answer became no.

Then spindle woke anyway.

Not because plot needed action.

Because old copper ground line finally cracked under survey weight three yards away.

Heat moved through wall.

Fast.

Stone along trench edge went red.

People shouted once.

Then stopped because professionals had jobs.

Merel's planes appeared first.

Not blocking heat.

Redirecting falling load away from road crews as mortar popped.

Sera stepped forward.

No chant.

No windup.

She raised two fingers.

A line of white fire cut across trench.

Not flame.

Too clean.

Heat pulled out of spreading wall into a single corridor of stone already marked empty.

Thirty yards of parapet glowed.

Then orange.

Then white at center.

The air punched my face from twenty paces.

Orris threw shell around us.

My own plane came up under his left heel without being asked.

He used it.

No discussion.

That felt very good.

Dangerous later.

Now useful.

Sera moved heat sideways.

Actually moved it.

The buried ward wanted to cook everything connected.

She gave it one path and then made that path hotter than rest could follow.

Stone split with sound like tree breaking.

Merel caught falling section in six pale surfaces and laid it down outside civilian lane.

Local warder severed spindle.

Whole event lasted maybe ninety seconds.

Then nothing.

No explosion.

No cheering.

A mason vomited into ditch.

Sera sat on stone immediately.

Her hands shook.

There.

Cost.

Merel handed her water.

Sera drank and said, "Still hate breakfast eggs."

Nobody laughed for second.

Then Orris did.

Pressure released.

I looked at trench.

One person could have described scene as Ash Crown saving everybody.

Wrong.

Without local mason, spindle would not have been found.

Without warder, it would have reconnected.

Without Merel, road crew might have died under wall.

Without Orris, heat wash could have burned us.

Sera had done the part nobody else there could do.

That was enough.

On walk back she fell beside me.

"You stare like historian."

My stomach tightened.

"Bad habit."

"You know Hollow Ford?"

I almost stopped walking.

She meant campaign.

Current Hollow Ford.

Not her death.

Of course.

"Some."

"I'm there next month."

There.

Future moved close enough to touch.

Old memory said she died at Hollow Ford.

Not when.

Not which campaign.

Not why.

Could be years.

Could be next month.

Songs and histories had compressed timeline until all deaths looked adjacent.

Faultglass lesson.

Renn lesson.

I did not tell her she was dead.

Not yet.

Sera looked sideways.

"You look sick."

"Socket."

Lie.

Technically socket existed.

She accepted it because people accepted ordinary explanations.

Merel, ten paces ahead, did not look back.

Which meant she had heard.

Wonderful.

That night I wrote **SERA VELL / HOLLOW FORD** in notebook.

Then under it:

**KNOWN: dies there sometime before old Greg reaches Gold.**

Then:

**UNKNOWN: date / cause / whether current deployment related.**

My hand wanted campaign.

Warn her.

Research.

Stop departure.

Save historical piece.

Chess board again.

I stared at words.

Sera was not a piece.

Also she might die.

Both true.

I closed notebook.

Did not sleep well.
