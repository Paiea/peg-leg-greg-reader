# D225: Six Weeks

Status: **SIX-WEEK TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Six weeks ended with paperwork.

Of course.

Morn Coast Guild gave Merel a private room because S-class Ranker paperwork apparently generated witnesses.

Orris got chair.

I got chair too.

Progress.

Merel read my field attachment evaluation aloud because she enjoyed cruelty.

"Load perception: strong."

"Correct."

"Ward interaction: narrow but reliable within declared scope."

"Also correct."

"Mobility judgment: improved."

I disliked improved.

"Why not strong?"

"Because you tried wet stairs in field peg on day twenty-eight."

Orris laughed.

"One stair."

"Still wet."

"Experimental science."

"Bad science."

She continued.

"Support integration: unusually strong."

I stopped joking.

Merel glanced up.

Then read.

"Subject improves higher-capability teammates by identifying redundant load, timing, and role overlap rather than attempting to match their output."

Orris tapped left heel on floor.

Eight percent.

Still irritating.

"Tendency to over-own outcomes," Merel read.

There.

"Tendency to treat information advantage as obligation to intervene."

Sera.

Renn.

Faultglass.

"Response to explicit boundaries: good. Response to ambiguous boundaries: annoying."

Orris said, "Official terminology?"

"I wrote report."

"Then yes."

"Current Silver rank appropriate. No Gold recommendation at this time."

That hit less than expected.

Interesting.

Six months ago I would have dissected why.

Three years ago I would have hated it.

Current me asked, "Why?"

Merel answered immediately.

"Because Gold is operating scope, not compliment. Your field mobility, sustained output, unknown-terrain escape margin, and independent hazard tolerance are not Gold yet."

"Fair."

Orris looked almost disappointed I had not argued.

"That's all?"

"Metal isn't identity," I said.

Merel's mouth moved slightly.

Not smile.

Close.

She pushed report across table.

No title recommendation.

Obviously.

Nobody named six weeks.

Good.

I signed.

Orris left to find lunch.

Merel stayed.

I knew conversation was not done because she had not dismissed me rudely.

"You asked about master," she said.

There.

My stomach did stupid thing.

"You said no."

"I did."

"Still no?"

Merel leaned back.

"Do you want a master?"

I almost said yes.

Stopped.

Question different.

Did I want Merel?

Her techniques?

Her access?

Her approval?

Her way of looking?

Did I want to become somebody who followed an S-class support Ranker until enough of her habits became mine?

Old Greg had learned from dozens of people without calling most of them master.

Current Greg had a dangerous appetite for systems that worked.

"I want you to teach me," I said.

Merel nodded once.

"Different answer."

"Yes."

"I already have."

"Annoying answer."

"Correct."

I waited.

She pulled another paper from folder.

Three dates.

Autumn.

Midwinter.

Spring.

Short attachments.

Two weeks each.

Different routes.

Not guaranteed because Ranker schedules apparently hated calendars.

"Come when you can," Merel said. "Don't come when you shouldn't."

"That's a year."

She looked at me.

I saw trap too late.

"You said time was cheap."

Bastard.

I laughed.

Actually laughed.

"Fair."

"If after those you still want the word master, ask again."

"And then?"

"I may still say no."

"Excellent institution."

"Very."

I folded dates.

Not apprenticeship.

Not nothing.

A road that did not require stealing six months from future.

Mail waited at front desk.

Three pieces.

Sella first.

**Hearthline prepared-site contract remains five days after expected return. Daro confirms. Do not be late because you met famous people.**

Good woman.

Mara second.

**Northbank survived without you. Barely. Pera's dog now has an office drawer. South vote is being rebuilt for next month. Come when your existing work is done. I mean that.**

Then lower:

**I miss you. Lemon cake still too large. This is not a complaint.**

I smiled.

Renn last.

Dunbarrow.

Shorter.

**Still here. Survey extended another ten days. Foundry row has old foundation voids and Bess says I'm useful. Tali says marriage remains acceptable. Nobody destroyed town. Stop checking first line for that.**

I looked around empty Guild hall.

He knew me too well.

Then:

**I told Mam about you. Not old-life part. She thinks you're either a bad influence or a cult. I said both possible.**

Good.

No request to come.

So I would not.

Sera had no letter.

Hollow Ford remained unresolved.

That sat differently.

Information given.

Decision hers.

Outcome not mine.

I hated it.

Less than before.

We left Morn Coast next morning.

Not with Merel.

Her route turned south again.

Orris went home to wife, children, and hostile roses.

I took ordinary Guild coach east.

Three future attachment dates in coat.

Hearthline job in five days.

Northbank after if roads worked.

Dunbarrow not mine unless Renn asked.

On second day homeward, coach stopped for axle grease.

I opened notebook.

Page still had:

**WHO IS GREG?**

**WHAT DO I WANT?**

Six weeks had not solved first.

Good.

Second felt less impossible.

I wrote one line.

**I want to be worth trusting.**

Stared.

Could decompose.

Reputation.

Consent.

Reliability.

Capability.

Restraint.

Results.

Did not.

Below it I almost wrote **TITLE?**

Stopped.

Old Greg had one.

A title people remembered after he died.

Or after he should have died.

Whatever.

Merel had Far Lantern.

Tessa had Last Door.

Sera had Ash Crown.

Renn might someday become Red Bell.

Names that compressed people until history could carry them.

I did not want compression yet.

Maybe ever.

Coach driver shouted ready.

I closed notebook.

Six weeks gone.

I had not become Gold.

Had not become Merel's student.

Had not saved Sera.

Had not saved Renn.

Mara still lived somewhere inconvenient.

Orris spent less mana on left-rear shell.

Tessa knew where flood cats hid.

Renn made ten copper a day in his hometown.

Sera knew a bad piece of her future and had chosen her own road anyway.

And I had three dates in my coat from one of the strongest support mages alive.

Apparently moving faster did not always look like arriving sooner.

Coach moved.

I went home.
