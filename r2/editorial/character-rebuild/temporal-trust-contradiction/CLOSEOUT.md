## Core result

Greg's value statement entering the run was:

> I want to be worth trusting.

The run introduces **Vessa Krail**, a civilian contract expediter who is materially useful, socially enjoyable, and knowingly unreliable.

She does not function as a hidden traitor or morality lesson.

Her private incentive pattern remains:

1. protect access;
2. protect income;
3. preserve optionality;
4. help when incentives align;
5. omit, reframe, or lie when truth would cost leverage;
6. keep some promises because reputation matters and some because she personally chose to keep them.

The contradiction survives the block:

Greg wants to be worth trusting while knowingly continuing to build around someone he does not trust.

He is not fooled about Vessa.

That is the point.

## Vessa Krail

### Role

Civilian contract expediter / broker.

No Guild operating rank and no Ranker title.

Her value is access, permits, procurement, route knowledge, client history, scheduling, vendor leverage, and knowing which office/person is actually blocking a job.

### Early calibration

At D230 she gets Hearthline into a municipal bathhouse job before second bell by selectively framing urgency to the city and pre-ordering duplicate brace clamps because she distrusts a supplier dispatcher.

Daro's standing practical rules:

- never trust Krail's time estimate without margin;
- if she says someone agreed, ask whether they literally said yes;
- read every invoice line.

Hearthline still uses her because she delivers.

### Supplier commission

At D239 Greg discovers Vessa receives supplier referral commissions.

Important nuance:

- Hearthline knows she may receive vendor money;
- Greg dislikes that exact commissions are not disclosed job by job;
- the selected supplier is still the cheapest qualified next-day option;
- Vessa admits that a sufficiently small price difference would be outweighed by a commission, while a large difference would not.

This establishes that her incentives are real rather than theatrical.

### Personal contract

At D270 Greg hires Vessa personally because his work/access calendar has become too complex to coordinate alone.

The contract explicitly protects:

- Greg's approval over commitments;
- disclosure of referral payments directly affecting his work;
- current Guild scope;
- Mara's office from relationship-based leverage;
- Renn Calder from proactive investigation/contact;
- future knowledge from being represented as current fact.

Vessa states plainly that she takes the job for money, access to Greg's unusual network, and because she likes working with him.

### Boundary kept when unseen

At D281 Vessa receives Renn's letter accidentally at the table, recognizes the name, turns it facedown, and does not ask about Dunbarrow.

Nobody would have known if she had broken the spirit of the boundary.

She keeps it anyway.

This prevents `untrustworthy` from collapsing into `incapable of loyalty or restraint`.

### Material breach

At D292 Vessa arranges a high-paying prepared-site job for Greg while knowingly withholding that the client has a history of paying contractors late.

Greg had explicitly asked why the rate was high.

Vessa gives a true answer, short notice, while withholding payment-history risk because she believes Greg would decline if told.

Her motive is mixed:

- she wants the commission;
- she genuinely believes the increased rate prices the risk;
- her own commission is also delayed until client pays;
- she decides Greg will be glad afterward.

Greg identifies the actual violation:

> You sold my risk tolerance without asking.

He fires her.

Vessa does not fight termination.

She purchases Greg's receivable at full face value the next morning, taking the payment risk herself and forfeiting commission.

This does not erase the breach.

### Relationship after firing

Greg calls Vessa the same afternoon for a North Gate permit problem.

He does not formally rehire her.

Over the following days he uses her per-task for:

- permit resolution;
- route planning;
- a conflict-of-interest warning;
- autumn lodging.

She charges per task.

No standing authority returns.

By D301 she is socially inside Brass Spoon card nights despite remaining formally fired.

Greg likes her.

That becomes part of the risk.

### High-level calibration

At D315 Merel Dane reveals she has used Vessa many times.

Merel and Orris both answer `no` when Greg asks whether they trust her.

Merel's operating rules include:

- do not give Krail secrets whose sale is worth more than preserving the relationship;
- do not let her define the principal's choices;
- verify information when truth matters more than speed;
- pay promptly;
- assume favors create memory even when they create no formal debt.

Merel once used Vessa during Red Marsh evacuation because Krail found fifty-eight of sixty urgently needed carts in one afternoon.

Merel explicitly refuses to turn this into moral permission:

> Useful people can be unsafe. Unsafe people can be worth using. None of that transfers responsibility away from the person choosing what to hand them.

Greg's unresolved problem therefore becomes **risk allocation**, not binary trust classification.
