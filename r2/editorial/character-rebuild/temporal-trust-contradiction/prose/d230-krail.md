# D230: Krail

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Daro had hired a liar.

I knew this twelve minutes after meeting her.

Possibly nine.

The prepared-site job was an old municipal bathhouse two streets south of East Market.

Nothing glamorous.

Good roof.

Bad lower wall.

Three ward anchors still live enough to complicate demolition.

Hearthline had the survey contract and a limited stabilization allowance.

I arrived with field peg, tool bag, and the pleasant knowledge that nobody expected me to prove anything to Far Lantern before breakfast.

Daro stood outside the bathhouse arguing with a city inspector.

Beside him was a woman I did not know.

Late twenties. Maybe thirty.

Brown coat cut too well for mud. Dark hair pinned with a brass needle. No Guild badge.

She was holding two permits.

The inspector pointed at the lower one.

"This says survey access."

"Correct," the woman said.

"They're carrying brace timber."

"Also correct."

"Survey access does not authorize structural alteration."

"It authorizes temporary safety measures necessary to conduct the survey."

The inspector looked at Daro.

Daro looked tired enough to confess to crimes he had not committed.

"Temporary brace," he said.

"No load transfer?"

"Not without second authorization."

The woman smiled.

"Which I have requested."

"Requested is not approved."

"No."

The inspector looked suspicious.

Correct instinct.

I stopped beside Nesha.

"Who?"

"Vessa Krail."

"Should I know that?"

"Only if you enjoy permits."

"I don't."

"Then no."

Vessa handed the inspector a folded note.

He read it.

Read it again.

His face changed from suspicious to irritated.

"Merek signed this?"

"This morning."

"He said he would not review until Thirdday."

"He changed his mind."

The inspector looked toward City Hall as if considering whether distance prevented murder.

"Fine. Survey access. Temporary safety bracing only. If load moves, you stop."

Daro said, "Obviously."

The inspector left.

Vessa watched him go.

Then checked the street clock.

"Eleven minutes."

Daro said, "You promised five."

"I promised you would be inside before second bell."

"You said five minutes."

"That was encouragement."

There.

Daro saw my face.

"Greg. Vessa Krail. Contract expediter."

Vessa looked down at my left leg.

Not rudely.

Professionally enough to be worse.

Then at my tool bag.

"You're Greg."

"Apparently."

"Faultglass Greg?"

I closed my eyes.

Daro said, "Don't."

Vessa held up one hand.

"I wasn't judging."

"That makes one of us," Daro said.

I looked at her.

"Returned Faultglass Greg."

"Longer brand."

"More accurate."

"Accuracy is expensive."

She said it casually.

That got my attention.

Inside, the bathhouse smelled like mineral water, wet stone, and old soap embedded deeply enough to survive municipal abandonment.

Daro gave roles.

Venn on upper structure.

Nesha outside and lower access.

Daro on wards.

Me on load interaction and brace limits.

Vessa did not have a role.

She walked in anyway.

I pointed.

"Client side?"

"Coordination."

"Meaning?"

"When you discover you need something nobody ordered, I make someone else suffer."

Useful category.

Annoying.

We mapped the lower wall.

The problem was exactly as advertised, which felt suspicious after Merel.

Cracked masonry.

One old repair.

Three anchors.

A timber brace installed fifteen years ago and never removed because governments respected temporary solutions more after paperwork aged.

I tested the floor.

Daro tested ward bleed.

Venn checked roof response.

No surprises.

For forty minutes.

Then Nesha called from outside.

"Daro."

He went.

I heard conversation through broken window.

Truck axle.

No.

Wagon.

Our replacement brace clamps had not arrived.

Daro returned looking murderous.

"Supplier sent the load to North Gate."

Vessa said, "No."

Daro looked at her.

"They did."

"They sent one load to North Gate."

"We only ordered one."

Vessa took a folded card from her coat.

"I ordered a second yesterday."

Silence.

Daro's face changed.

"With whose money?"

"Nobody's yet."

"Vessa."

"You are welcome."

"Where is it?"

"Five blocks."

"Why did you order two?"

"Because the first supplier's dispatcher lies when overloaded."

I laughed once.

Could not help it.

Vessa looked at me.

"Problem?"

"Interesting standard."

"I know my industry."

The second clamps arrived twelve minutes later.

Not five.

Daro glared at her.

Vessa said, "Traffic."

We installed temporary bracing without transferring load.

Inspector returned.

Approved continuation.

Nothing collapsed.

No hidden chamber.

No monster under municipal bathing infrastructure.

Good day.

At lunch, Hearthline took one corner table at Brass Spoon.

Vessa came too.

Noll looked at her.

"Pay first."

She stared.

"I always pay."

"Yes."

"Then why first?"

"You change orders after eating."

Vessa looked wounded.

"Once."

"Three."

Interesting.

She paid.

Exact amount.

No argument.

Noll handed her bowl.

I sat across from her.

"You told the inspector Merek changed his mind."

"He did."

"Why?"

"Because I told him the building had an active lower-wall question with Hearthline already on site and delaying inspection increased city exposure."

"Did you tell him Hearthline had not entered yet?"

"No."

"Did you imply they had?"

Vessa ate one spoonful.

Considered.

"I allowed urgency to remain emotionally useful."

Nesha made a choking sound.

Daro did not look up from food.

Apparently this was normal.

"That's lying," I said.

"No."

"Omission."

"Sometimes."

"Strategic ambiguity."

"If you're writing a complaint."

"What do you call it?"

She shrugged.

"Getting the permit."

Daro finally looked up.

"Greg, rule one. Never believe a time estimate from Krail."

Vessa pointed spoon at him.

"My estimates are aspirational."

"Rule two," Daro continued. "If she says somebody agreed to something, ask whether they used the word yes."

"Unfairly specific."

"Rule three. Never let her invoice without reading every line."

Vessa frowned.

"That rule applies to everybody."

"Especially you."

I looked at her.

She did not appear embarrassed.

That was almost refreshing.

"Why hire her?" I asked.

Daro answered immediately.

"Because we were inside before second bell."

Vessa smiled.

There it was.

I had spent six weeks thinking about trust like it was a clean material.

Reliable or unreliable.

Load-bearing or not.

Vessa Krail looked more like old masonry.

Do not assume.

Do not remove without knowing what else depends on it.

Probably dangerous to build a second floor on.

Still holding something up.

I disliked that thought.

Which meant I kept it.
