# D239: Margins

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Vessa Krail called it a margin.

I called it a secret commission.

She said those were emotionally different descriptions of the same three percent.

We were standing in a timber yard.

This did not improve philosophy.

Hearthline needed six treated crossbeams for a culvert reinforcement outside West Market.

Not exciting work.

The existing supplier wanted thirteen silver and delivery in five days.

Vessa found twelve silver, two copper, delivery tomorrow.

Daro approved immediately.

I did not.

Not because price bad.

Price was excellent.

Because the yard owner shook Vessa's hand with one coin already hidden under two fingers.

Small motion.

Old Greg had spent decades watching hands around procurement tables.

Current Greg had recently stolen an artifact and become professionally sensitive to people pretending ownership questions did not exist.

I waited until Daro went to inspect beam treatment.

"Three percent?" I asked.

Vessa looked at me.

Then at yard owner.

Then back.

"Two and a half."

"You lied about three."

"You guessed three. I corrected downward."

"You are taking a referral payment."

"Yes."

"Hearthline knows?"

"They know I get paid."

"By the vendor?"

"They know I don't survive on sunlight."

"That's not the same answer."

Vessa smiled.

Not charmingly.

More like somebody pleased a lock had clicked.

"No," she said.

There.

Clean enough.

I looked at the beams.

Good grain.

Proper treatment stain.

Price still below alternate quote.

No obvious harm.

Which was irritating.

"You steered them toward a supplier who pays you."

"I steered them toward the cheapest qualified supplier who could deliver tomorrow."

"And pays you."

"Yes."

"Would you have picked them if they didn't?"

Vessa tilted her head.

"If they were still cheapest and tomorrow?"

"Yes."

"Probably."

I hated probably less than I used to.

Not this probably.

"What if another yard was one copper cheaper and didn't pay you?"

"How far?"

"One copper."

"Then this yard."

"Five copper?"

"Depends delivery risk."

"Ten?"

"Other yard."

There.

A price on integrity.

Everybody had one.

Vessa's came with brackets.

"You should disclose the commission."

"Then Daro negotiates my fee down because he counts vendor money as money he already paid me."

"He didn't pay you that money."

"Exactly."

I stared.

Vessa folded arms.

"Greg."

"What?"

"You stole a future-famous artifact because the current market priced it wrong."

Low blow.

Accurate.

"Returned."

"You keep adding that like the first verb disappears."

I almost got angry.

Then didn't.

Annoying progress.

"This isn't about me."

"Everything is about you if you ask enough questions."

"No."

"Good. Then stop asking whether I would behave according to your preferred disclosure model."

I looked toward Daro.

He was scraping treatment wax with thumbnail.

"Does he know enough to make a choice?"

Vessa followed my look.

"He knows my total fee. He knows I sometimes receive vendor commissions. He does not know every commission on every invoice."

Different.

Still slippery.

"That is more disclosure than you implied five minutes ago."

"You asked better question."

There again.

I understood why Daro kept her.

Not trusted.

Interrogated.

Systems built around known failure modes.

Dangerous thought.

Useful thought.

The yard owner came back with delivery sheet.

Vessa checked weight, address, date.

Caught that the clerk had written West Canal instead of West Market.

Fixed it before signature.

Then she handed the sheet to me.

"Read."

"Why me?"

"Daro is touching wood and you're already suspicious."

Fair.

I read every line.

Correct.

At bottom:

**Expediting commission: 2.5% payable by supplier.**

I stopped.

Looked at her.

Vessa raised one eyebrow.

"It's on their copy."

"Not Hearthline's."

"Hearthline's invoice shows my fixed fee."

"You're impossible."

"No. Expensive. Different."

Daro returned.

I handed him sheet.

"You know she gets vendor commission?"

"Yes."

"Every time?"

"No idea."

"And you're fine with that?"

Daro shrugged.

"If she gives me a bad supplier, she's gone. If she gives me a worse price to feed herself, she's gone. If she keeps getting us good material faster than I can, she gets paid by whoever is stupid enough to pay her twice."

Vessa looked offended.

"Efficient enough."

Daro corrected.

"Expensive enough."

She smiled.

Apparently insult settled.

We signed.

On the way back, Vessa walked beside me because Daro took the cart and I was stupid enough to prefer two miles on walking leg over sitting on stacked brace clamps.

After half a mile she said, "You don't like me."

"Undecided."

"That's worse."

"Why?"

"People who dislike me are easy."

"What are people who like you?"

"Usually expensive."

I laughed.

She smiled without looking over.

"There," she said.

"What?"

"Progress."

"Do not optimize me."

That made her laugh hard enough to stop walking.

I waited.

My socket already felt warm at outer edge.

Two miles had been ambitious.

Current body.

Current evidence.

Vessa recovered.

"I like you," she said.

"Why?"

"You announce when you're judging me. Most people do it privately and then ask favors."

"You prefer honesty."

"From other people."

At least she knew.

We kept walking.

At first corner she changed route without telling me.

"Where are we going?"

"Shorter."

"You said river road."

"River road is nicer."

"I chose nicer."

"You are limping."

I looked down.

Slightly.

Damn.

"You could have said that."

"Then you might argue."

"So you lied about route."

"I changed route."

"Without asking."

"Yes."

I stopped.

Vessa stopped too.

She looked at my face.

For first time, smile went away.

"You want river road?"

There.

Choice returned late.

Not absent.

I looked at the shorter street.

Then at my leg.

"No."

"Good."

"Still ask next time."

Vessa considered that.

"Probably."

"Terrible answer."

"True one."

Mara would hate her.

That thought arrived immediately.

Which was unfair to Mara.

And probably correct.
