# D247: Northbank, Again

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Vessa Krail got off my coach in Northbank.

I stared at her from the step.

"No."

She looked up.

"Good morning."

"Why are you here?"

"Work."

"What work?"

"Mine."

"That is not an answer."

"It answered exactly one question."

The coachman unloaded my bag.

Then hers.

Then three document cases I had not noticed because Vessa apparently traveled with paperwork dense enough to affect axle load.

I took my bag.

"You said you were going east."

"Northbank is east of Carrow."

"You said river permits."

"Northbank has a river."

I looked at coachman.

He looked away.

Coward.

Vessa smiled.

"Enjoy romance."

"Do not know where Mara works."

"I know where half the reconciliation offices on this road work."

"That was not what I said."

"Then your concern is stranger."

She picked up two cases.

A porter took third.

Vessa walked away.

I stood in road feeling manipulated despite not actually knowing what she had manipulated.

Unpleasant skill.

Northbank smelled less unfamiliar this time.

Coal smoke near freight yards.

Sweet yeast from bakery on Bridge Street.

Wet rope.

Horse.

River mud.

I knew which corner had terrible cobbles for walking leg.

Avoided it without thinking.

That counted as knowing a city.

Mara's office occupied second floor above a counting house that hated windows.

She was not there.

Clerk said south warehouse meeting.

Back by third bell.

I had arrived early because road margin remained attractive even when time supposedly cheap.

I found dumplings instead.

Northbank did them better than Carrow.

Mara refused to admit this in letters because loyalty had apparently survived regional relocation.

I ate six.

Bought six more.

By third bell, Mara still had not returned.

By half after, I was sitting on office stairs reading an old canal notice.

A door opened below.

Vessa Krail came up carrying one document case.

I stared.

She stopped.

"This again?"

"Why are you here?"

"I have an appointment."

"With Mara?"

"No."

"Good."

"With her supervisor."

Worse.

"About?"

"Freight reconciliation records for three bonded warehouses."

"Why?"

"Client wants storage rights before winter."

"Who?"

"Client."

I hated her.

A little.

Mara came up behind Vessa.

Stopped.

Looked at Vessa.

Then me.

Then Vessa again.

"Krail."

Vessa smiled.

"Mara."

I stood.

"You know each other."

Mara handed me one look.

Yes.

Bad.

"Professionally," Mara said.

Vessa added, "She rejected two of my filings."

"They were incomplete."

"They were concise."

"They omitted beneficial ownership."

"Irrelevant to storage capacity."

"Relevant to reconciliation review."

Vessa considered.

"This is why I like you."

Mara looked at me.

"Why is she here with you?"

"She is not."

Vessa said, "Shared coach."

"Coincidence?"

I said, "Apparently."

Mara kept looking.

I added, "I hate that I don't know."

Vessa laughed.

Then her appointment opened and she disappeared into supervisor's office.

Mara watched door close.

"No."

"That's what I said."

"No what?"

"Her."

"I didn't bring her."

"You're working with her."

"Hearthline is."

"You're in Hearthline jobs."

"Sometimes."

Mara took dumpling bag from my hand.

Looked inside.

"Six?"

"For you."

"Good answer."

We left before Vessa finished appointment.

I did not wait to see whether coincidence remained believable.

Mara's room was six blocks north.

Second floor.

Narrow stairs.

One rail loose.

I had forgotten rail.

Did not forget this time.

Her room had changed.

More paper.

Better lamp.

Two plants, one healthy and one morally opposed to Northbank.

A second chair.

Not for me specifically.

Good.

She put dumplings on table.

Kissed me before removing coat.

Better.

We spent first hour not talking about work.

Mostly.

She told me downstairs tenant had a child who practiced flute at dawn.

I told her Noll bought one quarter of Brass Spoon.

She had already heard from Noll.

Apparently everybody wrote everyone except me.

We argued about whether Carrow dumplings were actually worse.

They were.

Mara remained wrong.

After food, she asked about Merel.

I told her six weeks.

Last Door.

Orris.

Ash Crown.

Hollow Ford.

Mara stopped at that.

"You told her?"

"Yes."

"And she went anyway."

"Yes."

"How are you doing with that?"

"Poorly."

"Good."

"Cruel."

"Means you're not pretending control."

I leaned back.

Second chair creaked.

"It might have been wrong operation."

"Might."

"Could be years later."

"Could."

"Could have changed it."

"Could."

I glared.

"You're not helping."

"You didn't ask me to solve it."

There.

Annoying woman.

I loved her.

That thought still felt slightly dangerous.

Less than before.

Mara ate cold dumpling.

Then said, "Krail."

I groaned.

"We had a good hour."

"I want to preserve it."

"By discussing Vessa?"

"By asking why you're fascinated."

"I am not fascinated."

Mara waited.

Damn.

"She's useful."

"So are knives."

"Knives don't find brace clamps in twelve minutes."

"You like that she admits some of the bad behavior."

I thought.

"Yes."

"Feels honest."

"Sometimes."

"Greg."

"What?"

"You wrote me that you want to be worth trusting."

I looked toward window.

"You remember too much mail."

"Occupational disease."

"I don't trust Vessa."

"You keep saying that like it solves something."

"It matters."

"Does it?"

"Yes. I know how she fails."

Mara sat back.

"Loose stairs are predictable too."

I looked toward her actual loose stair rail outside.

Mara followed my eyes.

"Shut up."

I laughed.

She did not.

Not immediately.

Then did.

"I'm serious," she said.

"I know."

"Working with someone unreliable isn't automatically stupid. Sometimes you need people like that."

"Thank you."

"I'm not done."

Of course.

"Knowing somebody lies does not mean you know which lie matters."

There.

I did not like that sentence.

Which meant it probably stayed.

"Daro uses her," I said.

"Daro has rules around her."

"So do I."

"Already?"

"Developing."

"That's not a yes."

I hated professionally trained women.

Mara reached across table.

Touched back of my hand.

"I'm not telling you not to work with her."

"I know."

"I'm telling you I don't like her."

"Also know."

"And if she gets me fired, I will kill you first."

"Why me?"

"Access."

I stared.

Mara smiled.

Apparently Vessa had contaminated vocabulary already.

That evening we walked the north quay.

No work.

Mostly.

Mara pointed out a warehouse her office had finally reconciled.

I did not ask how.

She noticed.

"You can ask."

"You said not to optimize."

"Questions aren't optimization."

"Dangerous distinction."

"You'll survive."

We stopped at bridge.

Northbank river wider than Carrow's there.

Different traffic.

More barges.

Fewer small boats.

I thought about Vessa somewhere behind us, probably telling somebody exactly enough truth to get a signature.

Then about Sera at Hollow Ford.

Then Renn under bell-foundry row.

Then Mara beside me.

People everywhere.

Independent.

Useful.

Dangerous.

Loved.

Unknown.

I had spent years becoming good at load paths.

Human ones refused chalk marks.

Mara bumped shoulder against mine.

"Stop thinking."

"Impossible."

"Try quieter."

So I did.
