# D270: Terms

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

I hired Vessa Krail on D270.

This was probably a mistake.

The contract took three hours.

That was first warning.

We used a back table at Brass Spoon after lunch rush.

Noll put tea down.

Looked at Vessa.

Looked at me.

"No credit."

"I know," Vessa said.

Noll looked at me.

"For both."

"Why me?"

"You hiring her."

He left.

Vessa smiled.

"Your people like me."

"No."

"They remember me."

"Different category."

I slid first draft across table.

Vessa read.

Laughed.

Not good sign.

"No."

"Which part?"

"Most."

"Specific."

She tapped second line.

**Vessa Krail may not make commitments in Greg's name without prior approval.**

"Impossible."

"Why?"

"If a route clerk asks whether you're available Thirdday and I say I have to find you first, slot goes to somebody else."

"Then it goes."

"You don't mean that."

"I do."

She studied me.

"Interesting."

"What?"

"Old version of you would've wanted me to hold every slot until decision."

I did not like how quickly she had begun inventing old versions of me.

"You don't know old version."

"I know stories."

"Bad source."

"You keep telling me that about everyone."

Fair.

She crossed out sentence.

Wrote:

**Vessa may hold provisional opportunities for no longer than one day without Greg's approval. Provisional holds must be disclosed as provisional.**

I read.

"Better."

"Obviously."

Next line.

**No lying in Greg's name.**

Vessa looked at me.

"Define lying."

"No."

"Then no."

"Vessa."

"I will not sign a rule where you get to decide after fact what counts."

Annoyingly reasonable.

"Knowingly false factual statements."

"Fine."

"Material omissions designed to create false impression."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because half negotiation is deciding what not to say."

"That's exactly problem."

"Then hire priest."

"I don't need priest."

"You need me."

There.

I hated confidence because it was correct.

My work had changed.

Hearthline jobs.

Independent municipal support.

Merel attachments.

Northbank visits.

Bera fittings.

Guild reviews.

Letters.

Route windows.

I was spending increasing amount of time coordinating access instead of doing work once access existed.

Old Greg would have delegated earlier.

Current Greg distrusted delegation after Faultglass because apparently one bad moral decision could turn every administrative choice into philosophy.

"I need somebody," I said.

"Correct."

"I don't need you specifically."

Vessa leaned back.

"Then don't hire me."

No sales pitch.

Interesting.

"Who else?" I asked.

She named three people.

One cheaper.

One more reliable.

One with better Northbank reach.

I stared.

"Why tell me?"

"Because if you hire me after knowing alternatives, I spend less time wondering when you discover them."

Optionality.

There it was.

"Which would you hire?"

"For you?"

"Yes."

"Me."

"Of course."

"You need weird mix. Guild. municipal. private teams. travel. people with titles. people who hate titles. You also ask too many questions, so someone more respectable will either charge double or quit."

"And you won't?"

"I charge different."

We moved to payment.

Fixed retainer too high.

Hourly impossible.

Per contract created incentives I hated.

Vessa proposed:

Small monthly base.

Flat filing fees.

Five percent of any negotiated increase above Greg's initial offered rate.

Zero commission on medical, prosthetic, housing, or personal travel.

Supplier/referral payments involving Greg's jobs disclosed monthly in aggregate, with named vendors if directly affecting Greg's cost.

I stared at that line.

"Monthly aggregate."

"Yes."

"Not every payment."

"Correct."

"Why?"

"Because my other clients exist."

"If vendor affects my job?"

"Named."

"Amount?"

Vessa hesitated.

There.

"Amount," I said.

"Fine."

We wrote amount.

Then boundaries.

No contacting Mara's office using Greg's relationship.

No contacting Renn Calder at all without Greg's explicit request.

No representing future knowledge as current fact.

No accepting work above Greg's current Guild scope without written provisional status.

Vessa stopped at Renn line.

"Why him?"

"Private."

"Then why name?"

"Because you know name already."

She smiled slightly.

"Good."

"What?"

"You noticed."

"Noticed what?"

"I asked about him twice and you never answered."

I had forgotten first time.

Damn.

"Why were you asking?"

"Because people around you say his name like they are not supposed to."

Dangerous woman.

"Do not investigate him."

Vessa looked at contract.

"That's broader than don't contact."

"Yes."

"Public records?"

"No."

"Guild board?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because he asked me not to turn him into project. You would become extension of me doing exactly that."

Vessa considered.

Then wrote:

**No proactive information gathering concerning Renn Calder unless Greg or Renn requests it. Incidental information need not be ignored, but may not be pursued.**

I read twice.

"Good."

"I know."

Last section.

Termination.

Either party, immediate.

Outstanding fees due.

No exclusivity.

No ownership of contacts.

Vessa added that one.

Optionality again.

At end, two copies.

Signed.

She put hers into case.

I put mine in coat.

Then sat there.

Noll came by.

"Done?"

"Unfortunately."

He looked at Vessa.

"Greg trust you?"

I said, "No."

Vessa said, "No."

Same time.

Noll nodded.

"Good."

I stared at him.

"Why good?"

"He know."

Then he left.

Vessa laughed quietly.

"I like Noll."

"He charges you before food."

"Boundaries."

I looked at her.

"Why did you take this?"

"Money."

"Not enough."

"Access."

There.

"To what?"

"You."

I waited.

"You're weirdly connected for Silver," she said. "Hearthline. Far Lantern. Ash Crown. Northbank reconciliation. Municipal work. A future-famous theft everybody still talks about."

"Not future-famous."

"Current embarrassing. Better."

"So I'm network asset."

"Yes."

Clean answer.

"Anything else?"

Vessa thought.

"I like working with you."

That landed worse than access.

"Why?"

"You make rules when scared instead of pretending not scared."

I looked down at contract.

Six pages.

Possibly excessive evidence.

"And you?"

"What?"

"What do you do when scared?"

Vessa smiled.

"Keep exits."

There.

I had hired exactly wrong person for a man who wanted to be worth trusting.

Or exactly useful one.

I could not tell yet.

That was probably why I signed.
