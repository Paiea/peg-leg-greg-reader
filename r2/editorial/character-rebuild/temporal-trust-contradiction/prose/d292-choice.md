# D292: Choice

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Vessa sold my risk tolerance without asking.

That was worse than lying.

The job itself went well.

Private grain warehouse near South Gate.

Prepared site.

Known lower floor.

One old ward line crossing a support wall they wanted opened for new loading door.

Exactly my scope.

Client paid specialist rate high enough that I read number twice.

Vessa had negotiated it.

"Why so high?" I asked before signing.

"Short notice."

"That's all?"

"They need door before harvest carts start."

True.

Not complete.

I signed.

Daro was not involved.

Independent work under municipal clearance with licensed ward hand named Pellan Tor.

Pellan was competent.

Client foreman listened.

Wall stayed standing.

We found ward line shallower than survey suggested, moved cut six inches, installed header, finished by fifth bell.

Good job.

I liked good jobs.

No hidden lesson required.

Then Pellan asked me how long Esker usually took to pay.

I stopped packing chalk.

"Usually?"

He looked up.

"You haven't worked for them?"

"No."

Pellan's face changed.

Not dramatically.

Enough.

"Ask Krail."

Cold feeling.

"Why?"

He put tools away.

"They paid my last invoice eighteen days late. Mason crew before me still waiting on final third."

I went very still.

"How long?"

"Month, maybe."

"And you came back."

"Rate's good."

"You knew before today."

"Yes."

"Vessa knew?"

Pellan laughed once.

"Krail knows what people had for breakfast if invoice touches it."

There.

I found Vessa outside talking to client clerk.

She saw me.

Finished sentence.

Did not pretend not to know.

"How late are they?" I asked.

Clerk went quiet.

Vessa said, "Which invoice?"

"Mine."

"Not late yet."

"History."

Clerk looked at Vessa.

She waved him away.

He left gratefully.

"Average?" she asked.

"Do not do this."

Vessa's mouth tightened.

"Eleven days beyond term over last six contracts. One at eighteen. One currently twenty-nine and disputed."

"You knew."

"Yes."

"Before I signed."

"Yes."

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because rate includes payment risk."

I stared.

"I asked why rate high."

"I said short notice."

"Which was true."

"Yes."

"And deliberately incomplete."

Vessa did not answer.

Answer enough.

"Would I have taken job if you told me?"

"I thought probably not."

There.

Clean.

Ugly.

"So you removed choice."

"I priced risk."

"My risk."

"Mine too. My five percent doesn't exist until they pay."

"That is not same risk."

"No."

I wanted her to argue.

Would have been easier.

She didn't.

"You breached contract."

"Maybe."

"Material omission designed to create false impression."

"We struck that line."

I hated her.

For exactly remembering.

"No commitments without approval."

"You approved job."

"Based on information you withheld because you believed it would change approval."

Vessa folded arms.

"Then contract was badly written."

I laughed.

One sharp sound.

"That's your answer?"

"No. My answer is I wanted commission and I thought job was good enough that you would be glad after."

"I am not."

"Clearly."

"You don't get to decide what risk I accept."

"No."

"Then why did you?"

Vessa looked toward warehouse.

Harvest wagons already lining south road.

"Because I thought I knew better."

There.

That sentence belonged to me too often.

Made it worse, not better.

"You're fired," I said.

Vessa nodded.

Immediately.

No argument.

That surprised me enough to make me angrier.

"That's it?"

"You have termination right."

"You don't want to explain?"

"I did."

"Apologize?"

Vessa looked at me.

"I'm sorry I took choice."

Not sorry she took job.

Not sorry for rate.

Specific.

I believed it.

Probably.

Damn.

She opened document case.

Pulled my schedule sheet.

"Hearthline Firstday. Bera Thirdday. Northbank after that not booked yet. Merel autumn notice expected within three weeks. I have two provisional municipal inquiries held until tomorrow. I'll release them."

Professional until end.

I took sheet.

"Invoice?"

"I can buy it from you."

I stopped.

"What?"

"Face value less my commission. You get paid today. I collect when Esker pays."

"Why?"

"Because I priced risk."

There.

She was willing to own it.

Did not restore choice.

Changed something anyway.

"At full face," I said.

Vessa blinked.

"No."

"Your commission was payment for arranging job. You arranged job by withholding material risk. You don't get commission."

She thought.

"Fair."

"Full face."

"Tomorrow. I don't carry that much."

"Fine."

"And then done?"

Question landed.

I looked at her.

Actually looked.

I wanted to be worth trusting.

She was not.

Simple.

Except not simple.

She had followed Renn boundary.

Found clamps.

Made good contracts.

Given casualty list free.

Taken choice here.

Would do something like this again if incentives aligned and rule did not stop her.

Probably.

No.

Almost certainly.

"Yes," I said.

Vessa nodded.

Then left.

I walked home alone.

No expediter.

No calendar help.

No clever routes.

That should have felt clean.

It felt like losing useful tool because tool occasionally cut hand.

Bad analogy.

People were not tools.

Merel would hit me with paper for that one.

At Brass Spoon, Noll asked, "Krail?"

"Fired."

He nodded.

"Why?"

I told him.

Noll listened.

Then said, "Good."

I waited.

"Good fired or good reason?"

"Both."

Annoying clarity.

Next morning Vessa arrived before first bell with envelope.

Full invoice amount.

No commission deducted.

I counted.

Exact.

She handed me signed receivable transfer.

"Esker owes me now."

"Yes."

"You have two municipal inquiries likely still open if you want names."

"No."

"Fine."

She turned.

I said, "Vessa."

She stopped.

I did not know why I had called.

Maybe because clean endings made me suspicious now.

"You really thought I'd be glad after."

"Yes."

"That's worse."

"I know."

She left.

Five days later I discovered I had no idea who to call about a delayed North Gate permit.

I knew one person.

Did not call.

That lasted until afternoon.
