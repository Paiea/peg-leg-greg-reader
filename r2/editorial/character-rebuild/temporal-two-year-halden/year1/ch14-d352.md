# D352: Standing

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Standing required four people and a chair.

This seemed excessive.

"Again," Tovan said.

"I was upright."

"You were falling politely."

"Still vertical."

"Temporarily."

I looked at the chair.

Wooden arms.

Straight back.

No visible hostility.

The chair had become my destination.

Three feet from the bed.

Possibly four.

I had crossed bridges with worse odds.

That thought arrived before the rest of me could stop it.

Bad comparison.

I left it alone.

Ressa stood on my left with one hand under my arm.

Tovan stood on my right.

A Brell orderly named Fen held the chair still despite the chair having made no escape attempt.

My crutches leaned against the wall.

Not mine.

Brell's.

Temporary ash wood, padded under the arms with folded wool and leather tied around the grips.

I had spent ten minutes learning that crutches were not sticks with ambition.

There were angles.

There were heights.

There were ways to put weight through the hands without destroying the armpits.

There were apparently ways to fall that Tovan considered educational and ways he considered expensive.

"Hands," Ressa said.

I put both palms on the bed.

Right foot flat.

Left knee bent slightly because the bandaged end of my leg did not appreciate hanging.

My missing foot was still cold.

It had also developed an itch between the second and third toes sometime during breakfast.

I had spent several minutes trying not to scratch a place that no longer existed.

This had not improved my mood.

"Push," Tovan said.

I pushed.

Right leg took everything.

My balance went left because forty-nine years of first life and nineteen years of current anatomy had agreed that the floor should meet a second foot there.

It did not.

Ressa caught my shoulder.

Tovan caught my hip.

I swore.

Fen kept the chair from escaping heroically.

"That's the part," Tovan said.

"Excellent diagnosis."

"Again."

I sat back on the bed.

My stump throbbed beneath the bandage.

Stump.

I disliked the word.

Not because it was cruel.

Because it was accurate too quickly.

Tovan waited.

He had learned not to fill silence for me.

Useful man.

I breathed until the room stopped tilting.

Then tried again.

This time I expected the missing floor.

That helped.

Push.

Right foot.

Hands.

Ressa close but not lifting.

Tovan's palm near my elbow.

I stood.

Actually stood.

One leg.

Two hands on the bed.

My body leaned right automatically.

Then too far.

Corrected.

The phantom left foot pressed flat against a floor it did not touch.

That was somehow worse than the cold.

"Stay," Tovan said.

"Where am I going?"

"Down."

Fair.

I stayed upright for maybe five breaths.

Six.

My right thigh began shaking.

Not weakness exactly.

New job.

Every muscle that had once shared the work was discovering management had been reorganized overnight.

"Sit," Ressa said.

"Chair."

Tovan looked at me.

"You want the chair?"

"It has been staring at me."

Fen smiled.

Tovan did not.

"One pivot. Not hop."

"I know what a pivot is."

"Your body doesn't yet."

Rude.

Correct.

They moved the chair closer.

I shifted my right foot.

My missing left foot shifted too.

No.

The feeling of it did.

My brain supplied a heel turn.

Nothing happened below the knee.

I lurched.

Ressa caught me harder this time.

Pain flared through the bandage.

White enough that I stopped speaking.

Tovan put me back on the bed.

"Done."

"Chair."

"Later."

"You said standing."

"You stood."

"I also nearly sat."

"On Ressa."

Ressa said, "I charge more."

Good healer.

I lay back.

Not defeated.

Temporarily horizontal.

The door opened before I could decide whether that distinction helped.

Jorren came in carrying my coat.

Mud still on one boot.

Of course.

He stopped inside the room.

Looked at me.

Looked at the blanket.

Did not look away quickly enough to pretend he had not.

"Morning," he said.

It was afternoon.

"Road bad?"

"Detour."

"You made it."

"Eventually."

There.

His word.

It landed differently now.

He put my coat on the chair I had failed to reach.

Inside pocket bulged slightly.

Mara's letter.

Halden slip.

Probably both.

"Kessa?" I asked.

"Bridge."

"Still?"

"Pell sent her home. She went back."

Of course.

"Noll?"

"Coming. Vessa wouldn't let him leave lunch before replacement got there."

That sentence helped more than if Noll had abandoned the kitchen immediately.

His life had not stopped because mine changed shape.

Good.

Annoying.

Good.

Jorren stayed standing.

There was a second chair now.

Not my second chair.

Brell hospital had two.

He ignored both.

"You see it?" I asked.

His face tightened.

"What?"

"The bridge."

"No."

"Kessa tell you?"

"Some."

"What some?"

Jorren looked toward Tovan and Ressa.

They were pretending to organize bandages while listening to everything.

Professional privacy.

"Bank went," he said. "Brace moved. You got caught."

"That's most."

"She said everyone was clear."

"We were."

He looked at the blanket again.

"Then how?"

There was the question.

Not accusation.

Almost worse.

"Clear wasn't far enough."

Jorren's jaw moved.

He looked angry.

At the bridge.

At Kessa.

At me.

At physics.

Maybe all.

"She put you on repair?"

"I put me on repair."

"Kessa asked."

"Vell asked if I was staying. I asked the pay."

Jorren stared.

"You asked the pay."

"It was good."

His face did something between laughing and hitting me.

Neither won.

"Idiot."

"Silver idiot."

"One week."

"What?"

"You were Silver one week."

"Thirty-six days."

"Worse."

I smiled.

He didn't.

Then I stopped.

His eyes were wet.

Not much.

Enough.

Jorren rubbed his face with both hands.

"Fuck."

There.

No clean answer.

I looked toward the window.

"Yes."

He sat finally.

Not on my destination chair.

The other one.

Good.

"They take all of it?"

"Below knee."

I moved the blanket enough to show where the bandage ended.

Jorren looked.

Actually looked.

No flinch.

Then nodded once.

"Knee's good."

"Apparently."

"That's something."

"Tovan says it matters for fitting."

Jorren looked at Tovan.

"He good?"

Tovan answered from the shelf.

"No."

Jorren blinked.

Tovan continued.

"Adequate."

I liked him more every hour.

Jorren looked back at me.

"Home when?"

There.

Actual problem.

"Tovan says five days if closure stays clean. Maybe seven."

"Your room."

"Third floor."

"I know."

"Everyone knows my stairs."

"Bad stairs."

"Fourth squeaks."

"Third."

I looked at him.

"Mara says third."

"She's wrong."

Good.

Some things could still be argued.

Jorren leaned back.

"We can carry you."

My whole body rejected the sentence before pride had time to claim responsibility.

"No."

"Then hop."

"Also no."

"Crawl?"

"Leave."

He almost smiled.

Then his face settled again.

"Maud has ground room."

I looked at him.

"What ground room?"

"Back of cookroom. Storage."

"That's not a room."

"Has bed."

"Why?"

"Old tenant."

"How old?"

"Dead, maybe."

"Helpful."

"Maud says you can use it."

I stared.

"She said that?"

"For extra."

There she was.

Relief came so hard it was almost affection.

"How much?"

Jorren told me.

I became less affectionate.

"Criminal."

"Said you'd say that."

"Did she reduce it?"

"No."

Strong landlord.

Tovan came over.

"Ground floor is better initially."

"You are not helping negotiations."

"I don't care."

Also strong.

Jorren picked up my coat again.

"Need anything?"

I looked at the bulging inside pocket.

"Paper."

He stopped.

"Mara?"

"Eventually."

"Kessa sent packet yesterday."

My stomach tightened.

"What did she write?"

"Facts."

Good.

I had asked for that.

Still terrifying.

"When does it reach?"

"Three days if river. Four maybe."

"So tomorrow."

"Maybe."

I made the noise.

Jorren smiled.

"There he is."

I hated that line enough to feel normal.

"Paper," I repeated.

He left to find some.

Tovan checked the bandage.

Ressa asked whether the phantom foot still hurt.

"Itches."

"Where?"

"Between toes."

"Which?"

"Second and third."

She nodded like this made sense.

"Can I scratch it?"

"You can try."

"That sounds useless."

"Sometimes touching the end of the limb helps the brain place what's left. Sometimes it doesn't."

"Strong medicine."

"Bodies are rude."

Finally, expertise I understood.

She put my hand over the blanket above the bandaged end.

"Not hard. Just know where it stops."

I did.

My palm rested over bandage.

Warm.

Thick.

Below that, my phantom foot remained cold six inches beyond the end of my body.

I moved my hand away.

"Terrible system."

"Yes."

No lesson.

Good.

Jorren came back with paper, ink, and Noll.

Noll entered carrying a covered bowl.

Of course.

He looked at me.

At the blanket.

At Tovan.

Then held up the bowl.

"Soup."

"That's your reaction?"

"You eat?"

"Yes."

"Then soup."

He set it on the table.

Not the chair.

Everyone respected the chair now.

Noll pulled the lid off.

Steam.

Chicken.

Ginger.

Rice.

Better than hospital food by smell alone.

"Vessa?" I asked.

"Me."

I looked at him.

"You made this?"

"Work in kitchen."

Right.

Nine months ago Noll's dumplings leaned left.

Now he brought soup across Carrow because he knew what a recovering person could keep down.

He did not explain the accumulation.

He handed me a spoon.

I ate.

The first mouthful was too hot.

I burned my tongue.

Perfect.

Noll sat on the chair Jorren had used.

Jorren leaned against the wall.

No one said sorry.

No one told me I was lucky.

No one promised I would be the same.

Noll complained that Vessa docked his break because he left early.

Jorren told him to bill the bridge.

I laughed and nearly spilled soup.

My stump hurt when my abdomen tightened.

New information.

After they left, paper remained on the table.

I wrote Mara.

Not immediately.

I slept first.

Woke near evening.

Wrote then.

**Mara,**

**Kessa's packet probably reached before this. Facts are still facts. The bridge repair went wrong after we stopped the work. My left leg was crushed below the knee. Tovan removed it yesterday. Knee is still mine. Everything below it is apparently committed elsewhere.**

I stopped.

Too funny?

Maybe.

Mine anyway.

**I am alive. I can sit. I can stand badly. Brell owns a chair I have not yet defeated. Noll brought soup. Jorren has developed opinions about stairs.**

I looked at the inside pocket of my coat hanging from the chair.

The Vey slip was still there.

I did not take it out.

**I don't know what work looks like yet. I don't know what walking looks like yet. Tovan says fitted legs exist and Carrow is a good place for them. I am trying not to make decisions for a body I met yesterday.**

That sentence stayed.

Then the dangerous part.

The promise.

I could write it.

I could reassure her.

I could make my injury immediately about proving something two cities away.

No.

Mara had not asked.

I wrote instead:

**You do not need to come unless you want to. I mean that literally, not nobly. Your work is still your work. Tell me what you want when you know it.**

Then:

**I would like to see you. Both things are true. Unfortunately.**

That was enough.

At the bottom I wrote:

**The second chair remains by the window.**

I did not write the number of days left.

For the first time since the river stairs, I genuinely did not want to know.

I folded the letter.

The phantom foot still itched.

The real bandage hurt.

Tomorrow I would try the chair again.

That was close enough to a future.
