# D358: Ground Floor

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Maud had put a price on gravity.

Four copper a week.

"That's robbery," I said.

"Ground floor."

"The room is half the size."

"Closer to ground."

"That is not a luxury."

Maud looked at the crutches under my arms.

Then at the stairs behind her.

Then back at me.

"Today it is."

Hostile market conditions.

I stood in the Lower Rook front hall six days after the operation with one boot, two crutches, one bandaged left leg, and enough exhaustion from the wagon ride from Brell to make the stairs look personally ambitious.

The fourth one squeaked.

I could hear it from the floor.

Mara had been wrong all along.

I had never felt less interested in winning that argument.

Noll stood behind me carrying my field bag.

Jorren carried everything else because apparently losing part of one leg transferred luggage rights automatically.

Kessa had ridden from Brell with us and left ten minutes earlier after confirming Maud's ground room existed and had a door.

Her standards had changed.

The room was behind the cookroom.

I had passed the door perhaps a hundred times and assumed it contained buckets.

It did.

Also a narrow bed.

Maud had removed most of the buckets.

Generous.

"Four copper," she repeated.

"Two."

"Four."

"Three, and you fix the window latch."

"Window works."

"It opens when the wind decides."

"Fresh air."

I looked at her.

She looked at me.

No pity.

Excellent landlord.

"Three and a half."

"Four."

"Criminal."

"Paid until Firstday."

Jorren laughed behind me.

I turned too fast.

The crutch tip slid half an inch on the hall board.

My body tried to catch itself with a left foot that remained imaginary.

Jorren stopped laughing immediately.

I caught the wall instead.

No fall.

Barely.

Everyone went still.

That was worse than falling.

"Continue," I said.

Jorren's face tightened.

"What?"

"Mocking the rent. We were doing well."

Noll said, "Four copper."

"Thank you."

The room had one step down from the cookroom.

Of course it did.

Not a stair.

A single drop of perhaps six inches.

The smallest possible architecture capable of becoming relevant.

Tovan had taught me how to manage one step before discharge.

Crutches down.

Right foot follows.

Do not hop while tired.

Do not trust the missing foot sensation.

Do not put the healing limb end against anything hard.

I knew the sequence.

Knowing and doing remained different professions.

I placed both crutches on the lower floor.

Shifted weight through my hands.

Moved right foot down.

My body leaned left.

Corrected.

Done.

One step.

My palms hurt.

The room waited.

Bed against the back wall.

Small table.

One chair.

Not mine.

The window looked into the rear alley at approximately cart-wheel height.

A man outside rolled a barrel past while I was entering.

For one strange second the barrel occupied more of the view than the sky.

Ground floor indeed.

Jorren put my belongings on the bed.

Not many.

Two shirts.

Two trousers.

Wash things.

Kettle.

Books.

Tool box.

Good sword.

Letters.

The burned seven.

The Halden slip.

My upstairs room still held the rest because no one had declared me dead and Maud continued charging rent there too.

"Both rooms?" I asked.

"Until Firstday," Maud said from the doorway.

"You are charging me for two rooms."

"You asked to keep upstairs."

I had.

Yesterday, from Brell, when Jorren asked what to move.

I had said not the room.

Meaning don't dismantle my life because I had not seen it yet.

Maud had interpreted that financially.

Strong institution.

"Fine."

"I know."

She left.

Noll put my field bag under the table.

I looked at it.

"Why there?"

He shrugged.

"Where else?"

I did not know.

The bag had lived beside my upstairs door for months.

Ready for road.

Then unpacked.

Then ready again.

Now beneath a table in a room where I could reach the door without stairs.

No symbolic meaning required.

It was simply in the wrong place because every place was new.

Jorren picked up the sword.

"Wall?"

There were no hooks.

"Bed."

He laid it along the far side.

I sat in the chair.

Too quickly.

The healing leg throbbed.

I put it on the low stool Kessa had borrowed from the repair camp and sent with us.

No one spoke for a few seconds.

Noll looked around.

"Bad room."

"Correct."

"Cookroom close."

"Advantage."

"Food."

"Another."

Jorren looked at the window.

"You can see wheels."

"Leave."

He smiled.

Good.

The front door opened in the hall.

My whole body turned toward the sound.

Packet runner.

Maybe.

No.

Footsteps.

Maud said something.

Then a voice I did not know.

I stopped listening.

Mara's answer had not arrived yet.

Kessa's first packet should have reached Halden four days ago.

Mine perhaps two.

If routes behaved.

Routes had developed a philosophy against behavior.

I had decided not to count.

This worked badly.

Noll looked at me.

"Waiting?"

"No."

"Bad liar."

"Private."

"Ground floor."

Apparently privacy had altitude requirements.

He left for the kitchen and returned with a bowl of rice and soft eggs.

"I'm not sick."

"Tovan said food."

"He did not specify eggs."

"I do."

New authority.

I ate.

Jorren unpacked the kettle and found that the room table leaned toward the wall.

He put a folded scrap beneath one leg.

"You learned from Brass Spoon."

"Table taught me."

The afternoon became small problems.

Getting from chair to bed without letting the crutches slide.

Finding somewhere to hang them where they would not fall when I reached for them.

Realizing the washbasin sat too low to use comfortably while standing on one leg.

Moving it to the table.

Discovering that carrying water while using two crutches required either a third hand or humility.

I chose a covered jug and a strap.

Noll chose carrying it for me.

We argued.

He won by leaving while holding the jug.

At third bell, I needed the privy.

That became an expedition.

Across cookroom.

Down the hall.

One threshold.

Turn sideways because the door was narrow.

Crutch tips on old boards polished by decades of people who had never needed to think about them.

I thought about all of them.

Unfairly.

The return trip was worse because my hands had started hurting.

I reached the room sweating.

Noll was chopping onions in the cookroom.

He looked up once.

Did not offer help.

Good.

Then he moved a bucket out of my path without comment.

Also good.

I sat.

My phantom foot felt like it had gone numb from being sat on.

Impossible complaint.

I rubbed the end of my bandage lightly the way Ressa had shown me.

The sensation changed.

Not vanished.

The imaginary toes became pins and needles instead of cold.

Improvement by technicality.

Someone knocked at the front door again.

This time Maud called, "Greg."

I stood too fast.

No.

I tried to.

My right leg took weight before the crutches were positioned.

The room tilted.

I caught the table.

The kettle fell.

Metal hit floor.

Water everywhere.

Noll appeared in the doorway before I finished swearing.

He looked at me.

Then the kettle.

Then the wet floor.

"You fall?"

"No."

"Kettle did."

"Traitor."

Maud called again.

"Packet."

There.

Every stupid muscle in my body wanted speed.

Noll saw.

He did not pick up my crutches.

He kicked one gently toward me with his foot.

Then the other.

I hated him for exactly one second.

Then understood.

My pace.

My hands.

My problem.

I positioned the crutches.

Stood properly.

Waited until balance settled.

Then crossed the cookroom.

Slow.

Too slow.

The packet runner waited in the hall holding a Halden envelope.

He knew me now.

His eyes went to the missing lower leg.

Then back to my face.

He did not hide the first look well.

Most people wouldn't.

"Greg?"

"Still."

He handed me the letter.

Mara's handwriting.

No countdown on the outside.

I noticed immediately.

That frightened me more than if there had been one.

"Answer?" the runner asked.

"Probably."

He nodded.

Left.

I returned to the room.

Not fast.

Noll had mopped the spilled water.

The kettle sat upside down on a cloth.

No speech.

I sat on the bed.

Opened Mara's letter.

The first line was not a joke.

**I got Kessa's packet yesterday morning and yours this afternoon.**

I kept reading.

**I am glad you are alive. I am furious you were hurt. I know those are not opposite statements. I still dislike that sentence structure because you keep making it necessary.**

There.

Mara.

My chest loosened slightly.

**Vey can release me for five days starting next Fourday. Travel takes three if the river cooperates, which means I would spend six days using five. This is apparently advanced accounting. I asked anyway. They said no.**

I stopped.

Read it again.

She had asked.

They said no.

No dramatic resignation.

No barge already underway.

Relief and disappointment arrived together and immediately began arguing.

Mara continued.

**Sera offered to cover the desk if I take unpaid leave. I can afford some. I have not decided. Before you tell me not to come, I read your sentence. You said unless I want to. I want to. That is not the same as being able to leave tomorrow.**

I closed my eyes.

Good.

Bad.

Both.

**Tell me what Tovan says about travel, fitting, healing, and whether I can help without becoming another person moving your water cup while you glare at them. I am asking because I know you.**

Too late.

Noll had already moved the water.

**Also, if you tell me the bridge was nobody's fault before anyone finishes looking at it, I will come to Carrow specifically to hit you with your own crutch. Kessa's packet sounded guilty. Yours sounded careful. Neither of you gets to decide the investigation because you are sad.**

I smiled.

Of course.

**I am not canceling Halden. I am not asking you about the stone steps right now. I assume you know why.**

That one hurt.

Not because she doubted.

Because she refused to make my missing leg perform reassurance on command.

Correct.

Annoying.

**Write me what today actually looks like. Not what you think six months will look like. Today.**

At the bottom:

**Mara.**

No number.

I read the whole letter again.

Then looked around the ground-floor room.

Today.

One chair.

One bed.

One window at barrel height.

Kettle drying upside down because I had tried to stand too fast.

Crutches leaning against my knee.

Noll chopping onions eight feet away.

Maud charging four copper for gravity.

Phantom toes tingling.

Right palm beginning to blister beneath the thumb from the crutch grip.

Good sword lying beside a bed I had not yet figured out how to make without kneeling.

I found paper.

**Today I came home. Maud charges four copper a week for the privilege of not climbing stairs. I attempted negotiation and lost.**

I continued.

**I can cross the cookroom and reach the privy. It takes long enough that I now respect doors. My hands hurt more than my right leg. The left one hurts in places that exist and itches in places that do not. Noll made eggs and has become unbearable about medical nutrition. Jorren moved my sword and I allowed it because I was too tired to argue efficiently.**

I looked at the kettle.

**I knocked over the kettle because your letter arrived and I tried to stand before I had both crutches. This is your fault only if we use Vey accounting.**

Better.

Then:

**Tovan says no travel until the closure is stable and the stitches come out. He said two weeks minimum before he wants me doing anything clever, which I pointed out is not a medical category. He disagreed. Final fitted leg is months away. Temporary support may be earlier. I do not yet know what that means.**

I stopped.

Mara had asked if she could help.

Current answer.

**If you can come without breaking your own life to do it, I want you here. If you cannot yet, write. I mean both parts.**

That was enough.

I folded the letter.

Outside my low window, another barrel rolled past.

I could see only the bottom half of the man pushing it.

The world had become strangely framed.

Noll appeared at the doorway.

"Good?"

I looked at the letter.

"Complicated."

He nodded.

No follow-up.

Then he pointed at the overturned kettle.

"You buy new?"

"It still works."

"Dented."

"Character."

"Trash."

Different discipline.

He went back to his onions.

I put Mara's letter beside the Vey slip.

Not on top of it.

Beside.

Today first.

Tomorrow could wait until morning.
