# D541: Two Legs

Status: **TEMPORAL REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

---

Bera put my leg on the table and said, "This one is too useful."

I stared at her.

"That sentence is defective."

"No."

She tapped the ash foot.

"You walk streets in it. You stand at work in it. You take it over broken floor. You keep asking it to turn faster. You want better mud grip. You want quieter heel strike. You want more flex at the toe. You want less flex when shaping."

"Those are improvements."

"Those are different legs."

There.

Annoying evidence.

I sat on the fitting bench with my left trouser leg rolled above the socket liner.

Current leg on the table.

Actual limb ending below my knee.

Still strange when both were visible at once.

Less strange than three months ago.

Not ordinary yet.

Bera opened a drawer.

Pulled out two lower shafts.

One ash.

One darker wood with an iron cap at the bottom.

No carved foot.

Just a tapered peg.

I looked at it.

"No."

"You haven't tried it."

"It is a stick."

"Correct."

"I have dignity."

Bera looked at the current wooden foot.

Then at me.

"Debatable."

Hostile craft.

She fitted the peg beneath a spare socket shell.

Same upper geometry.

Different lower job.

Shorter.

Narrower.

No imitation ankle.

Iron tip wrapped in replaceable hard leather.

"Field," Bera said. "Standing. Mud. Broken floor. Easier to place exactly. Less to catch."

"Walking?"

"Worse."

"Stairs?"

"Different."

"Running?"

She stared at me.

"Eventually you people always ask running."

"You people?"

"Men."

Apparently sex created biomechanics.

I strapped in.

Stood.

The first sensation was wrong.

No foot shape beneath me.

No fake heel.

No attempt to convince the body this was a normal leg.

Just one hard point under the left side.

I shifted weight.

The peg held.

Very little roll.

Very little uncertainty about where contact happened.

I shifted again.

My hips hated it.

My brain liked it immediately.

One point.

Known point.

Load went there.

"Walk," Bera said.

I did.

Awful.

Not painful.

Mechanical.

Right foot step.

Peg placement.

Hip rise.

Right foot again.

No heel-to-toe roll.

No attempt at natural stride.

I sounded like furniture being moved.

"Terrible," I said.

"Good."

"You have corrupted language."

"Stand."

I stopped.

That changed everything.

The peg sat directly beneath the socket.

No soft foot section.

No uncertain flex.

When I formed a small force plane against the fitting bench, my left side transmitted the counterload into the floor cleanly.

Not more power.

Less argument.

I increased pressure.

Bera slapped the table.

"No magic testing without warning."

I dropped it.

"Sorry."

She looked surprised.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You expected an argument."

"Yes."

Fair.

I sat.

Looked at both lower legs on the table.

One had a foot.

One was a peg.

Neither was me.

Both could become mine.

That distinction had taken too long.

"Cost?"

Bera told me.

I swore.

"It's half a leg."

"Then pay half."

"That's not how fractions work."

"It is here."

I did the arithmetic.

Gold contract money made the answer irritating instead of impossible.

That was new.

"Can we reuse the socket hardware?"

"Some."

"Quick change?"

Bera narrowed her eyes.

"Define quick."

"Minutes."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the attachment that changes in minutes will also discover it can change while you're standing."

Good reason.

"Ten minutes?"

"Maybe. Tools. Bench. Not roadside."

"Fine."

I wrote it down.

Bera watched me.

"You don't need every answer now."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"No."

That earned a smile.

The peg came home with me three days later.

Noll saw it first.

He looked at my left side.

Then the spare walking leg under my arm.

Then back at the peg.

"Now actual peg."

I stopped.

There it was.

The stupidest possible sentence.

Also true.

"Apparently."

Noll circled once like he was inspecting a chair.

"Looks worse."

"Thank you."

"You walk worse."

"Also thank you."

"Work better?"

I looked at him.

"Yes."

He nodded.

Problem solved.

At the Guild the next week, I wore the peg for a warehouse brace job.

It looked less like a leg and worked better for the actual task.

That bothered me for approximately twenty minutes.

Then Kessa put a lateral load through a timber shore and I held the counterforce without the fake foot flexing under me.

The plane stayed cleaner.

My hip stayed quieter.

We finished early.

Bera had been right.

Again.

I hated patterns.

On the walk home I changed back to the footed leg at her shop.

Twelve minutes.

Not quick.

Acceptable.

Outside, street walking became easier immediately.

Different tool.

Different job.

My first life had taught me to value weapons that could solve many problems.

Age had eventually taught me the opposite lesson too.

The thing that did one job perfectly was sometimes more dangerous than the thing that did ten adequately.

I looked down at the walking foot.

Then back toward Bera's shop where the peg waited for tomorrow's work.

For the first time since Brell, I stopped asking when I would have my leg back.

Wrong question.

I was accumulating legs.
