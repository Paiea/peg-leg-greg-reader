# D071: The Third Hound

The contract said two ash hounds.

There were three.

This was not unusual enough to be interesting.

What was unusual was the third one staying out of sight.

"Left ridge," Nera said.

I looked left.

Nothing.

The first ash hound came through the brush at knee height, gray hide smoking where its shoulders rubbed against wet leaves.

Nera met it with a spear.

She was Silver and deserved it.

No flourish.

One step sideways, shaft under the jaw, point driving through the softer seam behind the foreleg as the thing turned.

The hound hit the ground hard enough to throw black dust.

The second came at Pellin.

Pellin was Bronze, twenty-something, carrying a shield large enough to suggest unresolved fear.

He set it correctly.

The hound hit him anyway.

Correct did not mean comfortable.

He went backward three feet, boots digging twin trenches in the wet road.

I put a plane behind the bottom of his shield.

Tiny.

Angled.

The impact spread through the shape and into my skull.

Pellin stopped moving.

His eyes widened.

"Now," I said.

He stabbed over the rim.

Missed.

Nera was already moving.

Her spear went into the hound's flank.

It twisted toward her.

Pellin hit it again.

That one landed.

Two down.

The brush went quiet.

Nobody celebrated.

Good team.

Nera pulled her spear free.

"Third."

The contract runner had found tracks for two.

We had found three sets at the stream.

The third set was smaller.

Smaller did not mean child.

Ash hounds did not organize family outings according to human sentiment.

I listened.

Rain on leaves.

Pellin breathing too hard.

A cart horse thirty yards behind us stamping against its traces.

The driver whispering something religious.

No hound.

I looked at the road.

Mud.

Two bodies.

The first hound's track came from the brush.

The second had crossed lower.

The third set at the stream had been behind both.

Not smaller.

Narrower because it walked in their prints.

"Back," I said.

Nera's head moved toward me.

"Where?"

"Cart."

The horse screamed.

The third ash hound came out behind us.

Not from the ridge.

From the ditch beside the cart.

It had circled while the first two made noise.

Old behavior.

I remembered a larger version of the same species in the south woods forty years from now.

Or thirty-eight.

Or somebody telling me about it over beer.

No time to authenticate the archive.

The hound went for the horse.

If it reached the traces, we got a dead horse, an uncontrolled cart, and possibly a driver beneath both.

Nera was too far.

Pellin turned the wrong direction first.

I was closest.

Unfortunate.

I drew my sword.

It felt exactly as unimpressive as yesterday.

The hound accelerated.

I could not make a barrier strong enough to stop it.

I didn't try.

Its front right paw came down.

I put a plane under the outside half of the paw.

Not wall.

Wedge.

The hound's own weight did the rest.

Its foot landed one inch higher on one side than the other.

At a walk, nothing.

At a sprint, disaster.

The leg folded.

The hound went sideways into the cart wheel.

Wood cracked.

The horse kicked.

The driver screamed.

I stepped in because apparently surviving nineteen had not improved judgment as much as advertised.

The hound snapped from the ground.

I knew exactly how far its head could reach.

My body did not.

I was an inch late.

Teeth caught my coat and tore cloth instead of stomach.

Excellent tailoring.

I stabbed down.

Bad angle.

The blade entered shoulder and stopped.

The hound twisted.

My wrist nearly went with it.

Then Nera's spear punched through the base of its skull.

The animal went still.

I remained attached to my sword.

Barely.

Nera stood over it breathing through her nose.

"You knew it was behind us."

"Suspected."

"You said back before the horse screamed."

"Strong suspicion."

Pellin arrived.

Looked at the third hound.

Looked at my torn coat.

"What did you do to its leg?"

"Made the ground slightly worse."

He waited.

"Magic."

"I gathered that."

Nera crouched by the paw.

No wound.

Nothing dramatic.

Mud on the claws.

"Again," she said.

"What?"

"Do that again."

"I need another charging ash hound."

"No."

"Then difficult to reproduce."

She stood.

"At the Guild. Sand yard."

Ah.

Evaluation.

Less fun than monsters.

The cart wheel had lost two spokes.

The driver wanted to continue anyway.

Nera told him no.

He argued.

She pointed at the wheel.

He argued more.

I sat on a roadside stone and checked my coat.

The tear ran from my left hip nearly to the pocket.

Mara's latest letter had been in that pocket yesterday.

Today it held half a heel of bread.

The bread had survived.

Priorities again.

We walked the cart back to Carrow at half speed.

The Guild paid the contract plus a hound-count adjustment.

Three instead of two.

Pellin wanted drinks.

I wanted a tailor.

Nera caught me before I left the hall.

"Tomorrow. Third bell."

"Sand yard?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because if you can do that on purpose, I want to know what else you can do on purpose."

That was a dangerous question.

I had spent forty years answering it badly.

"Not much," I said.

Nera looked at the three ash-hound tags on the clerk's desk.

"We'll see."

The tailor charged five copper.

I argued him down to four by pointing out that the hound had supplied most of the opening already.

He did not appreciate the logic.

My total profit from clever battlefield judgment became eleven copper and a coat with thread that did not match.

At dinner, Noll stared at the repair.

"You fought something."

"Technically."

"Win?"

"The tailor did."

He nodded.

That made sense to him.
