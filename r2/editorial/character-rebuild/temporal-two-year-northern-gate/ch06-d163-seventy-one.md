# D163: Seventy-One

Iven Marr became a master mason on a Tuesday and got drunk enough to forget he hated adventurers.

This lasted approximately forty minutes.

"You're all thieves," he told Nera.

Nera lifted her cup.

"Congratulations."

"Not you specifically."

"Less meaningful."

We were at a tavern called the Red Rooster because Iven's wife had forbidden the celebration from happening in their kitchen.

I had not known Iven had a wife until that afternoon.

This seemed like information a person should mention sometime before asking another person to spend four months inside drains with him.

Her name was Vessa.

She was shorter than Iven, louder than Iven, and apparently responsible for the fact that he owned shirts without mortar on them.

Their daughter, Rell, was nine and had spent the first half hour trying to determine whether Nera had ever killed a troll.

Nera had not.

Rell did not believe her.

Iven's practical score was eighty-four.

The written seventy-one remained framed in his workshop because spite was an important professional resource.

"Seventy-one," he said now, pointing at me with his drink. "One point is enough."

"You've explained."

"People think eighty is better."

"Mathematically."

"Passed is passed."

"Also mathematically."

He frowned.

"Don't ruin this."

Vessa put a plate of fried potatoes between us.

"He has been saying this since noon."

"I earned it."

"You earned the certificate. The speech appears free."

I liked her too.

Rell had abandoned Nera and moved to me.

"Papa says you hold rocks in the air."

"Your father lies."

Iven pointed again.

"Structural assistance."

"See?"

Rell narrowed her eyes.

"Show me."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because your father will charge me for the table."

"Would," Iven said.

Vessa nodded.

Good family agreement.

I had known Iven for five months.

Until tonight I knew his examination score, preferred mortar mix, opinion of city procurement, and the exact sound he made when somebody handed him a bent measuring pin.

I had not known Rell existed.

That bothered me.

Not because Iven owed me a family inventory.

Because I kept doing this.

Remembering people by what they did near me.

Nera was Silver, spear, good judgment.

Iven was stone, drain, handwriting grievance.

Mara had spent half a year teaching me that people continued existing outside the room.

Apparently the lesson required maintenance.

Rell stole one of my potatoes.

That helped.

"Thief."

"Adventurer," she said.

Iven nearly choked.

Vessa laughed hard enough to sit down.

I surrendered the potato.

Later, when Rell had been taken home because nine-year-olds apparently lacked professional drinking stamina, Iven slid into the empty chair beside me.

Less drunk now.

Or more carefully drunk.

"City offered North District."

I looked at him.

"Your own crew?"

"Small one. Maintenance first. Walls, drains, two gatehouses."

Northern Gate.

I did not say it.

"You taking it?"

"Vessa says yes."

"So yes."

"Probably."

I stared.

He stared back.

Then smiled.

"Excellent word."

Corruption spread quickly.

"Better pay?"

"A little."

"More paperwork?"

"A lot."

"Rell will be proud."

"Rell thinks I fight buildings."

"Accurate."

Iven looked across the tavern at Nera arguing with an off-duty guard about spear length.

"You still doing city work?"

"When it pays."

"That's not what I asked."

"Then yes."

"I might need somebody for inspections. Your weird little planes save time."

"They cost extra now."

"Why?"

"Professional growth."

"You're Bronze."

"Rude."

"True."

Both.

I had done seven contracts with Nera since the ash hounds.

Marsh beetles.

Road escort.

A cellar mimic that was actually a cellar mimic, which felt almost nostalgic.

Two days hunting a mud-backed thing whose Guild name I had forgotten before we killed it.

Nera had submitted my name for Silver review once.

The Guild had returned it with `insufficient field breadth.`

Reasonable.

I hated them for perhaps an hour.

Then took a warehouse ward job for eight copper because resentment did not cover lunch.

My mana had improved.

Not enormously.

The angled planes were cleaner now.

I could make three small surfaces without one becoming embarrassed and disappearing.

My sword remained average.

My feet had stopped trying to cross each other during side steps.

Mostly.

I had nineteen silver and thirty-one copper saved.

That sounded like money until I priced a better sword.

The sword remained at the shop.

Mara had spent three weeks in Dell and come back to Halden with a promotion in responsibility and no change in title.

She complained about this across six pages.

We had exchanged nine letters.

Ten if the one currently somewhere between Carrow and Halden survived the river.

She had kissed a line of ink onto the bottom of the last one by accident after dropping her pen.

I had stared at it long enough to become stupid.

Then folded it away.

Life was going well.

Not spectacularly.

Well.

This made me suspicious in an entirely different way.

Iven knocked his cup against mine.

"North District."

"Congratulations."

"You coming Monday?"

"For what?"

"Drain survey."

"No."

"Fourteen copper."

"What time?"

He laughed.

Nera returned to the table.

"He recruit you?"

"Temporary structural assistance."

"That's not a job."

"Twelve copper normally. Fourteen now."

"That's barely a job."

"You pay me twenty-two to get bitten."

"You don't usually get bitten."

"Coat disagrees."

Iven lifted his cup.

"Thieves."

Nera lifted hers.

"Masons."

I lifted mine.

"Employment."

Nobody toasted that.

Cowards.

I got back to Lower Rook after midnight.

Maud had left a lamp burning in the hall and a note under it.

**RAIL FIXED. DO NOT TEST IT.**

I tested it.

Solid.

Upstairs, Mara's latest letter waited where I had left it.

Twenty months remained until Halden.

I did not count the days.

Months were respectable.

I put Iven's celebration ribbon on the table beside the letter.

Then went to sleep because North District apparently required drains on Mondays too.
