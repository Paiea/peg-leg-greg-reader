# D252: Silver

The Silver examination confirmed three things.

My judgment had survived forty years better than my legs.

My barrier control was far ahead of my reserve.

And Examiner Holt enjoyed making people repeat things.

"Again," he said.

I reset my stance.

The practice exchange was not going well enough for vanity and not badly enough for despair.

Holt was older, heavier, and very good at making ordinary movement inconvenient.

I read his first approach correctly and still moved too late.

The second time I moved early and gave away the answer.

On the third, I stopped trying to make my nineteen-year-old body reproduce someone else's forty-year-old timing.

I put a small force plane behind my rear heel.

Holt expected another half step.

I stopped sooner.

His practice blade passed my shoulder.

Mine touched his chest.

He looked down.

Then at my foot.

"That legal?"

The other examiner shrugged.

"Magic allowed."

"Annoying," Holt said.

"Thank you."

The field portion went better.

They gave me a road problem on paper. Bad weather, damaged wagon, injured porter, uncertain tracks near the next crossing.

"Turn back," I said.

The examiner looked up.

"Immediately?"

"No. That's the default unless you give me a reason it isn't."

She did.

Then I asked enough questions to annoy her properly.

Distance to the dry junction.

What cargo mattered.

What could be carried without the wagon.

Whether the tracks crossed our route or followed it.

Where the nearest shelter was.

She changed two facts halfway through.

I changed the plan.

That was the point.

The magic portion remained rude.

The Guild reader checked my reserve twice.

"Low," she said.

"I've noticed."

"Control is excellent."

"Less rude."

"Three split planes?"

"Briefly."

"Two while moving?"

"Reliably."

"Anchor one to the floor."

They gave me a weighted practice frame.

First attempt failed.

Second held badly.

Third time I stopped carrying the whole load through myself and gave the force somewhere else to go.

The little plane stayed clean.

The frame stopped shifting.

The pressure behind my eyes remained manageable.

The reader wrote something down.

"Again."

Apparently Holt had founded a religion.

At sunset they sent me into the hall.

Nera waited with tea.

"Well?"

"I have been assessed thoroughly."

"That's not an answer."

"Then I fit right in."

The examination door opened.

Holt came out with a dull silver Guild token in one hand.

I stopped talking.

He held it toward me.

I took it.

Cold metal.

Almost the same weight as Bronze.

Not the same thing.

"Provisional Silver road and support scope for thirty days," Holt said. "No independent upper-hazard contracts without review. Your field breadth is enough. Your reserve still isn't."

"Fair."

"Three votes to one."

"Who voted no?"

He smiled.

"Silver behavior starts tomorrow."

Then he left.

Nera looked at the token.

"Drink?"

"I have tea."

"A drink."

We compromised badly.

At Lower Rook, Maud noticed the token before I reached the stairs.

"Silver?"

"Apparently."

"Rent still due Friday."

There it was.

Continuity.

"I thought rank came with housing."

"It comes with stairs."

"Bronze had stairs."

"Same stairs."

Terrible advancement system.

My savings had climbed over thirty silver and then fallen again because boots, examination fees, and food remained loyal to their professions.

Twenty-four silver and change.

Better than spring.

Not wealth.

I put the old Bronze token in the tin beneath my bed and set the Silver one beside Mara's latest letter.

Her first line read:

**Do not become Gold before I see you or I'll assume fraud.**

I wrote back:

**Silver. Barely. Rent unchanged. Fraud unsuccessful.**

Then I lay down.

The new rank opened better work in the morning.

My body remained exactly the one that had taken three tries to anchor a practice frame.

Both facts could stay.
