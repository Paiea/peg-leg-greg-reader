# D331: Northern Gate

The alarm bell changed the city.

People who had been complaining started moving.

Guards stopped explaining.

Shopkeepers stopped asking whether they could get one more crate.

The sound rolled down North Street in repeated iron blows and came back from the roofs as something larger.

Northern Gate answered with stone.

A strip of facing broke loose from the east tower.

It fell thirty feet and shattered where a vegetable cart had been ten minutes earlier.

Nobody screamed.

Not there.

The market had cleared.

That fact landed harder than the stone.

"Sella!" Beret shouted.

"Five!"

Five minutes to isolate the wardline.

Too long.

The east tower moved.

I saw it because a rain gutter stopped hanging straight.

Maybe an inch.

Maybe less.

Enough.

"Everybody off the north approach," I said.

Iven grabbed a city worker by the back of his coat and shoved him south.

Beret did not ask why this time.

"Move! Tanner Lane line! Nobody north of it!"

The guards repeated her.

People moved.

A Guild horn sounded downhill.

Response teams.

Good.

The tower groaned.

Not one sound.

Dozens.

Stone joints opening one after another too quickly to count.

I had heard fortifications fail before.

Not this fortification.

Not this body.

The knowledge arrived anyway.

Do not watch the falling thing.

Watch what it is attached to.

The east tower leaned inward.

The arch took the load.

The arch pushed west.

West tower had new piers.

Strong.

Stronger than the old connection between tower and wall.

Shit.

"West wall!" I shouted.

Beret looked.

Saw it.

"Clear the inside west corner!"

Two guards were still there moving a barricade.

They abandoned it.

Good men.

Good decision.

The arch cracked.

This one was loud.

A white line ran through the old stone above the gate opening.

The city ward flared blue along it.

Sella swore.

"It's pulling!"

"Drop it!" I yelled.

She stared at me.

"The whole north line?"

"Drop the gate anchor!"

"It'll arc!"

"Into what?"

She looked up.

West cap.

Still live.

Still connected.

Too slow.

I remembered a support captain named Den with half his face burned because somebody had tried to preserve a ward network during a wall breach instead of letting one section die.

Different city.

Different war.

Same mistake.

"Kill the gate anchor," I said. "Let the wall lose it."

Sella moved.

No argument.

That scared me almost as much as the tower.

She drove both hands against the wardstone.

Blue light spread across her wrists.

The carved line in the arch brightened.

Then went black.

Every hair on my arms lifted.

A flash ran west across the wall.

Not lightning.

Close enough that the distinction became private.

The west tower cap discharged in a sheet of blue-white force over the empty market.

Three awnings tore off their frames.

A shutter crossed the street sideways.

One guard went down.

Then the ward went dark.

Sella dropped to one knee.

Alive.

Good.

The arch failed.

The middle third did not fall immediately.

It sagged.

For one impossible second several tons of stone hung in a shape that no longer knew why it was an arch.

Then gravity remembered.

"Down!"

I was already moving.

Iven hit the ground beside me.

Beret went behind a watering trough.

Stone came down.

The sound was not one crash.

It was weather.

Blocks striking road.

Timbers snapping inside the towers.

Roof tiles becoming shrapnel.

The east tower rotated toward the opening as its footing vanished beneath it.

The upper room peeled open.

A desk fell out.

A chair.

A stack of inspection boards.

Then half the tower followed.

Dust erased the gate.

I held my breath.

Bad idea.

Dust entered anyway.

Something hit my back.

Small stone.

Pain, but not important yet.

I kept my head down until the first wave passed.

Then stood.

Couldn't see twenty feet.

Couldn't hear properly.

Bell still ringing somewhere.

Or my head.

"Iven!"

"Here!"

Close.

"Beret?"

No answer.

"Beret!"

"Stop shouting."

Also close.

Good.

Sella coughed to my right.

Four known alive.

The dust thinned.

Northern Gate was gone.

Not all of it.

The west tower still stood to the second floor, split open along the inner face. The east tower had collapsed into the approach and taken most of the arch with it. The outer road disappeared beneath a mound of stone high enough to reach a man's chest in places.

The connection to the west wall had torn exactly where we feared.

A thirty-foot section of parapet had come down inside the city.

Where the two guards had been.

Where they were not now.

My legs weakened for half a second.

Not injury.

Arithmetic.

Ten minutes later and the vegetable cart would have been there.

Six blocks of market behind it.

I did not finish counting.

Movement in the dust.

A man staggered toward us bleeding from one ear.

Not ours.

Caravan driver caught outside the cleared line.

Iven went to him.

I looked downhill.

Guild teams were coming.

Nera at the front.

Silver spear strapped across her back because rescue work had different priorities.

Behind her came two healers, six adventurers, and a Gold I recognized by face but not name.

Good.

Power.

Use it.

Nera reached me.

"Where?"

Not what happened.

Where.

Good question.

I pointed.

"East approach first. Caravan driver says two wagons were outside the barrier. West interior second. Wall section came down but guards cleared. Keep everyone out of the arch footprint. It may continue rotating."

The Gold looked at me.

"You in charge?"

"No. Beret."

Beret had already found the guard captain.

Better.

"She's city structure. Sella's ward. I'm annoying."

Nera said, "Accurate."

The Gold almost smiled.

Then the remaining west tower made a cracking sound.

Everyone looked up.

Wrong.

"Move east!" I shouted.

Nera moved because she knew my voice now.

The others followed because Nera moved.

A corner stone dropped from the west tower and struck where the Gold had been standing.

He looked at the shattered block.

Then at me.

No time for introductions.

"Can you hold debris?" I asked.

"What kind?"

"The kind that kills people."

"Briefly."

"Good. Save it. Don't hold anything until someone is under it."

His eyebrows went up.

I had just ordered a Gold not to use magic.

Technically requested.

Poorly.

Beret arrived.

"Teams."

I shut up.

Mostly.

She divided the site.

East approach rescue under Nera.

West wall under the Gold, whose name turned out to be Cal Ven.

Sella and two warders isolating damaged line sections.

Iven with city workers assessing safe lanes.

He pointed at me.

"With me."

I went.

For the next hour, Northern Gate became smaller pieces.

Not because the disaster shrank.

Because nobody could work on a disaster.

You worked on a hand under a beam.

A blocked doorway.

A wagon with somebody calling from beneath the canvas.

A wall that might stand for ten minutes or ten seconds.

A man who kept trying to walk on a broken ankle because his brother was somewhere ahead.

Specific things.

Specific things could be done.

At the first wagon, a block pinned the rear axle and trapped the driver by his coat.

He was not injured badly enough to justify the noise he made.

Good.

Noise meant lungs.

Cal wanted to lift the whole block.

"One corner," I said.

He looked at me again.

"Why?"

"Whole block shifts the axle. Axle shifts wagon. Wagon is on him."

He looked once.

Saw it.

"Corner."

His magic lifted three inches of stone as if weight had become negotiable.

Iven cut the trapped coat.

Driver out.

Next.

One alive.

Two not.

No names I knew.

We marked the wagon and moved because the living woman in the ditch took priority.

This was not cruelty.

It felt like cruelty anyway.

At the west wall, a guard was trapped beneath parapet timber.

Nera's team had exposed him but the remaining wall leaned over the lane.

"We can hold it," Cal said.

"For how long?"

"Long enough."

Bad answer.

It made better uncertainty available.

Not to hold it.

To listen.

Steady.

Rainwater still moving under rubble.

"No rescue?"

"Not from this side. Wall's walking."

"Hey."

I loved her for not making the sentence complicated.

Knocked a hole in the back wall.

Took twelve extra minutes.

Cal watched it hit the empty lane.

"Who are you?"

I was carrying the guard's boot because it had come off during extraction.

"Greg."

"Rank?"

"Silver."

Nera nodded.

"Unfortunately."

Persistent.

"Vessa will kill me."

Small sound.

Alive sound.

"You did."

"It still fell."

"Yes."

No comfort theater.

Good.

"East cellar. They heard tapping."

History could wait.
