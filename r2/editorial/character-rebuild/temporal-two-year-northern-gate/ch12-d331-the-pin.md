# D331: The Pin

The tapping came from beneath a cooper's storeroom.

The cooper was standing in the rain insisting the building had never had a cellar.

Iven stared at him.

"Then somebody is trapped under a floor for recreational reasons."

The cooper looked offended.

Beret pointed south.

"Go behind the line."

He went.

Professional authority had improved dramatically since morning.

The storeroom sat against the east approach where falling stone had punched through the roof and dropped half the second floor into the ground level. The front wall still stood. The back had leaned into the alley and stopped against the neighboring building.

Not safe.

Nothing was safe anymore.

Safe enough had become the useful category.

Iven crawled to the broken doorway and listened.

Tap.

Tap tap.

Pause.

Tap.

"Two?" I asked.

"Maybe."

"Bad word."

He looked back at me.

Dust, rain, blood dried at one temple from a cut I had not seen happen.

"Excellent word."

I hated him a little for that.

Beret sent for a rescue frame.

Nera arrived with two Guild hands while we waited.

One was Pellin from the ash-hound road.

He looked older than D071.

Probably I did too.

"What do you need?" Nera asked.

Iven pointed.

"Access through side wall. Don't touch the front lintel."

Nera did not touch the front lintel.

Beautiful woman.

Not romantically.

Procedurally.

We opened a hole through the neighboring warehouse instead.

Brick by brick at first.

Then Nera used the butt of her spear where the mortar had already cracked.

Pellin cleared debris.

I used little planes to catch loose pieces long enough for hands to move.

Not impressive magic.

Useful magic.

The tapping stopped.

Everyone froze.

Then came three quick knocks.

Good.

Alive.

The rescue frame arrived.

Wood A-frame, iron screw jack, chain, rated for more weight than anything we intended to ask from it.

A city rigger named Sol checked every connection before allowing us to bring it inside.

Then checked them again after we moved it.

Good.

The trapped space was lower than expected.

The cellar did exist.

The cooper had apparently owned the building for eleven years without discovering stairs beneath shelving.

Old cities.

A floor beam had fallen across the stair opening.

Below it, someone called weakly.

"Two of us."

Woman's voice.

Iven leaned toward the gap.

"Names?"

"Eris. Tom."

"Injuries?"

Pause.

"Tom's arm. I'm stuck at the ankle."

"Anything on your chest or neck?"

"No."

"Can you breathe normally?"

"I was before you started asking questions."

Iven smiled.

"Good. Keep doing that."

We rigged the beam.

Sol inspected where the chain bit into the wood.

Iven inspected the floor around it.

I put a force plane against the beam's lower face and listened.

Most of the load went down through broken joists toward the cellar wall.

Some pulled sideways into the leaning front wall.

"Lift only enough for the stairs," I said.

Sol nodded.

"Finger width at a time."

"Stop if I say stop."

"Same."

No ego.

Good crew.

The jack turned.

Iron clicked.

Beam rose.

One finger.

Two.

I held the sideways load with a plane the size of a plate.

Pressure behind my eyes.

Manageable.

Pellin slid a hardwood block beneath the beam.

Jack relaxed onto it.

Stable.

Again.

Second block.

Stable.

We opened enough room for Nera to crawl under.

She disappeared down the stairs.

"Tom first," she called.

A young man emerged ten minutes later pale and shaking, one arm tied against his chest with a strip of somebody's shirt.

He tried to thank everyone.

Beret pushed him toward the healer.

One alive.

Eris was harder.

A stone slab pinned her boot and ankle against the cellar floor.

Not crushing badly, according to Nera.

Just enough that she could not pull free.

The slab was too large to shift through the opening.

We needed another four inches beneath the beam so Cal or another lifter could reach the cellar safely.

Cal was west-side, holding part of a wall while three people came out.

No one suggested waiting for him.

People had been waiting under stone all day.

We could do four inches.

Probably.

I did not say the word aloud.

Sol reset the chain.

Checked the screw jack.

"Pin seated," he said.

Iven checked it too.

Small iron retaining pin through the collar.

Thumb-thick.

Black with grease.

Ordinary object.

"Good," Iven said.

We lifted.

First inch.

Plane clean.

Second.

The leaning wall gave a tiny creak.

"Hold," I said.

Everyone stopped.

I listened through the plane.

Load steady.

Not getting worse.

"Continue."

Third inch.

Pellin set another block.

Fourth.

Enough.

"Stop. Block it."

Sol stopped turning.

Pellin reached for the cribbing.

The iron pin snapped.

It did not explode.

No dramatic spark.

A little metallic tick.

Smaller than the first stone that had fallen from Northern Gate.

The jack collar kicked sideways.

The rescue frame dropped one corner.

The beam moved perhaps eight inches.

Eight inches was enough.

Iven was inside the line.

So was I.

I saw him turn.

Saw Pellin drop the block and reach.

My force plane took the sideways load for less than a second.

Then something much larger found it.

The plane broke.

The world hit me from the left.

I went down.

No pain at first.

Just noise.

Wood.

People shouting.

Nera below yelling something.

I tried to stand.

My right leg moved.

My left did not.

That was bad.

I looked.

A stone support block and the end of the dropped beam had caught my lower left leg against the broken stair curb.

I could see my knee.

I could see my boot.

The distance between them had become structurally complicated.

I looked away.

Pain arrived.

Late.

Enormous.

It removed language for several seconds.

Somebody grabbed my shoulders.

Pellin.

"Don't move."

Excellent advice.

I laughed once.

Bad decision.

"Iven?"

Pellin's face changed.

No.

I tried to turn my head.

He stopped me.

"Greg."

"Iven."

No answer.

Behind Pellin, Sol was on his knees beside a shape half hidden by fallen timber.

I saw Iven's hand.

All five fingers.

Mortar still beneath one nail.

Not moving.

The pain in my leg became irrelevant for perhaps one second.

Then came back angry about being ignored.

Nera crawled up from the cellar.

Blood on one sleeve that did not appear to be hers.

"Eris is clear."

Good.

Someone alive.

She saw me.

Saw Iven.

Her face emptied.

Then work returned.

"Healer!"

Pellin shouted it too.

I put my head back against the wet floor.

Above us, rain tapped through the broken roof.

Small sound.

The Northern Gate had fallen around half the district.

The west wall had come down.

Wardlight had crossed the market like lightning.

I had spent the morning worrying about towers, arches, crowds, secondary collapse, and several tons of stone.

A pin had done it.

A fucking pin.

The healer reached me.

Hands.

Questions.

Could I feel this?

Yes.

This?

Yes.

This?

Less.

That was worse.

"Iven," I said.

Nobody answered.

I knew.

Fifty-nine-year-old me had known enough dead people to recognize the shape people made around one.

The healer cut my trouser leg.

Nera knelt near my head.

"Look at me."

"Don't want to."

"Do it anyway."

I looked.

"Eris?"

"Out. Tom too."

"Good."

"Yes."

"Iven?"

Nera's jaw tightened.

There it was.

"No."

One word.

No argument available.

The healer gave me something bitter.

I swallowed because pride was not currently load-bearing.

They had to lift the beam again.

Different jack.

Different frame.

Cal arrived and took most of the weight himself.

Gold magic.

The beam rose smoothly this time.

My leg came free.

Pain became white.

After that the day broke into pieces.

A stretcher.

Rain on my face.

North Street full of people behind ropes.

Someone saying my name who I did not know.

A healer asking what day it was.

D331.

I knew that one.

A cart ride that lasted forever.

Then a ceiling.

Clean ceiling.

No rain.

Somewhere before sleep took me, I remembered Rell standing in the Brass Spoon that morning.

**Tell him I did it.**

I had.

I had told him.

That was nothing.

That was everything.
