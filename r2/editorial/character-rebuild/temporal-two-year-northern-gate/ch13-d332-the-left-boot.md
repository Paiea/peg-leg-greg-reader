# D332: The Left Boot

I woke looking for my boot.

Not consciously.

My body did it first.

Right foot under blanket.

Left foot somewhere farther down.

Except it wasn't.

I knew before opening my eyes completely.

There are kinds of absence the body reports as presence.

My left foot hurt.

Specifically the heel.

Sharp pressure at the back, like a boot rubbed raw after a long road.

I moved it.

Nothing moved.

The heel still hurt.

I opened my eyes.

White ceiling.

City infirmary.

Window gray with rain.

Someone asleep in a chair beside the bed.

Noll.

His mouth hung open.

Good to know disaster had not improved him aesthetically.

I looked down.

Blanket.

Right leg making the expected shape.

Left leg ending much earlier.

Below the knee.

Wrapped.

Clean.

Small enough under the blanket that my brain refused it for perhaps three breaths.

Then accepted everything at once.

"Huh."

Noll woke so violently he nearly fell sideways.

"You're awake."

"Apparently."

He stood.

Sat again.

Stood.

"I'll get the healer."

"Wait."

He stopped.

I looked at the blanket.

"They took it."

Noll followed my eyes.

"Yeah."

His voice broke on one stupid little sound.

He hated that.

I pretended not to notice.

"Below knee?"

"Yeah."

Good.

Good was the wrong word.

Still useful.

I had a knee.

I remembered enough prosthetics from my first life to know the difference mattered.

I also remembered people who had lost more.

That information arrived professionally.

Emotion took longer.

"Iven?"

Noll sat down.

There it was again.

"No."

"I know."

"They brought him out before you."

I closed my eyes.

Not because I wanted sleep.

Because the ceiling was suddenly too involved.

Iven's hand under the timber.

All five fingers.

Seventy-one.

Rell running through rain.

Vessa laughing because her husband had apparently given a speech about passing scores for the hundredth time.

"Vessa knows?"

"Yes."

"Rell?"

Noll hesitated.

"Yes."

My missing heel hurt worse.

Ridiculous.

I rubbed my face.

"He was right there."

"I know."

"No."

Noll waited.

I did not know what I meant either.

The healer arrived before I had to find out.

Her name was Doctor Senna, although `doctor` here meant city surgical healer and not the version of the title I remembered from later years.

She was small, gray-haired, and looked at me like I had already attempted something stupid while unconscious.

"Pain?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

I almost said left heel.

Stopped.

Then said it anyway.

"Heel."

She nodded.

No surprise.

"Phantom sensation. Likely for some time. It may change."

"Good."

"Not necessarily."

"I meant good that I'm not insane."

"I haven't assessed that."

Noll made a noise that might have been a laugh.

Doctor Senna checked the bandaging.

Asked what I remembered.

I told her enough.

The pin.

Beam.

Pain.

Iven.

Then cart.

"We tried to preserve the lower leg," she said.

Plain voice.

I appreciated it.

"The crush destroyed too much of the blood supply. Healing magic can close vessels. It cannot replace long sections that no longer carry blood. By midnight the tissue below the injury was failing. Keeping it would have threatened the rest of you."

"So you took it."

"Yes."

"How much?"

She showed me with her hand.

Below the knee.

Enough length for a good fitting later if healing went well.

Later.

There was suddenly a later built around an object I did not have.

"When can I stand?"

Doctor Senna looked at me.

Noll looked at me.

"What?"

"You woke six minutes ago."

"Approximately."

"Today, you lie there."

"Tomorrow?"

"Tomorrow you probably also lie there."

Terrible treatment plan.

"Crutches?"

"Later."

"Define later."

"When I stop wanting to tie you to the bed."

Noll smiled.

Traitor.

She left after making me drink water and promising actual food if I kept it down.

Noll sat beside me again.

For a while neither of us spoke.

Rain ticked against the window.

My heel hurt.

My toes felt cramped.

I tried to uncurl them.

Nothing happened.

That was going to become annoying.

"How bad was the gate?" I asked.

Noll stared.

"You were there."

"After I left."

He rubbed both hands over his knees.

"They found eleven dead by morning."

Eleven.

The number entered the room and sat down.

"More?"

"Maybe. They think two missing are outside the city, not under it. Thirty-something hurt badly enough for infirmaries. Lots smaller."

Eleven.

If the market had been full.

I stopped that thought.

Not because it was false.

Because arithmetic with corpses became ugly quickly.

"Beret?"

"Alive."

"Sella?"

"Alive. Burned hand."

"Nera?"

"Alive. She was here."

"Cal?"

"Gold guy?"

"Yes."

"Alive. Apparently he held a wall for nine minutes and yelled at everyone who thanked him."

That sounded healthy.

"Mira?"

"Alive."

Good.

One after another.

Names surviving.

Then Iven.

No.

The city had lost eleven people.

I had lost one leg.

The comparison appeared without invitation.

Could have been worse.

True.

Completely true.

I hated it immediately.

I was alive.

I had a knee.

Eleven people had neither problem.

Iven's daughter had gone home without him.

All true.

My foot still wasn't there.

Also true.

Apparently facts were not required to resolve one another.

Noll reached toward the water cup.

"Want some?"

"No."

"You should."

"Senna recruit you?"

"She threatened me."

"Reasonable."

He handed me the cup.

I drank.

Then looked down at the blanket again.

The left side still ended early.

No revelation arrived.

No gratitude.

No screaming either.

Just a bed, rain, Noll's terrible chair posture, and a heel that hurt despite no longer existing.

"My boot?" I asked.

Noll went very still.

"What?"

"The left one. New boots. Cost me eight silver."

For one second he looked horrified.

Then he laughed.

Not politely.

Bent forward, hands over his face, shoulders shaking.

I started laughing too.

It hurt somewhere in my side.

I stopped.

Noll did not.

"Asshole," he managed.

"Eight silver."

"Your leg is gone."

"Which makes the boot difficult to amortize."

That made it worse.

A nurse looked in, judged us both, and left.

When the laughter ended, the room became quiet again.

I looked at my missing left foot.

Felt the heel.

Thought of Iven.

Could have been worse.

Yes.

Still bad.

Both could stay.
