# D341: Marr

Doctor Senna said no to the funeral three times.

The fourth time she said, "Two hours. Chair. No heroics."

"I don't know what heroics look like in a chair."

"You will discover them if I underestimate you."

Fair.

Noll borrowed a wheeled invalid chair from the infirmary and immediately drove one wheel into a doorframe.

"Excellent start."

"You want to push?"

I looked down at the blanket over my lap.

"Currently difficult."

He apologized with his face.

I pretended not to see that too.

We went slowly.

Carrow had changed in ten days.

Not physically enough.

The rain had stopped.

North Street was still closed above Tanner Lane. Wagons rerouted east. Broken roof tiles had been swept from the lower streets. The city had hung black-and-white ribbons from public lamps for the dead.

Eleven ribbons at the Guild.

I counted.

Of course I did.

People recognized me.

That was new.

A woman outside a bakery looked at the chair, then at my face.

"Gate," she said quietly to the man beside her.

I heard it.

Noll heard it too.

Neither of us reacted.

At the next corner, an old man touched two fingers to his brow as we passed.

I looked behind me to see who he meant.

Nobody.

Uncomfortable.

Iven was buried in the north stoneworkers' ground because masons apparently preferred soil near things they had built.

That sounded like something Iven would have mocked.

The turnout was larger than I expected.

North District workers.

City clerks.

Beret Halm.

Mira.

Torr, Pela, Senn, Ors.

Nera stood near the back.

Cal Ven too, Gold token mostly hidden under his coat.

People I did not know.

That was the point.

Iven had known them without me.

Vessa stood beside Rell near the grave.

I had spent the entire ride deciding what to say to her.

Every version became terrible before we arrived.

I'm sorry.

True.

I was there.

Worse.

He died helping people.

Probably true and somehow unbearable.

It could have been me.

Never.

Vessa solved the problem by seeing me first.

She crossed the grass.

I tried to stand.

Forgot.

Actually forgot.

My hands went to the chair arms and my body prepared to put two feet under me.

Nothing happened except a burst of phantom pain through the missing left heel and Senna's voice in my memory calling me an idiot.

Vessa stopped in front of me.

Her eyes went down once.

Back to my face.

"Don't."

"Wasn't."

"Liar."

Apparently marriage transferred vocabulary.

I looked at her.

"Vessa."

That was all I had.

Her mouth tightened.

Then she leaned down and put both arms around me.

Not gentle enough for an invalid.

Good.

I held her.

She shook once.

Then twice.

Then stopped before I did.

When she stood, she wiped her face angrily.

"He liked you."

"I liked him."

"He complained about you constantly."

"Yes."

"That's how I know."

I almost laughed.

Did not.

Rell stayed where she was.

Watching.

Nine years old.

New boots dirty with cemetery mud.

I went to her after the service.

Noll pushed me close and then wandered away with enough theatrical casualness to deserve violence later.

Rell looked at the blanket.

"Your leg is gone."

Children remained efficient.

"Yes."

"Because the gate?"

"Because a jack broke while we were getting people out."

"Papa too?"

There it was.

"Same room."

She looked at the grave.

A fresh mound of dark earth.

No marker yet.

"Did I bring you fast enough?"

My throat closed.

Wrong question.

Not wrong for her.

"Yes."

"Really?"

"Rell. You found me at breakfast. We closed the gate. We got the market out. You were fast enough."

She stared at me like she could detect mercy if I added any.

I did not add any.

"Did you tell Papa I did it?"

"Yes."

"Before?"

I knew what she meant.

Before he died.

"Yes."

Her shoulders dropped a fraction.

"Good."

Nothing fixed.

One thing answered.

She reached into her coat and took out a pencil.

New.

Unchewed.

"Mira gave these to us."

"Dangerous."

Rell looked offended.

"Why?"

"Your father ate pencils."

She laughed.

One sound.

Then cried immediately afterward.

I sat there because there was nowhere useful to go.

She cried standing up.

I cried enough that I had to pretend my eyes disliked wind.

Nobody believed me.

At the end, each North District worker placed a small piece of clean stone beside the grave.

Not on it.

Beside.

Mira explained that city masons did it when one of theirs died on a work site.

The stones would be used in the marker later.

Not symbolic stone from the Gate.

Just sound material selected by people who knew the difference.

That felt like Iven.

Vessa put his wooden measuring wedge with the stones for perhaps ten seconds.

Then took it back.

Also felt like Iven.

On the way out, Beret stopped beside my chair.

"City council wants your statement tomorrow."

"About the jack?"

"Everything. Gate closure. Ward isolation. Market clear. Rescue sequence."

"I'll give it."

She nodded.

Then did not leave.

"Greg."

"What?"

"The north market was almost full when we closed it."

I knew.

"Yes."

"They've counted eleven dead."

Also knew.

"Yes."

"If we'd only cleared the frontage..."

"Don't."

She stopped.

Good.

I did not want the alternate arithmetic.

Not at Iven's grave.

Maybe not ever.

Beret looked at me a long second.

"Fine. Tomorrow."

She left.

Noll returned to push the chair.

At the cemetery gate, another stranger recognized me.

This time a man in a butcher's apron.

He stepped aside and said, "Thank you."

I did not know what to do with that.

"You're welcome" sounded like I had performed a service he ordered.

"It wasn't just me" sounded like refusal.

So I nodded.

He nodded back.

Noll pushed me toward the infirmary.

Halfway there, my left foot began itching.

The missing one.

I scratched the blanket uselessly.

Noll noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You look angry."

"My foot itches."

He looked down.

Then wisely looked forward again.

"Don't."

"Wasn't going to."

"Good."

For once he actually listened.
