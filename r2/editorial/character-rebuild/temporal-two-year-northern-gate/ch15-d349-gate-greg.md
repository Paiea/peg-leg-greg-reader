# D349: Gate Greg

The first person to call me Gate Greg was a nurse.

I disliked her immediately.

"Gate Greg needs to sit down."

"Gate Greg is standing."

"Poorly."

That was true.

I had two crutches, one right leg, one left knee, and an unreasonable amount of sweat for somebody who had crossed nine feet of infirmary floor.

Doctor Senna stood behind me with her arms folded.

No sympathy.

Good.

"Again," she said.

Everyone had joined the religion.

I shifted both crutches.

Moved my right foot.

My body tried to move the missing left foot next.

Nothing happened.

Balance went somewhere else.

I caught it late.

The nurse caught my shoulder.

"Sit."

"I had it."

"You had the floor."

"Reliable object."

I sat.

My hands hurt more than expected.

Not enough callus in the right places.

My shoulders had started objecting too.

I had spent years in my first life watching injured adventurers discover that mobility aids merely moved work into different body parts.

Knowing this did nothing useful for my palms.

The nurse handed me water.

"Again tomorrow, Gate Greg."

"Stop calling me that."

She smiled.

"No."

Renown.

Wonderful.

It had taken less than three weeks for Carrow to build a version of Northern Gate that I barely recognized.

In one version, I had known the exact hour the gate would fall and personally ordered the entire market evacuated.

False.

In another, I held the west tower upright while thirty children escaped.

Very false.

One broadsheet claimed I had `severed the failing wardline with a single barrier stroke.`

I did not know what that meant.

Sella bought three copies because she thought it was funny.

The actual city inquiry was less dramatic.

Beret said I recognized the need for broader evacuation before visible failure.

Sella said I identified the danger of leaving the gate anchor loaded while the structure moved.

Nera said I repeatedly identified secondary collapse lanes during rescue.

Cal Ven, Gold adventurer, gave a statement that apparently mattered more because his token was a different color.

He said, **When Greg tells you a wall is moving, move first and ask after.**

That sentence escaped the inquiry room and ruined my life.

Two days later a baker sent bread to the infirmary.

Then a north-market spice seller sent a jar of something expensive enough that I gave it to Senna before I could become accustomed to quality.

Three taverns offered free drinks.

Maud Pell came to the infirmary specifically to inform me that free drinks were not legal tender.

"Rent," she said.

"I nearly died."

"You didn't."

"Excellent compassion."

"City paying you?"

That was complicated.

The city covered infirmary costs directly tied to the Gate response.

Good.

It also issued seventeen silver in emergency-service pay and injury compensation.

Better.

My Guild savings had been twenty-four silver before the collapse.

Also good.

Then I stopped working.

Bad.

Rent continued.

Food continued.

Replacement clothing continued.

Crutches technically belonged to the infirmary.

A proper walking limb later would cost enough that nobody gave me a number without first asking whether I preferred wood, laminated horn, or metal fittings.

I preferred not needing one.

That option had poor availability.

The city money bought time.

Not a new life.

Maud approved of this analysis.

"Four weeks," she said.

"What?"

"Your room. Four weeks at half rent while you figure stairs."

I stared at her.

"Why?"

"Because I'm kind."

"No."

"Because you're famous and I can charge visitors."

"Also no."

She looked away.

There it was.

Kindness hiding under rent collection.

Disgusting.

"Thank you."

"Don't make it emotional."

"Wouldn't dream of it."

She left.

The next visitor was Nera.

She brought contracts.

I stared at the stack.

"Cruel."

"Thought you'd enjoy suffering."

She put them on my blanket.

Three slips.

All Silver.

All addressed specifically enough that my name appeared in the request line.

**Support preferred: Greg, Northern Gate.**

One road survey.

One warehouse reinforcement job.

One three-day ruin approach with a team I recognized and had never met.

"This is stupid."

"Yes."

"I can't take any of these."

"Yes."

"Why show me?"

Nera sat in the chair.

"Because two months ago you were complaining nobody knew what you did."

"I was not."

"You were complaining people only hired you as temporary structural assistance."

"That is a noble profession."

"Now people know."

I looked at the slips.

My name.

Not Nera's support.

Not Marr's little mage.

Greg.

Northern Gate.

I hated how good that felt.

I hated that I couldn't stand long enough to walk to the Guild desk.

Both feelings remained rude.

"Rank?" I asked.

"Still Silver."

"Good."

Nera raised an eyebrow.

"Actually?"

"Yes. I didn't become magically stronger because a building fell."

"Your control report from the rescue is absurd."

"My reserve is still small."

"Cal wants to test you when you're mobile."

"For what?"

"He said, and I quote, `I want to know why a Silver saw three things my team didn't.`"

That was dangerous.

Not bad.

Dangerous.

"Later."

"Obviously."

Nera tapped my missing-leg side through the blanket with one finger.

Not the end.

The knee.

"You're busy."

I slapped her hand away.

"Asshole."

"Gate Greg."

"Leave."

She laughed.

Then her face changed.

"Mara sent something."

Everything in me became more attentive.

Nera handed over a Vey express envelope.

Blue seal.

Two forwarding marks.

Opened once by city dispatch and resealed because disaster apparently had paperwork even for romance.

Mara's handwriting filled both sides of one sheet.

No greeting.

**I found out from Pell before your letter reached me, which is an excellent system and I hate everyone.**

Good start.

She had asked Vey for emergency leave.

Denied.

Not because Vey hated love.

Because her route counterpart was already in Dell with fever and the east reconciliation desk had exactly two people who could sign current freight corrections.

Mara had written three paragraphs about this, two of them angry.

Then:

**I can be in Carrow on D381 with the south packet if nothing changes. That is the earliest route Pell will approve without breaking the office. I want to come now. I also know you will tell me not to burn my job down. I resent that you are correct before you've even answered.**

I read it twice.

D381.

Thirty-two days.

Long.

Not two years.

At the bottom:

**Are we still doing Halden? Do not answer because you think I need reassurance. Answer later when you are less drugged and more annoying.**

I laughed.

Nera waited.

"Good?"

"Complicated."

"Dangerous answer."

"Very."

I folded the letter carefully.

My missing heel started hurting again.

Of course.

Someone knocked at the infirmary door.

A young Guild runner leaned in.

"Greg?"

"Depends."

"They said Gate Greg."

Nera made a choking sound.

I closed my eyes.

Local renown was going extremely well.
