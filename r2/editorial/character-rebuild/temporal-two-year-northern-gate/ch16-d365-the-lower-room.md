# D365: The Lower Room

Maud Pell put me in the old account room.

This was not generosity.

She explained that immediately.

"Temporary."

"Of course."

"Four weeks."

"You said half rent."

"For your room upstairs. This is storage."

I looked around.

The account room had one narrow bed, one chair, six empty shelves, and a desk with a burn mark shaped like a continent nobody had discovered.

"Storage has improved."

"Noll moved the bed."

"Ah."

"Jorren moved the desk."

"Explains the burn."

"Burn was there."

"Then history."

Maud pointed at the door.

"Privy is twelve steps. Kitchen is twenty-two. No stairs unless somebody is with you."

"I can do stairs."

She looked at my crutches.

Then at me.

"No stairs unless somebody is with you."

"Tyranny."

"Rent due Friday."

She left.

Home.

Apparently.

Doctor Senna had discharged me that morning after thirty-four days in the infirmary.

Not healed.

Healed enough to become somebody else's problem.

The end of my left leg remained wrapped and tender. Swelling changed by the hour. The skin pulled strangely when I moved my knee. Phantom sensations had become less constant and more creative.

Some mornings the missing foot felt cold.

Sometimes cramped.

Once I spent ten minutes convinced there was grit between toes that did not exist.

Today the heel itched.

Old favorite.

My hands had developed crutch calluses.

My shoulders hurt less.

I could cross a room without thinking about every placement.

Doors remained enemies.

Anything carried in two hands had become theoretical.

I stood in the lower room and looked at my belongings.

Noll and Jorren had brought most of them down.

Sword in the corner.

Clothes on shelves.

Mara's letters tied in two bundles on the desk.

Tin money box beneath them.

The old Bronze token.

My Silver token.

A folded city commendation I had not asked for.

One broadsheet with **THE SILVER AT NORTH GATE** printed large enough to become a personal attack.

Noll had saved that deliberately.

I would kill him when mobility improved.

The commendation came with six silver.

Not seventeen.

Seventeen had been emergency service and injury compensation.

Six was specifically for `material contribution to civilian evacuation and ward-failure mitigation.`

Very romantic.

I had spent two already on clothes altered around the bandaging and another on food not produced by an infirmary.

The rest joined the tin.

Renown had become twenty-three total silver after a month without regular work.

Good.

Not enough to stop caring.

My first prosthetic consultation was in twelve days.

The fitter had told me not to buy anything yet because the limb would change shape through healing.

Reasonable.

I disliked him already.

The city had cleared enough of Northern Gate to open a pedestrian lane beside the ruins.

Freight still went east.

I had not seen the site since the funeral.

Did not want to.

Wanted to.

Both.

I sat at the desk.

Standing without purpose remained expensive.

Mara's newest letter lay on top of the pile.

D381 confirmed.

Sixteen days.

She had managed a Carrow route packet by trading an Orin reconciliation trip with Sivi, the clerk who once corrected her arithmetic and bought tea afterward.

Sivi apparently had family near Orin and considered the trade a victory.

Everyone else's life continued producing logistics.

Good.

I had finally answered the Halden question.

Three days ago.

Short letter.

**Yes. Still doing Halden.**

Then, because Mara had specifically told me not to answer from a drugged need to reassure her, I added:

**Ask again after you see me if you think I'm being stupid.**

She had not received that yet.

River mail remained committed to suspense.

A knock came at the open door.

Jorren leaned in.

"Food?"

"Yes."

He held up a bowl.

"You coming or want it here?"

I looked toward the hall.

Twenty-two steps to the kitchen.

Flat.

Easy.

I stood.

"Coming."

Jorren did not offer to carry me.

Also did not leave.

Good balance.

We made it eleven steps before somebody came through the front entrance too fast.

Young man.

Delivery coat.

He saw me.

Stopped.

"Oh."

I waited.

Recognition happened visibly.

"You're Greg."

"Sometimes."

"North Gate."

There it was.

"Also other places."

He laughed because he thought I was joking.

Maybe I was.

He shifted the parcel under one arm.

"My aunt had a stall by the west approach. They cleared her out before it fell."

I did not know his aunt.

Did not know the stall.

"Good."

"She says you made them clear farther."

"Beret ordered it. Guards did the work."

"Yeah."

He still looked at me the same way.

Facts did not repair stories as quickly as stories formed.

"Thanks," he said.

Then went upstairs with the parcel.

Jorren waited beside me.

"Happens a lot?"

"Enough."

"You hate it?"

I thought about the baker's bread.

The free drinks.

The contracts with my name on them.

The butcher at the cemetery.

Cal Ven wanting to test me later.

The broadsheet I had not thrown away.

"Not entirely."

Jorren smiled.

"Gross."

"Very."

We reached the kitchen.

Soup.

Bread.

Noll already at the table.

He saw me and immediately looked toward the broadsheet under his elbow.

Another copy.

"Don't."

"Didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

"Gate Greg."

I threw a piece of bread at him.

Bad throw.

He caught it and ate it.

Disability had empowered my enemies.

After dinner I went back to the lower room alone.

Twenty-two steps.

Then twenty-two back because I had forgotten the water cup.

Forty-four.

Progress measured strangely now.

At the desk I opened the tin.

Twenty-three silver and change.

Silver Guild token.

Bronze token beneath it.

I closed the tin.

On the wall above the desk, someone had left an old calendar nail.

I hung Mara's terrible Halden map from it.

Blue square.

Wavy river.

Stone steps.

Exactly one year remained.

I knew because this time counting was not pretending to be scheduling.

Then I dropped the water cup lid.

It rolled under the bed.

I stared at it.

The floor was approximately three feet away and politically hostile.

"Fuck."

Home.
