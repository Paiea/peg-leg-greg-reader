# R2 Temporal Year Experiment Audit

Status: **EXPERIMENT AUDIT / ZERO AUTHORITY**

Branch: `experiment/r2-temporal-year-043-plus`

Accepted R2 story authority remains Chapter 42. Nothing in this audit promotes rehearsal prose, events, dates, characters, rank changes, artifact behavior, or the Black Stair year into canon.

## Executive result

The temporal-year experiment succeeds at its main structural question and misses its original prose-volume target.

The strongest result is not that forty speculative chapters were produced. It is that the same amount of broad rehearsal space that previously compressed into roughly one or two hypothetical weeks was forced across **365 elapsed days**, and the story improved because consequences had time to age.

The year begins with Bronze Greg writing:

> One year from today, I am going to stand at the Black Stair.

It ends on Firstday of Spring, Year 313 with Silver Greg physically standing on and touching the first black step after traveling there as the load/force specialist and reserve blade of Field Support Three.

The final date never moves.

The deadline also does not become a stupidity engine. At D360 Greg recommends the safer route even though it likely means arriving one day late. The expedition later chooses the old Load Road only after new avalanche evidence changes the route calculation independently of Greg's personal promise.

That is the cleanest proof that the goal matured during the year rather than merely controlling him.

## 1. Measured scope

### Calendar

- Opening: `Y312 / D001 / Firstday of Spring`.
- Closing: `Y313 / D001 / 365 days elapsed / Firstday of Spring`.
- Forty dated rehearsal chapters.
- Ten Spring chapters.
- Ten Summer chapters.
- Ten Autumn chapters.
- Ten Winter/Westreach chapters.
- Westreach leaves Carrow on D326.
- Thirty-nine elapsed expedition days lead to the Black Stair on D365.

### Prose volume

GitHub file metadata for the forty chapter files totals approximately **422,752 UTF-8 bytes**:

- Spring: ~104,324 bytes.
- Summer: ~103,087 bytes.
- Autumn: ~103,065 bytes.
- Winter: ~112,276 bytes.

A trustworthy exact word count was not available through the connected GitHub interface used for this run. Based on the prose density and file sizes, the year is likely in the rough neighborhood of **65,000 to 75,000 words**, not the intended ~120,000 words.

Therefore the experiment again demonstrates that an instruction to target ~3,000 words does not mechanically hold length. Many chapters are probably closer to the high-1,000s or low-2,000s, while several larger action chapters approach the intended band.

**Result:** calendar target passed; prose-volume target failed.

Do not redefine the target after the fact. If future rehearsal genuinely requires 120k, word count needs a mechanical gate rather than an aspiration.

## 2. Did the reader feel one year?

**Yes, substantially more than the 4x10 experiment.**

The improvement comes from accumulated off-page change rather than date labels alone.

Examples across the year include:

- Greg's boots wear, are replaced from earnings, break in, and later winter gear replaces ordinary road gear.
- injuries persist across gaps and alter later choices;
- the Guild file physically grows from thin Bronze evidence to a serious Silver/Westreach record;
- money is repeatedly earned, spent, depleted by gear, converted into expedition share, and recovered through work;
- letters with Mara become ordinary and ongoing rather than every exchange carrying maximum emotional significance;
- Noll accumulates Brass Spoon responsibility;
- Jorren develops a real factor/ledger identity while retaining road competence;
- Tavin remains mobile and is sometimes physically absent when Greg would prefer him present;
- Halden and Kelm become places Greg can return to rather than one-scene unlocks;
- winter changes travel, equipment, food, animals, shelter, and expedition speed;
- the Westreach train physically degrades from its departure state as wagons, mules, tools, clothing, weapons, and people acquire repairs.

The strongest time effect is that supporting characters clearly continue living when Greg is not present.

The principal weakness is that elapsed-time openings can become mechanically legible. Several chapters begin by explicitly inventorying what changed during the gap. That is useful for this experiment but would become formulaic in polished prose.

Future use should vary temporal evidence: weather, social absence, changed habits, worn objects, new responsibilities, letters, reputation, body condition, money, and environmental familiarity should sometimes reveal time indirectly rather than through opening summaries.

## 3. Character result

### Greg

The year produces a better version of the central contradiction than the compressed rehearsal did.

Greg can think one thing and physically do another without the prose treating the mismatch as an error to solve.

He wants independence and still seeks validation. He wants danger while increasingly choosing the job the team actually needs. He likes being recognized as unusually capable. He becomes better at admitting fear and desire directly, but not uniformly. He remains curious enough to overreach at D272 even after months of improvement.

Most importantly, the year allows Greg to become **genuinely competent without immediately punishing every competence beat**.

He wins fights. People are impressed. His sword clearly exceeds ordinary Bronze expectations before his rank catches up. His load/force specialty becomes real. He earns Silver. He earns a Westreach slot against credible candidates. He performs expedition-scale work that Spring Greg could not have performed.

The D272 prototype injury is separate from the prior clean victory, so consequence comes from a new bad decision rather than authorial guilt over letting Greg look cool.

### Repetition risk

The older pattern remains a danger:

`Greg over-models -> current expert corrects him -> Greg updates -> joke.`

The year improves this by giving Greg more correct calls, clean wins, teaching moments, leadership moments, and role ownership. Still, the prose sometimes relies heavily on the same short Greg rhythm: `Good.`, `Correct.`, `There.`, `Not X. Y.` and polished interpretive afterthoughts.

That voice is useful. Frequency needs reduction in a future authority pass.

## 4. Relationship result

Time helps Mara more than almost any other element.

Instead of one compressed Halden burst, Greg and Mara have months of letters, work conflicts, scheduling, absence, visits in both directions, and ordinary details. Halden becomes Mara's actual current life rather than a set Greg visits for exposition.

The relationship works especially well when each city belongs to the person living there:

- Greg visits Mara's Halden and discovers routines, coworkers, obligations and geography that do not organize around him.
- Mara later visits Greg's Carrow and sees Lower Rook, Noll, Jorren, work habits, gear, and the life that continued while she was west.

This makes reunion and absence carry more weight without manufacturing relationship crises.

Noll, Jorren and Tavin also improve because the elapsed gaps require them to accumulate choices Greg did not cause.

**Portable discovery:** relationship progression becomes stronger when time permits ordinary repetition, missed availability, work, letters, and independent change between the emotionally loud scenes.

## 5. Setting result

The year makes geography recursive.

Roads stop being transitions and become societies with rules, labor, animal knowledge, weather, repairs, tolls, food, delays and reputations. Kelm and Halden can be revisited. Carrow remains home even after long absence. Westreach feels like a moving settlement rather than seven heroes walking west.

The strongest large-scale setting discovery is the interaction between fantasy and infrastructure:

- old blackglass systems;
- load fields;
- bridges;
- pumping works;
- ruins;
- ward networks;
- freight;
- storm shelters;
- mountain passes;
- repair culture.

The ordinary economic layer survives the higher fantasy escalation. Monsters still create invoices, damaged gear, delayed freight, salvage, medical care and route changes.

Risk: infrastructure magic is now so effective as an R2 generator that it could become its own repetitive comfort zone. Future material should preserve this strength while adding more cultural, religious, political, ecological, domestic and purely strange fantasy that is not always another damaged public-works system.

## 6. Fantasy / isekai / power-fantasy result

The requested genre nudge is successful.

The year contains materially more:

- hostile magical fauna;
- conventional monster contracts;
- sword fights;
- bandits;
- proper ruins;
- loot and monster materials;
- artifact experimentation;
- build-like equipment evolution;
- rank progression;
- party trials;
- Gold-level benchmarks;
- winter expedition fantasy;
- increasingly large magic under pressure.

The most successful genre progression is when the fantasy reward remains materially integrated into the lived world.

Examples:

- monster material becomes an Anchor dampener rather than only cash;
- recovered hardware creates a limited splitter instead of an unlimited upgrade;
- the splitter carries a literal hard-limit tag;
- rimewing material later repairs ordinary expedition gear;
- Greg's Silver rank changes which contracts and responsibilities he can access;
- Westreach qualification requires money, kit, role fit, sponsorship and field judgment rather than a pure combat score.

The story can therefore be more recognizably fantasy and more satisfying as progression fiction without abandoning mundane density.

## 7. Action result

The action nudge is strongly positive.

The experiment is more physical even when no combat occurs. Greg and other characters haul, climb, crawl, brace, carry, rig, shovel, repair, drag, cook, pack, cross water, handle animals, stabilize structures, run roads and clean up afterward.

Combat also escalates across the year rather than arriving at a flat intensity:

- small hostile fauna and early sword work;
- larger road fauna;
- pale-drake ruin work;
- stronger Silver-scale monsters;
- hookfin power-fantasy win;
- human bandits;
- snow trolls;
- rimewings at Nine Teeth;
- expedition-scale hazards where fighting and engineering happen simultaneously.

The best action pattern remains:

**danger -> embodied solution -> aftermath -> changed material state.**

A fight is better when the next scene still contains the broken spear, bruised ribs, damaged dampener, dead mule, lost wagon, invoice, delayed schedule or changed route.

The final Westreach run demonstrates this especially well. The expedition does not reset between set pieces. It becomes visibly more patched and depleted.

## 8. Rank and progression result

The year gives rank enough elapsed evidence to feel earned.

- D001: Bronze with thin current record.
- D028: sword already tests in Silver training range, but field record is insufficient.
- Spring close: provisional Westreach specialist interest, still Bronze.
- Summer: repeat road and field work under Silver evaluation.
- D183: Silver earned from accumulated evidence.
- Autumn: Silver status changes contract scale, responsibility, pay, and consequence.
- D274: Westreach candidacy concrete.
- D304: Greg wins Field Support Three's open slot against candidates who are individually better wardwright/rigger specialists.
- D316: final role is `load/force specialist, reserve blade`.
- D326: expedition departs.
- D365: Greg arrives as Silver, not Gold.

This is a better power curve than forcing Gold inside one year.

The key portable idea is **capability can lead rank, while rank still matters institutionally**.

## 9. Blackglass Anchor result

The Anchor is one of the strongest discoveries of the experiment.

Its lifecycle is long enough to feel owned rather than bestowed:

1. discovered during ordinary salvage after weeks of current work;
2. legally released and purchased at meaningful cost;
3. characterized slowly;
4. mounted and repaired;
5. used under increasingly serious loads;
6. modified with monster-material dampening;
7. paired with a deliberately hard-limited splitter;
8. damaged by Greg's overreach;
9. repaired and de-rated;
10. interfaced with a larger old system at Storm Keep;
11. damaged again at Nine Teeth;
12. finally placed into a related portable-regulator socket on the Black Stair approach.

The D365 socket fit is satisfying because it answers **what kind of tool this might have been** rather than declaring Greg chosen.

The artifact works even if Greg never loses his leg. That is essential.

Its future peg-leg usefulness, if ever adopted, should be a later second life for an object already loved and understood, not the reason the object exists.

## 10. Westreach / party result

Field Support Three works as a progression target because Greg does not become every role.

The seven-person crew has real ownership boundaries. Greg wins the slot not because he is best at every category but because the crew already has better pure specialists and needs his hybrid combination of:

- load/force magic;
- blade competence;
- old-system intuition;
- rescue/field judgment;
- ability to switch jobs.

The expedition itself also avoids the generic fantasy-party problem. It contains wagons, animals, cooks, healers, ward crews, engineers, guards, scouts, Silvers, Golds and lower-ranked people whose route competence can matter more than rank.

The strongest upper-tier discovery remains that elite teams are not perfect mind-melds. They are practiced systems built around real people, equipment, role boundaries and disagreement.

## 11. The Black Stair deadline

The hard date is a major success.

It gives ordinary chapters forward pressure without requiring every chapter to advance the same plot directly.

The date becomes more useful as the year progresses:

- initially ridiculous aspiration;
- then paperwork deadlines;
- then rank/field requirements;
- then money and gear requirements;
- then an actual crew slot;
- then departure date;
- then physical route arithmetic;
- finally a value conflict at D360.

The most important moment is not D365. It is D360, when Greg is willing to miss the deadline rather than recommend an under-tested route.

That prevents the successful D365 arrival from making the year morally hollow.

**Portable structural discovery:** a hard dated destination can make slice-of-life progression cohere without turning every scene into plot machinery.

## 12. Comparison with the 4x10 rehearsal

The 4x10 experiment was useful horizontal search. It discovered multiple possible near futures and many strong individual materials.

Its weakness was temporal compression: approximately 60k-plus speculative words mostly explored several alternate versions of the first one or two weeks after Chapter 42.

The temporal-year version is stronger as a model for a long-running story because it asks:

> What does Greg's life become if he keeps doing this for a year?

rather than only:

> What could happen next?

The year keeps a surprising amount of 4x10 quarry while improving it through spacing:

- road desire;
- road ecology;
- Halden/Mara;
- Tavin becoming current;
- independent Noll/Jorren lives;
- Gold-party competence;
- infrastructure magic;
- action aftermath;
- Greg's thought/action mismatch.

The major positive change is **residue**. An event does not merely happen. Months later its gear, reputation, relationship, technique, injury, debt, habit or route knowledge can still exist.

## 13. Known scrub problems

The year is structurally complete but not publication-clean prose.

A targeted reread found several reader-facing drafting/meta leaks that should be removed before any continuous-reader presentation. Known examples include phrases equivalent to:

- `Power fantasy through receipts`;
- `No chapter could stay in goodbye`;
- live revision notes inside the D336 troll sequence such as questioning a detail and then correcting it in the same prose;
- a D345 line about a behavior potentially `proving the year`.

There are likely additional frequency-level issues even where lines are not literal author notes.

Because this is zero-authority rehearsal, these leaks do not invalidate the experiment's discoveries. They do demonstrate that the future engine needs a separate **reader-only scrub pass after generation** rather than asking the same pass to manage temporal architecture and invisible prose simultaneously.

Do not call the forty files publication-ready until that scrub is complete.

## 14. Length failure and recommendation

Do **not** expand every existing chapter solely to force this experiment to 120k.

The current 65k-75k-ish body already answered the most valuable question: spreading the material across a year substantially improved character, relationship, progression, setting scale and consequence.

Blindly adding ~50k to these exact events risks making zero-authority prose expensive enough to defend for sunk-cost reasons.

If another volume test is desired, the better experiment is either:

1. mechanically enforce 2,700-3,200 words on a smaller fresh seasonal run; or
2. extend beyond D365 into a second-year frontier horizon with actual new temporal territory rather than inflating scenes whose discoveries already landed.

## 15. Recommendation for R2 authority

Do not copy this year into canon.

Do not make the forty rehearsal chapters Chapter 43 onward by renumbering.

Instead treat the year as a **future-history quarry and progression proof**.

The strongest candidates to re-perform from accepted Chapter 42 are:

- a hard dated destination or equivalent one-year promise;
- Greg's road desire becoming repeated behavior;
- capability leading official rank;
- Silver earned through accumulated field evidence;
- recurring geography and roads;
- Mara relationship progression through elapsed time and independent life;
- the Blackglass Anchor or a close functional descendant;
- artifact progression through repair, bounded experimentation and monster material;
- more ordinary fantasy contracts and monsters;
- clean Greg wins;
- action with persistent aftermath;
- Field Support / party role ownership;
- Westreach as a mixed expedition rather than a heroic seven-person walking party;
- Black Stair as a future destination if it continues to feel alive after cooling off.

The exact event order, dates, names, fights, rank timing, monster species and Black Stair arrival remain disposable unless fresh authority writing earns them again.

## Bottom line

The temporal intervention is a clear positive result.

The year experiment is more interesting than the 4x10 version not because it contains more plot, but because elapsed time lets the same kinds of events acquire **weight, recurrence and consequences**.

The most valuable engine discovered here is:

**hard future date -> lived seasonal time -> repeated work/adventure -> visible progression -> independent supporting lives -> escalating fantasy/action -> deadline tested against judgment -> destination reached without ending life.**

That engine is worth preserving.

The forty rehearsal chapters themselves remain quarry.
