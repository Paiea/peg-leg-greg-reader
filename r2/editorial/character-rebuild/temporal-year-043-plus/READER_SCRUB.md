# Temporal Year Reader Scrub

Status: **EXPERIMENT QUALITY GATE / ZERO AUTHORITY**

Run after every chapter and again after every season.

A rehearsal is not considered stable enough to feed the next seasonal state until it passes this scrub.

## 1. Reader-facing prose only

Reject or rewrite any line that contains:

- `horizon` as experiment architecture;
- `rehearsal` as an author-side concept inside Greg's narrative;
- `this chapter`;
- `the writer` / `the generator` / `the model`;
- notes about what another future did;
- alternate-draft instructions;
- comments such as `this scene does not need...`;
- continuity-manager language;
- prose that explicitly discusses whether a fact is canon, authority, quarry, or experiment-only.

Editorial headers may contain experiment labels. Story prose may not.

## 2. Calendar integrity

For the current chapter:

- Date matches `YEAR_CLOCK.md`.
- `DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER` is correct.
- No later event is referenced as if already true.
- If the gap is more than two days, the first roughly 500 words show at least two concrete off-page consequences.
- Weather/daylight/season does not behave like no time passed.
- Injuries do not vanish without enough elapsed time or treatment.
- Letters/travel do not arrive faster than the world has established without explanation.
- Money and gear reflect jobs and spending that actually occurred.

## 3. Progression integrity

Compare against the previous row in `PROGRESSION_LEDGER.md`.

Reject unsupported jumps in:

- rank;
- magic control;
- sword endurance;
- artifact knowledge;
- gear;
- money;
- reputation;
- party trust;
- Westreach eligibility.

A time gap may contain practice or work, but the chapter must show evidence that it happened rather than silently installing the result.

## 4. 4x10 contamination check

The prior 4x10 run is quarry, not history.

Reject:

- a character relationship that only existed in one old horizon unless freshly established here;
- an event referenced as shared history only because it happened in A/B/C/D;
- the exact old chronology appearing by inertia;
- a Gold party or creature treated as known because the old experiment used it;
- Ossa/Beren/Sera/Veyra/etc. appearing automatically without this year introducing them;
- Greg learning a lesson because the writer remembers another horizon rather than because this chronology earned it.

Fresh re-invention is allowed. Silent import is not.

## 5. Greg pattern check

Challenge repeated dependence on:

`Greg assumes too much -> competent local corrects him -> Greg updates -> joke.`

Across the year Greg must also:

- make correct calls before others;
- teach something without becoming an exposition machine;
- win cleanly;
- misread people instead of systems;
- be emotionally petty while technically right;
- be technically wrong while emotionally perceptive;
- enjoy competence;
- choose badly despite knowing better;
- choose well without narrating a lesson afterward.

## 6. Power-fantasy check

Do not reflexively sabotage Greg every time he looks cool.

Ask:

- Did a win earn pleasure before consequence arrived?
- Is the consequence caused by the situation rather than author guilt about competence?
- Can another character simply be impressed sometimes?
- Does Greg's official rank sometimes lag his demonstrated ability in a satisfying way?
- Does stronger Greg create new kinds of problems instead of simply receiving harsher punishments?

## 7. Action check

Every chapter needs physical life.

At least one must be true:

- Greg is working with his body;
- traveling through a specific difficult environment;
- fighting/sparring/chasing;
- rescuing or being rescued;
- handling animals, loads, tools, ropes, weapons, fire, water, ruins, weather, or damaged infrastructure;
- recovering, repairing, cleaning, cooking, packing, healing, or paying for action consequences.

Action must change something that survives the moment.

## 8. Supporting-character agency

For Mara, Noll, Jorren, Tavin, and recurring workers ask:

- Did this person make at least one choice Greg did not cause?
- Do they have work/schedules/relationships that continue off-page?
- Are they allowed to be unavailable?
- Are they competent in domains Greg does not own?
- If months passed, did they change too?

If a recurring person exists only to react to Greg's progression, rewrite.

## 9. Magic / fantasy check

This experiment intentionally wants more genre pleasure.

Ask whether the season includes enough:

- monsters;
- materials/loot;
- ruins;
- magic used under pressure;
- visible technique growth;
- stronger contracts;
- better gear;
- rank/status pleasure;
- tactical party action;
- mystery that produces physical consequences.

Do not replace all fantasy payoff with workplace realism. Keep both.

## 10. Blackglass Anchor check

The Anchor must remain bounded.

At every use ask:

- What does Greg know it can do *as of this date*?
- What does it cost or require?
- What load/charge did it actually affect?
- Could Greg solve the same problem without it?
- Does the object create new technique space rather than automatic victory?
- Has maintenance, mounting, transport, legality, ownership, or repair mattered?

Reject any line implying the Anchor exists for a future prosthetic.

## 11. Prose-frequency check

Challenge overuse of:

- isolated `Good.` / `Correct.` / `There.`;
- `Not X. Y.` reversals;
- polished mini-theses immediately after an action already demonstrated the point;
- Greg announcing that he has learned something;
- repeated `my face betrayed me` variants;
- chapter-ending aphorisms.

These tools are allowed. Frequency is the problem.

## 12. Style check

- No em dashes in story prose.
- Dialogue attribution remains clear for audio-first reading.
- Paragraph rhythm can breathe during ordinary life and tighten during action.
- Physical referents remain understandable without rereading.
- Old Greg memory fragments are sensory/associative where possible, not encyclopedic downloads.
- Humor comes from people and situations, not constant narrator button-pushing.

## 13. Length gate

Target 2,700-3,200 words.

- Under 2,500: expand with a real lived phase or document a structural exception in the chapter header metadata.
- 2,500-2,699: challenge for missing aftermath, transition, relationship residue, or physical phase.
- 2,700-3,200: target band.
- 3,201-3,500: acceptable if density holds.
- Over 3,500: challenge for repeated explanation or redundant scene grammar.

Never expand solely by restating thoughts.

## Seasonal scrub

After ten chapters, read the season continuously and answer:

1. Can I feel the first date and last date are months apart?
2. Did Greg's body change?
3. Did money change?
4. Did relationships change?
5. Did gear change?
6. Did magic change through evidence?
7. Did rank/reputation change through work?
8. Did the world move while Greg was elsewhere?
9. Did action become more capable without becoming weightless?
10. What should be killed before the next season inherits it?

Only the scrubbed seasonal endpoint enters the next season's experiment ledger.
