# Year Chapter 03: South Four

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D009 / SPRING`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 5`

`LOCATION: Carrow south drainage quarter`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

Five days gave my wrists enough time to stop hurting and Noll enough time to acquire an apron he hated.

The apron was brown.

This was apparently the problem.

"Makes me look old," he said.

He stood in my doorway before first bell wearing it over his work shirt.

"You're seventeen."

"Exactly."

"What color would make you younger?"

"Blue."

There was no logic to attack.

I had worked two more Stonehook shifts after resting a day, both boring enough that Mira's endorsement had begun to feel less like a miracle and more like a thing attached to actual work. I had also spent one evening at the Guild yard pushing against padded posts with force planes until my shoulders shook.

Not because anyone told me to.

Because eight inches had become a number I wanted to understand.

The second attempt at a lateral plane had failed after three inches.

The fourth lasted longer.

My current magic was less a collection of spells than an argument with geometry.

I liked it.

Today Stonehook did not need me.

The Guild did.

Or, more accurately, South Drainage needed bodies and the Guild supplied people willing to crawl into wet places for twelve copper.

The notice said:

**SOUTH FOUR CLEARANCE / MAGICAL FAUNA POSSIBLE / CITY LEAD / BRONZE ACCEPTED / BOOTS REQUIRED**

I owned boots.

Sella read my Stonehook endorsement before handing me the token.

"Mira wrote control limited."

"She also wrote response good."

"You saw the part you liked."

"Naturally."

Sella turned the token over.

"City lead is Vaska. Listen when she says out."

"I always listen."

She stared.

"Eventually."

Fair.

South Four sat beneath a row of dye sheds and cheap warehouses where the city canal narrowed before joining the river.

The access stair smelled like wet brick, sulfur soap, and old cabbage.

Vaska was already there.

Short woman, dark braid, waterproof coat despite clear weather, three iron keys on a ring large enough to anchor a small boat.

She looked at my token.

"Stonehook?"

"Four days."

"Drain is not hoist."

"I had suspected."

"Good."

Everyone had the word.

The other Guild hires were Evin and Lessa.

Evin carried a short spear and wore Bronze tab polished too brightly. He was older than me by maybe five years, broad in the shoulders, cheerful in a way that felt medically suspicious before sunrise.

Lessa had a healer's satchel, a long knife, and the tired eyes of someone who had already been asked whether she was `the healer` instead of a person.

"Apprentice chirurgeon," she said before I could ask.

"I wasn't going to."

"Everyone is."

"I was going to ask whether that bag has food."

She looked at me.

"No."

Relationship established.

Vaska laid the problem out on the stair landing.

South Four's lower grate had stopped draining properly after spring rain. Two city workers cleared ordinary blockage yesterday and found bite marks on a wooden rake.

"Bite marks?" Evin asked.

"Four rows. Mineral residue."

"Channel hound?"

Vaska nodded.

I knew the name from current Guild bestiary more than Old Greg.

Channel hounds were not dogs.

They were long-bodied amphibious scavengers that nested around warm drains and charged runoff, with pale skin and hard mineral ridges along jaw and spine. Usually avoided people.

Usually was a word animals enjoyed disproving.

"One?" I asked.

"One set tracks."

"That isn't one animal."

Vaska looked at me.

"Correct."

Nice.

"Why Bronze?"

"Because if there are six, we leave and I hire Silver."

Clear threshold.

She gave each of us a waxed lamp.

"Rules. I lead route. Evin owns front reach. Greg behind him. Lessa rear until somebody bleeds. Nobody chases through an unmarked branch. If I say out, out. If a hound runs, let it run unless it blocks exit. City pays removal bounty only for confirmed aggressive animals."

Evin frowned.

"Removal means kill?"

"If needed. We prefer drive."

Good.

We went down.

South Four was a brick tunnel tall enough for me to walk bent slightly, which meant it had been designed by shorter people and hatred.

Water ran ankle deep in center channel. Narrow stone ledges on both sides gave footing until silt made them slippery.

The first two hundred feet contained nothing dangerous except broken glass and a dead rat large enough to have had political influence.

Evin poked it with spear.

"Don't," Lessa said.

"Checking."

"Dead."

"Very."

I laughed.

Then smelled something metallic.

Not blood.

Wet stone after lightning.

Mana charge.

"Hold."

Vaska stopped immediately.

That mattered.

I crouched near water.

Faint blue sheen moved along surface around a patch of black silt.

"Charge?" she asked.

"Yes. Low. Moving from ahead."

"Natural?"

"Could be runoff."

I did not say probably.

Progress.

Vaska marked wall with chalk and continued more slowly.

Twenty yards later we found the rake.

Half of it.

Wood handle splintered and gnawed. Mineral dust glittered in bite grooves.

Evin whispered, "Big."

"Or several," I said.

Something splashed ahead.

Everyone froze.

Lamp light reached perhaps thirty feet.

Two red points reflected low to water.

Then four.

Then six.

Three animals.

Vaska said, "Out."

No debate.

We backed.

The hounds moved toward us.

Not charging.

Following.

One emerged fully into light.

Long as a large dog but lower, pale gray body, four splayed feet, jaw ridge glittering green-white. It opened mouth and hissed through mineral teeth.

Evin kept spear point low.

"Keep moving."

Vaska's voice steady.

We moved.

Then rear water erupted.

Fourth animal.

It had come through side grate behind us.

Lessa turned.

The hound lunged.

No ambiguity.

I was closer than Evin.

Sword came out.

The old motion fit current hand better than most things did.

Draw, step, cut diagonal behind jaw.

The hound's mineral ridge caught edge and threw sparks.

My blade bit through softer neck below it.

Hot dark blood hit my sleeve.

The animal twisted violently.

I pivoted before tail struck knee.

Second cut under forelimb.

It collapsed into water.

Seven heartbeats maybe.

I felt good.

Not morally.

Physically.

The sword had gone where I wanted. My feet had obeyed. Current body had not lagged behind memory for once.

There was no time to feel guilty about enjoying competence because the other three hounds charged at the blood smell.

"Move!" Vaska shouted.

Evin thrust at first.

Spear glanced off jaw ridge.

Second hound climbed wall ledge and came high.

I stepped beside him.

"Low one yours."

He nodded.

No rank discussion.

High hound leapt.

I could cut it.

Lessa was behind me.

If I missed, it reached her.

I used force instead.

A flat shove, not catch.

Mana struck its chest midair.

My wrists remembered Stonehook and complained.

The hound's leap changed enough to hit wall shoulder-first.

It fell.

I killed it before it recovered.

Evin pinned his hound against channel with spear, but the third came around him in water.

Vaska swung iron key ring like club.

This was unexpectedly effective.

Metal caught mineral jaw with a crack.

Animal veered.

Lessa drove long knife into side when it passed.

Not healer now.

Person with knife.

Hound thrashed, tail struck her shin, and she went down.

I moved toward it.

Vaska said, "Leave!"

Why?

Then I saw.

The hound wasn't attacking.

It was fleeing toward side grate.

I stopped.

It squeezed through bars missing two slats and vanished.

Evin's pinned animal pulled free of spear and followed.

Gone.

Silence returned hard.

Water carried blood downstream.

Lessa sat with one trouser leg torn.

"Shin?"

"Bruised."

She checked herself before anyone else could.

No cut.

Vaska counted.

"Two dead. Two fled. We leave."

Evin looked down tunnel.

"Nest?"

"Silver or trap crew tomorrow."

"We can finish."

Vaska looked at him.

"No."

He glanced at me.

I expected to want to continue.

Part of me did.

Two hounds had fled. We knew direction. I had just killed two cleanly. My body felt sharp.

That was exactly why leaving mattered.

"Out," I said.

Evin sighed.

We went.

The dead hounds were heavy.

Of course they were.

We did not leave them because city needed confirmation and Guild bounty required bodies or tagged jaw plates.

Dragging two wet animals through a drain was less heroic than killing them.

More difficult too.

We tied hind legs with line. Evin and I each dragged one while Vaska led and Lessa limped rear.

Mineral ridges scraped brick.

My back began hurting before halfway.

At access stair, daylight looked theatrical.

City workers helped haul carcasses up.

One whistled at size.

"Four?"

Vaska nodded.

"At least."

"You kill all?"

"Two left."

He looked disappointed, which was easy when he had not been in tunnel.

Guild bounty clerk came after midday.

Two aggressive removals at four copper each split across field crew after city share.

My portion was two copper plus base twelve.

Lessa received injury supplement despite insisting bruise did not count.

"It counts if you limp," Vaska said.

"Then I'll stop."

She tried walking normally.

Failed.

Everyone laughed except her.

I cleaned sword at a drain trough.

Dark blood had dried near guard.

The blade edge showed a tiny bright chip where it struck mineral jaw.

There.

Action left equipment evidence.

I ran thumb near it, not on edge.

Repair cost.

Power had receipts.

Vaska came beside me.

"You have done hounds before?"

"No."

"Sword says yes."

I looked at her.

"Sword has done other things."

Not exactly true in this body.

But technique had.

She nodded.

"Force shove was useful."

I liked hearing it.

"Limited."

"Everything is."

Better than lecture.

She signed my token.

`SOUTH FOUR. FAUNA CONTACT. FOLLOWED EXIT. TWO REMOVALS, ONE DIRECT. SENSE USEFUL.`

One direct.

She had apparently credited the second hound to team because force plus cut.

Fair.

"Westreach?" she asked.

I frowned.

"How?"

"You have date written on back."

I had used the same paper beneath token at desk.

Privacy failure.

"Yes."

"Black Stair route has worse than drain dogs."

"I know."

"No. You remember. Different."

That irritated me because it was good enough to keep.

"Probably."

Vaska smiled slightly.

"There."

I stared.

She had weaponized my word.

At Guild that afternoon, Sella read both new endorsements.

Stonehook.

South Four.

"Five days," she said.

"Nine."

"Five since Stonehook."

"Yes."

"You are going fast."

I liked that too.

Then she added, "Do not confuse fast beginning with year."

I did not like that.

Probably why I needed it.

I paid a bladesmith three copper to dress the chip from my sword.

The repair took an hour.

I spent it sitting outside eating meat pie and staring at the Westreach notice across the square.

My first monster fight had lasted maybe thirty seconds.

My sword repair lasted longer.

The year had 356 days left.

For once, that did not feel like plenty.
