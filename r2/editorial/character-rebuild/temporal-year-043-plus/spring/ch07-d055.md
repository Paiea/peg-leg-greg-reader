# Year Chapter 07: The Tusker

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D055 / SPRING`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 14`

`LOCATION: Carrow north vineyards`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

Tavin had been gone eight days when the vineyard owner told me Bronze was not enough.

This would have hurt more two weeks earlier.

Now I had paperwork.

I unfolded my Guild endorsements on his kitchen table.

Stonehook.

South Four.

North Edge.

Blade assessment.

Two more short contract marks from the last fourteen days, one warehouse watch and one road escort where nothing attacked us except rain.

Tavin had spent six days in Carrow before Venn sent him south again. Long enough to learn Maud charged for extra eggs. Long enough to get banned from touching Noll's blue apron with wax hands. Long enough to borrow three copper and claim it reduced his historical debt from four to one.

He left on D047.

I did not go to the gate.

Venn departed before dawn and I had a Guild shift at Stonehook.

I considered changing it.

Tavin told me not to become dramatic.

So I worked.

He sent a note from the road two days later anyway:

**You not coming was normal. Disturbing progress.**

I kept it.

Noll had started closing Brass Spoon two nights a week and opening one morning. Jorren's desk drawer now contained two socks because apparently institutional growth required redundancy.

Mara's last letter was nine days old.

She had signed it `M.` instead of `Mara`, then filled two pages with Vey complaints and one line asking when I was coming west.

I had not answered the when.

I had answered everything else.

Current relationships accumulated unfinished sentences while I worked.

The vineyard owner tapped my Bronze tab.

"Posting says Silver or two endorsed Bronze."

"We are two."

I pointed at Evin.

He stood by pantry door holding spear and trying not to look amused.

The owner, Hesk, looked at him.

"You?"

Evin held up his own endorsements.

"Three fauna jobs. One boar."

"One?"

"Normal boar."

"This isn't normal."

We knew.

The posting called it a ridge tusker.

Large boar-like magical scavenger with mineral plates forming along shoulders after feeding on certain hillside ores. Usually solitary. Usually avoided farms unless food got easier than forest.

This one had discovered grape-root stores.

It had broken two fences in five nights, killed a dog, and put a farmhand into bed with a cracked pelvis.

Hesk wanted Silver.

Guild had none available until tomorrow.

Weather report said rain tonight.

Tusker tracks already inside upper vineyard.

"We can wait," I said.

Hesk stared.

He expected salesmanship.

"You said you wanted work."

"I do. If you want Silver more, wait."

Evin looked at me.

I ignored him.

Hesk rubbed beard.

"If it comes tonight, upper root shed is open wall."

"Then close it."

"Door broke."

"Board it."

"You want job or not?"

"Yes. I also don't need to invent urgency."

This was annoyingly mature.

I missed being simpler.

Hesk finally said, "Guild says two endorsed Bronze acceptable."

"Yes."

"You kill it, twenty-eight copper plus material share. Drive it off, eighteen. Damage comes from contract if caused by you."

"If caused by tusker?"

"Mine."

Fair.

We signed.

Evin waited until outside.

"You could have told him we're good."

"Are we?"

"I am. You are weird."

"Strong team."

He laughed.

The upper vineyard climbed a south-facing slope beyond Carrow's north wall. Rows of spring vines barely knee high, leaves bright green. Stone retaining walls divided terraces. Root shed sat near tree line where vineyard became rough forest.

Tracks were obvious.

Three-toed hoof marks as wide as my spread hand.

Fence post snapped near base.

Dark hair caught on splinter.

Evin crouched.

"Big."

"Yes."

"Good."

I looked at him.

"You say it too."

"What?"

"Nothing."

We spent two hours preparing instead of hunting immediately.

This surprised Hesk.

We cleared workers from upper terraces.

Boarded the root shed with planks but left one narrow bait opening because the tusker already knew food was there. Moved two carts away. Opened the lower stone gate so the animal had a downhill exit if it chose flight.

Evin carried a heavy boar spear from Hesk's wall because his normal spear was too light for charge.

I kept sword.

We built no pit.

No trap elaborate enough to fail creatively.

A local hunter named Pela joined for four copper as tracker, not fighter. She found fresh droppings at tree line and showed us gray stone fragments inside.

"Eating quarry spoil," she said.

A small abandoned slate cut sat half mile north.

Mineral plates likely thicker than normal.

Useful current information.

Old Greg remembered killing ridge tuskers later in life.

The memory offered a dozen clean strikes.

Most assumed stronger body and better magic.

I kept them as possibilities, not instructions.

At third bell we heard brush break above vineyard.

Pela pointed.

"Coming."

We took positions around root shed.

Evin behind low wall with spear braced.

Me left side near open lane.

Pela farther back with short bow only if animal turned toward houses.

Hesk had been told to stay downhill.

He stayed twenty yards downhill instead of fifty.

Humans.

The tusker emerged.

Bigger than I wanted.

Shoulder nearly chest high on me. Dark brown hide under overlapping gray plates along neck and upper back. One tusk broken. The other long enough to reach my forearm.

It smelled sour even at distance.

It walked toward root shed slowly.

Not enraged.

Hungry.

We could still let it eat bait and maybe drive.

Evin watched me.

I watched animal.

The tusker stopped.

Head lifted.

Wind shifted from us to it.

It smelled people.

Body changed.

Shoulders rose.

Hooves dug once.

"Ready," Evin said quietly.

Tusker charged.

Not root shed.

Me.

Excellent.

I had wanted action.

The body arrived before philosophical regret.

I moved sideways toward open lane.

Do not meet mass.

Old Greg knew.

Current Greg agreed.

Tusker adjusted faster than expected.

Mineral-plated shoulder turned with me.

I threw a force plane at an angle across its front step.

Not stop.

Unweight one hoof.

Mana caught for an instant.

The tusker stumbled.

Not fell.

Head swung.

Tusk passed inches from thigh.

I cut at neck below plate.

Blade bit shallow.

Animal screamed.

Momentum carried past.

Evin moved.

Boar spear struck behind shoulder.

The tusker hit shaft sideways instead of straight.

Wood bowed.

Evin let go rather than get dragged.

Good.

Animal turned uphill.

Blood dark along side.

It could run.

Open gate downhill behind us, not uphill.

We had prepared escape wrong for its chosen direction.

Current reality rejected plan.

Pela shouted, "Tree line!"

Workers? One boy stood near fence beyond.

Why?

Hesk had missed one.

The tusker saw movement.

Charged toward boy.

Now no drive option.

I ran.

Current nineteen-year-old body was fast.

Not Old Greg fast.

Enough.

The boy froze.

"Wall!"

He moved this time, scrambling over retaining wall.

Tusker reached it.

Front legs climbed stone.

I caught up from side.

The mineral plates left a gap behind jaw when head lifted.

Old memory and current anatomy aligned.

I did not hesitate.

Sword entered under plate.

Deep.

The tusker twisted.

My grip nearly tore free.

I released one hand, turned with body, kept blade angled away from bone.

Animal threw me.

I hit vines shoulder-first.

Air left lungs.

Tusker staggered.

Sword still in it.

Bad.

Evin had recovered spear.

He drove it into chest when animal turned.

This time straight.

The crossbar caught against hide and stopped overpenetration.

Tusker shoved.

Evin slid backward.

Pela's arrow hit eye.

Not cinematic timing.

Three people doing jobs.

Tusker collapsed onto front knees.

I got up coughing.

Reached sword.

Pulled free.

Then finished with cut behind jaw.

Silence after was immediate.

My lungs hurt.

Shoulder hurt.

Hands steady.

The boy on wall stared.

Hesk ran uphill shouting his name.

Apparently nephew.

Of course.

Evin sat down in dirt.

"Big."

"You said."

"Bigger now."

Pela laughed once.

I looked at dead tusker.

I felt good again.

Not because the animal was dead.

Because the fight had been real and I had been good in it.

The difference mattered and I did not need to punish myself for knowing it.

My first cut had been too shallow.

My escape lane wrong for actual direction.

I had still moved fast, used force under motion, found the gap, and trusted Evin/Pela when the animal kept fighting.

No one had carried me.

No one had needed to.

Hesk reached nephew and slapped back of his head before hugging him.

Then turned to us.

"You killed it."

"Yes."

He looked at Bronze tabs.

That look was satisfying.

I let it be.

Afterward came work.

A lot.

Ridge tuskers were valuable because mineral plates could be cut and sold for ward composites. Tusks, glands, hide, meat if handled quickly.

Pela knew butchery.

We did not.

She became boss.

For three hours we skinned, cut plates, hauled sections downhill, washed blood from stone, repaired the gate we had opened, and filled the hole the animal tore in vine row.

My shoulder stiffened.

Evin's palms blistered from spear shaft.

Pela charged Hesk two extra copper because his nephew had been inside cleared zone.

He paid without argument.

Our material share sold that evening for more than base contract.

My total: thirty-four copper after fees.

Largest single job payout of the year.

Enough to matter.

I bought a better sword belt before going home.

Not magical.

Reinforced frog, wider hip strap, quick-release buckle for climbing.

Nine copper.

The old belt had rubbed wrong when running.

Now I had evidence for purchase.

At Guild, contract lead wrote:

`RIDGE TUSKER. TWO BRONZE + TRACKER. GREG: BLADE ABOVE RANK. FORCE USED UNDER CHARGE. HELD ROLE AFTER PLAN CHANGED.`

Sella read it twice.

"You look pleased."

"I am."

No denial.

She smiled.

"Better."

I almost objected to evaluator word.

Did not.

"Silver?"

"Not yet."

"Why?"

"Because Silver isn't a sword compliment. You need broader record, team marks, sustained field judgment."

I could accept that today because the fight still belonged to me.

"How many?"

"No magic number."

"Terrible system."

"Yes."

I touched new belt.

Outside the Guild, a Westreach team poster had been added beneath expedition notice.

Applications not open yet.

Just categories.

Field guard.

Ward specialist.

Freight crew.

Survey.

Healer.

Animal team.

I read `FIELD GUARD` first.

Then `WARD SPECIALIST`.

For the first time I wondered whether I might not have to choose.
