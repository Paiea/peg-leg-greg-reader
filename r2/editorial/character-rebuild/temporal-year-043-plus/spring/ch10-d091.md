# Year Chapter 10: The Registry

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D091 / SPRING`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 9`

`LOCATION: Carrow Guild`

`RANK: Bronze`

---

By the last day of spring, the Blackglass Anchor had acquired a leather cover, my left thigh had stopped hurting from the glasshorn kick, and the Guild had finally moved my file into a larger folder.

Sella pretended this was not an achievement.

"Stationery," she said again.

"Thick stationery."

"Do not become proud of paper."

I put the folder down carefully.

It contained eighty-nine days of current Greg.

Not every day.

Enough pieces that the stack had weight.

Stonehook freight.

South Four hounds.

West Spur.

North Edge.

Ridge tusker.

Old canal salvage.

Glasshorn station.

Three short warehouse jobs.

Two road escorts where nothing happened except weather and one broken axle.

A handful of training marks.

One blade assessment saying `SILVER TRAINING RANGE` in handwriting I had memorized against my will.

I had earned money, spent too much of it on boots and black glass, chipped my sword, repaired it, bruised both thighs on different animals, learned Murn liked apple peel more than me, watched Noll become a person his kitchen trusted with keys, and learned Jorren could be happy with a desk drawer containing socks.

Mara had not become easier because I was busier.

Our letters had become more frequent and less dramatic.

That frightened me slightly.

Her last packet contained a grocery list on the back because she had reused paper without noticing.

Eggs.

Soap.

Blue thread.

Lamp wick.

I liked that list more than some of her prettier sentences.

D128 remained written at the top of my room calendar.

Thirty-seven days.

Tavin's last packet arrived from Venn three days ago.

He had added a drawing of an exploding black oval with stick legs.

Noll thought it was funny.

I did not.

Mostly.

Today was Westreach preliminary registry day.

Not selection.

Not application deadline.

The expedition office ran an open skills review each season so people had time to discover they were delusional before winter.

Sella had phrased it exactly that way.

I registered under two categories:

Field guard.

Ward/load specialist.

The clerk at Westreach desk looked at the combination, then at Bronze tab.

"Which are you?"

"Both maybe."

"Maybe costs us time."

"Then test both."

She raised eyebrow.

"Confident."

"Curious."

"Worse."

Her name was Ressa Deyn, expedition quartermaster assistant. Not a famous adventurer. No Gold tab. Plain gray Guild coat with brass key chain and a writing board thick enough to stop arrows.

She cared about what went into a wagon more than what legends said.

I liked her immediately.

Dangerous pattern.

Ressa took my file.

Read Stonehook note.

South Four.

Ridge tusker.

Glasshorn.

Then looked at Anchor under leather cover.

"That the regulator?"

"Blackglass Anchor."

"Who named?"

"Me."

"Then regulator."

Hostile.

"Old public works salvage."

"Yes."

"Appraised?"

I showed release paper.

She checked ownership chain before magic.

Westreach apparently believed theft was less interesting than logistics.

"What can it do?"

"Redistribute force between connected anchors. Some charge too, I think."

"You think?"

"Charge interaction is less characterized."

Better language.

"How much force?"

"Enough to redirect a glasshorn charge with two wall points. Not enough that I enjoyed it."

"Repeatable?"

"Unknown."

"Then today we learn."

The open review took place in Guild west yard.

Six stations.

Not all combat.

A healer triage tent.

Freight balance lane.

Rope climb.

Ward fault board.

Animal handling pen.

Combat ring.

People moved between based on claimed role.

Silver and Bronze mostly. A few tradespeople with no Guild rank.

One old cook carrying three knives better than my sword.

The Westreach Expedition looked less like adventurers and more like a town preparing to walk somewhere.

That changed the goal.

I had imagined earning a place beside powerful people.

Ressa imagined getting three hundred people, animals, food, tools, wards, medicine, and freight through winter passes without losing half to stupidity.

Field guard test came first.

Combat ring against two padded attackers, not duel.

Objective: keep them from crossing chalk line behind me for sixty seconds.

Not kill.

Not score.

Protect route.

I liked it.

First attacker had staff.

Second short shield and padded club.

Both Silver assistants.

They did not wait turns.

Bell rang.

Staff came high while shield man went wide.

I caught staff on sword, stepped inside, shoved wielder into shield man's line.

Three seconds.

They recovered.

Shield pushed body, not weapon.

I gave ground without crossing chalk.

Staff hooked my ankle.

Almost fell.

Current field reality.

I used force plane against shield face.

Small pulse.

Not enough to throw man.

Enough to spoil step.

I cut padded edge to wrist.

He dropped club deliberately because hit counted disable.

Staff attacker got around me.

I sprinted, shoulder checked before chalk.

We both went down.

I rolled first.

Twenty seconds.

The test became ugly.

No elegant duel.

Hands, body, spacing.

At forty seconds I took staff hit across ribs hard enough to make breath vanish.

Still held line.

At fifty-eight, shield man feinted right and staff went left.

I guessed wrong.

Staff attacker crossed chalk with one foot as bell rang.

Failed?

Ressa wrote.

"One breach at time. Good enough for Bronze field endorsement. Not Silver guard standard."

I breathed.

"Because breach?"

"Because you chased body instead of line twice."

Fair.

No lecture beyond evidence.

Ward/load station next.

That was stranger.

A wooden platform hung from four chains inside tall frame. Three barrels lashed on it. Test operator could change tension in chains independently using hidden cranks.

Objective: identify failing load path and keep platform within painted tilt limit for thirty breaths while two workers crossed it carrying sacks.

"Magic allowed?" I asked.

"Anything safe."

I unclipped Anchor cover.

Black glass looked plain in sunlight.

Three mounting lines.

I clipped first to left frame eye.

Second right.

Third to my force handle at center plate.

Ressa watched.

"You are physically in system."

"Currently."

"Why?"

"I don't have a better control interface."

"Then if you fall?"

"Bad."

"Useful diagnosis."

We started.

Platform level.

Workers crossed.

At tenth breath, back-right chain lost tension.

Platform tilted.

I felt it through Anchor before seeing.

Force entered right line unevenly.

I redirected some load to left frame.

Platform slowed tilt.

Not stopped.

Workers adjusted feet.

"Keep moving," test lead told them.

At fifteen breath, left-front chain tightened sharply.

Opposite fault.

I shifted.

The Anchor's silver threads brightened.

My shoulders took less than glasshorn because frame carried most.

This was the object's actual language.

Not blocking a monster.

Balancing changing relationships.

At twenty breaths, operator cut one chain entirely.

Platform dropped six inches on corner.

One worker stumbled.

I fed force too hard into center.

The Anchor spread it, but my left frame line went taut enough to hum.

Wrong.

I reduced.

Use geometry, not panic.

Shifted load into back eye.

Platform stabilized just inside painted limit.

Workers crossed.

Thirty.

Bell.

My hands shook mildly.

Anchor warm.

No damage.

Test lead looked at Ressa.

"Again?"

She said, "No. Enough."

I wanted again.

That was exactly why no mattered.

Ressa wrote for a while.

Then handed card.

`PROVISIONAL LOAD/FORCE SPECIALIST INTEREST. UNUSUAL SALVAGED REGULATOR. CURRENT CONTROL FIELD-USEFUL. REQUIRES REPEAT EVIDENCE, TEAM ENDORSEMENTS, AND WINTER TRIAL.`

I read it twice.

"What does provisional mean?"

"It means we noticed you without promising anything."

"Excellent."

"For D274 path declaration, bring either Silver standing or four current field endorsements relevant to your claimed specialty plus one Silver-or-higher field sponsor willing to sign for a trial slot. By D304, you pass role trial. By D316, you pay kit share and get crew assignment."

Specific.

"How much kit share?"

She named an amount in silver that made my current purse feel decorative.

"That much?"

"Winter food, tent share, animal reserve, rope, medical pool, ward supplies. Your personal gear is separate."

There.

The Black Stair acquired price.

"Can work it off?"

"Some expedition labor credits. Not all."

"Sponsor?"

"Silver or Gold field lead who has actually worked with you. Not friend signature."

"Mira?"

"Trade endorsement, not Guild Silver. Useful but not sponsor."

"Vaska?"

"City professional. Same."

"Callen?"

"Training assistant. Has he fielded with you?"

"No."

"Then no."

Good system.

Annoying.

"So Silver is simpler."

"Usually."

"How close?"

Ressa handed my folder back.

"Ask rank desk. I build expedition, not ego."

I liked her less.

At rank desk, Sella already knew why I came.

"Silver review after summer block," she said.

"How many endorsements?"

"No fixed number."

"I hate this."

"You need a Silver lead to say they trust you on real work, not just sword ring. You need at least two multi-day contracts. You need one complex field job where you did not create the problem."

"That last part targeted?"

"Very."

"I have road jobs."

"Short."

"Glasshorn."

"One day."

"Tusker."

"One day."

"South Four."

"Half."

She tapped file.

"You are doing well. You are also doing small things quickly. Silver needs evidence you remain useful after hour three."

That landed.

The year had felt active because many events happened.

Endurance was different from moments.

I took my folder.

"Road board?"

Sella smiled.

"Summer postings tomorrow."

Of course.

Outside, spring sunlight had become almost hot.

Market awnings threw hard shadows.

The Westreach notice remained behind glass.

I took out the D001 paper.

Front:

**One year from today, I am going to stand at the Black Stair.**

Back:

D274.

D304.

D316.

D326.

I added beneath them:

**Silver review: late summer.**

Then:

**Kit share: expensive as hell.**

Not official terminology.

Accurate.

Ninety-one days had passed.

A quarter of the year almost.

I was still Bronze.

I also had a field file, new boots, a better belt, three scars I had not started with, a strange artifact clipped to my hip, money that came and went faster because I now had things worth buying, a date to see Mara, a current friendship with Tavin instead of a hole, and a professional problem called sponsorship.

The Black Stair was not closer geographically.

It was harder to dismiss.

That was enough for spring.
