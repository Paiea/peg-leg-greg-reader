# Year Chapter 14: Halden Again

Status: **TEMPORAL YEAR REHEARSAL / ZERO AUTHORITY**

`Y312 / D128 / SUMMER`

`DAYS SINCE PRIOR CHAPTER: 12`

`LOCATION: Halden`

`RANK: Bronze`

I reached Halden on the day I said I would.

This felt suspiciously competent.

The twelve days between Kelm and the river stair had been less elegant.

Beren's return cargo cleared on D117.

We left Kelm before sunrise D118.

The road gave us two hot days, one axle repair, and a bridge queue long enough that Siva taught me three ways to tie the same load line just to remain sane.

We reached Carrow D122.

Siva signed my Silver review sheet before taking payment.

`MULTI-DAY ROAD. GREG REMAINS USEFUL AFTER NOVELTY. LOAD SPECIALTY REAL. BLADE STRONG. JUDGMENT IMPROVING. WOULD FIELD AGAIN.`

I read `WOULD FIELD AGAIN` four times.

She saw.

"Don't make weird."

"Too late."

The signature mattered more than praise because it came from someone who had watched me be bored.

I spent D123 repairing gear.

D124 at Bell Tile because Dena had a filter line issue and because returning to Carrow only to leave again felt rude even when nobody asked.

D125 with Noll and Jorren for dinner.

Noll now had a new kitchen runner named Peto who apparently cut onions badly and cried when criticized.

"You criticize?" I asked.

"Yes."

"Cruelly?"

"Correctly."

Current Noll had become somebody's difficult senior.

Jorren's factor rival, the new counter he disliked, had become his friend.

Her name was Bessa.

He still disliked that she counted faster.

"Then practice," I said.

"I do."

There.

People changed while I traveled.

D126 I packed for Halden.

D127 I took river packet west.

Now D128.

Summer Halden looked nothing like the city in my letters.

It was louder.

Hotter.

The river had dropped enough to expose dark stone along lower steps. Market awnings stretched over streets. Vey barges crowded quay. Someone had painted half a building yellow and stopped midway.

No one waited for my arrival.

Mara had warned me.

Her last note:

**Boat gets in around first bell. I work until second. Green Finch knows your name. Do not become tragic.**

I had considered arriving at Vey anyway.

Instead I went to Finch.

Growth.

The blue sign was still blue.

The innkeeper remembered me? No, this was first Halden visit in this year chronology, though old quarry had one. Important. She did not know me.

"Room?"

"Greg. Mara may have left name."

She checked slate.

"Tall Greg."

I closed eyes.

"Apparently."

The room was small, clean enough, one bed, one chair, washstand, window toward roof instead of river.

I set pack down.

Blackglass Anchor made a dull knock against bedframe.

I unpacked slowly.

New boots.

Better belt.

Replacement bracer.

Glasshorn shard wrapped in cloth.

Four months of objects.

Then I bathed because road and river travel had become a social crime.

At second bell I walked to Vey.

Not early.

Mostly.

Halden streets were steeper than Carrow and apparently designed to punish people carrying luggage. I had none. Still complained internally.

Vey river yard announced itself through yellow marks and shouting.

A gate clerk stopped me.

"Business?"

"Mara."

"Which?"

Good institution.

"Mara Vale. Freight office."

He checked board.

"Working."

"I know."

"Then wait."

I waited.

Ten minutes.

Fifteen.

At twenty, a man with yellow cord came out.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

"Mara says if you are going to stand there looking abandoned, come carry boxes."

I looked at gate clerk.

He was trying not to smile.

"I was standing normally."

"I'm Emmet."

"You are immediately suspect."

He led me into yard.

Mara was not waiting at romantic river stairs.

She was on a loading platform arguing with a woman holding three ledgers.

Mara wore work coat sleeves rolled to elbows, hair tied badly, red ink on two fingers.

Four months of letters collapsed into one current body.

My chest tightened.

She saw me.

Expression changed.

Not enough to stop work.

"You're on time."

"I know. Disturbing."

"Give me twenty minutes."

"Your letter promised second bell."

"It is second bell."

"This is contract fraud."

The ledger woman looked at me.

"This him?"

Mara closed eyes.

"Sera, Greg. Greg, Sera. Do not enjoy this."

Sera smiled.

I had imagined Sera incorrectly from letters.

Current woman was broad, maybe forty, one side of head shaved, red ink across fingers, expression professionally hostile.

"Carrow problem."

"I have been branded."

"Mara complains efficiently."

"I do not complain about him," Mara said.

Sera laughed.

Jealousy I had not admitted existing disappeared partly and was replaced by embarrassment.

Good.

Mara pointed at a stack of waxed boxes.

"Those to blue platform. Emmet knows which blue."

There were five blue platforms.

I looked at Emmet.

He smiled too pleasantly.

"This way."

So my Halden reunion became freight labor.

Better than ceremony.

The boxes held ledger copies first.

Then brass buckles.

Then straps.

The strap boxes were heavier.

I carried two until Emmet said, "One."

"I can carry two."

"I know. Stack count is by trip."

Current system beat muscles.

I carried one.

Mara moved around yard without centering me. She checked marks, answered Sera, sent a runner, crossed to river clerk, came back with different board.

I watched between trips.

Emmet noticed.

"You know she comes back."

"I am not worried."

"Didn't say."

I disliked him.

Fifty-three minutes later, Mara handed her last board to Sera.

"Leaving."

Sera looked at me.

"Return him?"

"Probably."

"Paper?"

"No claim."

Terrible people.

Mara walked toward gate beside me.

We made it halfway.

Then a cart rail snapped.

Not dramatic enough to become disaster.

Three long crates leaned outward under canvas.

Workers shouted.

One brace pole went up.

Mara stopped.

Her whole body changed into work.

I stopped too.

Sera ran past us.

"Hold rear!"

A loader caught second brace.

Crates contained dyed cloth, lighter than they looked but stacked high.

Bottom box had slid off chock.

Mara looked at me.

"Can lift?"

"Yes."

"No magic unless asked."

"Understood."

Emmet and I took top crate.

Two other workers took second.

We moved them to ground.

The bottom shifted again.

Brace creaked.

Sera said, "Stop."

Everyone stopped.

One worker had hand between crate and rail.

He pulled slowly.

Clear.

Then we removed load from rear instead of side.

Ten minutes.

No collapse.

No hero.

Work.

When safe, Sera looked at me.

"Useful."

"Mara says that like insult."

"I can too."

Mara was laughing.

I loved that she looked different laughing at work than in letters.

Then we finally left.

Outside Vey gate, she turned toward me.

For one second neither of us spoke.

Four months of distance.

Packets.

Missed dates.

Work.

Hard.
