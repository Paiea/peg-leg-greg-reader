# B HARD — Brass Spoon

Status: **HIGH-FIDELITY REPLACEABLE FUTURE / NOT NUMBERED / NOT REBUILD AUTHORITY**

Fresh re-performance required before any A promotion.

---

Noll paid me back during dinner.

This was inconvenient because I was eating dinner in his new kitchen.

Not inside the kitchen.

The Brass Spoon had standards.

I sat at a small table near the rear wall where I could see through an open hatch into heat, steam, and several people moving as if the floor had started charging rent by the second.

Noll moved among them.

Different.

That was the first problem.

At Lower Rook, Noll moved like furniture had personally insulted him.

Doors hit shoulders.

Pans arrived loudly.

Knives existed wherever he had last used them.

Here, his body had discovered corners.

Knife down.

Bowl left.

Two steps back before the woman carrying stock came through.

Turn.

Pan.

Plate.

No wasted collision.

Mostly.

He nearly backed into another cook once.

The other man said, "Move your ass."

Noll moved his ass.

Institutional learning.

I had come because his fourth trial evening was tonight.

That was the official explanation.

The less official explanation was that Lower Rook had become quiet enough after dark that I had started discovering how much of my reading concentration depended on someone swearing through a wall.

Also Mara was due tomorrow or the day after.

Depending on river.

Depending on Vey.

Depending on every person who had ever touched a packet sack between Halden and Carrow.

I was not thinking about that constantly.

Only whenever time moved.

The Brass Spoon offered another place to put an evening.

A woman came to my table.

Not server.

Too much authority.

Dark hair pinned high.

Sleeves rolled past both elbows.

A thin burn scar along one forearm.

"You Greg?"

I looked toward Noll.

He was pretending not to hear.

"Depends."

The woman's expression did not move.

"He borrowed money from you."

"Then unfortunately yes."

"Vessa."

There.

Current person.

No gray hair from whatever old memory had tried to invent.

No scarred spoon hand either.

Just a woman perhaps thirty-five with a kitchen that clearly knew her name better than I did.

"He said you eat."

"Strong reference."

"What do you want?"

I looked at the menu board.

Prices confirmed that chairs were part of the meal.

"Dumplings."

Vessa's eyes shifted toward the hatch.

Noll's head remained down.

"Of course."

She left.

I smiled.

Noll looked up just long enough to see it.

He mouthed something I could not hear.

Probably affectionate.

A spoon hit the wall inside the kitchen.

Hard.

Nobody screamed.

I leaned slightly to see.

A broad man near the stove had one empty hand raised.

Vessa turned her head.

"Domm."

One word.

He looked at the spoon on the floor.

Then at her.

"It slipped."

"Again and I'll slip you."

Domm picked it up.

Management remained culturally diverse.

Noll did not react.

That bothered me more than the spoon.

At Lower Rook he would have had three comments ready before metal stopped ringing.

Here he plated something, wiped the edge with a cloth, and passed it forward.

Work-self.

I had known the man for weeks through noise, fire, soap, food, and unauthorized access to my room.

Apparently that did not include all available Nolls.

The dumplings arrived.

Six.

Not Noll's old fold.

Tighter at the top.

Smaller.

They sat in broth with chopped green onion and something spicy enough to smell before tasting.

I waited until Noll looked toward the hatch.

Then ate one.

Better.

This was intolerable.

The wrapper held.

Filling had changed too.

Less pork.

More onion.

Sharper vinegar.

No.

Different vinegar.

I knew enough to avoid turning dinner into an audit.

I ate a second.

Noll's mouth twitched from across the room.

Bastard.

The rush came in waves.

Tables filled.

Emptied.

Filled again.

I had never spent much time watching restaurants from the customer side.

Old Greg had eaten in hundreds.

Usually between things.

Meals were fuel with chairs attached.

Later money had improved the chairs.

I remembered complaining about service in one city after a dungeon run where three people had nearly died six hours earlier.

That seemed embarrassing now.

A bowl reached a table because a washer kept bowls moving.

A server remembered who ordered what.

Domm stopped throwing spoons long enough to produce a sauce that made my third dumpling better than the first two.

Noll worked a station I could not name.

Vessa controlled enough moving parts to qualify as hostile weather.

None of them knew or cared that I had once been S-class.

Excellent restaurant.

I ordered beer.

The server charged me before bringing it.

Less excellent.

Near the end of the rush, Noll burned his wrist.

Small.

He touched the edge of a hot pan with the wrong part of his hand, jerked away, swore once, shoved the wrist under a water crock tap, then went back to the board.

I stood halfway.

Stopped.

No one else had moved.

Vessa saw him.

"Skin?"

Noll looked.

"Red."

"Blister?"

"No."

"Then work."

He worked.

I sat.

That was harder than it should have been.

Not because Vessa was wrong.

Because the man through my wall had acquired a burn inside a life that did not contain my intervention.

Apparently friendship had jurisdiction problems.

I drank the beer.

By late bell, three tables remained.

The kitchen sound changed.

Not quiet.

Less compressed.

Noll started speaking again.

I heard him call Domm something rude.

Domm threw a towel instead of a spoon.

Improvement.

Vessa came to my table carrying my bill.

I looked at the number.

"The dumplings cost this much?"

"Chair."

"I knew it."

"Noll says you're cheap."

"Noll owes me money."

"He says that too."

I paid.

No discount.

I respected her less professionally and more personally.

Noll emerged twenty minutes later with damp hair at the temples and flour on his cuff.

Not the same flour Jorren had carried home.

Different occupational hazard.

He sat across from me.

Then immediately stood again.

"Forgot."

He went back through the hatch.

Returned with two copper.

Put them on the table.

Exactly two.

I looked at them.

"You waited until I paid full price."

"Yes."

"Strategic."

"Vessa said don't give friends things."

I looked toward the kitchen.

Vessa was counting something beside the register.

"She runs your finances too?"

"She runs everything."

"Terrifying."

Noll pushed the coins closer.

"Pork."

There they were again.

Tavin's disputed debt.

Noll's ingredients.

Now repayment on a table expensive enough to charge for sitting.

I put the coins in my purse.

No ceremony.

Mostly.

"Well?" Noll asked.

"What?"

"Dumplings."

I stared at him.

He smiled.

Current revenge.

"Very good."

"Better."

"You changed vinegar."

"Vessa."

"Of course."

"And fold."

"I noticed."

"Too slow."

"You were here four nights."

"She does twice as fast."

There was no self-pity in it.

Just measurement.

He wanted that speed.

Not because anyone had assigned him progression.

Because he had seen a person do the thing he wanted to do better than he could.

I understood that feeling too well to insult it.

"She keeping you?"

Noll looked toward the kitchen.

Vessa was talking to one of the servers.

"Maybe."

"You don't know?"

"She said wait."

"For what?"

"Her."

Right.

We waited.

This was new territory for both of us.

Noll tapped two fingers against the table.

Not nervous.

Probably.

I chose not to classify it.

"You like it?" I asked.

He stopped tapping.

"Mostly."

"Domm?"

"Sauce is good."

Apparently that excused several crimes.

"Hours?"

"Bad."

"Pay?"

"Better."

"Lower Rook?"

Noll looked at me.

"Still there."

"I meant kitchen."

"I know."

He looked toward the front windows.

"They want five nights if she keeps me."

That was more than the trial.

"You want five?"

"No."

Interesting.

I left it alone long enough for him to continue.

"Four."

"Why?"

"Lower Rook gets carters fifth night. Maud pays me if I cook."

There.

Not sentiment.

Money.

Also perhaps something else.

No need to improve it.

"Pays enough?"

"Different."

"How different?"

"Less."

"Then why?"

Noll frowned at me.

"I live there."

Simple enough to hurt.

Vessa arrived before I could make it complicated.

She stood at the table.

Noll looked up.

"Four," she said.

Noll's eyebrows moved.

"Four?"

"Four nights. Fifth if I ask before noon and you want it. Two meals. Same pay per shift."

Noll stared at her.

Then at me.

I kept my face still.

This was not my negotiation.

"Why?" he asked.

Vessa folded her arms.

"Because you said five makes you leave the other kitchen."

"Yes."

"I don't need you five enough to pay what would make you stop caring."

That was a strange sentence.

Noll seemed to understand it.

"Four."

"Trial wage one more week. Then up two copper a shift if you stop opening wrappers in the pot."

"They opened once tonight."

"Three."

"Two."

Vessa looked at me.

"Does he always negotiate things he cannot count?"

"Yes."

Noll looked betrayed.

"Three," Vessa said.

"Fine."

She held out her hand.

Noll shook it.

Done.

No paper.

No guild mark.

Kitchen employment apparently lived dangerously.

Vessa walked away.

Noll remained sitting.

I waited.

His face did not do much.

Then he looked at the two empty copper spots on the table where repayment had been.

"Four."

"Yes."

He smiled.

Small.

"Good."

I smiled back.

"Very good."

He kicked my boot under the table.

We walked home after the last chairs went upside down.

Carrow was colder at night now.

Noll carried a paper packet Vessa had given him.

"What's that?"

"Bones."

"Why?"

"Broth tomorrow."

"Lower Rook?"

"Yes."

The Brass Spoon had hired him and sent its bones home with him.

Complicated institution.

At Lower Rook, he went into the cookroom instead of upstairs.

Put the bones in the cold box.

Checked the stove.

Then climbed the stairs beside me.

At my door he stopped.

"Mara tomorrow?"

"Maybe."

Noll looked at me.

Right.

Forbidden word.

"River," I said.

"Still maybe."

"I promised to be here. I did not promise physics."

"Dangerous."

"Go to bed."

He did.

His door closed.

A chair scraped.

Then quiet.

Four nights a week, apparently, my wall belonged partly to The Brass Spoon now...
