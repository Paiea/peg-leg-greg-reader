# Chapter 165: The Contractor

The day after Merek's contract closed, nobody needed me.

I checked twice.

Not the same nobody both times.

Noll needed someone to move a crate of dried beans from the front room to the pantry, but he had already paid Kess to do it and objected when I offered because apparently employment had boundaries now.

Pell needed three measurements from the field peg washer.

He did not need me present while he took them.

Jorren was still out on city coordination work.

Mara was in Northbank making ferry schedules obey her.

Merek had not appeared at the Guild asking for another favor.

Good.

I had specifically decided not to check whether he had applied for anything.

I checked the public board instead.

Different crime.

Carrow Guild at first bell smelled like wet coats, ink, old wood, and the sausage cart outside that had learned exactly how far smoke could travel through the open doors before Tessa came around the desk to complain.

The cart had not learned enough.

Neither had Tessa.

She shut the door.

The smoke continued through the upper windows.

I approved of persistence.

The board held the normal mix.

Warehouse inspection.

Two-day escort to East Mill.

Roof load review.

Drainage survey.

One notice for a missing goat that had been reposted so many times the paper had acquired authority through age.

I was reading the goat description when Tessa said, "Don't."

I looked over.

"I didn't say anything."

"You were about to."

"Goat looks capable."

"Greg."

"Strong horns."

She put a packet on the counter.

Thick.

Red thread.

Guild wax over a western-road seal.

I stopped caring about the goat.

"That's rude," I said.

"To the goat?"

"To my morning."

"You can keep reading livestock notices."

I crossed the room.

Tessa put one finger on the packet before I could take it.

"Hazard premium."

"Good start."

"Eight contracted days. Twelve-day maximum hold if weather closes."

"Less good."

"Western coast. Storm Road."

I looked at her.

She nodded once.

There were several roads people called storm roads when they wanted travelers to understand why the inns were cheap.

This one earned the capital letters.

Old coastal freight route.

Cliff cut.

Suspended spans.

Stone shelters every few miles where the old builders had expected weather to become an enemy with scheduling rights.

Still used because the alternative added six days to coastal freight.

Mostly safe in the proper season.

Mostly.

"Early storm?" I asked.

"Three days ago."

She released the packet.

I opened it.

The first page was not a contract.

It was a road notice.

**STORM ROAD WESTERN SECTION: LIMITED PASSAGE.**

**WARD COVER PARTIAL FAILURE BETWEEN MARKERS 18–31.**

**TWO SUSPENDED SPANS REQUIRE CONTROLLED LOAD CROSSING.**

**NEXT MAJOR WEATHER BAND ESTIMATED TEN TO FOURTEEN DAYS.**

Estimated.

Weather loved professional language.

I turned the page.

Consortium convoy.

Mixed cargo.

Medicine crates.

Machine bearings.

Lamp crystal.

Dyed cloth.

Two sealed merchant chests with values listed separately and with enough zeroes to become interesting.

Nothing that would destroy a city if lost.

Several things people would be angry not to receive.

Better.

Third page.

Requested support categories.

Wind specialist.

Road-span mechanic.

Animal control / close defense.

Moving-load support.

That last line had been added in darker ink.

I read it again.

"Who wrote that category?"

"Iria."

"Who is Iria?"

"Convoy lead."

"How does Iria know I exist?"

Tessa looked at me over the counter.

"You keep filing work."

"Suspicious."

"The West Bramble closeout mentions moving anchors. Hearthline's last bounded consult mentions controlled wall travel instead of static resistance. Red Quay filed you as useful field-load support twice."

"They filed me?"

"People do paperwork when you leave."

Disturbing system.

I turned the page.

Pay.

Then turned it back.

Then forward again.

"That's high."

"Hazard premium."

"Still high."

"Weather window."

There was also a completion share if the convoy reached the western road gate before the next major closure.

Not enormous.

Enough to make people make poor decisions near the end of a long day.

Good contract design.

I checked injury terms.

Better than expected.

Equipment damage allowance.

Moderate.

Prosthetic-specific reimbursement line.

I looked at Tessa.

"That wasn't standard."

"No."

"You added it."

"I asked them to add it."

"Why?"

"Because you keep paying for your own socket damage on contracts where the employer specifically benefits from you having a usable leg."

I considered this.

"Aggressive administration."

"You're welcome."

I read the route summary.

Carrow to Halden by coach.

West from Halden with the convoy to the roadhead.

Then the Storm Road proper.

Five days expected crossing if weather behaved.

Weather had already demonstrated attitude.

"When?"

"Coach leaves tomorrow before first bell."

"Of course it does."

"You can say no."

I read the moving-load category again.

There were jobs I could do because I had lived long enough to become tolerable at many things.

There were jobs where people hired a better wardwright.

A better rigger.

A better swordsman.

A better route reader.

Good.

They should.

This one had a box I had not seen on many contracts before.

Not structural support.

Not general support.

Moving-load support.

Someone had named the problem closer to what I actually did.

That was irritatingly effective.

"Who else?" I asked.

Tessa pulled a smaller sheet from under the counter.

"Lead is Iria Dane. Silver logistics certification, caravan command, twelve western crossings. Evin Holt on wind. Kelm Orr on span hardware. Sera on close defense and animals. Head teamster is Pavo Renn."

I stopped at one name.

"Sera already signed?"

"Yesterday."

That made sense.

Sera liked jobs with clear pay and no requirement to pretend personal growth was the point.

Useful worldview.

"Kelm?"

"Road mechanic. No Guild combat rank."

"Magic?"

"Minimal."

"How old?"

"Why?"

"Curiosity."

Tessa checked the sheet.

"Fifty-one."

"Good."

"Why is that good?"

"Means if he's bad at the road he's had time to become impressively bad."

"Or he knows it."

"Possible."

I read Evin's line.

Wind shaping.

Charged-air bleed.

Storm vane calibration.

Not my lane.

Excellent.

Iria had left one handwritten note at the bottom.

**Need support who can work around specialists without replacing them.**

I read that twice.

Tessa noticed.

"Still thinking about the goat?"

"Goat seems simpler."

"Pays four silver."

"Storm Road?"

She tapped the contract.

"You can count."

I could.

That was part of the problem.

I had enough Carrow work to survive.

Enough saved to avoid panic.

No urgent Harrow payment anymore.

No Mara clock in the next twelve days.

Northbank was farther away than I liked whether I stayed home or went west.

This was not necessity.

Just good money attached to a job I wanted to understand.

Those were more dangerous than necessity sometimes.

I signed the interest line.

Not acceptance.

Tessa took the packet back.

"Briefing fourth bell."

"Today?"

"Tomorrow's coach."

"Hostile scheduling."

"You can still find the goat."

I left before she could improve the argument.

Outside, the sausage cart had won.

I bought one.

Then another because planning required calories.

Noll was in the pantry when I got home, reorganizing jars by some system that treated dried beans as morally superior to lentils.

"Going west," I said.

He did not turn around.

"When?"

"Tomorrow."

"How long?"

"Eight days contracted. Maybe twelve."

"Dangerous?"

"Moderately."

He turned then.

"Your moderately or normal moderately?"

"Contract moderately."

"Worse."

"Probably."

Noll picked up one jar, moved it three inches, and put it back exactly where it had been.

"Dinner tonight."

"Yes."

"Not seventh bell after work."

"Yes."

"Actual dinner."

"You have become controlling."

"You missed the last one."

"Different chapter."

He stared at me.

I had no idea why I had said that.

"Different week," I corrected.

"You work too much."

"Apparently."

I went upstairs.

The blue-green ceramic snail from West Bramble sat on the shelf beside two loose bolts and a folded Northbank letter.

Legal salvage.

Excellent object.

I moved it aside and took out paper.

Mara's last letter had arrived yesterday.

She had changed the ferry cutoff by another six minutes.

A clerk named Bess hated her.

A clerk named Torren had started bringing her tea without asking.

She had found a bakery that was better than Halden's and more expensive than dignity allowed.

At the bottom she had written:

**Come when you can. Not when you think you should.**

Annoying sentence.

I wrote beneath a fresh date.

**Going west for a road contract. Eight days if weather behaves. Twelve if it remembers what it is. Sera is on the crew. I do not know the others yet. Pay is offensively good.**

I stopped.

Then added:

**I want the job.**

That felt worth clarifying.

I folded the letter.

Packed two shirts.

Unpacked one.

Packed it again.

Field peg.

Walking leg.

Socket liners.

Repair cord.

Sword.

Notebook.

No magical lens stolen from anyone.

Progress.

At dinner, Noll made cabbage stew with too much pepper.

I ate two bowls.

He asked nothing about specialization, destiny, or whether I was becoming the sort of man who priced himself by failure paths.

He asked whether I had packed dry socks.

I had not.

I went upstairs and added three pairs.

Important systems first.
