# Chapter 172: The Customer

Maelin charged by the day.

This offended me less than the sale price.

"Rental?" I asked.

"Field cuff. Three days. Deposit separate."

"How separate?"

She named the number.

I looked at Kelm.

He was pretending not to listen while sorting bronze keeper pins on a bench.

"That's robbery."

Maelin shrugged.

"Then don't rent it."

"Strong sales technique."

"I set storm vanes. Selling things is what I do when someone interrupts me."

Fair.

Her repair shed smelled like metal oil, wet stone, old leather, and the sharp clean scent that followed small magical discharge.

Six cuffs hung on pegs along one wall.

Not identical.

Two narrow brass field models.

One heavy iron thing with four glass nodes.

One leather-backed band that looked newer than the rest.

And two so old their metal had gone almost brown.

The flea-market cuff in Halden had looked like the narrow brass type.

Dead inventory.

Here it was just equipment.

I liked that less than I expected.

Mystery made refusal easier.

Tools came with rates.

"Explain it from the beginning," I said.

Maelin stared.

"You already know what it does."

"I know what a seller in Halden told me."

"Ah."

That explained something to her.

"Dead inventory?"

"Yes."

"Then he probably told you it makes repeated patterns easier."

"Faster."

"Close enough for thieves."

She took one narrow cuff off the wall.

Three brass bands joined by dark leather.

Four small glass nodes along the inner side.

No spikes.

No teeth.

No obvious evil.

Disappointing.

"Cuff doesn't make the shape," Maelin said. "You do. It notices which path you keep taking to get there. Then it starts laying that path down flatter."

"Laying?"

"Bad word."

"Very."

"Fine. You know when you've done one technique enough that body reaches before thought finishes?"

"Yes."

"That. Faster."

"How much faster?"

"Depends person, family, duration, how stupid they are."

"Useful categories."

She handed me the cuff.

Cold metal.

Lighter than it looked.

"You prime it with one family," she said. "Not one exact shape. Family. Mine is rotational pressure around fixed hardware."

"Could prime moving fixed points."

"If that's a coherent family in your head."

"It is."

Kelm snorted from the bench.

"Of course it is."

Maelin continued.

"Once it's primed, every time you use that family, cuff helps the route arrive cleaner. Less correction. Less hesitation."

"And other families?"

"Nothing happens to them directly."

I waited.

She smiled.

"You hate that answer."

"It sounds incomplete."

"It is."

She leaned against the worktable.

"Problem is your head likes the easy door. Make one door very easy and you start using it first. Other doors get dusty."

"That's training, not magic."

"Both. Cuff pushes harder than ordinary training. Glass holds the path after you stop thinking about it."

"Reversible?"

"Sometimes."

"Bad answer."

"True answer."

I looked at her left hand.

"Your broad barrier."

"Used to be faster."

"Could you retrain it?"

"Some."

"Why don't you?"

"Because I don't want the job that needs it."

There.

Again.

Not damage in the abstract.

Life preference.

"How long did you use your first cuff?"

"Six weeks on and off."

"Continuous?"

"No. Work blocks. Vane setting. Returned home. Took it off. Put it back on next morning."

"When did you notice broad work slowing?"

"Second week."

"Permanent by?"

"No idea."

"You didn't test?"

"I was twenty-five and making triple hazard pay."

Reasonable.

Dangerous.

Familiar.

"Field use for hours?" I asked.

"Bias. Maybe a day or two of weirdness. Maybe longer if you push hard."

"One day?"

"Could stick."

"How likely?"

"Nobody honest gives you a percentage."

"Someone dishonest?"

"Seventy-two."

"Very specific."

"Costs extra."

Kelm laughed from the pins.

I turned the cuff over.

Small maker's mark inside.

Three triangles around a dot.

Same mark as the Halden cuff.

"Same maker."

Maelin leaned closer.

"Old Derren line. They made thousands."

"Vendor acted like it was rare."

"Vendor had one customer in three years. Rare enough for him."

Good.

The universe had not arranged a singular artifact around me.

Better.

"Can I fit it without priming?"

"Yes."

She took my left wrist.

I moved my hand before remembering she had asked nothing.

Maelin stopped.

"No?"

"Fine."

She waited until I put my wrist back.

Good habit.

She wrapped the leather beneath the brass bands and tightened until two fingers still fit under the edge.

"Too loose for use," she said. "Right for sizing."

I flexed.

Weight barely noticeable.

"Why left?"

"You cast more from left?"

"Sometimes."

"You touched my cuff with left first."

I looked at her.

"That's not a reason."

"Worked."

Annoying woman.

She loosened it again.

"Field model has a break cord."

She showed me a thin red thread through the rear clasp.

"Pull hard, clasp opens. Doesn't undo whatever patterning already happened. Just gets metal off you if node overheats or you need full skin contact for something else."

"Overheat common?"

"No."

"Possible?"

"Everything useful gets hot eventually."

Kelm said, "Put that on a sign."

I asked, "Why do road crews keep them if you hate them?"

"I don't hate them," Kelm said.

"You told Maelin not to sell me one."

"Because you haven't met a trade you don't think you can out-negotiate."

"Unfair."

"Faultglass."

I went still.

He did not know the whole story.

Apparently enough traveled.

Kelm looked at me over the bench.

"Road people talk."

"Bad habit."

"Useful one."

Maelin did not ask.

Good.

Kelm picked up a keeper pin.

"Cuffs are fine. Like rope. Like braces. Like strong drink. Fine until user decides tool means consequence stopped existing."

"You ever use one?"

"No."

"Why?"

He held up both hands.

"My magic is shit."

Simple.

Maelin said, "His hardware sense is better than most setters'."

Kelm scowled.

"Hands aren't magic."

"Still true."

I looked at the cuff.

My current problem was not unknown anymore.

Half a heartbeat.

Hook.

Wheel.

Mule.

Wirewing.

Tarp.

Crate.

Each solution had existed.

Arrival was late.

That was exactly what the cuff offered to attack.

Not power.

Path length.

"Three-day rental," I said.

Kelm swore.

Maelin named the deposit again.

"Two days," I countered.

"Road takes three."

"If weather holds."

"If weather doesn't, you'll want four."

"Three-day price for four if road closure traps us."

She considered.

"Closure certified by Iria."

"Fine."

"Deposit full."

"Half."

"Full."

"Three quarters."

"Full."

"You're bad at bargaining."

"I'm not bargaining."

Strong position.

I paid full deposit.

Not from panic.

Not from necessity.

From available cash that would hurt enough to matter if I lost it.

Maelin wrote a rental sheet.

**FIELD SETTER CUFF. Derren narrow three-band.**

**UNPRIMED AT ISSUE.**

**KNOWN EFFECT: acceleration / reinforcement of repeated casting family.**

**KNOWN TRADEOFF: access latency may increase in non-reinforced families. Duration variable. Persistence possible.**

There.

Documentation.

Kelm read over my shoulder.

"Happy?"

"More."

"Of course."

Maelin wrapped the cuff in oiled cloth.

Then in a second cloth.

Then put it into a narrow wooden box.

No ceremony.

She handed it to me.

"Don't prime it accidentally."

"How?"

"You can't. Needs deliberate first sequence. Three casts, same family, clasp closed, nodes in contact."

"Good."

"And don't put it on because you paid for it."

I looked at her.

"Kelm already said that."

"Then maybe listen to one of us."

I put the box into my pack.

Did not open it again.

At dinner, Sera noticed the extra box immediately.

"You bought a bad idea."

"Rented."

"Economical bad idea."

"Exactly."

Iria held out her hand.

Not asking for the cuff.

Paper.

I gave her the rental sheet.

She read every line.

Then read it again.

"This comes on road under my command."

"It's mine."

"Your wrist is yours. Whether we pause convoy so you can experiment is mine."

"Fair."

"You prime it only after you tell me."

I considered objecting.

Did not.

"Agreed."

"If I order retreat, cuff doesn't change that."

"Agreed."

She handed the sheet back.

Sera said, "This is disappointingly responsible."

"Give us time," I said.

Kelm threw a crust at me.

It hit my shoulder.

Station bread finally found purpose.
