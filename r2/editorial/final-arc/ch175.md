# Chapter 175: The Pacer

The weather arrived as pressure before it arrived as cloud.

I woke before first bell because my left ear hurt.

Not badly.

Just enough that I swallowed twice and sat up annoyed.

Across the shelter, Evin was already awake.

He had both rods laid across his knees.

Neither touched anything.

Both hummed.

"That bad?" I asked.

"Not yet."

"Useful phrase."

"You asked before coffee."

Fair.

Outside, the sky was still clear over the road.

West was different.

A dark line sat low above the sea.

Not tall storm cloud.

Flat.

Dense.

Like someone had drawn charcoal along the horizon and was waiting to smear it inland.

Iria came out with boots unlaced.

Evin did not need to say anything.

She looked west once.

"How much?"

"Band sped up overnight."

"Again."

"Yes."

"Far gate?"

"Possible today if we keep moving."

"Tomorrow?"

Evin watched the dark line.

"I don't like tomorrow."

Iria turned back inside.

"Wake everyone."

Breakfast became bread, cold sausage, and whatever could be eaten while tying cargo.

Pavo complained only once.

Serious weather.

We left before the sun cleared the cliff.

Road from twenty-eight to thirty ran mostly inland through broken black hills.

Faster terrain.

Less drop.

More old storm posts.

We made good time.

Iria called ten-minute checks instead of long halts.

Pavo hated them because ten minutes was enough to discover problems and not enough to enjoy sitting.

Kelm loved them because sitting was suspicious.

At marker twenty-nine, one storm post had split down the center.

Evin stopped.

Kelm said, "We can pass."

Evin said, "Charge is bleeding into ground wrong."

"Still pass."

"I didn't say we couldn't."

"Then why stop?"

"Because I enjoy your voice."

Iria said, "Two minutes."

They solved it in three.

Kelm drove a copper spike into the downhill soil while Evin rerouted the post line with a temporary braid.

I held nothing.

Pavo moved nothing.

Sera ate dried pear.

Then we continued.

The cuff box rode under my spare shirt.

I knew exactly where it was without checking.

Annoying.

I started timing myself instead.

Not with a clock.

With Pavo.

Harness movement.

Tongue movement.

Bed response.

Wheel consequence.

At each turn I tried to form the correction before the wheel told me I was right.

Not cast.

Just form decision.

Sometimes I was early and wrong.

Sometimes late and right.

Pavo noticed around third bell.

"Stop predicting mule."

"I'm not."

"You made that face before Toma moved."

"What face?"

"The one everyone complains about."

Conspiracy.

He pointed at the lead harness.

"Don't guess. Read."

"I am reading."

"You're reading next page."

Sera, walking beside the wagon, laughed.

"I told him that with swords."

"Everyone is very proud of being right at me."

Pavo ignored this.

"Watch shoulder."

I watched the mule's shoulder.

Not harness.

Muscle tightened before the step.

Tiny.

The animal shifted left to avoid a stone.

Harness ring followed.

Tongue after.

Earlier signal.

"There," Pavo said.

"You could have told me shoulder yesterday."

"You weren't ready."

"That sounds invented."

"Worked though."

I wanted to dislike him.

Could not spare attention.

The road began climbing again near noon.

Sea returned on our right.

Wind stronger.

Not dangerous yet.

Enough that every loose strap spoke.

We passed an eastbound road crew at marker thirty.

Six workers.

One little cart.

All tired.

Their lead stopped Iria.

"Thirty-one vane is down."

Kelm swore.

"Which?"

"Upper return fin. Whole arm. Fell road-side, not cliff."

"Span?"

"Still open. West crosswind ugly after noon."

Evin asked, "Charge?"

"High at vane. Lower on deck."

Iria said, "Shelter?"

"Thirty-one open."

"Far gate?"

The road lead looked west.

"We left at dawn. Weather was behind us."

Evin said, "Not anymore."

They traded route notes for three minutes.

Then the eastbound crew moved on.

No prophecy.

No dying warning.

Just workers going home.

Kelm walked beside me after.

"Down vane matters."

"How?"

"Upper return fin keeps wind from hammering final approach sideways."

"Can repair?"

"Not today."

"Can compensate?"

"Evin can tell us that."

Good answer.

We reached shelter thirty-one at fourth bell.

Nobody wanted to stop.

Iria stopped anyway.

Full check.

Pavo found one hot wheel bearing.

Not failing.

Hot.

He repacked grease.

Kelm climbed the shelter roof to see final vane line.

Evin went with him.

Sera walked animals under cover.

I took the field peg off and checked skin.

Fine.

Better than yesterday.

I ate cheese standing up.

Ten minutes became twenty.

Kelm and Evin came down.

Evin's face had changed.

Not fear.

Calculation tightened.

"Band is visible from roof."

Iria said, "Time."

"Maybe three hours to first real front."

"Far gate?"

Pavo answered.

"Two and a half if road clear."

Silence.

Merchant factor said, "Then we go."

Iria looked at him.

He remembered hierarchy.

Kelm spread the final road sketch on a barrel.

From shelter thirty-one, road crossed one short ledge, then a long exposed traverse, then the last suspended span before the far freight gate.

The fallen vane sat near start of traverse.

Span itself was sound on last survey.

Weather was the problem.

Evin pointed at the approach.

"Without upper return fin, side gusts will hit road harder. Once front arrives, I don't want wagons on traverse."

"Before?" Iria asked.

"Passable."

"How passable?"

"I hate that question."

"Answer anyway."

"If we move now, likely fine. Wind rising. Charge rising. Front timing uncertain by maybe half an hour."

"Retreat point?"

Kelm tapped shelter thirty-one.

"Here. Once we commit past fallen vane, turning wagons around on traverse is ugly."

"Can shelter on far side?"

"Small stone bay before final span. Fits people and maybe two wagons, not whole convoy."

Iria thought.

Then looked at all of us.

"We move to fallen vane. Assess there. No completion premium decides weather. If Evin says stop, we stop. If Kelm says road hardware is wrong, we stop. If Pavo says teams can't hold, we stop."

Then at me.

"If you say you're getting behind the problem, we stop."

That last one hit differently.

"Understood."

"Cuff stays packed unless I approve priming."

"Understood."

We repacked.

The merchant factor did not argue.

Good sign.

Bad weather.

The convoy rolled out again.

I walked beside wagon three.

Shoulder.

Harness.

Tongue.

Bed.

Wheel.

Earlier information.

Not prediction.

Pace.

The black line over the sea had become a wall.

Still far.

Not far enough.

I timed my breathing to the wagon movement.

One correction.

Release.

Another.

No cuff.

Faster than three days ago.

Still human speed.

For now, enough.
