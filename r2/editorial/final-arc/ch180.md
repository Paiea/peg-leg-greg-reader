# Chapter 180: The Earner

The storm lasted eleven hours.

This mattered less than the accounting.

At least to the merchant factor.

By morning, the far freight gate yard had become mud, broken branches, wet rope, and people discovering which parts of yesterday's emergency now belonged to paperwork.

Four wagons stood under sheds.

All intact enough to bill somebody.

One mule had a burned shoulder patch from the fallen vane discharge.

Another had lost two harness straps and gained an opinion about Sera.

Sera had a cut forearm, bruised boot, and no sympathy for the mule.

Pavo had not slept much.

Kelm had slept sitting against a wheel.

Evin looked worse than weather.

I felt excellent.

This was suspicious.

Not rested.

Everything hurt in normal amounts.

Right shoulder from the bridge.

Lower back from travel.

Socket edge tender.

Left wrist red where cuff had sat.

But my head felt clear.

Too clear around one thing.

I noticed it when a loose tarp corner snapped in the yard.

My hand moved.

A small plane appeared beside the rope before I consciously decided whether the rope needed help.

It did not.

The rope was tied.

Plane vanished.

I looked at my wrist.

No cuff.

Interesting.

Kelm was awake enough to notice.

"Still there?"

"Some."

"Maelin told you."

"She did."

"You look pleased."

"It was fast."

"Of course you look pleased."

He stood with a sound usually associated with damaged furniture.

"Try something else."

"What?"

Kelm pointed at a warped shed door.

Wind had pulled the upper hinge partly out.

Door leaned enough that it would not close.

Broad brace problem.

Simple.

I had done hundreds.

I raised one hand.

Built a flat plane across upper third.

Nothing happened for half a breath.

Not nothing.

The shape was there in my head.

Path longer.

I reached again.

Plane formed.

Edges sloppy for an instant.

Then settled.

I pushed door into frame while a worker reset hinge pin.

Easy.

Slow.

Kelm watched.

"Again."

"No."

"Why?"

"Worker fixed door."

"Coward."

I found a different wall.

Formed broad plane.

Same delay.

Maybe one full breath this time because I was paying attention.

It arrived.

Held fine.

No weakness once built.

Just farther away.

I dropped it.

Then made a moving palm-sized plane beside my boot.

Instant.

Moved it around the boot.

Up.

Down.

Rotate.

Release.

Like touching a familiar tool in dark.

Kelm scratched beard.

"There."

"Yes."

"How bad?"

I formed broad plane again.

Slower.

Still available.

"Annoying."

"That's your assessment?"

"Accurate."

"Manageable?"

I looked at him.

He smiled.

Bastard knew me already.

"Probably."

"There it is."

He walked away.

I tested twice more when nobody was looking.

Broad barrier slower.

Simple static brace slower.

One old lifting shape took two attempts before it felt clean.

Moving correction family did not merely remain fast.

It felt natural in a new way.

I did not have to assemble orientation, reference, travel limit, release.

Those pieces had stopped being separate decisions.

That was what I had paid for.

I wrote it down.

Not because writing fixed anything.

Because future me deserved accurate terms.

**Cuff effect persists after removal. Moving reference / last-travel family substantially faster. Broad static family accessible but delayed. Strength unchanged once formed. Test after rest.**

No dramatic underline.

Then breakfast.

Far-gate kitchen served fried potatoes, eggs, and coffee strong enough to repair small structural flaws.

I ate beside Sera.

She had bandage off her arm now.

Cut ugly but shallow.

"How's magic?" she asked.

"Narrower."

"Worth it?"

I ate potato.

"Yesterday?"

"Yes."

"Today?"

"Ask later."

"Annoying answer."

"Accurate."

She stole one of my potatoes.

Punishment.

At second bell, Iria called closeout.

Cargo first.

Wagon one medicine complete.

Wagon two lamp crystal complete, one crate corner cracked, contents intact.

Wagon three machine bearings complete, merchant-chest cradle absent.

Wagon four copper wire complete, dyed cloth complete except one rain-damaged bale, glass instruments complete, maintenance tool chest damaged with two missing small tools and one bent wrench.

Pavo disputed the wrench because it had been bent before the storm.

The tool owner disputed Pavo.

Iria wrote **PREEXISTING / CONTESTED** and moved on.

Civilization.

People.

All present.

Animals.

All present.

Injuries.

Minor.

Road authority had already sent a recovery crew east after wind dropped.

They expected to reach the chained merchant cradle by midday if Span remained open.

Merchant factor became almost human again after hearing this.

"If the chests are recovered—"

Iria said, "Then they are recovered."

"Completion value—"

"Accounting will calculate after road authority signs condition."

He visibly suffered.

Good.

Pay packets came at fourth bell.

Base contract.

Hazard premium.

Completion share provisional pending chest recovery.

Equipment damage reimbursement.

Pavo got harness straps approved.

Sera's boot reimbursement denied because kicking loose iron was classified as combat use, not equipment failure.

She appealed.

Of course.

My prosthetic line paid for one replacement liner and socket service if needed within ten days.

Tessa had done good work.

I counted money twice.

Offensively good remained accurate.

Even without completion share.

Cuff rental deposit came next.

Road station clerk had Maelin's paperwork.

Storm closure certified.

Fourth day fee waived per rental terms.

I handed cuff over wrapped in original cloth.

Clerk inspected bands.

Glass nodes.

Break cord missing because I had used it.

Normal field consumption.

Deposit returned minus one small cleaning fee.

"Cleaning?"

Clerk pointed at salt stain.

"Weather."

"Still cleaning."

"Hostile industry."

I paid.

No artifact kept.

No secret decision to steal one because it helped.

Progress again.

At fifth bell, recovery crew signal came by road mirror.

Merchant cradle located.

Both chests intact.

One chain anchor bent.

Recovery scheduled after span inspection.

Merchant factor almost cried.

Completion share moved from provisional to likely.

Iria closed packet.

"We're done."

No speech.

No medals.

People immediately developed travel problems.

Sera had another escort offer leaving from the west gate in two days.

Pavo's freight crew stayed with wagons for unloading.

Evin planned to sleep twelve hours, then inspect a coastal tower because weather apparently had not respected his contract end.

Kelm was going back east with the road crew after one day of rest.

"You're going back onto span?" I asked.

"Someone has to replace keeper before merchant chest comes through."

"You're fifty-one."

"And?"

"Nothing."

"Good."

He left before I could say anything sentimental.

Iria found me packing.

"Consortium wants your contact rate."

I stopped.

"For what?"

"Future moving-load work."

There it was already.

"General structural?"

"They asked moving-load and failure routing specifically."

I looked at my left wrist.

Skin red.

No cuff.

"Rate?"

"You tell me."

I named a number higher than my Carrow standard.

Iria did not blink.

"Too low."

I stared.

"That is offensive."

"Yesterday was expensive."

She named a higher number.

I did math.

Then more math because pride was free and money was not.

"Fine."

"I'll file it."

She walked away.

The world had noticed quickly.

Of course it had.

People noticed useful things when invoices were involved.

I packed the returned deposit.

Stormglass shards.

Notebook.

Three pairs of socks, two still dry.

Field peg.

Walking leg.

No cuff.

Before leaving the gate, I tried one more broad plane against a warehouse wall.

Slow.

Came anyway.

Then a tiny moving plane around a swinging sign.

Instant.

I caught the sign before it struck stone.

The gate clerk said, "Thanks."

I said, "You're welcome."

No one knew that had been easier than holding a door.

I did.

That was enough for now.
