# Connected Read — Candidate Chapters 149–159

Status: **EDITORIAL CHECK / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

## Overall verdict

**STRONG SURVIVOR MOVEMENT. KEEP THE ADVENTURE SHAPE AND MOST CHAPTER BOUNDARIES.**

The 11-chapter movement successfully shifts the candidate book from field-exchange/professional-repair texture into overt fantasy adventure while still advancing the party-search experiment.

The movement reads as:

1. Greg intentionally tries to build a crew.
2. His obvious people are unavailable.
3. The world gives him a different roster.
4. The roster enters a visually distinct fantasy site.
5. Specialists earn their value through behavior.
6. The site produces monsters, living magic, old labor infrastructure, legal loot, and one stronger artifact-scale object.
7. The crew leaves before solving everything.
8. They are marked preferred for a possible second survey, then immediately return to separate lives.

This is much closer to the book's desired fantasy/party engine than the earlier professional-procedural stretch.

## Chapter rulings

### 149 `The Recruiter`
KEEP.

Greg's first deliberate team-building move fails through schedules and prior commitments. Tomel chooses better-paid Whitebank work. Lysa keeps her existing ward setup commitment. Sera already applied independently. This is the right first lesson because it is logistical before moral.

### 150 `The Guide`
KEEP.

Fen establishes the crew's actual operating structure and the site reveal is strong. The `failure question` briefing is useful, but future crews should not copy this exact ritual mechanically.

### 151 `The Antiquary`
KEEP.

Dessa provides bounded historical facts, Nemi's signal begins changing the site, and the garden wakes before the monster appears.

### 152 `The Blade`
KEEP.

Sera clearly owns the fight. Greg's three-foot launch correction is a clean support contribution without stealing the scene. The surviving bank drake preserves local ecology pressure.

### 153 `The Listener`
KEEP.

Camp and passive listening slow the arc appropriately. Overnight plant growth is one of the strongest fantasy images in the movement.

### 154 `The Warder`
KEEP.

Orra's exposure call stops curiosity from automatically winning. The newly revealed seam earns the next layer.

### 155 `The Curator`
KEEP.

Dessa is smart and wrong about the door. Service-gallery reveal adds old labor texture and legal treasure. The snail is memorable and should remain primarily funny.

### 156 `The Gardener`
KEEP.

Caretaker construct behaves like maintenance equipment rather than a boss. The half-working rail switch gives the arc mundane mechanical failure inside magical ruins.

### 157 `The Surveyor`
KEEP.

Three vertical sheets of standing water are the strongest lower-level visual. The intact `rain tooth` creates artifact-scale temptation without repeating Faultglass or centering Greg.

### 158 `The Caller`
KEEP.

Fen owns extraction. Active-cycle water and pollen make the site dangerous without collapse/boss logic. Leaving the rain tooth in place is earned.

### 159 `The Salvager`
KEEP SHAPE.

Pay, report, salvage, terms, and dispersal close the contract cleanly. Preferred-roster language leaves recurrence possible without declaring a party.

## Party-search result

The strongest discovery is that Greg does not need to personally recruit everyone for a Greg-shaped asymmetric team to emerge.

This crew works because:

- Fen gives boundaries/route/retreat;
- Sera is a narrow, very good fighter;
- Nemi produces usable uncertainty rather than answers;
- Orra owns protection and bodily stop conditions;
- Greg changes local conditions where that matters;
- Dessa adds site knowledge but is explicitly not field staff.

Greg's original `Lysa + Tomel + me` composition was rational and still not the team he got.

This should remain important.

Do not conclude that Fen/Sera/Nemi/Orra is the permanent party. Instead treat them as strong evidence for what kinds of asymmetry Greg works well inside.

## Greg character result

Several pressures advance without speeches:

- he recruits people intentionally for the first time;
- accepts no from Lysa/Tomel;
- works under another field lead without trying to seize command;
- allows Sera to own violence;
- asks Nemi for bounded tests rather than redefining her skill;
- accepts Orra's retreat calls;
- leaves the rain tooth in place;
- enjoys legal low-value loot;
- returns home after a successful adventure rather than chasing the next layer indefinitely.

Important: do not over-credit this as moral transformation. Greg still prices risk aggressively and still wants useful things. The point is behavior under current conditions, not redemption.

## Artifact pressure

The rain tooth is useful because it is **not Greg's current temptation**.

Nemi cares more directly about it. Dessa/city has clear claim. Greg observes the object without the story immediately turning it into his progression item.

Protect this. If the site recurs, the rain tooth can become:

- research evidence;
- city-controlled tool;
- Nemi-centered temptation;
- a source of understanding Greg later borrows indirectly.

Do not make Greg steal or acquire it merely because Faultglass history exists.

## Repetition watch

- `Good / Fine / Fair / Excellent` still appear often.
- `Maybe` is strong Nemi/Dessa language but can become a bit if every uncertainty is voiced the same way.

- Greg's small-plane correction appears repeatedly. Stop explaining why small is better. It is established now.
- several chapters end with restraint / leaving something unresolved. Correct for this arc, but not every adventure should resolve by withdrawal.
