## Route search

### Route A — Stay in Halden

Greg treats Halden as a real work base after Mara leaves.

Possible sequence:

1. He takes a local marsh-animal bounty because it pays and because the Guild needs another licensed blade more than a structural specialist.
2. Veteran hunter **Ista Vale** owns spoor, wind, marsh routes, animal behavior, and kill timing. Greg's load magic is marginal until the final engagement.
3. Greg discovers that being a good sword with strange support magic is enough to be employable without every job validating his new specialty.
4. Pera keeps iterating the field-peg washer.
5. Greg gets more Halden jobs.
6. Red Quay eventually returns and asks again.

What works:

- breaks structural-procedural repetition;
- keeps Halden alive beyond Mara;
- gives Greg another specialist to respect;
- lets Red Quay recur naturally later.

What weakens:

- Halden risks becoming the new static work hub immediately after the story just freed Greg from the promise;
- Carrow cast stays offstage too long;
- several recent chapters already made Halden extremely legible.

Verdict: **GOOD IMMEDIATE EPISODE, WEAK LONGER SPINE.**

Keep the hunt possibility. Do not commit to Halden as the next base yet.

### Route B — Chase the chemistry

Greg regrets declining Esven and finds a separate contract that intersects Red Quay's return route, or simply waits for them.

Possible sequence:

1. Greg does one local job.
2. Learns Red Quay's Esven work extended.
3. Takes work toward Esven or their likely return corridor.
4. Rejoins Veya/Korr/Mett/Asha/Renn quickly.
5. Repeated work accelerates team chemistry.

What works:

- party chemistry is fun;
- specialization deepens quickly;
- strong adventure engine.

What weakens badly:

- converts Greg's first meaningful refusal in Chapter 130 into a temporary scheduling obstacle;
- makes Red Quay feel protagonist-magnetic;
- risks declaring the party by repetition before the world has tested alternatives;
- narrows setting instead of opening it.

Verdict: **DO NOT TAKE NOW.**

Let Red Quay succeed elsewhere. Greg should hear about them later or cross them again after enough separate life has happened.

### Route C — One Halden hunt, then choose home

Greg does not flee Halden the moment Mara leaves. He takes one ordinary Guild job that has little to do with structures, then chooses Carrow because he actually wants to go home and no longer needs an external deadline to justify the route.

Possible sequence:

**Day 1–2:** Greg is restless after departure. Pera check on the washer. Guild offers a marsh predator bounty needing licensed bodies. Greg accepts.

**Day 2–3:** Hunt outside Halden with a veteran local hunter, one hide-worker/monster processor, and two ordinary Guild blades. Greg's structural specialty is mostly useless in reeds and mud. He follows someone else's terrain knowledge, fights competently, gets dirty, earns money/material, and maybe contributes one small force intervention at the kill rather than owning the hunt.

**Day 4:** Greg receives two unrelated pieces of mail:

- a short transit note from Mara written at Mill Junction or equivalent. She complains about another passenger, route food, staff paperwork she is already reading, and does not ask Greg to come;
- a Carrow note from Pell/Noll/Jorren with mundane life residue. No emergency summons. Something like Pell asking about the crawler tendon sample, Noll mentioning rent/food/room, Jorren reporting his own work advancement.

Greg realizes he wants to go home.

Not because Carrow has the next plot.

Because home exists.

**Day 5–8:** Greg takes a south/east caravan or packet route back toward Carrow. He may attach to a freight movement for discounted fare or light paid watch, but the travel should not become another large contract unless something earns it.

Possible road pleasure:

- strange roadside shrine;
- one ugly inn;
- card game with freight hands;
- minor night creature problem;
- Greg discovers the tourist bird in his bag and refuses to explain it;
- field-peg washer gets real road testing.

**Day 9:** Carrow return.

Noll, Jorren, Pell, neighborhood, room, food, and Hearthline have all continued.

Important changes to test:

- Jorren has advanced in road/route work and has his own opinions Greg did not teach him;
- Pell has incorporated other people's solutions in the workshop and does not need Greg as idea source;
- Noll's life contains routines Greg was absent for;
- Daro has not forgiven Faultglass simply because Greg did competent things in Halden.

**Day 10–12:** Greg does ordinary Carrow work or helps Pell test the crawler tendon. Then a controlled professional opportunity involving Hearthline appears. Daro does not invite Greg back emotionally. A client/site has a weird load problem, Greg's recent technique is relevant, and Greg is allowed under explicit supervision/restrictions because the value is real enough to price against distrust.

**Day 13–16:** Carrow posts or prepares an open field exchange / practical Guild event. This can later become the place where old specialists such as Lysa Kerr and Tomel Rusk re-enter naturally, but do not force the event immediately if another stronger toy appears.

**Mara:** first Northbank-arrival letter reaches Carrow later than Greg wants. It contains staff names, desk problems, temporary housing complaints, and at least one choice she made without him.

What works:

- uses Halden once more without making it permanent;
- gives Greg a non-structural fantasy job;
- opens travel and home texture;
- restores old people/settings without treating them as static;
- lets Hearthline trust become plot/material consequence rather than forgiveness scene;
- delays Red Quay long enough that future recurrence remains meaningful;
- naturally creates room for the people-development / field-exchange material later.

Risk:

- could feel like retreat to familiar setting if Carrow arrival is too comfortable;
- must show that Greg returning is not narrative reset. Everyone changed.

Verdict: **STRONGEST ROUTE.**

### Route D — Northbank pull

Greg waits only a few days, then takes work geographically toward Northbank before Mara requests a visit.

What works:

- relationship geography becomes plot geography.

What fails now:

- violates the fresh boundary they just negotiated;
- makes Mara's career immediately Greg-centered;
- wastes the value of Greg having no clock.

Verdict: **NOT NOW.**
