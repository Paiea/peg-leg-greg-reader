# Candidate Chapter 124: The Line

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Mett heard the first hollow before I did.

This annoyed me enough to become educational.

We were maybe fifty yards inside by the corridor and thirty by actual displacement.

The buried structure did not respect efficient travel.

The first passage bent left under the quarry face, descended six shallow steps, then doubled back around a section of wall that had been repaired by someone with different stone and no interest in matching anything.

Wet limestone near the entrance gave way to dark gray blocks below.

Old mortar.

Old water stains.

Several generations of patch work.

The air smelled like mineral damp and the faint sour residue of lamp smoke.

Our own lanterns threw too much movement across the walls.

Nobody talked unless they had something worth adding.

Korr lasted almost four minutes.

"How far?" he asked.

Mett did not look back.

"Farther than four minutes ago."

"Surveyors are bad company."

"We survive conversations by shortening them."

Renn said, "Save romance for daylight."

Korr went quiet.

For another two minutes.

The others adjusted to my movement without anyone making it a discussion.

Field peg on the flatter stones.

Right boot.

Staff only when broken stair edges or loose grit made a third point cheaper than pride.

Korr stopped walking directly behind me after one chunk of limestone rolled under the peg and hit his boot.

Veya stopped walking directly ahead after my staff tip came within an inch of her heel.

Asha stayed two places back because center meant she could reach forward or retreat without climbing over everyone.

Renn moved between front and rear whenever the corridor widened enough to allow it.

Mett led.

He raised one fist.

Everyone stopped.

No question first.

Mett crouched beside the right wall and opened a little leather pouch.

He took out two brass pins and a shallow cup no wider than a coin.

I knew neither tool.

He pressed the cup flat against the stone.

Tapped one brass pin against the wall.

Listened.

Moved the cup six inches.

Tapped again.

Then again.

"Void right," Mett said.

I looked at the wall.

Solid block.

No visible seam except the mortar joints.

I put one gloved hand against it.

Cool.

Slight damp.

Nothing else.

I formed a tiny force plane against the face and added pressure.

Stone pushed back.

Load traveled up and down the wall.

No useful echo.

I tried a second angle.

Same.

Mett watched my face.

"You don't hear it."

"No."

He smiled.

Not mean.

Worse.

Pleased.

"Good."

"Everyone uses that word badly."

Mett tapped again.

Then he held the little brass cup against the lower end of my staff.

"Hand here."

I moved my grip.

"Loose."

I loosened it.

He struck the wall once.

Flat vibration through the staff.

Second strike six inches over.

Almost the same.

Third farther right.

There.

A softness after impact.

Not an echo exactly.

More like the vibration had somewhere to go before returning.

"Room?" I asked.

"Maybe."

"Crack?"

"Maybe."

"You are very expensive for maybe."

"Twenty years costs money."

Renn looked back from the bend.

"Route?"

Mett marked the wall with blue chalk.

"Main passage stays left. We don't open this."

And that was it.

No demonstration explaining how the brass worked.

No invitation for me to practice until I became Mett by noon.

He put the tools away and walked.

I followed.

The feeling in my hand stayed with me.

Maybe I could learn to notice it eventually.

Maybe not.

Either outcome was acceptable, which felt suspiciously mature.

We found blood at the next landing.

Asha saw it first.

Small smear on the wall at shoulder height.

Then two drops on the floor.

She crouched without touching either.

"Hours old," Asha said. "Not much."

Renn asked, "Direction?"

Mett pointed down the next stair.

Veya said, "Wait."

Her voice was low enough that everyone froze again.

She looked at the ceiling.

I listened.

Nothing.

Then a scratch.

Pause.

Scratch-scratch.

Korr brought the short hammer into both hands.

"Attached to building?"

"Technically," I whispered.

Korr looked at me.

"Greg."

"Do not hit the ceiling."

"Cruel."

Something dropped.

Renn moved before I understood the shape.

Shield came off her back and up.

The creature hit it with a noise like a shovel thrown against a door.

Dog-sized.

Pale mineral plates along the back.

Too many jointed legs folded underneath.

Its head barely existed. The mouth opened sideways between two armored plates and scraped Renn's shield rim with flat stone-colored teeth.

Lime crawler.

Probably.

I had seen cousins of the type in other regions.

Naming could wait until it stopped trying to eat us.

Renn did not swing at it.

She turned the shield.

The crawler slid across the curved face and hit the wall sideways.

Veya moved.

One copper rod into a mortar joint at shoulder height.

The second into a crack low on the opposite block.

A pale white line appeared between them.

Thin enough to miss if you blinked.

Straight.

Perfectly straight.

"Left," Veya said.

I thought she meant herself.

Then she looked directly at me.

"Move it left."

No time to ask what the line did.

The crawler climbed sideways along the wall, front legs finding tiny seams I could barely see.

I could cut it with the sword.

Probably.

Broad shove might peel it off the wall.

Also probably.

Veya already had something established.

I did not need to know everything about it.

I only needed the instruction.

Left.

Small feathered plane against the wall ahead of the crawler.

Not a barrier.

A bad place to put its next foot.

The first leg hit the plane and slipped.

The creature corrected downward.

Second plane low, leaving left as the easier path.

It took it.

The crawler crossed Veya's white line.

She closed two fingers.

Flash.

The creature fell in two pieces.

Korr stared at the remains.

"That's disgusting."

Veya pulled out her copper rods.

"Thank you."

I looked at the cut.

Clean through the armor.

Cleaner than my sword would have managed.

"How thick?" I asked.

Veya wiped one rod on a rag.

"Today?"

"Today."

"Four inches of decent limestone if my points don't move. Less through active warding. Metal depends on what kind."

"Curves?"

"No."

"Moving targets?"

She pointed at the crawler.

"Apparently I hired you."

"Nobody discussed rate."

"You're already here."

Renn said, "Do this later."

Veya and I both looked at her.

"Do what?" Veya asked.

"Whatever that was."

Korr laughed.

The laugh became a warning by accident.

A second crawler came out of a drainage slot beneath the broken stair.

It lunged toward Mett because apparently stone expertise offended local wildlife.

Asha grabbed the back of Mett's coat and hauled him sideways.

Renn stepped between.

Crawler hit shield.

Korr moved past me.

Fast.

Much faster than his size suggested.

One step.

Hammer low.

He struck the floor beside the crawler.

Not the creature.

The impact traveled through the stone with a deep compression I felt in my teeth.

The crawler lifted six inches off the floor.

Renn rolled it with the shield.

Korr hit again.

This one landed on plate.

Crack.

The crawler stopped moving.

Korr did too.

He stayed bent slightly forward with both hands on the hammer.

One breath.

Two.

Three.

His right knee shook once.

Four.

Five.

He straightened.

I looked at him.

"You count?"

"Everyone counts."

Mett brushed dust from his coat.

"He lies after six."

"Surveyor."

"Hammer."

They had done this conversation before.

Renn looked at me.

"Structure?"

I checked the floor where Korr had struck.

Fresh chip.

No spreading crack.

Old load path remained where I expected.

"Fine. Do not use more than that in here until I say otherwise."

Korr's eyebrows rose.

"More than that?"

"You have more."

"Maybe."

Mett said, "He has more."

Korr looked offended by betrayal.

"How much more?" I asked.

Renn answered for him.

"Enough that we don't test it under a hill."

Excellent policy.

We moved again.

Ten minutes later the corridor narrowed enough that Renn turned sideways to clear one shoulder.

Asha found another blood mark.

Fresh enough to stay dark.

Then we heard breathing.

Fast.

Wet.

Human.

"Fenn?" Renn called.

The breathing stopped.

"Red Quay rescue. Halden Guild. Fenn Orris?"

A voice came from around the bend.

"Here."

One word.

Barely.

We found him behind the remains of an old door frame.

The frame had fallen diagonally across the passage. One end rested on the floor. The other had punched into the wall hard enough to open a crack above it.

Fenn lay in the pocket behind it.

Left arm trapped under a timber-dark stone crosspiece.

Face gray with pain.

Lantern beside him almost dry.

He started crying when Asha said his name.

No dramatic breakdown.

His face just stopped holding whatever it had been holding.

Asha went in first.

Renn stayed behind her.

Mett checked the floor around the frame.

I checked what the frame was carrying.

Veya remained back because there was nothing a straight cut could safely improve yet.

Korr looked at the trapped crosspiece and kept the hammer down.

That impressed me more than the hammer had.

Asha touched Fenn's hand.

"Can you feel this?"

"Yes."

"Fingers?"

"Yes."

"Move them."

He did.

Poorly.

Asha nodded once.

"Arm is broken. Blood still moving. We're getting you out."

Fenn swore at her.

"That is fine too," Asha said.

Renn looked at me.

"Frame?"

I set the staff against the crosspiece.

Plane under the lower edge.

Very small pressure.

The wall answered.

The frame was debris now, but the collapse had made it structural debris.

Lift it too much and the cracked wall above would settle into the space it left.

"We take weight before we take Fenn."

Korr crouched.

"Hands?"

"Hands. No impact."

"Boring."

"Survive it."

He got both hands under the crosspiece.

I placed two feathered planes.

One beneath the trapped end.

One against the cracked wall, not to hold it still, only to keep any shift from snapping inward.

"Lift until I say."

Korr lifted.

Slow.

The stone moved a fraction.

My plane took load.

The wall pressed back.

"Stop."

Korr stopped instantly.

Asha slid one hand under Fenn's elbow.

"Again."

Another fraction.

Fenn screamed.

Asha did not tell him not to.

"Enough," she said.

Renn dropped to one knee and helped pull.

Fenn's arm cleared.

Then the rest of him.

Korr lowered on my count.

The crosspiece settled exactly where it had been, minus one quarry worker.

Fenn vomited on Korr's boot.

Korr looked down.

"Rescue complete."

Asha said, "Shut up."

Fenn made a noise that might have been a laugh and might have been another attempt to vomit.

We gave him water in tiny amounts.

Asha wrapped the arm against his chest.

Renn waited until his breathing slowed before asking about Haro and Lis.

Fenn closed his eyes.

"Down."

"How far?" Renn asked.

"Don't know. We heard the bell. Haro thought it was the shift bell."

"The quarry shift bell?"

"Sounded like it. Then voices. He went. Lis went after him. Floor dropped here before I could follow."

Fenn opened his eyes again.

"White things came after."

Korr looked at the two dead crawlers.

"Those?"

"Bigger one. Maybe. I don't know."

Renn looked at Asha.

"Can he move?"

"With help. Slowly. He needs surface light and a real splint."

Fenn grabbed Renn's sleeve.

"Haro's hurt."

Renn did not pull away.

"Then I'm not using the only healer we have to carry two injured men at once if I can avoid it. We take you out. Then we come back."

Fenn's grip tightened.

For a second I thought he would argue.

Then he let go.

"Bring them."

"That's the job," Renn said.

We turned around.

The return took almost twice as long.

Asha and Renn managed Fenn.

Mett called the safe foot placements where the old stair had broken.

Korr carried more of Fenn's weight than his expression suggested was comfortable.

Veya moved ahead twice to check corners.

I stayed where I could see the walls around the extra load.

Nobody did everything.

Nobody needed to.

Near the first crawler, Veya fell into step beside me.

"You could've killed it yourself," she said.

"Probably."

"Why didn't you?"

I looked at the copper rods at her belt.

"You already had the line."

Veya considered that.

Then nodded once.

No compliment.

No lesson.

We kept walking.

At the entrance, Fenn's wife crossed the lantern line before Tessa could stop her.

Renn did not make an issue of it.

Asha did.

"Do not grab the arm," she said sharply.

The woman froze.

Then took Fenn's face in both hands instead.

He cried again.

So did she.

The quarry became loud around them.

Workers asking questions.

Tessa calling for the infirmary cart.

Arven swearing when he saw the broken arm.

Someone bringing blankets nobody had needed five minutes earlier.

Renn stepped away from the noise and looked back at the hole.

I knew the look.

We were going down again.

She looked at me.

"Structure?"

"Still holding."

"You?"

The socket had started to ache from the descent and climb.

Not the red skin spot.

General pressure.

Manageable.

"Still holding."

Renn's mouth shifted.

"Bad answer."

"Accurate enough."

She pointed toward the water barrel.

"Drink. Check the leg. Ten minutes."

I almost said I did not need ten.

Then remembered Pera, warehouse stone, and skin that did not care about professional pride.

"Fine."

Renn turned toward Mett.

"You too. I want the lower route before we move."

Mett was already spreading the sketch map across Tessa's table.

Apparently obedience had specialty boundaries.

I sat on an overturned crate and loosened the field socket.

Behind me, Fenn's wife kept saying his name like repetition could make him more returned.

Ahead, somewhere under the hill, Haro and Lis were still waiting.

The first descent had answered one question.

The structure could hurt us.

The people beside me were better at several things than I was.

That second answer bothered me much less than it would have once...
