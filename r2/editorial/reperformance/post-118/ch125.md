# Candidate Chapter 125: Three Seconds

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

The second descent felt worse because we knew somebody was alive down there.

Probably.

Fenn had heard Haro after the floor broke.

Hours ago.

That was enough for Asha to walk faster than she had on the first descent and enough for Renn to tell her twice to slow down.

"If you fall," Renn said, "I lose the healer."

"If he loses the foot, you lose the patient."

"If you fall, he loses the healer and the foot."

Asha slowed by maybe five percent.

Compromise.

My field socket had cooled during the break.

The red outer spot remained red and nothing more. I changed back to the thicker liner because the descent would be less lateral than the warehouse floor and because skin had become a topic I preferred deciding before someone else decided it for me.

Renn saw me retighten the strap.

"Changed?"

"No."

"Pain?"

"Manageable."

She nodded.

No speech about bravery.

We went down.

Fenn's rescue site looked smaller without a trapped man behind the frame.

The vomit on Korr's boot had dried badly.

Veya noticed.

"You kept wearing that."

Korr looked down.

"Work boot."

"Human vomit."

"Also work."

Asha said, "I hate every one of you."

Mett raised one hand.

Silence.

We stopped before the next stair.

He listened with the little brass cup against the floor.

Then moved it.

Tapped again.

"Lower void begins sooner than the plan."

Renn asked, "Crossable?"

"Currently."

Korr looked at me.

"He likes that word."

"It is an honest word."

Mett stood.

"Try honesty sometime."

We crossed one at a time.

No rope yet.

The old floor sounded solid under my peg until it did not.

Third bell.

This time the vibration reached me before anyone heard the tone.

The floor shifted under my right boot.

Half an inch.

Maybe less.

Enough.

"Stop."

Everyone stopped.

Korr froze mid-step with one foot lifted and somehow remained there.

The bell arrived through air.

Low bronze under stone.

One note.

Long.

The vibration continued after the sound began to fade.

Not shaking.

Load movement.

I set the staff against the floor.

Small plane.

Light contact.

Pressure traveled west.

Then down.

Something old had changed where it was carrying itself.

Renn spoke from behind me.

"Back?"

"Wait."

I changed the plane angle.

No broad hold.

No attempt to become the whole floor.

Just touch and answer.

The west wall took more than before.

Center took less.

Mett crouched beside me and tapped twice.

His face tightened.

"Bigger void under the next room."

"Did the bell open it?" Renn asked.

"No idea," Mett said.

"Can we cross?"

Mett listened again.

"One at a time. Keep right."

I added, "No Korr impacts."

Korr whispered, "Oppression."

"Survive it."

He nodded.

We moved.

The next room had once been circular.

That was the generous description.

Stone ribs rose from the floor toward a central ring overhead. Three were intact. Two had cracked along old repairs. One had fallen across the eastern half of the chamber and come to rest against the wall.

The room had not collapsed.

It had negotiated.

Haro was under the fallen rib.

Alive.

Lis was beside him.

Not.

Asha knew before she crossed the threshold.

Renn did too.

I did not look at Lis longer than I needed to know there was no rescue action there.

Haro's eyes opened when Renn called his name.

Blood had dried from his hairline down one side of his face. His right leg disappeared beneath the fallen stone rib below the knee.

"Don't ring it," he whispered.

Renn kept her voice level.

"We're not."

"It rings itself."

"We know."

Asha moved toward him.

Mett stopped her with one hand across the corridor.

"Center tile."

Asha looked down.

Hairline crack beneath dust.

She moved left.

Mett led her around it.

That small correction probably mattered more than anything I was thinking.

Asha reached Haro and started with his face, pupils, pulse, hands.

Not the trapped leg.

I walked the room edge.

Peg.

Boot.

Staff.

No step on the central tiles.

The fallen rib looked like debris.

It was not debris anymore.

It leaned into the east wall and had become an ugly brace for the central ring.

Lift the rib directly and we might free Haro by dropping the rest of the room on him.

Korr came beside me.

"I can lift that."

"I know."

"Then?"

"No."

He followed my eyes upward.

"Ah."

I liked that he did not need the next five sentences.

Renn asked, "Plan?"

"Need to move the load before the rib."

"Tell us."

I looked around again.

North rib carrying too much.

West rib carrying almost nothing because its base had walked inward years ago.

East broken rib carrying sideways into the wall.

The room had survived by making several bad decisions that happened to add up to standing.

Familiar enough.

I did not need to make it metaphorical while a man was losing circulation.

Asha called from Haro.

"Sensation is fading in the foot."

Time entered the room.

I pointed at the western doorway jamb.

"Renn. Shield there. Hard contact. Keep the opening square. Do not try to lift anything."

She moved without asking why.

"Mett, foundation west. How far does the solid shelf go?"

He was already tapping.

Three strikes.

Move.

Two more.

"At least four feet from the wall. Maybe six."

"Four is enough."

Veya stood near the entrance with both copper rods in one hand.

I pointed at a cracked section hanging from the central ring.

Old metal strap still joined it to the north rib.

"Can you cut that strap without taking stone?"

Veya looked at the angle.

"If I have two points."

"North rib and wall."

She frowned.

"North rib is moving."

"Slowly."

"Greg."

"Can you establish the line?"

She studied it again.

"Yes. But if the rib jumps before I finish, line goes somewhere else."

"I can keep your upper point from jumping."

Veya looked at me.

"Keep the point. Not the whole rib."

"Yes."

"Say that next time."

I filed the irritation for later because Haro's foot remained attached to a clock.

"Korr."

He smiled immediately.

"Finally."

I pointed at the base of the dead west rib.

"One hit. I need the base two inches toward the wall."

Korr's smile thinned.

"Two inches."

"Not broken. Not three."

"You want precision."

"I want Haro."

That ended the joke.

Korr crouched and touched the stone with the hammer head.

"Tell me when."

I formed the first feathered plane beneath the north load path.

Wider center.

Soft edges.

Not taking all the weight.

Giving some of it somewhere else to go.

Second plane against the fallen east rib so it could unload without kicking sideways into Haro.

Third beneath the central ring.

Tiny.

Almost insultingly tiny compared with the amount of stone above us.

That was fine.

I did not need to hold the room.

I needed three moments to happen in an order the room could survive.

"Renn, pressure."

Her shield seated against the jamb.

The doorway shifted a fraction and stopped.

"Veya."

She set the rods.

A pale line appeared through the old metal strap.

"Line set," Veya said.

"Mett?"

His palm stayed on the floor.

"West shelf sound. Center still hollow."

"Asha?"

"Do it."

Haro had both hands over his face now.

Dust protection.

Or fear.

No reason it could not be both.

"Veya, cut."

White flash.

The metal strap separated.

The hanging stone section dropped two inches.

North rib groaned.

Load moved into my first plane.

More than expected.

I let the plane deform.

Did not harden it.

Pressure bled west.

"Korr. Now."

He struck.

Deep sound.

Not loud.

The dead west rib moved.

One inch.

My plane caught the impulse and spread it into the foundation shelf Mett had found.

Second inch.

The base seated.

The central ring dropped.

Half an inch.

Every part of my body wanted to stop it.

I did not.

The drop needed somewhere to go.

I turned it west.

Renn's shield took lateral load.

She swore.

Doorway stayed square.

The broken east rib lifted a finger width off Haro's leg.

"Korr!"

He was already moving.

Hammer down.

Both hands under the stone.

Not lifting the room.

Only holding the little space we had created.

Asha grabbed Haro under the arms.

Renn left the shield braced with shoulder and one arm, reached with the other.

"Pull."

Haro screamed.

Asha pulled.

One second.

His knee cleared.

Two.

Boot caught on broken stone.

Asha swore.

Renn kicked the fragment away.

Three.

Haro came free.

"Out!"

Korr lowered.

I let the east plane release gradually.

The rib settled.

The ring shifted again.

Then stopped.

Nobody moved for one breath.

Two.

I kept the three planes connected.

Success had a bad habit of convincing people the danger had ended exactly when it had only changed shape.

Mett tapped the floor.

Once.

Twice.

"Holding."

Only then did I release north.

Wait.

Center.

Wait.

East.

Stone remained where stone had agreed to remain.

I sat down hard against the wall.

Not collapse.

Deliberate sitting.

Mostly.

Korr flexed both hands.

"Two inches."

I looked at the west rib.

"Maybe two and a quarter."

He looked offended.

"Stone moved after impact."

"Convenient defense."

Asha was already working on Haro's leg.

"Stop flirting with measurements," she said. "I need the carry sheet."

Veya brought it.

Renn checked the room while Asha splinted Haro.

Then Renn looked at Lis.

The energy changed.

Rescue had been loud because living bodies demanded action.

Death did not become less important because it waited quietly.

Lis lay on her side near the north wall.

Dust on her hair.

One hand beneath her chest.

Twenty-two, Tessa's sheet had said.

I had known her name for less than an hour and already wanted the number to be wrong.

Asha finished Haro's splint.

"We can carry both," she said.

Renn looked at me.

"Structure?"

I checked the exit path.

"Yes. One at a time through the center edge. Stay west."

We wrapped Lis first.

Haro watched.

Nobody told him not to.

On the way out, Haro drifted in and out from whatever Asha had given him.

Lis remained quiet between Korr and Renn.

Mett called each bad floor section before the load reached it.

I watched the structure.

Veya walked beside me for eleven steps before speaking.

"You used my cut to move the ring."

"Yes."

"You told me to cut a strap."

"Yes."

"You did not tell me the whole room was going to move when I did it."

I started with the fast answer.

There wasn't time.

The answer was true.

It was also trying to win.

I let it die before speaking.

"No. I didn't."

Veya waited.

"I should have told you what the cut was doing."

"Yes."

"Would you have cut it?"

"Probably."

Annoying.

"Then why does it matter?"

She stopped.

I stopped because apparently ancient corridors had become social offices.

Veya held one copper rod between two fingers.

"Because it's my line."

Simple.

Not my plan.

Not my result.

Her line.

Renn called from ahead.

"Moving."

Veya started walking again.

I followed.

Outside, Tessa saw Haro first.

Relief crossed her face.

Then she saw the wrapped body behind him.

The relief did not disappear.

It changed shape.

Fenn's wife stood near the infirmary cart with her good hand wrapped around Fenn's uninjured one.

Haro's brother pushed through two quarry workers when he recognized him.

Nobody stopped him until Asha said, "Not the leg."

He grabbed Haro's shoulder instead.

Lis had no one waiting at the lantern line.

Not yet.

Tessa took the name sheet from Renn and crossed nothing out.

She made three marks beside the names instead.

Alive.

Alive.

Recovered.

The word bothered me.

Probably because it was the correct administrative word and the wrong human one.

The quarry filled with the work that came after danger.

Cart wheels.

Statements.

Asha giving orders nobody argued with.

Arven standing very still beside Lis until Tessa asked him who needed to send the family notice.

Korr finally noticed Fenn's dried vomit still on his boot and looked offended all over again.

I reached the water barrel.

Renn caught me before I drank.

"That room."

I looked at her.

"What about it?"

"Good work."

No qualifier.

No lesson.

Just the thing.

It landed harder than it should have.

I drank half the cup.

My hands had started shaking slightly now that they were allowed.

The rescue had worked.

Not almost.

Worked.

Haro was alive because six people had done six different things at the right time.

And because for three seconds I had known exactly where the force needed to go.

I let myself enjoy that before finding a reason not to...
