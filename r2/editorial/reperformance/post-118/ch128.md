# Candidate Chapter 128: Fixed Points

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

The crawler-tendon washer survived one day before becoming boring.

This was ideal.

I wore the field peg back to the quarry for Red Quay's second survey block and forgot about the washer before we reached the first buried stair.

My knee noticed less sharp impact.

My head stopped noticing the knee.

Successful equipment should become disrespectfully uninteresting.

Veya noticed instead.

She pointed at the peg while we waited for Tessa to open the site log.

"New?"

"Washer."

"Monster?"

Korr turned immediately.

"Monster leg."

"No."

Asha said, "I supplied the monster."

"You supplied one tendon strip."

Mett looked over the top of the map.

"Monster leg."

I looked at Renn.

She signed the log.

"We leaving or naming Greg's equipment?"

Korr considered.

"Both possible."

"Leaving."

Authority remained valuable.

Today's survey went below the Bell Room.

Tessa wanted the lower load network mapped far enough that quarry engineers could decide whether reopening the west face would make anyone famous for the wrong reason.

No missing workers.

No active rescue.

Lower danger, theoretically.

Theoretically remained a word used before evidence.

Mett led us through the service passage he had found yesterday.

The route below the Bell Room was less grand than the upper ruin.

Maintenance space.

Narrow galleries.

Counterweight shafts.

Drain channels.

Iron access rings set into walls at shoulder height.

Someone had built the place expecting repairs.

That bothered me more than architecture designed for eternity.

People who planned maintenance understood failure.

The first hour consisted mostly of stopping because Mett wanted to listen to something.

Tap.

Wait.

Mark.

Move.

Veya found three active brass lines embedded in the floor and one dead line crossing a wall.

Asha marked a side passage bad after her lamp flame turned yellow at the edge.

Korr carried a canvas sack for stone samples and complained about being reduced to freight while adding extra samples nobody had requested.

Renn let him.

I watched where load moved between the old channels and tried not to turn every quiet stretch into a personal experiment.

This became harder after the Bell Room.

The cuff architecture kept returning to mind whenever I saw a repeated repair shape.

I did not need to buy anything to think about it.

Annoying loophole.

Veya stopped at a counterweight door.

The slab blocked most of the corridor.

Old stone, six feet high, riding in two bronze tracks. It had stopped with maybe eighteen inches open at one side.

Mett could fit.

Asha probably could.

Veya maybe.

Korr looked at the gap, turned sideways, and attempted to become a different person.

The slab declined.

"Rude," he said.

Mett crouched at the track.

"Counterweight shaft left."

He tapped the wall.

Then higher.

"Still connected."

"Alive?" Renn asked.

"Ask it."

Korr lifted his hammer.

Renn said, "No."

"I was going to gesture."

"Gesture without the hammer."

He lowered it.

Veya examined the lower track.

A bronze catch had twisted sideways and jammed the slab.

"I can cut this," she said.

I looked at the counterweight channel.

"Then the slab moves."

"Usually the point of a door."

"Direction?"

Veya looked at the slab.

"Whichever the counterweight decides."

Korr said, "Excellent system."

Mett listened at the shaft again.

"Weight is above. Chain still has tension."

"How much?" I asked.

Mett gave me a flat look.

"Listen harder."

Right.

Renn asked, "Other route?"

Mett unfolded the lower sketch.

"Back through the cracked circular room, then maybe south gallery. Longer and worse."

Nobody volunteered for longer and worse.

I looked at Veya's cut.

Straight bronze tongue.

Easy target for her, assuming the line stayed where she put it.

Her lower rod could seat in the floor track.

Upper rod would need the slab itself.

The slab was the moving object.

"How much can your fixed point move before the line fails?" I asked.

Veya looked at me.

"It can't."

"Nothing can't."

"Mine can. That's why it's called fixed."

"Hair width?"

"Maybe."

"Two?"

"Then I might cut beside the catch."

Korr stepped backward.

"I enjoy this less now."

I put a small feathered plane against the slab where Veya wanted the upper rod.

Not pushing.

Not holding the door shut.

Just making one hand-sized patch resist tiny lateral movement.

"Try there."

Veya pressed two fingers to the spot.

"Too broad."

I narrowed the plane.

"Again."

Smaller.

Her fingers stayed on the stone.

"Better."

I changed the edge softness.

Veya nodded.

"That."

Renn watched us.

"Can you cut?"

Veya said, "If Greg keeps this exact patch quiet."

Renn looked at me.

"Can you?"

"Probably."

Veya said, "He means yes with an escape route."

"Correct."

We rigged before cutting.

That part took longer than the magic.

Rope through an iron wall ring Mett cleared as sound.

Korr on the primary line.

Renn on the second.

Asha behind the corner because being the healer next to the falling door would have been bad planning.

Mett at the counterweight wall with one hand touching stone.

Veya placed her lower rod.

Then the upper into the patch I held stable.

A pale line appeared across the twisted bronze catch.

"Set," Veya said.

Mett raised one finger.

Everyone waited.

Something moved far above inside the shaft.

Dust fell from the wall.

Korr tightened the rope.

The movement stopped.

Mett lowered his finger.

"Now."

Veya cut.

White flash.

The catch fell in two pieces.

The slab dropped three inches.

Rope snapped tight.

Korr grunted.

Renn leaned into the second line.

My fixed patch tried to move with the slab.

I let the rest of the stone go and held only Veya's rod point for one extra heartbeat.

Her line was already gone.

No reason to keep it.

Habit.

I released.

The door stopped.

Then started upward.

Slowly.

The counterweight still worked.

Korr laughed.

"Building likes me."

Mett said, "Building doesn't know you."

"Pure instinct."

The slab rose far enough for Korr.

Barely.

He turned sideways and sucked in his stomach anyway.

Veya walked through normally.

This seemed to offend him.

On the other side, she pulled the upper rod from the mortar seam and looked at me.

"That was useful."

I waited.

"The plane?"

"The point."

Small distinction.

Maybe important.

We moved.

The lower gallery ended at a long chamber with a mineral seam running through both walls.

Lime crawlers covered it.

At first I counted six.

Then one shifted and revealed three behind it.

Then the ceiling moved.

I stopped counting.

Korr lifted the hammer.

Renn stepped backward.

"Out."

He looked at her.

"Could clear."

"Why?"

Korr opened his mouth.

Nothing came out.

We backed into the corridor.

Mett checked the map.

"Lower load channel is at the far end of that room."

There.

A reason.

Renn looked at me.

"Structure if Korr hits?"

I checked the floor and wall seam from the doorway.

"One impact point left of center. Half power. No ceiling."

Korr sighed.

"Everyone hates art."

Veya had already started placing rods.

Two lines across the narrowest section of the chamber.

One low.

One diagonal.

"These stay," she said. "Do not move my points."

This time I understood the sentence differently.

"Tell me what needs to stay fixed."

Veya pointed.

"Those four contacts. Everything else can be ugly."

Better.

Renn set her shield at the retreat corridor.

Asha stayed behind her.

Mett stayed completely out of combat position because his job was knowing whether our fight changed the room beneath us.

Korr chose his impact point.

I drew the sword.

Then looked at the crawlers.

My sword was not the interesting part.

The first crawler started down the left wall.

I put a tiny plane ahead of its right forelegs.

Bad grip.

Open stone to the left.

It chose the open path.

Crossed Veya's line.

Flash.

One dead.

Second came high.

I changed two contact points under it.

The creature dropped exactly where gravity had wanted it to go anyway.

Korr hit the floor.

Deep pulse.

Three crawlers lifted from the mineral seam.

Renn caught one with the shield and shoved it through the lower line.

Veya cut.

Korr went still after the impact.

Breathing.

One.

Two.

Three.

We knew this now.

Renn held the corridor while he recovered.

A crawler came across the ceiling where Veya's lines did nothing.

Sword became interesting again.

I stepped under the first drop and cut the soft joint below its plate.

Killed it.

The next came faster.

I missed.

Current Greg remained capable of missing.

Renn's shield caught it.

Asha leaned around her and drove a narrow medical knife through the underside.

The crawler twitched once.

Asha stared at the knife.

"Stop making me do this."

"Tell it," I said.

"Dead."

"Strong communication."

We cleared the chamber in less than a minute.

Nobody looked heroic during most of it.

Korr spent several seconds doing nothing but breathing.

Veya barely moved from the place she had chosen before the first crawler attacked.

Mett never drew a weapon.

Asha killed one creature because it entered her space and seemed personally offended by the paperwork this would create.

I killed one with the sword.

Several more died because I made one piece of wall or floor less convenient than another.

At the far end, Mett found the load channel.

He listened for almost a minute.

Then marked the map.

"Enough for today."

Renn did not ask whether we could push farther.

We turned around.

On the climb out, Veya handed me one of her spare copper rods.

"Hold this."

I took it before thinking.

"Why?"

"I have twelve."

"That sounds correct."

"You make thirteen."

"Then don't give it to me."

"Spare point."

"I'm not carrying your equipment."

Veya walked ahead.

The rod remained in my hand.

Korr saw it.

"Party gift."

"Inventory error."

"Romantic."

"I can put monster tendon in your boots."

He considered this.

"Would it help?"

Damn him.

At the surface, the tendon washer was still boring.

Veya's copper rod was still in my hand.

The second fact bothered me more...
