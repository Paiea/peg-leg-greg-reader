# Candidate Chapter 130: Again

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Red Quay finished the quarry job by making it somebody else's problem.

This was apparently success.

Tessa had three new folders by midday.

Historical review.

Quarry engineering.

Guild hazard control.

A fourth sat empty because nobody had yet decided who owned the argument about old artifacts found under private extraction rights.

"That one will grow," Tessa said.

"Water it carefully."

"Leave."

I stayed long enough to sign the last survey sheet.

The lower load channel ended where Mett had marked it. Three unsafe rooms closed. One counterweight door functional enough to become an engineer's future complaint. Lime crawler chamber cleared only as far as survey need. Bell Room untouched except chalk and measurements.

No treasure vault.

No hidden king.

No ancient voice explaining why I mattered.

Tessa looked delighted.

"You understand how rare a useful report is?"

"I have met reports."

"Then you know."

The quarry would remain partly closed while Halden decided whether to reroute extraction around the buried structure or turn the west face into a controlled dig.

That decision belonged to people who enjoyed meetings enough to deserve it.

Red Quay packed around us.

Asha counted bandages and complained that Korr had used two wraps on his hammer handle and classified them as medical supplies.

"Grip injury prevention," Korr said.

"The hammer is not a patient."

"It works harder than Mett."

Mett sat on a stone block cleaning dust from his brass listening cup with waxed thread.

"Hammer has better conversation."

"Thank you."

"Not what I said."

Veya came toward me with one hand out.

I knew what she wanted.

The copper rod had ridden in the side loop of my field bag since yesterday.

I pulled it free.

"Inventory returned."

Veya took it.

Counted the bundle at her belt.

Then counted again.

"Thirteen."

"Congratulations."

She handed one back.

I stared at it.

"No."

"Spare point."

"It is your rod."

"I have twelve."

"Yesterday twelve was not enough."

"Yesterday I didn't have a person who could hold one patch of wall still."

That sentence was better than it had any right to be.

I refused to reward it.

"This sounds like inventory fraud."

"Correct."

"Temporary custody."

"Call it whatever helps."

I put the rod back in my bag.

No ceremony.

Still annoying.

Renn found me while I was constructing legal fiction.

"Quarry closes here for us," she said.

"I gathered."

"We have an old tollhouse job outside Esven in nine days."

There it was.

The obvious next thing.

I waited.

Renn continued.

"Road vault collapsed into its own cellar. Local Guild thinks warded storage underneath. Two days out. One to three on site. Maybe two back depending where we finish."

"Structural?"

"Almost certainly."

"Same team?"

Question came out before I decided whether I wanted to ask it.

Renn noticed.

Of course.

"Mett, Veya, Korr, Asha, me. Assuming nobody gets bought away before then."

Veya, still close enough to hear, said, "I have a mill job the day before."

Renn nodded.

"Then assuming you return with all thirteen rods."

"Twelve."

She pointed at my bag.

"Thirteen."

Veya considered the accounting and gave up.

Renn looked back at me.

"Same attached-specialist share. Expenses. Hazard adjustment if the lower vault is active."

Nine days.

Mara left in twelve.

My head solved it immediately.

Two days to Esven.

Minimum one on site.

Two back.

Five.

Leave in nine, return around fourteen.

No.

If the job stayed one day and roads behaved, maybe return on the same day Mara departed.

Bad.

Could leave directly from Esven toward Northbank.

Worse.

Could ask Mara to shift one day.

Absolutely not.

Could take a faster river packet from...

I stopped.

The answer had arrived before the calculations.

I had simply refused to acknowledge it until the calculations failed politely.

"No," I said.

Renn blinked.

Not offended.

Actually surprised.

So was I.

"Schedule?" she asked.

"Mara leaves for Northbank in twelve days."

Renn nodded once.

"Next job then."

No persuasion.

No reminder that this was a good contract.

No promise the next job would exist.

That last part made the refusal feel real.

Korr had been listening from beside the supply wagon.

"He said no?"

Asha did not look up from her medical case.

"Practice hearing it."

Mett added, "Useful skill."

"Everyone hostile today."

Veya looked at me.

"You could probably fit it."

There was the loophole my brain had been building.

"Probably."

"You want the job."

"Yes."

"And you're saying no."

"Also yes."

Veya frowned slightly.

Not judgment.

Calculation maybe.

"Why?"

I almost said because Mara leaves.

Already stated.

Veya wanted the next layer.

"Because if I turn her last twelve days into route buffer, then I already left."

Veya stared at me for a second.

"That's disgusting."

"What?"

"Reasonable relationship thought."

"Take it back."

She walked away.

Terrible woman.

Renn adjusted the shield strap over one shoulder.

"We should be back through Halden around sixteen days from now. If the Esven job doesn't grow teeth."

"And if it does?"

"Then later."

No guarantee.

Again.

That was what I liked and disliked about actual work.

People left.

Schedules changed.

The same useful combination did not exist merely because I wanted to study it.

"I'll still have your rod," I said.

Veya called without turning, "Collateral."

"For what?"

"Future labor."

"Illegal contract."

"Prove it."

Renn smiled.

I did not.

Probably.

Tessa called me back to the table before I could leave.

"Payment."

Better word.

The quarry survey added a smaller second specialist payment to the rescue share. Reimbursement for field food. No equipment claim because nothing had broken. Crawler tendon already deducted through salvage.

I counted the coins.

Tessa watched.

"Again?"

"Money becomes mine after I count it."

"Still not law."

"Consistent law."

Harrow's next payment sat ready in the envelope inside my bag.

For once I did not need this money immediately.

That changed the shape of it.

Not emergency money.

Choice money.

Dangerous category.

I stopped at the Guild office on the way back into Halden and sent Harrow's payment before I could decide it had another purpose.

Receipt folded into the notebook.

One obligation smaller.

Then Pera.

She made me walk with the crawler washer on the shop floor, threshold, alley stone, and a loose board she kept specifically to make customers dislike her.

"Compressed unevenly," she said.

"Bad?"

"Expected."

"Different word."

"Second version."

She cut two thinner tendon layers and crossed their grain before sealing them between waxed leather.

"Why crossed?"

"One resists where the other gives."

"Useful."

Pera looked at me.

I looked away.

Everyone had heard that word enough.

She charged less because she wanted me to bring the washer back after three days of walking.

Research discount.

Dangerous precedent.

At the inn, I put the new washer into the field peg and the old one in a little paper packet rather than throwing it away.

Data.

Maybe trash later.

The copper rod went beside it.

I opened the notebook.

Mara leaves: twelve days.

Harrow: paid.

Pera check: three days.

Red Quay: Esven, unavailable.

Veya rod: temporary custody.

Northbank route times waited on the facing page.

I could have started planning visits.

The old answer was already there.

Dates.

Costs.

Weather buffers.

Minimum useful stay.

Maximum work loss.

All legitimate questions.

None needed answering before dinner.

I closed the notebook.

Not because I had become healthy.

Dinner was getting cold.

That was enough reason for one evening...
