# Candidate Chapter 150: The Guide

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Fen Orl carried no sword.

This was the first thing I noticed and probably the least important.

He stood outside Carrow's west gate beside a narrow two-wheel survey cart with one hand on a mule's cheek and the other holding our contract roster.

Late thirties maybe.

Brown coat cut short for walking.

Gray scarf.

One long field knife at the belt, which technically ruined my first observation but not enough to matter.

No Silver clasp displayed at chest.

His was pinned to the strap of his map case where rain would probably destroy it eventually.

Practical disrespect for rank.

I liked him provisionally.

Sera was already there.

Of course.

She sat on the low gate wall eating something wrapped in paper.

Curved sword at her hip today, real one, dark sheath.

When she saw me, she looked at the field peg.

"You brought the ugly leg."

"You lost after beating me."

"Still beat you."

"Unfortunately."

She took another bite.

Relationship restored.

Two other people stood near the cart.

One woman in a dark green traveling coat with three short brass rods strapped along one forearm.

No obvious weapon besides a narrow knife.

Small leather case at her feet.

Probably Nemi Voss.

The other wore a gray ward coat reinforced at the shoulders and carried enough gear to open a small clinic or siege a pantry.

Two folded ward stakes over one shoulder.

Medical satchel across the other.

Short hammer at belt.

Probably Orra Tel.

Fen looked up from the roster.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

"Field support?"

"Structural mostly. Sword enough."

"Enough for what?"

Good question.

"Depends what tries to eat us."

Sera said, "He loses to short women."

I looked at her.

Fen did not.

"Useful limitation," he said.

Maybe I liked him less.

He pointed at the green-coated woman.

"Nemi Voss. Resonance survey."

Nemi raised two fingers.

No smile.

"Greg."

"What does resonance survey mean?"

"It means people ask me that before hello."

"Hello. What does resonance survey mean?"

Sera laughed into breakfast.

Nemi considered me.

"Better."

She tapped one of the brass rods on her forearm.

"I excite material or active residue with a known pulse and listen to what repeats."

"Listen how?"

"Mostly ears. Sometimes teeth. Occasionally bad decisions."

There went the clean explanation.

"You identify spells?"

"No."

Immediate.

"Functions?"

"Sometimes."

"Age?"

"Sometimes."

"Danger?"

"Sometimes."

"Very reassuring profession."

"People hear one ringing stone and decide I know what dead people wanted. I don't. I know when the same pulse comes back twice."

Useful boundary.

I did not say it.

Fen pointed at the ward-coated woman.

"Orra Tel. Ward. Field medical basic."

Orra held out her hand.

I shook it.

Strong grip.

Meaning nothing.

"Structural support?" she asked.

"Mostly."

"Can you cast shelter?"

"Yes. Not as well as you, probably."

"Good."

Allowed use, maybe.

"If I say leave my ward alone, leave it alone."

"Yes."

She looked mildly surprised by the speed.

Faultglass remained the gift that kept making conversations strange.

Fen folded the roster.

"Dessa Kelm meets us at site. City antiquary observer. She is not field staff. Do not give her field jobs because she asks."

"She does that?" Sera asked.

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because she has hands."

Sera nodded.

Understandable problem.

Fen looked at all four of us.

"Cart carries camp, survey gear, tagged sacks, and anything the city claims. Personal kit stays personal. Road is two hours. Last mile is footpath. Bank is unstable near the cut. Nobody goes below Fen marker without me."

Nemi said, "You're Fen."

"Correct."

"Third person sounds important."

"Makes rules stronger."

Sera dropped from the wall.

"Who owns fight?"

Fen pointed at her.

"If it's obviously a fight, you."

Then at Orra.

"If protection is failing, Orra."

At Nemi.

"If old magic starts doing something none of us understand, Nemi says what she actually knows and we listen to the uncertainty too."

Then me.

"If stone, load, footing, or movement becomes the main problem, Greg."

"And you?" I asked.

"Route, site boundary, retreat, contract."

There.

Clear enough.

"Who calls if two things happen?" I asked.

Fen looked at me.

"Whoever sees the part that kills us first."

Less bureaucratic than expected.

I approved privately.

We loaded.

The survey cart did not have seats because Guild operations remained philosophically opposed to joy.

Fen walked the mule.

Orra walked beside the left wheel.

Nemi climbed onto the rear edge for exactly thirty seconds before Fen made her get off because the cart springs were rated for equipment, not people.

Sera laughed until Fen told her the same rule applied to fighters.

I used the walking leg for road.

Field peg strapped to the cart where I could change before the river path.

The west road out of Carrow ran between low farms, tile yards, and enough drainage ditches to make Jorren emotional.

Spring had flattened the grass near every culvert.

Mud remained in shaded ruts.

No monsters attacked us.

No bandits appeared.

We walked.

Nemi spent the first mile complaining about the cart wheel squeak because it sat almost exactly on the tone she used for one of her brass rods.

"Change rod," Sera said.

"Change wheel."

"Wheel has seniority."

"Bad institution."

Orra asked Fen about the no-flame rule.

Finally.

Fen said, "Site air test found volatile pollen or resin dust in two enclosed pockets. Nobody knows source yet. Antiquary office says old garden complex. Guild says don't create ignition until we know."

"Active plants?" I asked.

"Maybe."

"After how long?"

Fen shrugged.

"Old things don't always know they're old."

That was almost poetic.

I distrusted it.

Sera said, "So no cooking?"

"Camp is outside site boundary. Cook there."

"Then acceptable."

Correct priorities.

After the second mile, Fen started asking questions.

Not biographies.

Failure questions.

"Orra. What do you stop doing when ward draw spikes?"

She answered immediately.

"Adding area."

"Nemi. When do you stop interpreting?"

"When the return changes after I move the source. Means I don't know if I'm hearing site or myself."

"Sera."

"When I can't see both hands."

Fen nodded.

Then looked at me.

"Greg."

I thought.

Too many answers.

Broad plane when local movement works.

Planning second move before first exists.

Using body after socket warms.

Treating acceptable cost as permission.

"When I start solving the next problem before this one happens," I said.

Sera turned.

"He learned one thing."

"You hit me repeatedly."

"Educational service."

Fen did not laugh.

He wrote something on the roster margin.

"What?" I asked.

"People lie less about failure than strength if you ask fast."

"You do this every crew?"

"Every crew I don't know."

"Does it work?"

"Sometimes."

Nemi pointed at herself.

"My profession."

The road narrowed after a small village called Bramble End.

Five houses.

One shrine beneath a split cedar.

A bakery that smelled better than Northbank apparently deserved.

Mara would need to know eventually.

I did not buy bread for mailing.

Travel food had limits.

West Bramble river ran another half mile beyond the village.

Normally broad and brown.

Now wider.

Spring flood had chewed the near bank into raw layers.

Fen stopped the cart well before the water.

"Change gear here."

I switched to the field peg.

Sera watched without comment this time.

Progress or boredom.

Orra tightened both ward-stake straps.

Nemi moved the brass rods from forearm to belt where she could reach them separately.

Fen unloaded one coil of marker rope and a bundle of blue stakes.

We left the cart with a local Guild keeper and took the footpath south along the river.

The first flood scar appeared as uprooted willow and exposed red earth.

The second took half the path.

Fen rerouted us uphill through wet grass.

No one argued.

At the third bend, he raised one hand.

We stopped.

"Site starts below."

I saw nothing at first.

River.

Broken bank.

Pale stone where dirt had fallen away.

Then the angle changed as we moved three steps farther.

Oh.

The flood had not uncovered a doorway.

It had removed one side of a hill.

Below us, maybe forty feet down, a wide stone courtyard leaned toward the river at a shallow angle.

Open to sky.

Half drowned in mud.

The far wall remained buried beneath roots and earth.

The near edge ended where the river had bitten through it.

Blue-green glazed channels crossed the courtyard in branching lines.

Not cracks.

Built channels.

Some still held water.

Sunlight hit the glaze and moved color across the wet stone.

Broken statues stood along the upper side.

Not people exactly.

Long bodies wrapped in carved leaves, each holding a shallow stone bowl over one shoulder.

Three had heads.

Two did not.

A fourth lay face-down in mud.

At the courtyard center sat a circular basin wider than Lower Rook's ground room.

Dry.

Completely dry despite everything around it being wet.

That bothered me immediately.

Nemi made a small sound.

Not fear.

Attention.

"What?" Fen asked.

She had not touched a brass rod yet.

"Nothing I can claim."

Good answer.

Orra pointed lower.

"There."

Tracks crossed one strip of mud near the far wall.

Four toes.

Long heel.

Too large for dog.

Too narrow for grazer.

Several sets.

Sera crouched beside me.

"Finally."

"You don't know what made them."

"Still finally."

Fen planted the first blue marker stake above the slope.

"Nobody goes below this until Kelm arrives and we establish descent."

Sera looked at the tracks.

Nemi looked at the dry basin.

Orra looked at the glazed channels.

I looked at the entire tilted courtyard and started trying to decide what moved first.

Then stopped.

Nothing had moved yet.

Better to meet the place before solving it.
