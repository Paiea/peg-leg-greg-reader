# Candidate Chapter 153: The Listener

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Nemi hated camp spoons.

Not all spoons.

Our spoons.

"They ring," she said.

Sera looked at the wooden bowl in her hand.

"They're wood."

"The handles aren't."

Guild had provided folding camp spoons with thin metal handles riveted into wooden heads.

Every time someone dropped one against a pot, Nemi flinched.

Fen looked toward the cooking fire.

"Move pot ten feet."

Orra said, "Or she could survive dinner."

Nemi pointed at her.

"Medical cruelty."

Camp sat above the flood cut on a patch of dry grass outside the no-flame boundary.

Two small tents.

One canvas lean-to for gear.

Survey cart under a tarp.

Mule tied where it could eat without walking into the river.

The courtyard remained visible below through the broken bank.

Mist still turned over the dry basin.

The channels still carried water toward it.

Nothing had exploded.

This disappointed Sera enough that she checked the dead drakes twice for useful teeth.

Fen told her salvage tags first.

She told Fen she understood.

Then checked the teeth again without taking them.

Professional growth varied.

Dessa and Nemi had spent most of the afternoon at the observation line.

No more pulses.

Just watching.

The results were stranger than the fight.

Every twenty or thirty minutes, one of the leaf-bodied statues tilted its bowl.

Not always the same statue.

A thin stream of water appeared from nowhere visible, ran through one branch of glazed channel, then vanished toward the basin.

The basin remained dry.

Mist thickened slightly each time.

By evening, the air over it looked like a low white ring.

Dessa had filled nine pages.

Nemi had filled half of one.

I trusted both documents for different reasons.

After dinner, Fen spread the site sketch on a crate.

"Tomorrow."

We gathered.

Sera sat on the ground with her back against a pack.

Orra remained standing because ward people apparently disliked comfort.

Nemi held her bowl as far from the metal spoons as possible.

Dessa knelt beside the map.

Fen tapped the upper courtyard.

"We cleared this only. Far bank stays closed until we know if the injured drake nested there. Center basin stays outside approach. Buried left wall stays outside approach."

Dessa inhaled.

Fen looked at her.

She exhaled.

Progress.

"Tomorrow we map upper channels, inspect statue bases, and establish whether there's a safe route to the next terrace. No deep entry."

Sera said, "What next terrace?"

Dessa pointed to the sketch.

"You can see the paving continue under flood silt here and here. The visible courtyard may be upper level of a larger garden."

"May," Nemi said.

Dessa nodded.

No fight.

Healthy profession.

I looked at Nemi.

"You heard the injured drake through the channels?"

"I heard displacement change a continuous return."

"Same result."

"No."

Right.

"Can you tell direction from that?"

"Sometimes."

"Distance?"

"Badly."

"Number?"

"Worse."

Sera said, "Can you tell if something big is coming?"

Nemi thought.

"If it crosses active glaze, probably. If it moves through mud only, maybe not."

Sera nodded.

Simple enough.

I asked, "Can you map which channels are connected without waking more of the court?"

Nemi looked toward the ruin.

"Passive, partly. Active, better."

Fen said, "Define active."

"One low pulse from above marker. Smaller than noon. I listen for path only. If site response continues after source stops, we don't repeat."

Fen looked at Orra.

"Ward concern?"

"From the pulse itself? No. From what answers? Unknown."

"Kelm?"

Dessa said, "If the complex is hydraulic, knowing connected runs before stepping on them helps."

Fen looked at me last.

"Structural?"

"No objection from here."

Not my test.

That felt increasingly normal.

Fen nodded.

"One pulse before dark. Observation line only."

Nemi put her bowl down.

Immediately.

Apparently dinner had been a delay in science.

We walked to the upper marker together.

Nobody descended.

The sunset turned the river copper behind the courtyard.

Blue-green glaze below caught the opposite color and looked almost black.

Mist over the basin stayed white.

Nemi opened her leather case.

She did not choose the same rod as before.

Shorter.

Higher tone.

"Why that one?" I asked.

"Lower tone traveled too far into buried wall. I want surface network."

"Will higher stop there?"

"Maybe."

There it was again.

A profession built honestly around maybe.

Nemi set the brass rod against the top edge of an exposed channel.

Fen said, "Everyone still."

We were.

Nemi struck once.

Ting.

Smaller than noon.

The channel answered immediately.

One line of water below shivered.

Nemi closed her eyes.

Not mystical.

Listening.

Her head turned slightly right.

Then center.

Then left.

Three returns.

The basin's low tone changed.

Nemi lifted the rod.

Source stopped.

The basin stopped changing.

No statue moved.

No water appeared.

Fen waited.

Ten breaths.

Twenty.

Nothing else.

"Report," he said.

Nemi opened her eyes.

"Upper channels are not one network."

Dessa leaned forward.

"How many?"

"At least three from this point. Maybe four."

She pointed without crossing the marker.

"Near branches connect to the first two statues. Center run connects to basin. Left run goes under buried wall and returns through something hollow."

"Room?" Dessa asked.

"Hollow."

"Pipe?"

"Hollow."

Dessa sighed.

Nemi ignored her.

"One more thing."

Fen waited.

Nemi tapped the side of her own jaw.

"The center return comes back before the pulse should reach the basin at that material speed."

I frowned.

"Reflection from something closer?"

"Possible."

"Hidden branch?"

"Possible."

"Magic?"

She looked at me.

"Everything here is magic. Bad category."

Fair.

Dessa asked, "Could the basin be sending instead of returning?"

Nemi went still.

Not dramatic.

Just attention.

"Maybe."

Fen said, "Meaning?"

Nemi looked at the courtyard.

"My pulse may not be causing every answer. It might only be arriving while the court is already doing its own cycle."

That changed the test.

Not much operationally.

A lot conceptually.

"Can we separate them?" I asked.

Nemi looked at the sky.

Then the mist.

Then her sand timer.

"Don't pulse. Listen long enough."

Sera groaned.

"How long?"

"I don't know."

"Terrible answer."

"Accurate answer."

Fen looked at the remaining light.

"Twenty minutes. Then camp."

Nemi sat down at the marker.

Cross-legged.

One brass rod held loosely against the channel.

No striking block.

The rest of us waited.

Waiting became its own kind of field work.

Sera lasted four minutes before sitting.

Orra lasted seven.

Dessa never stopped writing.

I shifted weight twice because field peg and stillness remained enemies.

Fen watched the river more than the ruin.

At eleven minutes, one statue bowl tipped.

Water appeared.

Nemi raised one finger.

Nobody spoke.

The stream entered the upper channel.

We could hear it faintly from the slope.

Trickle.

Turn.

Then the basin tone rose.

Nemi's finger moved.

One.

Two.

Three beats.

The tone fell.

Another statue tipped on the opposite side.

Water appeared there.

Nemi counted silently again.

I started seeing a pattern.

Stopped myself from deciding it.

At seventeen minutes, the third event began.

No statue moved.

The basin tone rose anyway.

Nemi's eyes opened.

The brass rod under her hand vibrated.

Once.

Pause.

Twice.

Pause.

Once.

From the buried left wall came the soft double tap we had heard earlier.

No pulse from us.

Everyone heard it this time.

Sera stood.

Hand on sword.

Fen said, "Position only."

She stopped.

The double tap came again.

Then scraping.

Slow.

Stone or shell against stone.

Not the high wet scream of a bank drake.

Different.

Nemi took her hand off the rod.

"That's not echo."

Nobody argued.

Dessa stared at the buried wall.

"Could be mechanism."

Sera said, "Could be teeth."

"Both," I said.

Everyone looked at me.

"What?"

Sera smiled.

"Finally useful optimism."

Fen checked the light.

"We don't open unknown cavities at dusk. Back to camp."

Again, nobody argued.

That was starting to worry me.

We walked back.

Nemi carried the rod in one hand instead of putting it away.

At camp she skipped the metal spoon entirely and ate stew with bread.

Professional adaptation.

Later, after dark, I woke to the sound of rain.

Except the tent roof was dry.

I sat up.

Listened.

Not rain.

Water moving below us.

A lot more than before.

Outside, Nemi was already awake.

She stood at the edge of camp in bare feet, coat thrown over her sleeping shirt, staring down at the courtyard.

Moonlight caught the basin.

Still dry.

The rest of the court had changed.

Pale shoots pushed from cracks beside the glazed channels.

Dozens.

Maybe hundreds.

Thin white stems unfolding where there had been only mud that afternoon.

No leaves yet.

Just growth.

Nemi said quietly, "I didn't hear that part."

Neither had I.

The garden had started growing anyway.
