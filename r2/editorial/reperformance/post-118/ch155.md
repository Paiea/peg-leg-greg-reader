# Candidate Chapter 155: The Curator

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Dessa was certain the seam was a door.

This should have warned us.

"Look at the proportions," she said.

We stood above the upper courtyard shortly after sunrise.

The garden had gone quiet overnight.

White plants remained folded along the channels.

No pollen visible.

No water moving.

Mist over the basin gone.

Orra had sampled the air twice and allowed masks off above the marker, masks on below.

Sera's eyes were normal again.

She had announced this by blinking at Orra aggressively until Orra told her to stop being twelve.

The seam in the buried left wall was clearer now.

Yesterday, one vertical line through mud.

This morning, after the garden shut down and dried slightly, a second line showed near the base.

Dessa pointed from above.

"Vertical edge. Bottom threshold. Width maybe three feet if the other side is under that root mat. Service door."

Fen said, "Maybe."

Dessa looked offended.

"Construction logic."

Nemi said, "Still maybe."

Dessa looked at her.

"You are worse than he is."

"Different profession."

We descended.

No new symptoms.

No bank drake appeared.

Fen still kept Sera on the far side and Orra between the plants and the rest of us where growth remained dense.

The folded leaves did not release visible pollen while inactive.

Good enough for short work.

At the buried wall, we stopped ten feet back.

The seam ran through pale stone plastered with old mud.

Roots crossed above it.

One glazed channel disappeared directly beneath the wall.

That mattered.

Dessa crouched.

"See? Door beside water run. Maintenance access."

I looked at the lower seam.

Too low for a threshold.

Maybe.

"Can I clear mud?" I asked.

Fen looked at Orra.

She looked at the plants.

"Dry section only. Gloves."

Dessa handed me a wooden scraper before I asked.

Prepared criminality.

I cleared a hand-width around the lower seam.

Stone.

Then another inch.

Metal.

Not hinge.

A dark bronze strip set horizontally behind the plaster face.

Dessa leaned closer.

"Door frame."

"Maybe."

She stared at me.

I smiled behind the cloth mask.

Nemi pressed one brass rod against the wall two feet above the seam.

No strike.

"Hollow behind."

Fen asked, "How far?"

"Close. Less than arm length to first hard return."

"Room?"

Nemi looked at Dessa.

"Hollow."

Dessa made an impatient noise.

Sera said, "I support Voss."

"Of course you do."

"She says fewer nouns."

Fair.

Fen allowed one active pulse.

Nemi used a short rod.

Tap.

The wall answered immediately.

Then something lower answered farther right.

Nemi moved the rod down.

Tapped again.

Return shifted.

She frowned.

"The hollow changes across height."

Dessa said, "Stairs."

Nemi said nothing.

I looked at the bronze strip.

"Or moving panel."

Dessa turned toward me.

"Why?"

"Because threshold shouldn't be bronze all the way through."

"Could be reinforced."

"Could."

No winner yet.

Fen said, "How do we open without forcing?"

Dessa pointed at the vertical seam.

"There should be latch hardware. Mud-covered."

We cleared more carefully.

No latch.

Found a second bronze strip running vertically.

Then a shallow recessed grip at waist height.

Dessa smiled.

"Door."

Sera took the grip.

Fen said, "Stop."

She let go.

"I was touching."

"You were about to touch harder."

"True."

Orra placed one ward stake behind us, facing the opening.

Nemi moved to the side where she could listen without standing directly in front.

Fen put Sera opposite me.

"Greg. Any load?"

I put one small plane against the panel edge.

Not pushing.

Feeling.

"Panel carries almost nothing vertical."

"Horizontal?"

I shifted.

The plane felt resistance toward the river side.

"Something seats it from right."

Dessa said, "Latch."

"Maybe."

She made a threatening historian sound.

Fen ignored both of us.

"Sera. Pull grip one inch only."

Sera pulled.

The panel did not swing.

It moved down.

Half an inch.

Everyone stopped.

Dessa stared.

Sera looked delighted.

"Door goes down."

"Not a door," I said.

Dessa said, "Vertical sliding door."

"You are fighting this."

"Terminology matters."

Sera pulled another half inch under Fen's call.

The bronze lower strip descended into the floor channel.

A narrow gap opened at the top.

Air came through.

Dry.

Dusty.

Orra held up one hand.

"Wait."

We waited.

She moved a clean cloth near the gap.

Smelled.

No sweet resin.

No visible pollen.

"Old air. Not plant-heavy."

Fen said, "Another inch."

Sera pulled.

Panel moved down again.

Not a hinged door.

A shutter.

Maybe six feet wide once the mud was cleared properly, not three.

Dessa's invisible room had become a service opening.

She looked at it.

Then at me.

"Still access."

"You said door."

"Access door."

"Shutter."

"Functional door."

Nemi said, "Can we enter before language kills us?"

Fen smiled for the first time since I met him.

Small.

Gone quickly.

We cleared enough mud to lower the shutter to knee height.

Then waist.

Behind it sat a narrow covered walkway running parallel to the buried wall.

Not room.

Gallery maybe.

Five feet wide.

Low ceiling.

One side solid stone.

Other side pierced every few yards by small grated openings into the soil and channel network beyond.

Bronze rails ran along the wall at shoulder and ankle height.

Hooks hung from the upper rail.

Shelves built into niches held ceramic jars, stone weights, rotted basket fragments, and dozens of little colored tiles.

Service space.

Caretaker space.

Dessa forgot to be wrong immediately.

"Oh."

Good sound.

Fen said, "Nobody enters until air and floor."

Orra tested air through the opening.

Nemi tapped the threshold once.

The gallery returned a clean hollow note.

No delayed double.

I checked the visible floor.

Stone slabs over something firm.

No fresh cracks.

Sera crouched and looked down the passage.

"Empty."

"Visible empty," Fen corrected.

"Your profession is exhausting."

"Alive though."

"So far."

We entered in single file.

Fen first.

Sera.

Nemi.

Greg.

Orra.

Dessa last because Fen apparently enjoyed historical suffering.

The gallery smelled like dry earth and old copper.

No light except what came through the open shutter and the grated channel vents.

No flame rule.

Dessa produced two cold-light beads from her document case.

Sera stared.

"You had lights?"

"I work in archives."

"And yesterday we sat in dark?"

"You had moon."

Personal resentment formed quickly.

Cold light showed more of the wall niches.

Most containers had cracked.

Inside one, seeds had turned to black powder.

Inside another, dozens of hard ceramic disks remained stacked like coins.

Blue.

Green.

White.

Each stamped with a tiny symbol.

Leaf.

Wave.

Open circle.

Bird.

Snail.

I stopped.

The snail was stupid.

Round blue shell.

Tiny green body.

No practical function visible.

Dessa saw me looking.

"Planting markers. Probably."

"Probably?"

"Could be work tokens. Could mark channel sections. Common icon sets at garden sites."

Sera picked up one white disk with a fish.

"Salvage?"

Dessa held out a tag.

"Loose. Duplicate type. No inscription. Tag it and city decides at exit."

Sera tagged the fish.

I looked at the snail.

Then at Dessa.

She handed me a tag without expression.

"No theft required."

I closed my eyes.

Professional gossip had achieved immortality.

"I hate everyone."

"Tag the snail."

I tagged the snail.

Legally.

Terrible feeling.

Excellent snail.

Nemi ignored the entire economy.

She stood beside the bronze wall rail.

One hand hovering near it.

"This carries resonance."

Fen looked over.

"Active?"

"Not now. But same metal family as channel underlayer."

Dessa moved toward her.

"Maintenance signal line?"

"Could be."

"For what?"

Nemi looked down the gallery.

"Something that needed to know where work was happening."

Sera held up the fish token.

"Garden workers?"

Nemi said, "Maybe."

I watched the bronze rail.

Upper rail held hooks.

Lower rail disappeared into darkness.

At regular intervals, shallow wear marks crossed the stone floor beneath it.

Not footsteps.

Too straight.

Wheel or runner.

"Something traveled here," I said.

Fen crouched.

"Cart?"

"Narrow. Against wall."

Sera looked at the upper rail.

"Tool rack?"

Dessa lifted one cold-light bead higher.

Thirty feet down the gallery, something reflected.

Metal.

A curved shape tucked into a wall recess.

Fen raised one hand.

Nobody advanced.

Nemi put the tip of her shortest rod against the bronze rail.

No strike.

Her face changed.

"Movement."

Sera drew her sword.

"Where?"

Nemi pointed down the rail.

"Coming this way."

Fen said, "Back to shutter. Controlled."

We moved.

No running.

Dessa clutched her cold-light beads and document case.

Orra opened one ward face across the gallery behind us as we passed the first niche.

Not full block.

Delay surface.

The reflected metal shape moved into view.

Not a person.

Not an animal.

A narrow stone basket rode the lower wall rail on four bronze wheels.

Above it rose three jointed brass arms.

One ended in a hooked blade.

One in a two-pronged clamp.

One in a round brush gone mostly bald.

The whole thing was about waist high.

Garden cart.

Caretaker.

Whatever word.

It rolled toward us slowly.

Click.

Click.

Click.

No eyes.

No face.

No dramatic glow.

The hooked blade opened.

Sera raised her sword.

Dessa whispered, "Do not break it."

Sera looked back at her.

"Then tell it."

The old garden had produced its first employee.
