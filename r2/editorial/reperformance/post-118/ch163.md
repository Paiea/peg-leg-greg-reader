# Candidate Chapter 163: The Sponsor

Status: **BOOK RE-PERFORMANCE CANDIDATE / NOT SELECTED AUTHORITY**

Sponsoring Merek Sol required one form.

This seemed insufficiently dangerous.

Tessa put it on the counter.

"Read."

Everyone in Carrow had become Venn.

I read.

**PROVISIONAL RELIABILITY SPONSORSHIP.**

One contract.

Sponsor confirms applicant attendance, conduct, closeout.

Sponsor does not guarantee skill beyond assigned work.

Sponsor may withdraw before departure.

Applicant retains normal pay share.

Any misconduct gets recorded against applicant, not automatically sponsor, unless sponsor conceals it.

Reasonable.

Too reasonable.

"What job?" I asked.

Tessa pointed at our revised southwest map.

"City wants a same-day reroute verification before updating permanent marker four. Two people. One must know current line."

"I know current line now."

"Then why sponsor Merek?"

Direct clerk.

I looked at the form.

Because Ash Road Sol could read terrain better than almost anyone I had known.

Because future knowledge said his skill compounded until armies paid for it.

Because maybe if someone gave him cleaner work earlier, Narrows Gate changed.

None of those answers belonged at the desk.

"Because he called the bank failure from an old map," I said. "I want his route read on the revised line."

Tessa watched me.

"That's a work reason."

"Yes."

"Keep it one."

There.

Clerks occasionally offered prophecy accidentally.

"Where is he?"

"Not my current problem."

She pointed toward the side benches.

Merek sat there.

Of course.

One patched boot stretched forward.

Paper packet in his lap.

He was reading a contract notice and making an expression usually reserved for spoiled milk.

I took the sponsor form over.

He looked up.

Saw it.

Then me.

"No."

Excellent start.

"You don't know what it is."

"Sponsor form."

"You recognize it."

"I've been rejected with it before."

That complicated the clean narrative immediately.

I sat on the other end of the bench.

"Why?"

"Lead didn't want his name on mine after crew office complained."

"Before or after asking?"

"After they checked file."

"They should've checked first."

Merek stared.

"That's your concern?"

"Partly."

He folded the contract notice.

"Why you?"

"Southwest marker four is gone."

His face changed.

Tiny.

Satisfied.

"Told you."

"Yes."

"Bank too?"

"Thirty feet."

"More than I thought."

Good.

He could be right without pretending exactness after the fact.

"City wants revised route verified," I said. "One day. I want you on it."

Merek looked at the sponsor form again.

"Why?"

"You know the route better than I do."

"You walked it yesterday."

"Once."

"You Silver."

"Unrelated."

He looked suspicious now.

Healthy.

"Pay?"

I named it.

"Full split?"

"Normal contract share."

"You take sponsor cut?"

"There is no sponsor cut."

"Could be private."

"No."

"Then why do it?"

There it was again.

People disliked unexplained incentives.

Reasonably.

I could say I liked route talent.

True.

Could say I needed a partner.

Also true.

Could say I had known him in another life as a man whose decisions killed eleven people and saved two hundred thirty-one.

Less useful.

"Because I think your file is compressing two different problems into one reliability mark," I said.

Merek went still.

"What problems?"

"One employer may have stolen wages. One closeout you missed because your brother was in clinic."

His face hardened.

"Tessa told you?"

"Work history is visible to sponsor. Brother detail came from desk yesterday."

"Not your business."

"Correct."

That surprised him.

I kept going before surprise became friendship.

"I don't need brother detail for contract. I need one thing. If you leave, tell me before you leave unless you're unconscious."

"That's it?"

"And file closeout."

"Paper."

"Yes."

He looked personally betrayed by paperwork.

"I do field work."

"Then the field work should be easy."

Merek leaned back.

"You always irritating?"

"Increasingly."

"If my brother goes clinic?"

There.

Current life asserting terms.

"Tell me. We decide whether job can end, reroute, or I finish."

"You don't get to decide clinic."

"Correct. I decide contract response."

He watched me longer.

Then held out his hand for the form.

He read every line.

Good.

Asked one question about conduct note.

Good.

Asked whether sponsor could keep his pay.

No.

Asked whether city route verification counted toward clearing restriction.

Tessa answered from across the room without being invited.

"One clean sponsored closeout removes provisional block. It does not erase old notes."

Merek looked at her.

"You listening?"

"Desk."

Institutional surveillance.

He looked back at me.

"You write conduct note?"

"Yes."

"What if you hate me?"

"Then I write what happened while hating you."

"Very comforting."

"I try."

He took Tessa's pen.

Signed applicant line.

I signed sponsor line.

One form.

No lightning.

No future altered visibly.

Tessa stamped it.

"Departure tomorrow first bell. Southwest six. Verify ridge bypass, locate permanent marker site, check creek approach, return same day."

Merek said, "Need rope."

"Guild has one route kit."

"Bad rope?"

"Standard rope."

"So bad."

Dellen would have liked him.

Maybe hated him.

Could not assume chemistry from complaints.

Tessa handed Merek a temporary route badge.

Not rank.

Contract authorization.

He clipped it inside his coat instead of outside.

"Why hide?" I asked.

"Not rank."

"Still authority."

"Temporary."

Interesting.

I did not make it about his future command career.

Barely.

Merek stood.

"I need clinic before evening."

Not apology.

Information.

"Fine."

"First bell tomorrow."

"Yes."

He started toward the door.

I said, "Merek."

He turned.

"Why did you quit the first crew?"

His face closed.

Maybe too far.

Then he said, "They stopped paying after second interval. Crew lead said city was late. City said crew already paid."

"Night routes?"

"Unregistered."

"Why stay four days?"

"Needed money."

"Why leave then?"

Merek looked at me like the answer was insulting.

"Because after four days I still needed money."

Simple.

Not noble.

Not villainous.

Economics.

"Did you report them?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Didn't think Guild would care."

There.

Actual current flaw.

Not just paperwork.

He made decisions about institutions without testing them.

Maybe accurately sometimes.

Maybe not.

Future Merek had made decisions about roads the same way.

I felt the pattern trying to close again.

Stopped it.

"Tomorrow," I said.

"Tomorrow."

He left.

Tessa came over after.

"You sponsor many people?"

"First."

"Of course."

"What does that mean?"

"Nothing."

Clerk revenge.

She took the duplicate form and filed it.

I kept my copy.

On the walk home, I caught myself planning Merek's next three contracts.

Route verification.

Then maybe caravan scout.

Then a standing crew that would teach paperwork without humiliating him.

Then maybe no Ash Road.

Easy.

I stopped in the middle of Bell Tile street.

A cart driver swore at me.

Fair.

One contract.

That was what I had actually done.

One.

I resumed walking before my brain promoted itself to author of another man's life.
