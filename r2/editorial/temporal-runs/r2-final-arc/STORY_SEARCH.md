# R2 Final Arc Story Search

Status: **SPECULATIVE / ZERO AUTHORITY**

Authority: selected through Chapter 164.

This search exists to choose the final R2 movement, not to create more open-ended future.

## Search criteria

A winning route should:

- feel like a satisfying fantasy arc even if the reader ignores the meta/theme;
- use Greg's current specialization without making him secretly best at everything;
- give several other people real ownership;
- create a meaningful ugly-vs-safe choice that Greg can rationally choose;
- let the ugly choice materially work;
- leave a durable post-victory consequence suitable for 1–3 ordinary landing chapters;
- avoid repeating Quarry Bell or West Bramble;
- generate enough action, travel, money, gear, food, loot/salvage, and weird magic to feel like an ending arc;
- plausibly fit inside roughly 12–16 full chapters plus landing.

## Route A — The Storm Road

### Premise

A seasonal high-road contract opens after an early magical storm strips old ward-cover from a cliff route above the western coast. The road is not a buried ruin. It is a still-used, dangerous piece of infrastructure threaded through exposed cliffs, suspended spans, wind channels, and old storm hardware.

A valuable caravan / Guild convoy must cross before the weather closes again. Several specialists are hired because ordinary road crews cannot safely move the cargo through the damaged section.

The job has a hard environmental clock without being a trapped-worker rescue.

### Fantasy pleasure

- cliff roads above open sea;
- wind magic, lightning residue, hanging spans, old storm vanes;
- pack animals and cargo under magical weather;
- rival convoy / competing contract pressure;
- aerial or cliff-dwelling monsters drawn to charged metal;
- stormglass / wind-metal salvage;
- exposed camps and miserable food;
- named pieces of old road equipment that workers actually use rather than worship;
- one large moving combat where terrain and force routing matter more than raw output.

### Greg pressure

The road repeatedly demands very fast tiny corrections across many moving bodies / loads.

Greg's current support specialty is useful, but not fast enough to cover a late catastrophic sequence without either:

1. taking the slower route, abandoning valuable cargo / losing the contract / waiting out the storm and accepting major material loss; or
2. using a setter's cuff or related professional accelerator to lock himself much harder into one structural family for the duration.

The ugly path works.

Possible durable result: Greg comes out substantially faster and more reflexive at his narrow lane, but broad structural work now feels farther away / slower / requires deliberate rebuilding. Not crippling. Not a curse. A real specialization cost he knowingly accepted.

### People pressure

Greg tries to request some known specialists but gets a mixed roster shaped by availability and rates.

At least one person should be someone Greg would not have selected.

At least one specialist should refuse his proposed sideways experiment because the storm is not the place to test identity.

Someone else should identify a Greg-side adaptation he missed.

### Ending potential

Strong.

The final question becomes concrete: Greg spends future breadth to make the present survivable and successful.

The convoy arrives.

People get paid.

The choice worked.

Then ordinary life reveals that some doors are now genuinely harder for him to open.

## Route B — The Blackwater Race

### Premise

A rare magical flood shifts the Blackwater delta and exposes several high-value deposits / wreck sites at once. Guild crews, salvagers, merchants, and local families race to claim legal salvage before the water returns.

Greg takes a contract not to explore a ruin but to move a specialist salvage team across unstable islands and tidal channels while other crews compete for the same prizes.

### Fantasy pleasure

- boats, mudflats, temporary islands, half-drowned roads;
- salvage law and actual greed;
- rival crews who are not villains;
- monsters driven into shallow water by the flood;
- auction / claim stakes;
- strange objects, wreckage, old cargo, monster materials;
- camp politics and stolen-but-legal opportunities;
- one spectacular chase / retreat when the water returns early.

### Greg pressure

Greg increasingly treats route risk, people, and equipment as one optimization problem.

The ugly choice is not necessarily an artifact. He may knowingly use an unstable shortcut / partial technique that places the largest permanent risk on himself because he believes his adaptation cost is lower than losing the team / claim.

### People pressure

Excellent for team composition and consent. Poorer for Greg's current magic specialization unless the terrain is designed very carefully.

### Ending potential

Medium-high.

Fun and materially rich, but danger of feeling like West Bramble plus competition unless visual / mechanical identity stays extremely distinct.

## Route C — The Broken Tournament

### Premise

A regional field exchange / ranked team event begins as ordinary competition and turns into a real emergency when a demonstration-grade magical construct, bound monster, or old arena system fails under public load.

The people already present are the only specialists available immediately.

The first half is genuine tournament / festival pleasure. The second half becomes real stakes.

### Fantasy pleasure

- team events;
- weird specialized techniques;
- prizes, food, betting-adjacent social energy without gambling focus;
- rivals;
- public demonstrations;
- Greg getting beaten at things;
- Greg's weird team composition working in an event nobody designed for them;
- emergency action in a crowded public magical venue.

### Greg pressure

The emergency requires Greg to use a tool / technique he had rejected for competition because the irreversible cost was not worth a prize.

Now other people are at risk.

He takes the ugly option.

### People pressure

Very strong. Many recurring people can appear organically without becoming permanent party members.

### Ending potential

Medium.

Could be extremely fun but risks feeling too convenient if the tournament exists only so everyone can be present for the final emergency. Also less travel / discovery than preferred for the last R2 fantasy arc.

## Route D — The White Spine Contract

### Premise

A monster migration opens a temporary path across a high white-stone range that has been closed for years. A consortium hires a mixed expedition to cross, establish whether the route is commercially viable, and retrieve proof / material from the far side before the migration closes it again.

The route passes through giant bone-white ridges, wind-carved caves, living mineral growths, and territory used by large migratory creatures.

This is exploration, but not an archaeological ruin.

### Fantasy pleasure

- true expedition travel;
- big monsters;
- strange high-altitude ecology;
- camp, food, cold, gear, prosthetic problems;
- route finding;
- valuable monster / mineral salvage;
- rival commercial interests;
- one huge environmental set piece;
- discovery of a place Greg never saw in Life One or remembers incorrectly.

### Greg pressure

His old-life knowledge is weak here. He must depend on current specialists.

Late in the crossing, a route failure creates the safe-vs-ugly decision. The ugly path could involve permanent magical specialization, artifact use, or accepting a professional / social commitment that changes what work he can do afterward.

### People pressure

Strongest route for an asymmetric temporary crew becoming meaningful through lived time.

Also provides room for Greg to see someone from Life One differently without centering Merek.

### Ending potential

Very high, but highest sprawl risk. Must stay one contract, one crossing, one return/landing. Do not let the far side open Book Two.

## Current engine preference

**Route A — The Storm Road** is the strongest current final-arc candidate.

Why:

- visually and mechanically distinct from the recent ruin/garden work;
- naturally uses Greg's support specialization without requiring another research arc;
- produces travel, action, money, weather, cargo, monsters, gear, and team pressure;
- makes the setter's cuff relevant as an earned professional temptation rather than a random cursed-object callback;
- creates a clean irreversible tradeoff that can genuinely work;
- has a natural end state: the convoy either crosses or it does not;
- when the convoy arrives, the story can stop escalating.

## Challenge before selection

Do not select Route A merely because it currently ranks first.

Before full prose:

- rehearse one short fork where Greg refuses the cuff / accelerator and accepts material loss;
- rehearse one where he uses it and the convoy succeeds;
- confirm the cost feels like a **choice Greg would make**, not an author-designed theme machine;
- confirm at least three non-Greg people have independent reasons for being on the road;
- confirm Mara's Northbank life can intersect emotionally without being physically dragged into the expedition;
- confirm the ending can land within 1–3 ordinary chapters after the crossing.

If those checks fail, prefer Route D rather than patching Route A endlessly.
