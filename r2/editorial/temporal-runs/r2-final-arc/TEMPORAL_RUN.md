# R2 Final Arc Temporal Run

Status: **ACTIVE SPECULATIVE DEVELOPMENT / ZERO AUTHORITY UNTIL SELECTED**

Parent contract: `r2/editorial/R2_COMPLETION_CONTRACT.md`

Authority anchor: selected R2 through Chapter 164.

## Clock

Define **F0** as the first ordinary morning after the Chapter 164 sponsored-route success has had time to settle.

Search window: approximately **F0 through F35**.

This is enough time for one substantial fantasy arc plus ordinary consequence. Do not use the clock to force exact chapter count.

## Stop condition

Stop speculative forward generation at the first of:

- a causally earned final-arc resolution plus ordinary-life landing;
- approximately Chapter 180 worth of selected material;
- a strong final image that resolves R2's central question;
- Chapter 184 hard ceiling from the completion contract.

Do not open another major arc after the final problem resolves.

## Current state entering the run

- Greg has broad competence but is narrowing toward a distinctive support specialization around movement, force, fixed points, failure paths, and creating usable conditions for other specialists.
- His people-side analogue is also emerging: he can notice unused capability and create conditions for safe experiments, but capability is not aspiration and other people own their growth.
- Mara is established independently in Northbank with her own work and authority.
- Hearthline has reopened only bounded professional access after the Faultglass theft; trust is not restored.
- Red Quay proved Greg works well with sharply specialized professionals without becoming their center.
- Lysa and Tomel remain independent specialists rather than assigned permanent party members.
- West Bramble established that a temporary crew can become highly functional without becoming a declared party.
- Merek Sol received one bounded sponsorship from Greg, succeeded, and improved his Guild standing. Leave that success alone long enough to become evidence in Greg's head.
- Faultglass was stolen, returned, and costly, but the knowledge Greg extracted survived. Greg has evidence that a bad artifact decision can still leave durable value.
- The setter's cuff remains unresolved temptation, not mandatory plot.

## Hard constraints

- Final arc must be satisfying fantasy, not a thematic seminar.
- Greg may be wrong, but do not make him stupid.
- Greg's risky logic must substantially work before its deeper cost becomes visible.
- Do not require another body-part loss.
- Do not erase existing permanent consequences.
- Mara, specialists, Guild workers, friends, rivals, and temporary teammates retain independent schedules and wants.
- No person becomes available merely because Greg needs a party slot.
- No artifact becomes evil merely to punish Greg for touching it.
- No third reincarnation is assumed.
- Death is possible only if causally earned, not as a required ending.
- Do not use a giant villain/lore reveal solely because this is the end of R2.
- Do not exceed the completion contract's Chapter 184 ceiling.

## Soft pressure

Prefer a final problem that naturally combines several things R2 learned to do well:

- travel to a memorable location;
- a real fantasy objective rather than another building consultation;
- sharply specialized people whose odd limitations create interesting team composition;
- monsters, ruins, magical infrastructure, rival crews, dangerous terrain, salvage, or a valuable object;
- ordinary contract/pay/food/gear/socket consequences;
- a situation where Greg's unusual support specialization is genuinely superior to generic power;
- an opportunity for Greg to make someone better **only if they want the experiment**;
- at least one person who teaches or corrects Greg;
- one meaningful disagreement that competence cannot instantly solve;
- at least one clean dopamine win.

## The final decision

The arc should organically produce a decision with two real paths.

One path is slower, safer, broader, socially easier, or preserves more future options.

The other path is uglier, faster, narrower, more irreversible, or costs Greg something he believes he can successfully route around.

Greg chooses the uglier path because his lived evidence supports the belief that he can adapt.

The choice should materially help solve the final problem.

Do not immediately invalidate it.

The closing consequence should instead show that adaptation changes the shape of the life available afterward.

## Ending tone

R2 should not end in despair.

Greg should still be Greg: funny, practical, hungry, interested in work, capable of affection, and willing to look at the next strange thing.

The final unease is not that he is doomed.

It is that he has become extremely good at making almost any outcome livable.

## Search method

1. Generate broad 3–5-path story search from F0.
2. Prefer paths with strong fantasy pleasure and distinct setting.
3. Rehearse forks cheaply before selecting.
4. Fresh-write full listening-first chapters only after a path survives.
5. Read 4–6 chapter blocks connected.
6. Kill repeated chapter grammar, repeated professional-problem texture, and explanatory theme statements.
7. When the major arc resolves, stop searching outward and write the landing.

## Provisional no-go repetitions

Avoid making the final arc feel like:

- Quarry Bell again;
- West Bramble again;
- another trapped-worker rescue;
- another sequence of warehouses/bridges/counting houses;
- another expert team arriving solely to demonstrate competence;
- another Faultglass theft with a different object;
- Greg assembling exactly tank/healer/rogue/mage equivalents;
- every chapter demonstrating one specialist's lesson in turn.

The final arc should use R2's discoveries without reenacting the experiments that discovered them.
