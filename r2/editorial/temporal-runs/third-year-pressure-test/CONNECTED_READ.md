# R2 Third-Year Pressure Test — Connected Read 119–122

Status: **SPECULATIVE EVALUATION / ZERO AUTHORITY**

Read as a four-scene listening run, not as isolated candidates.

## Overall verdict

**The experiment found forward energy.**

The sequence moves naturally from the end of the Halden promise into ordinary life, magical shopping, paid structural work, and then a recognizable adventure hook. The new Greg boundary pressure appears without becoming the chapter subject. The setter’s cuff is planted as temptation but does not drive the first adventure. This is the desired ratio.

The strongest result is not a single line or artifact idea. It is the transition:

**relationship residue -> mundane capability -> magical market -> paid competence -> Guild interruption -> ruin / stronger party.**

That feels like a story beginning to move again rather than an engine searching for something to say.

## Candidate 119 — Five Days

**Verdict: LIGHT FINISH / strong causal survivor.**

What wins:

- Direct continuation from First Bell.
- Mara’s three offers remain hers.
- Greg’s planning instinct produces friction without making him an idiot.
- The comparison-table gag is character-specific and creates a useful post-promise problem: Greg has lost the giant external clock and immediately tries to manufacture a smaller one.
- Breakfast and bill payment make the relationship feel lived rather than summarized.

What to challenge before A selection:

- The internal run of “No route clock / No promise / No hospital ceiling / No artifact” is slightly too aware of prior arc inventory. A fresh re-performance may keep less of it.
- Several fragment reversals are house-style familiar. Preserve only the ones that sound like Greg rather than learned R2 cadence.
- Do not resolve Mara’s job in the next chapter merely to service geography.

## Candidate 120 — Dead Inventory

**Verdict: SOURCE WIN conceptually; likely LIGHT FINISH on prose.**

What wins:

- Prosthetic adjustment and magical flea market belong in the same chapter without feeling engineered because the repair creates two free hours in Halden.
- `NOT WYRM` is exactly the kind of low-stakes fantasy texture R2 benefits from.
- Greg does not buy the cuff. Asking the price is enough movement.
- The cuff’s attraction is professional convenience, not evil power.
- Buying socks after bargaining over a dangerous artifact is the right mundane-fantasy ratio.

What to challenge before A selection:

- `Corven` and the exact future history are speculative invention. Preserve the function of Greg’s memory but fresh-read current Story State before hardening a named first-life example.
- The sentence cluster explicitly contrasting Old Greg/current Greg is useful search evidence but may be one beat too explanatory in final prose.
- Verify the cuff mechanism against accepted R2 magic before publication. The object must fit the world rather than exist solely as a character metaphor.

## Candidate 121 — Six Millimeters

**Verdict: SOURCE WIN after one continuity cleanup.**

What wins:

- This is the best mundane-capability chapter of the four.
- The problem is boring in a good way and lets feathered planes prove value without an artifact.
- Greg stopping to check socket skin keeps disability material without turning the scene inspirational.
- Money advances. Harrow’s obligation matters.
- The Guild runner interruption earns the adventure hook because Greg has just completed a normal workday.

Required cleanup before any promotion:

- Replace the line `Then stopped because twenty-year-old pride had already cost me enough body parts.` It overstates/warps causality around the leg loss and may also harden an age fact unnecessarily. Keep the behavioral truth only: Greg has learned not to convert skin damage into a pride contest.

Possible fresh form:

> I ignored it for twenty minutes. Then stopped. Skin did not care whether the reason was professional pride or bad scheduling.

Do not patch the rehearsal merely to make it look finished. Re-perform near A if selected.

## Candidate 122 — Quarry Bell

**Verdict: STRONG SOURCE WIN / best chapter-shaped survivor.**

What wins:

- Immediate concrete danger without apocalypse.
- Quarry workers and Guild staff behave like people with procedures rather than NPCs waiting for Greg.
- Greg changes to the field peg because terrain demands it.
- The ruin responds to his structural magic in a way that creates curiosity without solving itself.
- The bell arrives physically through stone before becoming explanation.
- The Gold party introduction is excellent selection pressure: they look competent because of what they check first, not because the narration announces giant power numbers.
- Greg asks for the contract rather than charging heroically into the hole.

What to protect:

> `Gold-caliber party. You could tell because nobody looked at the hole first.`

The exact line may or may not survive later re-performance, but the observation should. Strong teams should read as strong through judgment.

Ruling for experiment stage: **do not polish globally.** The purpose of the run is story selection. If 119–122 move toward A, fresh re-performance should challenge repeated cadence then.
