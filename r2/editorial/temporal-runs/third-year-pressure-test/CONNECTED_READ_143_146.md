# Connected Read — Candidate 143–146

Status: **EDITORIAL CHALLENGER / ZERO AUTHORITY**

## Verdict

The user’s new nudge materially improves the specialization concept.

Greg’s emerging lane should not be only **force/geometry control**. A co-equal half can be **developmental pressure on people**: Greg often sees where a specialist’s self-definition has become narrower than their actual usable capability and creates a condition that encourages one sideways move.

The crucial boundary is that Greg does **not** own the destination.

Candidate 145 is the strongest evidence. Greg correctly notices that Lysa can do more than stand behind a full ward frame, but his proposed solution, simply abandoning the frame, is not the mature answer. Lysa tries the uncomfortable version, discovers value, then independently proposes a smaller wrist frame. Greg supplied pressure, not design authority.

Candidate 146 completes the reciprocity requirement. Tomel pushes Greg out of his own preferred problem-solving shape by making him route a wagon without magic. Then Tomel/Lysa identify a possible Greg strength he has not conceptualized: his body orientation and visible attention may already function as pre-verbal team signaling. Greg cannot become the only character who sees hidden potential in others. Other competent people must be able to see him sideways too.

## Strong survivors

- **Two-half specialization:**
  1. Greg changes physical conditions.
  2. Greg changes developmental conditions for people.

- The second half is not generic mentorship. Greg’s useful move is usually small: change the task, remove a crutch, swap ownership, ask someone to lead, create a safer failure condition, or identify a transferable piece of existing competence.
- Specialists remain specialists. Sideways growth should make them more interesting, not blur everyone into generalists.
- Greg can be wrong about the proposed sideways move even when he is right that a person has unused range.
- The person owns adoption. Refusal is valid. Modification is valid. A different discovered edge is better than obedience.
- Growth should remain reciprocal. People who work with Greg should also identify his blind spots and unused capabilities.
- Repeated coordination can create a group whose members actively improve one another rather than merely combine fixed combat roles.

## Risks / anti-patterns

- Do not make Greg walk through the novel identifying everyone’s secret second class.
- Do not make every nudge succeed.
- Do not convert refusal into evidence that Greg was right all along.
- Do not let “people development” become speeches. Prefer changed assignments, bounded experiments, or physical conditions.
- Do not make Greg’s old-age knowledge automatically superior to somebody’s self-knowledge.
- Do not make all specialists secretly crave broader competence. Some people should prefer staying narrow and be correct.
- Do not use Greg to homogenize an asymmetric party. The goal is sharper people with one or two surprising adjacent moves, not six flexible generalists.

## Next useful pressure

The concept now needs an **earned miss**.

Within the next 4–10 speculative chapters, Greg should push someone sideways and be materially wrong about either:

- what capability transfers;
- what the person actually wants;
- the cost of the experiment;
- or whether broadening is valuable at all.

The miss should not prove the whole instinct bad. It should teach a boundary: creating a condition for discovery is different from deciding another person ought to change.

A second useful test is an actual dangerous job where somebody’s sideways capability, discovered earlier in low-stakes practice, becomes useful without becoming a magical surprise power-up.

Do not formalize a party yet. The stronger target is a network of people who increasingly know **how to make each other better**.
