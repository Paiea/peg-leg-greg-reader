# Connected Read — Candidate 147–154

Status: **EDITORIAL CHALLENGER / ZERO AUTHORITY**

## Verdict

This run materially improves the people-development half of Greg's specialization because it gives the instinct a real boundary rather than converting it into benevolent protagonist omniscience.

The strongest discovery is simple:

**Greg can be right about capability and wrong about aspiration.**

Lysa genuinely has useful mobile ward capacity beyond her full-frame identity. Greg still oversteps when he tells an evaluator what that should mean before Lysa answers for herself. Her later roadside success with the wrist frame does not retroactively justify the overstep. The tool becomes valuable because Lysa chooses, redesigns, carries, uses, repairs, and limits it herself.

Tomel reinforces the same boundary from another angle. He is good at route/sequence leadership and can own it in a task. He does not want a general leadership career. Competence does not create obligation.

## Strong survivors

- Greg's two-half specialization remains promising:
  1. physical conditions: load, force, timing, position, allowed failure;
  2. developmental conditions: altered task, removed crutch, safe experiment, ownership swap, one sideways question.

- The developmental half needs a consent/aspiration gate. A useful default behavioral progression is:
  - notice potential;
  - ask what the person wants or whether they want an idea;
  - create a bounded condition if invited;
  - let the person interpret the result;
  - do not convert success into a career prescription.

- Lysa's full frame remains her core strength. The wrist frame is an adjacent tool, not a class change.
- Tomel remains a rigger/recovery specialist. Route ownership is a situational extension, not a promotion track.
- Greg's own specialization is becoming less about resisting force and more about deciding where force/failure can resolve safely.
- Greg's communication problem is now specific: he often sees the next usable configuration before ownership is socially clear.
- Asking first is useful, but it should not become a formula or cure. People can still say yes and dislike the experiment, say no for reasons Greg does not understand, or change their minds later.

## Risks

- Do not turn `Do you want an idea?` into repeated dialogue machinery. The underlying consent boundary matters, not the phrase.
- Do not make Greg a therapist, life coach, or universal talent detector.
- Do not let every adjacent skill become useful in danger immediately. Many experiments should remain niche, fail, or never matter again.
- Do not make party members dependent on Greg for growth. Lysa designed the wrist-frame solution; Tomel sees Greg sideways; other people should teach each other directly.
- Do not over-moralize the distinction between people and structures. Greg can still make blunt emergency calls when time requires it.
- Do not make Lysa the automatic first permanent party member simply because this run gave her the richest development.

## New pressure generated

1. **Mutual improvement as party glue:** repeated people may become valuable not just because their combat abilities combine, but because they make one another sharper between jobs.
2. **Aspiration as independent state:** track what recurring people actually want, not merely what they can do.
3. **Greg as chooser of conditions, not outcomes:** he can set drills, ask questions, alter constraints, or create safer failure. The other person owns interpretation.
4. **Network before party:** Greg now has multiple possible repeat-work relationships. Let availability, desire, money, geography, and disagreement determine which recur.
5. **Real-world cost remains:** injury, travel, other contracts, relationships, and ordinary life should regularly break otherwise-good party combinations.
