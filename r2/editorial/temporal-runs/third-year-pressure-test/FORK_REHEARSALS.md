# R2 Third-Year Pressure Test — Fork Rehearsals

Status: **SPECULATIVE FORK COMPARISON / ZERO AUTHORITY**

All three forks inherit the same useful foundation:

- First Bell has ended the two-year promise clock.
- Mara’s job decision remains hers.
- Greg picks up ordinary Halden work and pays down real obligations.
- The Lantern Rows introduce an obsolete setter’s cuff Greg remembers dismissing in first life.
- A quarry breach opens a buried structural ruin.
- Greg joins a Gold-caliber field party as structural support.
- Greg earns one clean collapse-save win.
- The ruin reveals that setter’s cuffs were real professional tools, not cartoon cursed objects.
- Monster material may improve one mundane part of Greg’s prosthetic setup.
- Orren Pell functions as a technically excellent rival rather than an asshole-shaped punching bag.

The experiment begins to fork when Greg has enough evidence to make an informed choice.

---

# FORK A — THE CUFF

## Experiment question

What happens if Greg’s new hubris first expresses itself through his own magic: he accepts a permanent narrowing risk because he believes adaptability makes the downside correctly priced for him?

## Entry

Greg does not impulse-buy the cuff.

He behaves like Greg.

He gets the Guild report. Talks to the Halden works master. Watches supervised use. Makes Orren use one first because Orren volunteered and Greg considers this excellent science. Learns that the old horror stories are approximately true but badly framed.

The cuff does not burn mana channels.

It trains them.

Repeated use makes one structural family arrive with less conscious construction. Hold the same kind of plane enough times and the body begins treating it like stance instead of calculation.

The price is interference. Less-used structures become slower to find. Not unavailable. Less native.

First-life Greg rejected this because broad flexibility was one of his greatest strategic strengths.

Current Greg has a different problem.

His feathered planes are good enough to save people and still slow enough to fail under repeated moving loads.

## D803 — Borrowed Tool

Greg negotiates six supervised field sessions instead of buying the cuff.

The artificer thinks this proves maturity.

Greg thinks it proves he is not stupid enough to pay retail.

During the first session the cuff barely matters.

During the fourth, his feathered plane arrives before he finishes naming the geometry in his own head.

That scares him.

Then delights him.

This is important. The artifact has to be good.

## D809 — Red Quay Job

A moving road failure under a loaded convoy creates exactly the situation Greg has struggled with: three supports, changing load, no time to rebuild every plane from first principles.

With the cuff, he catches the first shift, then the second, then the third almost rhythmically.

A wagon that should roll does not.

A driver who should break a leg goes home angry about cargo damage instead.

The Gold party lead says, “Keep that.”

Greg does not explain the cost.

Not because he is hiding it.

Because for the first time the cost feels theoretical and the benefit has a face.

## D814 — First Drift

Back in Halden, Greg tries to form an old broad pressure shelf for a warehouse repair.

It feels wrong.

Not impossible.

Like reaching for a familiar tool and finding someone moved it one drawer lower.

He pauses long enough that Orren notices.

Orren says, “There.”

Greg says, “I know.”

No panic.

They test.

Feathered planes are faster.

Broad shelves are slower.

Fine shaping is unchanged.

The system is not collapsing. It is specializing.

Greg’s first thought is not regret.

It is substitution.

That is the danger.

## D819 — Purchase

The loan period ends.

The vendor’s cuff is still available.

Greg pays Harrow first.

Then buys the cuff.

This matters. He is not throwing responsibilities into a furnace for power. He is choosing the ugly tool after meeting the ordinary bill.

Mara asks the obvious question:

“Can it be undone?”

Greg says, “Not cleanly.”

“Then why?”

“Because I know what it costs now.”

Mara says, “No. You know what it has cost so far.”

That line survives conceptually even if wording changes.

## D824 — The New Build

Greg begins deliberately routing certain work away from the structures that now feel less natural.

Instead of trying to preserve everything, he builds around the specialization.

Field peg for bad terrain.

Walking leg for city.

Feathered planes for moving load.

Other casters or physical braces for broad static support when available.

He becomes better as a party specialist and slightly worse as a universal municipal mage.

The world does not call this a tragedy.

The Guild starts paying him more for the thing he now does unusually well.

Positive reinforcement.

## D830 — Edge

A job requires a structure Greg used to form casually.

He can still do it.

It takes long enough that somebody else solves the problem first.

Greg feels irritation before fear.

Later that night he writes in the notebook:

**Cuff is working.**

Then:

**Other work moving.**

Then, after a long pause:

**Acceptable?**

He does not answer.

### What this fork teaches

**Strengths**

- Directly embodies the new Greg idea through ordinary fantasy progression.
- Gives him a weird, earned specialist build rather than a generic power increase.
- The cost can accumulate gradually instead of arriving as melodramatic punishment.
- Lets Greg be right often enough for the hubris to become dangerous.
- Excellent action utility.

**Risks**

- Too much focus on the cuff could turn the story into artifact thesis delivery.
- Need clear magic continuity before promotion.
- If every lost option immediately produces a superior substitute, consequence becomes fake.

**Verdict:** **STRONG SURVIVOR / REPERFORM LATER.** Best as a slow secondary pressure inside a larger adventure spine, not the whole plot.

# FORK B — THE MAN

## Experiment question

What happens if Greg’s new hubris first expresses itself socially: he knowingly works with a person he remembers becoming dangerous because he believes the failure path can be redirected?

## Entry

Greg refuses the cuff for now.

Not because he concludes it is morally wrong.

Because the safe teacher gives him a viable training route and his finances are ugly enough that buying a permanent magical tradeoff while paying Harrow feels like bad arithmetic.

Then he sees **Salen Veyr** on an expedition intake sheet.

Placeholder identity until a fresh authority audit confirms no collision.

First-life Greg remembers the name from years later.

Not as a legendary villain.

Worse, in a way.

A capable expedition captain who eventually became known for abandoning low-value people and salvaging high-value objectives. Greg had hated him because the man’s decisions were often defensible on paper.

Current Salen is not that man yet.

He is broke, sharp, under-ranked, and very good at route reading.

## D802 — Interview

Red Quay’s scout slot needs a temporary replacement.

Greg is not the hiring authority, but the party lead asks whether he knows Salen because Greg reacted to the name.

Greg lies badly:

“By reputation.”

Technically future reputation.

He recommends a trial.

Why?

Because Greg remembers the exact expedition where Salen’s reputation began to rot and thinks the early problem was simpler: debt, status pressure, and a leader who rewarded salvage value over people.

Greg believes variables can be changed.

## D807 — Salen Is Good

Salen is excellent.

Not secretly sinister.

He reads terrain, marks routes, remembers water, jokes badly, and notices Greg’s field-peg wear before Greg does.

The party likes him.

Greg experiences the deeply inconvenient possibility that the future person is not hiding inside the present person waiting to emerge.

## D812 — First Bad Choice

On a salvage job Salen pockets a small marked component before inventory.

Greg sees him.

Salen does not know Greg saw.

The component is valuable enough to matter but not enough to destroy the contract.

Greg remembers later Salen doing something similar at enormous scale.

This feels like proof.

Then Greg learns why.

Salen owes a healer for his younger brother’s long treatment. Guild wages are being garnished through a debt arrangement that leaves him almost nothing.

Reason is not innocence.

But it is a variable.

Greg does not report him immediately.

## D815 — Greg Fixes It

Greg does what Greg does.

He calculates.

He gets the component returned without public accusation. Helps Salen renegotiate the medical debt through a contact. Pushes Red Quay to structure part of the scout pay as hazard reimbursement that cannot be garnished the same way.

It works.

Salen stops stealing.

Greg feels vindicated.

This is essential.

## D821 — The Actual Cost

The party lead discovers Greg concealed the first theft.

Not because Salen betrays him. Because inventories are boring and competent people notice boring things.

The lead is furious with Greg.

Not primarily with Salen.

“You decided I could afford not to know.”

Greg’s reasoning was good.

The outcome with Salen was good.

He still spent **someone else’s trust** without permission.

That is the social version of the cuff.

## D825 — Salen Chooses

Salen learns Greg knew something about him before they met. Not the full reincarnation truth, but enough to realize Greg was watching him for a failure he had not committed yet.

Salen hates this.

He does not become grateful because Greg helped him.

He leaves the party or refuses Greg’s next recommendation.

Independent agency survives.

## D830 — Edge

Weeks later, Salen returns a piece of route information that saves Red Quay time on a dangerous job.

No apology.

No friendship reset.

Just useful action from an angry person.

Greg has technically improved the future.

He has also damaged two relationships doing it.

The result is not solved or broken.

### What this fork teaches

**Strengths**

- Extremely good regression-specific hubris.
- Makes “I can make it work” apply to people instead of only mechanics.
- Consequence can be relational without pretending Greg’s intervention failed.
- Salen can become a genuinely independent long-term character if he earns it.

**Risks**

- New-character burden is high.
- Easy to accidentally make Salen a morality lesson or puppet for Greg’s psychology.
- Requires care with first-life knowledge so the story does not become prophecy management.

**Verdict:** **STRONG DISCOVERY / KEEP WARM.** Excellent later pressure. Do not rush this into Chapter 119 before the adventure engine is re-established.

# FORK C — THE PARTY

## Experiment question

What happens if we deliberately do less clever shit and simply let R2 be a satisfying fantasy story for a season, allowing Greg’s changing risk tolerance to emerge through ordinary mission choices?

## Entry

Greg refuses the cuff for now.

Greg does not recruit Salen.

He takes the Red Quay second contract.

That is the experiment.

No thesis object.

No future villain.

Just a Silver specialist joining people better than him at several things and going somewhere dangerous for money.

## D801 — Real Party Slot

Red Quay gives Greg an actual share structure.

Lower than the Gold members.

Higher than consultant day pay.

He argues about expenses.

The healer charges party consumables before split.

Greg discovers that professional adventuring is mostly violence wrapped in accounting.

He approves.

## D805 — Gear Day

Greg spends a stupid amount of time on field-peg ferrules, strap wear, rain protection, food weight, staff mounting, and whether the monster-tendon shock insert is actually worth using.

Somebody else buys an enchanted knife.

Greg buys socks.

Fantasy remains fantasy anyway.

## D810 — Road Monster

A recognizable monster encounter with no cosmic importance.

Something territorial hits the convoy at dusk.

The Gold shield lead owns the first ten seconds.

Greg owns the ground after that.

He changes where people can safely stand, pins one route with a feathered plane, and kills one creature when it overcommits.

He looks cool.

The party looks cool.

Nobody gives a speech.

## D814 — Bad Inn

Rain. Wet gear. Greg’s socket skin is angry. The healer tells him to leave the leg off for the evening.

He hates crawling the three steps between bed and washroom more than he hated the monster.

The party learns this without becoming inspirational about it.

Someone puts his pack closer the next night and never mentions why.

## D819 — Ruin Annex

The party clears a secondary chamber connected to the original quarry structure. Orren joins as a specialist for one day.

Greg and Orren compete over a load puzzle while everyone else gets increasingly annoyed.

Orren wins the elegant solution.

Greg’s ugly solution survives the monster interruption.

Both claim victory.

## D823 — Money

Greg receives the biggest single contract share he has earned since Brell.

He pays Harrow.

Replaces a worn socket liner.

Buys dinner.

Has money left.

This should feel like progression.

Not every number exists to set up disaster.

## D827 — The Contract Nobody Wants

A Guild clerk offers Red Quay an unpleasant road job after another Silver structural specialist declines it due permanent-injury risk and poor rescue access.

The pay is excellent but not absurd.

The Gold members debate it normally.

Greg’s internal reaction surprises him.

He does not think, **too dangerous**.

He thinks, **manageable**.

Not because he has the cuff.

Not because he knows a future villain.

Because Brell changed his personal pricing of permanent damage.

This is the new idea appearing naturally inside conventional fantasy.

## D830 — Edge

Red Quay accepts, declines, or renegotiates the job based on the party’s collective judgment.

The important discovery is Greg’s reaction.

A teammate notices Greg is often the person arguing that a bad downside is survivable.

Not always arguing yes.

Just pricing it differently.

The run ends with an adventure decision, not an existential thesis.

### What this fork teaches

**Strengths**

- Highest fun-to-machinery ratio.
- Re-establishes action, party dynamics, loot, travel, money, gear, monsters, rivalry, and mundane fantasy immediately.
- Proves the story does not need the new hubris concept to drive every scene.
- Gives us a baseline against which later artifact/person choices can feel meaningful.
- Easy to write forward from Chapter 118 without overcommitting.

**Risks**

- Could become generic contract-of-the-week if recurring human pressures do not accumulate.
- Needs Mara/Daro/home clocks to keep adventure from floating free of life.
- Greg’s deeper change can become invisible if every job ends cleanly.

**Verdict:** **BEST BACKBONE.** This should probably carry the season, with selected material from Fork A and later Fork B layered into it rather than replacing it.

# Provisional selection

**Use Fork C as the main temporal backbone.**

Layer Fork A in slowly:

- D731–D760: Greg sees the cuff, learns what it is, does not use it.
- D760–D800: conventional party work reveals the exact problem the cuff would solve.
- Only then rehearse borrow/buy/refuse at higher fidelity.

Keep Fork B in warm storage until the story has enough forward momentum to support another major person. If a future-known person naturally appears through the adventure spine, revive the concept without protecting the placeholder Salen implementation.

This gives R2 the thing it currently needs most: **a fun fantasy machine first, dangerous Greg second.**
