# Candidate Chapter 123: Terms

The woman with the shield was named Renn Saye.

Gold.

Red Quay Guild mark beneath it.

She introduced herself after asking whether the ceiling would kill anyone before midnight.

I respected the order.

"No blasting," I said. "No carts within twenty feet of the face. Keep people off the exposed floor groove. If the bell rings again, I want everyone outside the lantern line until I check the wall."

Renn looked at the groove beneath my field peg.

"Why?"

"Because it is not quarry floor. It's part of the buried structure."

"Does the bell move it?"

"I don't know."

"Good answer."

She turned away before I could resent the approval.

Her people had already spread themselves across the quarry without looking like they had.

Asha, healer, was cleaning the foreman's sleeve because apparently some of the blood had become his while I was busy with stone.

Mett had the quarry map pinned under three rocks and was adding marks with a blue pencil.

Veya stood at the exposed arch with two thin copper rods held between her fingers, not touching anything.

Korr walked the lantern perimeter carrying a short hammer that looked too small for his shoulders.

Nobody asked me what class they were.

Professional adulthood had advantages.

Tessa Mar dragged the folding field table closer.

"Terms."

There it was.

Much safer word than bell.

The quarry owner had authorized rescue work through first light and structural survey through the next noon. Red Quay held hazard lead. Halden Guild held site authority. Quarry crews stayed outside unless requested. Anything obviously belonging to missing workers came out with them. Anything old enough to cause arguments went into sealed Guild crates until ownership stopped being philosophical.

"Salvage share?" I asked.

Tessa looked offended on behalf of three missing men.

"After rescue."

"Still part of the contract."

Renn looked at me.

Not angry.

Measuring.

"He's right," she said.

Tessa sighed.

Red Quay had a fixed hazard fee plus licensed salvage split after Guild appraisal. I was being attached as local structural specialist at one specialist share, expenses covered, no claim on Red Quay's standing retainers.

"How much is a specialist share?" I asked.

Renn named it.

I considered Harrow.

Considered Pera.

Considered the fact that one of my good socks was already damp from quarry dust finding ways to become mud.

"Fine."

Tessa blinked.

"No negotiation?"

"I can start if you want."

"Please don't."

Excellent.

Renn pulled the quarry plan toward herself.

"Now the part I care about. What are you?"

"Silver."

"I can read."

"Structural work. Force planes. Field support. Sword if something has teeth."

"That's a resume. What do I hand you and stop thinking about?"

I opened my mouth.

Closed it.

Field Support Three had asked a similar question without saying it that way.

Maren had picked me because I could cover gaps.

Useful then.

Renn was asking for something narrower.

The buried arch sat behind her.

Load moved through it farther than it should.

The floor groove connected to something under our feet.

Three men were inside a system nobody understood.

"Moving structure," I said.

Renn waited.

"If stone, ward load, footing, or support starts changing under us, that's mine."

"Can you stop it?"

"Sometimes."

"Can you tell me when you can't?"

"Yes."

That answer came faster.

She nodded once.

"Good. I don't need another fighter."

Korr called from the perimeter.

"Cruel."

"You count as three fighters and half a person," Renn called back.

"Which half?"

"The expensive one."

Korr seemed satisfied.

Veya came over with the copper rods.

She was maybe thirty, maybe younger. Hard to tell because her hair had been cut with either a knife or contempt.

Silver tab.

No visible weapon.

"Arch ward isn't active," she said. "There are lines in it, but they're dead or sleeping."

"Ward-breaker?" I asked.

"No."

She held up the two rods.

"I cut straight lines."

I waited for the rest.

There was no rest.

"In wards?"

"Anything I can establish between two points. Stone if thin enough. Metal if thinner. Active magic if I can find its edge."

"Curves?"

"No."

"Planes?"

"One line."

"At a time?"

"Do you want it cut or decorated?"

I liked her less immediately.

Promising.

Korr returned.

"I hit things."

"I gathered," I said.

"Hard."

"Specialist."

"Exactly."

Mett spoke without looking up from the map.

"He can hit once every eight breaths if you want his knees afterward. Five if you don't."

Korr pointed at him.

"This is why nobody likes surveyors."

Mett finally looked up.

Bronze clasp.

Older than all of us except maybe Renn. Thin hands. Chalk under every fingernail.

"I listen to stone," he said.

I glanced at the arch.

"Magic?"

"Some. Mostly stone."

"Difference?"

"About twenty years."

Fair.

Asha joined us last.

"I keep blood inside people. I would appreciate cooperation."

That completed the team with unsettling efficiency.

Renn looked at Tessa.

"Missing?"

Three quarry workers.

Fenn, Haro, and Lis.

Fenn had a wife waiting near the upper road despite being told to go home.

Haro's brother worked the night shift and was currently arguing with two guards about joining the search.

Lis had no listed family in Halden. Quarry bunkhouse. Twenty-two.

People acquired details as soon as rescue became real.

Renn tapped the entrance mark.

"We go six. Asha center. Mett calls route. Greg calls structure. Veya does not cut anything unless Mett or Greg clears the material and I clear the action. Korr does not hit anything attached to the building unless Greg says the building deserves it."

Korr frowned.

"Buildings deserve many things."

"Greg decides."

He looked at me.

"You fun?"

"Not usually."

"Shame."

Renn continued.

"Rescue first. Mapping enough to leave safely. No salvage detours. If we find the bell, we mark it and decide there, not from here. If anybody says out, we discuss it outside."

I looked at her.

"Anybody?"

"Anybody."

"Even Mett?"

Mett said, "Especially me."

Renn folded the plan.

There was no speech about trust.

No hand stack.

No declaration that six strangers were now companions.

We checked rope.

Lantern oil.

Water.

Chalk.

Asha made Korr remove one loose buckle because she said she was tired of sewing avoidable holes in people.

Veya sharpened one copper rod with a file.

Mett put his ear against the quarry floor for almost a minute and told everyone to stop walking while he did it.

I checked my field peg strap twice.

Then once more because twice had not improved certainty.

Renn caught me.

"Problem?"

"No."

"Then stop checking."

I disliked leadership when applied correctly.

We crossed Tessa's lantern line.

Six people.

Three missing.

One bell somewhere under the hill.

My job was moving structure.

Narrower than support.

I found that I liked the sound of it.

# Candidate Chapter 124: The Line

# Candidate Chapter 126: Bell Room

Major improvement.
