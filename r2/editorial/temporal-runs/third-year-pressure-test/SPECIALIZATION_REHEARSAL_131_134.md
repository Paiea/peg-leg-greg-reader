# Candidate Chapter 132: Three Seconds

The canal gate house was uglier than the quarry.

This improved morale.

Ruins invited imagination.

Municipal water infrastructure invited complaints.

The east intake sat where an old canal entered Halden beneath two newer streets. Brick above. Dark stone below. Three iron gates controlled the current into settling channels that fed half the river district.

One gate leaned.

Not visibly if you had healthy priorities.

Very visibly if someone told you.

The new masonry around it had cracked in a diagonal fan.

The city engineer, Sor Tallen, hated the crack personally.

"It was straight six months ago."

"Most things were," I said.

He did not appreciate history.

Mett spent twenty minutes tapping the lower wall.

Veya spent fifteen trying not to ask whether she could cut something.

Korr was ordered to carry sandbags and became the most expensive laborer in Halden.

Asha inspected the maintenance crew because one man had a cough she disliked.

Renn asked everyone questions and wrote almost nothing down.

I walked the gate line twice.

Field peg today.

The monster washer still held.

Small improvement.

Forgotten most of the time.

Best kind.

The problem was under the floor.

Old stone channel beneath the new masonry had shifted at one corner. Not collapsed. Rotated.

A wedge of foundation now carried gate load through one narrow rib instead of across the full base.

Every time the gate opened, water pressure pushed that rib sideways.

Six months of sideways had become the crack.

Sor Tallen frowned at my chalk marks.

"Can you brace it?"

"Temporarily."

"How temporarily?"

"Depends what you mean by brace."

He hated that.

Good engineer.

Bad client.

The permanent repair required closing Gate Two, draining the lower channel, lifting part of the frame, rebuilding the rotated corner, and explaining to several thousand people why their water tasted like holding tanks for a day.

The temporary repair needed one hour.

Renn looked at the plans.

"One hour buys what?"

"Enough stability to open Gate One fully and take pressure off Two. Then city can schedule the drain instead of doing it tonight."

"Failure mode?"

"Brace slips, crack grows, we close everything."

"People?"

"No one below the floor."

Good.

We prepared.

This time I did not do everything.

That was harder than it should have been.

Sor's crew built timber shores because they were faster at municipal framing than I was.

Mett marked the only two floor sections he trusted for heavy load.

Veya cut four rusted bolts because that was cleaner than three men sawing them while swearing.

Korr moved the gate crank through half a turn when the mechanism stuck, then sat on a bucket for eight breaths while pretending he had chosen the bucket for aesthetic reasons.

Asha monitored nobody because nobody was injured.

She looked almost bored.

Renn seemed delighted.

"Ready?" she asked.

Almost.

The temporary brace needed a force path from the shifted foundation rib into the intact west wall.

Not one plane.

Three.

First caught lateral push.

Second redirected down.

Third spread the down-load into two masonry feet Mett had cleared.

Feathered.

Thin.

Specific.

Not strong enough alone.

The timber did most of the work.

Good.

I opened the first plane.

Then second.

Then third.

Pressure settled through them.

I could feel the system without Faultglass now.

Not see it.

Not truly.

But enough repetition had taught my body where bad load felt wrong.

"Gate One," I said.

Sor signaled the crew.

Crank moved.

Water changed tone beneath us.

The brace took load.

Timber creaked.

Plane one flexed.

Plane two held.

Plane three spread.

Good.

Then Mett said, "Stop."

Everyone stopped.

Even the water crew.

Renn looked at him.

Mett had one palm against the floor.

"Something underneath."

Sor said, "What?"

Mett ignored him.

Tapped once.

Twice.

His face changed.

"Void moved."

That sentence was incorrect enough to become frightening.

I felt it a breath later.

Plane three lost one of its feet.

Not physically gone.

The stone under it dropped maybe half an inch.

Enough.

Load snapped toward plane two.

The gate frame twisted.

Sor shouted.

Korr moved toward the crank.

Veya moved toward the cracked bolt line.

Renn said, "Greg."

Not a question.

Three seconds.

Maybe less.

Old Greg would have issued six commands.

Current Greg almost did.

Korr on crank.

Veya cut upper hinge.

Mett off floor.

Sor close Gate Two.

Renn brace east.

Asha clear crew.

All of it arrived in my head at once.

People as positions.

Useful.

Dangerous.

I chose two.

"Korr, hold crank. Veya, do nothing."

Veya froze mid-step.

Good.

I dropped plane three entirely.

Let the load move.

Ugly.

Controlled.

Plane two took too much.

I rotated it rather than strengthening.

Redirect into the timber shore.

The shore slammed against its wedge.

Korr caught the crank when it kicked backward.

His whole body moved one foot.

He did not release.

Renn shouted to Sor, "Close Two!"

Not me.

Good.

Sor's crew moved.

Water pressure dropped.

Plane one relaxed.

Plane two stopped screaming through my shoulders.

The foundation settled another quarter inch.

Then stopped.

Silence except water.

My breathing.

Korr's breathing faster.

Three.

Four.

Five.

He released the crank and sat on the floor this time.

No bucket dignity available.

"Everyone?" Asha asked.

Fine.

Mostly.

Sor looked pale.

"What happened?"

Mett answered.

"Old washout below foundation. Your new work bridged it. Gate load finally broke crust."

Sor looked at me.

"You knew?"

"No."

Important answer.

He seemed almost relieved.

Renn looked at the crack.

"Job changed."

"Yes."

No temporary brace.

No one-hour fix.

Close Gate Two.

City problem now.

We backed off.

Veya waited until we were outside before speaking to me.

"Why tell me do nothing?"

Tone flat.

Not angry exactly.

Worse.

Asking.

"You were going to cut the hinge."

"Yes."

"If you cut it while the frame twisted, gate weight would move into the east jamb."

"Probably."

"Bad."

"Probably."

"So I stopped you."

Veya looked at me for a long second.

"You could have said hinge bad."

Three seconds.

"No time."

"There was time for four words."

I counted.

**Veya, do nothing.**

Three, depending on professional cruelty toward names.

**Hinge bad. Hold.**

Three.

Fuck.

Not satisfied.

But answer accepted.

"Next time tell me what you need, not what I am."

There.

"Medical flirting."

"I can make you unconscious."

Renn looked at me.

No rescue.

Good leader.

I looked back at Veya.

"Next time: hinge bad. Hold."

"Better."

Then she handed me a copper rod.

"You still have thirteen?"

I checked my bag.

Her previous spare remained there.

"Fourteen."

She frowned.

"That's too many."

"Take one back."

"No."

"Why?"

"Fixed points."

I was beginning to suspect inventory drift had intent.

# Candidate Chapter 133: Six Days

Mara packed badly on purpose.

I knew this because I had seen her pack well.

Two-year Halden transfer had required lists, crate weights, route labels, backup copies, waxed document sleeves, and enough twine to restrain a small horse.

Northbank got one open trunk, one closed trunk, three stacks of clothes, and a mug sitting inside a boot.

I held up the mug.

"Explain."

"Padding."

"For the mug or boot?"

"Yes."

Unacceptable.

We were in her room after work.

Six days before departure.

The quarry contract was finished enough that Red Quay had gone back to their rented rooms near the west gate.

The canal problem had become a city excavation and therefore someone else's bureaucracy for at least a week.

I had no excuse not to help pack.

Mara knew this.

Possibly why packing had become malicious.

I put the mug on the table.

"Documents first."

"No."

"They crease."

"Greg."

"Fine. Clothes first."

"No."

"What system are you using?"

"I am putting my things into boxes until my things are in boxes."

Barbarism.

I folded a shirt anyway.

Mara watched.

"You hate this."

"I hate your method."

"Same."

"No."

"You know you don't have to come to Northbank."

There it was.

Not first time.

Different now because the trunks existed.

"I know."

"And I don't mean ever. I mean now."

I folded another shirt.

"I know."

"You have work here."

"Temporary work."

"So does everyone until they don't."

Annoying sentence.

Good sentence.

I sat on the edge of the bed.

Walking leg tonight.

Field peg by the door because I had come directly from the canal.

Two legs in one room again.

Mara's life in boxes.

"I don't know what I'm doing," I said.

She stopped packing.

That got her attention more than any declaration could have.

"With what?"

"After."

Northbank.

Halden.

Red Quay.

Hearthline maybe eventually.

A job that did not exist yet because I was becoming useful in a shape nobody had named cleanly.

Messier.

"It did have opinions."

"Greg."

"I like the work."

She smiled a little.

"I know."

"I like them."

More difficult.

Mara waited.

"Red Quay."

"I know who you mean."

"They're weird."

"You say that like you're not carrying a dead monster in your leg."

"Washer."

"Monster leg."

Betrayal traveled fast.

"Who told you?"

"Yes."

No giant promise clock anymore.

Current Greg found it suspicious.

"I know."

Smaller clock.

"Wasteful."

Close enough to become dangerous.

# Candidate Chapter 134: Shape

Staff in hand.

A municipal warder named Pellis held a heat screen across the loading door while six workers dragged crates out beneath it.

Good screen.

Strong.

Too broad.

Load moved toward the surviving post.

I saw it.

Not because of Faultglass exactly.

Not anymore.

Because this was becoming the thing my attention chose first.

"Can you narrow that screen?"

Competently.

Small.

Not enough to hold roof.

Enough to change where it failed.

"Everyone clear left side!"

Fire captain repeated it louder.

People moved.

The truss gave.

My plane rotated the falling end inward instead of out through the doorway.

Timber crashed into burning shelving.

Roof sagged but did not pancake.

Pellis held the heat screen.

Workers got three more crates out.

Then the captain called retreat.

Good.

No one inside.

No Gold team.

Because holding was expensive.

Because stronger was not always better.

Too many words.

"Huh."

Good enough.

Less than Red Quay.

Still money.

Smooth.

A little.
