# R2 Third-Year Pressure Test — Specialization Rehearsal 143–146

Status: **SPECULATIVE RE-PERFORMANCE / ZERO AUTHORITY**

This pass uses selected R2 authority through Chapter 118 plus surviving discoveries from this temporal run. Legacy forward Chapters 119–304 remain quarry only. Useful ingredients may be recycled and re-performed, but their old plot is not inherited.

New author pressure: Greg’s specialization should have **two co-equal halves**.

1. He changes force, geometry, timing, and usable conditions.
2. He changes what people believe they are capable of by nudging them one step sideways from their own self-concept.

The second half is not mind-reading, motivational speeches, or making everyone broadly versatile. Greg should notice when a specialist’s identity has become narrower than their actual capability, then create a condition where they can safely discover another edge. Sometimes he is right. Sometimes they reject him. Sometimes his proposed edge is wrong and the person discovers a different one.

# Candidate Chapter 143: The Form

I signed the field exchange form because Jorren stood there until embarrassment became cheaper than refusal.

Probably.

No tournament banners.

No noble boxes.

“You point with your whole body before words.”

“That sounds inefficient.”

“It is visible.”

Tomel said, “Try doing it on purpose.”

There.

Sideways.

My turn.

I had spent days thinking Greg might become the person who saw what other specialists could become.

Annoying possibility:

Other specialists could see Greg too.

The exchange ended with us in the food lane arguing about whether visible command movement counted as technique.

No answer.

Lysa bought three potato cakes because she had decided the vendor undercharged for salt.

Tomel tried to recruit Harn for a road crew and got refused because Harn had two children and hated travel.

Sera won her spar final while we were eating and left before the prize announcement because she had an afternoon dock shift.

People remained offensively complete.

I liked them better that way.

On the walk home, I stopped at South Yard gate and looked back once.

Three days.

No rank change.

No artifact.

No new party.

One bad spar.

One fifth-place course.

A wardwright considering a wrist frame.

A rigger who had led route.

Me thinking about whether command could be something people saw before they heard.

Enough.

More than enough...
