# R2 Third-Year Pressure Test — 100-Day Story Search

Status: **SPECULATIVE SEARCH MAP / ZERO AUTHORITY**

This is not an outline and not a chapter list. It is a quarry of possible lived episodes beginning after Chapter 118. Kill freely. Recombine freely. Exact dates are soft except where elapsed time matters.

## Search thesis

The run should feel like a fantasy story again before it feels like an argument about Greg.

Give Greg jobs, monsters, ruins, gear, travel, specialists, rivals, loot, meals, bills, bad lodging, people with their own plans, and opportunities to be impressive. Let the new boundary pressure appear mostly through what he is willing to consider.

The important Greg mutation is subtle:

> First-life Greg protected future options. Current Greg has survived enough permanent loss to suspect that preserving every option is overrated.

That belief may be wise in one scene and dangerous in another.

## PHASE 1 — THE CLOCK IS GONE
### D731–D744

### 1. Second Bell Breakfast

**Situation:** Mara finishes the ledger close and actually comes back for breakfast. Halden morning. Cheap place near the east office, too crowded, bad chairs for Greg’s walking leg.

**Greg wants:** the answer about Mara’s offers immediately, while pretending not to press.

**Mara wants:** one meal where her career is not a problem Greg can solve.

**What moves:** They establish that the two-year promise ending did not magically resolve the relationship. Greg has nowhere urgent to be for the first time in years and dislikes the sensation more than expected.

**Pleasure:** mundane fantasy city, food, romance, comedy.

**Killable:** exact restaurant, exact offer details.

### 2. Five Days Means Five Days

**Situation:** Greg starts unconsciously gathering route times and rent figures for all three cities. Mara catches him doing campaign math he promised not to do before she chooses.

**Greg wants:** to be useful without controlling the answer.

**Mara wants:** to make the decision while still wanting Greg in the result.

**What moves:** Greg fails socially in a small, recognizable way. No clean moral lesson. He stops showing her the tables. He does not stop making them.

**Pleasure:** relationship friction, old-man brain comedy.

### 3. Halden Socket Shop

**Situation:** The fitter who adjusted Greg’s leg before First Bell wants to inspect the wear pattern after several days in the city. The shop is half prosthetics, half orthotics, half arguments about leather despite mathematics objecting to three halves.

**Greg wants:** less lateral slip on wet stone.

**Fitter wants:** Greg to stop treating the field peg like a universal answer.

**What moves:** A small redesign gives Greg a city leg that turns better but transmits more impact uphill. Improvement creates another trade rather than erasing disability.

**Pleasure:** mundane capability, gear porn without becoming catalog prose.

### 4. The Lantern Rows

**Situation:** Halden runs a weekly salvage market under hanging ward-lamps. Broken artificer tools, monster parts, failed apprentice work, military leftovers, river salvage.

**Greg wants:** nothing. This immediately becomes false.

**Vendor wants:** to move dead inventory before inspection day.

**What moves:** Greg recognizes three objects from later life. Two are junk exactly as remembered. One is a **setter’s cuff**, an old artificer brace that makes one repeatedly cast structure easier to recall and hold but is notorious for making other casting feel progressively less natural. First-life Greg considered the devices professionally stupid.

He does not buy it.

He asks the price.

**Pleasure:** magical flea market, dangerous item, bargaining.

**Boundary pressure:** first clean “Old Greg would not even have asked.”

### 5. A Normal Contract

**Situation:** A Halden Guild desk recognizes Greg’s Silver file and Brell/bridge credentials. A boring warehouse support job pays well because one wall is moving six millimeters a week.

**Greg wants:** money. Harrow’s first payment is approaching.

**Client wants:** the wall to stop moving without closing the warehouse.

**What moves:** Greg solves it cleanly with feathered support and earns a professional win with no catastrophe attached.

**Pleasure:** competence, getting paid, structural magic used like a trade.

**Important:** Let the bastard simply be good.

### 6. Dinner With No Quest

**Situation:** Greg, Mara, and two of Mara’s Halden coworkers eat after work. One coworker knows Greg only as “the man from the letters.”

**Greg wants:** not to become a story.

**Mara wants:** her lives to overlap without Greg taking over the room.

**What moves:** Greg discovers Mara has jokes, grudges, and work relationships that do not include him. He likes this and is jealous of it in an embarrassing proportion.

**Pleasure:** ordinary life, social mess.

## PHASE 2 — A CLASSIC FANTASY PROBLEM
### D745–D770

### 7. Quarry Bell

**Situation:** A limestone quarry west of Halden breaks through into a buried pre-city structure after a blasting spell opens a hollow. Three workers vanish. Something inside rings once despite no visible bell.

**Greg wants:** initially, nothing to do with it.

**Guild wants:** structural specialists before adventurers start pulling load-bearing artifacts off walls.

**What moves:** Greg takes a short survey contract because the pay is ugly-good and because someone says “buried ward structure.”

**Pleasure:** dungeon/ruin setup, Guild contract, mystery.

### 8. The Red Quay Party

**Situation:** Greg meets the contracted Gold-caliber field party assigned to the deeper ruin. They are not swaggering idiots. They are frightening because they know exactly what not to touch.

Possible composition: veteran shield lead, fast scout, ward-breaker, healer, material specialist. Greg is not secretly better than all of them. His value is structural judgment and old tactical pattern recognition.

**Greg wants:** professional boundaries.

**Party lead wants:** Greg close enough to stop ceilings from killing them.

**What moves:** Greg gets to experience being the specialist inside a stronger team rather than the center of every competence stack.

**Pleasure:** party roles, rank/status mismatch, grown-up adventuring.

### 9. First Descent

**Situation:** Actual ruin crawl. Narrow access, chalk dust, old ward seams, stale water. A colony of stone-feeding crawler things has turned several support ribs into lace.

**Greg wants:** keep the route open.

**Scout wants:** move fast before the creatures retreat deeper.

**What moves:** Greg’s field peg is better than the walking leg here. His missing leg changes route selection and stance, but does not become the emotional subject of the scene.

**Action:** first ugly close-quarters encounter. Greg does not duel like old S-class Greg. He shapes footing, cuts a creature when it commits, and makes the party’s jobs easier.

### 10. Clean Win

**Situation:** A chamber floor starts peeling away under the party after a ward-break. Greg sees the failure earlier than the others because Faultglass permanently changed what kinds of stress patterns he notices.

He throws feathered planes in sequence, redirects the collapse, and gives the party exactly enough safe geometry to cross.

**What moves:** A Gold veteran tells Greg, without flattery, “That was good work.”

Greg enjoys it.

No hidden price. No punishment. No moral correction.

**Pleasure:** dopamine.

### 11. The Bell Room

**Situation:** They reach the source of the ringing. No giant boss. A vertical resonator built into the ruin, cracked and periodically discharging pressure through the stone.

**Greg wants:** understand it.

**Material specialist wants:** salvage before the Guild seals the room.

**What moves:** The room contains several setter’s cuffs built into a larger training/work rig. The flea-market object was not random. It was part of a real historical practice.

Old Greg remembered the cuffs as obsolete shortcuts that narrowed casters. Here he sees what they were originally for: teams doing thousands of identical structural casts under dangerous conditions.

The artifact becomes more complicated than “cursed item.”

### 12. Monster Material

**Situation:** The ruin crawlers have flexible mineral tendon running through their lower joints. The party’s material specialist thinks it could be useful in a prosthetic shock layer.

**Greg wants:** absolutely not to become the client while on a job.

**Everyone else wants:** to make jokes.

**What moves:** Greg takes a small sample to the Halden fitter. This may or may not work. If it works, the improvement should be ordinary and partial.

**Pleasure:** monster loot becomes mundane equipment.

### 13. Rival By Accident

**Situation:** A younger Halden structural mage, **Orren Pell** (placeholder name), arrives from a formal works school to inspect the ruin. He is technically excellent and immediately hates Greg’s feathered-plane geometry because it violates three teaching conventions and works anyway.

**Greg wants:** not a rival.

**Orren wants:** to prove Greg’s technique is situational rather than superior.

**What moves:** They disagree productively. Orren is not evil, just twenty and professionally offended.

**Pleasure:** rival trope, technique comparison.

### 14. Mara Chooses

This should occur only after enough independent Halden life has been shown that the choice belongs to Mara rather than the run needing a home base.

Three viable versions remain alive:

- **Carrow:** she chooses familiarity, relationships, and a role with authority she actually wants. Greg gets what he wanted and still has to adapt because she did not choose it for him.
- **Northbank:** strongest career move, creates distance/travel and forces Greg to test whether “we figure out the cost after” was real.
- **Halden:** she stays, but on different terms, allowing the new adventure engine to grow around a city that is no longer just her temporary posting.

Do not decide this by convenience. Branch later if needed.

## PHASE 3 — OPTIONS FIRST-LIFE GREG DISMISSED
### D771–D800

### 15. Setter’s Cuff Demonstration

**Situation:** The ruin’s surviving cuffs are catalogued. One can still function under supervision. Orren wants to test it because he considers old stories exaggerated. Greg knows they are not exaggerated, but also knows the stories omit why the cuffs were useful.

**Greg wants:** data.

**Orren wants:** proof.

**What moves:** Greg watches someone else use one first. The cuff is excellent at its job. The cost is not immediate agony. It is convenience. A repeated cast becomes easier, faster, cleaner. Other shapes feel slightly less available afterward.

That is more seductive than a cursed scream.

### 16. The Price Is Stupid

**Situation:** Greg returns to the Lantern Rows. The vendor still has the cuff. Price dropped because the Guild report makes resale harder.

First-life Greg would have laughed and walked away.

Current Greg asks whether the seller will take three payments.

Then hates himself because Harrow is already on three payments.

**Mundane pressure:** dangerous magical temptation collides with debt, rent, food, prosthetic maintenance, and basic arithmetic.

He does not buy it yet.

### 17. Party Invitation

**Situation:** Red Quay offers Greg a limited second contract farther from Halden. Better money, more danger, real party slot rather than consultant status.

**Greg wants:** the work.

**Party lead wants:** a structural specialist who does not freeze when geometry becomes violence.

**What moves:** Greg has a conventional progression opportunity that competes with the weird shortcut. This prevents the artifact from becoming the only path forward.

### 18. Daro In Person

**Situation:** Daro arrives in Halden for unrelated work or Greg passes through Carrow between contracts. They finally speak.

**Greg wants:** some version of repair.

**Daro wants:** Greg to stop treating consequences as numbers that become acceptable once priced.

**What moves:** Daro’s anger sharpens the boundary theme without resolving it. He does not say “never take risks.” He says Greg has begun deciding other people can survive the fallout because Greg can.

This should sting because Daro has evidence.

### 19. The Man From Later

**Situation:** On a Guild board or party intake, Greg sees a name he remembers from first life: **Salen Veyr** (placeholder). Later Greg knew him as a brutal expedition captain / raider / betrayer during a much later crisis. Current Salen is younger, poor, competent, and has not done any of those things.

**Greg wants:** to understand whether the future person is already present.

**Salen wants:** work and status.

**What moves:** Greg considers recommending him for the Red Quay contract because he believes he knows the failure path and can change the variables.

**Boundary pressure:** people now enter the same category as dangerous artifacts: “I know the downside. I can manage it.”

### 20. Cheap Inn, Expensive Thoughts

**Situation:** Greg lies awake in an inn after the Salen encounter. Field peg against the wall. Walking leg under the chair. Harrow payment folded in his notebook. Setter’s cuff price written below it.

No action.

This scene earns existence only if Greg’s actual thought is messy rather than thesis delivery.

He remembers first-life Greg refusing the cuff because he had better options.

Current Greg realizes he keeps comparing his current life to a pristine build he no longer has and may never want.

He does not arrive at a principle.

He sleeps badly.

### 21. Festival Ward Trial

**Situation:** Halden holds a civic/festival works demonstration. Orren enters a structural trial. Greg gets dragged in through professional teasing or a side wager.

**Greg wants:** money or to shut Orren up, preferably both.

**Orren wants:** a clean technical comparison.

**What moves:** Greg wins one event and loses another. Formal training beats him where formal training should. His weird adaptive technique beats Orren under an ugly changing-load problem.

**Pleasure:** tournament-lite, crowd, food stalls, rival banter, visible magic.

### 22. A Good Night

**Situation:** After the festival. Greg has money in pocket, food he did not cook, somebody telling the story of his win incorrectly, and no emergency.

Let him like being alive.

This matters if later choices risk the life he has built.

## PHASE 4 — THE FIRST REAL TEST OF THE NEW RULE
### D801–D830

### 23. Second Red Quay Job

**Situation:** A road contract / ruin annex / monster nest. Stronger hazard than the quarry but not apocalypse. Salen may be present in one branch.

**Greg wants:** prove he can hold a real party role consistently.

**Party lead wants:** everybody home.

**What moves:** Greg’s feathered planes are useful but too slow under repeated moving failures. The exact weakness the setter’s cuff would address becomes concrete in action.

### 24. Failure Without Death

**Situation:** Greg misses one support timing. Someone gets hurt, not killed. The injury is survivable and not solely Greg’s fault, but he knows faster repeat casting would have changed the outcome.

**Important:** Do not manufacture blame just to sell the artifact. The party should have multiple causal contributors.

**Boundary pressure:** now the risky tool is not merely greed. Refusing it also has costs.

### 25. The Safe Teacher

**Situation:** Orren or an older Halden works master offers Greg a slower method: months of drills to reduce feathered-plane setup time without narrowing his casting habits.

**Greg wants:** the result sooner.

**Teacher wants:** him to stop confusing urgency with necessity.

**What moves:** The safe path is real and respectable. It is not secretly useless.

### 26. Buy / Borrow / Refuse

This is the first major fork hinge.

Three live forms:

- **BUY:** Greg purchases the setter’s cuff and accepts the possibility that repeated use may permanently bias his casting toward a narrower family of structures.
- **BORROW:** Greg negotiates supervised access for limited field trials, believing he can measure the point of no return.
- **REFUSE:** Greg chooses slower training but keeps working with the people/party risk instead, moving the hubris pressure into human judgment rather than his own magic.

Do not decide by thematic neatness. Rehearse all three.

### 27. Salen’s First Bad Choice

If Salen survives into the run, give him a bad decision that is understandable and smaller than future evil. He hides a salvage find, lies about route knowledge, abandons a low-value objective to protect someone, or takes an ugly side payment.

Greg recognizes a seed from later life.

The interesting question is not “was the future fixed?”

It is whether Greg responds to Salen as a person or as a known failure mode.

### 28. The 100-Day Edge

Close the search on a state, not a mandatory cliffhanger.

Candidate end states:

- Greg has used the cuff enough that one family of structural casts now arrives unnaturally fast and another feels subtly wrong. He is thrilled and unsettled.
- Greg refused the cuff but vouched for Salen, and the human gamble is now the more dangerous experiment.
- Greg rejected both shortcuts, took the party route, and discovered his hubris is showing up elsewhere: he accepts missions other Silver specialists refuse because he prices permanent damage differently.
- A job goes genuinely bad and Greg loses another capability, relationship, object, or status despite making a defensible decision.
- Greg dies. Only if the causal run honestly gets there. Do not bend toward it.

The edge should leave a question worth re-performing near Chapter 119 rather than a pile of predetermined chapters.

# Search Survivors — First Pass

These are the eight ideas currently worth spending more fidelity on.

## S1 — Second Bell / Mara Decision

Why it survives: immediate human continuity from 118. Prevents adventure reset. Establishes that the long promise ended but ordinary life did not.

Likely fidelity: A/B near-edge.

## S2 — Lantern Rows + Setter’s Cuff

Why it survives: recognizable magical-market pleasure and a precise embodiment of Greg’s changing relationship with irreversible downside. Importantly, he only asks the price at first.

Likely fidelity: B.

## S3 — Quarry Ruin + Gold-Caliber Party

Why it survives: gives R2 a normal fantasy machine again. Action, roles, monsters, loot, stronger professionals, structural magic, travel, and danger without needing to turn Greg into the strongest person present.

Likely fidelity: B.

## S4 — Clean Collapse Save

Why it survives: Greg needs uncomplicated wins. Faultglass residue becomes capability rather than another artifact dependency.

Likely fidelity: B.

## S5 — Orren Rival / Festival Trial

Why it survives: classic rival/tournament pleasure at human scale. Lets formal training beat Greg sometimes while his weird adaptive method wins under ugly conditions.

Likely fidelity: C until personality earns promotion.

## S6 — Monster Material -> Prosthetic Upgrade

Why it survives: this is extremely R2. Fantasy loot becomes ordinary-life capability. It keeps the leg integrated without making the leg the chapter thesis.

Likely fidelity: C/B.

## S7 — Salen, the Man From Later

Why it survives: tests the user’s broader point that Greg’s hubris can apply to people. A regression protagonist believing he can alter a known future person is familiar fantasy material with specific Greg danger.

Likely fidelity: C until the new person earns independent character life.

## S8 — Buy / Borrow / Refuse the Cuff

Why it survives: clean three-way future fork. None is obviously stupid. All can generate different kinds of story.

Likely fidelity: fork rehearsal now.

# What to kill unless later evidence revives it

- A giant immediate boss at the quarry. The ruin is more interesting if professionals treat it as dangerous work rather than destiny.
- An artifact that simply converts Greg’s missing leg or old injury into bonus power. Too tailored, too game-like.
- A villain who calls Greg broken and gets humiliated. Cheap.
- Mara conveniently choosing the city the plot needs without a life-based reason.
- Greg deciding “permanent consequences are good actually” in a monologue. The belief should emerge through repeated choices.
- An immediate second body-part loss. Too on-the-nose after today’s workshop. Let the broader risk boundary establish first.
- Every old-life memory being perfectly accurate. Present people and places must be allowed to disagree with Greg’s archive.

# Next action

Rehearse three materially different trajectories from the same S1–S4 foundation:

1. **FORK A — THE CUFF:** Greg eventually uses the setter’s cuff. Test whether the benefit is genuinely worth wanting and whether the cost changes his build without turning into a cheat.
2. **FORK B — THE MAN:** Greg refuses the cuff but chooses to work with / vouch for Salen. Put the hubris into human causality.
3. **FORK C — THE PARTY:** Greg refuses both dramatic gambles for now, takes the conventional Red Quay route, and test whether action/tropes/mundane progression alone generate enough forward energy. Let his risk boundary emerge through mission selection instead.
