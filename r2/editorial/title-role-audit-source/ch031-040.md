Not today.

"Good," I said.

Sella looked at me.

Waiting for more.

I gave her none.

Growth again.

I took one short warehouse inventory shift for the afternoon.

It paid three copper and ended before dinner.

The warehouse had no monsters.

No magical wall.

No structural emergency.

Thirty-two crates of lamp oil.

Fourteen of nails.

Eleven sacks of dried peas.

One crate mislabeled as rope that contained rope.

Disappointing.

I counted everything twice because the foreman paid for accurate numbers, not personal development.

By evening, north-road carts were audible from Lower Rook again.

Different rhythm.

Wood wheels on cold stone.

Harness bells.

Drivers arguing farther away.

Noll heard them too.

He stood at the cookroom window with a bowl in one hand.

"Gate?"

"One lane."

"Good."

That was all he needed.

Not later collapse.

Not structural ambiguity.

One lane meant coal came easier.

Food came easier.

Somebody's cousin could visit without walking the east detour.

Current life had standards history did not.

I went upstairs.

Put the loaf on my shelf.

Still unsliced.

The baker would survive.

So would the gate tonight.

Probably tomorrow too.

I had no evidence against it.

That sentence used to feel like surrender.

It was starting to feel like citizenship...
