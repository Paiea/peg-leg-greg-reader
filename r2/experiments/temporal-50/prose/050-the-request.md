# Chapter 50: The Request

My new room acquired a professional address on a Thursday.

I had not authorized this.

Someone knocked three times while I was shaving badly.

Not Noll.

Noll knocked on walls when doors felt inefficient.

I wiped half my face and opened it.

A boy stood in the hall holding a folded paper and the expression of someone who had climbed too many stairs for another man's career.

"Greg?"

"Usually."

He checked the paper.

"Guild."

Of course.

I took it.

He remained.

"Do I sign?"

"No."

"Then why are you still here?"

"Stairs."

Fair.

I let him breathe.

The note was short.

Sella had learned efficiency from fire.

**ORRIN KESH ASKED FOR YOU. EAST ANNEX BEFORE NOON IF INTERESTED. THREE DAYS NORTH. POSSIBLY FOUR.**

Below that:

**NO, JORREN IS NOT ALSO ASSIGNED.**

I stared at the second line.

The Guild had become hostile through prediction.

The boy leaned against the doorframe.

"Bad?"

"Personal."

"Sella wrote it?"

"Yes."

He nodded.

"Usually personal."

Institutional culture survived location.

I gave him one copper for the stairs.

He looked surprised.

So did I.

"Don't tell Sella."

"Why?"

Good question.

"Professional mystery."

He left.

I finished shaving.

Mostly.

One patch below my left jaw remained old forest under new management.

Current priorities.

Three days north.

Possibly four.

Orrin Kesh.

Medicine cart.

Removable brake handle.

Three road thieves who had learned very little about fever tincture except its security budget.

That job had been ordinary enough to become useful.

Apparently useful had remembered my address.

The cabbage smell had weakened.

The Guild had not.

Same wool hat.

Same scar through one eyebrow.

Different coat.

Better patch at the shoulder.

He saw me.

"You came."

"You requested stairs."

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Sit."

I sat.

No contract board involved.

That was new.

A folded client slip sat between Sella's hands.

"North-road Apothecary Cooperative," she said. "Carrow to Stonecross. One light cart. Medicine, lamp glass, sealed tinctures, two cases instrument glass. Three-day plan if Old Fenn crossing stays open. Four allowed without amendment for road closure or weather."

Stonecross.

The name existed in later Greg's head.

Small market town then.

Larger than Wicker Ford.

Not large enough that I had ever cared about its history.

Good place.

Later memory offered a road.

Current map on Sella's desk offered a different one.

Good start.

"Why me?" I asked.

Orrin answered.

"They told me pick a Bronze I trusted."

There.

Not the cooperative.

Him.

"You trust me?"

"I trust you not to chase a man into trees while my cart rolls away."

Specific reputation.

I liked it.

"High standard."

"Most guards fail differently."

Sella's mouth twitched.

One human emotion.

Orrin continued.

"Also you checked my axle pins before we left last time."

"They were there."

"Exactly."

I looked at the client slip.

"Why not somebody already working north?"

Sella answered this one.

"Could use one. Orrin asked for you. Named requests get preference if availability and rank fit. They don't get ownership."

Good distinction.

"And if I say no?"

"He picks somebody else."

Orrin nodded.

The world would continue.

Excellent contract.

Pay line.

Winter-road premium.

Delay day paid at half escort rate if road authority formally closed a crossing.

That interested me.

"Formal closure matters?"

"Yes," Sella said.

"If Orrin decides the road is bad?"

"Then he owns the commercial delay and the cooperative decides what they pay him. Your escort clock follows the registered road status unless the client authorizes otherwise."

Ugly.

Useful.
