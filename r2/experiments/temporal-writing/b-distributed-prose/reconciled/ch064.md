# Chapter 64: The Call

Meren Voss requested me by name for a bakery door.

My professional reputation had become extremely specific.

The bakery was two streets south of Market Hall.

Its loading door had an installed ward that helped keep winter drafts from turning the proofing room into a brick.

The ward had started shoving the door closed while carts were passing through.

This was unpopular.

Meren stood beside the brass channel with a clamp tool already in place.

"Your job is not the door," he said.

"Good."

"Your job is the tray rack when I isolate the lower feed."

He pointed.

A wheeled rack full of dough trays sat on a slight slope toward the door.

"It moves?"

"When the floor gets wet."

The baker said, "The floor will get wet."

Of course.

Meren looked at me.

"Objective?"

Neris had trained this into me enough that the answer arrived before cleverness.

"Keep the rack out of the work lane."

"Not hold the door."

"Not hold the door."

"Not fix my ward."

"Tempting."

"Don't."

He killed the lower feed.

The door ward snapped weakly once.

A baker's assistant dragged a wet cloth across the floor at exactly the wrong time.

The rack moved.

"RACK!" I called.

Five-inch plane low against the front wheel.

Direction uphill.

Load modest.

The rack stopped.

Meren worked without looking at me.

That was the part I noticed.

Not praise.

Trust sufficient to stop checking.

"GONE," I called when the assistant chocked the wheel.

Released.

Meren changed the ceramic link.

The door ward settled.

We tested twice.

Second time the rack did not move because somebody had remembered the chock.

Excellent improvement.

The baker paid Meren.

Meren paid me the agreed hand rate.

"Next week?" he asked.

"For what?"

"Nothing scheduled."

"Then maybe."

He nodded.

Harl's answer spreading through professions.

I left with flour on one sleeve and money in my pocket.

Not bigger magic.

Cleaner work...
