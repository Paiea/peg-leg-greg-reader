Correctly.

# Chapter 99: The Observer

Meral told me after the job that she had been evaluating me.

This was rude.

The job itself gave no warning.

Two-day ridge relay inspection.

Meral leading.

One road technician named Varo Pell, no relation to either Pell I already knew as far as administrative civilization could establish.

Me as Bronze field hand.

No Jorren.

No creature contract.

Mostly walking, carrying, and making sure an old line had not become a dangerous line while winter loosened its grip.

On the second morning a replacement post shifted while Varo was tensioning the upper wire.

"RIGHT!" he called.

Not my word.

His.

I formed small at the base where the post wanted to kick.

Five inches.

Plane angled with the soil line.

Enough to change the first movement.

"HOLD."

I held.

Meral reset the brace stake.

Varo changed the line.

"GONE."

I released.

No one congratulated me.

We continued.

At camp a feral dog pack came close enough that I drew my sword.

They decided three armed adults and one cooking pot were not worth it.

I sheathed without combat.

Current professional range included animals choosing correctly.

The closeout happened at East Annex the following afternoon.

Meral signed the field sheet.

Then kept the pen.

"Your judgment is better."

I waited.

"That's suspicious."

"Sella asked whether I'd sign a readiness note if I saw enough."

There.

Rank pressure arrived from a conversation I had not been part of.

"Silver?"

"Application readiness. Not promotion."

Important distinction.

"Did you sign?"

"Yes."

For one breath I felt very nineteen.

Not old Greg reviewing a familiar ladder.

Current Greg being told another professional had changed her opinion of him.

Unpleasantly good feeling.

"Why?"

Meral looked annoyed by the question.

"Because you stopped trying to own every problem. Because your sword does not frighten me anymore. Because people can understand your support calls. Because when you don't know, you have started saying so before touching things."

Specific.

Better than praise.

"Means I won't object if you do."

She handed me the closeout copy.

"Good."

"One opinion is dangerous."

"There he is."

I was paid for the relay job.

The readiness note paid nothing.

It sat somewhere in Guild paperwork anyway.

That evening I ate alone because Noll was working and Jorren was gone on a cart run.

Belonging had calendars.

So did rank...

# Chapter 100: The Applicant

Sella put the Silver application sheet in front of me and did not tell me to sign it.

Good institution.

The Guild intake desk had moved back into the repaired east offices three days earlier.

Not all operations.

Not the archive.

Not every room.

Enough.

Dark slate covered most of the west roof now.

The temporary vegetable-hall era was ending in pieces.

"Meral signed readiness," Sella said.

"She told me."

"Neris will sign current support competency if asked."

I looked at her.

"She volunteered?"

"No. I asked whether she would."

There went destiny.

Good.

"Why?"

Sella tapped the sheet.

"Because this is my job."

Also good.

I read the requirements.

Observed field assessment.

Record review.

Two current Silver endorsements or one Gold.

Fee.

Of course fee.

"How much?"

She pointed.

I winced.

Progress remained monetized.

"What changes if I pass?"

"Contract eligibility. Rate floor on some work. Permission to lead certain Bronze assignments. Higher responsibility expectation."

"What changes if I fail?"

"You remain Bronze."

"Publicly embarrassing?"

"Only if you make it public."

Excellent.

I looked at the blank signature line.

Later young Greg had reached Silver as a warrior.

That fact had lived in my head for months like a marker beside a road.

I had treated it carefully because markers were not destinations.

Current body was stronger than when I woke.

Sword work cleaner.

Still not old Greg.

Support stranger and smaller than old memory wanted, but operational in ways it had not been early.

Work history real.

People trusted me for specific things.

Not everything.

Specific things.

Meral trusted my field judgment enough to sign.

Neris apparently trusted my support work enough not to refuse.

Jorren trusted me enough to request me for longer work and enough not to request me for everything.

That last one might have mattered most.

"Can I take this?" I asked.

Sella nodded.

"It isn't sacred."

"Paper in this building has had a difficult year."

She stared.

"Take it."

I folded the application.

Put it inside my coat.

Did not sign.

Outside, a roofer was removing one of the last scaffold braces from the west side.

Dark roof above him.

Not because history had arrived.

Because he had finished his part of the job.

I walked home with a Silver application in my pocket and no requirement to become anything before dinner...
