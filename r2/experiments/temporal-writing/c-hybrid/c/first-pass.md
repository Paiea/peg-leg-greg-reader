# Chapter 111: The Clerk

Mara came back to Carrow carrying three ledgers and no luggage I was allowed to touch.

"Work," she said before hello.

"I assumed the ledgers were decorative."

"Vey is reviewing the east handoff. Two days. Maybe three."

Then she hugged me.

Order of operations mattered.

Vey had put her in a room above the packet office, which solved several questions nobody needed to ask.

I carried one ledger anyway because she handed it to me.

Heavy.

"What's in this?"

"Arguments."

"By weight?"

"Mostly."

She had changed since Halden without becoming transformed for my benefit.

Better coat.

Ink scar near one thumb.

A habit of checking cart marks while talking.

Current work lived in the body too...

# Chapter 112: The Handoff

Vey's Carrow handoff problem involved four warehouses, two packet schedules, and a bridge toll twenty miles away.

I understood perhaps half.

Mara understood the half that mattered.

She brought me to the east packet yard because one route clerk had claimed North Gate delays were still the main cost.

"Are they?" she asked.

"Sometimes."

"Useful."

"I can lie more confidently if needed."

"Please don't."

We watched a flour cart clear the gate inspection in less than ten minutes.

Mara wrote that down.

Later a ceramic wagon waited forty because its load papers disagreed with the axle count.

She wrote that down too.

No single answer.

No Greg infrastructure oracle.

By lunch Mara had three new questions and fewer conclusions.

I liked watching someone else become less certain professionally...

# Chapter 113: The Dinner

Mara ate at The Brass Spoon because Noll had become impossible to describe without evidence.

He saw her from the kitchen hatch.

"Mara?"

She pointed at him.

"You got taller."

"You left."

Apparently they had known each other enough for this.

Young Greg's life remained communal property.

Noll sent dumplings we had not ordered.

Vessa charged us anyway.

Good restaurant.

Mara told me Vey might move the east handoff north of Carrow.

"Would that move you?"

"For some weeks. Maybe."

No promise.

I told her about regional road work and a ferry profession later history erased.

She listened more carefully to the ferry economics than the magic fish.

Mara remained Mara...

# Chapter 114: The Guest

Mara came to Lower Rook after dinner and left before midnight.

That sentence required less bravery than it once would have.

She inspected the larger room.

Two windows.

Sword rack.

More than two bowls now.

"You bought bowls."

"Travel changed me."

"Heroic."

We played one hand with Tavin's deck.

Burned seven rule remained disputed by correspondence.

Mara remembered the burn itself.

"You were losing."

"Tavin said that."

"Tavin was right."

Conspiracy expanded.

When the hallway clock struck late bell, Mara stood.

Vey had paid for a room.

She used it.

Relationship movement did not require ignoring logistics...

# Chapter 115: The Route

Vey moved the east packet handoff north.

Not because Mara decided it.

She helped count why.

The new site cut one wagon transfer and added a longer local cart leg for Carrow merchants.

Some people won.

Some complained.

Mara left on the first trial packet.

"Back west?" I asked.

"Eventually. North first."

Her life kept acquiring directions.

At the gate she said, "You should see Halden before I stop living there."

Not invitation with date.

Not plea.

Possibility.

"I want to."

Better answer than probably.

She smiled.

Then boarded because packet schedules had opinions...

# Chapter 116: The Buyer

Pell Orst offered to buy my ugly spare brace ring.

"Why?"

"Because three people asked if I had another one."

There.

My attention had become market information.

Not much.

Three people.

Enough.

"How much?"

He offered eleven copper.

I had effectively paid almost nothing for the spare inside the original damaged lot.

Profit.

Future knowledge.

Tempting.

I looked at the ring.

Ugly.

Unrepaired.

Potential parts.

"Twelve."

"Ten."

"You started at eleven."

"Negotiation."

We settled at eleven and a free inspection on the useful ring next month.

An artifact could become money instead of destiny...

# Chapter 117: The Copy

Two weeks later a merchant tried to sell me four old brace rings for thirty silver.

Not copper.

Silver.

I laughed.

He did not.

"Early pattern. Rare."

"Broken."

"Restorable."

"Maybe."

Pell's shop had apparently created a rumor: old three-tab hardware was valuable because a Bronze adventurer had used one successfully.

I had become part of the price.

Dangerous.

I did not buy.

The merchant lowered to twenty-four silver.

I still did not buy.

Future knowledge had become less profitable once the present learned I wanted something...

# Chapter 118: The Marsh

Edda took me west of the river flats for a reed-cutting escort because spring water had pushed marshbeasts closer to the work camps.

Not monsters worth songs.

Long-legged gray things that disliked noise and stole salt fish.

Locals called them stilts.

Old Greg had another name.

Local name won.

A cutter named Jessa knew where they nested.

Edda knew how to keep workers together.

I knew one stilt shoulder twitch meant charge rather than curiosity.

We used all three kinds of knowledge.

Nothing attacked.

The cutters finished before rain...

# Chapter 119: The Reedline

Marsh magic looked like bad weather until Jessa told me where to stand.

A pale line moved through the reeds against the wind.

"Don't cross when it's narrow," she said.

"Why?"

"Makes your legs heavy."

No school name.

No explanation.

We waited until the line widened and broke apart.

Then crossed.

I wanted mechanics.

Jessa wanted to finish cutting before rain.

Her priority won.

Later I wrote **REEDLINE — LOCAL TIMING MATTERS** in my field book.

Enough for now...

# Chapter 120: The Wage

Pellin's apprentice wage rose because Arlo stopped rechecking every simple chamber body.

Ivena showed me the numbers.

"He earns it."

Arlo complained about the phrase and approved the raise.

Dena asked for another half-day route helper instead of a full second driver.

Different bottleneck.

The venture hired a widow named Sera for two afternoons a week to clean returns and prep route racks.

No empire.

Five people doing more work than three people had done before.

Current growth smelled like wet clay and soap...

# Chapter 121: The Quiet Week

Nothing important happened for six days.

I worked twice.

Practiced sword three times.

Sent one letter west.

Ate at The Brass Spoon once.

Fixed a loose window latch.

Lost two copper to Jorren at cards.

The seventh day felt different only because my body was no longer sore from the marsh.

Time had passed without producing evidence for anyone's thesis.

Useful...

# Chapter 122: The Endorsement

Neris asked whether I intended to stay Bronze forever.

This was not a promotion offer.

It was irritation.

We had finished a paid support block and I had spent fifteen minutes asking how Silver field assessments treated mixed sword/support work.

"I don't know," I said.

"Then stop asking like you do."

Fair.

"Would you endorse me?"

Neris considered.

Not immediate.

"For support competency? Yes. For being sensible? Conditional."

One professional opinion.

No title yet...

# Chapter 123: The Form

The Silver application fee had increased by two copper since the last printed sheet.

This offended me more than the assessment.

Sella pointed at the new rate.

"Administrative reconstruction cost."

"The building burned and I pay more?"

"Everyone pays more."

Fairness through shared suffering.

I took the form home unsigned.

Not fear exactly.

I wanted to know whether I was applying because current work had reached Silver shape or because old memory expected the word eventually.

The distinction deserved one night...

# Chapter 124: The Signature

I signed the Silver application after breakfast.

No revelation arrived overnight.

I slept.

Woke.

Ate eggs.

Checked the form again.

The current evidence remained the same.

Enough field history.

Enough endorsements to be assessed.

Enough money to lose the fee without threatening rent.

Not old Greg.

Not young Greg reconstructed from history.

Current hand.
