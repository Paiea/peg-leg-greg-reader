# Chapter 88: The Returner

The return trip contained less danger than the outbound trip and more wet wool.

I preferred the danger.

Wool held water with a sense of duty.

By the second morning our canvas smelled like an animal had learned bookkeeping.

Jorren walked beside the rear cart, checking tie-downs after every bad rut.

"Frame shifted."

I looked.

One machine frame had moved half a handspan toward the wheel side.

"Stop?"

"Now."

The driver sighed.

We stopped.

No attack appeared while we retied it.

No creature used the tactical opening.

The world allowed maintenance without punishment.

Good.

The river crossing stayed ordinary on the way back.

The ferryman still tested the water before loading us.

No silver lines.

No explanation repeated for my benefit.

He took the fee.

We crossed.

That was all.

By the fifth day the road had become familiar enough that I started recognizing bends before later memory supplied them.

Current memory.

Better kind.

A split oak near a drainage ditch.

A farmhouse with three blue shutters.

An inn where the stew had too much salt and one good bench near the stove.

Things I had learned this week rather than forty years from now.

The distinction mattered less until I noticed it.

Then it mattered too much.

I stopped grading myself and ate the stew.

On the sixth morning one axle pin began walking out of the lead cart.

I saw the bright line where metal had shifted.

"Hold."

The driver stopped before I finished pointing.

He climbed down.

"Damn."

Orrin Kesh had taught me to look at axle pins because he was a driver and liked arriving alive.

No future memory required.

Current work had become useful memory too.

We reset the pin, wired the retainer, and continued.

Jorren saw me smiling.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Bad face for nothing."

"I learned that here."

He looked at the axle.

"Congratulations on the pin."

"Thank you."

Friendship supported achievement accurately.

Carrow appeared after midday.

Not dramatically.

First smoke.

Then outer fields.

Then the north road widening into carts and people who had somewhere specific to be.

North Gate remained open under the same restrictions.

One lane.

Load check.

Work crews still inspecting stone.

Current repair had survived six more days without asking history for permission.

At East Annex the caravan closeout took longer than the entire final mile.

One damaged ceramic fitting.

One ferry delay.

One return machine-frame retie.

No cargo loss.

No injury.

Six contracted days plus one recognized delay adjustment.

Sella read the client note.

"You were gone seven."

"River had fish."

She looked at me.

"I am not putting that in the ledger."

"Spawning lightfin altered ferry conditions."

She wrote that.

Civilization.

Jorren signed first because he had accepted the job first.

That still mattered.

We were paid.

No bonus for learning about fish.

Correct.

Outside, Jorren stretched both arms above his head.

"Bath."

"Food."

"Bath first."

"You have become wealthy."

"I have become offensive."

Both.

He turned toward East Market.

I turned toward Lower Rook.

No discussion about tomorrow.

No automatic next contract.

Seven days together had not made our schedules one thing.

At home my room smelled closed.

Cold ashes.

Dry leather.

A little dust on the shelf.

Two bowls.

Still two.

Noll had left a folded scrap on the table.

**STEW BELOW. DON'T EAT THE POT.**

Useful continuity.

I put my pack down.

Sword rack.

Boots off.

Field clothes separated before they contaminated everything else.

Then I sat on the bed and did nothing for almost a minute.

Current body knew seven days of cart vibration now.

Hips tired.

Shoulders tired.

No wound.

Good tired.

I had gone farther north-east than Current Greg had ever gone.

Then I had come home.

Both parts counted...
