# Chapter 89: The Roofer

The Guild roof had started becoming the roof I remembered while I was gone.

Not all at once.

That would have been cleaner.

The west side still wore scaffolding and tar cloth, but one repaired rectangle above the old archive rooms had been covered in dark slate.

I stopped across the street with a meat pie in one hand.

Later memory had given the Guild a dark roof as if buildings were born with architectural decisions already attached.

Current roof required ropes.

Invoices.

Mud.

A slate stack blocking half the alley.

One roofer shouting that the north flashing had been cut wrong.

"Too short!"

Another roofer shouted something about measuring from the wrong lip.

History had omitted this debate.

Sella came through the temporary fence wearing a hard leather cap.

I stared.

"What?" she asked.

"New rank?"

"Roof debris."

"Prestigious."

"You working?"

"Eating."

She looked at the pie.

"Useful."

"I try."

The fire investigation board remained posted beside the temporary entrance.

**CAUSE: UNDETERMINED.**

Not delayed.

Undetermined.

I read the smaller notes.

Likely origin still west roof void.

Flue wall too damaged to establish pre-fire condition.

Old return channel known to have carried load after ignition, not enough evidence that it caused ignition.

No confirmed sabotage.

No convenient culprit created by elapsed time.

Good investigators.

Annoying history.

"Still nothing?" I asked.

Sella followed my eyes.

"Nothing enough."

Better answer.

A roofer above us set another slate.

Tap.

Tap.

Dark square joined dark square.

"When do you move back?"

"Some offices next week if inspection clears. Intake later. Yard sooner."

Partial function.

The institution had learned to exist in pieces.

"Training yard?"

"Ask Lera."

There.

One old D pressure reached current time naturally.

I finished the pie before walking around the block to the yard entrance.

Half the west edge remained fenced for salvage.

One rack had smoke staining.

Practice dummies had been moved under the east awning.

Lera was arguing with a carpenter about whether a repaired fence post could take body weight.

She saw me.

"Back?"

"Apparently."

"How was north-east?"

"Fish interrupted commerce."

"Normal."

Everyone knew everything except me.

"Yard open?"

"Tomorrow. Limited lanes."

"My block?"

"You don't have one booked."

Right.

Again.

I had spent seven days away and expected the world to preserve a little appointment-shaped hole for me.

It had not.

Good.

Lera tested the fence post with both hands.

It moved.

She looked at the carpenter.

He sighed.

Current authority.

I left them to it.

Across the street another slate went down.

Later roof.

Current labor.

The difference was becoming easier to see...
