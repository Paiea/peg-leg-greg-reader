# Experiment C — Cold Adversarial Audit 087–138

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL REHEARSAL EVIDENCE ONLY**

Authority audited: accepted R2 prose on `main`, Chapters **87–138**.

Purpose: challenge a 52-chapter accepted run as a novel, without using A/B/C/D provenance or prior source-win verdicts as a reason to preserve or reject anything.

## Overall verdict

**RUN WIN WITH FORWARD VARIANCE PRESSURE.**

Do not roll back or rewrite this run by quota. The run remains coherent and distinctly R2. The strongest continuing qualities are independent work, material causality, widening geography, current-life memory accumulation, specific relationship language, practical magic, money, and institutions that function without Greg.

The useful challenger is not plot continuity. It is **successful-form convergence**.

## What is strongly working

- Time genuinely passes. Letters, jobs, training absence, travel, business growth, and friendships operate on independent clocks.
- Supporting characters increasingly have professions rather than functions for Greg: Lera can leave for better pay; Noll learns inside a messy kitchen; Mara's route work changes her map; Jorren accumulates road competence independently; Arlo/Ivena/Dena/Pellin have their own business arguments.
- Geography widens through ordinary logistics rather than quest escalation.
- Money remains causal and specific without becoming accounting theatre.
- Magic remains embedded in work and place: shardback processing, moving support, reedline timing, drying cords, lightfin ferry practice.
- Greg's later-life knowledge increasingly behaves as hypothesis, comparison, or missing context rather than free authority.
- Current-life learning now competes with future memory in a productive way: axle pins, road habits, current places and people become memories Greg actually earned this life.
- Relationship scenes with Tavin, Mara, Noll, and Jorren contain some of the run's best breath and specificity because they allow affection, irritation, old asymmetry, jokes, silence, and practical constraints to coexist.

## Forward challenger 1 — repeated rhetorical landing

A noticeable number of chapters resolve through a similar surface grammar:

`material situation → professional correction / boundary recognition → Greg identifies the right-sized lesson → compact thesis landing → ellipsis`

Related recurring language includes forms of:

- `Good.` / `Correct.` / `Better.`
- `Good institution.` / `Good world.` / `Professional...`
- `Current X...`
- `No X. No Y.`
- a final abstract sentence explaining what the scene already demonstrated

These are legitimate Greg voice tools. The issue is frequency, not existence.

**Pressure:** in new A-zone prose, sometimes let the physical action, another person's line, an unresolved feeling, a joke, an interruption, or an ordinary next task end the chapter without Greg naming the principle.

Do not ban fragments or thesis landings. Make them earn themselves again.

## Forward challenger 2 — Greg has become very good at being correctly modest

The run repeatedly rewards Greg for:

- not owning another professional's problem
- listening to the local worker
- saying `I don't know`
- declining to overuse magic
- allowing other systems to function
- correctly recognizing his authority boundary

This is earned progression and should remain true.

But if nearly every ambiguous situation proves Greg right for being appropriately restrained, **restraint itself becomes protagonist perfection**.

**Pressure:** create situations where two legitimate values conflict and there is no cost-free version of good judgment. Examples of pressure classes, not prescribed events:

- client need versus worker safety
- better-paying work versus an existing promise
- Guild responsibility versus friendship availability
- truthful uncertainty versus somebody needing a decision now
- using future knowledge early versus respecting present evidence
- business reserve versus a worthwhile growth opportunity
- helping somebody versus letting them own the consequence
- body limits versus a commitment that genuinely matters

Greg may still choose well. A good choice may still cost money, trust, comfort, time, opportunity, or somebody else's approval.

## Forward challenger 3 — outside professionals are often too uniformly clean

Ressa, Lera, Tavin, Noll's kitchen, Pellin, difficult merchants, and some road people feel especially alive because competence coexists with temperament, irritation, vanity, error, bad habits, or conflicting incentives.

Elsewhere, many outside professionals arrive as extremely sensible domain authorities who efficiently teach the correct boundary.

**Pressure:** preserve professional competence but allow more human variance:

- somebody competent can be impatient, territorial, funny, petty, superstitious, tired, wrong about an adjacent domain, or personally difficult
- two competent people may disagree for real reasons
- a local rule may work without being optimal
- an institution may be functional while still annoying, unfair, or politically constrained

Do not create incompetence merely to generate conflict.

## Forward challenger 4 — compressed one-premise chapters

Many chapters efficiently establish one strong movement and stop. This has been a strength of R2 and protects the long serial from sludge.

Across 52 chapters, however, repeated compression can make the book feel like a sequence of expertly selected demonstrations rather than continuous lived time.

**Pressure:** occasionally let a promising A-zone scene breathe past its first successful premise. Allow:

- a second social beat after the work result
- physical cleanup or travel that changes the emotional reading
- another character to react imperfectly
- a decision to remain unresolved inside the chapter
- small contradictory feelings without resolving them into one lesson

Do not add length by quota.

## Forward challenger 5 — preserve what is NOT converging

Do not "fix" these strengths:

- mundane work as legitimate fantasy content
- no compulsory combat escalation
- money/material reality
- institutions and local professionals owning their domains
- Greg not being causally central to every change
- current geography replacing remembered geography
- supporting people leaving, returning, or changing work independently
- small magical evidence without taxonomy
- clean wins when Greg genuinely earns them

The goal is more **human variance inside the same R2 world**, not messier plotting for its own sake.

## Round 03 instruction

Feed this audit into A/B re-performance as a challenger alongside survivor rehearsal.

Before selecting each A-zone chapter ask:

1. Is this chapter using familiar R2 grammar because the scene wants it, or because the generator knows it works?
2. Does Greg's good judgment have any real competing value here, or is the world arranged to confirm his restraint?
3. Do outside people feel like people with temperaments and incentives, not domain-correctness delivery systems?
4. Does this scene want to stop at the first clean lesson, or does something materially better happen if it breathes one beat longer?
5. Can the ending trust the scene without explaining the principle again?

Default remains **SOURCE WIN**. Variance is diagnostic, not a rewrite quota.
