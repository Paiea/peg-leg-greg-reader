# Chapter 125: The Caller

A warehouse foreman asked for "the Bronze who shouts first."

Sella looked at me.

"Apparently that's you."

"Concerning brand."

The warehouse used a suspended loading gate on a chain counterweight. One lower hinge kicked sideways when the gate reached the last foot of travel.

I did not fix hinges.

A mechanic named Rusk did.

My job was to stand where Rusk told me and keep one predictable path from becoming a worker's rib problem while she tested adjustments.

"Call before the plane," she said.

"Trying to make that a habit."

"Make it faster."

Fair.

First test.

"LEFT."

Plane.

Five inches.

The gate kicked.

Iron hit force, changed path, and slammed harmlessly against an empty timber stop.

Gone.

Rusk adjusted the hinge pin.

Second test needed no plane.

Third test did.

Then the gate tracked clean.

The foreman paid Rusk more than me.

Correct.

On the way out he said, "You're smaller than I expected."

I looked down at myself.

"Six feet is a difficult burden."

"I meant the Barrier."

Professional identity remained narrow enough to be useful...

# Chapter 126: The Other Greg

A client requested me for a river warehouse I had never entered.

"You did the south crane job," Sella said.

"I did not."

She checked the slip.

"Greg. Bronze. Tall."

"Uncomfortably broad description."

The other Greg arrived while we were still arguing.

He was five foot nine.

This made the client's record worse.

He had dark hair, a scar through one eyebrow, and the immediate expression of a man who had been professionally inconvenienced by my existence.

"You Greg?" he asked.

"Unfortunately."

"They sent your brick escort to me last month."

"I didn't know I had one."

"Neither did I."

Sella corrected both records.

The other Greg worked mostly docks and rigging. No Barrier. Better knots than me. Worse height for clerical mythology.

"Last name?" I asked.

"Senn."

"Useful."

"You?"

I told him.

He nodded.

"Try using it."

Excellent advice.

Reputation had developed paperwork errors before becoming impressive...

# Chapter 127: The Key

Noll moved because sleep had become more valuable than Lower Rook.

He told me over breakfast after a Brass Spoon closing shift.

"Room above a baker. East Market."

"More rent?"

"Two copper a week."

"Worth it?"

"Forty minutes less walking after close."

Excellent accounting.

He had already spoken to Maud Pell.

Of course he had.

His life did not require my meeting attendance.

On Sixthday morning he carried two bags, one pot, and a knife roll downstairs.

That was most of it.

I helped with the pot.

Maud took his key.

"You keeping the dining shifts?" she asked.

"If they keep me."

"They'd be stupid not to."

Noll looked at me.

"See? Professional endorsement."

We walked his things east.

That evening I came home and his old door was dark.

The hallway had not changed.

My expectation had.

People happening acquired addresses...

# Chapter 128: The Baker

Noll's new room smelled like bread at dawn and onions after midnight.

"Improvement?" I asked.

"Depends which direction time is moving."

One bed.

One chair.

One narrow shelf.

The Brass Spoon was five minutes away if he cut through the produce alley.

That mattered more than square footage.

I brought two bowls.

He stared at them.

"You own enough bowls to give bowls now."

"Wealth changes men."

"Terrible."

We ate bread from the bakery downstairs because the baker had discovered that Noll worked at the Brass Spoon and was already trying to trade breakfast for kitchen favors.

Local economies formed quickly.

Noll moving out had not ended anything.

It had added a place I could go.

On the walk back to Lower Rook, I caught myself turning toward his old door before remembering.

Tiny error.

Current life required updates too...

# Chapter 129: The West Packet

Mara's letter came from Tern.

I had to ask a packet clerk where Tern was.

This offended my geographical dignity.

West of Halden.

Smaller than Bracken.

Temporary route office.

Mara wrote three lines about a warehouse roof, four about a clerk named Sessi who refused to round figures "for the comfort of men who dislike arithmetic," and one about missing Carrow bread.

No invitation.

No warning she might appear.

Just an address that had not existed in my mental version of her life.

I read it twice.

Then I wrote back about Noll's move, the road wheel, and the existence of another Greg.

I did not ask when she was coming back.

She would tell me when there was an answer...

# Chapter 130: The Long Job

Jorren asked whether I could disappear east for twelve days.

"Disappear?"

"Professionally."

The job was a survey escort.

Two cargo carts carrying markers and tools.

One records clerk.

Two Bronze requested because the route crossed several lightly maintained stretches and one district boundary whose paperwork had apparently become a feud.

Twelve days.

Not three.

I checked my life before answering.

Rent paid ahead enough.

No filtration meeting I had promised.

Noll no longer depended on our hallway for friendship.

No letter required a body in Carrow.

The Guild could survive my absence.

That last one had always been true.

My life could tolerate twelve days away.

That felt larger than the job.

"Yes."

Jorren nodded.

"Good. I already said probably."

"Professional."

"I learn from you."

We left three mornings later...

# Chapter 131: The Map

The records clerk's name was Hessa Pell.

No relation to Maud.

She told us this before either of us asked because apparently the question happened often enough to become defensive infrastructure.

Her map disagreed with my memory everywhere useful.

Current road bent south around a salt meadow.

Later road crossed it directly.

Current village sat beside a mill stream where later maps showed a mile marker and nothing else.

I stopped treating differences as errors before lunch.

Hessa noticed me staring at one fork.

"You know another road?"

"Later one."

"Then later people can walk it."

Good policy.

We followed the road that physically existed...

# Chapter 132: The Drying House

We slept beside a salt store where the rafters dripped on purpose.

This required explanation.

Braided reed cords hung from the ceiling in long loops. Workers ran their hands down them every few hours and squeezed clear water into buckets.

The storage air stayed dry enough that the salt did not cake.

Not a ward like Meren's.

Not any support method I knew.

"What do you call it?" I asked the forewoman.

"Drying."

"The magic."

She looked at me.

"Drying."

Corrected.

One worker replaced a frayed cord while another checked the wall vents.

Magic was part of the building's maintenance, not a lecture waiting for an adventurer.

I helped carry buckets because that required no theory...

# Chapter 133: The Detour

A bridge closure made Hessa ask me which road we should take.

I did not know.

This disappointed nobody except the part of me that still believed age should produce answers automatically.

Jorren knew one detour from grain work.

A local cartwright knew another because she had used it yesterday.

Her route was longer by distance and shorter by mud.

We took hers.

Hessa marked the change.

"Your map?" I asked.

"Old map."

"Updating?"

"Arguing first."

She wrote the cartwright's name beside the route.

Good institution.

Being the oldest person in my head did not make me the local expert...

# Chapter 134: The Weight

My left knee began complaining on day nine.

Not injury.

Accumulation.

Wet boots.

Two long walking days beside the carts.

One bad night on a floor harder than its price justified.

I noticed the ache going downhill before I noticed it on level ground.

Old Greg would have adjusted sooner.

Current Greg had apparently needed the reminder physically.

At camp I rebalanced my pack.

Wrapped the knee.

Handed Jorren a coil of rope.

He took it without comment.

Five minutes later he said, "Old?"

"Ancient."

"Nineteen."

"Cruel arithmetic."

The knee felt better the next morning.

Not healed.

Less loaded.

Competence did not remove the body from the route...

# Chapter 135: The Marker

We found a road marker carrying a name I remembered from a battle monument.

**VEL KES.**

Later, the name belonged to a ridge where several hundred people died.

Here it belonged to a farm lane.

Maybe the same family.

Maybe coincidence.

Maybe later history had eaten several ordinary meanings and left only the famous one.

The farmer beside the marker did not care.

He wanted to know whether the survey would move his drainage ditch.

Hessa explained that it might change maintenance ownership, not the ditch itself.

He asked six better questions.

I said nothing about battles.

Current question won...

# Chapter 136: The Return Road

Twelve days away made Carrow feel smaller for roughly ten minutes.

Then North Gate trapped us behind a wool cart whose papers listed eight bales and visibly contained nine.

Scale corrected.

Hessa took the survey packets to the Guild.

Jorren peeled toward East Market.

I walked toward Lower Rook.

At the second corner I almost turned toward Noll's old door in my head.

Wrong address.

Again.

Current life had changed before I left and still required remembering after I returned.

My room was quiet.

Mine.

I put my pack down and discovered twelve days of road dust had achieved structural intimacy with everything I owned...

# Chapter 137: The Notice

The Guild had sent a note while I was east.

Not urgent.

That was the first line.

I appreciated it.

The rest concerned Silver assessment scheduling.

Apparently I had applied at some point in the projected life leading here.

The assessment would not be a duel.

It would not be a spell demonstration arranged for convenience.

One assessor would review current work history, then observe me during ordinary eligible field work selected from available contracts.

No guarantee Jorren would be present.

No guarantee support magic would matter.

No guarantee sword work would matter.

Assessment through life rather than stagecraft.

I liked the institution more immediately.

The proposed window began in nine days.

I was tired enough from twelve days east that nine sounded excellent...

# Chapter 138: The Window

The assessment packet had three pages.

Two were administrative.

The third was worse because it was useful.

**Candidate is responsible for ordinary contract obligations. Assessment does not replace client authority, site safety, or lead-worker ownership.**

Good.

**Assessor may decline to evaluate a situation that becomes unsuitable for assessment conditions.**

Better.

**Passing requires sufficient evidence. Lack of dramatic incident is not grounds for automatic rescheduling.**

Best.

I could apparently become Silver while nothing exciting happened.

Or fail while nothing exciting happened.

Professional advancement had escaped theatre.

I put the packet beside my field book.

Then spent the afternoon cleaning road grit from my sword belt because the assessment window had not changed the fact that leather cracked if neglected...
