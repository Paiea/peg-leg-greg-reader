# Chapter 139: The Examiner

Kesra Dain came back to Carrow to tell me I had too much experience in the wrong shape.

This was not exactly why she came back.

It was simply what she said after reading my Silver file.

"You again."

"I have been difficult to eliminate."

She ignored that and turned another page.

Months earlier, Kesra had been the Silver examiner who told me I knew things I could not do.

Current evidence had apparently complicated the diagnosis.

"Road work. Warehouse work. Support blocks. Sword notes. Commercial wards. Public works. Creature retrieval."

She looked up.

"This reads like three Bronze members wearing one coat."

"Large coat."

"Still one fee."

Sella made a sound from the intake desk that might have been a laugh and might have been a cough designed by administration.

Kesra kept reading.

She had not become kinder while traveling.

Good.

Maybe.

"Meral says your field judgment improved. Neris says your support is useful within a narrow envelope. Lera says your sword is no longer embarrassing."

"High praise."

"Lera used different language."

I believed that immediately.

Kesra closed the file.

"I am not here to teach you."

"You mentioned that last time."

"You needed the reminder last time."

Still did, apparently.

The assessment window did not contain a hidden obstacle course.

Tomorrow there was an ordinary North Quay freight contract that accepted observation conditions. A warehouse crew would be shifting two iron-bound press frames from river delivery to an upper work floor.

The client owned the lift.

Their rigger owned the rigging.

I would be contracted as a Guild safety hand.

Kesra would watch.

"What are you looking for?" I asked.

"Evidence."

"Specific."

"If I tell you exactly what I want, you will try to become it."

Annoying.

Accurate enough to be more annoying.

She tucked my file under one arm.

"North Quay. First bell. Wear clothes you can ruin."

Then she left with my future in a folder and did not even let me carry it...

# Chapter 140: The Hoist

Marn Vetch hated three things before breakfast.

Loose knots.

People who said **easy lift**.

Guild observers.

I learned all three in under five minutes.

"Examiner stays behind yellow chalk," she told Kesra.

Kesra looked at the chalk line.

"I can see from there."

"Excellent. We agree already."

Marn turned to me.

"You."

"Greg."

"You are the test. I don't care. You're safety hand until I say otherwise."

Better than ceremony.

North Quay Warehouse Four had a two-story lifting bay with an old timber hoist above the river doors. The first press frame came off the delivery cart cleanly.

Heavy iron rectangle.

Four slings.

Two tag lines.

Marn called every movement herself.

The second frame made it six feet.

Then the near sling crept.

Not failure.

Movement.

Enough that the lower corner rolled toward a porter holding the right tag line.

"BACK!" Marn called.

The porter stepped.

His heel caught the raised threshold.

One bad half-step.

His hand stayed on the rope.

The frame kept coming.

"RIGHT CLEAR!"

My call.

Plane under the low corner.

Small.

Moving.

The frame met force, slowed, and changed its swing by less than a handspan.

Marn swore hard enough to qualify as weather.

The porter got his hand free.

"DOWN! DOWN!"

Marn's crew lowered the frame onto blocks.

Gone.

I released.

Nobody moved for one breath.

Then everyone moved at once.

Marn checked the porter first.

No broken fingers.

Scraped heel.

She checked the sling second.

Twisted eye under load.

Then she came to me.

"You changed my swing."

"Yes."

"I was bringing it down."

"He was still tied to the line."

"I saw him."

The porter, sitting on a crate while someone wrapped his heel, said, "I didn't."

Marn looked at him.

"You were supposed to let go."

"I was busy falling."

A fair defense.

Marn rubbed both hands over her face.

"Nobody says easy lift. Ever."

No one had.

Apparently the rule covered thoughts.

We spent forty minutes resetting the sling.

My intervention had prevented one problem and added work to another.

Marn made me help re-chalk the floor because my boot had smeared her safety line.

Kesra watched me sweep yellow dust back into a straight edge and wrote something I could not read...

# Chapter 141: The Complaint

Marn filed a complaint about me and a favorable incident note on the same sheet.

I respected the efficiency.

The complaint said:

**Candidate introduced unplanned force into suspended load path without rigger authorization.**

The incident note said:

**Intervention likely prevented hand contact while porter failed to release tag line during stumble.**

Both were true.

That was inconvenient.

Kesra read the sheet twice.

"Why did you act?"

"His hand was still on the line."

"Marn had called him back."

"He wasn't back."

"Would her lowering call have cleared him?"

"Maybe."

"You like maybe less than you used to."

I did not answer.

"Would you do it again?"

That answer came faster.

"If I saw the same hand in the same place, yes."

Marn was across the room signing freight papers. She heard me anyway.

"Then learn to shout half a second sooner."

Not forgiveness.

Useful.

Kesra marked something in my file.

"Support evidence is sufficient. I still don't have ordinary leadership evidence."

"So another job."

"If one fits."

The next eligible slot was three days later.

It overlapped a Meren Voss ward inspection that would have paid better than any single assessment day cost me.

I asked whether Kesra could move the observation.

"No."

"Could the Guild?"

"Probably. In two or three weeks."

There it was.

Money versus calendar.

Not morality.

I sent Meren a note declining the ward inspection.

Her reply came before evening.

**Annoying. I found someone else. Tell me earlier next time.**

No blessing.

No disaster.

Someone else earned the money.

I put the note beside my assessment packet and disliked how reasonable everyone had been about costing me silver...

# Chapter 142: The Bad Batch

Rain erased my second assessment day before breakfast.

The city drainage contract Kesra had been assigned to observe was postponed because the lower road had become too wet to put workers on it safely.

Nobody replaced it with a convenient test.

I went to the filtration workshop instead.

Pellin had ruined three chambers and was prepared to explain why this was mostly Arlo's fault.

"Blue rack," Pellin said.

"I said the rack under the blue hook," Arlo said.

"There are two blue hooks."

"There were not yesterday."

Dena raised one hand.

"I added the second hook."

Everyone looked at her.

"My route straps needed somewhere to dry."

Ivena put both palms on the ledger.

"Excellent. We have achieved distributed ownership of a stupid problem."

Eight chamber bodies had been moved between service categories before firing.

Five could be retested and relabeled.

Three had collars fitted for the wrong expansion tolerance and had fired slightly out of round.

Not dangerous if caught here.

Useless for sale.

Arlo was furious.

Pellin was embarrassed enough to become defensive.

Dena remained convinced the second hook had been a good idea in every context except this one.

I had opinions.

Nobody needed them first.

Ivena counted the loss.

Fourteen copper including firing space.

Pellin stopped arguing when the number arrived.

"You're not docking his wage," Ivena told Arlo.

"I didn't say I was."

"Your face did."

Arlo's face had several accounting methods.

Pellin lost independent collar fitting for two days instead.

He hated that more.

Then we spent the afternoon replacing colored-hook memory with wooden tags that had actual words burned into them.

Dena kept both blue hooks.

Compromise had limits.

When I left, Pellin was at the wash bench scraping old chalk from fifteen chamber feet and muttering that literacy had become oppressive...

# Chapter 143: The Ditch

The city wanted the road open before the rain returned.

Toma Ree wanted the city to stop calling every ten minutes to ask whether the road was open.

These were related goals.

Toma was the works foreman on my second observed contract.

She had mud to both knees, a voice like broken gravel, and no interest in Silver assessment etiquette.

"I own the site," she told me.

"Yes."

"You coordinate your two Guild hands. If I say out, you get them out. If you say your people are unsafe, I want the reason in one sentence."

"Understood."

My two Bronze were Lent Varo and Pevi Oss.

Pevi recognized me from nothing and disliked the assessment cord Kesra wore immediately.

"We getting paid extra for being part of his test?"

"No," Toma said.

"Then I like it less."

Reasonable.

The work was simple until it was not.

Clear a blocked culvert mouth.

Cut one collapsed brush line.

Mark the upper ditch where thaw water had eaten the bank.

We finished the culvert first.

At the upper ditch, Pevi drove a marker while Lent pulled loose roots.

The bank made a wet sucking sound.

Not collapse.

Warning.

"Stop."

Pevi looked at me.

"We're two posts short."

Water appeared through the soil below his boot.

"Out."

Toma was thirty feet away arguing with a merchant whose cart wanted the road now.

She looked over.

"Why?"

"New seep under the bank."

One sentence.

Toma came closer, saw it, and swore.

The merchant saw only three workers stopping.

"Road opens today," he said.

"Not if that shoulder goes," Toma said.

"You said noon."

"I was young then."

It was twenty minutes before noon.

The city engineer could not arrive until afternoon.

Without the last two markers, the work order would count incomplete.

Pevi knew exactly what that meant.

"Completion bonus is on this sheet."

I knew.

"We're out."

He stared at me.

Then pulled his marker and climbed back.

The merchant called us cowards.

Toma told him he was welcome to stand on the bank personally.

He did not.

Rain started again while we were packing the tools...

# Chapter 144: The Deduction

The bank slipped before the engineer arrived.

Not enough to take the road.

Enough to bury the two marker positions we had left unfinished.

Pevi did not look pleased about being right to leave.

He looked angry about the money.

The city paid base contract rate and withheld the completion bonus because the marked section was incomplete.

Toma signed the sheet herself.

**WORK SUSPENDED FOR ACTIVE SEEP. LATER BANK MOVEMENT CONFIRMED.**

Then, below it:

**BONUS CONDITION NOT MET.**

Both facts fit on one page.

Pevi read them.

"So we lose copper for not getting buried."

Toma looked at him.

"You lose bonus for not finishing the work. You get base pay for the work you did."

"That's stupid."

"Probably. It is also the contract."

He turned to me.

"You're the lead. Argue."

Candidate coordinator, technically.

Close enough for anger.

I looked at the bonus clause.

Completion required all marked stations.

We had not completed them.

"She's right."

Pevi laughed once without humor.

"Of course she is."

He signed his pay receipt hard enough to tear the corner.

Kesra waited until he left.

"You knew the bonus was at risk?"

"Yes."

"Would you pull them out again?"

"Yes."

"You are very certain today."

"There is a hole where Pevi was standing."

Kesra closed the file.

"Leadership evidence."

That was all she gave me.

Outside, Pevi was already halfway down the street.

I considered catching him.

I did not know what I would say that would put the copper back in his hand.

So I let him be angry at me...

# Chapter 145: The Review

Kesra made me leave the Guild for an hour while she reviewed my file.

This was cruel because there was nowhere useful to put an hour containing possible Silver.

I went to The Brass Spoon.

Noll was working.

Of course he was.

He saw me at the back table and sent out stew without asking.

Vessa charged me for it.

Stability.

I ate too fast.

Then slower because that was obviously not helping.

Domm argued with someone in the kitchen about onions.

"They are sweet enough."

"They are vegetables."

"That is not an argument."

A spoon hit a metal pan.

Noll shouted, "Not the wall. Vessa said."

Noll came out for thirty seconds.

"Result?"

"Reviewing."

Friendship.

Waited.

Kesra finished one page.

Then another.

Finally she placed the file flat between us.

My first thought was that I had expected more paper after the sentence.

There was, apparently, plenty...

# Chapter 146: The Cord

"You passed," Kesra said again, because I had failed to become intelligent after the first attempt.

"Right."

"Try enthusiasm later. Registry first."

The assessment result was not immediate field authority.

Sella needed the examiner signature.

Guild registry needed the new grade entered.

My Bronze token needed replacement.

The clerk doing replacement punched the wrong month into the first Silver token.

She stared at it.

I stared at it.

"Can we agree this is not my fault?" I asked.

"Absolutely not."

She threw it into a scrap drawer and made another.

Institutional advancement consumed brass.

The cord itself was plain silver-gray braid.

No glow.

No stat increase.

No old strength returned because someone tied a different color near my shoulder.

Still, when Sella handed it over, my fingers tightened around it.

Nineteen had opinions.

Fifty-nine had too many memories.

Current me stood between them holding string.

Kesra signed the last line.

"Do not make me regret this."

"Personal standard or Guild standard?"

"Personal. The Guild has forms."

She left before I could thank her properly.

Maybe that was intentional.

Jorren was out on road work.

Noll would not finish until late.

Mara was somewhere west of places I knew well enough to imagine badly.

I went home alone.

Set the new cord on the table.

The old Bronze cord beside it looked smaller only because I knew which one I was staring at.

Then the window latch rattled in the wind and I had to get up to fix it...

# Chapter 147: The Board

Silver paid better before it made me stronger.

This became obvious the first morning I read the contract board with a different eligibility column.

One ward-stabilization job paid enough to make me read it twice.

Sustained hold expected.

Two workers.

Minimum reserve declaration above a line I could not honestly reach.

I took the slip to Sella anyway.

"Can the hold be split?"

"Client requested one continuous support hand."

"Why?"

"Cheaper staffing."

Of course.

"Could they take two people at lower duration?"

"They could. They declined."

The number on the rate line remained beautiful.

I put the slip back.

A Silver woman behind me took it three minutes later.

She read the reserve requirement once.

"Fine."

Then it was hers.

I disliked her briefly for possessing a metabolism.

My second option paid less.

Two Bronze field hands under Silver coordination for a warehouse safety re-mark and damaged barrier reset near South Market.

Ordinary work.

Signature responsibility.

I took that one.

Sella did not congratulate me for choosing appropriately.

She stamped the contract and said, "Your people are Pevi Oss and Miko Serr."

I looked at her.

"Pevi?"

"Available."

"Does he know?"

"He can read."

The contract board suddenly seemed less profitable...

# Chapter 149: The Crate

The damaged crate happened after our work was technically finished.

That made it worse.

The warehouse clerk asked us to shift one stack away from the blocked lane so freight could turn around the missing barrier.

Inside contract scope.

Barely.

Pevi took one end of a long crate.

Miko took the other.

I cleared the path.

"Wide around the post," I said.

Pevi looked at the distance.

"Fits."

"Wide."

He went narrow anyway.

The crate corner scraped the brick column.

Small sound.

Everyone heard it.

We set the crate down.

The wood had lost a strip of paint and maybe an eighth inch at one corner.

The clerk reacted as if we had killed a horse.

"Machined fittings."

"Crate is marked external scuff allowed," Pevi said.

It was.

The clerk pointed at me.

"You're lead."

New sentence.

I opened the crate with him.

No visible internal damage.

Packing intact.

The client still wanted a full damage hold against our closeout until the buyer inspected the fittings.

Pevi wanted me to refuse.

The clerk wanted me to sign acceptance.

Miko wanted to disappear into the wall.

**External corner scrape. Packing intact on visual check. Internal condition unverified pending buyer inspection. Guild team does not accept full-loss classification without inspection.**

The clerk read it.

"That delays my claim."

"Yes."

Pevi read it.

"That delays our bonus."

Also yes.

Nobody left happy.

The crate sat between us looking mostly like a crate...

# Chapter 150: The Signature

My first Silver closeout had a dispute attached to it.

Sella read the incident report.

Then the client note.

Then my report again.

"You inspected the contents?"

"Visual only. We did not unload packed fittings."

"Good. Don't write inspected if you mean looked."

She crossed one word in the draft and handed it back.

Pevi waited at the side desk.

Miko waited beside him.

Base pay could clear today.

Completion bonus would wait for the buyer's damage finding.

Miko raised one hand slightly.

"How long?"

Sella said, "Could be tomorrow. Could be a week."

His face changed.

Small.

Enough.

"Rent?" I asked quietly when we stepped outside.

He looked embarrassed.

"Due Sixthday. Base covers it. Bonus was food."

I had enough copper to advance him the bonus personally.

I almost offered.

Then stopped because I would be turning team pay into debt to the new Silver lead.

That felt wrong in a different direction.

Pevi saw me thinking.

"Don't."

I looked at him.

"Wasn't asking you."

"Still don't. He's not your nephew."

Miko said, "I am standing here."

Good point.

We walked to the pay window.

I signed the closeout as responsible Guild lead.

The pen felt exactly like every other pen I had used.

Miko collected base pay.

Pevi collected base pay.

I collected mine.

The bonus line stayed blank.

When we left, Pevi held the door for Miko and let it close in my face.

Not accidentally...

# Chapter 151: The Road Hand

Jorren came back with mud on his coat and a field-lead endorsement in his pocket.

I learned those facts in the wrong order.

He saw the Silver cord first.

"Ugly."

"Thank you."

"Expensive?"

"Emotionally."

"Worse."

We ate at a market stall because Noll was working and neither of us had earned indoor chairs.

Jorren had been north-west on culvert work.

Six-person road crew.

Silver lead took a fever on day three.

The works office kept the job moving under its own foreman, but Jorren handled the two Guild hands and signed their daily field notes until a replacement arrived.

"You signed?"

"Temporary."

"Still signed."

He shrugged.

Then pulled a folded note from his coat.

A Silver road lead had endorsed him for field coordination readiness.

Not application.

Not promotion.

Evidence that had happened without me.

I wanted to tell him to file immediately.

The thought reached my mouth and stopped there.

Jorren noticed.

"Say it."

"No."

"Coward."

"Trying something new."

He chewed bread.

"Was it worth it?"

I looked at my cord.

Then at the ink stain still on my thumb from the disputed crate closeout.

"Ask me after the buyer opens a box."

# Chapter 152: The Narrow Room

Ivena wanted a room.

Arlo wanted a kiln.

Dena wanted everyone to stop putting route racks where she needed to walk.

Pellin wanted more clay shelves.

Sera wanted a door that locked.

Narrow.

Windowless.

Dry.

Previously used by a cobbler who had left enough nail holes in the wall to constitute texture.

Six-week lease trial.

Cheap enough to test.

Expensive enough that Arlo considered it proof of moral decline.

"A kiln makes product," he said.

"A room lets us find product," Dena said.

"Shelves," Pellin added.

"You are not voting," Ivena told him.

"Then shelves without political representation."

I liked him more when he was oppressed by labels.

The ledger supported the room before it supported a kiln.

Not forever.

Now.

Ivena voted yes.

Arlo voted no.

I voted yes.

Arlo stared at me.

"Silver changed you."

"I was irritating before."

"True."

We signed six weeks.

Then discovered the largest return rack was half an inch wider than the new doorway.

Dena measured it twice.

Arlo looked at Ivena.

Ivena looked at me.

Pellin, who had no vote, started laughing so hard he had to sit on the floor...
