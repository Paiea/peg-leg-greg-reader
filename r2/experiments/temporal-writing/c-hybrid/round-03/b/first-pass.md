# Chapter 151: The Empty Slot

My assessment window began with no job.

This was educational in the least efficient way possible.

Kesra had a file.

I had a cord still marked Bronze.

The Guild had three eligible contracts that morning.

One canceled before first bell.

One client refused an observer after discovering what observer meant.

The third needed sustained ward output above my declared reserve.

Kesra looked at the slip.

Then at me.

"No."

"I agree."

"Good. That saves us ten minutes."

The window did not owe me content.

I spent the morning at the Guild anyway because another contract might appear.

None did.

At noon Kesra left to inspect somebody else's problem.

"Tomorrow?" I asked.

"If work exists."

That was apparently the institution's answer to destiny.

I went home with four unused hours and enough irritation to clean my entire field kit badly...

# Chapter 152: The Storage Door

The filtration workshop needed a room before it needed my assessment.

Ivena found one next door.

Six-week trial lease.

Dry.

Narrow.

Cheap.

Arlo objected because every copper not spent on a kiln had become personally offensive.

Dena objected because the doorway was too narrow for the largest return rack.

Pellin objected because nobody had asked him.

"You don't own anything," Ivena said.

"I own feelings."

"Store those somewhere else."

We measured the doorway.

Then the rack.

Half an inch problem.

Arlo became delighted because physical evidence had joined his political party.

Dena said, "Take the wheels off."

We did.

The rack fit.

Arlo's victory lasted forty seconds.

We signed the trial lease...

# Chapter 153: The Carrier

Our first customer outside the normal Carrow route loop wanted six replacement chambers and no interest in how we delivered them.

Dena had interest for everyone.

"I am not spending a whole day north for six chambers."

The customer was a mill outside Brenn Road.

Too far for her normal loop.

Too small for a dedicated trip.

Arlo suggested buying another cart.

Ivena looked at him until he apologized without words.

The solution belonged to a flour carrier named Hesk who already made the route twice a week.

He would take a sealed service crate north for two copper each way if we accepted his timetable and did not ask him to become a filtration employee.

"I don't wait," Hesk said.

"We don't need you to."

"Good. Everyone says that and then discovers emotions."

Dena packed the crate herself.

Ivena wrote a transfer receipt.

Hesk signed with an X and a witness because reading route marks and reading contracts were apparently separate professional achievements.

The chambers left on somebody else's cart.

Arlo watched them go.

"We could own the cart."

"We could own a horse too," Dena said.

"I like horses."

"Exactly."

Hesk drove away before the argument matured...

# Chapter 154: The Crack

One chamber came back cracked.

Not from service.

Transit.

Probably.

The mill clerk said the crate had arrived with one corner soft.

Hesk said the crate had been soft when he received it.

Dena said that was impossible because she packed it.

Everyone became more certain as evidence decreased.

The crack ran from the chamber lip down one side.

Not repairable.

Customer needed a replacement.

Cost mattered less than who would pay.

Hesk crossed both arms.

"You hired freight. Freight moved."

Dena crossed hers.

"Freight broke."

"Prove it."

She could not.

Neither could he prove the opposite.

Ivena ended the argument by replacing the chamber from venture stock and charging the loss to service reserve while she rewrote the carrier handoff procedure.

Dena hated that.

Hesk hated the new corner-inspection line because it took time.

Arlo hated losing a chamber.

I mostly hated that all three were right enough to continue talking.

Next shipment, Hesk made Dena inspect every crate corner in front of him.

She made him initial each one.

They did not become friends...

# Chapter 155: The Closing Lead

Noll became closing lead because Vessa wanted one night off every week.

He described this as betrayal.

"More pay?" I asked.

"One copper a shift."

"Then expensive betrayal."

"I have to count spoons."

There it was.

The Brass Spoon after close looked different.

Chairs upside down.

Floor wet.

Kitchen fire banked low.

Noll stood at the back table with an inventory sheet and the expression of a man being slowly murdered by onions.

"Seven sacks yesterday. Five now."

"Used two?"

"Probably."

"Then solved."

He looked at me.

"Leave."

A kitchen runner had broken one small plate and hidden the pieces under a rag because he thought closing lead meant Noll could fire him.

Noll found them.

Did not fire him.

Did make him sweep twice.

Then forgot to lock the side pantry and had to walk back from the street after we had already left.

I waited outside.

"Silver responsibility," I said when he returned.

"Kitchen."

"Same suffering."

"No. Mine has soup."

Hard to argue...

# Chapter 156: The Rell Ford Letter

Tavin wrote from Rell Ford because freight had moved him there before any of us discussed visiting.

**Venn has a temporary yard contract. Six weeks if the bridge people stop changing dates.**

Below that:

**Rell Ford still smells like wet rope and bad beer. You would hate the road office and like one bakery.**

No invitation.

Then, three lines later:

**If work ever brings you south, I know where the bakery is. This is not an invitation because then you will turn it into planning.**

He knew me too well in a different direction than Mara did.

I wrote back:

**I refuse to be manipulated by bread.**

Then asked for the bakery name...

# Chapter 157: The East Mill Notice

East Mill came back through property law.

The old dry wall had waited months without becoming more mysterious for my convenience.

The city survey office finally obtained access to the neighboring cellar after a lease dispute ended.

Sella handed me the notice.

"Original field hand requested if available."

"Why?"

"You marked the warm section."

"I was not the only one there."

"Jorren is east. Meral is booked. You are here."

Availability remained powerful.

The survey lead was a woman named Issen Tole who disliked adventurers on principle and Guild paperwork on aesthetic grounds.

"You touched anything?" she asked me at the cellar door.

"Months ago? Wall surface."

"Why?"

"I was young."

She stared.

"Still are."

Unfortunate evidence.

We went underground...

# Chapter 158: The Access

The neighboring cellar belonged to a soap merchant who had spent three months refusing entry and one afternoon becoming cooperative after his landlord threatened court.

He remained angry at us personally.

"Nothing gets moved."

Issen looked at stacked soap barrels covering half the north wall.

"Then I inspect the half you did not bury."

"Those barrels are inventory."

"They are also in front of the wall."

"My wall."

"Landlord's wall. Your barrels. City's access order."

Three ownership systems entered one cellar and none were happy.

We inspected what we could.

The dry section continued through the party wall.

Faint warmth.

No glow.

No obvious active line.

I remembered later Carrow having a municipal service tunnel somewhere under this district.

Somewhere was not evidence.

Issen found a thin patch of mineral bloom near the floor and became interested for reasons she refused to explain until she had scraped a sample.

The soap merchant asked whether it was dangerous.

"Maybe."

He disliked that answer enough to finally move two barrels...

# Chapter 159: The Warm Wall

The warm wall did not hide a room.

It hid older masonry.

After the barrels moved, Issen found a vertical seam where newer cellar stone had been built against a broader older arch.

Not an opening we could use.

A shape.

The mineral bloom tested slightly salty.

Issen thought old drainage or heat-return infrastructure.

A city clerk named Renn thought the arch was an abandoned service conduit from a pre-fire district plan.

I remembered a later tunnel farther east.

Everyone disagreed by several yards.

"You remember an opening?" Issen asked.

"Later. East of here. Maybe."

"How much later?"

"Decades."

She snorted.

"Useful like weather next year."

Fair.

We measured the arch.

Then the soap merchant announced we had used our authorized hour and wanted everyone out.

Renn argued the order allowed two.

The merchant produced his own copy.

One hour.

Renn's copy was wrong.

We left.

The wall remained warm behind barrels and paperwork...

# Chapter 160: The Wrong Lead

Issen let me be wrong without being polite about it.

The next access order opened a second cellar east of the mill.

I expected the old masonry to continue there.

It did not.

Newer foundation.

Cold stone.

No seam.

"Your later tunnel?" Issen asked.

"Apparently not here."

"Historic."

Lera would have liked her.

Renn wanted to dig a test pit anyway because the district plan showed a line under the lane.

Issen refused without utility clearance.

They argued for fifteen minutes.

Both knew more than I did.

Neither convinced the other.

The work stopped pending records from the water office.

I had wanted the thread to become visible because I remembered enough to be curious.

The city wanted signatures.

The city won.

Issen handed me the measuring rod.

"Carry this upstairs. Your future can wait."

I carried it...

# Chapter 161: The Road Note

Jorren returned from north-west road work with a note he had not asked for.

A Silver lead had been sick two days.

The works foreman had put Jorren over the Guild hands because he already knew the culvert plan.

He signed daily field records.

No promotion.

No ceremony.

The Silver lead wrote afterward:

**Suitable for observed field coordination if he chooses to pursue it.**

Jorren showed me over beer.

"What do I do with this?"

"Keep it dry."

"Useful."

He waited.

I waited too.

"You're not going to tell me to apply?"

"Do you want to?"

"I asked first."

"Coward."

He smiled.

Then folded the note and put it back in his coat.

"Not yet."

That answer belonged to him.

We changed subjects before either of us could improve it...

# Chapter 162: The Customer North

The mill north of Brenn Road wanted regular filtration service.

Regular changed the math.

One crate had been an experiment.

Six chambers every month meant handoffs, returns, breakage responsibility, and somebody remembering when the flour carrier was late.

Hesk wanted a higher rate.

Dena wanted him to disappear into a ditch.

Ivena wanted a written service schedule.

The customer wanted everything cheaper.

I sat through the meeting because ownership occasionally punished me.

We did not buy a cart.

We did raise the customer route fee.

They complained.

Then accepted because replacing local boiled-cloth filters cost more.

Hesk got one extra copper per round trip and a crate condition line he could point at whenever Dena accused him of crimes.

Dena still accused him of crimes.

The business acquired a route outside Carrow without acquiring a horse...

# Chapter 163: The Silver Board

The Silver board contained better pay and worse signatures.

I learned that after three weeks of reading it as if the numbers were the important part.

Some jobs wanted force I did not have.

Some wanted a lead who could coordinate Bronze hands through ordinary work.

Some wanted someone willing to sign that a site had been left safe enough for the client to resume operations.

That last sentence paid more because it could become expensive later.

Sella saw me reading.

"You wanted Silver."

"I wanted the word. I am discovering the verbs."

"Too late."

I took a two-day warehouse access job with two Bronze and a client mechanic.

Not because it paid the most.

Because the mechanic had already written the work plan and the responsibility boundary was clear.

Sella stamped it.

"Dinner plans?"

"Noll after close tomorrow."

"Then finish on time."

The universe heard her and began preparing paperwork...

# Chapter 164: The Missed Dinner

The warehouse job finished on time.

The closeout did not.

The client mechanic found a cracked anchor plate during final inspection and refused to reopen the access lane.

Correctly.

The warehouse manager wanted it reopened anyway because night freight was already waiting.

Also understandably.

My signature sat on the line that would say the Guild portion was complete.

If I signed before the anchor issue was resolved, the manager would treat the lane as open.

If I refused, two night carts would wait until morning.

Noll's dinner waited somewhere else entirely.

I refused to sign.

The manager called a senior clerk.

The clerk called the Guild.

The Guild called nobody because the Guild office was closing.

We spent two hours writing an interim hold the manager hated and the mechanic accepted.

By the time I reached The Brass Spoon, chairs were already upside down.

Noll was counting spoons.

"You missed food."

"I know."

"I saved some."

Relief arrived too early.

"Then Domm ate it."

"Of course he did."

Noll kept counting.

I stood there waiting for him to say it was fine.

He did not.

"I should have sent word."

"Yes."

"I couldn't leave."

"You could send a runner."

Also yes.

No professional defense fixed the social mistake.

I helped stack the last chairs while he stayed annoyed with me...
