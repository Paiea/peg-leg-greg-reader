# Chapter 163: The West Contract

A road job took me toward Halden without caring that Mara had ever lived there.

City survey subcontract.

Six days west.

Two Bronze hands under me.

One drainage clerk named Ossa who thought Guild people charged too much for walking.

"We do charge while walking," I told her.

"Exactly."

Professional relationship established.

The route passed close enough to Halden that the name appeared on every second mile board.

I did not write Mara before leaving.

# Chapter 164: The Older Bronze

One of my Bronze hands was thirty-eight.

This bothered him more than me.

Darr Venn had twenty years of cart-road work and no interest in Guild advancement.

"You tell me sword things, fine," he said. "You tell me ditch things, I laugh."

"Agreed."

Then we reached a washout and I told him where to place the safety line.

He laughed.

Then moved it two feet because my position was wrong.

Also agreed...

# Chapter 165: The Later Road

The road I remembered did not exist yet.

Current traffic bent around a low orchard and crossed a wooden span over a narrow stream.

Later memory wanted a stone culvert and straight grade.

Ossa wanted to know where current spring runoff pooled.

Darr knew.

I did not.

We spent the afternoon measuring water stains on trees while future infrastructure remained imaginary...

# Chapter 166: The Halden Letter

Mara's reply caught us at a packet stop west of Brenn Road.

She was not in Halden.

Of course.

Vey had moved her farther west again.

**If you are near Halden, eat at Sera's if it still exists. Do not go looking for my old room. Someone else rents it and you will become strange.**

I had not planned to.

Now I was offended by the accuracy.

Ossa read the restaurant name upside down.

"Good?"

"Apparently."

"Then we are going."

The drainage survey acquired dinner plans...

# Chapter 167: The Flood Cut

A night storm erased half our next day's route.

The current detour was twelve miles.

Later memory offered a shorter ridge track.

Darr said the ridge track washed out every spring.

I remembered it paved.

Both facts could be true several decades apart.

Ossa needed a decision because the client expected markers by evening.

We took the long detour.

Arrived late.

Lost the day's completion bonus.

The ridge track collapsed that afternoon anyway.

I was right for the wrong amount of evidence and disliked the feeling...

# Chapter 168: The Toll

The detour crossed a private bridge.

Three copper per loaded cart.

Our survey cart counted loaded because bureaucracy respected tools.

Ossa argued for ten minutes.

The toll keeper enjoyed all ten.

We paid.

Client later rejected reimbursement because the original route had no toll.

My coordination decision had protected the crew and cost the contract six copper plus time.

Ossa wrote **necessary reroute** in the report and still complained about Guild walking rates...

# Chapter 169: The Bellgrass

Near Halden, roadside workers tied little bronze bells into a grass patch before cutting drainage reeds.

I asked why.

Darr said, "Mice."

They were not mice.

Something small under the roots disliked bell vibration and surfaced before scythes reached it.

Palm-sized green-backed burrowers climbed out, hissed, and moved into the next field.

No one named the magic.

The worker in charge cared only that nobody cut one in half.

I helped retie two bells after wind knocked them loose...

# Chapter 170: The Knee Again

My left knee hurt on day five.

Not dramatically.

Enough that Darr noticed me taking downhill steps sideways.

"Give."

"I can carry it."

He did not congratulate me for accepting help.

# Chapter 171: The Edge of Halden

The job ended three miles outside Halden.

We spent one night at the edge inn because the return packet had already left.

Halden's lights sat across the fields.

I did not walk there alone.

I did eat at Sera's.

# Chapter 172: The Forward Address

Mara's next letter came from farther west than the last.

Again.

**Vey may keep me moving through summer. I think I like the work. I do not like every person attached to the work. Those are different facts.**

# Chapter 173: The Return Loss

While I was west, the filtration venture lost nineteen copper on a bad firing sequence.

Not because I was absent.

That distinction mattered to nobody counting money.

Pellin had loaded two shelves too close after Bell Tile shortened their overflow window.

Arlo approved the load.

Three chamber throats warped.

Five more needed retest.

Pellin blamed time pressure.

Arlo blamed Pellin.

Ivena blamed the price they had accepted for the slot.

Everyone had a piece...

# Chapter 174: The Shout

Arlo yelled at Pellin.

Not instruction.

Anger.

Pellin yelled back.

The workshop stopped.

I had seen Arlo angry before.

This was different because he was scared about losing expensive work and found a person nearby to attach the fear to.

Ivena told both of them to leave for an hour.

Arlo said it was his workshop.

She said, "Then go be owner somewhere else."

He left.

So did Pellin.

Nobody resolved anything before lunch...

# Chapter 175: The Six Weeks

The narrow storage room survived its six-week trial.

Barely.

The rack fit after the wheels came off.

Returns stopped blocking the wash bench.

Sera could lock cleaned stock away from wet clay.

The room paid for itself in fewer stupid collisions, which was difficult to place in a ledger and obvious to everyone with elbows.

Arlo still wanted a kiln.

He had not become wrong just because the room worked...

# Chapter 176: The Other Form

Jorren took a Silver application home and did not sign it.

I knew because he told me after asking whether I had spare lamp oil.

"Sella gave me this."

Folded paper.

Same shape mine had once been.

"You want advice?"

"No."

"Excellent."

He borrowed the oil.

The form stayed folded in his coat.

His future went home with him...
