# Chapter 177: The Carrier Slip

The north mill returned the wrong crate.

Not ours.

Close enough to become our problem.

Hesk brought it into the shop with both hands and the expression of a man delivering evidence at trial.

"Look."

Dena looked.

"That's not ours."

"Excellent."

"Why did you take it?"

"Because their clerk pointed at my cart and said filtration."

The crate belonged to a soap maker two streets from the mill.

Same size.

Different iron corners.

No route mark except a chalk circle already half gone.

Ivena shut her eyes.

"We need better handoff marks."

Dena said, "They need better clerks."

Hesk said, "I need to be paid for bringing this back."

All three statements survived.

We spent the morning returning someone else's crate and writing our name on ours in a way rain could not erase.

Nobody bought a cart.

The route acquired a brass tag and one additional copper of Hesk's resentment...

# Chapter 178: The Road Request

Jorren sent a city clerk instead of a letter.

The clerk was small, severe, and carrying enough paper to establish a government.

"Greg?"

"Usually."

"Field Coordinator Jorren Vale requests one Guild Silver support hand for a drainage closeout west of Marker Twenty-Two."

The phrase **Field Coordinator Jorren Vale** took longer to process than it should have.

"He requests me?"

"He requests one Silver. Your name is in the note because he assumes the Guild enjoys efficiency."

"Dangerous assumption."

She did not smile.

"Tomorrow. First bell. City yard three."

I read the scope.

Jorren owned road crew sequencing, materials, traffic holds, and municipal closeout.

The Guild Silver owned magical-support safety and two Bronze hands during a loaded culvert lift.

Neither authority ate the other.

Interesting.

I signed availability.

The clerk took the page.

"He also says bring dry socks."

That sounded more like him...

# Chapter 179: The Coordinator

Jorren had acquired a clipboard.

This was the most alarming part of municipal advancement.

He stood beside a ditch wearing the same old boots, a city field coat, and the expression of a man who had already been asked where the gravel was six times.

"You're late."

"By how much?"

"Two minutes."

"Power changed you."

"Stand over there."

It had.

The work itself was ordinary.

A stone culvert mouth had cracked after spring washout. City crew replaced the lip and reset two heavy capstones. One section of old warded drainage pipe ran close enough that the lift needed a predictable support plane if the capstone shifted.

Jorren owned the road.

City engineer owned the repair design.

I owned my two Bronze and the support call.

Doss Fen, older than my current knees and less impressed by Silver, was one of them.

He looked at Jorren.

"You giving orders now?"

"Only expensive ones."

"Bad habit."

Jorren checked the crew positions.

Then he pointed at me.

"Guild side ready?"

"Ready."

"Road side moving."

The capstone lifted.

For five minutes nobody cared what rank anyone was.

That helped...

# Chapter 180: Under Jorren

The first disagreement came after lunch.

Jorren wanted the west lane reopened before the second capstone reset.

Not because he was reckless.

Because the detour was backing flour carts into the market road.

The city engineer agreed.

I did not.

The old drainage line was carrying a faint charge under the wet shoulder.

Opening traffic meant wagon weight before I knew whether the first reset had shifted it.

"How long?" Jorren asked.

"Twenty minutes."

"You said ten."

"Then the line changed."

He looked at the backed carts.

I looked at the wet shoulder.

Neither disappeared.

"Can you hold one lane?" I asked.

"Not with that turn."

"Then keep it closed."

Jorren's jaw moved once.

He turned to the road crew.

"Hold the closure. Tell market traffic twenty more."

Nobody applauded Silver judgment.

A flour driver called Jorren an incompetent bastard.

Jorren called back, "Get in line."

Twenty-three minutes later the line tested clean.

We opened the road.

Jorren handed me the closeout page.

"You owe me three minutes."

"Invoice the Guild."

"Coward."

Then he signed his line above mine...

# Chapter 181: The Cut

West Cut was supposed to change later.

I was nearly certain.

Nearly had become expensive.

The road office had moved the drainage phase forward after spring washout damage.

Jorren showed me the new cut line because one section crossed close to an old low-charge stone bed.

"You know this area?"

"Later."

He waited.

"Bad answer?"

"Getting worse."

Current stakes ran twenty yards east of the ditch I remembered.

The city engineer explained why.

Land access.

A newer retaining wall.

One farmer who had refused the original easement and apparently possessed the personality required to defeat geometry.

The later ditch in my memory could still happen.

Or not.

Jorren kicked one survey stake deeper with his boot.

"You look haunted."

"Municipal drainage. Terrifying."

"Good. Keeps you humble."

A laborer shouted for Jorren from the next cut.

He left me standing beside a future road that had not decided what it wanted to remember...

# Chapter 182: The Marker

I started marking uncertain memories with a small circle.

Not crossing them out.

Crossing out felt too confident.

Road dates.

One ferry landing.

The west causeway.

A warehouse district boundary I remembered differently.

Circle.

Circle.

Circle.

Noll found the notebook open while eating soup in my room.

"You planning crimes?"

"Poorly."

He pointed at three circles.

"Those targets?"

"Warnings."

"Against what?"

I considered explaining forty years of future memory becoming less useful as the present accumulated consequences.

Then Noll stole half my bread.

The moment improved.

"Against people who steal bread."

"Good system."

He drew a fourth circle around my plate...

# Chapter 183: Rell Ford

Work took me to Rell Ford before friendship did.

A freight-yard brace had twisted under a loaded transfer frame.

The yard wanted Guild support while their mechanic unloaded tension and replaced two iron pins.

Three-day job.

Silver lead.

Two Bronze.

Client authority over freight sequencing.

I accepted before I knew which yard.

Tavin was standing under the gate when we arrived.

He had a clipboard too.

Apparently this disease was spreading.

"Absolutely not," he said.

"Hello."

"You are the Guild Silver?"

"Apparently."

"I specifically requested someone reasonable."

The client mechanic looked between us.

"You know each other?"

"Unfortunately," Tavin said.

Then he hugged me.

Once.

Hard.

Then stepped back and pointed at the freight frame.

"If your people write HOLD across outbound two, I lose half a day."

"If outbound two is unsafe, you lose half a day."

"You haven't looked yet."

"Then why are we arguing?"

"Practice."

Friendship remained intact enough for professional hostility...

# Chapter 184: The Wording

The frame was safe unloaded.

Unsafe under full transfer weight.

Tavin wanted the closeout to say **restricted use**.

I wanted **no loaded transfer until pin replacement and retest**.

The mechanic wanted everyone to stop talking and let her work.

She won for forty minutes.

Afterward Tavin read my draft.

"This blocks the whole lane."

"Only loaded transfer."

"Yard clerk will read the first four words and block the whole lane."

"Then train your clerk."

His face changed.

Small change.

Bad change.

"You don't manage this yard."

"I manage the Guild condition."

"And I manage twelve carts waiting behind your condition."

Both of us had become professionally correct in ways that annoyed the other.

The mechanic leaned against the frame.

"Can I suggest a sentence before the childhood friendship becomes a transport delay?"

"We met as adults," I said.

"Worse."

She rewrote the line:

**LANE OPEN FOR EMPTY PASSAGE. LOADED TRANSFER PROHIBITED UNTIL PIN REPLACEMENT AND RETEST.**

Tavin liked it.

I liked it.

This irritated me.

"Fine," I said.

"Fine," Tavin said.

We signed without forgiving each other for the previous ten minutes...

# Chapter 185: The Morning After

Tavin was still angry at breakfast.

Not deeply.

Professionally.

I was still irritated that he had been right about his clerk.

Also professionally.

We ate fried bread beside the freight office without discussing either fact.

A driver dropped a chain hook in the yard.

Tavin shouted before I turned.

"CLEAR THE SWING."

Four people moved.

He had good yard voice now.

The chain was reset.

Nobody died.

Tavin sat back down.

"You stare more with Silver."

"You shout more with a clipboard."

"I get paid for mine."

"So do I."

"Tragic."

The replacement pins arrived before noon.

We retested under partial weight, then full.

The frame held.

I signed the Guild line.

Tavin signed the yard line.

He did not apologize.

Neither did I.

At departure he threw a wrapped piece of bread into my pack.

"For the road."

"Is this emotional reconciliation?"

"It's stale."

"Better."

We left Rell Ford still capable of disagreement...

# Chapter 186: The Third Route

The third outside customer broke the route ledger without breaking any chambers.

Progress.

North mill used Hesk.

East dye house used Marris.

The new customer was a bathhouse south of Carrow that wanted monthly service and refused both delivery days.

Dena proposed inventing a new day.

Ivena proposed inventing a clerk.

Arlo proposed inventing a larger kiln again.

Pellin proposed murder.

The half-day clerk arrived the following week.

Senn Marr had worked grain tallies and considered our paperwork charmingly feral.

"Why do two carriers use different condition codes?"

Dena said, "Because they're different people."

"Numbers survive personality."

"Unproven."

Senn built one route ledger.

The bathhouse paid a higher service fee for the inconvenient schedule.

We used a southbound linen carrier already making the run.

No horse.

No company cart.

Three outside routes now existed because other people's lives were already moving in useful directions...

# Chapter 187: The Kiln Vote

Arlo called it a vote.

It was not.

Ivena owned enough of the money to make that clear.

He wanted a larger kiln lease for six months.

Pellin wanted another drying rack and one more day of hold discipline.

Dena wanted shelving near the loading door.

Senn wanted everybody to stop buying things until the route ledger survived a full month.

I wanted lunch.

Arlo slapped the last bad-batch numbers onto the table.

"We lost thirty copper because the firing queue was crowded."

Pellin slapped the same page back.

"We lost thirty copper because you pushed wet work forward. Bigger kiln makes bigger bad batches if you keep doing that."

Arlo stood.

Pellin stood too.

Nobody became violent.

The room still changed.

Ivena said, "Sit down or leave. Both of you."

Arlo sat.

Pellin did not.

He walked outside.

The meeting continued without emotional repair.

We approved a second drying rack.

We did not approve the kiln.

Arlo voted against the decision he did not control...

# Chapter 188: The Keys

Noll had his own key ring now.

Not borrowed at close.

His.

Three keys.

Rear door.

Dry store.

Waste cabinet.

The last one amused me until I watched him use it.

A junior cook had been hiding spoiled greens under fresh stock to avoid recording waste.

Noll found them.

The cook was sixteen and nearly cried.

"I thought Domm would dock me."

"He might," Noll said.

"Then why would I write it?"

Noll rubbed both hands over his face.

There was no correct answer that made the boy happier.

"Because if rotten food goes into service, somebody gets sick and all of us lose more than greens. Write the waste. I'll talk to Domm."

"You'll tell him?"

"Yes."

The boy hated that too.

Noll wrote the entry himself.

Later he told me, "Authority is mostly having the key to the cabinet everybody wishes didn't exist."

"Guild has those."

"Of course it does."

He locked the waste cabinet and went back to work...

# Chapter 189: The Offer

The road office offered Jorren another twelve weeks.

More pay.

Same field-coordinator title.

One additional crew.

Authority to approve routine material substitutions inside city spec.

Still no Guild Silver.

Still no city-engineer signature.

He brought the letter to my room and put it beside his unsigned Silver application.

"Thoughts?"

"You want Guild work or road work?"

"Helpful."

"You asked."

He read the road offer again.

"I like the work."

"Then that's information."

"I also like not being owned by the city."

"Also information."

"You became Silver and got worse at advice."

"Costs more now too."

He laughed.

Then stopped.

The city offer expired in three days.

The Guild application expired whenever he got tired of carrying it.

For once there was no clean ladder in front of him.

Just two kinds of work and one person who had to choose...

# Chapter 190: The Unsigned

Jorren took the city extension.

He did not sign the Silver application.

Not rejection.

Not fear.

Not a philosophical statement about institutions.

He wanted twelve more weeks of road work.

"I can apply later," he said.

"Yes."

"You sound disappointed."

"I'm trying not to become the author of your career."

He stared at me.

I stared back.

"That was a strange sentence."

It was.

I tried again.

"I'm trying not to tell you what you should want because I got a cord."

"Better."

He folded the Silver application smaller and tucked it into the back of his field book.

The next morning he reported to city yard three.

A clerk gave him two crew rosters and informed him his handwriting remained unacceptable.

Jorren signed the road-office attendance sheet anyway...
