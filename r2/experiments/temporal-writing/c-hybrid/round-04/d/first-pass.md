# Chapter 201: The Conduit

East Mill acquired a fourth property owner before it acquired an answer.

The old service line ran under a warehouse annex nobody had included on the first parcel map.

Issen found the mistake because the wall thickness changed by six inches where the legal drawing said it should not.

"Wonderful," Renn said.

"You sound happy."

"I enjoy discovering additional people who can say no."

Then asked how much yes paid.

# Chapter 202: The Warm Line

We eventually got three hours beneath the annex.

The old conduit was narrow enough that nobody could enter it.

Good.

It carried no water.

Also good.

One stone seam remained warm while everything around it stayed cold.

Renn wanted a sample.

Issen wanted a permit.

The owner wanted compensation for dust.

I held a small plane over the seam while Renn tapped the wall.

Pressure moved sideways.

Not much.

Enough.

I remembered Kesra saying connection mattered more than force for some work.

That did not make this her magic.

It made it a question...

# Chapter 203: The Rope Moths

The west freight depot wrapped its support ropes in bitterleaf oil.

I learned why when one untreated coil began moving.

Not the rope.

The surface.

Tiny pale moths had nested between fibers and were eating the resin that kept the rope weather-tight.

The depot worker did not care what they were called.

"Oil the coil. Dry rack two days. Burn the sweepings."

"Magical?" I asked.

"Hungry."

Fair distinction.

We treated three coils and replaced one.

No one named a school...

# Chapter 204: The City Hand

More crews.

More signatures.

Better pay.

When I asked whether he still wanted Guild Silver, he said, "Not this month."

That was a complete answer.

The city gave him a second clipboard...

# Chapter 205: The Closure

A city road closure needed Guild support because a charged culvert seam ran under the repair zone.

Jorren owned the closure.

I owned the support condition.

A city engineer owned the repair.

The first driver who arrived at the barricade understood none of this and hated all three of us equally.

"I'm losing market!"

Jorren pointed at the detour board.

"You're losing twelve minutes."

"Who says?"

"Me. I measured it yesterday."

The driver looked at my Silver cord.

"You in charge?"

"Not of the road."

He looked offended by institutional complexity.

We kept the closure...

# Chapter 206: The Old Marker

I remembered a white road marker at the west bend.

It had been there for decades later.

Current road crew removed the older black marker and replaced it with blue stone because the district numbering changed.

Jorren supervised the swap.

I watched a future landmark disappear before it existed.

Maybe the white marker came later.

Maybe not.

I stopped using the word **remembered** as if it automatically meant **will happen**...

# Chapter 207: The Open Ferry

Old Greg remembered the South Reach ferry closing after a late-summer flood.

Current late summer came.

Rain came.

The ferry stayed open.

The new upstream spill channel had taken enough water off the approach to keep the landing usable.

The channel had been cut two years earlier after a road petition I had never remembered because it had not mattered to me then.

Now it mattered.

I stood on the landing while carts crossed behind me.

No disaster failed dramatically.

The ferry simply kept working...

# Chapter 211: The Junior Key

# Chapter 214: The Current Map
