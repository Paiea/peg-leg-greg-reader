# Chapter 172: The Pack

Rain started on the return leg.

Not dramatic rain.

Professional rain.

Enough to make straps swell, boots stay wet, and every hill become slightly more expensive.

By midday my left knee had begun reporting each downhill step individually.

I adjusted the pack twice.

Doss watched once.

At the next stop he took two iron survey stakes out of my load and strapped them to the cart.

"Those are mine."

"No. City owns them."

"I'm carrying them."

"You were."

"Doss."

He kept tying the strap.

"Need you walking at dusk. Don't need you honorable at noon."

I disliked how complete the sentence was.

Mira handed him a third stake.

Traitor.

I kept the measuring rod and one small tool roll.

My knee improved from complaint to muttering.

Two hours later Doss slipped in mud and sat down hard enough to invent a new expression.

Mira laughed first.

I laughed second.

Varin, who had spent four days preserving municipal dignity, laughed longest.

Doss stood with brown water to his thigh.

"Nobody writes that down."

Varin opened his field book.

"Public record."

We reached the inn after dark with wet boots, intact equipment, and one city employee threatening legal action over a puddle...
