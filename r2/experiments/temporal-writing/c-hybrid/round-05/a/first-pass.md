# Chapter 191: The Heating File

The water office found East Mill under heating.

Issen brought the folder to the Guild Annex and dropped it on the table hard enough to move Renn's inkpot.

"Municipal scholarship," she said.

Renn opened the cover.

**EAST MILL AUXILIARY SERVICE. HEAT / WATER / RETURN.**

Three pages had mildew.

One had been eaten along the corner by something with administrative taste.

The plan beneath them was older than the mill's current cellar walls.

A narrow service line crossed under the east foundation, turned beneath what was now a lane, and continued through three parcels that had not existed as separate properties when the plan was drawn.

"Tunnel," Renn said.

Issen pointed at the scale.

"Conduit."

"People can crawl."

"Small people."

"Useful people."

"No."

Renn looked at me.

Silver had apparently made me eligible for bad alliances.

"I prefer not crawling into municipal infrastructure that has spent seventy years being forgotten."

"Coward."

"Alive coward."

Issen tapped three current parcel numbers she had written in the margin.

"Before either of you discovers heroism, we need access permission."

One parcel belonged to East Mill.

One belonged to a cooper's warehouse.

One belonged to a woman named Hara Venn who had already sent the city a written complaint about surveyors leaning ladders against her wall.

Renn closed the folder.

"How long?"

Issen smiled without warmth.

"Property law is not measured in useful units."

The old warm wall had finally produced an answer.

The answer was paperwork...

# Chapter 192: The Three Owners

East Mill said yes.

The cooper said yes if the city paid for any damaged floorboards.

Hara Venn said no before Issen finished introducing herself.

Her house stood over the third parcel line with a narrow dye shop attached to the back.

Hara was sixty, square-shouldered, and wearing blue-stained gloves.

"No holes."

"We are requesting inspection access," Issen said.

"Inspection becomes measuring. Measuring becomes men with hammers. Men with hammers become invoices addressed to people who did not invite them."

Renn leaned toward me.

"I like her."

Hara heard him.

"I do not like you."

"Also fair."

Issen showed the old service plan.

Hara did not care.

Her grandfather had called the mill-side foundation stones bad winter stone and refused to store dye vats against them.

Renn asked why.

"Because he was not stupid."

"Technically useful answer."

"You are exactly why I said no."

I could have told her the exposed East Mill section stayed faintly warm without current water or heat.

That would have been true.

It also would have sounded exactly like the beginning of men with hammers.

Issen negotiated two hours in the dye-shop cellar with no cutting, no sampling, no moved shelving, and Hara present the entire time.

Hara added a copper deposit against broken jars.

The city clerk objected.

Hara closed the door.

The city clerk found the copper...

# Chapter 193: The Warm Joint

Hara's cellar was cold.

One stone joint was not.

Not hot.

Not glowing.

Warm enough that wet cloth dried there first.

Renn tested that twice because he enjoyed irritating property owners professionally.

Hara stood with her arms crossed.

"If that cloth stains my floor, I charge you."

"It's water."

"Water stains everything eventually."

Issen had marked the old conduit line from the plan.

The warm joint sat almost exactly above it.

Almost.

Renn used a wooden mallet on the adjacent masonry while I held a small plane across the exposed seam.

Tap.

Nothing.

Tap.

A faint sideways load pressed along the plane instead of into it.

I shifted the angle.

Tap.

There again.

Not force traveling through open air.

Something in the joint was carrying pressure along connection.

Kesra's tension sink came to mind.

That meant almost nothing.

Kesra had shown me one professional effect months ago. Similar behavior did not make this the same mechanism, any more than two wheels made every cart the same vehicle.

Renn watched my hand.

"You felt something."

"Yes."

"What?"

"Load moving sideways."

"Magic?"

"Probably."

He frowned.

"Useful?"

"Not yet."

Hara snorted.

"Excellent. You people have discovered warm stone."

She checked the clock on the shelf.

"Seventy-three minutes left."

Renn looked offended by time...

His insurer had discovered imagination.

"We are not opening the floor."

"Inspection may reveal rain."

"Tell them."

"I intend to."

Renn wanted to appeal directly to the owner.

Issen told him not to turn a legal delay into a personal one.

He disliked this.

I liked it because I was not the person being delayed.

Three mornings reserved.

Reserved time paid less than active field work.

I disliked the delay more accurately.

# Chapter 195: The Second Look

The cooper's insurer allowed non-invasive access nine days later.

They sent their own surveyor to make sure we obeyed the phrase.

Her name was Pell Ors.

She arrived with a brass measuring rule, polished boots, and the certainty that everyone else had already misunderstood the assignment.

Renn disliked her before she spoke.

This saved time.

The cooper's cellar floor had been rebuilt twice since the old conduit plan.

No exposed warm seam.

No useful cavity.

Pell measured wall thickness.

Issen checked parcel references.

Renn tapped three stones and got told to stop tapping stones.

I held no Barrier because there was nothing to support.

After forty minutes Pell found a six-inch discrepancy between the legal wall line and the actual foundation.

"There," Renn said.

"A discrepancy," Pell said.

"The conduit."

"A discrepancy."

"Do you charge extra per syllable?"

"Only when speaking to excavators."

Renn was not an excavator.

This did not help.

The six inches might be the service line.

Or an old foundation chase.

Or bad surveying seventy years ago.

We left with better measurements and no new opening.

Hara had been right about one thing.

Measuring did become more measuring...

# Chapter 197: The Other Cooling

The first outside batch came back perfect.

Arlo behaved badly about this.

Not cruelly.

Triumphantly.

Pellin ignored him.

The second batch cracked two lower collars.

Pellin behaved badly about that.

Balance restored.

The problem was not drying time.

Both damaged chambers had passed Pellin's hold marks.

The outside pottery yard cooled faster than ours because their kiln room vented straight into an alley.

Arlo blamed the other yard.

The other yard owner blamed our clay thickness.

Pellin blamed Arlo's face.

Ivena asked what changed if we slowed the first two hours of cooling.

The pottery owner said it would cost one copper extra per batch because the shelf stayed occupied longer.

Arlo objected.

Pellin offered to pay the copper personally if Arlo promised silence.

Arlo almost accepted.

Ivena approved the slower cool for batch three.

Nobody's theory survived intact enough to become victory...

# Chapter 198: The Missing Empty

Senn found the missing crate before anyone found the crate.

North mill said Hesk had collected it.

Hesk had a signed receipt saying he had collected six empties.

The shop had five.

Dena accused him of inventing arithmetic.

Hesk produced the receipt and sat down to enjoy himself.

Senn traced the handoff marks.

Six left the mill.

Six reached North Exchange yard.

Five were loaded onto Hesk's return cart.

One had been stacked with flour boxes during a rain shuffle.

"So the receipt is wrong," Dena said.

"The receipt is accurate at the first handoff," Senn said.

"Then useless."

"Incomplete."

"Fancy useless."

Recovery cost six copper because the exchange yard had already moved the crate into bonded storage.

Hesk refused to pay.

Correctly.

The mill refused to pay.

Understandably.

We paid because the crate was ours and six copper was cheaper than spending three days discovering a moral principle.

Senn added a second carrier-count line to the transfer sheet.

Paperwork improved after failing in a new way...

# Chapter 199: The Junior Close

Noll gave Miko the dry-store key for one close.

Miko was the same junior cook who had hidden spoiled greens months ago.

He had stopped hiding waste.

He had not stopped being sixteen.

"You're sure?" I asked.

Noll looked at me.

"No."

That was better than a speech about trust.

Miko counted sacks, checked the lower vent, locked the rear shelf, and forgot the upper vent.

Noll found it before they left.

"Again."

Miko groaned.

"Everything else is done."

"Upper vent."

"It's cold tonight."

"Upper vent."

"Domm leaves it closed sometimes."

Noll's mouth tightened.

"Then Domm can sign your close."

Miko opened the vent.

He was angry enough to do it loudly.

Noll gave him the key again the next night.

"Still sure?" I asked.

"Still no."

Miko heard us.

"I hate both of you."

"Check the vent," Noll said.

The key stayed in Miko's hand...

**The clerk moves ledgers when it rains. I asked why they don't fix the roof. He asked why I don't fix freight. We have reached professional understanding.**

Maybe five.

A route-account dispute had pulled her south after the western work I had last heard about.

No warning.

No meaningful relationship to my own recent west jobs.

I recognized the timber yard from the smell before I recognized the name.

Dry pine.

Lamp oil.

Hot resin.

Old Greg remembered a fire there.

Not a famous one.

Famous to the twelve men who had nearly died, probably.

To me it had been a winter delay and a story someone told badly at the Guild.

Current Greg was there because a warehouse-inspection team wanted one Silver support hand while they checked charged lamp fixtures beside dry stock.

The city inspector was a woman named Eda Marr.

She disliked timber merchants and expressed this as efficiency.

"Oil storage is moving."

The yard owner folded his arms.

"It has been there twenty years."

The post-Guild-fire inspection rules required a masonry separation wall between bulk lamp oil and kiln-dry scrap.

The owner argued about floor space.

Eda gave him thirty days.

Maybe the fire had another cause.

Maybe the wall would be removed.

Maybe memory had simplified the yard.

Eda saw me.

# Chapter 203: The South Reach

The South Reach ferry contract was two days of boring support work.

This made me suspicious only because I had become professionally damaged.

The ferry company was replacing an approach rail and checking two low-charge guide posts before autumn freight increased.

Silver support.

One Bronze.

Local ferry engineer in charge of everything I did not own.

Her name was Tessa Coil.

She had forearms like rope and no patience for Guild people who called the river predictable.

"It isn't," she said before I had said anything.

"I agree."

"Good. Saves time."

The Bronze was a nineteen-year-old named Henn who had never worked a ferry and admitted it immediately.

Tessa liked him more for that.

We replaced the first guide-post collar before noon.

At lunch I looked upstream.

A spill channel cut through the north bank farther than I remembered.

Newer work.

Tessa followed my gaze.

"Two summers back. After the petition."

"Sometimes."

# Chapter 204: The Open Ferry

Rain came that night.

Then more.

By dawn the South Reach was brown and loud enough that conversation required commitment.

Old Greg remembered this ferry closing in late-summer flood.

Not maybe.

I remembered sleeping in a freight shed because the landing had been underwater.

I remembered paying too much for bad stew.

I remembered the ferryman's daughter cheating three adventurers at cards while we waited two days.

Current Tessa walked the north approach at first light.

Water had filled the spill channel.

The main landing was wet.

Not underwater.

She drove one marker deeper, checked the guide posts, and opened passenger crossing on reduced load.

Freight waited until noon.

Then freight crossed too.

I stood beside the rail longer than I needed to.

Henn noticed.

"You hate ferries?"

"Historically."

"That's specific."

A cart rolled aboard behind him.

No closure.

No freight shed.

No bad stew.

Maybe the flood I remembered came another year.

Maybe another storm.

Maybe this channel had changed it.

Maybe the story I remembered as my life had already stopped being a clean comparison.

Tessa shouted for me to stop staring at water and help Henn with the south guide.

I went...
