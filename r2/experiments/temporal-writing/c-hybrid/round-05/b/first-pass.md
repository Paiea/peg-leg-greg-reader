# Chapter 203: The Firing Slot

Arlo found another kiln before he found patience.

The kiln belonged to Hesk Street Pottery and could be rented by firing slot.

Not bought.

Not leased for half a year.

Four batches.

Ivena liked the number four because it was small enough to survive being wrong.

Pellin disliked moving green chambers three streets.

"They crack when you look at them badly."

Arlo said, "Then don't look badly."

Pellin looked at him badly.

Dena wanted transport trays.

Senn wanted the pottery yard to use our batch numbers.

The pottery owner wanted everyone to understand that her cooling shelves were not public furniture.

I wanted the meeting to end before Pellin demonstrated greenware fragility on Arlo.

Ivena signed four firing slots.

"Then we stop," she said.

Arlo opened his mouth.

"Four means four."

He closed it.

Growth had acquired a number it could fail inside...

# Chapter 204: The Fast Cool

The first outside batch came back clean.

Arlo spent two days becoming difficult.

The second cracked two lower collars.

Pellin recovered spiritually.

Both chambers had passed the drying hold.

The difference was cooling.

Hesk Street Pottery vented straight into an alley and cleared shelves faster than our kiln room.

Their owner refused to slow the whole yard for our clay unless we paid for the occupied shelf time.

One copper per batch.

Arlo called that extortion.

The owner called it rent.

Pellin offered to pay the copper himself if Arlo promised to stop explaining kilns to a woman who owned one.

Ivena approved slower cooling for batch three.

Nobody got the argument they wanted...

# Chapter 205: The Third Batch

Batch three cooled slowly and survived.

Then one chamber broke on the way home.

Pellin stood over the pieces in the cart bed.

"Good."

Dena stared at him.

"You understand that is broken."

"The kiln didn't do it."

"Our standards are collapsing."

The transport tray had flexed when one wheel dropped into a street rut.

Not enough to damage fired pottery normally.

Enough for one thin lower collar resting at the wrong angle.

Arlo had been right that more firing capacity helped.

Pellin had been right that moving work created new failure.

The chamber remained broken despite philosophical balance.

We added two cross slats to the tray before batch four...

# Chapter 206: The Missing Empty

The north mill lost a crate between two signatures.

That was the annoying version of losing something.

Hesk had a receipt for six empties leaving the mill.

Senn had a route line showing five arriving at the shop.

Dena looked at Hesk.

"Where is six?"

"If I knew, I would charge it cart fare."

North Exchange had received all six before a rain shuffle moved freight under cover.

Five went onto Hesk's cart.

One went into bonded storage with flour boxes.

Every signature was accurate at the moment someone had made it.

Together they described a crate that had apparently teleported.

Recovery cost six copper.

Senn paid from the route-loss line, then added a second count at transfer yards.

"Does that solve it?" I asked.

"No."

"Excellent."

"It makes the next version more interesting."

I liked him less every week...

# Chapter 207: The Dry Store

Noll gave Miko the dry-store key and stayed in the kitchen.

This was apparently harder than going with him.

Miko was sixteen, capable, defensive, and still recovering from the discovery that inventory could be personal.

He came back eight minutes later.

"Done."

Noll did not move.

"Upper vent?"

Miko's face answered first.

He went back downstairs.

"You knew," I said.

"I suspected."

"Why not tell him before?"

"Because then I close the store with his legs."

That sounded wise until Miko came back furious.

"Domm leaves it shut when it's cold."

Noll rubbed one temple.

"Domm owns the kitchen."

"Then why do I have your key?"

Good question.

Noll looked at me as if I had caused it.

I concentrated on soup.

"Because tonight I'm responsible for close," Noll said. "And I gave you part of it. Open vent. Count salt again. Then we're done."

Miko did both.

He remained angry.

Noll gave him the key again the next week...

# Chapter 208: The Rope Loft

I knew what was eating the rope before the depot worker finished swearing.

That felt good.

Not spiritually.

Professionally.

The support coil had pale dust under it and tiny crescent cuts in the weather resin.

"Rope moths," I said.

The depot worker looked at me.

"Resin chewers."

Same creature.

Different useful name.

Old Greg had seen them destroy bridge line in wet country.

They did not eat hemp first.

They ate treated resin, nested between fibers, then left the exposed rope to weather badly.

"Don't load that coil," I said.

"Wasn't planning to."

The worker, Esa Porr, already had bitterleaf oil warming in a small iron pot.

She brushed three neighboring coils, moved them to the dry rack, and burned the sweepings in a covered pan.

"Two days," she said. "Then check the inner wrap."

"Three if rain stays."

She looked at the roof.

"Two here. Loft runs warm."

Fair.

Old experience had found the problem.

Current local experience knew the building.

Neither required a timeline...

# Chapter 209: The Service Line

East Mill returned because the water office found an old heating file.

Issen called me in because the plan showed a service conduit under three current parcels.

Renn called it a tunnel.

Issen called Renn a liability.

This was familiar enough to be comforting.

The first owner allowed access.

The second wanted an insurer present.

The third, Hara Venn, wanted two copper deposited against broken dye jars and no one touching her shelves.

Renn tried to object to the jar deposit.

Hara raised it to three.

He stopped helping.

Her cellar wall held one faintly warm joint almost above the old line.

Wet cloth dried there first.

That was all we knew before Renn began tapping...

# Chapter 210: The Side Load

I held a small plane against the warm joint while Renn used a wooden mallet two stones over.

Tap.

Nothing.

Tap.

Pressure moved sideways across the plane.

Faint.

Distinct.

Connection carried something the open wall did not.

Kesra's tension sink came to mind.

I did not say the name.

"Again?"

"Yes."

"Can we open it?"

Hara said, "No," from the stairs.

The wall, unconsulted, remained closed.

# Chapter 211: The River Clerk

Mara's letter came from South Vey Bend.

I had never been there.

Neither had the older version of me, as far as I could remember.

Her packet office had a leaking roof and a clerk who moved ledgers between buckets when it rained.

**I asked why they don't repair the roof. He asked why Vey doesn't repair freight. We have achieved diplomacy.**

Three weeks there.

Maybe five.

Temporary route-account work.

No relationship to my west-road contracts.

I marked South Vey Bend on my paper map.

Then checked an atlas and moved my mark west by half a thumb.

Current Mara corrected current geography without being in the room...

# Chapter 212: South Reach

A ferry job put me at South Reach during late-summer rain.

Two days of guide-post work.

Silver support.

One Bronze.

Local engineer named Tessa Coil who disliked anyone describing the river as predictable.

I did not.

This earned me six minutes of peace.

Upstream, a spill channel cut through the north bank farther than I remembered.

"When?" I asked.

"Two summers back," Tessa said. "Road petition finally got funded."

"Flood work?"

"Approach relief. Floods don't read contracts."

The channel looked ordinary.

Mud banks.

Cut reeds.

One timber gauge.

I remembered sleeping in a freight shed here during a late-summer closure years later.

Or this year, from this life's point of view.

Memory supplied the feeling before it supplied anything useful...

# Chapter 213: The Freight Shed

Rain filled the spill channel overnight.

The ferry stayed open.

Reduced load in the morning.

Full freight by afternoon.

I found the old freight shed anyway.

Same building.

Different paint.

Current use: feed storage.

The door was locked.

Old Greg had slept there for two nights while flood water covered the approach.

Bad stew.

Card game.

A ferryman's daughter who had cheated three adventurers and probably me.

The memory was specific enough to hurt.

The current landing remained above water.

Maybe the remembered closure belonged to another summer.

Maybe the spill channel changed it.

Maybe both memories and roads had dates I had never needed to preserve.

Tessa shouted from the landing.

"Silver! If you're done worshipping feed, I need the south post checked."

I stopped worshipping feed...

# Chapter 214: The Useful Memory

A week after South Reach, old Greg saved us fourteen copper.

No prophecy required.

A warehouse loft had a charged cinderwing nest above a dry-goods rack.

The client wanted smoke pots.

I said no before the mechanic finished describing them.

Cinderwings discharged when burning charge reached the wing joints.

Dry warehouse.

Bad combination.

We killed the mana lamps, opened daylight shutters, moved two cloth stacks, and waited for the adults to bleed charge.

Then we used low-charge bait outside.

The mechanic watched them migrate.

"You've done this before."

"Something close."

The nest came down cold.

No fire.

No smoke damage.

No fourteen-copper cleaning surcharge.

Road dates could drift.

Fieldcraft still had teeth...

# Chapter 215: The Wall Order

The city ordered a masonry separation wall at a timber yard I remembered burning later.

I did not tell anyone that.

The inspection rule already existed.

Bulk lamp oil beside kiln-dry scrap was enough.

The owner argued that twenty quiet years proved the arrangement safe.

Inspector Eda Marr said, "Twenty quiet years prove you have been lucky for twenty years."

He disliked her.

She did not require affection.

Thirty days.

Oil moved before the wall went up.

I wrote the yard under WATCH and went home...

# Chapter 216: The Road Letter

Jorren's city letter was mostly complaints about gravel.

Twelve more weeks had not improved his handwriting.

It had improved the size of the problems he was allowed to sign for.

Routine material substitutions.

Crew sequencing.

Two closure plans that still required engineer approval.

He wrote one line about the Guild application.

**Still in the field-book pocket. Stop making that face.**

I had not known letters could see me.

Then:

**West Cut drainage is ahead because the south crew finished early. If your future says otherwise, tell it to file a complaint.**

I put the letter beside the WATCH page.

Not because Jorren had disproved anything.

Because current life kept producing paperwork faster than memory could object...
