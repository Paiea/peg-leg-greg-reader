# Chapter 217: The Laundry Wall

The laundry wall was warm in two places.

Not warm enough to heat the room.

Warm enough to keep frost off a narrow strip of stone.

The laundry worker, Bessa, had been hanging wet aprons there for six winters.

"Dry faster," she said.

Renn looked delighted.

Issen looked worried about what delight might cost.

I held a small plane across the upper joint while Bessa slapped a wet apron against the lower wall.

A faint load moved through the seam.

Not enough to move the plane.

Enough to feel.

"Same as the mill?" Renn asked.

"Similar."

"Same line."

"Maybe."

Bessa wrung another apron.

"Does this change whether I can use the wall?"

Nobody had an answer.

That mattered more to her than the mechanism...

# Chapter 218: The Condition Sheet

A missing filtration crate taught Senn to distrust complete paperwork.

Every signature had been correct.

The crate had still vanished into the wrong bonded stack.

His solution was not more signatures.

It was one condition sheet that traveled with the crate.

Dena hated it because it had boxes.

Hesk hated it because Dena had a box labeled **CARRIER DAMAGE**.

Marris wanted a box labeled **CUSTOMER PACKING ERROR**.

Senn added both.

Then the south linen carrier wanted **WEATHER HOLD**.

The page grew until Ivena took the pen away.

"If the form needs its own clerk, it failed."

Senn looked personally attacked.

They cut it back to six lines.

The first route cycle came back with two lines crossed out, one muddy fingerprint, and no missing crate...

# Chapter 219: The Junior Key

Miko got the dry-store key twice a week.

He still forgot things.

Different things now.

That was probably progress.

One night he counted salt twice and forgot lamp oil.

Noll found it during final close.

"Again."

Miko swore.

"You said I could do this."

"You can."

"Then stop checking."

Noll went quiet.

That was worse than shouting.

"No."

Miko stared at him.

"Then it's not mine."

"It isn't. The key is mine. The close is mine. I'm letting you carry part because you need to learn it before I can stop watching."

Miko threw the chalk onto the shelf.

It broke.

Neither apologized.

They counted lamp oil with two smaller pieces of chalk...

Then the date.

# Chapter 222: The Wrong Season

I found the old freight-shed memory in my notebook and discovered I had never written the season.

Late summer existed in my head because the air in the memory was hot.

That was all.

Hot could mean three months.

The flood closure might still happen.

The timber fire might still happen.

The white road marker might appear next year.

WATCH was not a list of disproven futures.

It was a list of memories that had lost the right to boss current evidence around.

I closed the book and went to Lera's yard before I could make a philosophy out of uncertainty...

# Chapter 227: The Current Bridge

A west-road escort asked whether the old Meren bridge could take a six-ton dye wagon.

I remembered crossing it later with heavier freight.

Later.

The word had become an accusation.

Current inspection plate said five tons until spring repair.

The merchant pointed at my Silver cord.

"Can it take us?"

"Not on current certification."

"Can it physically?"

"Probably."

"Then why are we detouring?"

Because future memory was not a bridge permit.

I did not say that.

"Because five tons is the number we have."

The detour cost four hours and eight copper in road fees.

Nothing broke to vindicate us...

# Chapter 228: The Quarry Road

The detour took us past an abandoned quarry road I did not remember.

The local driver did.

"Used to haul red stone. Closed after the east shelf went soft."

I looked at the cut.

Old drill holes.

New scrub.

A road that had mattered before either of my useful maps cared about it.

The driver pointed ahead.

"Stay left after the birch. Right shoulder lies."

I believed him.

The right shoulder collapsed under an empty handcart ten minutes later.

Not prophecy.

Local knowledge...
