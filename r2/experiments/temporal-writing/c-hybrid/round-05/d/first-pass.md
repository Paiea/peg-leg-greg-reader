# Chapter 227: The Other Foreman

Jorren sent me a note from city yard three.

**NEED SILVER SUPPORT. CULVERT CREW. TWO DAYS. ASK SELLA IF YOU'RE FREE.**

No greeting.

His handwriting had improved enough to become merely hostile.

Sella read the note over my shoulder.

"He can request you now."

"Apparently."

"You sound wounded."

"I remember when he borrowed my floor."

"And now?"

"He borrows Guild Silver."

She stamped the assignment.

The culvert crew belonged to Jorren.

City laborers.

City material.

City sequence.

My authority began where the temporary charged brace crossed under the roadbed.

Jorren pointed at the trench.

"I need that held while we swap the lower beam. I do not need opinions about the drainage pitch."

"Cruel."

"The engineer already gave me those."

I set the plane.

Jorren called the lift.

For two hours I worked under a man who still had an unsigned Silver application in his field book.

Nothing about it felt backward...

# Chapter 228: The Cook's No

Noll refused a supplier while I was eating breakfast.

The man had brought six baskets of greens and a story about the rain.

Noll inspected the bottoms.

Mud.

Bruising.

Two baskets already warming under rot.

"Four."

The supplier stared at him.

"Six were ordered."

"Four are usable."

"Domm always takes six."

"Domm is sleeping."

"Wake him."

Noll looked at the kitchen clock.

"No."

It was not dramatic.

The supplier swore.

Noll paid for four.

Then spent the next hour rearranging lunch because the kitchen now had fewer greens than planned.

Authority turned out to include creating your own problems on purpose...

# Chapter 229: The East Parcel

Issen held two deeds.

Renn held three maps.

The current tenant held a broom.

"Which one of you is paying me for the morning?" she asked.

Issen pointed at Renn.

Renn pointed at the city.

The tenant pointed the broom toward the door.

We left.

Survey work resumed after lunch with a clerk who had actual compensation forms.

The warm line remained under the floor.

Ownership moved more slowly than heat...

"It is not leaking."

"It is wet."

"That is different."

"Not to linen."

Senn checked the route sheet.

We had written outbound condition.

Return timing.

Customer signature.

No return-state rule.

The carrier folded her arms.

"Dry it or find somebody else."

We found somebody else for that week.

Then bought two cheap draining racks for the customer sites.

# Chapter 231: The Old Bridge Song

He knew all six.

"Older than you."

"Insulting."

"Accurate."

He tapped the third pin.

Wrong sound.

We unloaded half the cart before pulling it.

Hairline split.

Current maintenance caught it.

Old Greg's memory of the bridge included no failure there.

It also included no mason singing about a miserable ferryman.

The pin got replaced.

The song continued...

# Chapter 232: The Half Shift

My left knee disliked twelve-hour days before the rest of me admitted they were twelve-hour days.

Nothing injured.

Nothing dramatic.

Stiffness behind the kneecap after long downhill work.

Lera noticed because Lera noticed things that could become expensive.

"Shorten tomorrow."

"I have a Guild job."

"Then shorten this."

She cut the yard block in half.

I objected.

She charged me the full rate.

I objected more accurately.

"You're paying for judgment."

"I preferred sword instruction."

"Then arrive with a knee that can use it."

I left after thirty minutes feeling cheated and better...

# Chapter 234: The Dry Stone

The East Mill line reached a place where the stone stopped being warm.

Abruptly.

One block carried the faint dry heat.

The next did not.

Renn wanted to open the joint.

Issen wanted authorization.

Bessa, the laundry worker, wanted us to stop stacking tools under her clothesline.

A water-office inspector crouched beside the seam and found a narrow mineral stain descending toward an old plugged fitting.

"Could be transfer."

"Mana?" Renn asked.

"Water."

Renn looked disappointed.

The inspector scraped the deposit.

"Water carrying something."

Renn recovered.

Nobody opened the wall.

We marked the fitting and left Bessa her clothesline back...

# Chapter 236: The Missing Fire

A warehouse owner asked me to inspect a lamp brace because his insurer had become irritating.

The brace was fine.

The storage plan was not.

Oil cloth sat beneath a charged fixture because somebody had moved shelving after the last inspection.

I wrote it down.

The owner moved the cloth while complaining about insurers, Guild paperwork, city inspectors, and Silver adventurers who had discovered pens.

Old Greg remembered a fire in this district.

Not this warehouse.

Maybe this block.

Maybe another winter.

Maybe after three rebuilds.

The memory would not sharpen because I wanted it to.

I added no prophecy to the report.

Only:

**MOVE COMBUSTIBLE STORAGE TWO PACES CLEAR OF CHARGED FIXTURES.**

The owner signed and kept complaining...

# Chapter 238: The River Person

# Chapter 239: The Late Cart

A client cart arrived three hours late because a mule had refused a hill.

The mule was correct.

Its shoe had twisted.

The driver had stopped rather than force it.

My Guild job lost the afternoon window.

The client wanted the delay charged to the carrier.

The carrier wanted it charged to animal failure.

The farrier wanted everyone to stop speaking before he finished removing the shoe.

I agreed with the farrier.

Nobody asked me.

We completed half the work and returned the next morning.

The mule got a new shoe.

# Chapter 240: The Road Without Me

Jorren's city crew reopened a washed shoulder while I was three districts away.

I learned about it from the road notice nailed outside the Guild.

**WEST SPUR OPEN. LOAD LIMIT RESTORED.**

His name sat near the bottom beneath the engineer and above two foremen.

I had not helped.

I had not advised.

I had not known the shoulder was closed until it was almost repaired.

The next morning a produce cart used it.

Then a brick wagon.

Then a woman pushing a handcart full of onions.

The world remained distressingly capable of improvement without consulting me...
