# Chapter 218: The Ridge Contract

The contract wanted five days and one Silver.

That was enough to make me suspicious of the noun.

**NORTH RIDGE IRRIGATION DISTRICT — GATE BRACE INSPECTION / TEMPORARY SUPPORT / WATER-TURN CONTINUITY.**

Sella tapped the second line.

"They have engineers."

"Comforting."

"You are not the engineer."

"More comforting."

The district had six hillside reservoirs feeding orchards and two grain terraces north of Carrow.

Old Greg remembered riding through the district later when half the upper channels had been stone-lined.

Current map showed dirt cuts, timber gates, and one maintenance road that disappeared beneath a coffee stain in the Guild copy.

The district engineer was Sava Kern.

She met me at the lower reservoir with mud to both knees and a pencil behind each ear.

"Silver?"

"Greg."

"Good. Silver is paperwork. Greg can carry that."

She handed me a coil of brace cord.

A canal tender named Ollo laughed into his sleeve.

I carried the cord.

The first gate had a charged timber brace that needed unloading while two carpenters replaced the lower shoe.

Sava owned the gate.

Ollo owned the water turn.

The carpenters owned the timber.

I owned a five-inch plane under one ugly corner while everybody else argued above me.

At noon Sava checked the temporary brace and said, "Fine. Tomorrow we do the west gate."

"Anything interesting there?"

"Peaches."

"The gate grows peaches?"

"The farmer does. He thinks that makes him an engineer."

Ollo laughed again.

Five days had begun...

# Chapter 219: The Peach Man

The peach farmer wanted his water forty minutes early.

His name was Berr Olan.

He had excellent peaches and a poor relationship with clocks.

"Upper row is drying."

Ollo checked the water board.

"Your turn starts at eleven."

"It is ten twenty."

"Excellent. You know time."

Berr pointed at his trees.

"Trees don't."

Sava was under the west gate measuring a warped shoe.

I was holding temporary load on the brace because the warped shoe had turned a simple replacement into a longer morning.

Berr looked at my Silver cord.

"Open it enough to wet the upper row."

"I don't own the water turn."

"You own the gate."

"Also no."

He disliked both answers.

"Then why are you here?"

"At the moment? So that piece of timber doesn't move while she measures it."

Sava raised one muddy hand without looking up.

"Correct."

Berr appealed to Ollo again.

Ollo refused again.

At ten fifty-three the carpenter finished shaving the replacement shoe.

At eleven exactly Ollo opened Berr's turn.

Water ran down the peach rows.

Berr gave Ollo two peaches and me none.

"Authority has consequences," Ollo said.

"Apparently."

Sava took one of his peaches without asking.

"Engineering too."

Berr shouted from the upper row that he had counted those.

Sava kept eating...

# Chapter 220: The Clear Weed

The third reservoir had glass growing in the ditch.

That was my first interpretation.

Thin clear sheets clung to the shaded stones below the overflow, catching afternoon light in hard little planes.

I stopped.

Ollo did not.

"Glaze moss," he said.

"Moss?"

"If you want to argue with the botanist, she comes Tuesdays."

He hooked one clear sheet with a wooden fork and peeled it from the stone.

It flexed before cracking.

Faint charge crawled along the broken edge and vanished into the wet fork.

"Don't touch bare."

"I had reached that conclusion."

"Good Silver."

The overflow had been running harder since a spring repair changed the upper spill angle.

Glaze moss liked shallow charged runoff.

Too much built a slick shell over drainage cuts and sent water where the ditch had not agreed to send it.

Ollo and two laborers scraped it every ten days during wet months.

No Guild contract.

No monster bounty.

Ditch maintenance.

"Useful?" I asked.

Ollo shrugged.

"Potter in town buys dry sheets sometimes. Says they grind it into glaze. I say he can buy all of it if he scrapes it himself."

He pointed at the fork in my hand.

I had apparently been promoted into municipal botany.

We scraped until the lower cut ran clean.

Then Sava arrived and asked why her Silver support hand was knee-deep in moss.

"Professional development."

Ollo said, "He volunteered."

I had not.

History remained vulnerable to witnesses...

# Chapter 221: The Maintenance Road

Old Greg knew a road that current Greg could not find.

It should have cut east from reservoir four and rejoined the north freight lane near the stone marker.

I remembered it because a wagon axle had broken there in sleet and I had spent six hours hating a wheel.

Sava's current map showed no road.

Ollo's current boots agreed with the map.

"There is a foot track," he said. "No cart road."

"Ever?"

"Before my time? Ask a ghost."

We were supposed to reach reservoir five before dusk.

The current maintenance route went north around a low ridge.

Forty extra minutes.

I looked east anyway.

Brush.

One old cut in the slope.

Something that could become a road later.

Or could have been a quarry track earlier.

Memory gave me confidence without a date.

That had become a familiar kind of useless.

We went north.

Halfway around the ridge Sava asked why I kept looking back.

"I remember a road there."

"There isn't one."

"Currently."

She stopped walking.

"That sentence sounds expensive."

"It usually is."

"Good. Keep it in your mouth unless somebody is paying."

We reached reservoir five before dark.

The nonexistent road remained nonexistent without taking it personally...

# Chapter 222: The Stable Room

The district lodging was above a stable.

The horses had opinions about sleep.

One kicked a stall wall at irregular intervals all night.

Another breathed directly beneath my floorboards like a large damp bellows.

By morning my left knee had stiffened from two days of hillside work and one mattress designed by a carpenter with unresolved anger.

Nothing injured.

Everything old enough to complain.

Sava noticed me taking the stairs sideways.

"Problem?"

"Stairs."

"They were here yesterday."

"I liked them less today."

She handed my field bag to Ollo.

I reached for it.

Ollo moved it away.

"Engineer said."

"Engineer owns water."

Sava said, "Engineer also owns whether the Silver hand arrives at reservoir six before lunch."

"That feels jurisdictionally ambitious."

"File a complaint."

I carried the lighter brace coil instead.

Nobody discussed accepting help.

Ollo complained that my bag contained too many pencils for a man who wrote so little.

I told him two belonged to Sava.

She checked behind both ears.

One pencil was missing.

Ollo had it.

The morning improved...

# Chapter 223: The Gate That Stayed Shut

Reservoir six did not need me.

The gate shoe was cracked.

That part was true.

The charged brace was old.

Also true.

But the replacement timber had not arrived.

A cart had thrown a wheel three miles south.

Sava looked at the empty road.

Then at the gate.

Then at the water board.

"We close the east turn early."

A grain farmer named Pell Darr objected before Ollo finished carrying the message.

"My lower terrace gets another hour."

"Not today."

"Contract says sunset."

"Gate says no."

"Gate doesn't sign contracts."

"Neither does a flood."

Pell looked at me.

I remained beautifully irrelevant.

The replacement cart did not arrive by noon.

Or two.

At three Sava cancelled the repair.

Ollo shifted the remaining water turns.

Pell lost forty minutes of irrigation.

He wrote a complaint to the district office while sitting on an overturned bucket.

Sava helped him spell the engineer's name correctly at the top.

"You are assisting?" I asked.

"I want them to know exactly who ruined his afternoon."

Pell heard her.

"You did."

"See? Agreement."

We spent the rest of the day securing a gate we had not fixed.

My Silver contract still paid because remaining available had been part of the work.

Pell's barley did not care...

# Chapter 224: The Return Cart

The timber arrived the morning we were leaving.

Of course it did.

Sava extended the district carpenter crew.

She did not extend me.

"You have enough hands now."

"I could stay."

"You could also go home."

This felt suspicious.

Ollo loaded my field bag onto the south cart.

The five-day contract had produced no dramatic repair, one scraped ditch, two angry farmers, and a new dislike of horses beneath bedrooms.

At the lower reservoir Berr Olan was waiting beside the road.

He handed Ollo a sack of peaches.

Then, after consideration, one peach to me.

"You still don't own the gate?"

"Tragically."

"Waste of silver."

He walked away.

Ollo inspected my peach.

"Bruised."

"Authority has consequences."

He laughed hard enough to drop his own.

The cart rolled south.

At the ridge turn I looked east for the road I remembered.

Still brush.

I wrote **ROAD? / NO CURRENT CART ACCESS** in the margin of my current map.

Not WATCH.

A question mark was cheaper...

# Chapter 225: The Wrong Crate

There was a crate of axle grease in the filtration room.

Dena had opened it.

This was how I knew it was axle grease and not a new phase of our business strategy.

"Your friend sent mud fat."

"Grease."

"I know what grease is. Why is it here?"

The shipping mark said:

**GREG / CARROW / FILTER GOODS**

Under that, in different ink:

**R. FORD FREIGHT**

I laughed before I should have.

Senn did not.

"It came through North Exchange under our bonded account."

"Tavin owes us route fees."

"Tavin owes us route fees."

A letter arrived the next morning.

**DO NOT SELL MY GREASE.**

Below it:

**NEW CLERK. I HAVE EXPLAINED ADDRESSES. BADLY, APPARENTLY.**

We sent the crate back with Hesk because Hesk was already hauling flour north and had developed a taste for other people's mistakes.

Senn invoiced Rell Ford Freight for the handling fee.

Tavin paid.

On the payment slip he wrote **THIEF** beside Senn's name.

Senn filed it under receivables.

"You're keeping that?" I asked.

"Paid invoices are records."

"Even insults?"

"Especially legible ones."

Dena wanted to frame it...

# Chapter 226: The Quiet Work

For six days nothing I remembered tried to become uncertain.

This was restful.

I did two Silver support jobs.

One warehouse brace replacement.

One mill intake inspection.

Both used old experience in boring ways.

A pulley rope showed interior weathering before the client loaded it.

A mill apprentice had set a temporary anchor too close to a wet return trench.

Neither discovery changed history.

Neither required prophecy.

The warehouse bought new rope.

The mill moved the anchor.

I ate lunch with Noll twice.

Lost three copper to Jorren at cards once.

Read Mara's last letter again without drawing anything new on the map.

WATCH stayed closed in my field book.

That felt important enough that I refused to make it important...

# Chapter 227: The Paper Bridge

The west freight office wanted a Silver sign-off on a detour plan.

Not the bridge.

The detour.

Spring repair had not started on the Meren bridge, and the five-ton plate was still posted.

A dye merchant wanted to move a six-ton wagon before the seasonal road fees increased.

He had already paid a clerk to prove that later maintenance records showed the bridge eventually carried heavier freight.

The clerk put the copied record in front of me.

"See? Twelve-ton certification seven years later."

"Seven years later."

The merchant spread both hands.

"Same bridge."

The road engineer said, "No. Same location."

I liked her immediately.

She had current inspection marks showing two joints scheduled for reset in spring.

Could the bridge physically hold six tons today?

Probably.

Maybe comfortably.

Current certification still said five.

I signed the detour plan.

The merchant lost four hours and paid eight copper in road fees.

He did not forgive me because the bridge remained standing after we left.

That was fair.

A correct current decision did not require the bridge to collapse behind him for dramatic support...

# Chapter 228: The Bent Pin

The detour crossed a timber bridge maintained by a village crew.

Their oldest mason sang while checking the hardware.

Six verses about a woman who married a ferryman and regretted the river, the ferry, the ferryman, and eventually marriage as a concept.

I knew four verses.

He knew six.

"Old song," I said.

"Older than your cord."

He struck the third retaining pin with a small hammer.

Wrong note.

Not magic.

Metal.

He struck it again.

"Unload half."

The merchant started objecting.

The engineer said, "Unload half."

We unloaded half.

The pin came out with a hairline split running from the lower hole.

The mason held it up.

"Would've lasted."

"How long?" I asked.

"Until it didn't."

The merchant made a noise that contained no useful language.

The crew replaced the pin from a tarred box under the bridge deck.

Then we reloaded the wagon.

The mason resumed verse five.

I asked him to teach me six.

He said my singing voice had not earned it...

# Chapter 229: The Current Sheet

My paper map needed a second page.

Not because the world had grown.

Because my confidence had shrunk into better handwriting.

North Ridge maintenance route.

No east cart road yet.

South Reach spill channel.

Five-ton bridge plate.

Mara's river office.

Carrier routes.

Two WATCH circles.

Three question marks.

Jorren's road notices.

Old Greg's map lived behind my eyes with cleaner roads and fewer temporary stupidities.

I could still travel by it faster.

I could also be wrong by seven years.

I taped the second page to the first.

Noll found me doing it at my table.

"You made the map bigger."

"The table was too useful."

He shifted his bowl away from the glue.

"Can you still find work?"

"Usually."

"Good. I was worried paper had won."

"Paper always wins eventually."

"Guild answer."

He ate my last piece of bread.

The map gained a grease stain near North Ridge...

# Chapter 230: The Apprentice's Fire

Pellin ran an outside firing without Arlo watching the door.

Not because Arlo had achieved peace.

Because Dena sent him across town to argue about clay delivery timing.

Pellin loaded the regular slot at Hesk Street Pottery, checked the revised transport tray, and shortened one hold mark when the greenware arrived late.

That part was his decision.

Two chambers warped.

Not broken.

Not good enough for matched customer spares.

When Arlo returned, Pellin told him before Arlo found the batch.

"I shortened the hold."

Arlo looked at the warped collars.

"Why?"

"We were going to lose the slot."

"So lose the slot."

Pellin stared at him.

Months ago Arlo would have pushed the work forward himself.

Now he was furious because Pellin had done it.

"You taught me to save the batch schedule."

"I taught you badly."

That silenced both of them.

The pottery owner walked past carrying a shovel.

"If this becomes emotional, take it outside."

"It is already outside," Pellin said.

She looked around the yard.

"Further outside."

They wrote the warped batch into the log.

Neither felt better...

# Chapter 231: The Road Opened

Jorren repaired a road without me again.

I found out because a fruit seller complained that the west spur had reopened and ruined the shortcut she had been using through East Market.

"City keeps changing things," she said.

This sounded personally offensive to her.

The notice board confirmed it.

**WEST SPUR — FULL LOAD RESTORED.**

Jorren's name sat below the engineer under field coordination.

I had not known the repair was scheduled.

That afternoon he appeared at the Guild Annex looking tired and carrying mud on one sleeve.

"Your road opened."

"City's road."

"The fruit seller says you ruined commerce."

"Which fruit seller?"

I described her.

"She owes us two copper for a barrier cone."

"That explains the political position."

He took my water cup without asking.

"What did you do this week?"

"Argued with a bridge using paperwork."

"Win?"

"The bridge declined comment."

"Good bridge."

He drank the rest of my water.

Neither of us mentioned his Silver application.

There were other things to talk about...
