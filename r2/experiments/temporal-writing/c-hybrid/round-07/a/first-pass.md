# Chapter 227: The Short Hold

Pellin warped two chambers without Arlo in the yard.

That was important mostly because Pellin told him first.

The Hesk Street firing slot started at noon.

Our greenware arrived late because a flour wagon lost a wheel across the lane and blocked the handcart for twenty-three minutes.

Pellin had three choices.

Miss the slot.

Ask Hesk Street to shift another customer's firing.

Shorten the final drying hold.

He shortened the hold.

Not much.

Enough.

Two lower collars came out slightly oval.

Not cracked.

Not saleable as matched replacements.

Arlo returned from arguing with the clay supplier while Pellin was measuring them.

"I shortened the hold," Pellin said.

Arlo stopped.

"Why?"

"We were going to lose the slot."

"Then lose the slot."

Pellin stared at him.

"You taught me to save the schedule."

Arlo opened his mouth.

Nothing came out.

That was rare enough that Dena came over to see whether he was injured.

Arlo picked up one warped chamber.

"I taught you badly."

Pellin did not look relieved.

"Still mine?"

"The batch?"

"The next one."

Arlo looked at Ivena.

Ivena kept writing in the loss ledger.

"Why are you asking him?" she said.

Pellin looked back at Arlo.

Arlo put the chamber down.

"Yes."

Nobody hugged.

The warped pair went onto the seconds shelf...

# Chapter 232: The Wrong Note

The village road hand heard the bad pin before I did.

We were replacing a low-charge guide collar on a timber footbridge north of Carrow.

My job was to hold a small plane while the carpenter lifted the old bracket.

The road hand, Tarl Fen, checked the retaining hardware with a hammer.

Ping.

Ping.

Thunk.

He stopped.

"Unload the near rail."

The carpenter looked up.

"Why?"

Tarl struck the pin again.

Thunk.

"Because that sounds married."

"To what?"

"Trouble."

Apparently local maintenance terminology had survived formal education by avoiding it.

We unloaded the near rail.

The pin came out with a hairline split from the lower hole.

I had seen split pins before.

I had not heard this one through the wood.

Tarl held it up.

"Would've lasted."

"How long?"

"Until it didn't."

He took a replacement from a tarred box under the bridge deck.

While he worked, he sang two lines about a ferryman's wife regretting the river.

I knew the song.

Different verses.

"Old song," I said.

"Old bridge."

Neither fact made the other one mine...

# Chapter 237: The Stone Under the Shed

That narrowed the search.

The surveyor did not want to measure through manure.

I was there because the city wanted a Silver support hand if the old marker still carried charge.

Stable.

No surge.

No hidden chamber.

No ancestral curse.
