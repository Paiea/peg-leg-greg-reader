# Chapter 239: The Half Day

Senn asked for another half day of himself.

Not an assistant.

Not a clerk.

Himself.

Three outside routes had become four during the last month.

Two used North Exchange.

One used Pera Sol and therefore required dryness sufficient to satisfy a woman who distrusted moisture personally.

One customer still signed the carrier line because their manager insisted the form was designed by enemies of left-handed people.

Senn had started finishing route entries after lunch on days he was not technically scheduled for route work.

Ivena noticed.

"How many hours?"

Senn showed her the ledger.

Dena leaned across the table.

"How much does one missing return cost?"

Arlo asked, "How much does another half day cost?"

Pellin said, "How much does this meeting cost?"

Nobody invited him to continue.

Ivena approved one additional paid half day each week.

Six weeks.

Trial.

Senn nodded once.

"Enough?" I asked later.

"For current routes."

"Good answer."

"I learned not to say permanent in this building."

Dena shouted from the other room that permanent was how furniture happened...

# Chapter 240: The Clerk Supper

Jorren brought two city clerks to dinner and forgot to warn me.

They argued about gravel.

Not whether gravel was useful.

Which gravel.

One preferred a smaller river mix under wheel ruts.

The other said that mix migrated under winter runoff and used the word **migration** as if gravel had made a political choice.

Jorren understood both of them.

That was new enough that I stopped eating for a moment.

One clerk pointed at me.

"You're the Guild Silver who worked under him on the west culvert crew."

"Different authority," Jorren said.

"I know."

"You asked like you didn't."

They resumed arguing.

Jorren had mud under one fingernail and a city packet folded beside his bowl.

I could see his old Silver application corner inside the field book under it.

He never mentioned it.

Neither did I.

When the clerks left, Jorren looked at the empty plates.

"Which gravel?"

"The one that leaves."

"You learned nothing."

"I learned gravel has ambitions."

He stole the last dumpling.

Apparently city authority had not improved his character...

# Chapter 241: The Archive Wagon

The Guild job carried paper instead of freight.

Six lockboxes of municipal copies.

Three villages east of Carrow.

Seven days.

One archivist who treated rain as a hostile profession.

My role existed because several copied records involved old charged boundary markers and the city wanted a Silver support hand available if anyone needed one exposed or stabilized.

The archivist was Pel Aro.

She inspected my pack before departure.

"Oilskin?"

"Yes."

"For the copies?"

"For me."

She stared.

I added another sheet.

The driver was named Venn.

The Bronze guard was a woman named Kess who had done two warehouse escorts and no archive work.

"What attacks paper?" she asked.

Pel said, "Water. Ignorance. Poor shelving."

"Bandits?"

"Less frequently than mildew."

We left by the east road with six boxes of dead people's handwriting and no expectation of adventure.

Pel still checked the clouds every ten minutes...

# Chapter 242: The Bell Field

The first village grew grass that rang when wet.

Not loudly.

A thin glassy chiming moved across the drainage meadow after the morning rain.

I stopped beside the road.

Venn did not.

"Bellgrass," he said.

"Useful?"

"If you like knowing when the lower ditch is carrying charge."

Children had tied red cloth around three patches where the pitch changed.

The village ditch keeper walked out with a wooden rake and pressed one ringing clump flat until the note dulled.

"Too much seep," she said.

No spell.

No Guild call.

She opened a side cut with the rake.

Water shifted.

The ringing eased.

"Goats eat it?" Kess asked.

"After drying. Fresh makes their mouths buzz."

Kess looked delighted.

Pel looked at the road.

"We have records to deliver."

Old Greg had crossed this region later.

I remembered the road.

I remembered none of the grass.

We kept moving while the field chimed behind us...

# Chapter 243: The Toll Seal

The toll house did not exist in my memory.

Current Greg still had to stop there.

Pel said municipal archive wagons were exempt.

The toll keeper said municipal archive wagons were exempt on official seal days.

"Today is an official seal day."

"Seal?"

Pel became very still.

The seal was in one of the six lockboxes.

She knew which village packet contained it.

She did not know which box.

We opened three under oilskin while the toll keeper watched with professional pleasure.

The seal was in the fourth.

He stamped the exemption sheet.

"No charge."

Pel looked at him.

"You knew the seal would be there."

"I knew you would eventually find out."

Kess covered a laugh with a cough.

We rolled through without paying two copper.

I marked the toll house on my current map.

Pel noticed.

"It will still exist tomorrow."

"Probably."

She disliked that word enough to check the lockboxes again...

# Chapter 244: Widow Sar's Bridge

Everyone called the village bridge Widow Sar's.

Widow Sar did not own it.

She owned the house beside it and had spent twenty years watching people overload it.

The posted limit said four tons.

Our archive wagon weighed less than two.

No problem.

The quarry wagon behind us did not.

Its driver said he had crossed heavier loads last month.

Widow Sar came outside carrying a broom.

"Not since the north pier shifted."

"Looks straight."

"So do you from far away."

The driver disliked her immediately.

The village road hand confirmed the pier had moved after thaw and the load notice had gone up six days ago.

The quarry wagon turned around.

Pel asked Widow Sar how she had noticed before the road hand.

"Kitchen window."

"You can see the pier?"

"I hear wheels change."

She went back inside.

Kess watched the door close.

"Guild doesn't certify that?"

"Kitchen windows?"

"Whatever that was."

I looked at the bridge.

"Probably not."

Pel made a note anyway...

# Chapter 245: The First Box

The first archive box did not settle the pasture dispute.

This surprised everyone except Pel.

The old record described a charged boundary stone beside a walnut tree.

The walnut tree was gone.

The marker was gone.

Two current fence lines disagreed by eleven feet.

Both families had documents.

Both documents were younger than Pel's copy.

The village mayor pointed at the page.

"So that is the line."

Pel pointed through the window at the field.

"Where?"

The mayor stopped.

I could have told them where the later east road boundary ran in my first life.

The later east road did not exist now.

Nobody had asked me to import a future road into somebody else's pasture.

The mayor scheduled a survey for the next morning.

The two families argued about whether the old walnut had stood north or south of the spring ditch.

The cows crossed both proposed lines without concern...

# Chapter 246: The Marker Below

The missing boundary marker was under a manure shed.

A farm boy solved what the documents had not.

"Grandfather built around a weird stone."

"Why?" Pel asked.

"Tingled when he pulled it."

That was excellent historical methodology.

The current farmer did not want the shed demolished.

The surveyor did not want to crawl through manure.

Pel wanted the inscription.

I wanted everyone to stop leaning shovels against the thing while arguing.

We removed three side boards.

The marker appeared behind a stack of broken tools.

Faint charge.

Stable.

I held a small plane while the surveyor brushed dirt from the top line.

No surge.

No hidden mechanism.

Letters.

The inscription matched the archive copy.

The survey line moved seven feet from one current fence and four from the other.

Neither family got the clean victory they had prepared speeches for.

The farmer's son looked at the marker.

"Can I put the boards back?"

Pel looked at the surveyor.

The surveyor nodded.

History returned under the manure shed...

# Chapter 247: The Upstairs Bed

The inn had one upstairs bed left.

Pel took it.

Not because she outranked anyone.

Because the lockboxes did not sleep under leaking roofs.

Kess took the stable loft and claimed hay was good for her back.

I got the remaining cot beside the far wall.

At two in the morning a goat climbed the stairs.

Nobody knew why.

It ate part of Venn's glove before he caught it.

My left knee had stiffened from two days of wagon work and one village survey spent standing beside a manure shed.

Nothing injured.

Enough to make the stairs irritating.

Pel appeared in the doorway wearing a blanket over her shoulders.

"Is the goat near the boxes?"

"No."

"Good."

"Driver's glove."

"Replaceable."

Venn heard her from below.

"It was a good glove!"

Pel closed the door.

At breakfast she added **GOAT ACCESS TO LODGING** to her travel notes.

The innkeeper said the goat was not included in the room rate.

Pel said that made the sentence worse...

# Chapter 248: The Reed Lights

The last village floated blue lights on irrigation ponds after sunset.

I assumed festival.

Wrong.

The lights were reed bundles tied around small porous stones.

They took on low charge during the day and released it slowly into the water overnight.

A woman setting them out said the glow kept biting insects away from the shallows.

"Magic?" Kess asked.

The woman looked at the blue pond.

"Probably."

"What are the insects called?"

"Biters."

"Proper name?"

"Ask somebody who likes insects."

Kess did not.

The village children knew which ponds needed two bundles and which needed four.

One old man said the western pond stopped working if the stones were scrubbed too clean.

Nobody agreed why.

Pel wrote down the village seal and ignored the lights entirely.

That felt healthy.

We slept with fewer bites...

# Chapter 249: The Wrong Copy

The seven-day archive job survived rain, toll law, charged stones, goats, and local insects.

Then Pel found the wrong receipt.

Not missing.

Wrong color.

The second village mayor had stamped the local copy instead of the city return copy.

Both contained the same words.

Only one belonged in the lockbox.

Pel stared at them as if paper had betrayed civilization.

"Can we use this one?" Kess asked.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because this one stays there."

"But it is here."

"Exactly."

We were already outside Carrow.

Pel sent a hired runner back with the local copy and a note containing no profanity but several sentences that wanted it.

I signed the Guild closeout.

Full pay.

No combat.

Kess asked whether all Silver work ended like this.

"Only the glamorous work."

Pel heard me.

"The records are dry."

"Very glamorous."

She checked them again...

# Chapter 250: The Return Ledger

Senn wanted to know why the city could lose a day to the wrong piece of paper and still call itself organized.

I had made the mistake of telling him about Pel's receipt.

"Different copy serves different custody," he said after I explained.

"Yes."

"So their failure was not information. It was boundary."

I looked at him.

"Do not become inspired."

He was already writing.

"I am not adding a line."

"Good."

"I am changing the customer-copy label."

"Worse."

Dena came in carrying two wrapped chambers.

"Why is he smiling?"

"Municipal government gave him ideas."

"Burn the city."

Senn changed **CUSTOMER COPY** to **CUSTOMER KEEPS** and **ROUTE COPY** to **RETURNS WITH CRATE**.

Ivena read both labels.

"Better."

I had betrayed us all...

# Chapter 251: The Road Office Supper

Jorren arrived late with two city clerks and a folded road plan under his arm.

Different clerks this time.

Same gravel.

I had underestimated gravel's institutional reach.

One clerk asked Jorren whether the north drainage crews could swap material grades without engineer review.

Jorren answered before sitting down.

"Inside winter spec, yes. Not on culvert fill."

The clerk nodded and kept eating.

Nobody looked at me.

Good.

Jorren had stopped translating his road work into Guild language for my benefit.

That felt more important than understanding the gravel.

After the clerks left, he rubbed both eyes.

"Long day?"

"Good day."

Those were apparently allowed together.

His field book fell open while he reached for water.

The old Silver application was still folded inside.

He closed the book without comment.

I passed him the cup.

We discussed soup instead...

# Chapter 252: The Old Routes

Pel sent me a copy I had not asked for.

Three old east-road alignments on one sheet.

One passed the bellgrass village.

One crossed Widow Sar's bridge.

One followed a low meadow and closed whenever spring water decided roads were optional.

The straight east road I remembered from my first life did not appear.

Not yet.

I spread the copy beside my current map.

The old routes looked inefficient.

That was because I knew which road eventually won.

Current villages did not live beside failed history.

They lived beside their roads.

I marked the toll house.

Then Widow Sar's bridge.

Then the bellgrass drainage meadow.

No arrows toward destiny.

Places.

Noll came in with soup and looked at the paper covering half the table.

"Can we still eat?"

I moved the map.

"Current use approved."

"Good city."

He put the bowls down...
