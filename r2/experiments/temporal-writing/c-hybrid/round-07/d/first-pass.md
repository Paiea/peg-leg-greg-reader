# Chapter 263: The Marker Cart

The winter-road contract came with twenty-four stones and one cart.

Not new stones.

Replacement caps, charge tabs, paint, two snow gates, and a regional surveyor named Tamar Rusk who considered maps temporary evidence.

Eight days east and north of Carrow.

My role was Silver support if an old route marker had to be unloaded, lifted, or isolated while a cap was replaced.

Tamar's role was everything involving where the road actually went.

The Bronze assistant was Fenn.

The driver was Soli, who knew more road names than the official sheet and fewer official names than Tamar preferred.

At the first fork Tamar pointed east.

Soli turned north.

"East," she said.

"Bridge crew."

"Notice says open."

"Notice is from yesterday."

Tamar looked at me.

I looked at Soli.

"He has the horse."

We went north.

Three miles later a road hand confirmed the east bridge was open to foot traffic only.

Tamar crossed one line off the official sheet.

"Temporary evidence," Soli said.

She did not enjoy him...

# Chapter 264: The Old Toll

The toll house stood on a road I remembered as free.

Current road disagreed.

One copper per loaded cart.

Half for local farm wagons.

No charge for road maintenance crews if Tamar produced the district seal.

She produced it immediately.

I was disappointed on Soli's behalf.

The toll keeper stamped the route sheet.

"Bypass vote next spring," he said.

Tamar grunted.

"Fourth vote."

"This one has money."

"Third one had money."

"Not enough."

Old Greg's road later ran around this hill.

No toll house.

No gate.

No man warming his hands over a brazier while arguing about half-copper farm rates.

Current Greg waited while Fenn bought a fried onion cake from the keeper's daughter.

"Want one?"

"Yes."

The toll house had already improved the route...

# Chapter 265: The Frost Lace

White lace covered the north face of marker seven.

I thought frost.

Tamar said, "Don't scrape that bare-handed."

Good timing.

The road keeper brought a wooden paddle and peeled the white film away in one flexible sheet.

Faint blue charge crawled through it before fading.

"Frost lace," he said.

"Plant?"

"Ask the university."

"Useful?"

"Tells me the cap is leaking."

The marker's charge tab had split along one rivet.

Low leak.

Not dangerous.

Enough to feed whatever the white film enjoyed.

I held a small plane around the cap while Tamar removed the old tab.

Fenn replaced it.

The road keeper rolled the dried frost lace into a bucket.

"Throw it away?"

"Dyer buys it."

"For what?"

"Blue wash."

Tamar looked at Fenn.

"Do not start a side business on district time."

Fenn had not spoken.

That made the warning suspiciously specific...

# Chapter 266: The Drift Stakes

The district map did not.

Tamar noticed because marker nine sat twelve feet outside the current stake line.

"Drift comes from the west now."

"Since Kelm cut the windbreak."

Kelm was apparently a farmer three fields uphill who had removed half a row of poplars two winters ago.

The snow now buried the old edge first.

Nobody had filed anything.

Tamar looked at the official winter-route sheet.

Then at the boy.

"Who sets these?"

"My aunt."

We found his aunt repairing a sled runner behind the cooperage.

She showed Tamar last winter's drift marks on a barn post.

Marker nine stayed where the legal road was.

The snow guidance moved where the road could be found in January.

Different systems.

Both correct.

Tamar added a note instead of moving the stone...

# Chapter 267: The Closed Cut

**EAST CUT CLOSED AFTER FIRST FREEZE.**

"Slide?" I asked.

"Ice shelf."

Soli pointed uphill.

Water seeped through a dark seam above the cut.

Once frozen, pieces broke loose onto the road.

"Later they roofed this," I said before I stopped myself.

"Later who?"

"Road office. Probably."

"Useful current roof?"

"No."

"Then current road goes around."

The winter detour added six miles.

It also passed two farms, a shrine, and a blacksmith who sold Fenn a new boot buckle cheaper than Carrow.

Old Greg remembered the improved cut.

# Chapter 269: The Glass Beetles

Marker twelve had beetles inside the lamp hood.

Small clear wings.

Copper-colored bodies.

Fenn reached for one.

The local road woman slapped his hand away.

"Seal eaters."

The beetles were chewing the waxed gasket around the low-charge lamp cup.

Not the lamp.

The sealant.

Once the gasket failed, rain would reach the contact plate.

The road woman warmed bitter resin in a spoon and painted a new ring around the hood.

"Keeps them off?" I asked.

"Mostly."

"What are they called?"

"Glass beetles."

"Because wings?"

"Because they look like glass."

Excellent taxonomy.

Old Greg knew three ways to kill charge-eating vermin in wet rope stores.

None were better than the local woman already holding the correct spoon.

I held the hood steady while she resealed it.

Fenn inspected his slapped hand for permanent damage.

There was none...

# Chapter 270: The Market Bend

The road bent around a village I did not remember.

Current village.

Two mills.

Old Greg's later road ran straight across the lower field and missed the village by nearly a mile.

"Market."

"Road came to market?"

"Market came to road. Then both got stubborn."

We stopped for feed.

I bought bread.

A wheelwright tightened one loose cart band while Soli argued about price.

It also fixed the wheel before it became a better story.

# Chapter 271: The Garden Stone

Marker fifteen had become part of a garden wall.

The road had shifted twelve feet south after a drainage ditch widened.

The legal marker stayed where it was.

A householder built around it with flat stone and planted mint on both sides.

Tamar wanted the cap inspected.

The householder wanted the wall to remain a wall.

Reasonable conflict.

We removed four loose stones from the back.

The marker cap was old but intact.

Charge low and stable.

Fenn repainted the survey number without moving anything.

The householder watched.

"Will people think the road comes through my mint?"

"No," Tamar said. "Marker is boundary reference. Road sheet carries current alignment."

"Then why does the marker stay?"

Tamar looked at the wall.

"Because moving reference points every time a ditch changes is how surveyors die young."

The householder accepted this as medical advice.

We put the stones back...

# Chapter 273: The Snow Gate

Then the snow poles.

Then the ridge.

# Chapter 276: The Road There Is

Two days later I walked past the east-road board and saw Tamar's winter closure notice posted cleanly beneath the freight rates.

The straight road in my first-life memory still did not exist.

The toll house still did.

Market Bend still bent.

The orchard still forced carts around its trees.

The east cut still waited for a roof nobody had built yet.

None of that felt wrong anymore.

It was simply the road there was.

A delivery driver beside me asked which way to Market Bend.

I pointed at the current sign.

"North fork. Lower road if the ridge gate is closed."

"Closed?"

"Check the board."

He did.
