# Chapter 227: The Short Hold

Pellin warped two chambers without Arlo in the yard.

That was important mostly because Pellin told him first.

The Hesk Street firing slot started at noon.

Our greenware arrived late because a flour wagon lost a wheel across the lane and blocked the handcart for twenty-three minutes.

Pellin had three choices.

Miss the slot.

Ask Hesk Street to shift another customer's firing.

Shorten the final drying hold.

He shortened the hold.

Not much.

Enough.

Two lower collars came out slightly oval.

Not cracked.

Not saleable as matched replacements.

Arlo returned from arguing with the clay supplier while Pellin was measuring them.

"I shortened the hold," Pellin said.

Arlo stopped.

"Why?"

"We were going to lose the slot."

"Then lose the slot."

Pellin stared at him.

"You taught me to save the schedule."

Arlo opened his mouth.

Nothing came out.

That was rare enough that Dena came over to see whether he was injured.

Arlo picked up one warped chamber.

"I taught you badly."

Pellin did not look relieved.

"Still mine?"

"The batch?"

"The next one."

Arlo looked at Ivena.

Ivena kept writing in the loss ledger.

"Why are you asking him?" she said.

Pellin looked back at Arlo.

Arlo put the chamber down.

"Yes."

Nobody hugged.

The warped pair went onto the seconds shelf...
