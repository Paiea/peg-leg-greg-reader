# Chapter 237: The Stone Under the Shed

The charged boundary marker was under a manure shed.

Nobody had planned this.

A city surveyor found an eighty-year-old deed describing a marked stone beside a walnut tree.

The walnut tree was gone.

Two current fences disagreed by eleven feet.

The stone was also gone until the farmer's son said his grandfather had built the shed around something that made his hands tingle when he tried to move it.

That narrowed the search.

The farmer did not want the shed demolished.

The surveyor did not want to measure through manure.

I was there because the city wanted a Silver support hand if the old marker still carried charge.

We removed three wall boards instead of the floor.

The top of the stone appeared behind a stack of tools.

Faint old charge.

Stable.

I held a small plane while the surveyor brushed dirt from one edge.

No surge.

No hidden chamber.

No ancestral curse.

An inscription.

The copied deed matched it.

The farmer looked at the two fences.

"So who owns the eleven feet?"

The surveyor sighed.

"That is why I brought paper too."

The stone had survived eighty years.

The property argument survived five more minutes without difficulty...
