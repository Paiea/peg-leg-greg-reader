# Chapter 265: The Short Block

Lera stopped the lesson before I expected.

Not because of my knee.

Because I had started anticipating the recovery step instead of finishing the cut.

"You are trying to arrive early to something that happens after."

"That sounds efficient."

"It looks stupid."

She made me repeat one cut twelve times.

No combination.

No clever variation.

Cut.

Arrive.

Recover.

On the ninth I stopped leaning toward the recovery before the blade finished.

On the twelfth she nodded.

Then ended the block ten minutes early.

"Full rate?" I asked.

"Obviously."

"You enjoy this."

"Immensely."

I paid.

The walk home hurt nothing except my finances...

# Chapter 266: The Fifth Basket

Noll bought five baskets of greens.

This would have been unremarkable if four and six had not already become political positions.

One basket showed bruising underneath.

Noll rejected it.

"Last time two were bad."

"Today one is bad."

"Yes."

"So five."

"You have discovered counting."

Miko laughed from the prep table.

Lunch still required changing because the fifth basket held less cabbage than the order assumed.

# Chapter 267: The Marker Cart

The winter road contract came with twenty-four marker stops and no promise that all twenty-four were reachable.

That clause made me like it immediately.

The district surveyor was Tamar Rusk.

She considered maps useful until the road contradicted them.

The driver, Soli, considered maps proof that office workers feared horses.

The Bronze assistant was Fenn, who had not yet learned that both opinions could become exhausting by noon.

My job was narrow.

Silver support if an old charged marker needed unloading, lifting, or isolation while a cap or tab was replaced.

At the first fork Tamar pointed east.

Soli turned north.

"East," she said.

"Bridge crew."

"Notice says open."

"Notice is yesterday."

Tamar looked at me.

I looked at Soli.

"He has the horse."

We went north.

Three miles later a road hand confirmed the east bridge was open to people and empty carts only.

Tamar crossed out one line on her sheet.

Soli did not say anything.

That was worse...

# Chapter 268: The White Film

Marker seven had frost growing on the wrong side.

Tamar stopped Fenn before he scraped it.

"Wood first."

The local road keeper brought a flat paddle and peeled the white film away in one flexible sheet.

Faint blue charge crawled through it before disappearing.

"Frost lace," he said.

I had never heard the term.

"Bad?"

"Useful. Means the cap leaks."

The charge tab had split beside one rivet.

Low leak.

Enough to feed the white growth.

Not enough to make the marker dangerous.

I held a four-inch plane around the cap while Tamar removed the old tab.

Fenn fitted the new one.

The road keeper rolled the dried film into a bucket.

"Trash?" I asked.

"Dyer buys it."

"Why?"

"Blue wash."

Fenn became visibly interested.

Tamar pointed at him without looking up.

"No side business on district time."

He had not spoken.

I respected her experience...

# Chapter 269: The Drift Line

The legal road and the winter road disagreed by twelve feet.

Nothing was wrong.

Marker nine sat exactly where the district survey said it should.

The village snow stakes curved east of it.

Tamar noticed.

A boy carrying firewood noticed us noticing.

"Drift moved."

"Snow drift?" Fenn asked.

The boy looked at the stakes.

"What other kind?"

His aunt set them each autumn.

We found her behind the cooperage repairing a sled runner.

Two years earlier a farmer uphill had cut half a windbreak.

Winter wind now laid the deep snow farther east.

The road boundary had not moved.

The useful winter line had.

Tamar copied the change onto the route sheet.

She did not move the stone.

"Different systems," she said.

The aunt shrugged.

"Different snow."

Both were better answers than mine...

# Chapter 270: The Closed Cut

Old Greg remembered using the east cut in winter.

Current road had a red gate across it before the first real snow.

**CLOSED AFTER FIRST FREEZE.**

Water seeped from a dark seam above the road.

Soli pointed at the rock shelf.

"Freezes under. Drops pieces."

"Later they roofed this," I said.

Tamar looked at me.

I should have kept that sentence internal.

"Current roof?"

"No."

"Useful current advice?"

"Take the lower road."

"Good."

The lower road added six miles.

It also passed two farms, one shrine, and a blacksmith where Fenn replaced a boot buckle for less than Carrow charged.

We bought apples at the second farm.

The closed cut did not become a mystery.

It remained closed...

# Chapter 271: The Wayhouse Bench

The district wayhouse had six beds and five mattresses.

Fenn arrived last.

He claimed the bench beside the stove by choice.

At midnight the bench won the argument.

My mattress had a hollow shaped like a smaller man with worse posture.

Three days climbing in and out of the marker cart left my left knee stiff enough that I sat on the edge before standing.

Tamar noticed.

"Need the cart?"

"I have the cart."

"Need to stay in it for the first hill?"

That was a better question.

"Yes."

Soli gave me the front seat in the morning without discussion.

Fenn complained that Silver rank had finally found a practical use.

Tamar handed him the paint bucket.

Rank lost again...

# Chapter 272: The Seal Eaters

Marker twelve had insects under the lamp hood.

Clear wings.

Copper bodies.

Fenn reached toward one.

The local road woman slapped his hand away.

"Seal eaters."

They were chewing the waxed gasket around the low-charge lamp cup.

Not the lamp.

Not the marker.

The gasket.

Once the seal opened, rain would reach the contact plate and turn a small maintenance problem into a wet one.

The road woman warmed bitter resin in a spoon.

"Kills them?" I asked.

"Keeps most off."

"Glass beetles?"

She shrugged.

"That's what children call them."

Old Greg knew charge pests from rope stores.

None of that helped more than the woman already holding the correct spoon.

I held the hood steady.

She resealed it.

Fenn inspected his slapped hand.

It remained attached...

# Chapter 273: The Market Bend

The road curved around a village that later memory had reduced to nothing.

Current village objected by existing.

Thirty houses.

Two mills.

A market square.

A cracked blue fountain.

Smoked pears.

Soli stopped for feed.

Tamar wanted ten minutes.

The horse wanted longer.

A wheelwright noticed one loose cart band while they argued and tightened it before Soli finished paying.

"Road bends for the market?" I asked.

Soli shook his head.

"Market came to road. Then road got busy. Then everybody complained about bend."

Tamar said the bypass had been studied twice.

Neither study had money.

We lost forty minutes.

We also did not lose a wheel later.

Fenn bought pears and declared the delay profitable.

Tamar made him carry the feed sack...

# Chapter 274: The Orchard Curve

The district road went around an orchard because a man seventy years ago had refused to sell.

His great-granddaughter wanted it straightened.

Tamar laughed before she could stop herself.

The woman pointed at three loaded carts waiting for each other on the curve.

"My dead relatives did not own wagons like these."

Fair.

Current road office had no approved straightening project.

Current compensation had not been negotiated.

Current apple trees were full.

We replaced two marker caps where wagon mud kept burying the numbers.

I remembered the later straight road.

That information belonged to no one here.

The owner sold us cider.

Then complained about her ancestors until Soli agreed with everything because the cider was free...

# Chapter 275: The Early Gate

Tamar closed the ridge road while the road was still dry.

The sky looked bad.

Not closed-road bad.

Soli said the cloud sat wrong.

A shepherd at the gate said the wind had changed at noon.

Tamar checked the district forecast.

Then the snow poles.

Then the ridge.

"Lower road."

A merchant behind us objected.

"I can reach Vale Cross before dark."

"Maybe," Soli said.

The merchant disliked maybe more than no.

We took the lower road.

Snow started three hours later.

Enough to whiten the upper turn.

Not enough to prove the merchant would have been trapped.

Tamar did not celebrate.

She ate stew at the wayhouse and fell asleep before Fenn finished complaining about room prices...

# Chapter 276: The Locked Gate

The twenty-fourth marker sat behind a locked farm gate.

The owner was away.

His hired man had a key and no permission to admit district crews because the previous crew had broken a latch and nobody had paid for it.

Tamar had a service order.

The hired man had a grievance.

Both were real.

She read the order twice.

Then wrote:

**ACCESS DEFERRED - OWNER NOTICE REQUIRED.**

Fenn looked at the marker visible beyond the field.

"We came eight days for twenty-four."

"We serviced twenty-three."

"Finished?"

"Accurate."

Nobody climbed the fence.

The district would send notice and another crew later.

My travel pay remained full.

The service units did not.

A job could end with one thing still undone and no one pretending otherwise...

"North fork. Lower road if the ridge gate is closed."

Open.

He thanked me and left.

I did not tell him where the later bypass would run.
