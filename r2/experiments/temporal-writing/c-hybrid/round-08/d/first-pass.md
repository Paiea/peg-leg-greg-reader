# Chapter 289: The River Bell

The winter-river job started with a bell nobody rang.

A bronze cup hung from a frame above the South Reach landing.

The ferryman struck the post instead.

"Bell freezes."

"Then why have it?"

"Works when not frozen."

Reasonable infrastructure.

The district contract covered six days of landing checks between Carrow and three packet stops downstream.

My role was Silver support around low-charge mooring plates and one old lift frame.

The river inspector was Yara Pell.

The pilot was Cenn.

A deckhand named Moro did most of the actual lifting and considered all written schedules suggestions made by people standing on dry ground.

We left on open water under a sky that looked colder than the river admitted...

# Chapter 290: The Reed Pole

The first channel marker was a reed pole painted red at the top.

I expected something more official.

Yara said, "It is in the official book."

Cenn said, "Book doesn't keep it upright."

The pole leaned because ice had shoved the lower bank overnight.

A local fisherman had already tied it back to a willow root.

Temporary.

Ugly.

Useful.

Yara measured the new angle and wrote a replacement order.

She did not remove the rope.

"Current navigation gets the current pole," she said.

The fisherman asked whether the district would pay for his rope.

Yara wrote his name down.

He looked surprised.

So did I...

# Chapter 291: The Rope House

Landing two stored mooring rope in a heated shed.

Not for comfort.

Frozen rope lied about flexibility.

The landing master made us wait while one coil warmed before he would let Moro load-test it.

"Looks fine," Moro said.

"Cold fine."

"Difference?"

The landing master bent one short sample.

The outer fibers moved.

The inner twist cracked audibly.

"That difference."

Old Greg knew wet rope.

The landing master knew this shed.

We discarded the coil.

The packet lost twenty minutes.

Nobody called the delay magical...

# Chapter 292: The Frozen Plate

The north mooring plate held charge but not evenly.

Frost had formed only along one edge.

Yara wanted the cover off.

The landing master wanted the packet gone first.

Current passengers won.

We waited forty minutes while six people, two baskets of eels, and one angry goat crossed the gangplank.

Then I held a narrow plane around the plate while Yara lifted the cover.

A drain hole had packed with silt.

Meltwater froze at the lower edge and pushed the backing shim crooked.

No magical failure.

Water first.

Charge second.

Moro cleaned the drain with a bent wire.

Yara reset the shim.

The plate loaded evenly again.

The goat watched from the road and remained unconvinced...

# Chapter 293: The Chain Sound

Cenn heard the ferry chain before I did.

Not breaking.

Dragging differently.

He reduced throttle and moved the packet three yards upstream before crossing the chain line.

"Debris?" I asked.

"Maybe."

At the far landing a crew pulled the chain guide and found a branch wedged under one roller.

No crack.

No near disaster.

Just wrong sound.

I knew enough machinery to understand after seeing it.

Cenn had understood before.

"How long?" I asked.

"How long what?"

"Until you hear that."

He shrugged.

"Long enough."

Useful education was often poorly packaged...

# Chapter 294: The Ice Window

The district sheet said the middle landing remained open until dusk.

Not dangerous yet.

Annoying enough that tomorrow morning's first packet would spend an hour breaking it if they kept churning the water until dark.

A freight clerk objected.

"Schedule says five."

"River says three."

Yara checked the temperature trend.

Then agreed with the landing master.

Two crates of onions stayed overnight.

The clerk wrote a complaint.

The onions survived...

# Chapter 295: The Low Channel

Old Greg remembered a deeper south channel.

Current sounding pole disagreed.

Silt had built around the inside bend after autumn flood work upstream.

Cenn moved us north.

"South is shorter."

"South is shallower."

"Later it wasn't."

He looked at me.

"Later boat here?"

"No."

"Then later can swim."

We took the north channel.

Ten minutes slower.

No grounding.

No proof that my remembered channel had ever been wrong.

Only a current pole touching bottom sooner than I expected...

# Chapter 296: The Warm Patch

Steam rose from one patch of riverbank in freezing air.

Not much.

Enough to make Fenn-like curiosity unnecessary because Moro stopped on his own.

"Spring seep."

Warm groundwater entered beneath the bank.

Small silver insects clustered where cold river met warm trickle.

"Good bait," Moro said.

Yara said, "Protected bank. Don't dig."

Nobody dug.

A fisherman downstream had a woven trap positioned just beyond the warm water.

Current ecology had already found the resource before we arrived.

I asked what the insects were called.

Moro said, "Warmflies."

Excellent scholarship survived into river work...

# Chapter 297: The Lost Day

We lost a whole day to wind.

Not storm.

Wind.

Enough crosswind at the open reach that Cenn refused to run the packet empty downstream.

Yara had three inspections scheduled.

Moro had opinions.

I had a paid travel day and nowhere useful to stand.

We stayed at the landing inn.

I cleaned my sword.

Yara rewrote the route order.

Cenn slept after lunch.

Moro played cards with two fishmongers and lost money aggressively.

At dusk the wind dropped.

Nobody suggested leaving in the dark to recover schedule.

Tomorrow remained available...

# Chapter 298: The Freight Order

Wind delay changed the cargo before it changed us.

The freight clerk wanted the original order preserved.

Cenn wanted weight balanced.

My pack ended under a sack of onions.

# Chapter 299: The River Inn

The river inn had warm rooms and bad stairs.

My knee preferred the room.

# Chapter 300: The Open Water

The last landing had no problem.

Guide posts straight.

"Water."

Full pass.

No corrective order.
