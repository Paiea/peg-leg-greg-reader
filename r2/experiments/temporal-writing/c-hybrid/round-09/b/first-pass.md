# Chapter 291: The Winter Price

The Guild raised the overnight travel allowance by one copper.

Nobody celebrated correctly.

Sella said inns had raised winter rates.

A Bronze courier said this was wage progress.

Sella said it was reimbursement.

The courier said coin did not care why it arrived.

I sided with the coin.

The new rate made one north-road contract slightly less annoying and no one richer.

A notice went on the board.

Three people read it.

Two complained that meals had risen more.

Government survived...

# Chapter 292: The Dry Coat

My winter coat finally dried before morning.

This was because I had stopped hanging it beside the colder window.

Noll had told me that six weeks earlier.

I had ignored him because the other hook was closer to the door.

Then I moved it.

The coat dried.

"Miracle," he said.

"Airflow."

"My advice."

"Unproven mechanism."

He threw a sock at me.

The sock was also dry...

# Chapter 293: The Landing Contract

The river contract listed three packet landings, six days, and one Silver support hand.

No guard requirement.

No monster clause.

No promise the river would cooperate.

I took it.

The district inspector was Yara Pell.

She wore two pairs of gloves and kept the outer pair tucked into her belt whenever she wrote.

The packet pilot was Cenn Varo.

He looked at the district schedule, folded it once, and put it under a mug.

The deckhand, Moro, said this was how navigation worked.

Yara ignored both of them.

My part was narrow: unload or isolate low-charge mooring plates and an old lift frame if inspection required opening them.

At South Reach a bronze bell hung above the landing.

Cenn struck the wooden post instead.

"Bell frozen?"

"Clapper is."

"Why keep bell?"

"Spring."

We boarded under clear sky and cold wind.

The river moved south whether the schedule approved or not...

# Chapter 294: The Red Pole

The first channel marker leaned toward the bank.

A red-topped reed pole.

Official enough to have a number in Yara's book.

Temporary enough to be tied to a willow root with somebody's rope.

A fisherman stood nearby repairing a net.

"Ice shoved it last night," he said.

Yara measured the angle.

"Who tied it?"

"Me."

"Your rope?"

"Also me."

She wrote his name down.

He looked suspicious.

"Am I in trouble?"

"District owes you rope if they keep the tie."

That was not the answer he expected.

Cenn said the marker was good enough for current depth.

Yara marked **REPLACE POLE / TEMP TIE ACCEPTED**.

Nobody untied the useful rope because the permanent solution had paperwork...

# Chapter 295: The Warm Rope

Landing Two kept mooring rope inside a heated shed.

The shed smelled of tar, wet wool, and old river.

Moro pulled one coil toward the door.

The landing master stopped him.

"Not that one."

"Looks fine."

"Cold fine."

He took a short sample from the same batch and bent it over his knee.

Outer fibers flexed.

The inner twist made a small dry crack.

I heard it.

Moro frowned.

"Frozen inside?"

"Wet inside. Frozen yesterday. Warm now. Damage stays."

Old Greg knew wet rope and weathering.

I would have distrusted the coil eventually.

The landing master distrusted it before loading because he knew how this shed's rope failed in winter.

We tagged the coil out.

A replacement came from the upper rack.

The packet left twenty-three minutes late.

Nobody died of schedule loss...

# Chapter 296: The Lower Drain

The north mooring plate carried uneven load.

Yara found it with a hand gauge before I felt anything.

One edge stayed colder than the rest.

Frost had built around the lower cover screw.

The landing master wanted passengers off before we opened it.

Six travelers crossed the gangplank.

Then two eel baskets.

Then a goat that objected to every plank individually.

Only after the packet cleared did I set a three-inch plane around the plate.

Yara opened the housing.

Meltwater had nowhere to go.

The drain hole was packed with black silt and one pine needle.

Ice had pushed the lower shim crooked.

Moro cleaned the drain with bent wire.

Yara reset the shim.

I released after the load settled back into the wall.

Second test.

Even.

The charge had not failed.

Drainage had introduced itself into magic again...

# Chapter 297: The Guide Roller

Cenn reduced power in the middle of the crossing.

Nothing visible had changed.

"What?" I asked.

He pointed downriver.

"Chain."

I heard it then.

A dragging pulse under the usual iron noise.

Not sharp.

Wrong rhythm.

He moved the packet upstream before crossing the guide line.

At the far bank the landing crew lifted the roller cover.

A branch had wedged beneath one side.

No cracked link.

No bent guide.

Just wood in the wrong place.

"How did you hear that from the deck?" I asked.

Cenn looked at the chain.

"It sounded wrong."

"Useful explanation."

"You asked."

Fair.

The crew cleared the branch.

Cenn listened to the next empty cycle before accepting the line.

The river did not reward us with danger for taking it seriously...

# Chapter 298: The Three O'Clock Close

The district schedule kept Middle Landing open until five.

The landing master closed at three ten.

Thin shore ice had begun building inside the guide posts.

A freight clerk held up the printed schedule.

"Five."

"If we keep running until five, first packet tomorrow spends an hour breaking what we churn tonight."

"That is tomorrow."

"I also work tomorrow."

Yara checked the temperature log.

Then the forecast.

Then the water edge.

"Close it."

Two onion crates remained on the dock.

The clerk wrote a complaint while the crew hauled the boarding ramp in.

No one pretended the onions had won a moral argument.

The landing opened on time the next morning...

# Chapter 299: The Sounding Pole

The south channel should have been deeper.

Old Greg remembered that clearly enough to be dangerous.

Cenn put the sounding pole down.

Mud came sooner than I expected.

Again.

Same.

Autumn flood work upstream had shifted silt around the inside bend.

Yara's current notice mentioned it.

I had not read that line because I had been looking at the mooring plate list.

"North channel," Cenn said.

"Longer."

"Deeper."

"Later the south is fine."

He looked at me.

I regretted speaking.

"Later packet here?"

"No."

"Then later packet can take it."

Moro laughed into his scarf.

We went north.

Ten extra minutes.

The river remained unimpressed by remembered efficiency...

# Chapter 300: The Wind Day

We lost Thursday to wind.

Not a storm.

The sky stayed blue in places.

The open reach still pushed hard enough sideways that Cenn refused to run the packet empty downstream.

Yara had two inspections and one revisit scheduled.

She crossed them out.

Moro said the wind might drop after lunch.

It did not.

I spent the morning cleaning my sword at the landing inn.

Yara reordered Friday.

Cenn slept for two hours.

Moro played cards with fishmongers and lost enough copper to become philosophical.

At dusk the flags softened.

Nobody suggested leaving in the dark to rescue a paper schedule.

The contract paid travel day.

It did not invent inspection units we had not completed...

# Chapter 301: The Oil First

Thursday's wind changed Friday's cargo.

Lamp oil had to move before flour because the upstream landing's winter storage limit would be full by afternoon.

The freight clerk wanted original sequence.

Cenn wanted weight balanced.

Yara wanted inspection gear accessible at Landing Three.

Moro wanted four people to stop blocking the gangway while discussing freight.

He won first.

They rebuilt the load order on the dock.

Oil forward-left.

Flour aft.

Inspection chest beside the center bench.

My pack under onions.

"Why mine?"

Moro said, "Fits."

Professional reasoning.

The clerk wrote the changed custody order.

The packet left balanced.

My spare socks smelled faintly of onion by evening...

# Chapter 302: The Warm Bank

Steam rose from the riverbank in freezing air.

A small patch beneath Landing Three.

Not enough to hide anything dramatic.

Enough to keep the stones dark while the rest were white.

Moro said, "Spring seep."

Warm groundwater entered below the bank.

Tiny silver insects collected where the warm trickle met cold river.

A fisherman had set a woven bait trap downstream of the seep.

"Good bait?" I asked.

"Very."

Yara pointed at the bank marker.

"Protected edge. No digging."

Nobody had intended to dig.

This still sounded like a rule earned by history.

Old Greg had crossed this reach later without storing any of it.

Current Moro knew where fishermen bought warmflies by the handful.

The river had ordinary magic and a local price...

# Chapter 303: The Clean Landing

The last landing had nothing wrong with it.

Yara checked twice.

Mooring bolts tight.

Drain clear.

Charge even.

Guide posts straight.

Current rope had been replaced two weeks earlier after the local crew found inner weathering.

"Nothing?" she asked the landing master.

He pointed at the river.

"Plenty. Not ours today."

We signed a clean inspection.

No corrective order.

No reserve used.

Moro looked disappointed only because clean work gave him less to complain about.

One place simply worked...

# Chapter 304: The Five Units

The district paid six travel days and five inspection units.

Wind had eaten one workday.

The contract had language for exactly this.

Yara signed inspection completion.

Cenn signed vessel time.

Moro signed cargo labor.

I signed Guild support.

Same six days.

Four different records.

The district clerk asked whether anything serious had happened.

Yara listed one bad rope coil, one clogged drain, one guide obstruction, and a temporary channel change.

"Serious?"

"Maintained," she said.

He accepted that.

On the walk home I added **MIDDLE LANDING / WINTER CLOSE EARLY IF SHORE ICE FORMS** to my map.

Then moved the map so Noll could put dinner down...
