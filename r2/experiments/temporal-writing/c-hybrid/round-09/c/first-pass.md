# Chapter 303: The Saturday Block

The support clinic had twelve chairs and nine people.

This apparently counted as good attendance.

Omi Tress ran it with a chalk board, three weighted frames, and the expression of a woman who had already seen every clever mistake I was likely to make.

My job was one forty-minute demonstration.

Not teaching.

Not mentoring.

Demonstrating short-form release timing under changing load.

Omi introduced me as "Greg, Silver, low reserve, annoyingly clean release."

I objected to only one adjective.

She ignored me.

Pell Arin sat in the second row.

I made a small plane under the first weighted frame.

Omi shifted load away.

I released.

Again.

Again.

Six times.

"What are you watching?" she asked the room.

Three people answered the frame.

Two answered Greg.

Pell said, "The path."

Omi nodded.

"Better."

I had been reduced to instructional furniture...

# Chapter 304: The Late Hold

Pell held too long on purpose.

Omi told her to.

The exercise used a small suspended crate and two support hands.

Pell's partner took the load.

Pell kept her plane anyway.

Five seconds.

Seven.

Her reserve dropped for no useful result.

Then Omi stopped the frame.

"Feel the difference?"

Pell frowned.

"I feel tired."

"Excellent. Now do it correctly."

The second run ended three seconds earlier.

Pell released when the load path left her side.

"That?"

"That," Omi said.

Pell looked at me.

"You do that automatically?"

"Sometimes."

Omi said, "No mythology in my clinic. He has practiced it more."

I liked her again.

The lesson belonged to the exercise, not my mysterious wisdom...

# Chapter 310: The Green Plate

Around it.

# Chapter 312: The Spacer

No new hole in masonry.

No alteration to the old plate.

"Ugly."

"That means ugly for longer."

Red thread won.
