# Chapter 317: The Upper Cut

Blackcut Quarry looked like a mountain had been opened with paperwork.

Terraces stepped down into blue-gray stone.

Timber hoists crossed two levels.

A narrow rail carried block carts toward the dressing sheds.

The quarry engineer was Ressa Cade.

She had white dust in her eyebrows and a notebook tied to her belt with cord.

"Guild Silver?"

"Greg."

"You support wedges. You do not tell cutters where the seam goes."

"Excellent."

The foreman, Jass Ren, pointed at the upper cut.

Three old charged load wedges sat under a suspended stone shelf while workers replaced a timber crib beneath it.

My job was not to hold the shelf.

That would have required a much more impressive Greg.

My job was to isolate one wedge at a time while Ressa checked its contact face and the crib carried the real load.

Plane.

Direction.

Load.

Small enough to matter.

The first wedge passed.

The second had a chipped corner.

Ressa marked it out.

No argument.

Stone had already voted...

# Chapter 318: The Red Cart

The quarry rail had no engine.

Gravity handled downhill.

People handled regret.

Loaded block carts used a brake arm and a drag shoe through the steep section.

One cart was painted red.

"Bad?" I asked.

Jass said, "Training."

Worse.

The red cart carried half load and one new brakeman.

An older rail hand walked beside him calling distances.

"Post."

"Brake."

"Ease."

"Again."

The new man pulled too hard at the second post.

The wheels skidded.

The older hand made him release and reset instead of praising caution.

"Locked wheel doesn't steer."

Useful.

They reached the lower yard without drama.

I had crossed Blackcut later in my first life and remembered powered winches.

Current quarry still taught gravity by painting a cart red...

# Chapter 319: The Warm Wedge

One charged wedge stayed warm after unload.

Not hot.

Wrong.

Ressa noticed because she handled them every day.

I noticed after she handed it to me.

"Residual?"

"Too long."

She set it on a stone bench away from the active rack.

The wedge held no useful load.

Its inner charge still bled slowly toward one face.

Old Greg knew enough to dislike that.

Ressa knew the quarry's repair history.

"Repacked last winter."

"Bad repack?"

"Maybe. Or cracked core."

She did not open it in the yard.

Tagged it.

Sent it to the tool house.

The crew took a spare.

No one waited for diagnosis because production had already budgeted spares.

Redundancy was less exciting than emergency and far more profitable...

# Chapter 320: The Dust Rule

Blue dust meant stop.

I learned this because everyone stopped before I knew why.

A cutter on the east face raised one hand.

Fine blue powder had begun spilling from a hairline seam above his work line.

Not magical.

Not necessarily dangerous.

Enough to mean the seam was opening under cold stress.

Jass cleared the face.

Ressa climbed the side stair and checked the upper terrace.

Nobody shouted.

Nobody ran.

The quarry waited twelve minutes while she measured two marks.

"Cold pull," she said. "Move cut six feet west. Leave this until sun."

The crew moved tools.

A client wagon waiting below lost half an hour.

The driver complained.

Jass pointed at the blue dust on the stone.

"You can have that block now if you stand under it."

The driver found patience...

# Chapter 321: The Dressing Shed

The quarry sold stone by categories I had never needed to know.

Bridge face.

Drain cap.

Foundation rough.

Stair stock.

Decorative second.

Reject.

A block I thought looked excellent became drain cap because one pale seam crossed the back.

"Still strong," I said.

The dresser nodded.

"Strong wrong direction."

That was annoyingly familiar.

She struck chalk lines around the useful cuts.

Nothing wasted immediately.

Offcuts became curb stone.

Thin pieces became channel covers.

The quarry did not measure stone by beauty alone.

Old Greg had walked over thousands of these decisions without storing any of them...

# Chapter 322: The Barracks Stove

Quarry lodging had a stove that worked too well and beds that worked selectively.

My room was hot at shoulder height and cold at floor level.

Boots dried.

Socks became a public issue.

Jass slept in the next room and snored through the wall with industrial consistency.

After four days of stairs between terraces, my left knee felt tired rather than hurt.

I sat closer to the stove and stretched it before bed.

Ressa saw me the next morning taking the lower stair instead of the steeper ladder route.

She said nothing.

Then assigned the tool-house inspection first.

# Chapter 323: The Tool House

The warm wedge had a bad packing ring.

Not cracked core.

The tool keeper opened it on a bench behind a low isolation frame.

Old fiber packing had compressed unevenly and hardened along one edge.

"Last winter?" I asked.

He checked the repair mark.

"Late winter. New apprentice packed it."

Ressa looked at him.

"Supervised?"

"By me."

No convenient blame.

They replaced the ring.

The keeper made the apprentice, now a full tool hand, repack the wedge again under current load test.

She did it cleanly.

"Still apprentice mistake?" I asked Ressa.

"Still our wedge."

Better answer.

The repaired tool cooled normally after three test cycles...

# Chapter 324: The Missing Block

A customer arrived for a bridge-face block that did not exist.

The paperwork did.

Block B-47 had been marked bridge face before dressing.

After the pale back seam appeared, it became drain cap.

The yard ledger changed.

The sales copy had not.

The wagon driver held an order for one block the quarry no longer intended to sell for that use.

Ressa refused to release it.

The customer clerk argued that the contract number matched.

The dresser brought the block map.

Jass brought the yard ledger.

Three records.

One stone.

The quarry offered B-52 instead after a two-hour delay.

The customer took it.

B-47 stayed in the yard.

Material truth beat paperwork by being too heavy to argue with...
