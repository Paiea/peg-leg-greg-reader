# Chapter 305: The Saturday Block

The support clinic had twelve chairs and nine people.

Omi Tress considered this adequate.

She considered most things adequate only after checking them herself.

Three weighted frames stood along one wall.

Two had old repair marks.

One had a new leather sling and a chalk note that said **DO NOT TRUST LEFT BUCKLE**.

I liked the room immediately.

Pell Arin sat in the second row.

She lifted one hand when I came in.

Not a student greeting.

More an acknowledgment that the person she had bothered in the stairwell had actually appeared.

Omi handed me a wooden frame with a twenty-pound sand sack suspended inside it.

"Forty minutes," she said.

"Sella promised forty."

"I promise thirty-eight if you talk too much."

"Good system."

She faced the room.

"Greg. Silver. Barrier support. Low reserve. Clean short release."

A young man in the back asked, "How low?"

"Low enough that wasting it is educational," Omi said.

I objected to none of this because she had already started the clock.

My demonstration was simple.

Plane under the sack.

Omi shifted the sling.

Load moved to the frame.

Release.

Again.

Again.

By the fifth cycle the room had stopped watching my hands.

Omi noticed.

"What changed?"

Pell answered first.

"The path."

Omi nodded.

"Good. Greg is not the lesson."

I had been promoted to equipment.

This was probably healthy...

# Chapter 306: The Bad Hold

My first useful mistake at the clinic was assigned.

Omi told me not to release.

"How long?"

"Until it becomes stupid."

"Technical unit?"

"Widely recognized."

The second frame held a smaller sack between two support points.

Pell took the opposite side.

Omi shifted the load fully onto Pell's sling.

My plane no longer carried anything useful.

I kept it anyway.

Three seconds.

Five.

Seven.

Reserve pulled down for no result.

The plane stayed clean.

That made the waste worse.

Omi tapped the frame.

"There. Stop."

I released.

The young man in back frowned.

"But it didn't fail."

"Correct," Omi said.

"Then why is it bad?"

Pell answered before I did.

"Because his side stopped mattering."

Omi pointed at her.

"Again."

This time Pell held too long.

Her plane wandered before her reserve did.

She swore quietly.

Omi approved of the honesty more than the language.

On the third run Pell released when the useful load left her side.

Nothing dramatic happened.

The sack stayed up.

She looked at me.

"That feels early."

"It is early if you're waiting for failure."

Omi cut in.

"And late if the load already left. Do not turn him into scripture."

I liked her more every minute.

At thirty-eight minutes she stopped the block exactly because I had spent two minutes answering a question about offset planes.

Promises were apparently enforceable...

# Chapter 307: The Demonstration Rate

The Guild paid me two copper for the clinic.

This was less than field work.

It was more than talking for free.

I found the distinction persuasive.

Sella added one line beneath my work note.

**SUPPORT CLINIC / DEMONSTRATION HAND / AS SCHEDULED.**

"That sounds almost official."

"It is official."

"Title?"

"No."

"Rank?"

"No."

"Then why write it?"

"So Omi doesn't have to explain why she wants you next time."

There was the institution again.

Making memory out of paperwork.

"Do I have to go next time?"

"No."

"Better institution."

Omi passed the desk carrying two cracked buckles and heard the last word.

"Next month. Same Saturday."

"Maybe."

"That means no."

Pell came down the stairs behind her.

"I told you."

I had apparently helped teach institutional language after all.

Sella put two copper in my hand and sent me away before the clinic invented another role for me...

# Chapter 308: The Named Request

A warehouse requested me by name.

This felt flattering until Sella read the problem.

Resin-chewer marks in treated hoist rope.

Of course.

The client had heard about the dry-goods loft and the later rope work through Esa Porr.

"Can you take it?" Sella asked.

I was already booked for a mill brace inspection that afternoon.

"No."

She moved one card on the board.

**NERIS HAL.**

Silver.

Wet-country rigging before I was born this time.

"Tell him bitterleaf oil works if the wax is already compromised."

Sella looked at me.

"He knows."

Right.

I went to the mill.

Neris went to the warehouse.

The city continued despite a scheduling catastrophe in which Greg could only be in one place.

Two days later I saw Neris carrying a sealed jar of bitterleaf through the Guild hall.

"How bad?"

"One coil. Four adjacent treated. Client tried to stand under the hoist while we unloaded."

"Learn anything?"

"He learned where I can shout from."

That sounded complete.

The warehouse had requested me.

The warehouse got its rope fixed anyway...

# Chapter 309: The Second Name

The rope warehouse came back three weeks later.

Not because the treatment failed.

They wanted the high rack inspected before spring damp.

The request card said:

**PREFERRED: NERIS HAL IF AVAILABLE.**

I read it twice.

Sella noticed.

"Problem?"

"No."

There was a small, stupid pinch somewhere behind my ribs.

I had declined the first job.

Neris had done it.

The client now wanted the person who had actually shown up.

This was offensively reasonable.

Neris came in ten minutes later eating a pear.

Sella handed him the card.

"Your admirers."

He read it.

"Terrible people."

"They pay on time."

"Excellent people."

He took the job.

I took a stairwell brace inspection on the south side.

By lunch the pinch had become embarrassing enough to stop having.

Jobs could belong to whoever did them.

Apparently this remained true even when I had touched the story first.

I did not write that down...

# Chapter 310: The Wrong Expert

My reputation produced the wrong request next.

A property manager wanted me for a retaining-wall assessment.

The note said:

**SILVER GREG / INFRASTRUCTURE EXPERIENCE.**

Sella read it.

Then crossed out my name.

"Structural Gold," she said.

"Yes."

"Then why did they ask for you?"

"They saw me at East Mill."

"East Mill is not a retaining wall."

"I remain aware."

She assigned Eda Vell, Gold structural assessor.

The client objected to the rate.

Sella explained that asking for a cheaper wrong person did not turn the wall into cheaper work.

The client asked whether I could at least come first and decide if Eda was necessary.

Sella said no.

I agreed with her so completely that it became irritating.

Eda passed us on the way out carrying a plumb line, a leather folder, and no interest in my feelings.

"Wall?" she asked Sella.

"South retaining."

"Old fill?"

"Probably."

"Good."

She left.

I took a warehouse support call instead.

Professional recognition had developed the ability to send me somewhere else.

Useful, if not heroic.

# Chapter 311: The Shelf

My room needed another shelf.

Not urgently.

That was how I knew the problem was real.

The existing shelf held Mara's letters, maintenance cards, three route copies, and two books I had somehow acquired without becoming a scholarly person.

My second map lived rolled beside the bed.

My winter gloves lived on the chair when the chair was not pretending to be furniture.

Maud Pell inspected the wall above the gear alcove.

"Three copper installed."

"Two if I install it."

"Four if you install it and split plaster."

"You have little faith."

"I manage rentals. Faith is not a maintenance category."

I paid three.

Her carpenter put up a narrow shelf in twenty minutes.

I placed the letters there.

Then moved them because steam from the wash basin reached that wall.

The route cards fit instead.

The letters moved to the old shelf.

The rolled map moved off the floor.

Nothing important had happened.

I had simply accumulated enough present to require another board.

Noll came in, looked at the new shelf, and said, "More paper."

"Vertical paper."

"Advanced."

He put dinner on the table the shelf had failed to replace.

# Chapter 312: The Tuesday No

Miko asked Tessa to do the oil count on Tuesday.

Tessa said no.

This surprised him enough that I heard about it at dinner.

"She said no?" Noll asked.

"Her mother needs her at the market stall."

"Did you ask before writing her name?"

Miko looked offended by the question because the answer was visible.

"She's done oil twice."

"That is not a schedule."

"I know that now."

Tessa had offered Thursday instead.

Thursday was also the day Miko wanted her on dry goods.

Delegation had apparently reached the advanced stage where other people possessed calendars.

Noll rubbed both hands over his face.

"What did you do?"

"Moved Jalen to dry goods Thursday. Tessa does oil. I do Tuesday count."

"Fine."

"You sound angry."

"I wanted Tuesday off."

Miko stared at him.

"You didn't tell me."

Noll stared back.

There was a long, beautiful silence in which management became hereditary.

I ate soup.

Nobody asked Greg to solve staffing...

# Chapter 313: The Tuesday Off

Noll took the next Tuesday off.

He informed Miko on Wednesday.

Miko said, "Next Wednesday?"

"This Wednesday."

"For next Tuesday."

"Correct."

Miko wrote it down with unnecessary force.

"Good communication," Noll said.

"I hate you."

"Also clear."

On Tuesday Noll did not go to the Brass Spoon.

This created a second problem.

He had nowhere he particularly wanted to go.

He ended up in my room at noon while I repaired a scabbard strap.

"Day off," I said.

"Apparently."

"Enjoying it?"

"No."

He lay on the floor for twenty minutes and then went to buy onions because the room made him feel unproductive.

He came back with onions and a cheap sweet bun.

"Better?"

"Slightly."

At four he started to stand because the Brass Spoon would be entering supper prep.

Then stopped.

"Miko has it."

"Probably."

"Do not say probably."

He stayed seated.

The restaurant did not send anyone running for him.

At six he looked offended by this success.

# Chapter 314: The River Letter

Mara's next letter came from the river again.

Not the same account.

The bankruptcy recovery had ended.

Vey moved her back because two regular customers needed someone who already knew which packet captains would accept advance payment in winter.

She hated one landing clerk.

Liked another landing's fried fish.

Had learned that Cenn Varo would carry paperwork in the mug rack if no one stopped him.

That caught me.

**You know Cenn?** I wrote.

Then stopped.

Of course she might know Cenn.

The river existed when I was not on it.

I crossed the line out.

My actual reply said:

**I spent six days on the packet inspection and finally learned why some schedules are decorative. If Vey makes you argue with Middle Landing, bring warmer socks.**

I told her about Noll's unsuccessful day off.

She wrote in the margin of her next sheet that this was the most alarming part of the letter.

Different lives kept discovering the same river without needing a meeting...

# Chapter 315: The Five-Copper Morning

The filtration business solved a customer problem without me.

This was not new.

What was new was the bill.

A restaurant returned two chambers because both service dates had been copied one week late from Senn's route sheet.

The ceramics were fine.

The water was fine.

The paperwork was wrong.

Senn found the error.

Dena checked the customer copy.

Ivena approved a five-copper service credit before Arlo arrived.

Pellin drove the replacement pair over himself because his packing bench had spare capacity that morning.

By the time I heard about it, the customer had already accepted the correction.

"Five copper?" Arlo asked.

"Cheaper than losing them," Ivena said.

Senn looked miserable.

"My copy error."

"Yes," Ivena said.

He waited for rescue.

She did not provide it.

"Fix the handoff."

He moved the service-date field beside the customer confirmation line.

No new field.

Dena drew a tiny skull beside the old position before they retired the form.

I had invested in a business that now generated mistakes, credits, and corrective paperwork without consulting me.

This seemed suspiciously like success.

I took my share of the smaller distribution when it came.

# Chapter 316: The Second Pair

Two pairs of work gloves became necessary only because one pair was wet.

I learned this after the river job.

The old pair had dried stiff enough that the fingers felt personally offended.

I bought a second pair for three copper.

Noll found both on the shelf below my map.

"Two."

"Correct."

"Of the same thing."

"One can be wet."

He looked at me carefully.

"You're becoming rich."

"Prepared."

"Worse."

Old Greg had owned enough gear that gloves had not qualified as memory.

Current Greg rotated two pairs near the stove and considered the system excellent.

The second pair cost less than one sword lesson.

I began calculating that relationship.

Then stopped because some knowledge was actively harmful...

# Chapter 317: The Uneven Stair

Lera made me work stairs.

Not sword forms on stairs.

That would have been stupid.

She stood at the bottom of the Guild service stair and told me to go up and down six times without hurrying.

"This is training?"

"This is you pretending your left leg arrives at the same time after long jobs."

I disliked her diagnostic style.

First pass fine.

Second fine.

Fourth, my left foot landed a fraction late on the descent.

No pain.

No buckle.

Just timing.

Lera heard it.

"Again slower."

"I am already walking."

"Then walk better."

I did.

She moved the sword lesson to flat footwork and cut the final sparring block.

I still paid full price.

"You removed work."

"I removed stupid work. Premium service."

At the end she made me climb the stairs once more.

The timing stayed even.

She did not praise it.

I did not need her to.

Outside, I chose the longer street home because it was flatter and immediately resented the wisdom of this choice.

# Chapter 318: The Empty Afternoon

I had nothing scheduled on Thursday afternoon.

This remained true after I checked twice.

No Guild call.

No East Mill access order.

No filtration meeting.

No sword lesson.

No letter requiring an answer before post.

Noll was working.

Jorren was somewhere on city roads.

Mara was on the river.

Arlo was firing chambers.

The world had distributed itself offensively well.

I walked to the market and bought apples.

One seller tried to charge me winter storage price for bruised fruit.

I told him the bruise was not a storage feature.

He told me to buy prettier apples somewhere else.

I bought the bruised ones for one copper less.

At home I cut away the bad spots.

Three apples became two and a half.

I read half a book.

Fell asleep in the chair.

Woke after sunset with the room cold and one sock missing because it had fallen behind the bed.

Nothing had advanced while I slept.

I found the sock anyway.
