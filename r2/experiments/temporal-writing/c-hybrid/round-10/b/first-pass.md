# Chapter 317: The Winter Hat

Noll bought me a hat.

I had not requested a hat.

This was apparently the problem.

"You own two pairs of gloves and no useful hat."

"My current hat is useful."

"Your current hat has a hole above the left ear."

"Ventilation."

He put the new one on my head while I was eating bread.

Dark wool.

Ear flaps.

Too warm indoors.

"How much?"

"Gift."

"That is not a price."

"Correct."

I disliked this accounting system.

The hat stayed.

So did the holey one because throwing away proven equipment remained emotionally advanced...

# Chapter 318: The Cracked Bracket

A stair bracket cracked at the Guild.

Not magical.

Not dramatic.

It held the rail away from the wall on the second-floor turn.

A porter found the split while sweeping.

He tied red cord across the stair.

People used the other side.

A carpenter arrived after lunch.

Removed the bracket.

Replaced it.

I watched for perhaps thirty seconds because I was waiting for Sella to finish a call.

"Support opinion?" the carpenter asked.

"Metal broke."

"Excellent."

He tightened the last screw.

The Guild survived an infrastructure event without a committee.

Sella came out and handed me a warehouse card.

We both left the repaired rail alone.

# Chapter 319: The Stone Sample

Three weeks after the river inspection, a piece of blue-gray stone appeared on the Guild desk.

Not rubble.

Sample.

A contract card sat beneath it.

**BLACKCUT QUARRY / WINTER LIFT SUPPORT / OLD CHARGED WEDGE INSPECTION / SIX DAYS.**

Sella moved the stone because it covered the rate.

"Quarry sends paperweights?"

"Quarry sends stone. Paper happened to be under it."

The rate was good.

Travel and lodging separate.

Silver support required because Blackcut still used several old charged load wedges in the upper cut.

"Engineer?" I asked.

"On site."

"Quarry foreman?"

"On site."

"Who owns load decisions?"

"They do. You own whatever plane they ask you to hold."

Promising.

I turned the sample over.

Fine grain.

Small silver flecks.

No charge.

Old Greg remembered Blackcut as a much deeper quarry with powered winches and three lower terraces.

Current contract said **UPPER CUT**.

That was all I needed to know.

I signed for six days.

The stone stayed on Sella's desk because she refused to let me steal the client's stationery...

# Chapter 320: The Upper Cut

Blackcut looked like someone had opened a mountain in layers.

Blue-gray terraces dropped away beneath the winter sky.

Timber hoists crossed two cuts.

Narrow rails carried block carts toward the dressing sheds.

No powered winches.

Not yet.

The quarry engineer was Ressa Cade.

White dust sat permanently in her eyebrows.

Her notebook was tied to her belt with cord because apparently quarry wind had opinions about records.

The foreman, Jass Ren, shook my hand once.

"Guild Silver?"

"Greg."

"You isolate wedges. You don't tell cutters where stone wants to break."

"Excellent division."

He pointed up.

Three old load wedges sat beneath a suspended shelf while carpenters replaced part of a timber crib.

The crib held the stone.

The wedges controlled small shifts while the new timber seated.

My plane did not hold the shelf.

My plane isolated one charged wedge at a time so Ressa could unload and inspect it without charge crossing into the neighboring tool.

Much smaller job.

Much more believable.

First wedge clean.

Second carried a chipped contact corner.

Ressa marked it out with white paint.

The cutter beside her said, "Still holds."

"Until it doesn't square," she said.

"We have three spares."

"Then why are we arguing?"

He considered this.

"Habit."

The chipped wedge went to the tool house.

Stone workers continued being stone workers without waiting for my opinion...

# Chapter 321: The Red Cart

One quarry cart was painted red.

I assumed danger.

Jass said, "Training."

Worse in a different way.

The rail from upper cut to dressing yard ran downhill.

Gravity provided motive power.

A brake arm and drag shoe provided civilization.

The red cart carried half load and a new brakeman named Ors.

An older rail hand walked beside him.

"Post."

Ors eased the arm.

"More."

He pulled harder.

The wheels locked and skidded two feet.

"Release."

Ors stared at him.

"It stopped."

"Locked wheel doesn't steer."

The older hand made him reset from the previous mark.

No praise for stopping too hard.

No humiliation either.

Again.

This time the cart slowed without locking.

At the bottom Ors looked pleased.

The rail hand said, "Tomorrow full half load."

"That was half load."

"Tomorrow you'll know it."

Old Greg remembered powered quarry winches.

Current Blackcut painted one cart red and taught gravity by repetition.

I stayed out of the lesson because no one had invited me into it.

# Chapter 322: The Warm Wedge

The third wedge stayed warm after unload.

Not hot.

Wrong.

Ressa noticed before I did because she touched these tools every week.

She set it on an empty stone bench.

"Residual too long."

I put two fingers near the face without touching.

Faint charge bled toward one side.

No active load.

"Repacked?"

"Late winter."

Jass looked toward the tool house.

"Cracked core?"

"Maybe."

Nobody opened it in the cut.

Ressa tagged the handle.

Jass pulled a spare from the rack.

The crib crew lost perhaps four minutes.

Old Greg had spent years in places where the wrong warm tool meant people gathered around it until somebody clever produced an answer.

Blackcut had three spares.

Production continued while the question walked downhill to the tool keeper.

At lunch I asked Ressa whether they always carried that many.

"Usually two."

"Why three?"

"One spare is currently chipped."

Right.

Redundancy could also become busy...

# Chapter 323: The Blue Dust

Everyone stopped before I understood why.

A cutter on the east face raised one hand.

Fine blue powder spilled from a hairline seam above his work line.

Not much.

Enough.

Jass cleared the face.

Ressa climbed the side stair and checked two chalk marks from the morning.

The waiting client wagon below immediately developed opinions.

"How long?" the driver called.

Jass ignored him.

Ressa measured again.

"Cold pull. Move cut west six feet. Leave this until sun."

The driver said, "My slot is now."

Jass pointed at the dust line.

"You can have that block now if you stand under it."

The driver discovered flexibility.

Tools moved.

The east face went quiet.

No magic.

No Guild action.

Twelve minutes of professional caution cost the quarry more than my entire morning reserve.

Later I asked the cutter what the blue dust meant exactly.

"Could mean nothing."

"Then why stop?"

"Could mean stone."

He seemed to believe this explained everything.

In context, it did.

# Chapter 324: The Dressing Grade

The dressing shed had six chalk words on one wall.

**BRIDGE FACE**

**DRAIN CAP**

**FOUNDATION ROUGH**

**STAIR STOCK**

**DECORATIVE SECOND**

**REJECT**

A block I thought looked excellent became drain cap.

The dresser, Ilya Renn, chalked a pale seam across its back.

"Strong," I said.

"Wrong direction."

That was irritatingly familiar.

She struck two new cut lines.

The bridge-face section disappeared from the block's future.

Nothing else did.

One slab became drain cap.

Two offcuts became curb stock.

A thin end piece went to channel covers.

"How much is reject actually rejected?"

Ilya looked at the pile outside.

"Depends who asks. Road fill buys half. Lime yard buys fines. Garden people buy anything flat and lie about it being rustic."

"Efficient."

"Annoying."

A sales clerk came in looking for the original bridge-face number.

Ilya pointed at the new chalk grade.

"Tell them it changed."

"They already sold it."

"Then tell them faster."

The clerk left unhappy.

Stone had changed category before paperwork learned about it.

That problem continued downhill...

# Chapter 325: The Barracks Floor

Blackcut lodging had six beds and one stove with territorial ambitions.

My room was hot above the waist and cold below the knee.

Boots dried.

Socks became public information.

Jass slept through one wall and snored with the confidence of heavy industry.

After four days of quarry stairs, my left knee felt tired.

Not hurt.

Not unstable.

Tired.

The next morning I took the longer lower stair instead of the ladder cut.

Ressa noticed.

"Need cart seat?"

"No."

"Need tool house first?"

Different question.

"Yes."

She changed the order on her sheet.

No meeting.

No concern ceremony.

"Greg's old," she said.

"Thirty-three," I said.

"Older than the apprentice."

I stopped defending myself.

# Chapter 326: The Packing Ring

The warm wedge had a bad packing ring.

Not cracked core.

The tool keeper opened it behind a low isolation frame while I held a plane across the active face.

Old fiber packing had compressed unevenly and hardened along one edge.

"Late winter repair?" I asked.

He checked the mark.

"Yes."

A young tool hand beside him went still.

"Mine."

The keeper said, "Supervised by me."

No convenient orphaned blame.

They cut a new ring.

The young hand packed it again.

This time the keeper made her narrate pressure around the circumference as she worked.

"Too much here."

"Why?"

"Trying to make up for the thin side."

"Then fix the thin side."

She pulled the ring and restarted.

Second packing clean.

We cycled the wedge under test load three times.

It cooled normally.

I asked Ressa afterward whether the repair remained the young hand's mistake.

"It remains our wedge."

Good answer.

The young hand heard her and looked annoyed rather than grateful.

Also good.

# Chapter 327: The Missing Block

The bridge-face block from yesterday did not exist anymore.

The customer paperwork disagreed.

Block B-47 had been sold before dressing.

After Ilya found the pale back seam, B-47 became drain cap.

The yard ledger changed.

The sales copy did not.

A wagon arrived with an order for one bridge-face B-47 and a driver who had already lost half a morning.

"Contract says B-47."

Ressa said, "B-47 isn't bridge face."

"It was when we paid."

Ilya brought the chalk map.

The yard clerk brought the revised ledger.

The customer clerk produced the old sale sheet.

Three records.

One enormous piece of stone that did not care.

The customer wanted a discount on B-52 instead.

Sales wanted full rate.

Jass wanted the wagon moved out of the loading lane.

Ressa wanted nobody to put B-47 into a bridge because everyone was tired of talking.

They argued for forty minutes.

I sat on a curb block and ate an apple.

Eventually B-52 left at a smaller discount than the customer wanted and a larger discount than sales wanted.

B-47 stayed.

Nobody achieved principle.

The wagon got stone.

# Chapter 328: The Lower Cut

I remembered Blackcut deeper.

Much deeper.

Current quarry ended at a drainage ditch and a wall of untouched blue-gray stone.

The lower terraces from my first life did not exist.

"Planning down?" I asked Ressa.

She unfolded a current expansion sheet.

Three possibilities.

One lower cut.

Two drainage routes.

A property boundary still under negotiation.

"Which one wins?"

"Money. Water. Stone."

"Helpful."

"Ask in ten years."

I had an answer from later.

Or one answer.

Old Greg had crossed a quarry twice this depth and never cared which decade paid for the hole.

Current Ressa had to care about a ditch, a neighbor's boundary, and where spring runoff would go if they removed thirty feet of rock.

I folded the map back along her existing crease.

"Nothing useful from me."

"Good. Means I don't have to explain the drainage proposal again."

We went back uphill.

The future lower cut remained stubbornly full of mountain.

# Chapter 329: The Sun Face

Sun reached the blue-dust seam on the fifth afternoon.

Ressa had left it alone since the stop.

The crack showed cleaner now.

Smaller than feared.

More definite than yesterday.

The cutters drilled relief holes from the safe side and dropped the loose block onto an empty bench.

No charged wedge.

No Guild plane.

No need for me at all.

I watched from the rail because I was paid until four.

Jass saw me.

"Bored?"

"Professionally."

"You can leave."

"Paid until four."

"Then suffer."

I suffered while stone workers solved stone.

The block came down exactly where they wanted it.

One cutter cheered.

Ilya shouted from the dressing shed that if they cracked another stair blank she would personally grade all of them as road fill.

Celebration ended.

At four I stopped being paid and left immediately.

# Chapter 330: The Blue Chip

Six days produced five inspected wedges.

Three passed without argument.

One chipped wedge left active service.

One bad packing ring returned after repair.

The quarry paid travel, lodging adjustment, and five support units.

Ressa signed the Guild sheet.

"Useful?" I asked.

"You kept charge off tools while we opened them."

"Glowing praise."

"Want poetry, ask sales."

Before I left she handed me the original blue-gray sample.

"Guild doesn't need this back."

"Neither do I."

"Then throw it away."

I put it in my bag.

At home Noll found it beside the map.

"Rock."

"Stone."

"Difference?"

"Six days of people correcting me."

He used it to hold one corner of the map flat while setting dinner down.

Blackcut had six classification categories for stone.

Lower Rook found the seventh.
