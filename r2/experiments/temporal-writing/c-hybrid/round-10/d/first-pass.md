# Chapter 341: The Early Freeze

The freight problem began with three cold nights and one river notice.

Middle Landing reduced late packets.

North road carts picked up the difference.

Then the east ridge closed early after ice.

Nobody called this a crisis because merchants dislike admitting logistics has become weather.

Senn called it Tuesday.

Three filtration deliveries moved two days.

One restaurant wanted its chambers anyway.

One bakery said Friday was fine.

One bathhouse asked whether "winter" counted as force majeure under a service agreement written before anyone had used that phrase in front of Dena.

# Chapter 342: The Yard Slot

Tavin had one open wagon slot north.

He also had six customers who wanted it.

I learned this because he came through Carrow arguing with a yard clerk before breakfast.

Filtration could use the slot.

So could a lamp-oil wholesaler.

So could two flour merchants.

"Friend rate?" I asked.

"Friend information," Tavin said. "Slot costs what it costs."

Fair.

Ivena asked the rate.

Then asked Senn what two delayed routes cost instead.

The delay was cheaper.

We did not buy Tavin's slot.

He sold it to lamp oil before lunch.

Friendship survived commerce by failing to become a discount...

# Chapter 343: The Onion Problem

The Brass Spoon ran short on onions.

Not out.

Short.

Noll treated the distinction as philosophical.

Two sacks had been delayed east of the ridge.

Domm changed three dishes.

Miko bought local leeks at a price Noll called criminal.

Tessa suggested serving fewer onions.

Everyone ignored her until Domm said the same thing louder.

Dinner still happened.

Noll came home smelling like leek soup and betrayal.

"How bad?"

"Customers liked it."

This was apparently the worst possible result...

Better argument.

They settled on one copper after six crates instead of from the first.

# Chapter 345: The Guild Cart

The Guild sent me to Tavin's yard for a freight-hoist support job during the same bad week.

Not for filtration.

A loaded cart had arrived with one warped side brace and needed partial unloading before the brace could be replaced.

Tavin owned the yard sequence.

The freight owner owned the goods.

A wagonwright owned the brace.

I owned one small support plane under a crate stack while two laborers shifted weight.

Filtration's empty chambers sat twenty yards away waiting for a different carrier.

I noticed them.

Tavin noticed me noticing them.

"Not your job."

"I know."

"Good."

He shouted at a laborer who was trying to move the wrong crate first.

Friendship remained available after the hoist was safe...

# Chapter 346: The Inside Information

Tavin asked whether I knew the south packet would reopen before noon.

I did not.

"Guild hear anything?"

"Not before you."

He watched me for a second too long.

"You work with city people now."

"Sometimes."

"Road people."

"Sometimes."

"Water people."

"Unfortunately."

He laughed once.

Then stopped.

"If you hear before the yard does, tell me."

There was the problem.

Useful information could become unfair information before anyone lied.

"If it's public, yes."

"And if it isn't?"

"No."

Tavin looked annoyed.

"Fine."

It was not fine.

We went back to work anyway...

# Chapter 347: The Public Board

The packet reopening notice went up at the Guild and freight exchange at the same hour.

Tavin saw it before I did.

He sent a boy to tell me purely so he could be irritating.

**PUBLIC ENOUGH FOR YOU?**

The note arrived folded around one dried plum.

I ate the plum.

Then sent back:

**BARELY.**

He bought two south slots before lunch.

Filtration got neither.

Senn had already moved those customers to Pera's cart schedule.

The business did not reorganize itself around my friendship fast enough to be inconvenienced...

# Chapter 351: The Freight Argument

Tavin and I finally argued properly over the public-information rule.

Not loudly.

Worse.

He thought I had become precious about information because I now worked around offices.

I thought he wanted friendship to become an unofficial early-warning service.

"I asked you to tell me if you hear something."

"You asked after listing who I work with."

"Because those people know things."

"Exactly."

He rubbed one hand over his face.

"You make simple requests expensive."

"You make expensive requests sound simple."

Neither of us improved.

He left before dinner.

I did not chase him.

The room stayed unpleasantly quiet after the door closed...

# Chapter 354: The Open Ridge

The east ridge reopened fully on a clear morning.

Freight rates did not immediately fall.

Pera still had feed bills.

Tavin still had booked slots.

The Brass Spoon still had too many leeks because Miko had overbought during the shortage.

Filtration still had one empty customer line.

A closed road had reopened.

Everything caused by the closure did not reverse with it.

Noll brought home leek pie.

"Again?"

"We have inventory."

I ate it.

The road stayed open outside without helping dinner.
