# PEG-LEG GREG — OUTWARD ADVENTURE DIRECTION

This file records a strong near-future development push, not plot canon and not a rigid outline.

## Core directional change

Carrow has accumulated enough ordinary life, work, money, relationships, routes, obligations, theatre texture, and social memory that the story can now earn a sustained departure from the city.

The next major structural expansion should increasingly create credible pressure for Greg to leave Carrow for a genuine adventure outside the established normal.

Do not turn the serial into generic quest fantasy. Carrow remains the emotional/home baseline. The value of departure comes from contrast with a place and life the reader already knows.

Preferred broad rhythm:

**NORMAL -> OUTWARD PRESSURE -> DEPARTURE -> TRAVEL / ADVENTURE -> CONSEQUENCE -> RETURN -> CHANGED NORMAL**

The return is part of the payoff. Carrow should continue while Greg is away. Work, relationships, debts, jokes, obligations, jobs, customer problems, theatre business, and other people's lives do not freeze because Greg left.

## How departure should happen

Prefer organic causes already compatible with Greg's life:

- paid escort / retrieval / inspection / delivery work;
- a lucrative but imperfectly explained contract;
- sourcing a difficult material or object;
- salvage, auction, provenance, or acquisition pressure;
- obligation to an existing person or institution;
- curiosity that already has practical value;
- a route Greg partly wants to travel anyway;
- a current city thread pointing outward;
- multiple modest reasons converging until leaving becomes rational.

Avoid a generic guild quest-board reset or sudden wanderlust solely because the plot needs adventure.

It can be useful for Greg to reject an early obvious adventure hook. His eventual departure should feel chosen under his own incentives rather than authorially forced.

## Suspicious opportunity logic

Greg may knowingly accept suspicious work. He should not become stupid so adventure can happen.

When an offer feels wrong, Greg should usually:

1. notice the inconsistency;
2. form a working explanation for what is being concealed;
3. estimate the likely hidden cost;
4. compare that cost against money, curiosity, obligation, access, strategic value, or another real incentive;
5. decide according to his unusually high competence and risk tolerance.

Sometimes his working explanation should be correct.

Sometimes the concealed truth should belong to a category he did not know to price.

A strong adventure door is:

**Greg makes a reasonable bet in a world larger than his model of it.**

Money can be a major lure, but do not make every adventure `mysterious person offers huge purse -> Greg says fuck it`. Vary the hook. "Shiny" may mean rare material, historical object, access, provenance, knowledge, favor, land, workshop opportunity, unusual magical item, or simply permission to investigate something he already finds interesting.

A payment so high that it increases suspicion is also valid.

## Greg does not have to own the myth

Major adventures may place Greg inside a conflict whose emotional, romantic, political, historical, or mythic center belongs to somebody else.

Greg does not need to be:

- the chosen person;
- the romantic prize;
- the primary love interest;
- the heir;
- the prophecy target;
- the person a supernatural being has been waiting for;
- the person whose family conflict actually drives the event.

Useful roles include:

- escort;
- witness;
- mediator;
- hired muscle;
- practical problem-solver;
- traveling companion;
- reluctant confidant;
- accidental accomplice;
- collateral stakeholder;
- older-minded observer who sees emotional disaster developing before the people inside it do.

A particularly promising shape is Greg becoming trapped in the consequences of another person's love story. He may like all sides, have obligations to several of them, recognize attraction before they do, and discover that staying neutral is itself consequential.

The world should sometimes give the feeling:

**Greg has realized he is standing inside somebody else's legend.**

## Travel should enlarge the world

Do not reduce departure to `city -> forest/dungeon -> city`.

Travel can reveal:

- roads and logistics;
- villages, towns, estates, crossings, farms, ports, ruins, and regional centers;
- different economies;
- different foods and material culture;
- accents / speech habits / etiquette;
- different local knowledge of magical ecology;
- places where adventurers are ordinary versus socially significant;
- inconsistent versions of Greg's own reputation;
- regions where Carrow's institutions barely matter;
- local rules whose logic is only partly understood by outsiders.

Reputation should remain networked and inconsistent. Some people may know absurd stories about Peg-Leg Greg. Some may know the old S-class reputation. Some may have never heard of him. Some may know a distorted legend without recognizing nineteen-year-old Greg.

## Power scale beyond adventurer rank

S-class is primarily an adventurer / professional classification. Do not make it a universal ontology.

Creatures, ancient beings, magical hazards, rare shifters, demons if they eventually exist, regional entities, or other races do not need a guild letter attached to them.

Some things may be describable only through practical judgment:

- `do not fight it`;
- `it is territorial, not hostile`;
- `I could probably survive that`;
- `I do not know if I could kill that`;
- `the guild does not issue contracts for those`;
- `leave it alone`.

This allows Greg to remain legitimately formidable without making the world terminate at his power level.

## Rare shapeshifters

Shapeshifting should stay unusual enough to matter.

Do not populate every tavern with obvious animal-people or make dramatic transformation a routine combat gimmick.

Possible gradation:

- lesser / bounded transformations, glamours, disguises, partial changes, or socially known shifting may exist more broadly if the setting earns them;
- true large-scale transformation or beings with radically different human / monstrous forms should remain rare;
- an entity capable of becoming something enormous should feel like an event, even if it is neutral and even if no fight occurs.

A rare shifter does not have to reveal itself quickly. A person may live, work, argue, trade, have neighbors, and participate in ordinary life while only small inconsistencies suggest something stranger.

Mystery is more valuable than taxonomy.

## Magical encounters should not all be fights

The existing magical-ecology direction remains authoritative.

Adventure can involve:

- navigation;
- observation;
- retrieval;
- protection;
- waiting;
- bargaining;
- local prohibitions;
- habitat conflict;
- weather / landscape effects;
- consequences of another person's supernatural conflict;
- choosing not to interfere;
- recognizing that a frightening thing is neutral;
- realizing that a beautiful or human-looking thing is dangerous;
- surviving proximity to power rather than defeating it.

Some apparently supernatural mysteries should still have mundane explanations. If every rumor is literally true, the world becomes predictable in a different way.

## Near-edge seeding

Do not force immediate departure in the next chapter solely because this file exists.

Instead, begin allowing outward pressure to accumulate through the current manuscript:

- mention a lucrative outside job;
- introduce a person with business beyond Carrow;
- let an object or material point outward;
- let a traveler bring a strange story or provenance problem;
- establish a route Greg has reason to know;
- let somebody else prepare to travel;
- have Greg dismiss or decline one possibility;
- let a later opportunity overlap with existing wants strongly enough that leaving becomes natural.

The story is ready to expand its physical range. It does not need to abandon the ordinary life that made that expansion meaningful.
