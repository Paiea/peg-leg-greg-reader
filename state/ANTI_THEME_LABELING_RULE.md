# ANTI THEME LABELING RULE

Purpose: keep thematic intelligence in scene construction instead of turning Greg into a commentator on the manuscript's themes.

## Core rule

If a scene already demonstrates the idea, do not make Greg name the idea afterward.

PLG is strongest when theme arrives as residue from choices, work, money, relationships, body, and repeated social behavior.

Avoid turning strong scenes into essays by adding explanatory conclusions such as:
- `This was growth.`
- `That was independence.`
- `This was economic progression too.`
- `Other people had lives.`
- `That was the lesson.`
- `Apparently wanting had become...`

Greg may make conclusions. He is analytical. But the conclusion should usually be specific to the immediate problem rather than a clean statement of the chapter's thematic function.

## Better replacements

Prefer:
- a changed choice later;
- a callback used differently;
- a physical action;
- another person's reaction;
- an unfinished thought;
- a practical note that reveals the changed thinking without naming it;
- silence.

## Theme density

A chapter may contain one especially clean thematic sentence when it genuinely belongs to Greg's voice. Do not stack several.

When a line sounds quotable because it perfectly summarizes what the chapter was about, inspect it aggressively.

Sometimes the best line is the one before it.

## Test

After drafting, identify the strongest scene beat. Search the next 1-3 paragraphs for explanation of why that beat mattered. Cut that explanation first and reread.
