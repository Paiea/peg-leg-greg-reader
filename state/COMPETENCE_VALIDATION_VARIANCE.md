# COMPETENCE VALIDATION VARIANCE

Durable characterization guardrail. Not canon.

## Problem
Recent chapters often end a task with a specialist validating Greg's restraint or competence: he read correctly, stopped correctly, followed procedure, asked the right question, or accepted a boundary.

This is satisfying in moderation. Repeated too often, it turns secondary experts into judges who certify the protagonist.

## Rule
Greg does not need external confirmation every time he behaves competently.

Possible outcomes:
- nobody comments because the work was ordinary;
- the specialist is too busy to praise him;
- Greg is corrected on one thing and useful on another;
- the result is ambiguous;
- someone is unimpressed;
- someone appreciates him for a reason unrelated to competence;
- Greg finishes and leaves;
- the work matters later without anybody naming why;
- Greg makes a small mistake that does not erase overall capability.

## Praise discipline
When praise occurs, vary its form and source. Avoid repeatedly packaging approval as a crisp professional assessment that confirms the backend rule the chapter was following.

A person can simply hire Greg again later.
A chair can already be set out for him.
A worker can hand him the next tool without hesitation.
A friend can assume he'll understand.
Behavioral trust often says more than `you did exactly what you were hired to do.`

## Test
If a scene's final professional beat exists mainly to tell the reader that Greg was appropriately bounded, delete it and see whether the scene already proves the point.
