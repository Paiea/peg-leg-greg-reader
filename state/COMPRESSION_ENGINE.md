# PEG-LEG GREG — COMPRESSION ENGINE

Durable structural-editing guidance for making long manuscript stretches denser without flattening the book.

This is not canon. It does not replace exact prose, current manuscript state, character voice files, or the active prose editor. It governs a dedicated structural-compression mode only.

## Core principle

**CUT REPETITION, NOT QUIETNESS.**

The target is not fewer chapters or fewer words for their own sake.

The target is **fewer repeated dramatic functions** and **more life per word**.

A quiet chapter may fully earn its space. A busy chapter may be redundant. Six chapters may remain six if each creates a new state. Six may become three if they repeatedly prove the same thing with different props.

## When this engine is allowed to run

Use structural compression only when the author explicitly requests it or invokes the compression prompt/workflow.

Do not run structural compression on manuscript ranges still being actively rewritten by the current dialogue/voice/prose editor unless a clear frozen checkpoint has been established first.

Ordinary forward drafting, light prose passes, and heavy prose passes do not gain permission from this file to delete, merge, relocate, or renumber chapters.

## Compression classifications

Every chapter or substantial scene in a compression batch receives one primary recommendation.

### KEEP

The material performs a distinct function that still earns full scene space.

Common reasons:
- first meaningful relationship state;
- new social memory or belonging beat;
- distinct world behavior or location texture;
- consequential economic choice;
- first or meaningful body/mobility change;
- consequential magic evidence;
- meaningful choice Greg could not have made earlier;
- setup that later pays off;
- unique comedy, intimacy, grief, leisure, embarrassment, or ordinary-life texture that loses its value if summarized;
- new role, person, workplace, route, object, institution, or social network becoming legible.

KEEP does not mean the prose is untouchable. It may still be tightened under the active prose/editor rules.

### TIGHTEN

The scene earns its existence but contains unnecessary internal repetition.

Targets may include:
- repeated setup;
- repeated body checks;
- repeated procedure;
- dialogue followed by narration explaining the same point;
- repeated inference restatement;
- cadence bloat;
- repeated stakes already established nearby.

Preserve outcome, relationship state, scene intent, and useful texture while reducing duplicated work.

### SUMMARIZE-IN-SCENE

The underlying time/event matters, but full dramatization no longer earns its length.

Preserve at least one concrete lived detail and compress the rest into a bridge, montage, remembered line, work rhythm, or transition inside a surviving chapter.

Do not replace lived material with encyclopedia summary.

### MERGE

Two or more neighboring chapters/scenes perform overlapping dramatic functions and can become one stronger unit without losing distinct residue.

Preserve the best concrete moments from each source. Remove duplicated setup, duplicated conclusions, repeated procedural architecture, and redundant explanation.

The merged unit must still preserve causality and later dependencies.

### CUT

The material performs no unique surviving function after neighboring material is considered, or its function is already better performed elsewhere.

CUT is the highest dependency-risk classification. It requires explicit downstream review before execution.

## Functional-value test

Before compressing a chapter or scene, identify what new function it contributes.

A scene may earn space through:
- relationship change;
- social memory;
- a joke/history that later persists;
- new information;
- changed evidence state;
- changed money/material state;
- changed body state;
- changed magic state;
- changed role or reputation;
- changed world understanding;
- changed emotional language;
- changed obligation;
- changed choice architecture;
- distinct atmosphere or leisure that deepens belonging;
- distinct ordinary-life texture not already shown;
- setup/payoff dependency;
- irreversible or memorable consequence.

If its only surviving function is another demonstration that Greg is careful, poor, analytical, physically limited, attracted to useful junk, increasingly accepted, under threat, evidence-disciplined, or reluctant to overclaim, the material is a compression candidate unless it adds a new consequence, relationship state, or later residue.

## High-value repetition targets

Aggressively inspect for:
- repeated procedural setup after the procedure is established;
- repeated magic setup where only the changed variable matters;
- repeated purse/debt arithmetic that does not alter a decision;
- repeated demonstrations that Greg does not overclaim after restraint is already obvious;
- repeated shopping deliberations with the same outcome and emotional function;
- repeated body inventories where nothing materially changed;
- repeated threat warnings without new consequence;
- repeated work scenes proving the same competence in the same way;
- repeated explanations of a relationship state the reader already understands;
- dialogue followed by interior narration restating the implication;
- neighboring chapters ending on essentially the same realization;
- repeated reminders that Greg is not an expert;
- repeated reminders that ordinary life matters;
- repeated setup for a payoff already delivered;
- arcs using five or six full scenes where three would carry the same causal and emotional movement.

## Protected material

Be conservative around:
- first meaningful scenes between recurring characters;
- relationship transitions;
- later callbacks and running jokes;
- exact magic evidence/count changes;
- money/debt changes affecting later choices;
- purchases, losses, repairs, and object provenance that matter later;
- injuries and recovery affecting later physical behavior;
- disability-specific functional changes;
- location introductions that establish later shorthand;
- promises, obligations, referrals, contracts, debts, favors, permissions;
- reputation events that later travel;
- theatre history that creates later group memory;
- emotional scenes whose power depends on being lived rather than summarized;
- Greg choices that demonstrate a changed self rather than a repeated trait.

Quietness alone is never a reason to cut.

## Anti-summary-sludge rule

Compression must not turn PLG into plot synopsis.

Bad:

> Greg spent several weeks working at the theatre and grew closer to the company.

Better structural behavior:
- keep the strongest concrete scene;
- preserve one specific sign of accumulated time;
- bridge only the repeated remainder;
- let later shorthand prove that time passed.

A surviving bridge should contain lived evidence: a prop-room key everyone now assumes Greg has, a joke no longer needing explanation, a routine handoff, a person who stops asking permission, an argument that has become familiar, a route Greg no longer thinks about.

The principle is **more life per word**, not less life.

## Scene-survival hierarchy

When several scenes perform the same broad function, prefer preserving the scene with the most:

1. strong character interaction;
2. future residue;
3. distinctive physical/world texture;
4. funny or emotionally specific detail;
5. causal consequence;
6. setup/payoff connection;
7. representative ordinary-life texture;
8. prose/voice potential after editing.

Do not automatically preserve the earliest or latest version.

## Strength levels

### CONSERVATIVE

Mostly TIGHTEN.

MERGE or CUT only obvious duplicate functions with low dependency risk.

Use when the range is beloved, continuity-dense, or insufficiently understood.

### MODERATE

Default.

Will merge repeated scene functions, summarize repeated procedure, and cut clearly redundant material while preserving lived-in texture and downstream residue.

### AGGRESSIVE

Only with explicit author request.

May substantially reduce scene/chapter count where long stretches repeatedly perform the same movement.

AGGRESSIVE still cannot casually alter canon outcomes, relationships, money, magic evidence, injuries, promises, or later causal facts.

## Dependency rule

Before executing MERGE or CUT, inspect downstream manuscript for dependencies involving:
- dialogue references;
- callback jokes;
- relationship memory;
- object ownership/provenance;
- money balance changes;
- magic evidence/counts;
- injury/fatigue state;
- job/referral consequences;
- promises/obligations;
- route/location familiarity;
- later reputation;
- illustration/public-reader references when relevant.

A later dependency does not force the full original scene to survive. It forces the causal fact and earned residue to survive in a stronger form.

## Compression math is not the goal

Do not set arbitrary targets such as:
- cut 25 percent;
- reduce every ten chapters to seven;
- remove all chapters under a certain plot score;
- force a fixed words-per-chapter target.

A successful pass may cut 5 percent or 40 percent depending on what the range actually contains.

## Success test

The compressed range should:
- move faster without feeling hurried;
- preserve Greg's voice;
- preserve social accumulation;
- preserve enough ordinary life that later contrast still works;
- make unique mundane scenes more visible because repeated ones are gone;
- preserve causal continuity and later callbacks;
- reduce repeated explanation and reenacted procedure;
- make chapter-to-chapter movement easier to feel;
- sound like PLG, not a synopsis of PLG;
- retain moments a reader would actually miss if removed.

Primary question:

**Does the compressed version make the world feel denser, not thinner?**

## Failure modes

Reject compression that:
- removes quiet scenes merely because they are quiet;
- preserves only plot-critical material;
- summarizes intimacy or belonging into abstraction;
- erases minor recurring people who make places socially legible;
- deletes the first occurrence but keeps weaker callbacks;
- makes economic or physical progression jump unnaturally;
- causes later jokes/relationships to feel unearned;
- compresses every work sequence into the same montage voice;
- converts uncertainty into certainty;
- changes money, magic, injuries, promises, or outcomes for convenience;
- flattens distinct characters into generic functional dialogue;
- makes the manuscript read like an outline;
- optimizes solely for word count or chapter count.

## Default human-facing invocation

`Run PLG compression map for Chapters [START]-[END] at moderate strength from current GitHub authority.`

That shorthand means: read current authority, map first, protect dependencies, do not rewrite prose yet, and stop for author approval before structural execution.
