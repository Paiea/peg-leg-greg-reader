# PEG-LEG GREG — DEPARTURE CONVERGENCE GATE

Non-canon Story Control guidance for deciding when outward pressure has accumulated enough that Greg should actually leave Carrow.

The story should not remain in permanent setup once departure is earned. It also should not launch because a development file exists.

## Departure is earned when several of these converge

- Greg has a concrete reason to go, not abstract wanderlust.
- The reward / obligation / curiosity / access is specific enough to matter.
- The destination or route cannot be substituted with an equally easy Carrow scene.
- At least one person besides Greg has independent stakes in the trip.
- The job / opportunity creates a question the reader can understand before all hidden layers are revealed.
- Greg has enough information to make a rational yes/no decision by his standards.
- Leaving creates real household / work / theatre / money / relationship consequences rather than pausing them.
- The route offers meaningful world expansion instead of merely a different backdrop.

No exact count is required. Use judgment.

## Useful sequence

A clean departure may develop as:

1. **HORIZON:** another place / route / person / object becomes relevant.
2. **HOOK:** a practical opportunity appears.
3. **FRICTION:** Greg notices a hidden cost, scheduling problem, relationship issue, money question, body/logistics issue, or incomplete explanation.
4. **CONVERGENCE:** another reason makes the same trip more worthwhile or harder to ignore.
5. **DECISION:** Greg says yes for reasons visible in prose.
6. **PREPARATION:** ordinary arrangements prove he is actually leaving.
7. **DEPARTURE:** the familiar city physically falls behind.

These do not need one chapter each.

## Preparation matters

Do not teleport from contract acceptance to wilderness.

Potential lived preparation:
- telling Lyssa / household;
- negotiating timing or payment;
- deciding what Greg can physically carry with two crutches;
- food / lodging / transport;
- clothing / weather;
- route knowledge;
- whether his existing bag is enough;
- arranging missed theatre or paid work if relevant;
- someone else handling an obligation while he is gone;
- Greg refusing to carry useless adventure junk;
- discovering that one mundane preparation is more annoying than the dramatic threat.

Preparation should not become a gear checklist. Use only what creates life, character, constraint, or later residue.

## Greg may decline

A credible offer can be refused.

Refusal is especially useful when:
- payment is suspiciously high with no plausible explanation;
- the hidden risk obviously exceeds Greg's appetite;
- timing collides with something he actually values;
- somebody is trying too hard to prevent questions;
- the client wants a kind of loyalty Greg did not agree to sell;
- the job is boring enough that money alone does not overcome the inconvenience.

A declined offer may still widen the horizon or return later in altered form.

## Do not fake convergence

Avoid:
- five unrelated hooks appearing in one chapter;
- everyone suddenly telling Greg he needs to travel;
- an arbitrary emergency forcing him out immediately because gradual setup felt slow;
- a client offering absurd money solely to override characterization;
- Greg accepting because `adventurers adventure`;
- retroactively claiming old threads pointed outward when prose did not establish that.

## End-state test

When Greg finally leaves, the reader should feel:

**Of course he is going. This is a real change, but the reasons grew out of the life he already had.**
