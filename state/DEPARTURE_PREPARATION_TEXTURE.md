# PEG-LEG GREG — DEPARTURE PREPARATION TEXTURE

Development guidance for the first sustained outward adventure. Non-canon until prose establishes specifics.

## Core principle

Preparation should make departure feel real without turning into a gear inventory chapter.

The reader should feel that Greg is disrupting an accumulated life in Carrow, not stepping through a quest portal.

Use preparation to expose character, cost, relationships, and practical constraints.

## Useful preparation pressures

Potential beats include:
- telling Lyssa where he is going and what he actually knows;
- discovering what Greg has not asked the client yet;
- advance payment and who physically holds it;
- travel food and lodging assumptions;
- route timing and missed work;
- what Greg can carry comfortably while using two crutches;
- weather clothing, spare wrappings, ordinary repair supplies, and bag limits;
- deciding what magical or artifact objects are inappropriate to bring because their safe use remains unresolved;
- leaving household obligations to continue without him;
- somebody assuming he needs stereotypical adventuring equipment that Greg considers useless;
- one ordinary inconvenience becoming more annoying than the dangerous part of the trip.

## Preserve body reality

Greg's body should matter through practical travel design, not through inspiration framing.

He may think about:
- long-day shoulder load;
- surfaces that are tolerable in Carrow but miserable over distance;
- wet ground;
- wagon entry / exit;
- how much he can carry without sabotaging his own mobility;
- whether transport is worth paying for;
- stopping before fatigue becomes stupidity.

His first-life experience means he should be good at this. Competence does not erase constraint.

## No adventurer cosplay

Avoid scenes where Greg suddenly assembles a symbolic fantasy loadout because the story has declared an Adventure Arc.

Prefer ordinary choices shaped by old experience:
- less equipment rather than more;
- repairable clothing;
- food he will actually eat;
- money separated sensibly;
- one useful knife/tool if canon supports it;
- refusing decorative or redundant gear;
- asking who is carrying shared supplies before agreeing to carry them himself.

## Household consequence

Leaving Carrow should alter the household even before anything dangerous happens.

Someone may need to:
- cover an errand;
- receive a message;
- answer a door;
- handle a payment;
- explain Greg's absence;
- continue work without him;
- decide something he would normally witness.

Do not manufacture melodrama. The point is simple: Greg's life continues to have weight when he is absent.

## Success condition

Preparation has done enough when the reader thinks:

**He is actually leaving.**

Then leave.

Do not spend five chapters packing because the process itself became comfortable.
