Status: ACTIVE EDITORIAL SUPPLEMENT

Exact manuscript prose remains authority. Existing voice pages remain the baseline authority for recurring character cognition, vocabulary, relationships, and established speech behavior. This layer exists to catch a narrower failure mode that can survive a correct line-by-line voice review:

**different characters can think differently while still performing dialogue in the same short, dry, optimized rhythm.**

Use this layer whenever dialogue variance is reviewed, especially in scenes with Greg plus one or more competent recurring speakers.

## Update 1: comparative performance fingerprints

A voice page answers what a person notices, values, knows, and tends to say. A performance fingerprint answers how that person typically behaves inside a live exchange relative to the other people present.

For important recurring speakers, review these axes when useful:

- compression level;
- willingness to extend a joke or argument;
- comfort with silence;
- usual explanation trigger;
- tendency to redirect into work or action;
- tolerance for unresolved disagreement;
- social messiness under low pressure;
- degradation under fatigue, embarrassment, fear, attraction, irritation, or cognitive load;
- whether the speaker usually finishes the conversational bit or leaves it unfinished.

These are relative tendencies, not numeric quotas and not permanent settings.

The main diagnostic is comparative:

**If two characters are both allowed to be short and dry, what makes their next move different?**

Examples:

- Greg often extends the wording or finds the shortest comic reduction.
- Antonius often ends the bit by returning to the decision, ledger, borrower, object, or work consequence.
- Arlo often returns to the bench, test, material, tool, time cost, or ownership boundary.
- Hessa often ends the bit by restoring the procedural question, stopping condition, evidence boundary, or silence.
- Jorren often lets the body, spar, road, fatigue, or physical task answer instead of continuing the argument.
- Alden is unusually allowed to keep up with Greg, but his speed should usually push toward initiative, testing, opportunity, or the next usable action.

See `state/voices/EARLY_BOOK_I_PERFORMANCE_MATRIX.md` for the first calibration matrix.

## Update 2: voice-evidence quality labels

When voice authority is created or revised from manuscript evidence, classify important evidence mentally or explicitly with one of these labels.

### CORE

A durable character-defining behavior worth reinforcing across contexts.

Examples include Antonius returning to repayment reality, Arlo protecting test integrity, Hessa separating permissions, Jorren trusting physical evidence, Alden taking initiative, and Greg extending wording into compressed comic structure.

### CONTEXTUAL

Valid and useful in a particular relationship, job, mood, or scene pressure, but not a universal voice rule.

Examples include Antonius being especially dry with Greg, Jorren teasing during sparring, or Alden matching Greg quickly during competitive play.

### SHARED HOUSE REGISTER

A line shape, curse, colloquialism, or dry Carrow rhythm that can belong to several people but should not be treated as identity evidence for any one speaker.

Shared register is allowed. It simply carries less weight when teaching future dialogue.

### CONTAMINATION WATCH

A repeated manuscript habit that may be readable and even funny but should not be reinforced as a fingerprint because it contributes to convergence.

Common candidates:

- predictive `No` ladders;
- multiple speakers reading Greg's face, breath, or hidden sentence as a comedy setup;
- `you problem` style transferable punchlines;
- every specialist turning a boundary into a polished aphorism;
- incidental strangers arriving fully calibrated to Greg;
- two or more supporting speakers repeatedly finishing Greg's joke architecture for him.

A CONTAMINATION WATCH label does not automatically require deletion. It means future editing should stop using the pattern as evidence that this is simply how the character talks.

## Update 3: exchange rhythm ownership gate

After local line-level voice review, perform one exchange-level pass.

Ask:

1. Who owns the rhythm of this exchange?
2. Who initiates the comic or argumentative pattern?
3. Who extends it?
4. Who exits it, redirects it, misses it, refuses it, or lets action replace another line?
5. Are two or more supporting speakers using the same response architecture in succession?
6. Would swapping several supporting lines leave the social texture mostly unchanged?

### Collision trigger

Review an exchange more closely when any of the following occurs:

- three or more consecutive turns are similarly compressed counters;
- Greg sets up a dry bit and multiple other speakers each improve or finish it;
- two unrelated professionals both manage Greg through the same predictive joke pattern;
- a supporting character repeatedly earns the cleanest final button even though their domain would naturally let them exit through work, silence, action, uncertainty, or a longer explanation;
- the same joke architecture appears in several neighboring scenes with different supporting characters.

### Repair preference

Do not make the character artificially verbose merely to create contrast.

Prefer changing the **mode** of the response:

- silence;
- work action;
- domain-specific instruction;
- partial answer;
- direct boundary;
- misunderstanding;
- delayed reaction;
- longer explanation because the variable actually matters;
- refusal to continue the joke;
- emotionally imperfect answer;
- letting the physical result end the argument.

Keep good short lines when they are earned. The target is asymmetry across an exchange, not maximal difference on every turn.

## Update 4: cross-chapter reader-ear audit

Line review can miss cumulative rhythm. Every completed dialogue-variance run should therefore include a continuous reader-ear audit after a useful span, normally 10 to 20 chapters.

This audit is deliberately different from the patch pass.

Read continuously and ignore, for a moment, whether each individual line is defensible by its voice guide. Listen instead for repeated machinery.

Record:

- which characters begin to feel interchangeable in cadence;
- repeated punchline shapes;
- repeated predictive `No` or face-reading structures;
- whether specialists keep exiting through their own work or keep becoming comedians;
- whether Greg still feels like the gravitational center of compressed comic reduction;
- whether current state and relationship meaningfully bend speech;
- whether incidental strangers sound too socially optimized;
- whether any character has become a gimmick after being differentiated too aggressively.

A reader-ear audit may return **NO CHANGE**. Do not manufacture patches to satisfy the audit.

When it does find a problem, prefer the smallest number of high-leverage exchange repairs over rewriting every instance of a shared word or sentence length.

## Update 5: early Book I calibration and intervention thresholds

The first calibration sample is the live illustrated reader's visible Chapters 1 through 20, whose source canon currently reaches through canon Chapter 26 because whole chapters are curated out of the Showcase.

See `state/editorial/dialogue-variance-pass/PERFORMANCE_CALIBRATION_VISIBLE_001_020.md`.

The sample establishes these intervention thresholds:

### Leave alone

- two characters are both terse, but their responsibility and exit behavior remain different;
- a specialist gives a short answer because the work genuinely only needs a short answer;
- Hessa uses a short safety refusal;
- Jorren gives a short physical correction;
- Antonius gives a short operational refusal;
- Alden matches Greg for a few turns because competitive initiative is part of their relationship;
- a one-scene worker is briefly funny but does not become a polished sparring partner.

### Review

- the same supporting character wins several consecutive dry volleys;
- two or more different supporting characters use the same Greg-management machinery within a short span;
- an incidental character understands Greg's hidden intention faster than the scene has earned;
- a specialist's domain could naturally end the exchange, but the prose repeatedly chooses another polished comeback instead;
- a chapter contains several individually valid dry exchanges whose aggregate cadence makes the cast feel uniform.

### Patch

Patch only when the reader-ear problem is noticeable at normal reading speed and a smaller mode change can preserve intent, canon, relationship, humor, and pace.

## Relationship to attribution work

Performance variance does not replace attribution clarity.

A voice can be distinct and still need a tag. A line can be perfectly attributed and still contribute to tonal convergence.

Continue to use the aggressive ownership rules in `state/DIALOGUE_VARIANCE_ENGINE.md` for speaker, action, reaction, addressee, pronoun, interruption, and paragraph clarity.

## Non-goals

Do not:

- make every isolated line pass a blind speaker-identification test;
- assign accents or caricature speech;
- force every character to have a gimmick;
- lengthen supporting dialogue globally;
- remove all supporting-character wit;
- make Greg the only funny person;
- treat a shared Carrow register as a defect by itself;
- reopen every completed batch merely because this layer now exists;
- use this layer as permission for unrelated structural rewriting.

The target remains:

**consistent person, variable performance, asymmetric exchange.**
