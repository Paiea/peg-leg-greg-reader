# PEG-LEG GREG — EDITORIAL INVISIBILITY RULES

Durable craft guardrail. This file is not canon.

## Core rule

The manuscript may obey backend logic. It must not narrate that it is obeying backend logic.

Story-control intelligence belongs backstage.

If a scene demonstrates a rule successfully, do not add narration explaining that the rule was successfully demonstrated.

## Forbidden leakage patterns

Avoid prose that sounds like commentary from an editor, state file, engine, workshop, or model evaluation layer.

Common failure forms include:

- `Not because X had plot relevance.`
- `Not that I thought in chapters.`
- `No chapter-ending applause.`
- `No conspiracy appeared.`
- `Growth continued.`
- `Other people had lives.`
- `Not my project.` when the scene already proves Greg is leaving it alone.
- narration announcing that somebody is not an exposition machine, not a quest giver, not a plot device, not a romantic center, or not a mystery.
- narration explaining that a trope has been played straight or subverted.
- narration signaling that a scene avoided escalation, overclaiming, protagonist centrality, procedural contamination, or another known backend failure mode.

These ideas can remain true. Their truth should be visible through behavior, scene structure, and consequence.

## Demonstrate, do not certify

If Senna is drawing a dog and continues her own activity while Greg happens to stop nearby, that already shows independent life. Do not append a sentence stating she is not there for plot.

If a tag mismatch remains mundane, let it remain mundane. Do not mention conspiracy avoidance.

If Greg follows a specialist's instruction and leaves diagnosis to them, that already shows bounded competence. Do not add a sentence explaining that he is not the investigator or expert.

If Lyssa makes a decision with her own money, let the scene carry her independence. Do not repeatedly narrate that her money is not Greg's ledger after the boundary is already established.

If Tavi's speech differs from Greg's, let the speech itself prove the difference. Never narrate the dialogue-variance engine.

## Greg may be self-aware, but not engine-aware

Greg can be funny about patterns in his own life. He can think in comparisons, habits, bad decisions, recurring social dynamics, and absurd personal categories.

He should not sound as if he has access to chapter architecture, editorial workshops, anti-pattern files, character-function labels, or production rules.

Meta humor is allowed only when it arises naturally from Greg's established worldview, not because the backend wants to reassure itself that a craft rule is being followed.

## Cleanup test

After drafting, search mentally for sentences that answer any of these questions:

- `Did we avoid a conspiracy?`
- `Did we prove this person has a life?`
- `Did we show growth?`
- `Did we avoid making Greg the center?`
- `Did we keep this bounded?`
- `Did we diversify dialogue?`
- `Did we avoid a trope mistake?`

If the prose contains an explicit answer, cut or rewrite the sentence unless Greg would have thought it for an independent in-story reason.

The reader should experience the craft result, not receive the editor's receipt.
