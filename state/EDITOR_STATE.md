## CRAFT UPDATES

### Greg's voice

Preserve:

- analytical attention;
- tangents and odd associations;
- uncertainty and self-correction;
- dry humor and natural profanity;
- physical awareness;
- occasional overthinking;
- nineteen-year-old embodiment carrying older-life memory;
- clipped or irregular pacing when intentional.

Do not polish Greg into a cleaner, more literary, more emotionally articulate, more omniscient, or more conventionally heroic narrator.

### Thinking on the page

Greg's natural operating pattern is:

**observation → hypothesis → test → contradiction → revision → another test**

He should distinguish what he **knows**, what he **thinks**, and what he can **check** without the prose turning this into a lesson for the reader.

Future memory is lived, associative, and incomplete. Confidence is not evidence. Preserve the feeling that Greg sometimes remembers significance, texture, consequence, or identity more reliably than exact sequence, date, or mechanism.

### Paragraph balance

Use fuller connected paragraphs as the normal baseline, especially where production drafts fragment prose mechanically. Preserve deliberate one-line beats, dialogue, clipped pivots, and Greg's short internal corrections when they create rhythm or emphasis.

Do not reconnect paragraphs merely to make pages look denser.

### Dialogue attribution

Add attribution or action beats only when the speaker could genuinely be unclear. Do not tag every line. Let established two-person exchanges breathe.

### Repetition

Remove accidental adjacent duplicates, duplicated anchor paragraphs, repeated production beats, and obvious assembly echoes. Preserve intentional rhetorical repetition and Greg's recursive thinking when it is doing character work.

### Meta / production leakage

Remove drafting-language or authorial-process phrasing that accidentally enters narration, especially lines that explicitly discuss:

- chapter construction;
- narrative convenience;
- plot / performance plotting;
- character development;
- metaphor as authorial technique;
- production notes or naming warnings.

Do not remove Greg's genuine self-aware humor merely because it is meta-adjacent. The test is whether the line sounds like Greg thinking inside the world or the author/editor talking about the book.

### Punctuation / em dashes

Avoid introducing em dashes as a default stylistic tool. Where an em dash creates a reader stumble or feels unlike the surrounding prose, prefer context-appropriate commas, periods, parentheses, or sentence restructuring. Do not mechanically rewrite older prose solely to purge punctuation.

### Physical prose

Keep physical systems concrete: grip, load, footing, pressure, route, furniture, tools, surfaces, fatigue, swelling, body-map error, recovery interval, and movement tradeoffs.

Post-amputation competence should coexist with cost. Do not erase mobility constraints, and do not exaggerate them into constant drama.

### Scene execution

Greg often enters a functioning system, observes it, asks questions, forms a model, gets limited or corrected, and updates. This is a character engine, not automatically a repetitive flaw.

Keep it fresh through:

- physical action;
- different kinds of systems;
- independent secondary characters;
- Greg sometimes being wrong longer;
- Greg not automatically owning the problem;
- environments continuing to exert pressure after introduction.

Object-centered and work-centered scenes are a strength. Protect them.

## ESTABLISHED VOICE / CHARACTERIZATION OBSERVATIONS

- Greg is strongest when watching specific things: hands, feet, tools, routes, loads, material behavior, tells, workflow, decisions.
- Abstract explanation weakens him when it replaces the physical system rather than emerging from it.
- Humor often works best when Greg's analytical process collides with bodily inconvenience, ordinary social life, or something embarrassingly nineteen-year-old.
- Greg's self-awareness is often funniest and most believable one beat late.
