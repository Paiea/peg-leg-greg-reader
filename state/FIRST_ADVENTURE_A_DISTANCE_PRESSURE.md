# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE A DISTANCE PRESSURE

Non-canon development guidance.

## Core rule

A should remain present after Greg leaves Carrow without physically dominating every chapter.

Her pressure can travel through:
- letters or messages;
- deadlines;
- retainers or intermediaries;
- prearranged stops;
- money held back until completion;
- instructions that become harder to interpret once B/C circumstances change;
- local people who know her reputation differently;
- consequences already in motion before Greg reaches the target.

## Adult ambiguity

A should have reasons to want timely completion beyond jealousy.

Possible pressures:
- legal or property consequences;
- family obligation;
- another party moving first;
- illness, inheritance, contract, custody, succession, debt, or public embarrassment;
- dangerous regional conditions making delay objectively worse;
- C possessing information, authority, or responsibility A genuinely needs returned.

A's strongest version is one where Greg occasionally thinks: **she is not wrong about this part.**

## Escalation

A's distant pressure should change meaning over time:

1. initial deadline feels like client impatience;
2. later information makes some urgency understandable;
3. B/C attachment makes literal compliance increasingly costly;
4. A's reaction becomes less predictable because Greg realizes he never understood the whole relationship;
5. return pressure becomes a real conflict rather than a simple completion step.

Avoid turning every message from A into a threat. Calm specificity can be more dangerous than melodrama.
