# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST OUTWARD ADVENTURE CANDIDATES

Non-canon Writers' Room / Story Control quarry for the first sustained departure from Carrow.

These are not scheduled arcs. Their job is to give development lanes a few strong shapes to pressure-test against current prose so the story can converge instead of generating infinite possibilities.

## Candidate A — Retrieval contract inside somebody else's love story

**Front door:** familiar fantasy retrieval / escort work.

A client or intermediary offers Greg unusually good compensation to help locate, escort, retrieve, protect, or accompany a person outside Carrow.

Greg notices that part of the explanation is missing. He forms a reasonable mundane theory: family embarrassment, inheritance, private dispute, elopement, debt, political inconvenience, reputation, or simple need for discretion.

The practical job is real. The hidden emotional structure is larger.

Possible deeper shape:
- Person A wants Person C returned / found / brought somewhere.
- Person B is sent, obligated, or independently motivated to help retrieve C.
- Greg joins for practical reasons and is not the romantic prize.
- Travel changes B/C before the original job is complete.
- Greg recognizes the developing attraction or loyalty problem before at least one participant does.
- A's claim on C may be emotionally understandable rather than cartoon-villain ownership.
- Returning C exactly as contracted may become more dangerous or morally complicated than failing.
- Greg may like A, B, and C enough that there is no clean side.

**Why this fits first:**
- requires no gods / cosmology declaration;
- uses a familiar fantasy doorway;
- naturally gets Greg onto roads and through multiple regions;
- allows moʻolelo-derived relationship scale without direct retelling;
- makes Greg important without making the legend about him;
- supports both ordinary travel texture and one or two genuinely strange encounters;
- produces strong return residue because the contract, payment, reputation, relationships, or consequences can follow him home.

**Main danger:** turning the triangle into YA romance observed by Greg. The emotional conflict needs material / social / geographic consequences and adult ambiguity.

## Candidate B — Provenance job becomes dangerous-route expedition

**Front door:** Greg recognizes or becomes interested in an object, maker, material, ruin lot, salvage mark, or acquisition opportunity connected to somewhere outside Carrow.

A practical opportunity gives him reason to travel: inspect source, verify provenance, escort goods, bid at an estate/auction, recover a paired object, or reach the original workshop/site.

The route itself carries the real story.

Possible deeper shape:
- recognizable treasure / artifact / ruin fantasy frame;
- local settlements have conflicting stories about the source;
- one supposedly supernatural problem is mundane;
- one supposedly mundane problem is not;
- Greg's old knowledge correctly identifies some practical dangers but fails to categorize one rare phenomenon;
- the object may not be the most valuable thing he learns or brings home.

**Why this fits:** strong integration with existing money / magic-item / provenance engines and gives Greg a selfish, practical reason to leave.

**Main danger:** becoming loot progression or artifact treadmill.

## Candidate C — Caravan / road contract through changing magical ecology

**Front door:** straightforward escort, delivery, caravan, route survey, or passenger protection.

The job is not secretly fake. The road has changed.

Possible pressures:
- migration;
- territorial magical ecology;
- damaged route infrastructure;
- local prohibitions outsiders misunderstand;
- bandit or human opportunism exploiting ecological disruption;
- a rare neutral entity whose presence is more dangerous than a conventional enemy;
- Greg's old fieldcraft remains useful and sometimes exactly correct.

**Why this fits:** directly pays off Greg's S-class past and magical-ecology direction while showing regions beyond Carrow.

**Main danger:** episodic road encounters / monster-of-the-week.

## Candidate D — Tournament / public contest with a private objective

**Front door:** recognizable tournament / contest / trial arc.

Greg does not primarily care about winning conventionally. He wants access, a particular lower-status prize, a person, an introduction, a route, money threshold, or provenance opportunity.

Could occur outside Carrow and become the practical reason to travel.

**Why this fits:** strong fantasy candy and a clean place to show competence, reputation distortion, other adventurers, and regional differences.

**Main danger:** power-ranking ladder swallowing the serial.

## Candidate E — Missing person / local legend with human center

**Front door:** missing-person or rescue contract.

The missing person may have left voluntarily, entered someone else's protection, crossed a local rule, been caught in a supernatural relationship, or become entangled with a place/entity that is not simply a captor.

The solution may require understanding what the missing person wants rather than defeating a monster.

**Why this fits:** lets the story borrow folklore / guardian / transformation logic without cosmology escalation.

**Main danger:** mystery-box inflation or making every local legend literally true.

## Current preference order

1. **Candidate A — retrieval / somebody else's love story**
2. **Candidate B — provenance / acquisition route**
3. **Candidate C — caravan / ecology route**
4. Candidate E — missing person / local legend
5. Candidate D — tournament / private objective

This ordering is not canon. A lower candidate should win if current manuscript pressure gives it a better organic doorway.

## Combination permission

A first adventure may combine **one primary candidate** with **one secondary pressure**.

Good examples:
- retrieval contract + dangerous ecology route;
- provenance job + local missing-person complication;
- tournament destination + retrieval obligation;
- caravan job + one rare guardian / shifter encounter.

Avoid stacking three or more primary adventure engines simply because they are available.

## Decision test

Prefer the candidate that can answer all four:
1. Why does Greg personally say yes?
2. Why does this require leaving Carrow rather than happening inside it?
3. What story continues independently of Greg once he arrives?
4. What durable residue comes home with him?
