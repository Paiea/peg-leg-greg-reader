# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE CAST A

Non-canon candidate geometry for the first sustained outward adventure.

## Role

A is the person whose need creates the original retrieval / escort / verification contract.

A should not read as an obvious villain, jealous monster, femme fatale, or exposition machine. Her claim on C should make emotional and practical sense even if it later becomes dangerous or impossible to satisfy cleanly.

## Strong candidate shape

A is capable, resourced enough to pay well, and used to solving difficult problems through competence rather than public drama.

She wants C found / confirmed / brought back / escorted home / made available for a necessary decision.

Possible legitimate reasons:
- C disappeared during a family, legal, property, inheritance, work, or political obligation;
- C left without resolving something that materially affects other people;
- C may be in danger and A has incomplete evidence;
- A and C have an intimate history that she does not want to explain to strangers;
- A promised someone else she would bring C back;
- A is trying to prevent a larger conflict by handling the matter privately first.

## Why Greg may like her

A should give Greg real reasons to respect her:
- she pays what she says;
- she understands logistics;
- she does not waste his time;
- she has her own work / obligations;
- she admits some limits even while concealing others;
- she may be funny in a register unlike Greg's;
- she does not treat his body or age as a spectacle.

Greg should be able to think that hiring him was reasonable and that A is probably hiding personal bullshit rather than supernatural catastrophe.

## Hidden pressure

A's omission is relational before it is mythic.

She may describe C as:
- an old associate;
- family-connected;
- under obligation;
- someone who needs to return;
- somebody she cannot simply abandon.

The wording is technically defensible but emotionally incomplete.

## Larger-world danger

A may or may not carry the strongest supernatural pressure.

If she does, avoid announcing it early. Better shapes:
- a rare inherited magical condition;
- ancient or regionally specific power;
- shifter nature kept private;
- relationship to a dangerous place / force;
- political or household power whose magical consequences exceed ordinary expectations.

She does not need to be divine.

## Failure mode to avoid

Do not turn A into `possessive woman sends heroes to retrieve boyfriend`.

Her claim needs enough legitimacy that Greg cannot solve the plot by saying `he left, get over it`.

The useful moral pressure is:

**A may be wrong about what should happen next while still being right that something unresolved cannot simply be ignored.**
