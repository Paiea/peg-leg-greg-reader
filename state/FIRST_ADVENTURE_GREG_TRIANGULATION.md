# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE GREG TRIANGULATION

Non-canon development guidance.

## Core rule

Greg should become triangulated because each person needs something practical from him, not because everyone chooses him as emotional confessor.

Possible pressures:
- A expects contractual compliance;
- B expects professional judgment or fair interpretation;
- C expects Greg to recognize adult agency rather than treat retrieval as ownership;
- local people treat Greg as the obvious outsider with enough authority to make a call;
- route or safety decisions force him to choose who gets informed first.

## Greg should resist therapist gravity

He does not need to mediate every feeling.

Prefer:
- blunt practical questions;
- refusing to carry messages that should be delivered directly;
- noticing what people omit;
- telling someone what he can and cannot promise;
- distinguishing contract terms from moral obligation;
- occasionally walking away from a conversation that is not his job.

## Neutrality has logistics

The story becomes interesting when neutrality stops being abstract.

Examples:
- who rides with whom;
- whether Greg reveals C's location;
- whether Greg accepts revised payment from A;
- whether he allows B to accompany C somewhere not covered by the contract;
- whether he physically participates in escorting an unwilling adult;
- whether he delays departure for safety knowing delay has relational consequences.

Greg's choices should often be defensible without being clean.

## Avoid

- Greg solving the triangle through superior emotional wisdom;
- everyone trusting Greg instantly;
- Greg becoming moral judge of all three;
- forced speeches explaining consent, love, or duty when action can reveal the conflict.

His power comes from being competent inside the mess, not from being above it.
