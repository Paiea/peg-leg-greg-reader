# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE LOCAL ENTITY INTERSECTION

Non-canon development guidance.

## Core rule

The rare local entity should intersect the route because the travelers need to pass through its lived geography, not because it has been waiting for Greg.

Preferred forms:
- ferry or crossing;
- water rights / irrigation boundary;
- inn, estate, mill, fishery, bridge, or road near territory locals treat carefully;
- a person everyone knows socially but describes indirectly;
- a local dispute where the apparently mundane property question has an older layer.

## Reveal ladder

1. locals use oddly precise language around one person/place;
2. Greg notices practical inconsistencies rather than mystical aura;
3. one explanation fits ordinary politics or ecology;
4. another detail does not fit;
5. Greg adjusts behavior before certainty;
6. confirmation arrives late and does not require combat.

## Relationship to A/B/C plot

The entity should complicate movement, timing, access, safety, or obligation without becoming the hidden mastermind of the romance.

Good intersections:
- passage requires respecting a local condition that delays the party;
- C has prior local history nobody mentioned;
- B accidentally earns or loses trust through ordinary behavior;
- A's instructions conflict with what locals know is safe;
- the entity provides one piece of information but refuses to solve the human conflict.

## Scale

The encounter should establish that Greg's S-class past does not define the world's ceiling.

The strongest beat may simply be Greg deciding not to test what the entity can do.

Avoid rank assignment, lore lecture, chosen-one recognition, or automatic hostility.
