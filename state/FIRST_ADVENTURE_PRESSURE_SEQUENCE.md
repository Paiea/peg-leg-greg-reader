# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE PRESSURE SEQUENCE

Non-canon near-edge sequence for the few chapters before departure.

Do not treat this as one chapter per beat. Several may occur in one chapter; some may be skipped.

## Pressure sequence

1. **OUTWARD FACT**
A person, route, object, or job beyond Carrow matters inside an otherwise complete scene.

2. **HUMAN PROBLEM**
The reader can understand the practical problem without knowing the hidden emotional or mythic layer.

3. **REFERRAL**
Greg is considered because of a narrow reputation already earned: careful, discreet, competent, does not fabricate explanations, can travel, does not panic.

4. **OFFER**
Terms are concrete enough to evaluate. Payment and logistics feel real.

5. **FISHY DETAIL**
Greg notices one omission or asymmetry, preferably about relationship or intent rather than spooky danger.

6. **SECOND INCENTIVE**
Something besides cash makes the same route more attractive: access, transport, provenance, person, object, material, or curiosity with practical value.

7. **HOME COST**
Leaving means missed work, household disruption, delayed opportunity, spent travel time, or another small sacrifice.

8. **YES**
Greg accepts for reasons already visible on-page.

9. **PREPARATION**
One or two lived arrangements make the departure real.

10. **CITY FALLS BEHIND**
The first actual travel chapter begins with movement, not another round of setup.

## Anti-stall rule

Once OFFER + FISHY DETAIL + SECOND INCENTIVE + HOME COST are all established strongly enough, stop generating more setup complications.

The next useful move is usually Greg deciding.
