# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE ROUTE SKETCH

Non-canon route sketch. This is a development scaffold, not a locked outline.

## Front door

Greg is referred for a private outside job because of mundane reliability, procedural restraint, travel competence, and his habit of not inventing facts.

The visible job is retrieval / accompaniment / verification involving another adult outside Carrow.

The client or intermediary omits relationship history more than operational detail.

Greg notices.

The terms are still good enough to justify continuing the conversation.

## Phase 1 — Contract before myth

Keep the first chapter or two of the hook socially legible.

Possible beats:
- Greg asks concrete questions about route, lodging, payment, authority, target consent, and what exactly counts as completion.
- The client answers practical questions well but relationship questions poorly.
- Person B is introduced as the person actually traveling with Greg or as the representative with the deepest stake.
- Greg suspects family / lover / inheritance trouble.
- He is not wrong enough to look stupid.

## Phase 2 — Lived road

Travel should initially feel like travel, not omen delivery.

Use:
- food;
- weather;
- lodging;
- route costs;
- body logistics;
- local work;
- companions learning each other's habits;
- Greg seeing how regional people talk about Carrow and adventurers;
- one familiar fantasy pleasure if useful, such as a dangerous road, caravan overlap, roadside fight, or remote inn.

## Phase 3 — Find the person, change the question

The target is found or clearly located.

The original job question should then weaken.

Possible discoveries:
- target is not missing in the ordinary sense;
- target left voluntarily;
- target is under another person's protection;
- target is injured, altered, legally dead, socially trapped, or simply refusing return;
- Person B and target have more chemistry / history / loyalty than Greg expected;
- the client has a legitimate claim but not a morally simple one.

This is the moment when Greg realizes the practical retrieval job is becoming somebody else's story.

## Phase 4 — One category violation

Only after the human conflict is established should the first genuinely larger-world element become undeniable.

Possible shapes:
- a rare shifter whose human identity has already mattered socially;
- a place-bound guardian tied to water / crossing / property;
- a local prohibition Greg had treated as partly practical proving only partly practical;
- a dangerous regional person or entity whose power does not fit adventurer rank cleanly;
- old landscape magic carrying residue from a prior intimate conflict.

Do not stack multiple major revelations.

## Phase 5 — Return becomes harder than retrieval

Strong transformed-moʻolelo shape:

Finding the target is not the climax.

Bringing everyone back toward the original client is where emotional and supernatural pressure compound.

Possible pressures:
- Person B and target are no longer emotionally neutral;
- old lover / prior claimant / family obligation resurfaces;
- delays matter;
- the client interprets delay or changed loyalties badly;
- collateral road / estate / local consequences begin before direct confrontation;
- Greg tries to finish the job without pretending he can solve other people's love lives.

## Phase 6 — Greg's actual choice

The real choice should concern obligation, not destiny.

Questions:
- What did Greg actually agree to do?
- Does target consent matter under the contract as written / understood?
- What does Greg owe Person B after traveling together?
- What does he owe the client who paid honestly about some things and dishonestly about others?
- Is neutrality still possible?
- Is there a practical third option nobody emotionally involved can see?

Greg may solve logistics without solving the relationship.

## Phase 7 — Return residue

Whatever happens, Carrow remains home.

Bring back some combination of:
- money or lost money;
- route familiarity;
- a new contact;
- a changed friendship;
- a favor;
- a dangerous person's memory of Greg;
- one object with provenance;
- a distorted story spreading ahead of him;
- a new heuristic for judging suspicious work;
- a reminder that S-class competence does not imply universal taxonomy.

Meanwhile Carrow should have moved without him.

## Core success condition

The arc should be understandable at every stage without knowledge of Hawaiian source material.

Its deeper structure may resonate with retrieval / love / jealousy / journey moʻolelo, but by the time it becomes prose it should feel fully native to PLG.
