# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE ROUTE STAGE 3: CONVENTIONAL PRESSURE

Non-canon route development.

## Purpose

Give the first outward adventure one satisfying piece of recognizable fantasy danger **before** the genuinely strange layer arrives.

This stage exists partly to calibrate Greg.

The reader should see him encounter a problem he understands well enough that his competence feels normal rather than revelatory.

Possible pressures:
- road ambush;
- conventional predatory monster;
- damaged caravan under attack;
- dangerous but known territorial creature;
- collapsed watch road with hostile scavengers;
- bandits exploiting a predictable choke point.

Use one primary problem. Do not stack a mini-dungeon onto it automatically.

## Greg competence

Greg should not need to become flashy.

Useful signals:
- recognizes likely approach or behavior early;
- positions himself and B before explaining much;
- chooses an efficient response;
- knows when pursuit is stupid;
- notices whether opponents are committed, frightened, hungry, trained, or improvising;
- treats aftermath and injury checks as part of the event.

His current Bronze status should remain socially visible even if his judgment exceeds what others expect.

Do not convert the scene into a rank reveal or admiration funnel.

## B function

B must remain useful.

Possible contributions:
- terrain knowledge;
- ranged or specialized support;
- calming animals;
- route decision;
- medical/practical aftermath;
- protecting documents, target information, transport, or bystanders;
- noticing something Greg misses because B knows the region.

The event should strengthen Greg/B trust through action rather than confession.

## Straightforward permission

The threat may simply be hostile.

No hidden moral inversion is required.
No ecological essay is required.
No larger conspiracy is required.

If bandits attack them, the bandits may genuinely be bandits.
If a predator tries to eat somebody, stopping it can be the correct answer.

## Structural payoff

This stage makes later uncertainty meaningful.

After seeing Greg correctly classify a familiar danger, a later moment where he cannot comfortably classify something should carry more weight.

## Exit condition

The problem ends. The road continues.

Do not immediately reveal that the conventional encounter was secretly controlled by the mythic entity unless later prose independently earns that connection.
