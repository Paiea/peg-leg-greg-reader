# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE ROUTE STAGE 4: C'S LOCATION

Non-canon route development.

## Purpose

Finding C should feel like arrival at a real place and the beginning of the harder problem, not the end of a search arc.

C's current location should support a temporary life that makes staying gone understandable.

Possible forms:
- small regional town;
- estate/work site;
- inn or boarding house tied to seasonal work;
- specialist settlement;
- river or lake community;
- trade stop where C has become useful enough to belong provisionally;
- place connected to a person or obligation C chose rather than merely hid behind.

Do not lock the exact place yet.

## First meeting rule

Avoid melodramatic reveal staging unless earned.

C may simply be:
- working;
- eating;
- repairing something;
- talking to someone;
- annoyed to see B;
- unsurprised that A eventually sent somebody;
- surprised only by Greg.

The scene should immediately establish that C has had a life while absent.

## Contract change

Once C is found, the governing question should change from:

**Where is C?**

to:

**What exactly did Greg agree to do now that C is standing here and has an opinion?**

C should not be physically helpless by default.
C may refuse, delay, negotiate, ask to finish something, demand to speak to A directly, or agree to return for reasons different from the client's assumptions.

## B/C chemistry

Do not turn the first meeting into instant romance confirmation.

Useful early shapes:
- prior knowledge or history that B underestimated;
- irritation that reveals familiarity;
- B understanding one practical problem C is facing;
- C respecting B's competence before emotional attraction becomes legible;
- Greg noticing small asymmetries in attention before either person names them.

## Greg position

Greg should not decide who is morally right in the first C scene.

His immediate concerns are narrower:
- is C safe;
- is C free to choose;
- is anybody lying about immediate danger;
- what does the contract actually require;
- what is the next practical move;
- where are they sleeping;
- does returning now create a worse problem than waiting.

## Exit condition

Stage 4 ends when the party has a new travel problem rather than a solved retrieval problem.

The return, delay, detour, or negotiation must now matter more than simply locating C.
