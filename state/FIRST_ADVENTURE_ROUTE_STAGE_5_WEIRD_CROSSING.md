# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ADVENTURE ROUTE STAGE 5: THE WEIRD CROSSING

Non-canon route development.

## Purpose

This stage is the first point where the larger-world strange layer becomes undeniable.

It should arrive after the reader has already seen:
- ordinary departure;
- lived road texture;
- Greg/B travel chemistry;
- at least one familiar fantasy danger Greg can classify;
- C as a person with a life and choices.

The contrast is the point.

## Default location shape

A crossing works especially well because it is simultaneously:
- practical infrastructure;
- social territory;
- economic bottleneck;
- environmental boundary;
- a place travelers cannot simply ignore.

Possible forms:
- river ford;
- ferry;
- lake crossing;
- causeway;
- marsh road;
- bridge with a locally preferred alternate route.

Do not lock the exact geography yet.

## Local behavior before explanation

Locals should behave as though the strange fact is already integrated into life.

Examples:
- people prefer the west crossing without calling the east one closed;
- ferrymen refuse certain hours without dramatic superstition;
- fishers know where not to tie boats;
- property lines or irrigation routes bend oddly around one bank;
- someone tells Greg not to argue with a particular woman rather than warning him she is a monster;
- children know a rule but not its history;
- merchants price delay into their schedules.

Avoid synchronized spooky exposition.

## Greg's sequence of inference

Greg should first generate ordinary or familiar explanations:
1. territorial creature;
2. dangerous current or magical instability;
3. local power arrangement;
4. shifter or old regional entity if evidence supports it.

His expertise should narrow possibilities rather than make him clueless.

The decisive moment is not `I have never seen anything like this.`

It is closer to:

**I know several things this is not.**

## The entity

Current preference remains an intelligent local being with an ordinary social presence and a radically larger physical or magical nature.

Possibilities:
- ferry owner/operator;
- fish seller or buyer;
- landholder;
- water-rights authority in practice if not in title;
- innkeeper or local elder near the crossing;
- person C already knows casually.

Do not make her a quest giver by default.
Do not make her wait for Greg.
Do not require combat.

Her normal life should remain visible enough that the transformation/confirmation enlarges her rather than replacing her with a boss form.

## Interaction with A/B/C plot

The crossing should pressure the human story rather than hijack it.

Possible functions:
- delay makes A's deadline worse;
- C knows a local rule B does not;
- B accidentally earns trust through ordinary courtesy or practical help;
- Greg chooses a slower route because the risk is not worth testing;
- someone must ask the entity for passage or permission;
- the party's internal tension makes following a simple local rule harder than it should be.

Do not reveal that the entity secretly caused the entire love triangle.

## Undeniable confirmation

Confirmation should be brief enough to preserve mystery.

Possible evidence:
- an impossible partial transformation;
- body scale visible beneath water;
- physical movement no human form could account for;
- local magical reaction centered on her without spellcasting grammar Greg recognizes;
- one moment where human proportions cease to be a useful assumption.

Greg reacts through practical recalculation first: distance, water, companions, exits, what not to provoke.

## Aftermath

The party may simply continue traveling.

That is desirable.

The first myth-scale encounter does not need to become the climax of the adventure. Its job is to prove the world exceeds familiar adventurer taxonomy while leaving the A/B/C return problem intact.

The next concern may immediately become food, lodging, rain, an argument, or whether A has sent another message.

## Success condition

The reader should come away thinking:

**That was much bigger than the story needed to explain, and these people still have to get home.**
