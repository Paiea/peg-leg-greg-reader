# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST OUTWARD CLIENT ARCHETYPES

Non-canon development quarry for the first sustained post-Ch352 outward adventure.

The purpose is not to lock a client. It is to narrow the kinds of people who can plausibly hire Greg through his current mundane reputation rather than because they somehow know his full first-life S-class history.

## Archetype A — Capable woman with an ugly personal logistics problem

She has money, access, or institutional weight, but the job itself is embarrassingly human: find someone, escort someone, retrieve someone, verify someone is where they claim to be, or accompany a representative who should not travel alone.

Why she fits:
- can plausibly pay above ordinary day labor without cartoon wealth;
- can be competent enough that Greg respects her while still recognizing withheld information;
- naturally supports the somebody-else's-love-story route;
- does not require divine or noble cosmology.

Useful ambiguity:
- patron, wealthy independent worker, estate manager, regional fixer, dangerous mage, retired adventurer, household head, merchant-adjacent operator, or something socially harder to classify.

Avoid:
- femme-fatale shorthand;
- obvious villain signals;
- perfect mysterious-lady dialogue;
- making her whole personality `powerful woman with secrets`.

## Archetype B — Intermediary hiring on behalf of someone absent

Greg never meets the true emotional center at first.

An agent, clerk, household manager, courier office, family representative, insurer-adjacent contact, or trade intermediary needs someone competent enough to travel and follow terms.

Why this fits:
- beautifully compatible with Greg's recent referral/procedure reputation;
- lets the first job look bureaucratic rather than mythic;
- creates room for later revelation that the named client is not the real source of pressure.

Best use:
Greg thinks the intermediary is omitting ugly family detail. The intermediary may not even know the deeper truth.

## Archetype C — Person B hires Greg to help retrieve Person C

This variant places the eventual emotional triangle on-page sooner.

Person B already has a reason to travel and needs someone who can:
- handle danger;
- read paperwork / route markers;
- not dominate the mission;
- keep quiet;
- make practical judgments.

Why this fits:
- gives Greg a travel companion before the target is found;
- lets the relationship between B and Greg develop independently of the later romance;
- makes Greg's decentered role clearer.

Risk:
Do not make B a blank courier who exists only to fall in love with C.

## Archetype D — Existing mundane institution indirectly points to private work

The hook grows from a current interface such as quay, road, theatre, Vale-adjacent clerical work, or another established contact.

The institution itself does not own the adventure. It only creates the referral.

Example logic:
`Need someone who reads what is there, does not improvise cause, can travel, and can keep a private matter private.`

Why this fits:
- maximizes continuity;
- avoids neon quest-board logic;
- makes Greg's ordinary competence compound into larger opportunity.

## Current preference

Prefer A or B, with D as the referral mechanism.

That gives us:

**ordinary referral -> socially legible private job -> omitted human history -> road -> somebody else's larger story**

Do not canonize a client from this file alone.
