# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST COMPANION ROAD DYNAMIC

Non-canon development guidance for Greg and the first major outward companion, especially Candidate B.

## Core principle

Do not make the first traveling companion feel like a romance-delivery mechanism or a new permanent sidekick audition.

The relationship should become specific because travel forces repeated practical contact.

## Early road dynamic

At departure, Greg and B should still have incomplete models of each other.

Useful initial asymmetries:
- B knows the client / target history better than Greg but withholds interpretation;
- Greg knows travel risk, pacing, fieldcraft, or danger better than B;
- B has a real specialty Greg lacks;
- one of them is more socially fluent in the region;
- one of them plans too much while the other adjusts better in motion;
- B may initially read Greg as merely careful / dry / physically constrained rather than unusually dangerous.

## Build familiarity through repetition

Let the relationship change through small repeated acts:
- who notices when the other is tired;
- who buys food without asking because they already know the answer;
- who takes first watch / last watch and why;
- who handles strangers better;
- who remembers a preference;
- who is bad at packing;
- who talks when nervous;
- who goes quiet after certain topics;
- who is actually funny when not trying;
- who irritates Greg in a way that becomes familiar rather than unbearable.

This should not become a montage checklist. Use only what the road earns.

## Dialogue variance

B should not become Greg Two.

Do not default to matching his short dry cadence.

Possible contrast registers:
- longer explanatory speech;
- formal precision;
- nervous qualification;
- bad metaphors;
- blunt emotional naming;
- excessive context;
- practical jargon;
- visible inability to keep up with one of Greg's clipped jokes;
- occasional perfect comeback precisely because it is not constant.

Mood and relationship may shift delivery without erasing baseline voice.

## Competence respect before affection

Greg should have a reason to respect B that has nothing to do with C.

Likewise B should discover Greg's competence in layers rather than receiving an exposition dump about S-class history.

A useful progression:

**annoying stranger -> useful professional -> reliable road person -> person whose judgment matters -> friend/confidant if earned**

Romantic tension belongs to B/C, not Greg/B.

## Success condition

By the time C enters fully, Greg and B should already have enough shared road history that C's presence changes an existing traveling relationship rather than creating the entire cast from zero.
