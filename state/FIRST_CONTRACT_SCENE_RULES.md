# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST CONTRACT SCENE RULES

Development guidance, not canon.

## Core principle

The first serious outward contract scene should feel like work before it feels like destiny.

Greg should ask practical questions because practical questions are how he protects himself and prices risk.

## Useful negotiation topics

- destination;
- expected duration;
- who is traveling;
- what exactly counts as completion;
- whether the target is expected to cooperate;
- transport;
- lodging / food;
- advance versus completion pay;
- what expenses are covered;
- whether Greg is being hired as escort, witness, finder, guard, courier, negotiator, or some combination;
- what authority he has if the situation changes;
- whether the client expects discretion;
- what information has deliberately been withheld.

Do not turn the scene into legalistic contract porn. Select the details that reveal character and later matter.

## Fishiness

The most useful early inconsistency is not `the client refuses to name the demon`.

Prefer something human:
- she can describe where C sleeps but not why C left;
- she knows C is alive but avoids saying how;
- she says `bring him back` when Greg asks whether C wants to return;
- B's role is described too carefully;
- the client is oddly precise about public facts and vague about private ones;
- payment is generous but not irrational enough to explain the evasiveness.

Greg may conclude that the hidden problem is family, romance, reputation, inheritance, obligation, or embarrassment.

That conclusion may be substantially correct.

## Scene ending

Do not require acceptance inside the first meeting.

A strong ending can be Greg taking the terms away, asking one person he trusts a narrow question, checking route cost, or simply deciding the offer is interesting enough not to dismiss.

When he does accept, the reader should understand why without needing a speech about adventure.
