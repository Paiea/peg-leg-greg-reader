# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST CONVENTIONAL FANTASY PAYOFF

Non-canon guidance for letting the first outward adventure deliver a recognizable fantasy pleasure without immediately subverting it.

## Core principle

After the story has earned departure, give the reader at least one clean piece of fantasy candy before asking the arc to become stranger.

The purpose is contrast and pleasure, not irony.

## Strong candidates

Useful first conventional payoffs include:
- a real road ambush;
- a dangerous but familiar monster encounter;
- a roadside inn full of travelers and adventurers;
- a ruined watchtower or old road structure that must be crossed / sheltered in;
- a caravan problem Greg can solve with experienced fieldcraft;
- a rival or overconfident local adventurer;
- a straightforward escort danger where Greg's competence genuinely matters.

Pick one. Do not stack them.

## Let Greg be good at something

This is a good place to remind the reader that Greg's old S-class life was real.

He does not need to reveal everything.

A conventional threat can allow:
- efficient threat reading;
- choosing ground;
- recognizing animal / monster behavior;
- refusing a stupid fight;
- ending a fight quickly once it becomes necessary;
- protecting B without turning B incompetent;
- using experience that looks disproportionate to his current Bronze status.

The scene should create questions for companions without requiring an immediate biography dump.

## No mandatory twist

If the monster is hostile, it may simply be hostile.

If bandits attack, they may simply want money or cargo.

If the inn is warm and crowded, it may simply be a warm crowded inn.

Do not reveal that every conventional fantasy beat secretly belongs to the mythic layer.

The stranger material becomes stronger when normal fantasy remains genuinely normal.

## Residue

Even a clean action beat should leave small consequences:
- damaged gear;
- spent money;
- delayed arrival;
- a new impression between Greg and B;
- local gossip;
- somebody's confidence changing;
- a practical problem the next morning.

No heroic applause required.

## Success condition

The reader gets to enjoy the simple thought:

**Oh right. Greg used to be extremely fucking good at this.**

Then the story keeps moving.
