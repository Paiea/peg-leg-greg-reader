# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST ROAD CHAPTER RULES

Development guidance, not canon.

## Core principle

Once Greg leaves Carrow, let the first road chapter prove that the story's physical scale has changed without immediately demanding spectacle.

Departure itself is the event.

## What the chapter should accomplish

Useful goals include:
- make Carrow physically recede;
- establish who is actually traveling together;
- show what Greg carries and what he deliberately leaves behind;
- establish transport / walking / lodging rhythm;
- let Greg's body affect travel practically without turning the chapter into injury drama;
- reveal one regional difference in speech, food, road practice, magical infrastructure, or etiquette;
- give B and Greg enough ordinary interaction to establish traveling rhythm;
- let A's instructions or expectations remain present without A physically dominating the scene;
- establish one question about C that remains unresolved.

## Do not rush the weirdness

The first road chapter does not need:
- monster attack;
- prophecy;
- rare shifter reveal;
- bandit ambush;
- ominous ruins;
- instant B/C romance;
- Greg demonstrating S-class power for strangers.

A roadside magical practice, unfamiliar local object, strange bit of infrastructure, or small ecological difference is enough.

## Familiar fantasy pleasure

Allow the road to feel like fantasy travel.

Inns, carts, roadside food, weather, road gossip, tolls, travelers, old bridges, animals, camp routine, and annoying gear are all allowed without subversion.

PLG identity comes from specificity and accumulated people, not from refusing recognizable travel imagery.

## End beat

A strong first-road ending should make the reader feel both:

**we are genuinely away from home now**

and

**the job still looks mostly like the job Greg agreed to.**

Save the first serious category violation until ordinary travel has enough weight to contrast with it.
