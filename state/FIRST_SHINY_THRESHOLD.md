# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST SHINY THRESHOLD

Non-canon development guidance for what can realistically tip Greg from suspicion into acceptance after Chapter 352.

Greg currently has 8c and remains undercapitalized relative to specialty opportunities. The first serious outward job should therefore feel materially meaningful without becoming a cartoon windfall.

## Shiny does not have to mean cash

Possible meaningful incentives:
- a solid cash payment for several days of work;
- covered travel, food, and lodging so Greg does not bleed his own purse;
- a meaningful advance;
- access to an estate lot, workshop, salvage yard, specialist, maker, or regional market he normally could not reach;
- first refusal on an object or material;
- transport already going somewhere Greg independently cares about;
- provenance information tied to artifacts or tools;
- a favor from someone with real local reach;
- permission to inspect something rare without buying it;
- a job that also gets him physically near a separate opportunity.

## Preferred convergence

The first adventure should ideally not be purchased by one giant number.

Better:

**good-enough money + covered costs + one unusually valuable access benefit + Greg already has a second reason to care about the route**

That makes his yes feel like arithmetic, curiosity, and opportunity rather than plot hypnosis.

## Greg's internal risk logic

Suspicion should increase the price he needs, not erase his judgment.

He may think:
- the omission is probably private relationship mess;
- the route is manageable;
- the client is paying for discretion and inconvenience;
- the advance limits his downside;
- he can leave if the job changes materially;
- the access is valuable enough to justify indulging his curiosity.

## Avoid

- money so huge refusal becomes implausible;
- a legendary artifact handed over upfront;
- paying Greg at hidden-S-class rates before anyone knows what he is;
- turning the job into an instant solution to Vale debt or long-term capital pressure;
- making Greg accept purely because the object is shiny and he loses all caution.

## Best emotional flavor

Greg should be able to look at the terms, notice the fishiness, and arrive at something like:

**I still think you're hiding something. I also think you're paying enough for the version of the problem I can currently see.**

The adventure begins when reality later proves that one category of risk was not visible in the original arithmetic.
