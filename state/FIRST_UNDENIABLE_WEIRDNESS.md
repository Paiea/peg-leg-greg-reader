# PEG-LEG GREG — FIRST UNDENIABLE WEIRDNESS

Non-canon guidance for the first moment in the outward adventure when transformed moʻolelo-derived story physics becomes impossible to dismiss as ordinary regional variation.

## Core principle

Delay certainty.

The strange layer should begin as a pattern of small facts that Greg can explain provisionally. The first undeniable moment matters because Greg is experienced enough that his uncertainty carries weight.

## Before confirmation

Useful pre-confirmation signs:
- several locals avoiding the same place for different stated reasons;
- a route rule nobody explains the same way twice;
- an ordinary person who is treated with slightly too much practical deference;
- animal or magical-ecology behavior that does not fit Greg's expected pattern;
- infrastructure built around a constraint no one names directly;
- old damage whose explanation is socially obvious to locals but mechanically strange to Greg;
- a person knowing something they should not reasonably know;
- an apparent superstition that keeps producing useful behavior.

Do not stack all of these.

## Greg should try normal explanations first

His provisional explanations may include:
- territorial magical fauna;
- dangerous current / unstable ground;
- local politics;
- smuggling;
- old warding;
- inherited safety custom;
- rich-person influence;
- ordinary fear exaggerated by story.

Several may be partly right.

This preserves competence. He is not blind. The category itself is incomplete.

## The confirmation beat

The first undeniable reveal should be simple enough that explanation cannot hide it.

Possible shapes:
- the ordinary local woman is briefly, unmistakably not physically human in scale or form;
- a body moves beneath water whose size is incompatible with the person standing on shore;
- a landscape / water response occurs as direct consequence of one person's emotion or movement;
- Greg witnesses a transformation only partially, with enough evidence to eliminate his mundane models;
- a supposedly local social rule is enforced by a phenomenon no known institution is producing.

Do not explain cosmology immediately afterward.

## Greg's reaction

Avoid wide-eyed novice awe.

Better reactions:
- immediate recalculation;
- moving B or C without explaining first;
- checking distance / exits / water / terrain;
- realizing a planned threat response is useless;
- mentally discarding one classification after another;
- deciding that testing the entity would be idiotic.

The strongest internal conclusion is not `I have no idea what that is.`

It is closer to:

**I know several things it is not, and that is worse.**

## No forced fight

The first undeniable myth-scale encounter should preferably not become a boss battle.

The entity may:
- ignore Greg;
- enforce a boundary;
- help for its own reasons;
- object to something B/C are doing;
- demand ordinary courtesy;
- resolve a local problem violently without targeting the party;
- reveal that locals have been living beside it for generations.

Greg's success may simply be recognizing that survival depends on not escalating.

## Relationship to the main adventure

The reveal should intersect the route without replacing the A/B/C human engine.

It can:
- delay them;
- force a different crossing;
- expose something about C's regional history;
- reveal B's useful specialty;
- worsen A's deadline pressure;
- create a debt or favor;
- change how Greg understands the world beyond Carrow.

It should not suddenly reveal that the entire love/retrieval story was secretly orchestrated by the entity.

## Success condition

After the scene, the reader and Greg both understand:

**the world is larger than the adventurer ranking system and older than Greg's comfortable categories.**

But the next morning people still need breakfast, transport, and a decision about where the hell they are going.
