# PEG-LEG GREG — HISTORICAL VOICE MATURITY RULE

Purpose: improve earlier manuscript prose using lessons from later PLG without flattening the story into one timeless, uniformly mature voice.

## Core distinction

When an early passage feels weaker than later PLG, ask:

**Is this weaker because the character/world/relationship is earlier, or because the writing system had not learned how to express it yet?**

Only the second category should be normalized aggressively.

## Preserve legitimate early-state differences

Do not erase:
- Greg being less socially rooted;
- relationships lacking shorthand they have not earned yet;
- people being more formal, cautious, awkward, or explanatory before familiarity develops;
- locations feeling less automatic before Greg learns them;
- early uncertainty that later becomes knowledge;
- jokes not yet carrying history;
- social groups not yet behaving like established organisms;
- Greg's current-life body knowledge being less developed at the appropriate point.

A mature edit must not backfill later intimacy into earlier scenes.

## Repair authoring immaturity

Do improve earlier material when weakness comes from craft rather than chronology:
- every character sharing Greg's dry cadence;
- unclear dialogue attribution;
- excessive fragment stacks;
- narrator explaining a good beat twice;
- Greg asking tourist questions about things his history says he broadly knows;
- exposition delivered only because the reader needs it;
- repeated procedural setup after the scene already established it;
- generic supporting-character dialogue where later evidence reveals a stronger recurring voice;
- relationships resetting because continuity memory was weaker during drafting;
- accidental contradictions in body, money, object, or social continuity.

## Later voice files are guidance, not retroactive possession

Current recurring-character voice pages should inform historical edits, but do not paste a fully developed late-book voice backward mechanically.

Ask:
- what traits were already present here?
- which later traits plausibly emerge from familiarity or experience?
- how would this person sound before this relationship became easy?
- what current mood/role would bend their baseline?

Preserve developmental trajectory.

## Greg rule

Greg's mature voice can be clarified earlier without making early Greg omniscient, emotionally settled, socially fluent, or already fully reinvested in life.

Preserve the difference between:
- old-life competence he already possesses;
- current-life practical adaptation he is still learning;
- social belonging he has not yet accumulated;
- future motivations he has not yet developed.

## Compression interaction

Do not misclassify an awkward but developmentally necessary scene as redundant merely because later relationships are smoother.

Conversely, do not preserve repeated weak scenes just because they document the historical drafting process.

The published manuscript should preserve **character development**, not **authoring mistakes**.

## Whole-book consistency target

The desired effect is not:

`Chapter 20 sounds exactly like Chapter 420.`

The desired effect is:

`Chapter 20 sounds like the same author and same characters at an earlier point in their lives.`

## Final test

After retrospective editing, a reader should be able to feel the book becoming deeper because the people and world accumulate, not because the first hundred chapters appear technically inferior.

**Preserve growth. Remove accidental crudity.**
