# PEG-LEG GREG — HYBRID ADVENTURE PATTERN

A strong future PLG adventure may combine a familiar fantasy scaffold with less-familiar story physics derived from transformed moʻolelo or other unusual narrative sources.

This is not mandatory structure. It is a reliable development pattern when the story needs both accessibility and freshness.

## Basic shape

**FAMILIAR DOOR -> SPECIFIC PLG LIFE -> STRANGER UNDERLYING LOGIC -> CONSEQUENCE -> CHANGED NORMAL**

Examples of familiar doors:
- escort;
- retrieval;
- tournament;
- ruin expedition;
- monster contract;
- caravan;
- missing person;
- artifact auction;
- noble invitation;
- dangerous road.

The familiar door helps the reader orient quickly.

The unusual layer may then enter through:
- transformed hospitality logic;
- unstable love / loyalty structures;
- rare shapeshifters;
- local prohibitions with degraded explanations;
- landscape carrying old interpersonal consequences;
- hidden identities;
- supernatural powers embedded in ordinary households or routes;
- impossible old labor / construction;
- old bargains whose social meaning outlived their original context;
- neutral entities whose scale exceeds normal adventurer classification.

## Do not reveal the strange layer too early

The hybrid pattern works best when the initial job remains legible on its own.

Greg may correctly understand most of the ordinary contract while missing only the category that matters later.

Example shape:

1. Greg accepts a retrieval job for rational reasons.
2. Travel behaves like travel: roads, meals, weather, costs, arguments, local work.
3. Greg realizes the target's relationship history matters.
4. A local rule or rare entity complicates movement.
5. The emotional center shifts away from the original contract.
6. Fulfilling the contract exactly may become the dangerous choice.
7. Greg must decide what obligation actually governs him.
8. Return leaves practical and relational residue.

## Keep Greg decentered when useful

The hybrid pattern is especially strong when Greg is not the mythic center.

He may be:
- the escort watching two people fall for each other;
- the hired expert realizing the client omitted a family history;
- the powerful outsider who can survive the blast radius but cannot resolve the relationship;
- the witness whose practical choices affect a legend that belongs to someone else;
- the person everyone later credits or blames incorrectly.

## Blend ratios

Do not make every adventure 50% familiar trope and 50% myth mutation.

Possible ratios:
- 80/20 familiar-to-strange for an early outward arc;
- 60/40 once the reader trusts the expanded world;
- 90/10 when the story simply wants a fun conventional adventure with one weird local element;
- 30/70 only when the manuscript has earned a genuinely uncanny region or event.

Treat these as thinking aids, not formulas.

## Success condition

A reader unfamiliar with the source inspirations should experience a coherent, specific fantasy adventure.

A reader familiar with the underlying source material may feel resonance, pattern-recognition, or a delayed `oh shit`, but source recognition should never be required for comprehension.

The result should feel like PLG first.
