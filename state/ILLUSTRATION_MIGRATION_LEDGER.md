# PEG-LEG GREG — ILLUSTRATION MIGRATION LEDGER

Purpose: preserve illustration history and make structural manuscript edits safe for existing art.

## Core rule

Illustrations belong to story beats and stable chapter identity, not permanently to a chapter number.

Structural compression may change where an image belongs. It must not silently orphan approved art or force weak chapters to survive because they already have pictures.

## Migration dispositions

Every illustrated source chapter affected by MERGE, CUT, title change, act change, or renumbering receives one disposition:

- KEEP WITH CHAPTER
- MOVE TO MERGED CHAPTER
- REASSIGN TO SURVIVING BEAT
- PROMOTE TO ACT / SECTION ART
- RETAIN AS GALLERY / BONUS ART
- ARCHIVE
- REGENERATE / REPLACE

## Ledger fields

For each affected image record:

- asset identifier/path
- source chapter_id
- source display chapter number
- source title
- scene/beat represented
- approval status
- character-reference value, if any
- new chapter_id
- new display chapter number when assigned
- new use/disposition
- crop/layout implications
- alt text/caption changes
- public reader impact
- migration status
- notes

## Beat-first reassignment

When chapters merge, ask what exact beat the art depicts.

If the beat survives in the merged chapter, move the image with that beat.

Do not simply attach every image from all source chapters to the merged chapter. Avoid illustration pileup.

If multiple strong images now compete inside one merged chapter, choose among:
- strongest narrative fit;
- best visual diversity;
- best character-continuity value;
- act/section promotion;
- gallery retention.

## Character-reference protection

An image may have value beyond its original chapter placement because it is a strong character reference.

Do not discard or regenerate such art merely because the source chapter is cut.

Preserve approved character-reference assets in the illustration registry even when their publication placement changes.

## Sunk-cost rule

Existing art is not structural immunity.

A weak/redundant chapter may still be merged or cut.

The correct response is migration, reassignment, or archival treatment, not preserving redundant prose solely to justify an image.

## Public reader safety

Before publishing a structural edit affecting illustrated chapters, verify:
- every previously published image has a disposition;
- no active image points at a retired chapter identity without an intentional archive path;
- current chapter art coverage is regenerated from stable identity/current numbering;
- image alt text/captions still match surviving prose;
- chapter/act card artwork remains attached to the intended role.

## Audit history

Do not erase old illustration associations. Preserve migration history so a future editor can answer:
- where did this image originate?
- why was it moved?
- what scene did it originally depict?
- is it still approved character-reference material?

## Goal

Structural editing should be free to improve the manuscript while the illustration system retains continuity, provenance, and useful assets instead of becoming collateral damage.
