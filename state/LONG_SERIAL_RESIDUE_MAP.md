# PEG-LEG GREG — LONG-SERIAL RESIDUE MAP

Developmental specialist brain for making long time visible without turning the serial into a rigid outline.

This file is **not canon**. Manuscript prose outranks it. It stores high-value channels where 02 / 03 / 04 can make accumulated life more legible over tens or hundreds of chapters.

Read with `STORY_NORTH_STAR.md`, `DEVELOPMENT_CYCLE.md`, current manuscript authority, and the relevant live lane state.

## Core rule

**MAKE TIME LEAVE RESIDUE.**

The serial should not merely remember old material. It should let prior chapters change how later life works.

Prefer:

**EVENT → MEMORY → CHANGED BEHAVIOR / OBJECT / RELATIONSHIP → OCCASIONAL RETURN**

not:

**EVENT → CALLBACK → CALLBACK → CALLBACK.**

A strong return usually changes something: meaning, consequence, owner, condition, shorthand, irritation, trust, cost, or reputation.

## Anti-bloat rule

Residue should usually cost only a line, action, object state, or altered interaction.

Good forms:
- someone automatically changes an object placement because they know another person's route
- a shopkeeper remembers what Lyssa usually buys
- an old explicit correction becomes one word or a look after repeated shared work
- two people are already halfway through an argument when Greg arrives
- a favor is referenced without retelling the day it was earned
- an object returns damaged, repaired, relabeled, borrowed, repurposed, or with a new owner
- Greg discovers a decision was already made while he was elsewhere

Avoid explanatory paragraphs whose main purpose is proving the cast has history.

## Ensemble: high-value independent causality

### Lyssa

**Established foothold:** independent garment work, customers, revisions, payment, errands, supply knowledge, and places such as Maren that she knows better than Greg.

**Development opportunity:** let her customer / referral / supply map recur through consequences. A familiar preference, referral, fitting, changed price, borrowed item, favor, delivery, customer relative, or job accepted before Greg knows can make her life continue offscreen.

**Protect:** her independence does not need to route into theatre. Do not conscript her as company wardrobe merely because she can sew.

### Hessa

**Established foothold:** professional evidence discipline, scheduling, apparatus continuity, stopping rules, and multiple plausible unanswered questions.

**Development opportunity:** Greg should increasingly feel like one question / obligation inside a working life rather than Hessa's entire profession. Plans can change because evidence changed; she can have other obligations; her availability need not perfectly match Greg's curiosity.

**Protect:** do not invent colleagues, mentors, or bureaucracy merely to prove she has a life.

### Nessa

**Established foothold:** prop identity, reset, storage, traffic, costume/object continuity, and practical backstage authority.

**Development opportunity:** she is one of the best social connectors because object continuity naturally intersects with many people without making her everyone's boss.

Strong existing-pair possibilities:
- Nessa ↔ Marek: protected objects, misplaced objects, recurring irritation, consequences of `helping`
- Nessa ↔ Pell: repairs, tools/materials, standards, object continuity
- Nessa ↔ Hara: fit/durability/readability/use of costume or prop
- Nessa ↔ Jori/Davin: stage geometry, hauling, repair, object routes
- Nessa ↔ Teren: physical continuity versus performance intention

**Protect:** demonstrated competence matters more than a formal title.

### Marek

**Established foothold:** strong performer, humor, prop-touching / theatrical-chaos history, ability to recover inside performance.

**Development opportunity:** let the same habit create different outcomes. Sometimes it is funny. Sometimes somebody else has to reset/repair/search. Sometimes Marek is the competent person who covers another actor. His history should mutate rather than become one permanent joke.

**Protect:** do not reduce him to comic prop sabotage.

### Hara

**Established foothold:** owns major audience beats, can recover a scene partner's mistake, has practical performance/costume opinions, and has manuscript evidence of plans / people outside Greg's immediate knowledge.

**Development opportunity:** let her arrive with context Greg missed, be unavailable because she already has plans, possess stage-partner shorthand with Pell/Marek/Nessa, or solve a live problem without teaching Greg anything.

**Protect:** excellence does not create a mentorship obligation.

### Rinna

**Established foothold:** business, payment, venues, records, suppliers, handbills, warning contacts, practical commercial defense.

**Development opportunity:** repeated merchants/suppliers and Rinna ↔ Teren coordination can make the theatre feel simultaneously artistic and commercial. Her network should carry history independently of Greg.

**Protect:** supplier relationships are ordinary commercial/social infrastructure, not a spy network.

### Teren

**Established foothold:** narrow acting / scene correction and practical rehearsal leadership.

**Development opportunity:** different actors can require different note timing/language. Greg may learn by watching a note meant for Hara, Pell, Marek, or another performer. Long shared work can compress correction without praise speeches.

**Protect:** do not make Teren a wisdom dispenser whose every note becomes Greg's philosophy of life.

### Pell

**Established foothold:** performance, repair/tinkering/object/food/furniture texture and repeated interaction with theatre labor.

**Development opportunity:** useful connector among Nessa, Marek, Hara, and technical work through borrowing, repairs, meals, covering, or object continuity.

**Protect:** do not turn practical competence into a universal-skill gimmick.

### Jori / Davin

**Established foothold:** repair, cart/set work, hauling, physical geometry, mutual credit/blame/denial texture.

**Development opportunity:** arguably the strongest existing non-Greg workplace pair. Let decisions, disagreements, repairs, and jokes happen before Greg arrives. Some problems should be solved so completely that Greg never learns they were problems.

**Protect:** Greg does not need to arbitrate or understand every disagreement.

## Greg: useful mess without scheduled flaws

Greg can become more human by sometimes being less central and less immediately correct.

Natural pressures:
- scarcity can make small money disproportionately salient
- growing competence can make him anticipate, over-help, or optimize the wrong system
- he can hold a wrong interpretation longer than one beat
- he can dislike uncertainty without being given an immediate lesson
- he can arrive after a joke, plan, repair, argument, or relationship change already happened
- other people can know theatre, Carrow, Lyssa, customers, or local habits better than he does
- somebody else can make the best joke or solve the practical problem

Do not schedule jealousy, pettiness, insecurity, avoidance, or failure as mandatory character beats. Let circumstance earn them.

## Research translation rule

External research is most valuable when it produces **recurring social behavior**, not trivia.

Translate:

**REAL PRACTICE → BEHAVIOR → MISTAKE / CONSTRAINT → WHO OWNS THE PROBLEM → WHO PAYS THE COST → WHAT MEMORY SURVIVES**

- prop routes / preset / reset / maintenance
- costume tracks / quick-change physicality

Do not keep researching theatre merely because more topics exist. Let 03 identify the next actual specificity gap.
