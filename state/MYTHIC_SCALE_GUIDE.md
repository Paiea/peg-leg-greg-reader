# PEG-LEG GREG — MYTHIC SCALE GUIDE

Non-canon development guidance for introducing larger fantasy without collapsing PLG into power-scaling fiction.

## Core distinction

**Adventurer rank measures adventurers. It does not measure reality.**

S-class can remain meaningful without becoming the top rung of every creature, entity, hazard, race, magical phenomenon, or ancient being in the world.

The Guild may classify contracts, professionals, known hazards, and response expectations. That does not require every extraordinary thing to fit the same ladder.

## Desired ceiling

Greg is legitimately formidable because Old Greg survived decades of dangerous work and reached S-class capability.

That should let him:

- recognize danger earlier than most people;
- survive situations that would kill ordinary travelers;
- make good field decisions under pressure;
- protect weaker companions;
- know when a fight is unnecessary or unwinnable;
- occasionally dominate a threat he truly understands;
- remain useful when the situation is not about combat.

It should **not** guarantee that every thing he encounters is beneath him.

Useful judgments include:

- I know how to kill that.
- I know how to avoid that.
- I know what it wants.
- I can survive that if I have to.
- I do not know what that is.
- I know what people call it, which is not the same thing.
- I would rather not learn whether I can kill it.

## Gods / religion

Do not rush PLG into explicit walking-god cosmology.

Religion and politics have not yet been developed enough to justify casually confirming a complete divine hierarchy.

Future possibilities may include:

- religions with competing explanations;
- entities some people call gods and others classify differently;
- ancient or regional beings whose actual nature remains disputed;
- supernatural powers embedded in landscape, ancestry, institutions, or old agreements;
- beings so dangerous that terminology is less useful than practical behavior.

Do not answer cosmological questions merely because source inspiration involved deities.

A source god may translate into a mage, shifter, ancient being, regional power, bloodline, political figure, or phenomenon, or may be split across several PLG elements.

## Shifter rarity

Maintain scarcity.

Possible rough world feel:

- minor disguise / glamour / bounded physical alteration: plausible but not necessarily common;
- socially known people with limited shifting: uncommon;
- dramatic full-body transformation into a radically different creature: rare;
- enormous transformation capable of changing the scale of a battlefield or landscape encounter: exceptional.

Do not lock exact population numbers yet.

The point is reader expectation: a true major transformation should still produce **holy shit** rather than “oh, another shifter.”

## Neutral overwhelming power

Some of the strongest fantasy moments should involve things that could destroy the party but have no reason to.

Examples of shape:

- an enormous territorial animal crossing a road;
- a rare intelligent being choosing not to escalate;
- a water-associated entity whose interest is local rather than predatory;
- a magical environmental process that cannot be fought;
- two powerful people whose private conflict makes the area unsafe;
- an old being participating in ordinary commerce while everyone quietly avoids certain topics.

Danger does not require villainy.

## Reveal discipline

Prefer partial revelation over taxonomy.

A powerful being may be established through:

- locals changing behavior around it;
- physical evidence;
- Greg recognizing a field sign;
- one impossible movement;
- an old scar in the landscape;
- an argument where nobody dares push too far;
- a transformation seen once;
- conflicting stories;
- an S-class Greg deciding not to test the matter.

Do not follow every reveal with a lecture explaining species, level, history, weakness, and magic system.

## Escalation test

Before adding a larger threat, ask:

1. Does this enlarge the world or merely inflate numbers?
2. Can it remain interesting without Greg fighting it?
3. Does Greg’s competence still matter?
4. Does the encounter create future social/material residue?
5. Is the mystery stronger if some explanation remains unresolved?

If the only new thing is “stronger enemy,” reject it.
