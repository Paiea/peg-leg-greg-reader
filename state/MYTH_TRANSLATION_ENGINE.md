# PEG-LEG GREG — MYTH TRANSLATION ENGINE

This file is a non-canon development tool for converting old story material into PLG-native possibilities.

## Core rule

Use source stories as **narrative geology**, not as surface skin.

The goal is not to disguise a retelling by renaming people. The goal is to extract unusual story mechanics, emotional structures, supernatural logic, social rules, landscape behavior, transformation patterns, bargains, taboos, rivalries, journeys, consequences, and relationship geometry, then mutate them until they belong naturally to Peg-Leg Greg.

## Translation pipeline

For any source seed:

1. **Extract the mechanism.** What actually makes the story move?
2. **Strip source surface.** Remove source-language names, deity names, cultural labels, recognizable titles, place names, and one-to-one fantasy substitutions.
3. **Rebuild through PLG.** Ask how the mechanism would exist inside current PLG magic, economy, institutions, geography, professions, class relations, relationships, and material life.
4. **Expand consequences.** If the phenomenon has existed for decades or centuries, what laws, rumors, scams, jobs, architecture, trade practices, warnings, children’s games, local etiquette, scholarly arguments, or insurance-like behaviors grew around it?
5. **Distort the sequence.** Do not preserve every original beat. Combine, invert, delay, relocate, split, or let Greg derail the source structure.
6. **Decenter Greg when useful.** He may enter somebody else’s love story, family conflict, local legend, feud, inheritance problem, or supernatural dispute.
7. **Preserve ambiguity.** The source seed does not require the PLG explanation to be theological, literal, or fully understood.
8. **Return consequences to normal life.** Adventure should leave residue in money, relationships, reputation, routes, objects, injuries, obligations, knowledge, and future choices.

## Mutation strength

Default to **heavy mutation**.

A Hawaiʻi-aware reader may occasionally feel a distant structural resonance. That is acceptable and can be pleasurable. The story must never require recognition, and recognition should not allow a reader to predict the entire sequence.

Prefer:

**SOURCE SHAPE -> PLG MECHANISM -> NEW SOCIAL WORLD -> NEW CONSEQUENCES -> NEW STORY**

over:

**SOURCE STORY -> renamed characters -> same beats**

## Cultural boundary

Hawaiian moʻolelo are inspiration quarry here, not PLG canon.

Do not automatically import Hawaiian language, deity names, cultural institutions, chiefly titles, sacred terminology, named places, genealogy, or ritual practice into PLG.

Do not flatten a culturally specific concept into a lazy one-word fantasy replacement either. Extract function first, then build a PLG-native analogue only if the story actually needs one.

The development file may retain source labels for provenance. Generated PLG prose normally should not.

## Combination rule

A strong PLG adventure may combine fragments from multiple seeds.

Examples of combination shape:

- retrieval journey + prohibited intimacy + rare water shifter;
- hospitality test + regional environmental consequence + disputed local history;
- hidden construction + labor politics + magical-object provenance;
- rival transformed beings + ordinary trade disruption + Greg hired for a completely different reason.

Combining sources reduces retelling gravity and helps the result become its own story.

## Greg filter

When Greg encounters a myth-shaped situation:

- he remains observant;
- he uses Old Greg fieldcraft when the category is genuinely familiar;
- he may identify that something is suspicious before knowing what it is;
- he should not become stupid to enter the plot;
- he may take a risk because money, curiosity, obligation, access, or material opportunity makes the bet reasonable;
- sometimes his theory is right;
- sometimes the world contains a category his model did not include;
- he need not defeat, explain, classify, or own every extraordinary thing he encounters.

A useful feeling is:

**Greg understands enough to know this is dangerous, but not enough to believe the world owes him an explanation.**

## Generation test

Before using a source-inspired possibility, ask:

- Would this still be interesting if the reader never knew the source?
- Does it fit PLG’s existing world rather than replacing it?
- Has it mutated beyond one-to-one correspondence?
- Does it create lived consequences rather than lore-only novelty?
- Can Greg collide with it through ordinary incentives?
- Does it enlarge the world without making everything secretly mythic?

If not, mutate further or discard it.
