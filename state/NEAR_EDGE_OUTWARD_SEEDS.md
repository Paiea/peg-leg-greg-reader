# PEG-LEG GREG — NEAR-EDGE OUTWARD SEEDS

Non-canon quarry for Chapters after 300. These are **small horizon-widening beats**, not scheduled scenes and not a five-item checklist.

Use at most one when current prose naturally creates the opening. It is completely valid for several chapters to pass before any seed appears.

## Seed 1 — The job Greg does not take

A familiar contact mentions paid work beyond Carrow.

The offer may involve escort, retrieval, delivery, inspection, salvage, caravan support, or a dangerous route.

Greg asks enough questions to expose one bad assumption or missing detail and declines.

Purpose:
- establishes that outward work exists;
- proves Greg does not accept every shiny hook;
- gives a route / region / employer a name that can return later;
- lets the reader feel the horizon widen without launching an arc.

Do not make the rejected job secretly the most important event in history by default.

## Seed 2 — Something ordinary came from far away

A material, tool, garment component, magical object, food, stage item, repair part, scrap lot, or customer's possession has provenance outside Carrow.

Greg recognizes the place / maker / broad category or asks one practical question about it.

Purpose:
- lets geography enter through use;
- can later connect to acquisition / provenance pressure;
- reminds the story that trade routes exist beyond the city.

Do not instantly turn the object into a legendary artifact.

## Seed 3 — Someone else is leaving

A recurring or one-scene person prepares for travel.

Greg is not invited.

He may hear about route conditions, a destination, seasonal timing, transport, lodging, local custom, family obligation, work, or danger as ordinary conversation.

Purpose:
- establishes independent mobility in the world;
- prevents Greg from being the only person whose travel matters;
- can create future social residue if that person returns changed.

## Seed 4 — A traveler has the wrong Greg

Someone recently arrived from elsewhere recognizes a distorted story about Peg-Leg Greg / Old Greg / an S-class deed but does not cleanly recognize nineteen-year-old Greg.

Purpose:
- previews how reputation changes across regions;
- makes travel socially interesting before departure;
- can give Greg information about where one version of his legend is circulating.

Do not turn this into universal fame or an identity reveal.

## Seed 5 — A route problem with no hook attached

Greg hears that a road, bridge, pass, ferry, caravan line, port, or regional path is behaving differently than he remembers or than locals expect.

Possible causes remain open:
- mundane repair;
- weather;
- trade conflict;
- migration;
- magical ecology;
- local politics;
- rumor;
- something stranger.

Purpose:
- creates geography with changing conditions;
- can later become useful if a separate job needs the same route;
- supports convergence rather than neon quest markers.

## Seed 6 — Attractive payment with a boring reason

Someone offers unusually good money for an outside job.

Greg suspects hidden danger.

The explanation turns out to be mostly mundane: ugly timing, confidentiality, family embarrassment, urgent replacement labor, difficult client, spoiled cargo, social inconvenience, or nobody else wanting the schedule.

Purpose:
- prevents `high pay = supernatural trap` from becoming predictable;
- preserves Greg's ability to judge risk correctly;
- teaches the reader not to treat every suspicious contract as mythic setup.

## Seed 7 — Attractive payment with one unpriced category

A later job may look similar to Seed 6.

Greg again notices missing information and forms a plausible explanation.

This time his model is incomplete because the concealed truth belongs to a category he did not expect.

Purpose:
- strong door into the eventual first major adventure;
- preserves competence because his reasoning was reasonable;
- lets the world exceed Greg's model without making him stupid.

Do not use this immediately after Seed 6 merely to demonstrate the pattern.

## Seed 8 — The shiny thing is access, not money

Greg is offered modest pay but unusually valuable access:
- first look at an estate lot;
- introduction to a maker;
- salvage rights;
- permission to inspect a collection;
- use of transport already going his direction;
- information about a workshop / ruin / route;
- chance to buy something normally unavailable.

Purpose:
- varies Greg's incentives;
- uses his tinkerer / provenance / future-knowledge brain;
- gives departure a reason other than purse size.

## Seed 9 — Somebody else's retrieval problem brushes Carrow

A person asks questions about someone who is absent, expected, overdue, avoiding contact, or living elsewhere.

Greg need not be hired.

The situation should initially read as ordinary human mess rather than myth.

Purpose:
- quietly seeds the strongest first-adventure candidate;
- allows Person A / B / C relationships to exist before Greg becomes relevant;
- gives Story Control room to decide later whether this is the actual outward arc.

## Seed 10 — Greg wants two things on the same road

A job alone is only mildly attractive.

Separately, Greg has another reason to care about the same destination or route: an object, person, maker, material, future-memory curiosity, theatre need, debt opportunity, old acquaintance, or practical errand.

Neither reason alone forces travel.

Together they make the trip rational.

Purpose:
- preferred convergence shape;
- makes departure feel like Greg choosing rather than plot dragging him;
- supports mixed familiar / strange adventure structure.

## Usage rule

The best near-edge seed is the one that can appear inside a scene that already has another reason to exist.

Prefer:
**ordinary scene + one outward-facing fact**

over:
**chapter whose sole purpose is to announce the future adventure**.
