# PEG-LEG GREG — OUTWARD ADVENTURE ROUTE QUEUE

This file is a non-canon queue of near-future structural nudges. It does not mandate chapter numbers or exact events. Use it to create outward pressure gradually from the current manuscript edge.

## Approved route shape

The first sustained adventure should feel like Greg leaves Carrow for a **real reason**, not because the genre suddenly demands a quest.

Preferred arc geometry:

**CITY PRESSURE -> OFFER / NEED -> SUSPICION -> DECISION -> ROAD -> OTHER PEOPLE'S STORY -> STRANGER WORLD -> CONSEQUENCE -> RETURN**

The exact arc may mutate as current prose develops.

## Queue 1 — First outward hook appears

**Status: APPROVED DIRECTIONAL NUDGE**

Introduce one credible reason to travel beyond Carrow.

Preferred forms:

- a paid escort, retrieval, delivery, inspection, sourcing, or provenance job;
- an object or material Greg wants that requires travel;
- a person already connected to the current social/economic web who needs someone competent;
- a job whose payment is good enough to be memorable but whose explanation is incomplete.

Do not force acceptance immediately.

The first hook may be declined, delayed, or merely noticed.

Purpose: establish that the world is beginning to pull outward.

## Queue 2 — Greg notices the fishy part

**Status: APPROVED DIRECTIONAL NUDGE**

When the eventual usable offer appears, Greg should identify at least one inconsistency or missing fact.

He forms a plausible theory for the concealment rather than behaving naively.

Examples of likely theories:

- family embarrassment;
- romantic history;
- political sensitivity;
- smuggling / provenance problem;
- reputation management;
- unusual urgency;
- distrust of the Guild;
- the client wants discretion more than protection.

His theory may be mostly right while still missing the deeper story.

Purpose: preserve Greg's competence while allowing asymmetric information to open the adventure.

## Queue 3 — Incentives converge enough for Greg to leave

**Status: APPROVED DIRECTIONAL NUDGE**

Greg eventually accepts because several reasons align.

Possible stack:

- unusually strong pay;
- rare object / material / access;
- route overlaps something he already wants;
- curiosity;
- obligation or favor;
- confidence that the known physical risk is manageable;
- the job appears bounded;
- current city economics make the purse genuinely useful.

Avoid making greed the only switch every time.

Purpose: the departure feels like Greg's decision rather than the author's shove.

## Queue 4 — The contract enters somebody else's story

**Status: APPROVED DIRECTIONAL NUDGE**

The job's literal objective should eventually intersect with a conflict whose real center belongs to other people.

Strong first-arc candidate:

A retrieval / escort job conceals a complicated romantic or family history. A person is being sent to retrieve someone for a powerful third party. Travel creates attraction, loyalty conflict, or renewed history. Greg becomes the older-minded witness and practical participant, not the romantic prize.

Useful complications:

- Greg likes multiple sides;
- the supposed target does not want to return;
- the original client is not simply evil;
- the traveler and target develop chemistry;
- an old lover appears during the return;
- Greg becomes confidant because everyone thinks he is neutral;
- fulfilling the contract exactly starts to look morally or practically disastrous.

Purpose: decenter Greg while keeping him consequential.

## Queue 5 — One rare mythic-scale encounter proves the world is bigger

**Status: APPROVED DIRECTIONAL NUDGE**

During travel, include one encounter or revelation that cannot be comfortably reduced to ordinary adventurer ranking.

Preferred first-wave shapes:

- a rare water-associated intelligent being;
- a dramatic but non-hostile shapeshifter;
- a regional entity tied to one crossing, lake, ruin, or settlement;
- a local rule whose underlying danger is only partly understood;
- evidence of a powerful thing rather than a full fight.

Greg's fieldcraft should matter. He may recognize enough to make a good survival decision while remaining uncertain about exact taxonomy or power.

Do not stack multiple giant reveals into the same early travel arc unless the prose truly earns them.

Purpose: establish a higher fantasy ceiling without turning the arc into a boss ladder.

## Return requirement

Whenever this first sustained adventure concludes, do not reset.

Carrow changed while Greg was gone.

Greg returns with some combination of:

- changed relationships;
- money or lost money;
- new obligation;
- reputation residue;
- an object;
- injury or physical habit;
- information;
- a person who may recur;
- a route he now understands;
- a new heuristic for suspicious work;
- a story other people tell incorrectly.

The adventure becomes part of ordinary life rather than detachable DLC.
