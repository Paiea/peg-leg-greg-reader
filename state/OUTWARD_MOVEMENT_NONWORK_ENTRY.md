# OUTWARD MOVEMENT NON-WORK ENTRY

Durable development guardrail. Not canon.

## Problem
PLG's strongest causal engine is work, money, referrals, and practical usefulness. The new outward-adventure architecture correctly uses that strength, but if every expansion begins as paid work, the larger world can feel like an extension of Greg's employment history rather than a place with independent social gravity.

## Rule
The first sustained outward movement may begin through work, but it does not have to.

Valid non-work or mixed entry pressures include:
- traveling with someone Greg already knows for their reason;
- attending a social obligation outside Carrow;
- accompanying a purchase, delivery, visit, funeral, celebration, hearing, performance, or family matter;
- curiosity attached to a real person rather than a mystery object;
- being asked for company rather than expertise;
- a favor where payment is secondary or absent;
- two independent reasons pointing toward the same road, only one of which is paid;
- an existing Carrow relationship developing an outside consequence.

## Preserve economics
Do not make Greg suddenly wander for free without considering food, lodging, transport, lost work, and return cost. Non-work entry does not mean money stops mattering.

## Preferred convergence
A particularly strong shape remains:

one practical outside opportunity + one independent personal/social reason on the same route -> Greg can rationally choose to go.

The second reason should not be manufactured solely to force departure.

## Social gravity
Outside movement should reveal that people Greg knows have relatives, obligations, histories, clients, lovers, debts, hometowns, grudges, and destinations beyond Carrow.

The world gets larger when other people's lives cross the city boundary before Greg's plot does.

## Test
If the only reason Greg ever leaves Carrow is `someone offered him a job`, ask whether an existing relationship or independent desire could share the causal load without turning the departure into wanderlust.
