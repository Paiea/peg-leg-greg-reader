# PEG-LEG GREG — OUTWARD PAY SCALE LOGIC

Development guidance, not canon.

## Current economic context

At Chapter 352 Greg holds 8 private copper and still carries a 26c Vale principal-equivalent debt. He is not destitute, but meaningful specialty purchases and sustained travel remain expensive relative to his liquid purse.

The first major outward offer should therefore feel materially consequential without functioning as authorial bribery.

## Pay principles

A serious multi-day outside job should pay enough that Greg notices the opportunity cost of refusing.

But avoid:
- absurd fantasy windfalls that erase current money texture;
- an amount so large that no rational person would ask questions;
- payment sized purely to force adventure;
- instantly solving Vale debt and capital scarcity in one contract unless the arc itself earns that consequence.

## What can make compensation attractive

Compensation can combine:
- cash;
- meals / lodging / transport covered;
- advance money;
- hazard premium;
- completion bonus;
- access to an otherwise unavailable buyer, maker, market, estate, route, or salvage claim;
- right of first refusal on an object or lot;
- a favor from somebody whose future access matters;
- expenses that would otherwise consume much of Greg's current purse.

A modest cash payment plus covered travel and valuable access can be more believable than a giant purse.

## Suspiciously high pay

If pay is unusually high, Greg should notice.

The amount should create questions, not erase them.

Possible reasonable explanations include:
- ugly timing;
- confidentiality;
- nobody local wants the interpersonal mess;
- remote route / poor transport;
- return trip not guaranteed;
- client needs someone independent of their household;
- job requires discretion plus actual competence;
- previous hired help quit for mundane reasons.

The deeper hidden category may still exist, but the visible premium should have a plausible surface explanation.

## Economic residue

The trip should cost something too.

Even when expenses are covered, Greg may lose local opportunities while away, spend on food / repairs / lodging, tie up money, or return with a different capital position than simple `contract pay added to purse` arithmetic.

Money should remain story texture, not become a quest reward screen.
