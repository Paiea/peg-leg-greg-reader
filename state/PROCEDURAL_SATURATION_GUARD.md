# PEG-LEG GREG — PROCEDURAL SATURATION GUARD

Durable rhythm guardrail. Not canon.

## Problem

PLG is very good at bounded competence:

- Greg gets a narrow job;
- role boundaries are clear;
- specialists own specialist decisions;
- money and time matter;
- mismatches stay evidence-bounded;
- Greg performs carefully and gets paid.

This is a strength.

It can also become a treadmill if several neighboring chapters use the same scene grammar with different props.

## Core rule

Do not suppress procedural competence. Rotate away from it before the reader begins predicting the entire chapter shape.

After one or two strong procedural chapters, seriously consider a chapter whose governing grammar is different.

Possible alternatives:

- social collision;
- leisure;
- argument;
- awkward visit;
- conventional fantasy danger;
- weather or city phenomenon;
- travel;
- physical inconvenience;
- romance or friendship movement;
- comedy with no work optimization;
- desire with no immediate solution;
- somebody else's problem Greg cannot administratively improve;
- ordinary fantasy pleasure such as an inn, roadside encounter, creature problem, or festival.

## Do not confuse distinct nouns with distinct scenes

A quay count and a route tag walk can be canonically different jobs while still sharing nearly identical reading rhythm:

instruction -> bounded role -> careful observations -> mismatch -> specialist verification -> restrained conclusion -> payment -> note.

If the reader experiences the same grammar, treat the sequence as saturated even when continuity says the jobs are different.

## Procedural scenes should vary internally too

Not every work chapter needs:

- explicit scope negotiation;
- three-option uncertainty list;
- repeated confirmation that Greg is not authorized to diagnose;
- clean completion paperwork;
- a supervisor who praises restraint;
- payment counted immediately on page;
- a closing maxim about accuracy.

Use those beats when story pressure requires them. Do not reproduce the whole competence package by default.

## Greg can sometimes simply do the job

Once a work pattern is established, compress what is familiar.

A chapter may begin after orientation.
A worker may know Greg already.
A mismatch may be handled in two lines.
Payment may occur off the main scene axis.
Greg may be bored.
Greg may be socially distracted.
The important event may happen during lunch rather than during the work.

## Exit test

Before choosing another procedural chapter, ask:

**What does this chapter give the reader that the last procedural chapter did not?**

If the answer is mainly a new workplace noun, choose another engine.
