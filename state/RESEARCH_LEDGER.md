## Research principles

- Research real practice when it can replace generic invented texture.
- Prefer primary, institutional, expert, or strong craft sources where possible.
- Community anecdotes are useful for lived texture but must be labeled anecdotal.
- Do not paste long source text. Distill the useful principle and preserve the source link.
- Do not turn historical practice into mandatory fantasy-world history.
- Translate knowledge into behavior and constraints rather than exposition dumps.
- Keep contradictory evidence when it matters.
- If a fact changes with time, record the date checked.

## THEATRE — REHEARSAL NOTES, AUTHORITY, AND RECOVERY

**Question:**
How do actors in ensemble settings receive and use rehearsal notes, when are notes commonly delivered, who has authority to give them, and how do scene partners handle mistakes without making the mistake disappear?

**Source / authority:**
- Kerry Hishon, Theatrefolk, `Communicating Rehearsal Notes in Three Different Ways`
  https://www.theatrefolk.com/blog/communicating-rehearsal-notes-in-three-different-ways
- Simi Horwitz, Backstage, `How Do You Give Notes?`, including working directors Scott Alan Evans and Tisa Chang
  https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/give-notes-60649/
- IAMA Theatre Company, `Professional Code of Conduct`
  https://www.iamatheatre.com/professional-code-of-conduct
- Gettysburg College Theatre Arts, `Student Handbook for Departmental Productions`
  https://www.gettysburg.edu/academic-programs/theatre-arts/productions/student-handbook-departmental-productions
- Christina Hamlett, Pioneer Drama Service, `Teaching Your Actors to Be Good Scene Partners`
  https://www.pioneerdrama.com/Newsletter/Articles/Scene_Partners.asp

**Supported finding:**
- A common rehearsal pattern is to let a run-through complete while notes accumulate, then give verbal notes afterward; immediate physical review/fixes may happen after the run. This is a common practice rather than a universal rule.
- Experienced companies can develop very compressed director/actor shorthand. Scott Alan Evans describes long-running company relationships where a look or gesture can carry a note that would require more explanation with a new actor. Note delivery is actor- and situation-specific, and some adjustments may be given privately.
- Formal note authority is role-bounded in many contemporary companies. IAMA explicitly bars actors from giving fellow actors official notes, routes artistic notes through directors during rehearsal, and gives stage management a relay/maintenance role. Gettysburg similarly describes stage management as logistical conduit rather than an independent artistic director.
- Strong scene partners are expected to keep listening rather than anticipate known lines or choices. Pioneer Drama specifically warns against reacting before a partner's cue and describes covering a flub or missed cue through a re-cue or limited improvisational bridge so the scene can continue.
- Recovery does not erase the original error. The performance can be protected in the moment and the underlying timing, line, blocking, or choice problem can still be corrected afterward.

**Confidence / limits:**
Mixed institutional, professional-practitioner, and theatre-education guidance. High confidence in the broad behavioral patterns: post-run notes are common, authority is usually bounded, ensemble shorthand develops, and partners often cover live mistakes. Exact hierarchy and etiquette vary substantially by company, era, contract, and production. Modern formal protocols are analogues for Carrow, not fantasy-world history.

**Story translation:**
- Chapter 225 already lands on a highly plausible note problem: Greg anticipates Hara's scripted choice and presents it early. Hara's live bridge protects the scene while Teren's later narrow correction preserves the mistake as useful residue.
- Let some full runs continue through non-safety acting errors rather than making Teren stop every imperfection. Immediate stops can remain more likely for dangerous traffic, broken physical business, or a problem that prevents the work from continuing.
- Treat note authority as distributed by domain. Teren can own acting/story adjustments; Nessa can own props, resets, traffic, and practical backstage continuity; Jori can own physical stage/build safety and geometry; Rinna can own house/business procedure. This is a story translation, not established hierarchy beyond what manuscript evidence supports.
- Earn shorthand over time. Greg may initially receive explicit corrections, then later understand a word, look, gesture, or repeated phrase because shared work has accumulated. Different relationships should develop different shorthand.
- Scene partners can rescue the play without rescuing Greg socially. Hara, Pell, Marek, and others can bridge each other's errors too, leaving room afterward for irritation, teasing, gratitude, or a terse note.
- Receiving notes can differentiate characters. Greg currently has a tendency to explain the mechanism of his mistake before resetting. Other actors may simply retry, ask one precise clarification, privately disagree, or joke and then make the adjustment. Do not assign these as fixed gimmicks without manuscript evidence.

**Canon status:**
POSSIBILITY / RESEARCH SUPPORT. Chapter 225 itself establishes Greg's premature help, Hara's recovery, and Teren's narrow correction; the broader rehearsal culture remains developmental material until established in prose or explicitly approved.
