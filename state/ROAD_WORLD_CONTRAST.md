# PEG-LEG GREG — ROAD WORLD CONTRAST

Development guidance for making the first sustained trip outside Carrow feel like expansion rather than backdrop replacement.

## Core principle

Travel should reveal that Carrow is one local solution among many.

Do not make every destination exotic. Difference is strongest when it appears in ordinary systems.

## Contrast categories

Useful regional differences include:
- road surfaces and maintenance;
- tolls, ferries, bridges, relay systems, and lodging expectations;
- what people eat when traveling;
- how water is accessed and priced;
- how magic is embedded in ordinary infrastructure;
- how much adventurer rank matters socially;
- local speech rhythm and etiquette;
- what counts as rude, cautious, generous, or suspicious;
- how tools, repairs, carts, animals, and buildings are adapted to local conditions;
- what locals consider completely ordinary that Greg finds regionally specific.

## Avoid lore-tourism

Do not stop the story so Greg can admire a region like a travel brochure.

Prefer difference entering through use:
- a room built around a heating method Carrow rarely uses;
- a road inn that assumes travelers sleep communally;
- a ferry fare structured around cargo rather than passengers;
- a town where adventurers are treated like seasonal labor;
- a bridge everyone crosses without commenting on the magical mechanism Greg notices;
- food that becomes memorable because it is cheap, filling, awful, excellent, or inconvenient.

## Greg's knowledge

Old Greg traveled extensively and should not behave like a sheltered tourist.

He may recognize broad categories while still noticing that this world, region, era, or local implementation differs from what he knew.

Prefer:
**I know what kind of thing this is. I do not yet know how these people do it here.**

over:
**I have never seen anything like this.**

Use true unfamiliarity selectively so it retains force.

## Social scale

Different places should know Greg differently.

Possible responses:
- no recognition at all;
- recognition of the name but not the nineteen-year-old body;
- a badly distorted Old Greg story;
- recognition through current mundane referral networks rather than legend;
- somebody knowing a real detail Greg wishes had not traveled;
- somebody assuming the famous version was taller, older, or dead.

Do not turn travel into an admiration tour.

## Success condition

After several road chapters, the reader should feel that returning to Carrow will mean returning to a specific place, not merely the generic city map where the story started.
