# ROLE TITLE INVISIBILITY RULE

Purpose: preserve the restored role-title system without letting title logic become visible manuscript machinery.

## Core rule

Chapter titles may identify the temporary role, identity, or social function Greg embodies. The prose does NOT need to explain that system.

The title is an external identity card, not a theme the narrator must consciously unpack.

If the chapter is titled `THE MODEL`, the scene should make Greg a model through action, payment, social treatment, body use, and consequence. Greg does not need to think `role acquired`, `temporarily`, `roles become dangerous`, or otherwise narrate the title architecture unless that thought would exist without the title system.

## Preferred method

SHOW ROLE THROUGH:
- what Greg is asked to do;
- what other people call him in context;
- what obligations attach to the role;
- what he can and cannot decide while occupying it;
- how the role ends;
- what residue remains afterward.

Do not convert this into a recurring philosophical motif about roles.

## Leak warning

Flag lines that appear to exist mainly because the backend knows the chapter title, including:
- `There. Role acquired.`
- `MODEL. TEMPORARILY.`
- generalized reflections about roles ending when the scene already demonstrated it;
- repeated language connecting every chapter identity to a life lesson.

One organic role-related thought can work. Repeated explicit role awareness becomes production leakage.

## Test

Ask:

**If this chapter had no title yet, would Greg still think this exact sentence?**

If no, cut or rewrite.

Title logic should sharpen structure backstage and disappear in performance.
