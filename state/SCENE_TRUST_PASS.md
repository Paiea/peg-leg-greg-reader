# PEG-LEG GREG — SCENE TRUST PASS

Durable post-draft craft check. Not canon.

## Purpose

PLG is strongest when the scene earns meaning and the prose trusts the reader to notice it.

The most common over-writing failure at the current manuscript edge is not bad plotting. It is a good scene followed by a sentence that explains why the scene was good, bounded, independent, funny, or non-cliche.

## Pass rule

After a chapter draft, inspect the final 10 to 20 percent of each major beat.

Ask:

**Did the scene already prove the point before Greg explained the point?**

If yes, cut the explanatory tail.

## High-risk tails

Watch especially for:

- dialogue followed by narration explaining the relationship dynamic;
- a competent specialist scene followed by narration explaining that Greg respected expertise;
- a side-character beat followed by narration stating they have independent lives;
- a mundane resolution followed by narration explaining that it did not become a mystery;
- a choice followed by a maxim summarizing the choice too neatly;
- a successful boundary followed by Greg restating the boundary in abstract language;
- humor followed by a second joke that only exists to underline the first;
- an emotionally clean beat followed by thematic interpretation.

## Prefer residue over thesis

Instead of ending a beat with a lesson, end with something material:

- somebody keeps working;
- food gets cold;
- Greg has to carry something awkwardly;
- a person changes the subject;
- a bill still exists;
- a door closes;
- weather keeps happening;
- somebody says something only half-related;
- Greg writes a narrower note rather than a life rule;
- another person misunderstands what mattered.

## Maxims

Greg can write rules and notes. This is part of his voice.

But do not make every chapter produce a polished lesson.

A note should often be operational, provisional, petty, specific, or incomplete rather than thematic.

Prefer:

`MARKER 9 / D-6 TAG / D-8 LIST / HOLD`

over:

`TRUTH IS WHAT REMAINS WHEN PAPER FAILS.`

unless the stronger thematic sentence is genuinely earned by Greg's character and not generated because the engine wants a chapter takeaway.

## Final test

Delete the last explanatory sentence of a beat in imagination.

If the beat becomes stronger, cleaner, funnier, or more emotionally alive, the sentence was probably editorial residue.
