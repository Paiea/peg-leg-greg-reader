# SOCIAL SCENE GRAMMAR ROTATION

Purpose: prevent the manuscript from solving procedural saturation merely by changing the profession or prop while keeping the same scene skeleton.

## Core rule

After a run of chapters driven by bounded work, verification, testing, buying, appraisal, or specialist procedure, prefer a chapter whose primary engine is social presence rather than task completion.

This does not mean `nothing happens`.

It means the chapter's movement can come from:
- an argument;
- an invitation;
- being stuck somewhere with someone;
- embarrassment;
- leisure;
- a meal;
- a stranger misreading Greg;
- old social residue returning;
- a favor with unclear boundaries;
- witnessing another person's problem;
- an ordinary fantasy venue such as an inn, bathhouse, public event, caravan stop, neighborhood gathering, guild hall, or market crowd where Greg is not there to audit a system.

## Grammar test

If a chapter can be summarized as:

`Greg receives scope -> clarifies rules -> performs bounded task -> finds discrepancy -> specialist verifies -> Greg avoids overclaim -> gets paid -> writes note`

then the next chapter should strongly consider different grammar, even if every individual scene is good.

Likewise, do not replace that skeleton with:

`Greg sees object -> asks condition -> tests narrow effect -> specialist limits claim -> Greg records result`

immediately afterward.

## Social chapters still accumulate

A social-first chapter should leave residue through relationship, reputation, invitation, misunderstanding, desire, obligation, route familiarity, humor, or changed shorthand.

It does not need a mystery or payment to justify itself.

## Adventure relevance

This is also a clean way to let outward adventure approach organically. New routes and people should first be allowed to exist socially before every one becomes a job lead or fantasy clue.
