# PEG-LEG GREG — SOMEBODY ELSE'S STORY ENTRY

Development guidance, not canon.

## Core idea

The first major outward arc is stronger if Greg enters a conflict that already has emotional momentum before he arrives.

He should not create the central relationship problem simply by existing.

## Quiet entry patterns

- a client has already been trying to get someone home;
- two people have history Greg does not initially know;
- a family dispute predates the contract;
- a traveler has already chosen sides before Greg joins;
- the person being retrieved has reasons for staying away;
- a powerful claimant has already made promises, mistakes, or enemies;
- local people already have opinions about the absent person;
- the route itself contains consequences from the ongoing conflict.

Greg may initially only see the practical edge.

## Decentering Greg

Greg can matter enormously without becoming the emotional protagonist.

Useful positions:
- the competent outsider who can travel safely enough for the others to keep making bad emotional decisions;
- the witness who sees attraction, resentment, loyalty, or fear before the people involved name it;
- the person asked for advice because he seems neutral;
- the contractor whose terms become morally harder to follow as the hidden story becomes legible;
- the person who can physically intervene in danger but cannot solve what the people actually want.

## Love-story version

If the retrieval / triangle seed is used, avoid a clean `A bad, B and C true love` setup.

Prefer:
- A has a real history and legitimate emotional claim without owning C;
- B has obligations that conflict with emerging attachment;
- C is not merely an object being retrieved;
- Greg can understand each side;
- the return trip may be the point where neutrality becomes impossible.

## Best feeling

Greg eventually realizes:

**This was never really my story. I just accepted a contract inside it.**

That realization should enlarge the world rather than diminish his importance.
