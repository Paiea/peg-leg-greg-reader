# PEG-LEG GREG — STORY ANTI-PATTERNS

This file is a compact negative-knowledge layer: recurring ways a plausible chapter, development idea, or edit can make Peg-Leg Greg worse.

It is **not canon**, not a plot outline, and not another mandatory every-chapter checklist. Exact manuscript prose and explicit author decisions outrank it. `STORY_NORTH_STAR.md` remains the positive artistic direction.

Consult this file when a narrative/development/editorial choice risks one of these known traps. The goal is to preserve scar tissue without making workers rediscover the same failures.

## Core operating rule

**Parallelize exploration and verification aggressively. Serialize authoritative story decisions.**

Cheaply generate alternatives, research, critiques, and continuity checks in parallel when useful. Do not let parallel workers independently canonize incompatible story moves. Accepted prose and durable state change through the normal authority path.

## Character / social anti-patterns

### Greg as generic mastermind
Do not make Greg's first interpretation correct by default, let old-life memory act like a searchable database, or turn ordinary competence into universal brilliance. Preserve narrow hypotheses, revision, mistakes, and other people's superior expertise.

### Protagonist gravity
Do not make every useful person, relationship, joke, solution, social event, or institutional change orbit Greg. Other people know each other, work without him, change offscreen, and sometimes own the strongest beat.

### Lyssa as Greg-support infrastructure
Do not collapse Lyssa into analyst, therapist, financial manager, research partner, exposition machine, or automatic validator. Her work, customers, routes, moods, mistakes, preferences, fatigue, and relationships remain independently causal.

### Secondary characters as functions
Do not flatten recurring people into one permanent job in Greg's story: skeptic, fixer, screwup, rival, caretaker, prop cop, helper, praise machine. Preserve contradiction, changing competence, and relationship-specific behavior.

### Everyone speaking the same social language
Do not turn relationships into interchangeable clever/supportive banter. Different relationships should tolerate different silences, jokes, corrections, tenderness, irritation, authority, and misunderstanding.

### Universal reputation
Do not let one clean public version of Greg replace networked partial reputation. Different groups may know different, distorted, outdated, or contradictory Gregs.

## Plot / pressure anti-patterns

### Escalation because escalation is available
Do not answer every clue immediately, add danger because a chapter feels quiet, or increase violence merely to prove stakes. Ordinary consequences, delay, uncertainty, and mundane defenses can remain dramatically meaningful.

### Proliferation before convergence
Before adding a new antagonist, institution, magic subsystem, quest, conspiracy, or named organization, test whether existing pressures can collide more interestingly.

### Chosen-one / power-fantasy drift
Do not convert bounded competence, reputation, magic evidence, or theatre skill into destiny, effortless dominance, generalized power, or repeated scenes of everyone recognizing Greg as exceptional.

### Threat certainty inflation
Do not promote description to identity, direction to destination, association to employment, interest to motive, warning to plan, or inference to fact. Preserve evidence ceilings.

### Theatre as secret future-training curriculum
Theatre must remain intrinsically worthwhile as people, work, art, failure, craft, and fun. Do not make current scenes wink at a future dangerous bluff or behave like covert combat/misdirection training.

### Plot swallowing ordinary life
Danger, magic, mystery, and reputation should collide with food, wages, customers, rehearsal, errands, fatigue, sex, household work, broken objects, weather, routes, and schedules rather than replacing them.

## Continuity / serial anti-patterns

### Callback parade
Do not revive old people, objects, jokes, or places merely to prove continuity. Prefer changed callbacks where prior history materially alters the return.

### Sitcom reset
Do not erase accumulated social, material, financial, bodily, work, or relationship consequences after a chapter or mini-arc unless the story genuinely establishes recovery or reversal.

### Economic reset through an empty purse
Do not let a temporary low cash-on-hand number erase Greg's accumulated earning capacity, assets, work access, city knowledge, bargaining position, or ability to choose. A later-book Greg with one copper in his room is not automatically equivalent to an early-book Greg whose entire economic life was one copper.

A liquidity dip can constrain a specific choice. It should not automatically make Greg anxious about every cheap purchase, praise himself for saving every coin, or narrate ordinary spending as if progress never happened.

### Inflation as fake progression
Do not make the economy feel like a game zone where every later loaf, bath, ride, wage, or repair simply gets a larger number because the protagonist progressed. Keep ordinary price layers locally coherent. Let the economic ladder widen upward through better work, larger tools, specialist services, valuable materials, travel, contracts, assets, and magical items.

### Every chapter services every engine
A chapter does not need to advance household, theatre, magic, threat, debt, friendship, and worldbuilding simultaneously. Let engines rotate and breathe.

### Every detail becomes setup
Do not convert ordinary texture into compulsory foreshadowing. Some objects, errands, jokes, customers, and mistakes are valuable because they make life feel inhabited even if they never become plot machinery.

### State-file pseudo-canon
Do not let a compact summary harden an inference, possibility, or stale endpoint into fact. Exact manuscript evidence remains the authority when wording, chronology, knowledge state, or scene detail matters.

## Prose / editorial anti-patterns

### Explain the moment after it lands
Do not restate the emotional meaning, joke, theme, or implication of a scene that already communicated it.

### Neat lesson endings
Do not force chapters or conversations into clean morals, symmetrical conclusions, or polished emotional takeaways. Awkwardness, interruption, uncertainty, and unfinished business are allowed.

### Mechanical AI cadence
Resist repeated fragment stacks, perfect rhetorical reversals, suspiciously balanced dialogue, everyone being equally witty, and identical chapter architecture. Use intentional variation rather than texture-by-formula.

### Research dump / procedure fetish
Research should change behavior, mistakes, objects, timing, hierarchy, shorthand, or consequences. Do not paste interesting procedure into prose after the story-relevant effect is already clear.

### Evidence inflation in magic
Do not turn one observation into repetition, repetition into reliability, reliability into generalization, or a tiny controlled effect into arbitrary range/combat/theatrical utility.

### Disability as lesson, tragedy, inspiration, or superpower
Keep Greg's body materially present through activity-specific geometry and cost without making every scene rehabilitation, suffering, moral instruction, or miraculous adaptation.

### Money as RPG abstraction or morality meter
Material improvement may stick. Debt and scarcity may remain real. Do not invent unsupported exact balances/conversions or make every ordinary purchase prove virtue or irresponsibility.

### Wallet melodrama
Do not automatically convert `this is all the cash I currently have` into `this object is too expensive`. Price, affordability, value, liquidity, and opportunity cost are different questions. Greg can afford something and decline it. He can be cash-light and still spend. He can regret a purchase without the narration declaring a moral failure.

See `ECONOMY_CONTINUITY.md` when a money scene carries real narrative weight.

## Use test

When something feels exciting but suspiciously easy, ask:

1. Is this supported by exact prose or merely convenient?
2. Does this give Greg knowledge, competence, attention, or control he has not earned?
3. Does it steal independent causality from another person?
4. Does it replace ordinary life instead of colliding with it?
5. Does it turn uncertainty into certainty too quickly?
6. Is this genuinely changed continuity, or just a callback?
7. Am I adding machinery when existing material could converge?
8. Would the scene be stronger if it trusted the reader and stopped earlier?
9. If money is driving the beat, is the reaction calibrated to Greg's current economic stage rather than only the purse snapshot?

If none of these traps apply, move on. Do not use this file to create fear of writing or to sand away useful surprise.
