# PEG-LEG GREG — STORY NORTH STAR

This file records durable artistic direction for the whole project. It is not plot canon and does not outrank manuscript prose. It exists so fresh Manuscript, Writers' Room, Story Control, editorial, character, setting, and research workers understand what the serial is trying to become.

## Audience / permission

Peg-Leg Greg is adult / mature crossover serial fantasy. Ordinary profanity, including strong profanity, is already part of the voice and does not need to be sanitized. Profanity is permission, not the project's identity.

The natural audience is readers who enjoy character-heavy fantasy, slice-of-life and workplace texture, competence without power-fantasy escalation, long continuity, slow accumulation, lived relationships, and a world worth inhabiting for a very long time.

## Core addictive engine: accumulation

The repeatable pleasure is not only plot advancement. It is **accumulation**.

Over time:
- people remember things
- jokes acquire history
- work creates shorthand
- mistakes leave residue
- relationships develop distinct languages
- places become socially legible
- Greg learns routes, bodies, jobs, audiences, systems, and limits
- reputation travels unevenly
- money and favors carry memory
- small objects, roles, customers, arguments, and habits can matter again much later

The desired feeling is:

**I know these people and this place, and I want to see what happens to them tomorrow.**

Long serial length is an artistic advantage when time leaves evidence behind.

## Emotional center: belonging through accumulation

A major emergent strength of the story is that a displaced person builds an ordinary life so slowly that one day it is no longer temporary.

Belonging should be earned through:
- work
- routes
- obligations
- money
- intimacy
- irritation
- people expecting Greg
- Greg expecting other people
- backstage and household routines
- favors and debts
- social memory
- competence that others quietly adapt around
- being missed, invited, wanted, needed, teased, corrected, and trusted in different ways

Do not announce belonging as a theme. Let accumulated behavior prove it.

## Make time leave residue

Every substantial stretch of chapters should leave changes in some combination of:
- relationships
- social roles
- jokes / nicknames / reputations
- work competence
- household routine
- money and material conditions
- city familiarity
- physical habits
- theatrical craft
- magic evidence
- unresolved pressure

Do not use sitcom reset unless the story specifically demands one.

## Structural boundaries follow story movement

Act and Book boundaries should be recognized when the manuscript's **governing question changes**, not reserved for predetermined chapter counts or symmetrical structures.

Book III may contain additional acts beyond **THE PRICE OF ATTENTION** if the actual prose has entered a distinct movement. Story Control should periodically inspect the recent **20–40 chapter run** for an earned act boundary rather than allowing an old act label to stretch indefinitely because no dramatic break was scheduled.

Likewise, if Book III's central movement has completed and a genuinely new governing phase has begun, allow **Book IV** rather than indefinitely extending Book III. A new Act means the existing life has developed a new governing concern; a new Book should mark a more substantial change in the story's operating condition.

Do **not** manufacture a dramatic event, climax, quest, danger spike, or arbitrary chapter milestone merely to justify a boundary. The boundary may become visible retrospectively through accumulated changes in Greg's work, wants, relationships, money, magic, social position, knowledge, or range of action.

Structure should describe what the story became, not force the story to become the structure.

## Social density over lore density

Carrow and later places should grow increasingly navigable through people, not only exposition.

Favor:
- repeat customers
- shopkeepers Greg recognizes
- people who know Lyssa before they know Greg
- audience members returning
- neighbors with their own concerns
- people already arguing when Greg arrives
- obligations that began off-page
- side characters changing jobs, dating, borrowing, owing, helping, resenting, celebrating, disappearing, returning
- rumors and reputations that are partial or wrong

The world must continue when Greg looks away.

## Relationships should be different species

Do not flatten every relationship into supportive banter or exposition.

Examples of distinct relationship languages:
- Greg / Lyssa: intimacy, sex, domestic labor, irritation, affection, money, independence, boredom, disagreement
- Greg / Hessa: intellectual trust, methodological discipline, evidence, irritation, limits
- Greg / Nessa: workplace shorthand, practical authority, correction, teasing, accumulated backstage trust
- Greg / Marek: affection, stupidity, rivalry, embarrassment, jokes, occasional surprise competence
- Greg / Teren: professional correction and slowly earned respect without mandatory sentimentality

Also develop relationships Greg is not part of.

## Greg may be messy

Greg is intelligent and observant, not a mastermind and not morally optimized.

He may be:
- petty
- jealous
- insecure
- avoidant
- stubborn
- wrong for more than one scene
- irrational about money
- overconfident after limited success
- unfair to someone
- annoying to Lyssa
- reluctant to admit a bad interpretation

Do not manufacture flaws on schedule. Let existing wants, scarcity, age, pride, fear, old-life assumptions, and second-life experience produce them naturally.

## Ordinary life remains important when stakes rise

Never let danger, magic, or future power erase the mundane material that made the story worth reading.

Even under serious pressure there can still be:
- rehearsal
- customers
- food
- cuffs altered after completion
- broken props
- wages
- errands
- bad sleep
- jokes
- people needing the cart
- somebody touching the thing Nessa said not to touch

High stakes should collide with ordinary life rather than replace it.

## Money remains real

Greg does not need to stay poor forever. Material improvement should be allowed to stick.

But coins, wages, debt, tools, housing, lending, saving, lost work, and risk should continue to feel materially real rather than becoming abstract RPG numbers.

## Reputation should be networked and inconsistent

Prefer many partial Gregs over one universal celebrity Greg.

Theatre people, Guild people, suppliers, neighbors, customers, creditors, audiences, and powerful people may know different stories about him. Reputation may arrive before him and be distorted.

Conventional fame is not required.

## Long-delay continuity

Use the serial's scale.

A thread may simmer for dozens of chapters if ordinary life remains active around it. A joke can return much later. A minor person can reappear after a long absence. A small lie, favor, mistake, costume, performance, customer, or object may acquire new meaning much later.

Prefer **micro-payoffs with macro-delay** over constant cliffhanger maintenance.

## Best major payoffs

The strongest "holy shit" moments should often be consequences of accumulated ordinary competence or social history.

Examples of shape, not mandated events:
- a bounded magic result changes the evidence model
- Greg performs genuinely well and realizes he belongs before anyone announces it
- an old joke becomes unexpectedly emotional
- a casual long-running character proves unexpectedly brave or capable
- Lyssa finally refuses something she has tolerated
- ordinary theatre/stagecraft later helps Greg survive a much stronger threat
- OLD GREG + SECOND-LIFE GREG + THEATRE GREG converge in a dangerous bluff that buys uncertainty or seconds rather than overpowering anyone

## Theatre remains intrinsically valuable

Visible theatre trajectory:

**FUN → PEOPLE → WORKPLACE → ACTING → STAGECRAFT → FAILURE → ANOTHER TURN**

Long-range possibility:

**THEATRE → PERFORMANCE → MISDIRECTION → ILLUSION → MASKS → CONTROL OF PERCEPTION → eventual dangerous bluff**

Do not expose the second trajectory as a training curriculum. The desired later reaction is:

**Oh shit. He learned that doing plays.**

## Anti-flattening / anti-AI watch

Major failure modes to actively resist:
- repeated tiny fragment stacks as default cadence
- neat rhetorical reversals everywhere
- every scene producing a clean lesson
- the prose explaining what an already-good moment means
- Greg noticing the right thing every time
- everyone sounding equally clever
- side characters existing only when Greg needs them
- Greg receiving every best joke / solution / insight
- thematic echoes that are too perfect
- repetitive chapter architecture
- procedural magic detail after the evidence is already clear
- power-fantasy escalation
- chosen-one logic
- grimdark escalation swallowing ordinary life
- theatre revealed as secret combat/bluff training
- relationships becoming Greg-support systems

Allow awkwardness, unfinished conversations, wrong interpretations, failed jokes, mundane scenes, interruptions, boredom, and human mess.

## Production test

When choosing between two otherwise valid directions, prefer the one that creates more **life per word**:
- more accumulated relationship
- more social memory
- more specific work
- more lived material consequence
- more future connective tissue

without bloating the prose or turning every detail into setup.
