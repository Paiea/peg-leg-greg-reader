# PEG-LEG GREG — STRAIGHTFORWARD ACTION PERMISSION

PLG has developed strong instincts around ambiguity, ecology, negotiation, evidence, and avoiding generic combat escalation.

Do not let those strengths become a ban on direct action.

Sometimes the satisfying answer is simply:

- the creature is trying to kill them;
- the ambush is real;
- Greg draws a weapon;
- somebody gets hurt;
- the fight has to be won;
- the dangerous thing cannot be negotiated with;
- escape fails and violence becomes necessary.

## Combat does not need an essay

A fight may reveal character, competence, fear, relationship, or world scale, but it does not need to produce a clean thematic lesson.

Sometimes action exists because danger is exciting.

Let physical stakes breathe without immediately translating every beat into meaning.

## Greg may win cleanly

Greg's old adventuring competence should sometimes produce decisive success.

Do not manufacture mistakes solely to avoid power fantasy.

A clean win can be satisfying when:
- the threat is within Greg's established competence range;
- he recognizes the problem correctly;
- preparation or field knowledge matters;
- the victory does not erase the wider world's scale;
- other characters remain useful and real.

## Greg may also be outmatched

Direct combat can help establish that S-class does not terminate the setting.

The useful distinction is not `Greg always wins` versus `Greg always struggles`.

It is:

**match the outcome to the actual situation.**

Some threats should fold quickly.
Some should require teamwork.
Some should require retreat.
Some should make Greg realize fighting would be idiotic.

## Preserve physical specificity

Prefer:
- terrain;
- distance;
- fatigue;
- footing;
- injury;
- equipment;
- body size;
- timing;
- visibility;
- panic;
- practiced movement;
- imperfect communication.

Avoid abstract RPG-stat combat and endless exchange-of-attacks choreography.

Action should feel lived in the same way the rest of PLG feels lived.
