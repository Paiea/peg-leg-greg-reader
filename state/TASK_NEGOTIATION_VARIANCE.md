# TASK NEGOTIATION VARIANCE

Durable scene-grammar guardrail. Not canon.

## Problem
Greg is appropriately careful about scope, pay, time, access, safety, and who owns which decision. That competence can become repetitive when too many scenes begin with explicit terms negotiation before the interesting human material starts.

## Rule
Do not make every offer scene prove Greg's intelligence through contract clarification.

Sometimes the terms are already clear.
Sometimes the job is too small to justify negotiation.
Sometimes Greg trusts the person enough to ask one question and go.
Sometimes he accepts first and clarifies later when the ambiguity actually matters.
Sometimes another person raises the scope problem before Greg does.
Sometimes Greg misses a term and pays for the oversight.
Sometimes the negotiation happens off-page or in one sentence.

## Preserve competence without ritual
Greg may remain financially and procedurally competent while using different behavior depending on:
- size of payment;
- risk;
- relationship;
- urgency;
- familiarity with the work;
- how much he cares;
- whether he is tired, distracted, amused, irritated, or curious.

## Escalation rule
Detailed negotiation earns page space when the terms themselves create pressure, reveal character, alter consent, or become important later.

If the reader already understands the offer and the exact terms do not matter, compress.

## Test
If a new work scene starts with `pay? / duration? / scope? / if it expands? / who signs?`, ask whether those questions are actually the scene or merely a familiar proof that Greg is careful.
