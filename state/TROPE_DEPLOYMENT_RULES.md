# PEG-LEG GREG — TROPE DEPLOYMENT RULES

This file governs when recognizable fantasy tropes should enter the story. It is development guidance, not plot canon.

## Core rule

Do not reject a trope because it is familiar. Reject it when it adds less life than it costs.

A trope earns space when it provides one or more of:
- immediate reader orientation;
- momentum;
- pleasure / fantasy candy;
- a clean reason to move characters;
- a useful social container;
- a recognizable structure that lets stranger PLG-specific material emerge without requiring constant explanation.

## Strong deployment moments

Use familiar fantasy structures when the story needs:
- a clean departure from Carrow;
- a temporary social arena with clear stakes;
- a reason to gather characters who would not otherwise meet;
- travel through new regions;
- a physical challenge after a long social/workplace stretch;
- a contained competition;
- acquisition pressure;
- contact with a new institution or class of people;
- contrast with the serial's ordinary-life baseline.

## Weak deployment moments

Avoid inserting a trope merely because the recent chapters feel quiet.

Do not use recognizable fantasy furniture as filler:
- random dungeon because fantasy should have dungeons;
- tournament because serials have tournament arcs;
- noble ball without social or plot pressure;
- rival party whose only purpose is to be rivals;
- monster contract with no lasting consequence;
- mysterious artifact that exists only to promise later importance.

## Familiar first, specific second

A trope may provide the reader-facing frame while PLG-specific pressures determine what actually matters.

Example shape:

`escort job` may actually become a family / love / political disaster.

`tournament` may actually become an acquisition problem, reputation problem, or relationship arena.

`ruin expedition` may actually become a labor, salvage-rights, provenance, ecology, or local-history conflict.

`monster hunt` may actually become a genuinely straightforward dangerous fight.

Do not force every trope to subvert itself. Sometimes the familiar pleasure should remain intact.

## Production test

Before deploying a recognizable trope, ask:

1. Why now?
2. What existing Greg want or pressure makes it plausible?
3. What PLG-specific texture will pass through it?
4. What consequence can remain afterward?
5. Would this still be fun if the reader recognizes the trope immediately?

If the answer to #5 is no, the execution is leaning too heavily on novelty.
