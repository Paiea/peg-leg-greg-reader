# PEG-LEG GREG — TROPE MUTATION GUIDE

Use familiar fantasy structures as scaffolding, not prefab rooms.

When a trope enters PLG, mutate it through at least several existing story systems before inventing new machinery.

Useful mutation lenses:

- Greg's age-asymmetry and old-life experience;
- money, debt, wages, material cost;
- theatre, work, craft, and practical competence;
- social memory and recurring relationships;
- local institutions and uneven reputation;
- magical ecology and fieldcraft;
- current relationship obligations;
- imperfect future knowledge;
- ordinary travel logistics;
- consequences that remain after the exciting scene.

## Mutation test

Before accepting a familiar setup, ask:

1. Why does Greg care beyond `adventure`?
2. Who else has a life inside this setup?
3. What material or social constraint makes this specifically PLG?
4. What does Greg know that changes the obvious response?
5. What does Greg not know that still leaves room for surprise?
6. What survives afterward?

Do not require all six to be dramatic. A few concrete answers are usually enough.

## Example mutations

### Tournament

Generic function: public contest and hierarchy.

PLG mutations may include:
- Greg wants a lower-status prize;
- participation costs time or wages;
- reputation differs by audience;
- another contestant's personal stakes matter more than Greg's;
- Greg's objective is acquisition, access, or information rather than victory;
- theatre / performance experience changes presentation but does not secretly become combat training.

### Ruin / dungeon

Generic function: enclosed dangerous exploration.

PLG mutations may include:
- salvage ownership and provenance;
- local guides with superior site-specific knowledge;
- old infrastructure still affecting nearby communities;
- boring logistics becoming critical under pressure;
- known monster behavior versus unknown historical magic;
- Greg returning with an object whose inconvenience continues for months.

### Monster contract

Generic function: dangerous creature must be handled.

Possible outcomes:
- kill it;
- redirect it;
- wait it out;
- discover the wrong creature was blamed;
- learn the local contract framing was incomplete;
- succeed cleanly because Greg actually knows this species.

Do not force every branch into moral ambiguity.

### Noble-family conflict

Generic function: concentrated wealth / power / inheritance.

PLG mutations:
- family members disagree privately;
- servants, creditors, spouses, lovers, tradespeople, and dependents have independent incentives;
- title does not erase money problems;
- Greg is useful without becoming politically central;
- a family scandal may matter more to the family than the supposedly epic issue.

## Preserve the pleasure

Mutation should enrich a trope, not dissolve the thing readers came for.

If using a tournament, let there actually be a tournament.
If using a ruin, let exploration matter.
If using a monster hunt, allow danger and combat.
If using a masquerade, let disguise, social performance, attraction, status, and secrets be fun.

Do not mutate until only an essay about the trope remains.
