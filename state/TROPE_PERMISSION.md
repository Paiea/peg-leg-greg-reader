# PEG-LEG GREG — TROPE PERMISSION

This file exists to correct an over-avoidance pattern.

Recognizable fantasy tropes are allowed.

Do not reject a direction merely because readers have seen the broad shape before. Familiar structures can provide pleasure, speed, orientation, payoff, and contrast.

The real test is not `is this trope familiar?`

The real test is:

**Does PLG metabolize the trope through its established character logic, economics, social density, continuity, ordinary-life texture, and world history?**

Useful familiar structures may include:

- tournaments;
- ruins / dungeons;
- dangerous escorts;
- retrieval missions;
- rival adventurers / rival parties;
- magical forests;
- cursed or coveted artifacts;
- noble-family pressure;
- road ambushes;
- caravans;
- monster contracts;
- masquerades;
- hidden societies;
- treasure maps;
- bounty work;
- missing-person jobs;
- dangerous expeditions;
- legacy weapons or relics;
- public contests with private motives.

These are not mandatory arcs and should not be spammed.

## Familiar shape, PLG consequence

A good PLG trope usually keeps the recognizable doorway while changing the consequences.

Examples of shape:

- tournament where Greg wants the wrong prize;
- ruin expedition where salvage rights, food, guides, debt, and local history matter as much as traps;
- monster contract where sometimes the creature truly does need killing and the story does not apologize for that;
- noble family that behaves like an actual family rather than a heraldic plot machine;
- rival adventurer who has rent, grudges, old injuries, embarrassing friends, and a life outside Greg;
- cursed object whose material inconvenience matters after the exciting acquisition scene;
- magical forest that also contains roads, workers, hunters, boundaries, and people who are tired of outsiders acting impressed.

## Do not overcomplicate everything

PLG does not need to subvert every trope.

Sometimes:

- the monster is hostile;
- the treasure is valuable;
- the ambush is an ambush;
- the rival really does want to beat Greg;
- the ruin contains dangerous old magic;
- the escort job really is about getting from A to B alive.

Complexity is not automatically depth.

Straightforward fantasy pleasure is allowed when it creates momentum, contrast, satisfaction, or a useful change of rhythm.

## Anti-overcorrection

Avoid:

- reflexively turning every fight into ecology discourse;
- making every villain secretly misunderstood;
- making every artifact undesirable;
- making every heroic structure ironic;
- treating every conventional fantasy beat as evidence of bad writing;
- replacing pleasure with cleverness;
- requiring every familiar setup to reveal a hidden second plot.

The story has already established a strong baseline. That baseline now gives it permission to use familiar fantasy grammar without losing identity.

A useful production phrase is:

**known shape, specific life.**
