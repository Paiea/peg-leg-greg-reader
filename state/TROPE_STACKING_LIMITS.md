# PEG-LEG GREG — TROPE STACKING LIMITS

Development guidance, not canon.

## Core rule

Do not make an arc feel richer by stacking every recognizable fantasy structure into it.

One strong primary trope is usually enough to orient an arc. A second may complicate it. More than that should emerge organically rather than being loaded in at design time.

Preferred pattern:

**PRIMARY FRAME + ONE COMPLICATION + PLG-SPECIFIC CONSEQUENCES**

Examples:
- escort job + hidden romantic conflict;
- tournament + unusual acquisition goal;
- ruin expedition + rare ecological hazard;
- caravan route + rival human interest;
- noble gathering + provenance / debt pressure.

Avoid:

`tournament + prophecy + demon cult + lost prince + cursed sword + secret royal bloodline + ancient dragon`

unless years of accumulated story actually earned those elements independently.

## Myth-seed restraint

Moʻolelo-derived mechanics should follow the same rule.

Do not combine five source seeds merely because they are individually interesting.

For a major adventure, prefer:
- one dominant emotional / mythic mechanism;
- zero to two secondary strange-world elements;
- normal travel, work, food, money, irritation, and social life between them.

The strange becomes stronger when surrounded by ordinary material.

## Reader load

If the reader must simultaneously learn:
- a new region;
- a new magic system;
- a new species;
- a new political hierarchy;
- a new religion;
- a new ancient history;
- and a new quest structure,

the story is probably overloading the departure.

Let new knowledge arrive through use and recurrence rather than preloading lore.

## Test

Before adding another trope or mythic mechanism, ask:

**Does this create a new kind of pressure, or merely another kind of fantasy object?**

If it is only another fantasy object, cut or delay it.
