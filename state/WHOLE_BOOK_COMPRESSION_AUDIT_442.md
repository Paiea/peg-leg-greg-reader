## Governing conclusion

The manuscript does **not** present as uniformly overlong.

The first full-machine pass over 442 published chapter texts found only **11 unusually similar adjacent pairs** at the conservative review threshold. All machine candidates were low-confidence until human review. The median current chapter length is approximately **2,738 words**.

The useful signal is **candidate concentration**, not individual lexical similarity.

The strongest compression opportunity is localized in stretches where the manuscript performs the same broad dramatic job repeatedly before changing state. Later manuscript material is materially cleaner by this measure: **no adjacent machine hotspot was detected from Chapter 329 through Chapter 442**. Chapter 327–328 was the last detected pair, and human review does not support treating it as a merge candidate.

This supports a selective-compression strategy rather than a global shortening mandate.

Core rule remains:

> **Compress repeated dramatic functions, not quietness.**

# Ranked human-reviewed candidates

## 1. Chapters 144–152 — first touring stop / rehearsal / two-show sequence

Stable range: `plg-ch-000144` through `plg-ch-000152`  
Confidence: **HIGH**  
Recommended operation: **TIGHTEN across the range; evaluate one or two local MERGE/SUMMARIZE-IN-SCENE moves after mapping dependencies**  
Structural target: roughly **20–30% reduction if the prose supports it**, not a mandatory quota.

### Why this is the strongest hotspot

Three separate machine hits occur inside only nine chapters:

- 144–145
- 149–150
- 151–152

That density matters more than any single pair score.

Chapter 144 establishes the stable, sleeping logistics, new theatre people, local work texture, and the fact that touring changes ordinary life. Those are real first functions and should survive.

By Chapters 149–152, however, the book spends substantial page time repeatedly showing:

- backstage disorder;
- costume/prop repair;
- Teren giving minimal directions;
- Greg learning that theatre survives mistakes;
- people eating/resting/repairing between work;
- audience reactions changing improvisation;
- repeated versions of Sword and Shopkeeper scenes;
- the company being messy but professionally adaptive.

Chapter 149 is valuable because Greg experiences an open rehearsal in front of a changing town audience and learns not to chase remembered laughs or visual cues. Chapter 150 is valuable because he finally understands touring theatre as paid work rather than a special event. Chapter 151 proves the first real show works under live failure. Chapter 152 proves the second show is genuinely different rather than a replay.

Those are four distinct functions. The problem is that each function currently carries a large amount of repeated theatre-process texture around it.

### Compression direction

Do **not** collapse the stop into a travel montage.

Prefer:

- preserve the arrival/stable material that makes touring physically specific;
- preserve the first meaningful audience-learning failures;
- preserve the `That's the work` realization;
- preserve the first real paid-show threshold;
- preserve enough of the second show to prove variation, audience feedback, and live adaptation;
- remove or summarize repeated prop/costume/backstage maintenance once the reader already understands how the company operates;
- reduce repeated examples of the same improvisation lesson;
- consider whether Chapter 150 can absorb selected bridge material from 149 or 151 without losing its work/identity function;
- consider whether the second show needs every repeated scene beat once its governing contrast is established.

### Protect

- Greg's newness to touring;
- disability-specific sleeping/access logistics;
- Serra/Marek/Iven differences as performers;
- money becoming real theatre income;
- the company's independent work competence;
- audience-specific variation;
- local-town texture;
- later callbacks whose first occurrence lives here.

## 2. Chapters 208–213 — Bren / wheel-threat pressure and theatre security loop

Stable range: `plg-ch-000208` through approximately `plg-ch-000213`  
Confidence: **HIGH**  
Recommended operation: **TIGHTEN + SUMMARIZE-IN-SCENE; evaluate selective merge only after dependency map**

### Why

This is the clearest example of a storyline whose **evidence changes**, but whose surrounding dramatic procedure repeats.

The sequence repeatedly returns to:

- checking whether there is a note under the cup;
- Greg/others restating what is and is not known;
- checking doors/cart/wheels;
- theatre members reacting to possible threat;
- Greg resisting conspiracy inflation;
- ordinary theatre work continuing beside the pressure.

The actual evidence is useful and should not be flattened:

- Chapter 208 establishes the post-threat security posture and the company's attempt to keep working.
- Chapter 209 introduces the possible name **Bren** and a separate cart-yard report using similar extortion language.
- Chapter 210 gives concrete escalation: the theatre cart's wheel retaining pin has been deliberately pulled.
- Chapter 211 explicitly narrows the inference again and brings in a new outside person/evidence lane rather than permitting Greg to invent an organization.
- Chapter 212 turns partly toward Vale debt/work, while still repeating some theatre wheel-checking residue.

The compression opportunity is therefore **not** “remove the Bren storyline.” It is to preserve the information-changing scenes while reducing the repeated security/checking wrapper around them.

### Compression direction

A good compressed version should feel like pressure accumulating faster:

1. threat aftermath / bounded precautions;
2. Bren name + independent corroboration;
3. actual wheel-pin sabotage;
4. one clean follow-up that narrows what this proves and moves the story onward.

Independent Vale, Lyssa, theatre, and work material should survive where it earns its own function.

Repeated morning `nothing under the cup`, cart-check, door-check, and evidence-recap beats are prime tightening material once their emotional purpose has already landed.

This is the stretch most likely to produce the user's earlier intuition that roughly six pressure scenes could become closer to three or four effective movements.

### Protect

- the distinction between rumor, observation, and corroboration;
- the fact that Bren probably is Bren without manufacturing a grand organization;
- Rinna/Jori/Davin/Pell reacting differently;
- ordinary theatre work continuing under pressure;
- Vale material as an independent lane;
- any first callback that later chapters rely on.

## 4. Chapters 60–61 — two-day road inspection

Stable IDs: `plg-ch-000060`, `plg-ch-000061`  
Confidence: **MEDIUM-HIGH**  
Recommended operation: **TIGHTEN, probably keep both chapters**

### Why

Chapter 60 teaches Greg and the reader the road-inspection grammar:

- complaint versus observed condition;
- maps versus physical history;
- priority based on consequence plus change;
- inspection versus repair;
- local infrastructure remembering older systems.

Chapter 61 earns its existence because rain changes the evidence. Several things that seemed stable on day one become active, and a seemingly obvious repair is complicated by an old unrecorded farm fix. This is a real progression and a good example of PLG's evidence discipline.

The repeated material is procedural:

- repeated culvert/site arrival structure;
- repeated probing / note-taking / priority classification;
- repeated Pessa correction cycles after the reader understands the framework.

### Compression direction

Keep Day One and Day Two.

Make Day One establish the grammar with fewer examples. Make Day Two cash it out by revisiting only the sites whose meaning actually changes after rain, plus one fresh mistake that proves Greg has not mastered the work instantly.

This should become **sharper fieldwork**, not less worldbuilding.

# Whole-book shape conclusions

## Book I / early material

The machine did **not** find a broad early-book structural repetition crisis.

Its early hits were primarily Chapters 60–61 and 75–76, both of which are multi-day physical/professional learning sequences with legitimate progression.

The earlier manuscript may still benefit strongly from prose maturity, voice normalization, and local tightening, but the first audit does **not** support taking a chainsaw to Book I.

Early chapters are often materially longer than the current median, so sentence/scene compression may produce meaningful word-count savings even when chapter structure remains intact.

## Mid-book / touring and theatre accumulation

This is currently the strongest compression territory.

The 140s–160s contain many valuable firsts, but also more repeated proof of theatre logistics and adaptability than the mature manuscript probably needs.

The correct operation is to keep the lived touring experience while reducing the number of examples needed to establish a pattern.

## Early Book III / pressure and work loops

The 190s–210s deserve targeted review, especially the Bren/wheel-pressure run.

This is where the story sometimes repeats a known condition or evidence posture before introducing the next meaningful variable.

That makes it strong `TIGHTEN / SUMMARIZE-IN-SCENE` territory.

## Chapters 249–328

There are isolated candidates rather than a continuous compression zone.

250–251 is worth tightening. 327–328 should mostly survive. A later audit pass may find non-adjacent repeated functions that simple lexical comparison cannot see, particularly artifact-shopping or work-loop patterns, but the current evidence does not justify broad cuts across this entire span.

## Chapters 329–442

**No adjacent machine repetition candidate crossed the audit threshold.**

This is one of the strongest findings of the first pass.

It matches the qualitative observation that later PLG chapters have become shorter, more varied in governing function, and more willing to let a scene make one move and leave.

Do not use the existence of a compression project as permission to homogenize or aggressively shorten the current adventure/travel material.

Late PLG should instead become a calibration reference for how much proof the manuscript now needs before moving on.

# Final editorial judgment

The first whole-book pass does **not** say, “PLG is a million words because every section is bloated.”

It says something more useful:

> PLG has accumulated several stretches where a valuable pattern was demonstrated more times than the mature version of the book now needs.

That is fixable without changing the book's identity.

The likely quality gain comes from making the reader experience **fewer redundant proofs and more distinct life per chapter**.

Do not optimize for a target word count.

Optimize for the point where the reader understands a pattern, then let the next chapter change the pattern.
