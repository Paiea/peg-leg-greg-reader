# PEG-LEG GREG — WHOLE-BOOK COMPRESSION PRIORITY

Purpose: prioritize whole-book structural audit work without assuming a fixed problem range or compressing every era equally.

## Core rule

Audit the whole manuscript.

Compress only where evidence shows repeated dramatic function, authoring immaturity, or avoidable drag.

Do not assume Chapters 150-300 are uniquely defective merely because they were initially suspected.

## Priority signals

A range rises in audit priority when several of these appear together:
- repeated scene architecture across neighboring chapters;
- repeated procedural demonstrations after the system is established;
- multiple chapters ending on nearly the same emotional conclusion;
- recurring characters whose voices are less differentiated than later authority establishes;
- threat pressure repeated without new consequence;
- shopping/work/magic/body scenes with similar decision structure and little state change;
- arcs already independently remembered by the author as overextended;
- later manuscript reveals that several scenes were all performing one setup function;
- reader/editor feedback repeatedly points to drag or anticlimax;
- chapter function can be summarized with the same sentence several times in a short run.

## Protection signals

A range drops in compression priority when it contains:
- first meaningful relationship development;
- foundational social-group formation;
- unique ordinary-life texture later reused as shorthand;
- major body/mobility transitions;
- major money/material changes;
- precise magic-evidence progression;
- first location/institution introduction;
- strong later callbacks;
- unusually effective humor/intimacy/atmosphere;
- a distinct story movement that has not yet repeated.

Low plot velocity alone does not raise priority.

## Audit sequence

Use a rolling whole-book map rather than one giant rewrite.

Recommended order:
1. finish/freeze the active prose/dialogue pass;
2. identify 10-25 chapter audit windows;
3. prioritize known repetition hotspots first for calibration;
4. sample early, middle, and later-book windows to avoid anchoring on one era;
5. compare how the manuscript's craft changed over time;
6. expand into lower-priority ranges only after the compression engine proves it can preserve life.

## Maturity-aware comparison

Later PLG may teach earlier PLG better craft, but later density is not automatically the standard every early range must match.

Use `state/HISTORICAL_VOICE_MATURITY_RULE.md`.

Ask whether a difference is:
- intentional character/world development;
- relationship history not yet earned;
- or simply earlier authoring machinery that can now be improved.

## Hotspot report

For each audited window, record:
- range;
- likely structural-compression value: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW;
- dominant repetition type;
- strongest protected material;
- likely classifications;
- dependency risk;
- illustration/publication complexity;
- recommended next action.

Do not convert this into a mechanical score that overrides editorial judgment.

## Expected pattern

It is plausible that middle-book stretches receive more compression than the opening or current edge because the manuscript had enough established engines to repeat before later craft rules improved.

This is a hypothesis, not a mandate.

The whole-book audit exists to test it.

## Goal

The final edited manuscript should feel like one increasingly deep story whose pace and voice mature naturally, not like early books were crudely rewritten into the late-book style or like every chapter was forced through the same efficiency target.
