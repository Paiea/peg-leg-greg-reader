# PEG-LEG GREG — WHOLE-BOOK STRUCTURAL AUDIT

Purpose: evaluate the entire manuscript for structural efficiency without assuming every range needs compression or that Chapters 150-350 are uniquely defective.

## Core rule

**Audit the whole book. Compress selectively.**

The manuscript evolved while it was being written. Early, middle, and late PLG may differ because:
- the author discovered character voices;
- Greg's social world accumulated;
- the prose rhythm matured;
- dialogue attribution improved;
- the book learned when to trust quietness;
- procedural engines became established;
- fantasy/adventure scope widened;
- action and travel became more confident;
- the story gained better long-range causal movement.

Do not assume one chapter range contains all structural debt.

## Audit is not surgery

A whole-book audit may classify every chapter or arc, but it does not authorize rewriting every chapter.

Use the audit to locate hotspots:
- repetitive arcs;
- duplicated dramatic function;
- obsolete explanation density;
- relationship reset;
- overlong procedure cycles;
- weak transitions;
- early voice flattening;
- stretches where the same engine receives pressure without transformation.

Then execute compression only where the expected gain is real.

## Audit dimensions

For each bounded range, assess:

### STRUCTURE
Does each chapter create a new state, consequence, relationship movement, world behavior, or distinct ordinary-life value?

### REPETITION
Are multiple scenes proving the same trait or problem after the reader already understands it?

### VOICE MATURITY
Are weak passages weak because the author had not yet found the character's voice, or because the character is intentionally less familiar/less confident at that point?

### SOCIAL ACCUMULATION
Does the range build memory and belonging, or repeatedly re-establish the same relationship facts?

### PROCEDURE
Once work/magic/theatre/economic routines are established, does the manuscript keep replaying full setup without a changed variable?

### FORWARD PULL
What question or desire carries the reader into the next chapter? Does a long range have enough cumulative movement even if individual chapters are quiet?

### PAYOFF VALUE
Do later chapters depend emotionally or causally on this material being fully lived rather than merely stated?

### PUBLICATION COST
Would a structural change affect art, URLs, act structure, or reader metadata? This affects migration planning, not editorial truth.

## Expected pattern, not predetermined verdict

A reasonable current hypothesis is:
- early Book I may need more voice/prose normalization than structural cutting;
- some middle stretches may contain the highest repeated-function density;
- later manuscript may need lighter structural compression but still deserves audit;
- specific arcs can violate that expectation in either direction.

Do not encode those guesses as automatic decisions.

## Batch strategy

Audit in coherent batches, usually 10-25 chapters or one clear arc segment.

For each batch produce:
- dominant engine(s);
- what changed across the range;
- repeated functions;
- strongest keepers;
- likely compression candidates;
- voice-maturity issues distinct from structural issues;
- continuity/art/publication risk;
- recommended next action: NONE / PROSE ONLY / VOICE ONLY / COMPRESSION MAP / HIGH-RISK REVIEW.

## Heatmap concept

The whole-book audit should eventually support a simple editorial heatmap rather than a single global cut target.

Useful labels:
- GREEN: structurally healthy, normal prose polish only;
- YELLOW: local repetition/tightening opportunity;
- ORANGE: likely merge/summarize hotspot;
- RED: structurally repetitive or dependency-tangled, requires deliberate map;
- BLUE: voice/prose maturity issue more than structural issue.

A range may carry more than one note, but avoid turning this into fake numerical precision.

## No global compression quota

Do not decide in advance that the book must lose 20 percent, 100 chapters, or a fixed number of words.

The manuscript may lose a lot in one arc and almost nothing in another.

The goal is stronger reading momentum and denser accumulation.

## Long-form reader test

At the end of each major audit region ask:

- If a reader loves PLG's ordinary-life promise, are we preserving what they came for?
- If a reader is getting impatient, which repeated functions are causing that impatience?
- Does later payoff become stronger if this material remains lived?
- Would deleting this make a later relationship, joke, competence reveal, homecoming, or adventure contrast feel thinner?
- Is the story asking the reader to understand something they already understand?

## Recommended sequencing after current editor pass

1. finish/freeze current dialogue/voice/prose authority;
2. build chapter identity/publication migration support before destructive renumbering;
3. run one known compression calibration batch;
4. evaluate result as a reader;
5. begin whole-book audit batch by batch;
6. compress hotspots, not every yellow flag;
7. periodically reread larger arcs after local edits to catch over-optimization.

## Final principle

The book's growth is evidence.

Later PLG can teach the editor what earlier PLG was trying to become, but the editor must not erase the fact that characters, relationships, and the world were genuinely earlier then.

**Use the mature book to clarify the early book, not impersonate it.**
