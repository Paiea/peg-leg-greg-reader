## High-value watch targets

- Greg owns the highest density of compressed dry counterpunches.
- Incidental strangers should not arrive calibrated to Greg at full speed.
- Recurring-character dryness is allowed; review machinery and density, not mere terseness.
- Distinguish safety `No`, job `No`, and comedy `No`.
- Repeated prediction is especially dangerous when stacked among several familiar characters.
- Threat pressure does not justify universal mind-reading. Alertness and suspicion should still route through visible evidence, spoken questions, rules, objects, and jurisdiction.
- A repeated phrase can become evidence of its own overdistribution; narration such as `Everyone said that now`, `leaning had become legally binding`, `Everyone keeps accusing me of looking`, `the entire city had agreed on one sentence`, or equivalent self-commentary is a warning to inspect the mechanism.
- A self-aware convergence alarm carries more weight after the pass has already identified the mechanism; identical later ladders have a higher burden to justify themselves.
- A wrong prediction can be healthy if the speaker has a plausible basis and the miss demonstrates fallibility.
- A recurring joke is healthier when the evidence becomes visible. `You leaned` is cheaper than `you were thinking` because the body actually did something.
- Relationship familiarity should change workflow and sharpen questions, not grant transcript access to Greg's unsaid sentence.
- Relationship shorthand is healthiest when it opens a question instead of replacing the other person's report.
- The protagonist can verbalize the impulse that a close relationship then challenges. This preserves intimacy without requiring the partner to narrate an exact unsaid plan.
- Let Greg voice the escalation. If he wants an address, search, connection, or larger claim, he can say it and let another character answer the stated idea.
- Schedule authority can answer behavior without diagnosing intent. A shared `Third bell` plus `Then eat` may be enough.
- Earned familiarity can compress directly to the current object. `Lamp? / Yes. / Get it.` is healthier shorthand than proving knowledge of an unsaid question when shared work history already identifies the subject.
- Visible affect is cheaper than hidden reasoning. Seeing excitement, strain, disappointment, pleasure, silence, leaning, or hypervigilance is not the same as knowing the exact inference behind it.
- Prefer current evidence: object placement, stopped movement, spoken argument, gaze target, route choice, body position, damaged equipment, schedule pressure, or work history.
- Current object or jurisdiction often beats generic perception: coat, debt history, wheel, route, apparatus, lamp, schedule, seal, food, cloth, contract, brace, knife, hat, pin, or current task can carry the exchange.
- A direct handling rule is often stronger than a personality read. `Do not touch those` can be complete without proving Greg intended to touch them.
- Naming the retired or protected object can replace predictive `No`. `Not the threads` is more Hessa-specific than rejecting an unsaid request.
- Object custody is especially useful under stress. Brace, knife, hat, runner pay, cart pin, repaired hardware, and other protected objects can carry boundaries without mental-process policing.
- Material authority should prefer the damaged object over the imagined action. `Then look lower` keeps Nessa centered on the cracked foot instead of Greg's hypothetical next move.
- Damaged-object custody can replace gaze interpretation. `Leave this one` is stronger Davin speech than debating what Greg was looking at.
- Craft authority can answer the object instead of the abstract question. `Not this one` / `It answers the cloth` can be stronger than either a lecture or a prediction ladder.
- Craft disagreement improves when both people state what they actually know: Greg can report how a stitch looks; Lyssa can report what it is doing.
- Possessive misunderstanding can carry social comedy without `you looked`. `Mine` / `Still mine` is enough when the joke is about ownership rather than perception.
- Front-house authority cares about timely usefulness. A thought that arrives after Rinna answered the customer has no sales value even if Greg truly had it.
- Spoken reasoning should outrank face-reading when both are available.
- Face-reading can be legitimate when observation is the scene mechanic, such as cards, acting, pain, visible physical strain, obvious relational affect, or marked hypervigilance.
- Face/body observation is especially legitimate when the body is physically part of the apparatus being controlled.
- Information-discipline voices should defend thresholds, not private thoughts. Antonius should protect the ledger threshold; Rinna should protect what is known; Hessa should protect the apparatus.
- Deliberately unsettling perception still needs provenance. Drell's unusual observation is safer because the text makes his viewing and secondhand information path part of the scene.
- Expert familiarity should improve the question, not eliminate the need to ask it.
- Expertise can demand the usable question. Hessa-style procedure is stronger when it asks Greg to state the specific testable question instead of rejecting a sentence he has not yet spoken.
- Let experts be wrong about Greg sometimes.
- Specialists should prefer current professional consequence over generic protagonist commentary.
- First-contact professional scope is stronger than instant Greg calibration. A courier, seller, worker, front clerk, runner, or venue contact can state what they use, protect, know, queue, or do not know without joining Greg's prediction game.
- Volunteer information is not mind-reading. Someone who came to discuss a rumor can simply state the relevant fact without claiming they caught an unasked sentence.
- Contract provenance can replace predictive prohibition. Ownership and customer-risk rules are often stronger than guessing what Greg intended to do with an object.
- Professional scope can be a stronger fingerprint than diction.
- Shared technical shortness is safe when responsibility remains visible.
- Shared workplace vocabulary such as `Again`, `No`, `Good`, and `Hold` is safe when authority remains local.
- Changed workflow is stronger relationship evidence than repeated banter.
- Rules and instructions are often better than predictions.
- A prediction can often become an instruction without losing the joke.
- Practical relationship care can become an immediate boundary instead of a prediction contest. Food, rest, route, or current work can carry concern without perfect mind-reading.
- Test attention is procedure, not personality. Control gaze/setup/sequence directly when those variables matter.
- Expectation control should regulate setup, sequence, target, phase, or interpretation rather than narrating Greg's internal optimization.
- Phase boundaries can replace personality policing.
- Apparatus changes can replace personality commentary.
- A specialist's stopping condition should come from what they protect.
- Professional self-limitation is voice.
- Not knowing can be professional competence when the missing information lies outside the character's responsibility.
- Attribution density and voice variance are separate problems.
- Name casing is continuity, not variance. Once `Evin` is source-settled, later reviewed `EvIn` drift should normalize separately rather than being treated as a voice edit.
- Stress should degrade optimization before it erases character.
- Practical care often sounds like logistics.
- Let the world continue without interpreting the protagonist.
- Let mundane competence stay mundane.
- Let Greg be socially young without turning ordinary desire into a system.
- Incidental strangers benefit from low narrative ambition.
- Ordinary workers should retain low narrative ambition during danger. Runners want their copper, customers want lamps fixed or tickets sold, witnesses have their own work waiting, and venue contacts have jobs to book.
- Underperformance is useful social variance. Plain approval, silence, awkwardness, or a small practical response can beat another polished comeback.
- Productive professional confusion creates voice. Bad explanations, changed notes, imperfect heuristics, improvisation, and local disagreement should not be optimized away.
- Overloaded speech can differentiate authority better than sharper banter.
- Productive repetition changes interpretation across attempts rather than merely restating the same conclusion.
- Expert disagreement can leave multiple live explanations.
- Being useful does not require being important.
- Workflow integration can establish belonging without anyone naming belonging.
- Imperfect art may stop at `works for the scene` where engineering would continue.
- Deferred maintenance can be character-specific dialogue when the current protected object outranks the repair.
- A failed apparatus can still produce good evidence. Parking an instrument is not the same as disproving the phenomenon.
- Social knowledge is safer when provenance stays ordinary and legible: gossip, public work, visible objects, shared history, runners, customers, or direct witnesses.
- Technical rules should usually be stated as rules rather than as proof the expert knows Greg privately considered breaking them.
- Schedule and assessment ownership can replace mental-process policing.
- Performance scenes may legitimately spend more attention on faces and reactions because attention itself is part of the craft evidence.
- Travel pressure naturally creates variance through fatigue, cramped space, missing information, moving work, meals, ropes, costumes, horses, bad roads, queues, broken schedules, threat precautions, and reduced travel inventory.
- Live work tolerates partial failure when no downstream beat depends on perfect recovery.
- Tour ecology protects variance through fatigue, money, weather, work interruption, local crew, transport, audience state, and incomplete information.
- Bad metaphors can be useful when they belong to the speaker. Partial, weird images may differentiate a character better than precise analysis.
- Naming can reduce attribution load without increasing exposition.
- Relationship inference is strongest when the evidence can be listed.
- Work can interrupt explanation. A knot, needle, wagon, costume, horse, wet stage, supper service, customer line, cloth table, runner, repair, or venue booking may end a conversation before anyone reaches a polished conclusion.
- Temporary comic convergence can be legitimate when shared improvisation is the current work. In an active comic performance, inspect whether voices re-separate offstage before patching the ensemble merely for sharing faster setup/payoff rhythm.
- A resource owner can often act instead of predicting. Covering a pot, protecting a count, moving an object, closing access, enforcing a schedule, withholding a hat, or keeping a knife/brace under one person's control can express authority more specifically than another anticipatory `No`.
- Different audiences should continue receiving different versions of the same event. Pell, Rinna, Teren, Lyssa, Nessa, Hessa, Antonius, Talla, customers, and rumor carriers do not need one shared definition of Greg or Bren because they are asking different questions from different evidence.
- Social overstatement can be deliberate variance rather than contamination when the text gives another character reason to resist the cleaner story.
- Short expert answers are safest when the apparatus or work has already earned them.
- Regular competence is current-state competence. Today's bill, route, storage, object use, customer need, threat status, or evidence strength can make yesterday's correct answer wrong.
- Role contamination is not automatically baseline voice convergence when the manuscript marks the wrong-role language as contamination and surrounding characters react to it as such.
- Marked contamination can stay. Greg borrowing Hessa's evidence language is useful when the narration notices it and Greg self-corrects instead of the cast adopting it as ordinary speech.
- Marked hypervigilance can stay. Greg noticing coats, hands, wheels, and door knocks under pressure is useful when the text also shows false positives and self-correction.
- Pressure scenes benefit from physical jurisdiction. Door, cart, axle, pin, hat, coat pegs, ticket box, and venue geometry keep multi-speaker scenes differentiated without extra cleverness.
- The extortion sequence should remain cumulative rather than conspiratorial. Each new fact may strengthen or limit a claim without manufacturing an organization around Bren.
- Messy systems are voice protection. Fair schedules, route queues, dinner service, contradictory workers, incomplete local directions, cue networks, changing backstage state, private-contract constraints, threat precautions, and workroom object ambiguity force people to speak from actual pressure instead of character-brand polish.
- No-change chapters are positive evidence. Do not patch to meet quota.
