# PEG-LEG GREG — ECONOMY SCALE NORMALIZATION

## Purpose

This file is the durable authority for correcting economic scale drift without unnecessarily rewriting story beats.

The manuscript has two truths that must coexist:

1. Old Greg came from a life where gold-scale and plausibly platinum-scale spending could become ordinary enough to disappear into background administration.
2. Nineteen-year-old Greg wakes with almost nothing liquid and must rebuild from a much smaller balance sheet.

The resulting early-book behavior should therefore read as **silver-active, cash-undisciplined, and psychologically miscalibrated**, not as someone for whom silver is inherently unimaginable.

## Old-life calibration

Old Greg's remembered economy was much larger than present Greg's starting purse.

He remembers:
- spending silver without noticing it;
- paying substantial specialist costs as routine expedition/business overhead;
- buying expensive equipment and luxuries;
- gold-scale objects and markets;
- an end-state life where other people handled ordinary expenses for him;
- spending behavior consistent with very large balances and possibly platinum-tier denomination/value becoming operationally ordinary.

**Do not establish an exact platinum-to-gold conversion unless exact prose intentionally does so.**

Authorial calibration may think of platinum as roughly a very large gold bundle, potentially around 100g in intuitive scale, but this is a narrative calibration aid, not a canon exchange rate until prose establishes one.

The important consequence is psychological:

**Old Greg's instincts are calibrated to large money. Young Greg's purse is not.**

That mismatch is one reason early Greg wastes money.

## Early-book economic model

Early Greg should not be rewritten as prudent poverty-survival Greg.

He is capable of moving silver almost immediately through:
- Antonius borrowing;
- gambling;
- speculative investment;
- seller financing;
- research spending;
- training;
- gear purchases;
- labor-credit arrangements;
- opportunistic trade.

His problem is not lack of access to silver as a concept.

His problem is:
- weak liquidity;
- volatile income;
- leverage;
- overconfidence;
- spending ahead of realized earnings;
- treating expected winnings as current capital;
- repeatedly funding several reasonable things at once;
- old-life taste exceeding current-life means;
- tying cash into tools, experiments, training, inventory, and opportunities;
- occasional outright stupid or ego-driven spending.

Preferred shorthand:

**EARLY GREG = SILVER-ACTIVE / CASH-POOR / CAPITAL-DISCIPLINE BAD**

not:

**EARLY GREG = COPPER-SCALE PERSON**

## Waste is character, not arithmetic error

Early waste should remain visible because it is one of the strongest ways to show that sixty-year-old confidence survived while sixty-year-old wealth did not.

Valid early drains include:
- overbuying gear because he can feel why cheaper gear is worse;
- paying specialists to accelerate uncertain ideas;
- repeated Arwick research batches;
- sword/instructor/training costs;
- gambling losses after gambling wins;
- borrowing against expected future wins;
- transport/storage/carrying costs;
- buying too much material because he thinks in future scale;
- paying for convenience because old Greg expects time to be more valuable than money;
- speculative acquisitions such as the Tere gauge;
- eating/traveling/spending somewhat better the moment liquidity appears;
- multiple small rational expenses becoming one irrational total.

Do not make him foolish in every transaction. The pattern works because many individual decisions are defensible.

## Tere gauge anchor

Chapter 7 is a major scale anchor.

Exact reader authority establishes:
- Antonius initially treats the object as trash;
- Greg identifies it as a likely Tere master calibration gauge;
- Greg states a possible right-buyer value of **40 gold or more**;
- Greg and Antonius settle on **5 silver**;
- payment is seller-financed to end of week;
- Greg cannot personally use the gauge;
- value depends on a tiny specialist buyer pool and successful authentication/market access.

This is not proof Greg has 40g of liquid wealth.

It is proof the manuscript entered high-value / low-liquidity / information-asymmetry markets almost immediately.

Preserve the distinction:

**theoretical/right-buyer value != immediate sale value != cash on hand**

## Earning progression ladder

Do not force exact universal wages. Use this as relative narrative calibration.

### Tier 0 — survival scraps
Bits/copper may matter because Greg has almost no liquid reserve.

### Tier 1 — early silver-active volatility
Greg can win, borrow, spend, invest, or owe silver. Silver amounts hurt because his balance sheet is small and unstable, not because silver itself is mythical.

### Tier 2 — recurring competent income
Ordinary copper-scale jobs become background. Greg can generate meaningful multi-copper and occasional silver income through skilled work, gambling, trade, coordination, or specialist-adjacent judgment.

### Tier 3 — leverage income
Deals/commissions/contracts operate on silver-scale underlying value and Greg's own cut increasingly becomes substantial. Single-copper gains are no longer financial milestones.

### Tier 4 — capital deployment / ownership
Greg can tie up meaningful silver or more in assets, inventory, specialist work, transport, partnerships, or ownership without each deployment resetting him to survival mode.

### Tier 5 — mature wealth
Later Greg may again operate comfortably in gold-scale and eventually larger wealth tiers if earned. The story should allow this ceiling to exist because old Greg's remembered life already proves such scale exists.

## Emotional reaction ratchet

Greg's reaction to money must progress as much as the amounts.

As he moves upward:
- 1c can become lunch, convenience, nuisance, or token pay rather than victory;
- 3c can be pleasant but not automatically a major event;
- multi-copper days can become normal competent work;
- silver becomes a real operating denomination rather than a mythical threshold;
- later silver gains should increasingly be judged against time, risk, alternatives, and capital opportunity;
- Greg should become dissatisfied with work that is objectively beneath his current earning capacity unless it provides another reason to do it.

Small work can remain for:
- friendship;
- theatre;
- access;
- information;
- fun;
- obligation;
- reputation;
- a bridge into larger work;
- ordinary life texture.

But do not narrate every small payment as progression.

## Vale continuity rule

Do not assume the recent nominal **23c** Vale account cleanly represents every historical Vale obligation.

Early authority includes multi-silver borrowing and the 5s Tere-gauge financing. A dedicated numeric normalization pass must trace:
- original principal;
- interest;
- later advances;
- labor credits;
- repayments;
- Tere gauge financing;
- any rollover/restructure/closure;
- the later account that recent state described as 23c.

Preferred editorial strategy:

**preserve the Vale story shape; repair nominal amounts and arithmetic.**

If early silver obligations were already cleared in exact prose, treat the later account as a separate smaller liability and reduce any prose that falsely frames it as the entire ancient mountain.

If the early ledger rolled forward and later numbers drifted downward accidentally, normalize the later balance upward while preserving the same repayment/closure beats.

Do not resurrect a debt after a corrected closure. The goal is one coherent ledger, not more debt story.

## Number-only-first editorial rule

For the normalization pass:

1. Preserve scene purpose, causality, relationships, jokes, and decisions.
2. Change nominal amounts first.
3. Change arithmetic references that depend on those amounts.
4. Change emotional reaction language only when the old reaction no longer fits the corrected amount.
5. Rewrite scene structure only when no coherent numeric correction can preserve it.

This is an economic continuity repair, not a manuscript rewrite.

## Hard guardrails

- Do not invent an exact copper/silver/gold/platinum exchange rate without exact prose authority.
- Do not globally multiply every price by the same factor.
- Do not turn early Greg into a disciplined saver.
- Do not make every gambling session profitable.
- Do not erase liquidity stress.
- Do not equate asset value with cash.
- Do not let later Greg emotionally celebrate trivial gains that early Greg was already capable of moving past.
- Do not use poverty reset as default stakes after capital/earning power improves.
- Do not make economic progression a smooth line. It should stair-step, overshoot, collapse locally, recover, and compound.
