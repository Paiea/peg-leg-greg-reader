# PEG-LEG GREG — REVISION ORDER / REDUNDANCY RULE

Purpose: protect the book's ordinary-life identity while preventing long-serial repetition from becoming drag.

This file is editorial process authority for broad revision work on `editor/voice-compression-pass`. Manuscript canon still outranks it.

## Core principle

**Mundane is not the problem. Repetition without new value is.**

Do not compress a chapter merely because it is quiet, procedural, domestic, social, work-focused, low-stakes, or about ordinary life.

Ordinary material earns its space when it gives the reader something new, including:
- a new problem or changed variable;
- a new place, person, relationship beat, consequence, discovery, joke, object, or piece of social texture;
- a new way of seeing the world;
- meaningful accumulation from an earlier routine;
- a familiar action whose meaning has changed because Greg, another character, or the surrounding world has changed.

The warning sign is **repeated narrative function**, not repeated subject matter.

A later chapter becomes a compression candidate when it substantially repeats an earlier chapter's:
- setup;
- procedure;
- conversational rhythm;
- conclusion;
- emotional movement;
- lesson;
- scene architecture;
without buying enough new value.

## Repetition debt

**Repetition creates a debt that later payoff must repay.**

Repeated routine may deliberately build expectation, normalcy, frustration, expertise, tension, attachment, or scientific confidence. That is allowed and often desirable.

But the more closely a sequence repeats, the more it must eventually deliver one or more of the following:
- escalation;
- a changed variable that matters;
- changed relationship meaning;
- a surprising break in the established pattern;
- a consequence that depends on the earlier repetition;
- a payoff that makes the reader reinterpret or appreciate the repeated setup.

If none arrives, compress, merge, or remove redundant delivery while preserving canon and the strongest lived detail.

## Revision order

Current priority remains:

**DIALOGUE / ATTRIBUTION FIRST -> REDUNDANCY REVIEW SECOND -> APPROVED STRUCTURAL SURGERY THIRD**

Do not interrupt the active dialogue/attribution pass with a manuscript-wide tightening campaign.

During dialogue/attribution work:
1. fix speaker clarity and character-specific cognition/cadence;
2. preserve current plot/canon/outcomes;
3. when a chapter or cluster appears functionally repetitive, record it in `state/editorial/REDUNDANCY_CANDIDATES.md`;
4. do not merge/cut a cluster merely because it was noticed during voice work unless the active structural map already authorizes that exact surgery.

Reason: voice homogenization can make two otherwise different scenes feel more repetitive than they really are. Judge redundancy from the edited voice state whenever possible.

## Function test

For each suspected cluster, ask:

1. What unique event does each chapter contain?
2. What unique relationship movement does each chapter contain?
3. What unique world, magic, work, social, or character information does each chapter contain?
4. Does the repeated routine change meaning over time?
5. Does a later payoff depend on the repetition?
6. If two chapters share the same subject, do they still perform different narrative jobs?
7. If two chapters perform the same narrative job, can their best material be combined without losing life?
8. Would cutting or merging remove lived texture, or mainly remove a function the reader already received?

## Decision rule

Classify only after evidence review:
- **KEEP** — earns its own space through new value or necessary accumulation.
- **KEEP / COMPRESS** — distinct function, but procedure/explanation repeats.
- **MERGE CANDIDATE** — neighboring chapters deliver substantially the same function and their best material would strengthen each other.
- **CUT CANDIDATE** — distinct value is already delivered elsewhere and salvageable beats can move cleanly.

Do not use chapter count, quietness, low stakes, or lack of conventional drama as reasons by themselves.

Do not impose a universal compression percentage.

## Special case: experiments / procedures

Repeated Hessa tests, work processes, theatre routines, errands, or other procedures are not automatically redundant.

Keep repeated procedures when the changed variable, result, relationship, consequence, or accumulated expectation is the point.

Compress the repeated setup once the reader knows it unless the setup itself changed.

If several experiments differ only in small result details, ask whether the sequence is building toward a payoff large enough to justify full-scene repetition. If yes, preserve the buildup. If no, compress intermediate confirmations while protecting exact magic evidence and counts.

## Anti-flattening guardrail

Do not "solve" repetition by deleting the book's texture.

Peg-Leg Greg is allowed to spend time on:
- work;
- maintenance;
- errands;
- trade;
- meals;
- travel;
- rehearsal;
- domestic life;
- ordinary social contact;
- low-stakes learning;
- repeated routines that visibly accumulate.

The standard is not "does something dramatic happen?"

The standard is:

**Does the reader receive something meaningfully new, or is the manuscript asking them to pay again for something they already bought?**

Protect mundane novelty. Compress redundant function. Let repetition stay when it earns its payoff.
