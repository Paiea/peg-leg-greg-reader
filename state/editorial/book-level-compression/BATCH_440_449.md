## 440 — THE LOOKOUT

**VERDICT: MERGE / RESIDUE into the 439-441 transition**

- Dask demonstrates healthy restraint around an active signal problem.

competent professionals + expensive schedule pressure + uncertain instrument/network evidence + Greg stays bounded + standard survives + operational problem resolves without villain.

The scene is good. The book has already paid for this shape repeatedly.

The repeated optical-confirmation sequences, passenger reactions, captain-versus-duty-officer argument, and post-resolution explanation can shrink to residue.

### Structural action

Preserve a compact Lerrin beacon incident and Lot 67 characterization around the end of 439 or opening of 441. Preserve Dask choosing Bellweather and Greg choosing water. Do not preserve 440 as a full independent chapter unless later dependency review reveals a stronger need.

### Downstream risk

Preserve exact Lot 67 limitation: intermittent false long hangs near mark four remain unresolved here and motivate later repair at Ternhaven.

### Compression pressure

- alternate landing logistics
- Salt Mouth bridge failure
- Kelp Isles route setup
- extended Lot 67 characterization
- missing-child report intake
- multiple search groups
- full resolution
- nickname gossip payoff

The missing-child sequence should survive, but it can lose substantial report-by-report logging. One or two contradictory/uncertain reports plus the fisherman's reed-islet sighting are enough to establish the method.

Lot 67 characterization can also compress to the finding: orientation reversal does not eliminate the intermittent mark-four hang; visible bead pause suggests track/bead friction.

### Downstream risk

Preserve Orric's actual causal chain: he went toward crab traps before the crossing fully failed, was cut off by tide, used the reed islet's higher patch, and was found alive. Do not rewrite him as foolishly wandering.
