### Voice note

The quartermaster is excellent one-chapter domain voice evidence:
- `Out.`
- `Leave it.`
- `It's flour.`
- `It's not ten people.`

He does not need a theory of safety. His responsibility is the storehouse and the people in it, so the decision is immediate. Do not retrofit Hessa-style reasoning into this kind of work authority.

Greg and Alden are also beginning to sound socially closer. That convergence is allowed. Their difference should remain underneath it: Greg keeps turning experience into models; Alden keeps asking what the next usable move is.

### Voice note - Octavia

Octavia has a very strong first major appearance. Her voice comes from freight coordination rather than generic precision:
- she asks for the missing dimensions of Greg's request because space allocation is literally her job;
- `free` and `temporarily unused` are different because the shed remains part of an operating freight system;
- there is no generic `any route`; food, nails, passengers, cows, couriers, wagons, drivers, and horses fail differently;
- she likes the work because she likes things arriving where they are supposed to;
- when Sevren may be missing, she refuses reassurance and asks whether deliveries need to move.

This is enough to flag Octavia as a likely future voice-page candidate if she recurs. Do not create a catchphrase file from one appearance alone.

### Voice note - Dema Rusk

Dema Rusk is unrelated in function to Antonius's Rusk and should not inherit `state/voices/RUSK.md` merely because they share a surname/name surface.

Her teaching voice is strongly institutional and field-recovery specific:
- `STOP. COUNT. MOVE. DECIDE.`
- she is teaching Bronze adventurers what to do after the plan fails;
- she prefers a followable imperfect decision over multiple disconnected good ideas in the specific context of party recovery;
- she repeatedly distinguishes what is known from what frightened civilians are saying, but the purpose is field triage and group action, not Hessa's magical methodology.

Do not let the shared word `Rusk` collapse these characters into one voice authority.
