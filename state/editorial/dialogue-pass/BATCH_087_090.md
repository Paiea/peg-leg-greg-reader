## Book II result through Chapter 90

This batch adds a useful distinction to the attribution rules. A crowded scene does not require constant tags if transient speakers are physically introduced right before their line. The danger point is **returning authority**: somebody new joins an already active table, an observer answers from outside the main exchange, or the person who actually owns the desk comes back into a room full of unresolved speakers. Those are the moments where a plain tag buys the most clarity.

It also sharpens the voice philosophy. Octavia and Merrin are both concise and highly competent, but their cognition is different. Octavia retains sequence, timing, route and social behavior to act on what happens next. Merrin retains permissions, records, routing and institutional ownership to know who may answer at all.
