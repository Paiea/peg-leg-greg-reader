## Book II result through Chapter 104

This batch reinforces two useful rules.

First, three-person domestic or peer scenes need attribution mainly where **referents overlap**, not because three bodies exist in the room. `She` becomes fragile when two women are active; a bare `Yes` becomes fragile when Greg and Jorren can both plausibly answer Alden. A proper name or one plain narrator tag solves the problem without tagging the whole scene.

Second, continuity errors can masquerade as attribution errors. When a pronoun makes a recurring person seem to change identity, check earlier exact prose before editing. Here the lodging keeper's Chapter 84 `she` and Nerin's Chapter 65 `he said` settle the later slips cleanly.

The voice separation also gets stronger without louder diction. Hessa simplifies by controlling test conditions and permissions. Pessa simplifies an exercise while refusing the fiction that body parts become isolated in real movement. Lyssa can choose ordinary time with Greg without making it a relationship event. Alden can seek access without treating access as owed.
