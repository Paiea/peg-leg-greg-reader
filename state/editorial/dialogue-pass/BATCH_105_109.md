### Voice update - Octavia

Chapter 108 materially sharpens Octavia's social reading without turning her into a mind reader.

Her strongest line is the distinction Greg keeps missing: `You think because I noticed the thing, I believed the message attached to the thing.` She can notice his performed tell, infer something else, remember how he behaved in the previous session, and still make a card decision that does not correspond to his intended signal.

Crucially, Octavia does not have to win every hand to preserve authority. Greg wins the final hand by simply having very good cards and leaves still down overall.

### Voice update - Hessa

Chapter 109 adds the next distinct control question: can Greg stop an alteration **without releasing the underlying draw**?

When Hessa says stop, Greg reduces the forward progression of the altered patch, but it widens sideways. Hessa refuses both easy interpretations. She does not call that clean stopping, and she does not say nothing happened. The resulting ladder is now meaningfully richer:

- change what it is doing;

- stop the alteration cleanly;

Hessa also gives the first bounded reason this matters for later magic with an external consequence: if every shaping change can only end by dropping the entire draw, Greg has less control than she wants before testing anything with an external effect. `External effect` is a future criterion category, not a promise that such a test is next.

## Book II result through Chapter 109

This batch adds two useful attribution rules.

First, recurring unnamed-role characters can accumulate continuity authority even without a personal name. Once exact prose has source-settled the lodging keeper as female, later `he/him` references are not harmless shorthand. They create false identity noise and should be corrected wherever they recur.

Second, crowded social scenes do not need blanket tagging. The Four Doors table works best when game action carries most turns and simple tags appear only where a third speaker interrupts, a running side joke changes owner, or table authority returns after a tangent. A plain `said` or `asked` is better than forcing Octavia, Jorren, and Greg into artificially distinct diction.

The voice separation continues to strengthen by **decision type** rather than catchphrase. Hessa asks what exact magical control has been demonstrated and what threshold comes next. Sevren asks whether this person fits this route and which deadline actually matters. Octavia asks what behavior changed this hand, not what universal psychological theory explains the person.
