## Book II result through Chapter 138

This batch adds four durable editorial distinctions.

First, **shared vocabulary does not imply shared voice**. `Again`, `No`, `Good`, and other short Carrow/theatre words can pass among several people while authority remains distinct through what each person is trying to make happen.

Second, **directional control earns a bounded upgrade before external magic does**. By Chapter 135 Hessa can call narrow direction-first control provisionally supported while external effect remains unestablished and casting remains uncleared.

Third, **better baseline description can weaken the interpretation of an earlier success without erasing the observation**. Chapter 137's neutral-shape map reveals asymmetry that the earlier directional sessions did not measure at the same level of detail.

Fourth, **rehearsal and performance require different attribution logic**. Rehearsal can stop, diagnose, and repeat. Live performance cannot. Once the audience is present, dialogue clarity depends more heavily on current partner, object, entrance, and local recovery than on reproducing a prior good run.

A later identity audit adds one more caution: **role labels are not identities**. Repeated descriptions such as `soot-shirt worker` can refer to more than one worker, and later naming can reveal that a prior pronoun normalization was overconfident. Preserve the uncertainty until stronger authority settles it.
