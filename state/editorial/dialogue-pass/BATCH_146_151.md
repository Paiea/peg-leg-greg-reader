## High-value findings through Chapter 151

First, **public presence does not automatically mean live-performance logic**. Open rehearsal can contain an audience and still permit stops, resets, notes, and reruns. Paid performance cannot.

Second, **a successful line is not the same thing as a reusable line**. Greg's recurring failure is waiting for yesterday's laugh, yesterday's partner movement, or yesterday's successful exchange. The current partner and current audience remain the live source.

Third, **partner recovery can either help or hide information depending on the mode**. In rehearsal, Serra may stop Marek from covering her skipped line because she needs to know she skipped. In a paid show, the same kind of recovery may be exactly what keeps the scene alive.

Fourth, **the troupe's competence is distributed and largely non-ceremonial**. A costume gets repaired, a bad board gets fixed, a missing prop gets substituted, a wrong line gets recovered, a room gets paid for, and a cue changes. No single named authority needs to narrate the system.

Fifth, **work identity arrives before self-definition**. By Chapter 151 Greg has been paid for a real show, but the manuscript still does not need a speech declaring what he now is.
