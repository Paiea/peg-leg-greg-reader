### Functional dialogue finding

Chapter 156 materially sharpens the difference between **exact wording** and **load-bearing scene information**.

Iven can accept a paraphrase when the scene function survives:

- `Seal.`
- `Wagons.`
- `King refuses.`
- Greg objects that a line is not exact.
- Iven still answers `Good.`

But not every detail has equal tolerance. North/south can be corrected and then treated as nonfatal. `FROM` versus `TO` cannot, because reversing that preposition reverses the bridge crime. The working actor's job is not to preserve every byte. It is to preserve the information another beat depends on.

That distinction survives into the live show. Greg mangles wording, converts lines, and accidentally reverses the bridge movement. Serra restores the missing destination information from inside the scene. The play can survive prose variation; it cannot survive losing the action that makes the next event make sense.

## High-value findings through Chapter 158

1. **Preserve load-bearing information, not every byte.** Emergency substitution can survive paraphrase if the facts another beat depends on remain intact.
2. **Not all factual errors have equal scene cost.** A location detail may be locally correctable; a reversed `FROM`/`TO` can invert the action that causes the next scene.
3. **Keyword prompts can be better than sentence prompts under pressure.** `Seal`, `Wagons`, `King refuses`, and `From` retrieve scene function without feeding Greg a whole performance.
4. **The object can carry attribution.** In backstage routing, who has the sword, coat, pins, fish, or poison jar often identifies the speaker more efficiently than repeated tags.
5. **Role contamination can be intentional comedy without becoming voice collapse.** Advocate language leaking into Shopkeeper/Sword works because the manuscript and other actors recognize the leak.
6. **Nothing happening can be a successful live result.** Iven's `Exactly` after an uneventful Sword scene protects boring competence from being mistaken for absence of craft.
7. **Emergency cuts are local to the constraint.** `Learn less` and Iven's line cutting solve a one-day replacement problem; they are not a universal manifesto against written text.
