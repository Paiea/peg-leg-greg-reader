## Chapter 323 — THE HELD WEIGHT

**Verdict: VOICE PATCH REQUIRED — COMIC REGISTER CONVERGENCE**

This is the first clear hit under the expanded variance rubric.

The scene contains legitimate fear humor and established workplace shorthand, but too many speakers repeatedly land clean, short, mutually compatible counters. Davin, Jessa, Mera, Greg, and ancillary workers begin to sound as though all of them can play Greg's conversational game at the same speed.

The fix is **not** to remove workplace humor. Keep one or two excellent releases. De-optimize selected exchanges so work cognition and stress own more of the rhythm.

### Patch 323.1 — Mera correction

Current:

`Davin pointed at Mera.`

`"She saw the return."`

`Mera said, "I said I couldn't read the return from there."`

`"Same difference."`

`"Those are opposite sentences."`

Replace with:

`Davin pointed at Mera.`

`"She saw the return."`

`Mera shook her head. "No, Davin. I said I couldn't read it from where I was standing. I saw the corner go white. That's all."`

`Davin frowned at the frame. "Fine."`

Reason: Mera is under equipment-failure pressure and has already shown defensive caution about being asked to bleed/read the system. Let her over-clarify the boundary instead of landing another polished counterpunch. Davin's `Fine` returns him to the machine.

## Chapter 324 — THE PRICE

**Verdict: VOICE PATCH REQUIRED — MARA IS TOO CONSISTENTLY GOOD AT GREG'S GAME**

Mara Quill legitimately owns precision, qualification, professional limits, and a practiced customer-facing confidence. Those traits should remain.

The problem is density. Almost every opening Greg gives her becomes a clean dry answer, and she wins or matches nearly every exchange. That creates Greg/Hessa-style reasoning architecture plus Greg-style punchline timing in a new specialist.

The repair should preserve her intelligence while making her more occupationally expansive in selected places. Let her explain because she has explained these distinctions for thirty years, become impatient with bad provenance stories, and occasionally answer the professional question rather than the joke.

### Patch 324.1 — impossible

Current:

`"You're impossible."`

`"Frequently."`

Replace with:

`"You're impossible."`

`Mara was already reaching for the next piece on her counter. "No. I'm expensive when people want me to certify a story instead of an object."`

Reason: gives her humor through occupational irritation and over-specific correction rather than a two-word comeback.

### Patch 324.2 — seller joke

Current:

`"Who called it that?"`

`"Seller called it a holdfast."`

`"Seller called himself handsome too, I assume."`

`"He did not."`

`"Missed opportunity."`

Replace with:

`"Who called it that?"`

`"Seller called it a holdfast."`

`Mara made a small dismissive sound. "Sellers call things whatever gets them onto the table. Until I see the housing, 'holdfast' tells me what he wanted you to think it was."`

Reason: same skepticism, much more Mara-shaped. She teaches from provenance habits rather than trading jokes with Greg.

### Patch 324.3 — one copper

Current:

`I looked at her.`

`"Rude."`

`"Also rude."`

`"Then don't bring me the object yet."`

Reason: the economic read remains sharp, but she reaches it through observation and explanation instead of a perfect Greg-compatible three-beat exchange.

### Patch 324.4 — thirty years

Keep:

`"It took thirty years to make not knowing useful."`

Reason: this is an excellent Mara line because it comes from professional history, not generic sarcasm. The pass should protect character-specific wit, not flatten everyone into seriousness.

### Patch 324.5 — limits principle

The line is strong but Hessa-adjacent. Keep it only because Mara's actual business model makes the distinction materially hers. Do not repeat this epistemic architecture elsewhere in her scene. The surrounding patches are sufficient separation.

## Chapter 325 — THE NIGHT LINE

Sel, however, matches Greg's counterpunch timing a little too readily in two places. He should remain an efficient event supervisor, not become another dry-comedy peer by default.

### Patch 325.1 — fire exchange

Current:

`"What if you're on fire?"`

Replace with:

`"What if something catches fire?"`

`"You tell me and start moving people back."`

`"What if you're on fire?"`

`Sel looked up from the paper. "Then use judgment, Greg."`

Reason: Sel's job is procedure. He can refuse Greg's joke without needing to beat him at it.

Reason: unlike the fire volley, this one is relationship/state specific. Sel gives the generic instruction, notices Greg's body constraint only after Greg pushes back, then awkwardly revises it. The joke comes from failed one-size-fits-all procedure and social adjustment, not merely shared sarcasm.
