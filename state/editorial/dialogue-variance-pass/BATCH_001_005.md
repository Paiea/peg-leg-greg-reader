The chapter is overwhelmingly Greg alone. The only external voice is the annoyed neighbor through the wall, and that voice does not become a comedy partner. Greg's dry register is properly dominant because there is almost nobody else available to compete with it.

No variance issue identified.

## Chapter 2 — THE BORROWER

**Verdict: VARIANCE REVIEWED — NO NEW VOICE PATCH REQUIRED**

The stall owner asks ordinary domain questions and laughs at Greg's `Bad decisions` answer rather than matching it. Sella is suspicious and blunt. Her `Freak` exit is not a polished counterpunch so much as a refusal to participate in Greg's strange framing.

Antonius is the important overlap case. His interrogation is very clipped, but the clipping is operational: collateral, property, guarantor, family, useful friends, information. His existing voice authority explicitly allows short declaratives and silence because he is pricing a borrower, not performing wit.

The scene therefore passes the new rule despite surface similarity.

### Finding

Early Antonius should be separated from Greg by **scope discipline**, not by artificially lengthening every answer. Greg expands the frame. Antonius keeps returning to the actual loan.

## Chapter 3 — THE INVESTOR

**Verdict: DIALOGUE-VARIANCE PATCH REQUIRED**

This is the first strong early-manuscript example of comic register convergence under the new rubric.

Arlo's workshop cognition is distinct, but too many exchanges let him land a polished dry reply at exactly Greg's speed. The result is not that Arlo lacks identity. The result is that Greg and Arlo both repeatedly sound optimized for the same joke architecture.

Preserve Arlo's technical corrections, cost boundaries, and refusal to guess. Reduce only the density of crafted counterpunches.

### Patch 3.V1 — opening cart exchange

Current:

`"You actually bought it," Arlo said.`

`"I said I would," I told him.`

`"People say many things," Arlo said.`

`"Most of them should stop," I said.`

Replace with:

`"You actually bought it," Arlo said.`

`"I said I would," I told him.`

`He looked past me at the cart.`

`"I thought you meant enough to test."`

`"Most of them should stop," I said.`

Reason: Greg keeps the compressed joke. Arlo answers from workshop capacity and what he believed the job required.

### Patch 3.V2 — fee stability

Current:

`"You said six yesterday," I said.`

`"And today it remains six. Remarkable stability," Arlo said.`

Replace with:

`"You said six yesterday," I said.`

`"Six for my time. Same as yesterday," Arlo said.`

Reason: the current line is good in isolation but too Greg-compatible in a chapter already saturated with dry volleying. The replacement preserves Arlo's ownership of labor and cost.

### Patch 3.V3 — Greg admits uncertainty

Current:

`"I was about to pretend I knew more than I do."`

`"That's new."`

`"I contain multitudes."`

Replace with:

`"I was about to pretend I knew more than I do."`

`Arlo looked at the shale chip in my hand.`

`"Then start with what you actually know."`

Reason: this is a prime place for Arlo's bench-first cognition to replace generic dry wit. Greg can still own the comic/performative register elsewhere in the exchange.

### Patch 3.V4 — guesses exchange

Current:

`"Those are different mechanisms."`

`"Yes," I said.`

`"Which one?"`

`"I don't know."`

`Arlo's expression flattened. I pointed at him. "That is why you're being paid."`

`"No. I'm being paid to test a hypothesis."`

`"Excellent. We have several."`

`"You have guesses."`

`"Those are cheaper."`

Replace only the final Arlo line:

`"Excellent. We have several."`

`Arlo shook his head.`

`"You have guesses. Give me the one you want tested first."`

Reason: Arlo should narrow the work, not merely win the joke. Greg's `Those are cheaper` counter can be dropped here because the scene already has plenty of Greg compression nearby.

### Craft finding

Arlo does **not** need to become verbose. His best differentiation is to convert Greg's abstraction into the next bench decision. That is stronger than simply giving him different jokes.

## Chapter 4 — THE EXPERT

**Verdict: VARIANCE REVIEWED — NO CHANGE REQUIRED**

The dealer and table players remain mostly functional rather than uniformly witty. The higher-stakes Senna exchange is dry, but its source is poker-table social pressure: she notices Greg staring, pushes him off her, refuses to become his experiment, and later reads his decision to leave.

`Learn quieter` is particularly useful because it is not Greg-style analysis. It is a direct social boundary from a player who knows she is being studied.

Do not infer that this Senna is identical to later same-name Senna without continuity evidence.

## Chapter 5 — THE WARRIOR

**Verdict: VARIANCE REVIEWED — NO CHANGE REQUIRED**

Jorren passes strongly. His short lines come from embodied observation rather than analytical comedy: Greg's body is tired, his movement looks old, and `Maybe tomorrow` ends the session because the physical system has already answered.

The later Antonius debt conversation is dry, but its repeated `You're very good at naming things` pattern is tied directly to Greg relabeling debt, gambling, training, and spending. Antonius is not matching every joke. He is identifying the same recurring borrower behavior and letting the ledger contradict Greg.

The chapter also provides useful state variance: Greg becomes euphoric in the yard, defensive around money, and slower to accept physical limits. Jorren does not mirror those states.
