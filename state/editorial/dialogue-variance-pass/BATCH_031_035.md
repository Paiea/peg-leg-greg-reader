## Chapter 31 - THE REPORTER

**Verdict: MODERATE DIALOGUE-VARIANCE PATCH REQUIRED**

Rinna is one of the strongest ordinary-work voices in the early manuscript. Her priorities are finish work, waste, stone quality, family labor, and making good stairs. Greg immediately tries to convert that competence into structural diagnostics and wall-breaking potential; Rinna keeps returning to the job she actually values. Protect that mismatch.

Hessa is also fundamentally sound. Her session with Rinna is measurement, correction, stopping condition, and bounded interpretation. The variance leak appears when Hessa identifies Greg's judgment through the now-project-wide `face` shorthand rather than through the specific thing he just did: turn Rinna's present craft into an imagined exam for a different career.

### Patch 31.V1 - Hessa names the behavior, not the universal face

Current:

`Hessa closed the ledger.`

`"How long are you going to insult her silently?"`

`"I am not insulting her."`

`"You have the face."`

`"What face?"`

`"The one where someone is failing an exam they did not know you gave them."`

Replace with:

`Hessa closed the ledger.`

`"How long are you going to keep redesigning her work?"`

`"I am not redesigning it."`

`"You turned stairs into wall failure before she packed her tools."`

Reason: Hessa has direct evidence. She heard Greg expand Rinna's stone sense into structural mapping, failure points, and stress redirection. Naming that observed escalation is more Hessa-shaped than another generic `Greg face` diagnosis.

### Keep

Keep Rinna's `I make good stairs.` This is excellent anti-optimization characterization. She is not failing to imagine her potential; she has a different definition of valuable mastery.

Keep Hessa asking `What could she do?` and forcing Greg to separate what he can imagine from what Rinna has demonstrated. That is true Hessa evidence discipline.

Keep Hessa's later supervised casting stop. Her shortness there is body/symptom control, not comic refusal.

Keep the chapter's ordinary social ecosystem. People know each other's chairs, work schedules, debts, mothers, baskets, and drinking habits independently of Greg.

## Durable craft findings

### 1. Earned familiarity still needs character-specific evidence

Knowing Greg well does not mean every recurring character should diagnose him through the same `face` shorthand.

Better familiarity:
- Hessa remembers the exact claim Greg just overextended;
- Arlo anticipates the next unsafe or untested mechanism and controls test order;
- Jorren notices Greg mentally leave an ordinary conversation and lets him keep the reason private;
- Alden notices behavior he directly witnessed.

Relationship history should make observation more specific, not more interchangeable.

### 2. Present vocation can resist future potential

Rinna is a positive reference. A character may have obvious broader potential and still rationally value the work they already do. `I make good stairs` gives the world independent definitions of success.

Do not let Greg's ability to imagine leverage silently become the manuscript's objective ranking of people's lives.

### 3. Collaboration improves when specialists can catch different failure modes

Arlo/Vessa joins Kellan/butcher as a positive reference. Arlo isolates flow instability mechanically; Vessa catches fixture-induced housing distortion. Neither expertise subsumes the other.

### 4. Repeated refusal should lose to action when action is available

Rusk crouching to inspect the wet pallet is stronger than another `No / useful / No` exchange. Arlo setting the regulator beside the hand pump is stronger than another prediction joke.

When a working character can demonstrate their priority physically, prefer the work.

### 5. Ordinary social ecosystems should exist without Greg

Chapter 35 is a positive reference. Mara, Jorren, Nessa, customers, the Five Roads player, and absent family members all have routines and relationships that do not originate with Greg.

This helps the world feel inhabited rather than assembled around the protagonist.
