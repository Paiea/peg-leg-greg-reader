## Durable craft findings

### 1. Shared technical shortness is safe when responsibility remains visible

Greg, Arlo, and Maret can all ask compact technical questions because the scene makes their ownership different. Same with Greg, Bram, Kell, and the arch clerk.

Do not diversify sentence length merely to manufacture contrast when job boundaries already create it.

### 2. Changed workflow is stronger relationship evidence than repeated banter

Antonius offering fixed debt periods says more about his evolving relationship with Greg than another clever exchange about Greg's face. Pessa selecting Greg from work history does the same.

When a relationship progresses, ask what people now do differently for each other.

### 3. Domain-specific anti-overgeneralization is better than personality policing

Pessa should not merely tell Greg that he turns things into religion. She can tell him why a road heuristic belongs in the margin: another road may fail differently.

Hessa can make the same kind of correction from medicine or magic, but the reason must belong to her domain.

### 4. Ordinary records can carry emotional weight without becoming hero status

Greg enjoying the existence of a work history is useful. The Guild file can quietly matter to him while still containing mundane evidence: he stops early, writes things down, notices roads, and completed bounded assignments.

Let the emotion live in Greg. Other characters do not need to narrate his growth for him.

### 5. Specific teasing beats generic teasing

Tam can tease Greg for looking at the stairs twice. Dorn can tease him for liking a work file. Jorren can tease a broken bathhouse promise. The more history a relationship has, the more specific the joke should become.
