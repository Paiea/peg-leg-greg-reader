## Durable craft findings

### 1. Attribution density and voice variance are different problems

A crowded danger scene may need many more tags while requiring no dialogue rewrite at all. Chapter 63 is the clearest example so far.

Do not use voice editing to solve speaker clarity.

### 2. Stress should degrade optimization before it erases character

Pain, drugs, blood loss, fear, and urgent work can make people repetitive, literal, controlling, overly focused, or terse. Preserve those changes. The goal is not for everyone to become equally solemn under pressure.

### 3. Ongoing investigations can justify anticipation without sentence prediction

Arlo can know Greg came to ask about the regulator. He can start with the current result. That is stronger than another `No / I haven't asked / you were going to` exchange.

A recurring relationship can anticipate the topic while still allowing the actual conversation to happen.

### 4. Medical humor should come from care tasks and uncertainty

Nerin can be funny about chamber pots, breathing, breakfast, and preventing spills. Sera can be dry about variable outcomes and premature planning. Hessa can be sharp about recovery and channel limits.

They should not all prove competence through identical mind-reading banter.

### 5. Catastrophe benefits from competent lanes

The mill-frame accident lands harder because Ressa, Marn, Dorn, Harl, and Greg are all doing reasonable work inside bounded responsibilities. Preserve competence and let material failure remain material failure.
