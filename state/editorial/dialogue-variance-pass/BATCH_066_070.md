## Durable craft findings

### 1. Rehabilitation gives better evidence than expression-reading

Crutch settings, grip, thresholds, stair counts, stopped movement, fatigue, and actual body position are richer evidence than `the face`. Medical and rehabilitation characters should usually speak from those variables.

### 2. Specific embarrassing history can legitimately become relationship shorthand

The `chamber pot face` works because it is not a universal Greg-reading device. It refers to one repeated, concrete, humiliating problem Jorren has personally witnessed.

Specific shorthand can deepen relationship while generic shorthand flattens the cast.

### 3. Practical care often sounds like logistics

`Need a stop?`, moving soup, counting stairs, arranging a delivery cart, putting water within reach, and keeping equipment settings stable can carry more affection and character than explicit reassurance.

### 4. Let the world continue without interpreting the protagonist

Chapter 70 is strong because jobs fill, Pessa leaves for another culvert, a clerk shrugs, a delivery boy names a price, and another Copper takes the posting. Greg's emotional meaning stays largely inside Greg.

### 5. Refusal density is safe when the object of refusal changes

Jorren can say `No` repeatedly while refusing window measurements, errands, chamber-pot avoidance, or unsafe logistics because each refusal is attached to a concrete request. The problem is not the word. The problem is when `No` becomes an interchangeable setup for the same predictive joke architecture.
