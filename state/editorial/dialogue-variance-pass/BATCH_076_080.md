## Chapter 78 - THE ARCHIVIST

**Verdict: MODERATE DIALOGUE-VARIANCE PATCH REQUIRED**

Merra is an excellent new voice once the work begins. Her precision comes from record provenance, unit meaning, archive handling rules, what a ledger actually supports, and where reconstruction must stop.

The chapter's main leak is first-contact calibration. Merra has only just met Greg, yet two beats let her speak as if she already knows his habitual internal expansion. Both can be replaced with archive-specific instruction using information already visible on the table.

### Patch 78.V1 - Merra controls motive by controlling the task

Current:

`"Fraud?"`

`"Probably copying."`

`"Probably."`

`"Do not investigate fraud."`

`"I wasn't."`

`She looked at me.`

`"You were."`

Replace with:

`"Fraud?"`

`"Probably copying."`

`"Probably."`

`Merra tapped the top ledger.`

`"Mark what the books support. Leave motive out of it."`

`"Good."`

Reason: Merra does not need instant Greg-reading. Her job already gives her the stronger boundary: reconstruction from records, not motive investigation.

### Patch 78.V2 - correct the unit problem instead of reading Greg's face

Current:

`"Why did audit flag it?"`

`"New clerk assumed."`

`"Ah."`

`"Do not mock new clerk."`

`"I didn't."`

`"Face."`

`Everyone.`

Replace with:

`"Why did audit flag it?"`

`"New clerk assumed."`

`"Ah."`

`Merra tapped the heading.`

`"Write the unit problem, not the clerk."`

`"Right."`

Reason: this keeps Merra inside archival discipline. The mistake is already concrete: a payment-entry count was treated like a crate count. She can correct how Greg records the error without inheriting the manuscript-wide face shorthand.

### Keep

Keep `Do not lick finger. Everyone says that.` This is archive-specific handling policy built from repeated public behavior, not Greg-specific mind reading.

Keep Merra throwing the copying boy the correct cloth without turning it into a speech. Practical institutional authority can be physical.

Keep `I was making record cleaner than it is.` / `Don't.` This is one of the strongest anti-compression moments in the chapter because the archivist protects uncertainty rather than sharpening it.

Keep `Because records lie differently than people` followed immediately by the mundane explanation that people forget what columns mean. The deflation keeps the line from becoming an authorial maxim.

Keep Merra not knowing where everything is. `I know where it should be` is archival competence with realistic limits.

## Durable craft findings

### 1. Expertise may overlap conceptually without overlapping voice

Holl and Pessa can both care about decision criteria. Holl's criterion is commercial classification and labor cost. Pessa's criterion is what a drill is actually training. Shared concepts are safe when the protected object differs.

### 2. First-contact authority should correct the work before correcting the person

Merra is strongest when she says what the books support, what units mean, what may be written, and where reconstruction stops. New specialists do not need immediate personality-level access to Greg to establish competence.

### 3. Physical rehabilitation creates legitimate repetition

`Sit`, `rest`, `again`, `no`, stair counts, and repeated body cues are allowed to recur because recovery itself is repetitive. Review whether the repetition has a changing physical object, not whether the same word appears again.

### 4. Incidental strangers benefit from low narrative ambition

The fruit seller solves the pear problem. The cart driver identifies a blue shirt. The copying boy sneezes. These people make Carrow feel larger precisely because they do not all become miniature character showcases or Greg interpreters.

### 5. Let the protagonist be socially young without converting it into a system

Clothes, attraction, embarrassment, bad bargaining, wanting to go somewhere, and spending four copper because blue looks better can remain ordinary desire. The surrounding cast does not need to explain what it means.
