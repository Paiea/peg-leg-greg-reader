## Durable craft findings

### 1. Established familiarity still benefits from current evidence

Antonius, the keeper, Lyssa, and Holl are allowed to know Greg. Familiarity does not require deleting every perceptive line. But when the scene provides a crossed work category, a shirt choice, a physical stop, or an explicit argument, that evidence is usually stronger than generic personality shorthand.

### 2. Early romance should accumulate its own shorthand slowly

Lyssa can notice Greg stops walking, stares too long, loses track of the date, or starts asking questions. Those concrete habits can gradually become relationship language. She does not need the same finished Greg-parser vocabulary as older relationships immediately.

### 3. Explicitly sourced prediction is different from ambient omniscience

Antonius telling Merrin what Greg will probably say can work because the prediction has a named source with established history, and Merrin merely reports it. The danger is density: if everyone begins carrying secondhand Greg scripts, the city compresses again.

### 4. Respond to the spoken argument before the hidden intention

Holl is stronger when he says Greg is already arguing against blaming Iris than when he claims to know Greg thought `fire`. Expertise plus disagreement should stay attached to what is actually being said and done.

### 5. Not knowing can be professional competence

Sevren does not need to know barrel contents, medical technique, or Greg's bodily limits when his job does not require them. Scope-limited ignorance can distinguish a professional voice just as strongly as specialized knowledge.
