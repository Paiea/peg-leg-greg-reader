## Durable craft findings

### 1. Face-reading can be the subject of a scene without becoming a manuscript leak

Four Doors is explicitly about visible behavior, bluffing, sequence memory, and performed tells. The correct question is not `does anyone read a face?` but `what evidence did they actually observe, and how much are they claiming from it?`

Octavia stays healthy because she remembers the first fast bird, notices performed eye movement, and limits conclusions to a hand. An incidental delivery boy has no such earned evidence and should not use the same machinery.

### 2. Repeated prediction is most dangerous when stacked

One relationship-earned `No` can work. Jorren and Alden using nearly identical predictive refusal architecture within seconds makes the house style visible. When two familiar characters share a scene, vary the mechanism even if both know Greg well.

### 3. A specialist's funniest line often comes from jurisdiction

The furniture seller is strongest when she talks about chairs, joints, floor space, delivery, and stairs. A generic comeback may be funny, but a boundary generated by the shop makes the speaker more specific at the same time.

### 4. Looking is evidence when the object is named

`You looked` is not automatically forbidden. Greg looking at Jorren for help, looking from food to crutches to Lyssa, or scanning a room for Jorren can legitimately support a response. The weak version is an unspecified face becoming shorthand for the exact hidden sentence.

### 5. Crowded service scenes naturally resist voice compression

Chapter 89 works because people arrive with different incomplete problems and different incentives. They do not all understand Greg, Merrin, or the system. Preserve that friction. A line, queue, wet-cloth basket, missing mark, charging cart, and broken latch create social variance without decorative quirks.
