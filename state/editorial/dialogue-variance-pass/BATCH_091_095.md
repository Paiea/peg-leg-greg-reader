## Durable craft findings

### 1. Expert familiarity should improve the question, not eliminate the need to ask it

Hessa and Pessa are allowed deep familiarity with Greg. Their expertise is strongest when it helps them ask a better question from a visible event: why did you stop the draw, what changed when you held the shield, what does the swelling do today, what are you trying to train. Perfectly stating the private explanation before Greg reports it makes the character feel less expert, not more.

### 2. Procedural authority can replace predictive authority

Pessa does not need `you were going to ask me` when she owns the drill. Hessa does not need `you were turning that into cleared` when she can state the clearance boundary directly. Jurisdiction is cleaner than mind-reading.

### 3. Repetition can be a voice move

In Chapter 93, Pessa returning Greg to `Again` is more character-specific than catching his eyebrows. Her method is physical accumulation before architecture. Sometimes the best dialogue variance patch is not a new clever line but another repetition of the task.

### 4. Physical causes can make expression-reading legitimate

Pepper changes eyes, breathing, chewing, and facial tension. A card game makes eye movement and performed expression part of the rules. Face-reading is not banned. The question is whether the observation has an actual visible cause and whether the conclusion stays proportional to it.

### 5. Social support can remain deliberately underperformed

Jorren's `Good` after Greg's first post-loss mana draw works because Greg tells the person who owns the phenomenology, Hessa, and tells Jorren only the milestone. Jorren does not need to counterfeit specialist curiosity or ceremonial emotion to prove friendship.
