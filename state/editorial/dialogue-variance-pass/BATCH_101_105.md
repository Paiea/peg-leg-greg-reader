## Durable craft findings

### 1. Let experts be wrong about the protagonist sometimes

Chapter 105 is valuable because Hessa believes Greg's main desire is another thumb attempt and misses the larger question. Deep familiarity should improve her guesses, not make them infallible. A wrong but reasonable read creates more humanity than another perfect prediction.

### 2. The protagonist should still own the dangerous register

Chapter 104 contains the same preemptive `No / I didn't say anything / you were going to` structure the pass often removes from supporting characters. It works better when Greg initiates it. The goal is not to ban the machinery; it is to restore asymmetry around Greg.

### 3. Apparatus changes can replace personality commentary

Two copper strips, a shield, and a towel are already conversational objects. When Greg notices the changed setup, the expert can answer, withhold, instruct, or redirect around the object rather than announcing what Greg's mind is doing.

### 4. Professional self-limitation is voice

Hessa declining a scheduled draw because she is tired enough to know she is tired is stronger characterization than another witty refusal. Expertise includes knowing when not to spend margin.

### 5. Integrated movement and controlled testing should stay distinct

Pessa simplifies movement to see it better without pretending the body is isolated. Hessa changes conditions to answer narrow causal or safety questions. Their dialogue can remain equally compact because the thing each woman protects is different.
