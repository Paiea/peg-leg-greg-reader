## Durable craft findings

### 1. A prediction can often become an instruction without losing the joke

Sevren does not need to predict that Greg will try to run. `Do not run` followed by `Then do not make bad decisions quickly` preserves relationship humor while keeping Sevren in route-safety authority.

### 2. Test attention is procedure, not personality

When gaze or attention can affect a shaping task, Hessa can control it directly. `Eyes up` is stronger than converting Greg's staring into a generalized personality slogan.

### 3. A scene can legitimately center face-reading when observation is the actual game

Chapter 108 demonstrates the safe version. Card behavior is visible, interpretations are fallible, signals can be noticed without being believed, and nobody wins perfect access to another mind.

### 4. Healthy refusal density can include repeated words when the attempted action changes

Pessa's repeated `No` to Alden in Chapter 110 is safe because Alden keeps proposing new ways to remain in the training space. The repeated word serves one concrete coaching boundary rather than proving Pessa can predict every sentence.

### 5. Expert disagreement is strongest when the uncertainty itself remains live

Hessa does not have to choose between `Greg deliberately redirected the alteration` and `nothing happened.` The pattern changed after the stop instruction, but degraded control remains another plausible explanation. Keeping both possibilities alive protects her from becoming a machine that always supplies the final interpretation.
