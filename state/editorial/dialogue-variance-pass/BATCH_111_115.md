## Durable craft findings

### 1. Visible affect and hidden reasoning are different claims

A character can see disappointment, excitement, fear, or strain without gaining access to the exact argument inside Greg's head. Emotional visibility is often safe where sentence prediction is not.

### 2. Familiarity should often improve the question

A close expert may know exactly which question is worth asking first. `Did you go back?` can demonstrate deep familiarity without requiring the expert to prove the answer before Greg speaks.

### 3. Productive professional confusion creates voice

Theatre rehearsal is especially healthy because participants can recognize a result before they can explain it cleanly. `Again`, bad notes, changed notes, improvised lines, and local disagreement are not defects to optimize away.

### 4. Stress can simplify language without flattening character

Lyssa moves from teasing and social uncertainty to short concrete emergency language once smoke becomes real. The shift is state-dependent voice, not inconsistency.

### 5. Background work can carry humor without becoming protagonist commentary

Burned boards, painted windows, squealing rope, dropped cups, stage traffic, and bad rehearsal timing generate comedy while allowing Greg to remain merely one person in the room.
