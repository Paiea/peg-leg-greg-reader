## Durable craft findings

### 1. A wrong prediction can be healthy relationship evidence

Chapter 119 is useful because Pessa predicts Greg's likely rope question and misses. Familiarity may create better guesses without creating perfect access. Do not automatically remove a prediction merely because it is a prediction; judge whether the character can be wrong and whether the miss matters.

### 2. Rough professional heuristics can stay if the work later corrects them

The rehearsal woman's `ask with his face` instruction is intentionally crude. Teren later shows that useful prompting cues are person-specific and that silence has multiple meanings. Productive professional development inside the scene is more interesting than making every authority correct immediately.

### 3. Spoken reasoning should outrank face-reading when both are available

If Greg has already counted the evidence aloud, Hessa should answer the argument he actually made. A face is weaker evidence than a spoken chain already sitting in the room.

### 4. Being useful does not require being important

Theatre repeatedly gives Greg temporary utility without converting him into the center of the institution. `You can read`, `approximately a person`, stand-in work, prompting, and furniture-level participation are strong social variance because the room's needs exist independently of him.

### 5. Repetition can become more human when the repeated attempt changes what people believe

Hessa's repeatability work and theatre rehearsal both repeat actions, but each repetition changes interpretation rather than merely restating the same conclusion. That is productive repetition, not dialogue sameness.
