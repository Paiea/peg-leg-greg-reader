## Durable craft findings

### 1. Relationship shorthand is healthier when it opens a question

Lyssa can say Greg is doing `the face` and then ask what he is thinking. The shorthand becomes an invitation to report rather than a substitute for his report.

### 2. Workflow integration is stronger than ceremonial belonging

Chapter 121 makes Greg a regular by making his body and presence part of ordinary sightlines, chair placement, page holding, prop handling, and traffic. Nobody needs to announce that he belongs.

### 3. A specialist can answer the current result instead of the protagonist's personality

Teren can say the horse scene still works. Hessa can say one differential observation. Specific professional consequence is stronger than another generalized statement about Greg's face or habits.

### 4. Expectation control should usually regulate setup, sequence, or interpretation

Hessa can say hands first, use today's mark, keep it bounded, or keep the count at one. Those are more Hessa-shaped than narrating the exact optimization Greg is performing internally.

### 5. Imperfect art can have a different stopping condition than precise engineering

The hoof board can be wrong, the cloth can be shapeless, the spare cup can alter the sound, and the theatre can still truthfully say `We have horse.` The protected object is the working scene, not technical perfection.
