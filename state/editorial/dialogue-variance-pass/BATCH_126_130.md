## Durable craft findings

### 1. Fallible knowledge can be stronger than clever inference

Sevren can know the current road better than Greg while still not understanding why Greg knows the old one. Competence does not require explanatory dominance.

### 2. Phase boundaries can replace personality policing

In technical scenes, `that happened during shape` is often stronger than `your face is interpreting again`. Naming the experimental phase protects the question without turning the expert into a mind-reader.

### 3. Deferred maintenance can be character-specific dialogue

Teren's `Leave the board` / `Then later` is a rehearsal decision. The raised seam is real, but if it does not stop the crossing, the current scene outranks floor repair for the moment.

### 4. A failed apparatus can still be a successful chapter of evidence

Hessa can park the thread setup because it is a poor use of finite tolerance while preserving the possibility that earlier movement mattered. Stopping an instrument is not the same as disproving the phenomenon.

### 5. Shared vocabulary is safest when authority remains local

`Again`, `No`, `Good`, `Hold`, and other short words may spread through a workplace. Voice remains distinct when Teren owns page/cue, the rehearsal woman owns staging/partner effect, workers own materials, and actors own playable behavior.
