## Durable craft findings

### 1. Social knowledge is safe when provenance stays ordinary

Gossip, work reputation, visible pages, and repeated public behavior can let recurring characters know more about Greg without turning them into mind-readers. Named or legible provenance matters.

### 2. Relationship shorthand can coexist with disagreement

Lyssa can recognize a face or habit and still ask whether Greg is okay, misunderstand technical details, or have priorities that are not about him. Familiarity should not collapse two people into one model.

### 3. Technical rules should usually be stated as rules

If Hessa wants Greg not to enlarge a response for easier reporting, she can say exactly that. The rule is stronger than proving he secretly considered violating it.

### 4. Schedule ownership can replace mental-process policing

`Tomorrow is not an unrecorded assessment` and `I have not chosen the next assessment` protect Hessa's jurisdiction more cleanly than `you are designing again`.

### 5. Performance scenes legitimately spend more attention on faces and reactions

Acting is one of the domains where gaze, timing, audience attention, and partner response are the evidence. The engine should judge the size of the inference, not mechanically purge expression language.
