## Durable craft findings

### 1. Travel pressure naturally creates variance

Fatigue, cramped wagons, missing information, work in motion, meals, ropes, costumes, horses, and bad roads create different sentence lengths and priorities without artificial voice gimmicks.

### 2. Bad metaphors are useful when they belong to the speaker

`Dead accountant` is not elegant analysis. That is why it helps. The servant actor reaches for an image that is weird, partial, and socially his.

### 3. Naming can reduce attribution load without increasing exposition

Once Davin, Serra, Iven, Marek, and Orin are named, crowded tour scenes become easier to follow while dialogue can remain locally messy.

### 4. Relationship inference is strongest when the evidence can be listed

Lyssa may reasonably conclude Greg is going east because she can point to what he carried, where he went, what he asked, and when he told her. That is different from `you have the face, therefore I know the hidden sentence.`

### 5. Work can interrupt explanation

A knot to tie, a needle near a finger, a wagon to load, a costume to fix, or a horse to water can end a conversation before everybody reaches a polished conclusion. Protect those interruptions.
