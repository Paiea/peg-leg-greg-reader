## Durable craft findings

### 1. A repeated phrase can become evidence of its own overdistribution

Chapter 154's narration `Everyone said that now` is a useful warning. When the manuscript itself notices that many people use the same `your face` mechanism, the pass should ask whether the line still belongs to the individual speaker.

### 2. Current object beats generic perception

Iven has the coat. Marek has an old local debt. Davin has wheels. Each can answer from that local object/history instead of proving they know Greg's unsaid sentence from his face.

### 3. Deliberately unsettling perception still needs provenance

Drell may be unusually observant because the chapter explicitly establishes that he watched Greg twice, knows Teren, and has gathered secondhand information. Stranger perceptiveness is not automatically a bug when the text makes the information path part of the scene.

### 4. Live work naturally tolerates partial failure

Dead lines, missed words, wrong entrances, late props, accidental audience responses, and incomplete repairs can remain in the scene when nothing downstream requires a perfect correction.

### 5. Tour ecology continues to protect variance

Fatigue, money, food, weather, transport, costumes, rooms, local crew, audience state, old history, and work interruption keep characters from converging into one optimized conversational rhythm.
