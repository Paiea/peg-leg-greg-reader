## Durable findings from 156-165

1. **Temporary comic convergence can be legitimate when shared improvisation is the work.** During an active comic performance, several actors may briefly share faster setup/payoff rhythm because they are collaboratively keeping the same live piece alive. Judge whether their offstage/work speech re-separates before diagnosing voice collapse.
2. **A resource owner can act instead of predicting.** Covering the pot, protecting a count, moving an object, or closing access can express authority more specifically than another anticipatory `No`.
3. **Visible object trouble should usually outrank unsaid-sentence prediction.** A missing coat, interrupted coin count, wall the company will not stop for, or loose thread can carry the exchange without another character proving they know Greg's next line.
4. **Role contamination is not automatically voice convergence.** When the manuscript identifies language as belonging to the wrong role, characters react to the contamination, and Greg recognizes it, the mismatch can be deliberate performance comedy rather than baseline voice leakage.
5. **Workplace compression remains healthy when responsibility stays visible.** Teren, Rinna, Davin, Nessa, Pell, Iven, and Marek can all speak briefly without becoming the same person if the current protected object and decision lane remain distinct.
