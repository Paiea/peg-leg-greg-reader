## Variance test

This batch tested the touring/fair/return sequence for:

- first-contact strangers arriving already calibrated to Greg's dry prediction game;
- repeated `No / I didn't ask / you were going to` ladders;
- repeated `you looked` or `you leaned` as proof of an unsaid sentence;
- specialists narrating Greg instead of protecting the current object, schedule, route, or resource;
- legitimate terse work language being mistaken for voice convergence;
- temporary performance-rhythm convergence being mistaken for baseline dialogue convergence;
- relationship familiarity replacing questions rather than sharpening them.

The road arc is healthier than its quantity of short dialogue suggests. Travel pressure, fair logistics, road geometry, stage timing, food, oats, carts, props, lanes, weather, and money constantly interrupt speech. Shared short words usually remain attached to different work jurisdictions.

The real leaks occur when the same predictive comedy mechanism appears in characters who do not need it, especially first-contact workers and specialists.

## Durable findings added by Chapters 166-175

1. **First-contact professional scope is stronger than instant Greg calibration.** A courier can say what he uses and what he does not know without first predicting Greg's question.
2. **Overloaded speech can differentiate authority better than sharper banter.** Harel becomes more specific when he protects a schedule that has already moved twice rather than joining `No / you looked` comedy.
3. **Earned familiarity can compress to the object without becoming mind-reading.** Nessa's `Lamp? / Yes. / Get it.` is relationship/work shorthand because the subject has a visible history.
4. **Relationship care can become a practical boundary instead of a prediction contest.** Lyssa can make Greg finish eating without needing to prove where his head turn would have taken him.
5. **Visible affect remains valid evidence.** Lyssa catching Greg looking pleased about the clasp is different from knowing an exact private inference.
6. **Expertise should demand the usable question.** Hessa's `Specific question` protects her method better than answering `No` to a sentence Greg has not yet said.
7. **Live performance remains a higher-tolerance zone for temporary rhythmic convergence.** Shared comic timing is often the current job; offstage re-separation is the stronger baseline test.
8. **Messy systems are voice protection.** Dast schedules, road queues, Harel's supper, route uncertainty, and imperfect local directions force people to speak from actual pressure rather than polished character-brand dialogue.
