## Durable findings added by this batch

1. **A direct handling rule is often stronger than a personality read.** If Hessa can say `Do not touch those`, she does not need to prove Greg was about to touch them.
2. **Naming the retired or protected object can replace predictive `No`.** `Not the threads` is more Hessa-specific than rejecting a request Greg has not spoken.
3. **Material authority should prefer the damaged object over the imagined action.** Nessa's `Then look lower` keeps the cracked foot central and removes a generic `you were about to` win.
4. **Different audiences should continue receiving different versions of the same event.** Pell, Rinna, Teren, Lyssa, Nessa, and Hessa do not need one shared definition of Greg's paper result because they are asking different questions of it.
5. **Social overstatement can be deliberate variance rather than contamination.** Pell compressing uncertain evidence into a better story works because Greg and Hessa explicitly resist that compression.
6. **Short expert answers are safest when the apparatus or work has already earned them.** Hessa's bounded `Yes` in Chapter 182 works because the prior trials carry the qualification.
7. **Regular competence is current-state competence.** Repeated settings stay fresh when today's bill, storage, route, object use, or customer need changes what the correct answer is.
