## Durable findings added by this batch

1. **Threat pressure does not justify universal mind-reading.** Characters may become alert, practical, or suspicious without gaining access to unsaid sentences.
2. **Object custody is especially useful under stress.** Brace, knife, hat, runner's copper, cart pin, and repaired hardware can carry boundaries more cleanly than `you were thinking`.
3. **Let Greg voice the escalation.** If he wants an address, a search, or a cross-lane connection, he can say it and let Rinna/Lyssa/Antonius answer the stated idea.
4. **A recurring joke can survive if the evidence becomes visible.** `You leaned` is healthier than `you were thinking` because the body actually did something.
5. **Information-discipline voices should defend thresholds, not private thoughts.** Antonius is strongest when he says half a name and one story are not enough; Rinna is strongest when she marks what is unknown; Hessa is strongest when she controls the apparatus.
6. **Volunteer information is not mind-reading.** A produce seller who came to discuss Talla can simply say no one approached her.
7. **Marked hypervigilance can remain character-specific.** Greg noticing coats, hands, wheels, and door knocks under pressure is useful because the narration also shows false positives and self-correction.
8. **Ordinary workers should retain low narrative ambition during danger.** Runners want their copper, customers want lamps fixed or tickets sold, Talla has a real wheel waiting, and Sera Venn has a wedding to book.
9. **Pressure scenes benefit from physical jurisdiction.** Door, cart, axle, pin, hat, coat pegs, ticket box, and venue geometry keep multi-speaker scenes differentiated without extra verbal cleverness.
10. **The extortion sequence remains cumulative rather than conspiratorial.** Each chapter strengthens or limits a claim without manufacturing an organization around Bren.
