## Durable findings added by this batch

1. **Wrong prediction can be positive variance.** A guess is useful when the text visibly lets it miss. Nessa guesses Greg wants a role and is wrong; Hara guesses Marek wants food and is immediately wrong before later behavior partly vindicates the suspicion.
2. **Work continuing can replace curiosity policing.** In a room Greg does not understand, the strongest way to preserve other people's independence may be to let them keep working instead of announcing that they know he wants an explanation.
3. **Predeclared procedure is stronger than predicted intention.** Hessa's written order and attempt rules already constrain Greg. She does not need `you were about to` to establish control.
4. **Social overstatement can stay when another explanation remains live.** The onion seller can call Greg afraid because he stood there for five minutes; Greg can call it incomplete instructions. The scene does not need to settle the inner truth.
5. **Object-specific anticipation remains cheaper than generic mind-reading.** Nessa protecting a particular table, box, coat, or prop is materially different from predicting Greg's unsaid sentence.
6. **Catchphrase self-policing can be positive evidence.** Greg noticing that he borrowed another seller's `strong standard` and deciding the joke should die prevents local phrasing from silently becoming city-wide voice glue.
