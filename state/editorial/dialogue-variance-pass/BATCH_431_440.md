## Durable findings

1. **Do not let a relationship joke become a regional diagnosis.** `Road face` is funny once; independent strangers repeating the same named shorthand turns character voice into author voice.
2. **Visible object attention is better evidence than facial transcript.** A driver can notice Greg staring at throat glass. A seller can notice he returned to the same stall. Sori can point at the brass object he is still examining.
3. **Earned shorthand may survive, but it should vary.** Sori has enough history to recognize Greg's acquisition/work modes. That does not mean every recognition should be phrased as `your face said X`.
4. **Greg may still own the inversion.** His face-jokes toward Dask work when Dask has already made the underlying desire obvious through speech, research, posture, or action.
5. **Role-specific humor remains the best protection against convergence.** Pera's jokes come from paid sorting and theft risk. Orla's come from institutional nouns and broken museum pieces. Varn's come from auction garbage and birds. They can all be funny without sounding like Greg.
6. **Institutional conflict remains major voice variance.** Market custody, observatory practice, Survey surplus, coach authority, harbor work, and beacon standards all produce different sentence pressures.
7. **Supporting characters continue to have independent roads.** Maro, Sori, and Dask now make materially different travel decisions. Protect that.
8. **First-person drift remains sparse but active.** Plain `Greg brain ...` self-reference should return to `my/I` unless it is clearly a deliberate labeled internal persona construction.
9. **Correction overlays outrank checkpoint drift.** Ch432 forewoman is Rika Dorn. Do not let editorial review accidentally re-legitimize the superseded Nessa Vale name.
10. **No-change remains a valid result.** Chapters 438-440 are strong evidence that the newer manuscript engine is already producing more differentiated ensemble dialogue under pressure.
