## What already improved

### Speaker ownership

The current first 20 are substantially easier to read at normal speed than the earlier dialogue state that motivated the attribution pass.

The cold read did not produce a recurring `who said that?` problem.

Three-person and larger exchanges were generally trackable without repeated backtracking. This supports keeping the aggressive ownership and re-anchoring rules already in the main dialogue variance engine.

### Local voice repair

The existing variance pass correctly identified many early contamination patterns before this calibration layer existed.

High-value prior decisions include:

- Arlo returning to the bench or test instead of winning every dry volley;
- Antonius returning to decision, borrower, ledger, or work consequence;
- Hessa replacing some joke answers with procedure and stopping conditions;
- Jorren returning to visible physical evidence and action;
- incidental professionals being pulled away from instant Greg-compatible sarcasm;
- Alden retaining more permission than most characters to keep up with Greg.

These were the right direction and should remain protected.

## Residual reader-ear finding

**Overall cadence: MILD CONVERGENCE**

The residual issue is not usually individual bad lines.

The issue is cumulative surface similarity among several competent early characters:

- short answer;
- dry correction;
- Greg response;
- cleaner supporting correction;
- compact final button.

Antonius, Arlo, Hessa, Jorren, and some incidental professionals often have distinct reasons for speaking, but enough of them remain capable of the same optimized surface rhythm that the reader can hear a shared house machine across chapters.

This means the prior pass was useful but its acceptance criteria were too local by themselves.

## Character calibration

### Greg

Strongly recognizable. Preserve his role as the person most likely to extend wording, rationalize, find the absurd reduction, and keep a bit alive.

Do not reduce Greg's comic compression merely to make the cast different.

### Antonius

Already one of the strongest early characters.

His distinction should come from scope discipline and willingness to stop, not from making him verbose or humorless.

Reader-ear watch: too many consecutive predictive refusals or polished final buttons can still make him feel like Greg's equal comedy machine rather than the man who returns to the actual decision.

### Arlo

Underlying cognition is highly distinct.

Reader-ear watch: when his material or test has already supplied a natural exit, another abstract dry counter is often less valuable than returning to the object.

### Hessa

Strongest when procedure itself governs the rhythm.

Reader-ear watch: rare dry humor is good. Repeated superior joke answers to Greg are not necessary and weaken the significance of the rare ones.

### Jorren

Strongest when physical evidence ends the debate.

Reader-ear watch: do not let shared familiarity with Greg turn him into a universal interpreter of Greg's face, hidden thought, or next sentence.

### Alden

Intentional overlap with Greg is a feature.

Do not flatten him to solve convergence.

The distinction is directional: Greg uses speed to interpret and extend; Alden uses speed to seize initiative, test something, or move toward the next opening.

## Calibration thresholds

### Preserve

Preserve a shared short or dry rhythm when responsibility, relationship, and exit behavior stay distinct.

Examples:

- Hessa's safety `No`;
- Jorren's short physical correction;
- Antonius's operational refusal;
- Arlo's short object-specific answer;
- Alden matching Greg during an earned competitive exchange.

### Flag

Flag when:

- three or more turns form a polished compact counter ladder;
- multiple supporting characters finish the same Greg-shaped joke architecture;
- a specialist keeps arguing after their own work could naturally end the exchange;
- two unrelated professionals read Greg's hidden intention using the same social shortcut;
- a chapter contains several individually valid dry exchanges whose aggregate cadence feels uniform.

### Patch only after reader confirmation

Do not reopen all visible Chapters 1-20 merely because the new layer exists.

Use this sample as a calibration lab. A future early-chapter repass should propose only high-leverage exchange changes that remain noticeable on a continuous read.

The target is not louder voices.

The target is **clearer asymmetry across scenes without damaging the shared Carrow social register or Greg's relationships.**
