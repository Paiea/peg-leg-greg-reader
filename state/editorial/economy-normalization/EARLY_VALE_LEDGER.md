# EARLY VALE / SILVER LEDGER

Status: **AUDITED THROUGH CHAPTER 20**

Purpose: trace Greg's early Vale obligations and the manuscript's silver-scale economic anchors before any normalization edits are made.

Authority used: exact reader prose in `chapters/001.html` through `chapters/020.html`. Chapter 21 was checked only as a boundary sanity check after the requested range.

## Executive finding

The early Vale debt does **not** get cleanly paid off inside Chapters 1-20.

Instead, the original Chapter 2 loan becomes a **composite running Vale account** by Chapter 5. Exact prose says the ledger contains:

- original loan;
- interest;
- second advance;
- third advance;
- payments;
- another advance;
- fees;
- a sword-instructor guild note;
- Arlo material costs;
- a gambling stake covered by Vale's man.

Greg has made some payments, because Antonius explicitly says he has been paying on time, but Greg has also continued borrowing. Chapter 5 says the debt has grown and Greg does not have enough liquid cash to clear it.

From Chapter 6 onward, creditor labor becomes part of servicing that running account. Exact debt-labor credit is not numerically stated in the first 20 chapters.

The Tere master-gauge purchase adds a second explicit Vale obligation, but its number drifts inside the early manuscript:

- Chapter 7: negotiated to **5 silver**, seller-financed to end of week.
- Chapter 10: described as still Greg's once he produces **10 silver**.

No on-page renegotiation between those chapters explains the change from 5s to 10s. Treat this as a **hard numeric continuity contradiction**, not as evidence of a hidden payment.

By Chapter 12 Greg is still explicitly treated as Vale debt labor. No chapter through 20 contains a clean balance-zero event, release, refinance document, or explicit statement that Greg's early Vale obligations have been fully settled.

Therefore:

**The later nominal 23c Vale account cannot currently be derived from the exact Chapters 1-20 ledger.**

The story shape is coherent: Greg borrows aggressively, earns, wastes, invests, makes partial payments, borrows again, and works for Vale. The numeric ledger is not yet coherent enough to connect that early silver-scale account to the much later 23c figure.

## Boundary check after requested range

Chapter 21 was checked only to determine whether Chapter 20 had silently closed an account between scenes.

It opens with Greg carrying **four coppers** while looking for covered practice space. There is no immediate statement that Vale has been paid off or that the early account was closed between Chapters 20 and 21.

This is not enough to extend the formal ledger beyond Chapter 20, but it supports the conclusion that the early Vale problem did not end cleanly at the requested boundary.

## What happened to the original Chapter 2 loan?

The most defensible answer from exact Chapters 1-20 is:

1. It begins as a short-term silver loan with a high percentage rate and creditor-labor fallback.
2. Greg partially services it while earning/gambling.
3. Greg repeatedly adds new borrowing and Vale-paid costs.
4. By Chapter 5, exact prose stops treating it as one simple loan and explicitly presents a **running composite ledger**.
5. Greg cannot clear that ledger in cash, so creditor labor becomes active from Chapter 6 onward.
6. The Tere gauge adds another seller-financed Vale obligation in Chapter 7.
7. The Tere nominal amount then drifts from **5s to 10s** by Chapter 10 with no visible reason.
8. Greg continues doing debt labor through at least Chapter 12.
9. Chapters 13-20 contain no explicit final settlement.

So the early debt did not disappear. **Its exact balance stopped being legible.**

That is the continuity problem the normalization pass needs to solve.

## What must NOT be concluded yet

Do not conclude any of the following from Chapters 1-20 alone:

- that Greg's original Vale principal was an exact guessed number;
- that creditor labor was worth an exact copper/silver rate;
- that the 5s Tere price or 10s Tere price is automatically correct;
- that calling the Tere set `Greg's` proves it was fully paid;
- that the later 23c balance is the residue of the early account;
- that the later 23c balance is definitely a separate account;
- that copper/silver/gold have an unstated fixed conversion.

## Normalization implications

### 1. Preserve early economic behavior
The early story is internally coherent at the behavioral level:

**Greg earns, borrows, wastes, spends, invests, pays some, borrows more, then works debt.**

Do not rewrite that into prudent poverty.

### 2. Silver is already normal operating money
By Chapter 2, specialist work costs 6s. By Chapters 4-5 Greg is gambling and spending silver. By Chapters 7-10 he is discussing 5s/10s obligations and 40g theoretical artifact value while Antonius handles ordinary multi-silver and gold loans.

Any later economy pass that makes silver feel mythical is wrong.

### 3. The recent 23c Vale arc is structurally reusable, numerically unproven
The later beats can survive:

- verify real balance;
- pay materially without emptying reserve;
- improve earning power;
- clear final balance almost casually;
- relationship changes after closure.

But **23c / 12c / 11c cannot yet be called normalized historical continuity**.
