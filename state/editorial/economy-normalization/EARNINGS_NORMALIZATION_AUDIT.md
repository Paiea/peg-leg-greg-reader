# EARNINGS NORMALIZATION AUDIT

Status: **AUTHOR APPROVED / QUEUED**

Purpose: audit paid work across the manuscript for scale regression, especially places where Greg's later scarce/high-risk contribution is compensated like generic early labor.

This audit is not a blanket raise pass. It classifies each economically meaningful paid scene by what the work is actually worth in context.

## Classification

Every reviewed earning scene receives one primary classification:

- **KEEP** — pay and reaction fit value/risk/current Greg.
- **RAISE** — nominal compensation is too low for value/risk/scarcity.
- **LOWER** — nominal compensation is implausibly high for the work or damages later progression.
- **REFRAME** — number can stay, but Greg's reaction/reason for accepting needs calibration.
- **NEEDS CONTEXT** — exact neighboring authority or obligation must be resolved first.

Secondary flags may include:
- CRIME PREMIUM
- SPECIALIST JUDGMENT
- COMMISSION
- MANAGEMENT
- BROKERAGE
- CAPITAL AT RISK
- ARTIFACT VALUE
- SOCIAL PAY
- TOKEN PAY
- URGENT WORK
- REPUTATION ACCESS

## Audit questions

For each scene, read the exact prose plus enough neighboring authority to answer:

1. What work did Greg actually do?
2. How replaceable was he?
3. What underlying value was affected?
4. What failure cost did the client face?
5. What legal/physical/reputational risk did Greg accept?
6. Did Greg deploy capital?
7. Was cash the main reward?
8. What other earning opportunities had Greg already demonstrated by then?
9. Did his reaction fit that demonstrated earning capacity?
10. Does the scene advance, preserve, or accidentally regress the economic ladder?

## Priority order

### Priority 1 — criminal / gray work
Audit every later scene involving:
- illegal freight;
- smuggling;
- contraband;
- deliberate evasion;
- hidden cargo;
- criminal counterparties;
- institutional/legal exposure;
- dangerous gray-market logistics.

Key test:

**Would a rational Greg accept this risk for this cash payment given his available legal alternatives?**

If no, either:
- raise compensation;
- establish valuable non-cash upside;
- establish coercion/desperation;
- or revise acceptance logic.

For serious illicit logistics, tens of silver are a plausible band. 30s+ may be appropriate where exposure/value is substantial. Gold-scale compensation is possible for major contraband or organized/high-value operations. These are calibration bands, not fixed rates.

### Priority 2 — appraisal / artifact / specialist knowledge
Early authority already pays Greg 6s for one valuable appraisal contribution.

Later specialist work should not automatically pay less simply because the manuscript drifted into copper-heavy bookkeeping.

Audit:
- artifact assessment;
- provenance work;
- unusual material identification;
- specialist magical inference;
- commercial judgment affecting valuable goods;
- rare information asymmetry.

### Priority 3 — brokerage / intermediation / commissions
Audit fees against:
- underlying transaction size;
- capital risk;
- replaceability;
- conflict/relationship burden;
- whether Greg saved counterparties time or prevented failure.

A commission can be a small percentage-feeling slice of a much larger transaction without establishing a fixed rate.

### Priority 4 — coordination / management / urgent contracts
Audit organizer and contractor work for:
- number of people/crews coordinated;
- commercial consequences of delay;
- scope responsibility;
- urgency;
- reputation/referral effects;
- what a substitute would cost.

Once Greg is coordinating valuable operations, generic day-labor pay should require explanation.

### Priority 5 — ordinary legal labor / theatre / social work
Preserve cheap work where it belongs.

Examples that can remain cheap:
- carrying flour;
- simple watch/escort work;
- casual theatre support;
- one-off manual help;
- friendship/socially motivated tasks.

The likely correction here is often **REFRAME**, not RAISE: the payment may be nice without being financially important.

## Representative-era strategy

Do not read 490 chapters linearly before learning anything.

Audit in representative eras first:

1. Chapters 1-20 — early silver-active baseline. **DONE via `EARLY_VALE_LEDGER.md`.**
2. Early/mid Book 1 — first repeat legal work and first higher-value opportunities.
3. Late Book 1 / early Book 2 — whether copper became accidentally sticky.
4. Mid Book 2 — theatre/social work versus commercial work.
5. Book 3 pre-road — management, specialist work, artifacts, commissions.
6. Road / gray / criminal work — priority high-risk calibration.
7. Chapters 470-current — reconcile recent 1c/3c/10c/14c compensation against the newly established value/risk engine.

After representative anchors are established, patch bounded chapter ranges.

## Patch method

For each approved correction:

1. Change nominal pay first.
2. Recalculate only arithmetic that depends on it.
3. Change purse/debt figures that directly depend on the transaction.
4. Adjust reaction language only if the new scale changes the emotional beat.
5. Preserve scene purpose, relationships, plot outcome, and causality.
6. Update durable economic state.
7. Verify later references to the changed number.

## Do not over-normalize

Do not make every later job silver or gold.

A healthy economy has simultaneous tiers.

Greg may earn 4c moving flour and 6s appraising collateral in the same era because those are different economic products.

The target is not smooth inflation.

The target is **coherent value-based differentiation plus visible upward access to better-paying classes of work.**

## Current high-priority hypothesis

The strongest likely underpayment errors are expected in:
- later crime/gray freight;
- high-risk transport;
- specialist appraisal/provenance;
- silver-scale brokerage paid in tiny copper;
- multi-party coordination/management;
- urgent commercial contracts.

Treat this as an audit hypothesis, not permission to change numbers without exact prose review.
