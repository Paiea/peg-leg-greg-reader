# FORWARD EARNING LADDER

This file constrains manuscript generation after the economy-scale correction was approved.

It does not establish universal wages or coin conversions. It establishes **relative scale and reaction**.

## Core rule

Greg's economic progression is not measured only by purse balance.

Track:
- what size opportunity he can credibly obtain;
- what size value he can move;
- what size fee/commission he can earn;
- how much capital he can deploy without collapse;
- how often work reaches him through reputation/referrals;
- whether he can refuse low-value work;
- how much a loss changes his next decision;
- whether he is buying time, judgment, ownership, and leverage rather than merely selling labor.

## Current correction

Do not use the recent copper-heavy chapters as proof Greg belongs economically at copper scale.

Exact early authority already shows a young Greg who can:
- borrow multiple silver;
- spend 6s on specialist testing;
- gamble at silver scale;
- finance equipment/objects at silver scale;
- identify gold-scale right-buyer value;
- accumulate debt because he repeatedly deploys money faster than he retains it.

That early pattern sets the floor for how ambitious his financial thinking can be.

## Relative tiers

### A. Incidental money
Bits / very small copper.

Use for:
- food;
- minor convenience;
- tiny theatre/support payments;
- cheap transport shares;
- social spending;
- small supplies.

Later-current Greg may notice these prices but should rarely experience them as progression.

### B. Ordinary working money
Copper-scale amounts that can cover a bounded task, ordinary tool, small repair, or routine paid block.

Use as:
- normal life texture;
- supplemental income;
- relationship/access work;
- short bridging work.

Do not repeatedly frame this tier as "Greg finally made real money" once he has demonstrated larger capacity.

### C. Skilled / meaningful day money
Multi-copper through low-silver-equivalent-feeling opportunities, without fixing conversion.

This tier should increasingly represent:
- competent coordination;
- specialized support judgment;
- urgent coverage;
- brokerage fees;
- successful gambling/trade outcomes;
- work where failure has meaningful commercial cost.

Greg should compare these offers against alternatives instead of automatically accepting.

### D. Silver-operating tier
Silver is a real operating denomination, not a fantasy threshold.

Use for:
- specialist services;
- serious training;
- equipment;
- research batches;
- deposits;
- resale inventory;
- artifact opportunities;
- meaningful commissions/contracts;
- travel/logistics packages;
- business-risk capital.

Greg can still be unable to afford a specific silver expense when his cash is tied up. The point is that the denomination itself is not psychologically alien.

### E. Tens-of-silver leverage tier
This tier becomes appropriate when Greg's contribution is scarce, commercially meaningful, urgent, risky, or difficult to replace.

Use for:
- serious coordination/management where delay or failure costs the client materially;
- high-value brokerage/intermediation;
- specialist appraisal or artifact/provenance judgment;
- meaningful gray-market logistics;
- criminal work with real arrest/seizure/retaliation exposure;
- high-value freight or contraband where Greg's discretion/route/judgment matters;
- commissions attached to much larger underlying value.

**30s+ is a plausible band for serious high-exposure work, not a universal minimum.**

This tier should increasingly become visible once Greg has demonstrated legal alternatives that make tiny-risk-premium criminal work irrational.

### F. Gold-value / gold-compensation tier
Gold-scale value should increasingly appear through:
- rare artifacts;
- businesses/assets;
- high-value contracts;
- major inventory;
- specialist systems;
- land/property/ownership if later canon moves there;
- cumulative capital positions;
- major contraband or organized high-risk operations where Greg's own cut can plausibly reach gold.

Gold-value does not imply gold liquidity.

The Tere gauge is the canonical early example: potentially 40g+ to the right buyer while Greg's acquisition obligation is only 5s and his ability to realize that value is uncertain.

### G. Mature capital tier
Eventually Greg may again operate where gold and possibly platinum-tier wealth are ordinary enough to be strategic rather than astonishing.

Do not rush this.

But do not artificially cap the book below it either. Old Greg's remembered life establishes that very large economic scale belongs in the world.

## Value-before-denomination rule

Before choosing a payment number, ask what economic product Greg is selling.

He may be selling:
- body/time;
- routine competence;
- scarce judgment;
- trusted access;
- coordination;
- management;
- information asymmetry;
- discretion;
- capital;
- legal/physical risk;
- ownership.

Do not price all of these like interchangeable labor.

Canonical contrast:
- moving flour can pay copper because the work is replaceable manual labor;
- the gauntlet appraisal can pay 6s because Greg's judgment contributes to a valuable lending decision.

Both can coexist in the same era without contradiction.

## Crime / gray-work premium

Use `PAY_VALUE_RISK_ENGINE.md` for full logic.

Forward shorthand:
- petty illegal work can still pay poorly;
- real smuggling / deliberate rule evasion should usually beat ordinary safe labor when risk is meaningful;
- serious illicit logistics / valuable contraband / institutional exposure can plausibly live in the **tens-of-silver** band;
- 30s+ can be reasonable when Greg's role is scarce and downside is real;
- major contraband, rare artifacts, dangerous routes, or organized operations may justify **gold-scale compensation**;
- none of these are automatic wage floors.

If Greg risks arrest for pay comparable to flour-moving, the manuscript must establish why he accepts the bad deal.

## Reaction progression

### Early reset Greg
Reaction can be contradictory:
- shocked by how little he has;
- casually proposes spending too much;
- knows 6s is currently painful but remembers when it was invisible;
- wins money and immediately treats it as deployable;
- feels richer too quickly;
- mistakes expected income for capital;
- resents low-quality gear because he remembers better gear.

### Rebuilding Greg
He starts distinguishing:
- liquid reserve;
- committed capital;
- debt;
- assets;
- income;
- expected income;
- speculative value.

He is still capable of breaking his own rules.

### Current/later Greg
He should increasingly think:
- "Is this worth my day?"
- "What else can this money do?"
- "Do I need to own the thing?"
- "Can I earn from moving it instead?"
- "Does this scale?"
- "What is my downside?"
- "How much capital does this trap?"
- "Who else can do this cheaper/better?"
- "Why am I taking this risk for that pay?"

The question should move from **can I scrape together the coin?** toward **what is the best use of my capacity?**

## Satisfaction rule

Greg should not be equally pleased by every gain.

Once his demonstrated earning capacity rises:
- 1c may be nice, funny, or socially validating, but not financially impressive;
- 3c may be a decent incidental gain, not a breakthrough;
- a multi-copper contract may be respectable but judged by time/risk;
- silver-scale fees/deals should become the meaningful near-term progression band;
- tens-of-silver opportunities should become visible when his leverage/risk warrants them;
- eventually even silver should become operating money rather than a climax.

This does not mean Greg becomes ungrateful.

It means his reference point changes.

## Waste remains allowed

Progression does not turn Greg into a perfect optimizer.

He can waste more money later because he has more money.

Valid later waste:
- overpaying for convenience;
- buying a weird artifact because he wants it;
- taking a bad speculative position;
- treating friends;
- upgrading gear before strictly necessary;
- paying specialists for curiosity;
- losing money on something entertaining;
- spending to save time;
- buying quality because he hates the cheap version;
- making an emotionally motivated purchase while fully aware it is not optimal.

The key difference is consequence scale.

A later bad purchase should usually cost opportunity or annoy Greg, not automatically reset him to survival poverty.

## Forward chapter gate

Before writing a paid opportunity, ask:

1. What tier is this opportunity?
2. What is Greg actually being paid for?
3. Has Greg already demonstrated access to a higher tier?
4. If yes, why is he taking this lower-tier work?
5. Is the pay itself important, or is another benefit the real reason?
6. Does the amount reflect the value/risk/urgency/scarcity of the work?
7. What does the client gain or avoid losing?
8. How replaceable is Greg?
9. Does Greg's reaction fit his current earning capacity?
10. Does this chapter move the economic ladder, preserve it, or intentionally step down for a concrete reason?
11. If illegal/gray: does the risk premium justify Greg choosing this over legal alternatives?

If the only answer is "Greg needs money," the opportunity probably needs more thought.
