# VALE LEVERAGE MODEL

Status: **AUTHOR APPROVED / ACTIVE NORMALIZATION AUTHORITY**

This file governs interpretation of Antonius Vale's lending relationship with Greg during economy normalization.

It supplements exact manuscript prose. Where old numeric prose conflicts with this approved correction, use the explicit normalization queue before patching rather than inventing a new explanation.

## Core interpretation

Antonius Vale's rates are genuinely predatory on paper.

The early **35% over eight days** term is not being softened or retconned into a friendly loan.

Antonius knowingly finances people conventional lenders often would not touch, and he structures those loans so that failure to repay cleanly leaves him with durable leverage.

He is not secretly benevolent.

He likes leverage.

He benefits when useful people remain in his orbit.

## What Antonius expects from many borrowers

Antonius does not assume every risky borrower will amortize the account neatly in cash.

A borrower may instead produce return through:
- bounded creditor labor;
- commercial information;
- introductions;
- appraisal or specialist help;
- first look at opportunities;
- occasional favors;
- availability for useful tasks;
- continued relationship/access.

Do not convert those relationship returns into invented exact coin credits unless exact prose establishes one.

## Face balance versus settlement balance

Vale accounts may contain two materially different economic concepts.

### Face ledger balance
The raw paper balance if principal, advances, financed purchases, agreed fees, and brutal accrued interest are allowed to accumulate.

### Settlement / collectible balance
What Antonius actually expects the borrower to clear after he decides how much accrued interest/soft charge he intends to enforce and after considering value already received through labor and relationship.

This is not charity.

Antonius can rationally decide that a nominal interest line has already done its job by keeping a useful borrower attached to him.

## Greg-specific interpretation

Greg is financially arrogant, not financially ignorant.

He understands that the rates are awful.

He accepts them anyway because old-life Greg is accustomed to solving money problems by making more money, and young Greg repeatedly assumes future earning power will arrive faster than it does.

Therefore Greg may correctly understand the contract and still later conclude, bluntly, that he was an idiot for taking it and then repeatedly adding to it.

## Late Vale settlement target

Current approved normalization target for the late verification sequence:

- **Face ledger balance:** approximately **96 silver**.
- **Antonius non-collects / waives:** approximately **72 silver** of accrued interest and soft charges.
- **Settlement balance Greg must actually clear:** **24 silver**.

These are normalization targets for the late prose patch, not a universal interest formula.

The face balance should make Greg recognize that he knowingly accepted insane financing and kept adding to it.

The settlement should still be expensive enough that the debt was real.

Antonius's decision to strip out the large accrued-interest component should be framed as a business judgment:
- Greg already produced labor and useful relationship value;
- Antonius did not need to financially cripple him to extract another nominal interest line;
- Greg has finally become independently productive enough that keeping the leash has diminishing value;
- the account has accomplished what Antonius wanted it to accomplish.

## Antonius emotional calibration

Antonius should be more impressed by normalized Greg than the old 23c version allowed.

He backed an undercapitalized, overconfident young man who:
- borrowed aggressively;
- gambled;
- funded research;
- bought strange things;
- repeatedly assumed future income would save him;
- nevertheless kept returning, working, learning, and becoming useful.

By the late closure, Greg is independently earning real silver through judgment, coordination, brokerage, and contracting.

Antonius can recognize that the wager worked.

Keep the reaction understated.

No speech about believing in Greg all along is required.

## Ethical guardrail

Do not rewrite Antonius as a saint.

His model is intentionally uncomfortable:
- he helps people others may reject;
- he may genuinely keep them operational;
- he also designs contracts that preserve leverage over them;
- some borrowers may reasonably view that as exploitative;
- he benefits from the asymmetry.

Greg's successful outcome does not prove the model is good for everyone.

## Relationship consequence

The important meaning of the final Vale payoff is not that Greg finally found a handful of coins.

It is that Greg has become strong enough economically to end a leverage relationship Antonius could otherwise have kept alive almost indefinitely.

That makes Chapter 491's social drink more meaningful:

there is finally no ledger under the joke.
