## SOURCE EXCERPT

"Stop," I said.

Both men looked at me.

Jorren lowered his sword.

Alden did not.

"Why?" Alden asked.

"Because you're doing the same thing three different ways," I said.

"Which thing?" Alden asked.

I walked closer.

"You think the exchange ends when you get what you wanted."

Alden's smile faded.

"Explain."

"First bout, you wanted distance before he settled. You got it. Then you kept pressing because the first success felt like proof the rest belonged to you. Second, you broke his center. Good. Then you chased the opening he gave you after you had already won the useful part."

"I lost."

"Eventually."

"And the third?"

"You were losing, so you stopped assuming anything belonged to you. You became patient. You gave him distance. You made him earn every step. You almost touched his hand."

Alden looked at Jorren.

Jorren nodded once.

I continued.

"You're better when the fight tells you you're in danger than when the fight tells you you're right."

Silence. The sentence had come out larger than the sword work. Jorren looked at me sharply. I adjusted.

"So stop trying to win twice."

Alden looked back at me.

"What?"

"If you take the first advantage, keep it. Don't immediately spend it proving the advantage was real. Make him solve the new problem."

Alden's eyes narrowed. Thinking, not accepting.

"That's it?"

"For now."

"Show me."

I smiled.

Jorren said, "No."

I looked at him.

"Again, you are answering questions directed at me."

"I'm saving time."

Alden looked at the sword on my hip, then at my stance.

"Can you?"

Old Greg would have demonstrated the principle beautifully. Current Greg could demonstrate the first half and then discover which part of the yard tasted best.

"Not reliably," I said.

Alden blinked.

Jorren smiled.

"Then how do you know?" Alden asked.

"Because doing and seeing are different skills."

"Convenient."

"Very."

He considered me for another moment, then looked at Jorren.

"Again."

Jorren raised his sword.

This time Alden did not attack.

Jorren moved first. Alden gave him a narrow outside line. Jorren took it cautiously. Alden caught the blade, stepped inside, and reached the same position as the second bout.

His body wanted the next strike. Then he stopped. Not froze. Stopped. He took one small step back instead.

Jorren had already begun the recovery that would have punished the chase. Now the recovery cut empty air.

Alden had kept the advantage.

Jorren had to rebuild distance from a bad angle.

Alden waited. Then Jorren moved.

Alden touched his shoulder. Clean.

The sound was barely anything. Wood against leather. My entire body reacted as though someone had struck a bell inside my chest. Pleasure. Pure and immediate. I had forgotten that part.

Just one observation placed in the correct mind at the correct time, and suddenly the person in front of me became harder to beat.

That had always felt good. Too good.

Alden stared at the place his practice sword had touched Jorren. Then he looked at me. There was no gratitude in his face. Excellent. There was appetite. Worse.

"Again," Alden said.

Jorren hit him in the mouth.

Alden stepped into the next exchange too eagerly, tried to recreate the correction as a trick rather than understand it, and Jorren punished him before he finished setting it up.

Alden sat in the sand with one hand over his lip.

Jorren looked at me.

"Your student," Jorren said.

"I have taught him one sentence," I said.

Alden checked his fingers for blood.

"Good sentence," Alden said.

"Bad religion," I said.

He laughed, which made his lip hurt, which made him swear.

## DRAMATIC SCRIPT

### LOCKED
- Greg identifies Alden's tendency to overspend an advantage after initial success.
- Greg owns the teaching insight and cannot reliably demonstrate it with his current body.
- Jorren owns the physical training domain and the actual sparring pressure.
- Alden successfully applies the advice once, then overapplies it as a trick and is punished physically by Jorren.

### REQUIRED
- Alden understands the usable correction well enough to test it.
- Greg experiences strong pleasure from seeing one useful observation improve another person.
- Alden's success increases appetite rather than producing instant mature understanding.
- The next attempt proves that the correction is not a universal trick.

### MUST NOT DRIFT
- Greg does not gain current physical ability he does not have.
- Jorren does not become Greg's philosophical interpreter.
- Alden does not become instantly disciplined after one lesson.
- No injury beyond the existing minor mouth/lip hit is introduced.

### STATE IN
- Greg is intellectually excited and pleased to be useful as a teacher.
- Alden is curious, confident, physically eager, and unusually receptive to correction that produces advantage.
- Jorren is practical, already aware of both men's tendencies, and more interested in what bodies prove than in extending their theories.

### STATE OUT
- Alden has one useful principle plus immediate evidence that he can misuse it.
- Greg rediscovers the pleasure and danger of making someone better through one correct observation.

### ENVIRONMENT
- Guild training yard with practice swords, active sparring, physical fatigue, public enough setting, and immediate feedback through contact.
