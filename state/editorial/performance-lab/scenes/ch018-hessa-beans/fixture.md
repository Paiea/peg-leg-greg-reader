## SOURCE EXCERPT

Hessa took one bean and placed it on the table between us.

"Move it."

I looked at her.

"With Barrier."

"I assumed."

"Did you?"

I hated teachers.

I gathered mana.

I formed the smallest plane I could manage beside the bean. I pressed. The bean skittered three inches and rolled off the table.

Hessa watched it fall.

"Again."

I bent to pick it up.

"No."

I froze.

"Use another."

"There are forty beans."

"Then we can afford your mistakes."

Second bean. This time I put the plane closer. Too close. The Barrier formed partly through the bean's edge, failed to stabilize, and collapsed before it fully existed. A needle of pain ran behind my right eye.

Hessa saw my face.

"What happened?"

"Placement."

"Meaning?"

"I intersected the target."

"You can put Barrier inside a cup but not beside a bean."

"The cup is larger."

"Excellent. You have discovered size."

"I knew about size."

"Your spell didn't."

I stared at her.

She slid another bean onto the table.

I tried again. The third moved. The fourth did not. The fifth jumped hard enough to strike the bowl. The sixth rolled in a beautiful straight line. The seventh taught me nothing. The eighth taught me that the seventh had probably taught me something and I had been too pleased with the sixth to notice.

I stopped.

Hessa stopped reaching for beans.

"What?"

"The anchor."

I eventually realized I was making a wall for a job that needed a tap.

"What would you change?"

"I don't know."

"Good."

Hessa pushed the bowl toward me.

"Find out."

I took a bean. This time I did not cast. I turned it between my fingers.

"What does Barrier need to be Barrier?"

Hessa sighed.

"That is the wrong question."

She tapped the bean.

"What does this exercise need?"

I almost answered immediately. Then I understood why she had asked.

Given a bean and a table, I had tried to solve Barrier. Not move the bean. Barrier. The whole spell.

I put the bean down.

"It needs the bean to move."

"How far?"

"You didn't say."

"Then ask."

"How far?"

"One finger."

I formed the plane close to the bean and let it exist for as little time as I could manage. The Barrier flickered. The bean clicked sideways. About two fingers.

The mana cost had been tiny.

Hessa's gaze sharpened.

"Again."

I did. Too weak. Again. Too close. Collapse. Again. The bean moved half a finger. Again. One and a half. My headache remained a pressure instead of becoming a spike.

Duration. I had been shortening duration because I wanted lower cost. Except there was a difference between a short Barrier and a Barrier that never intended to remain.

My pulse quickened.

"Again," I said.

Hessa reached across the table and covered the bowl with the cloth.

I looked at her hand.

"What are you doing?"

"Stopping."

"I've barely started."

"You've cast nineteen times."

"That cannot be right."

"It is."

I counted backward. I lost track at twelve.

"I feel fine."

"No, you feel interested."

"Those are compatible states."

"Not for you."

I leaned back. The pressure behind my eye had spread toward my temple.

Hessa folded her arms.

"Tell me what you learned."

## DRAMATIC SCRIPT

### LOCKED
- Hessa owns the exercise procedure and the decision to stop it.
- Greg owns his Barrier attempts, physical self-report, and realization that he is solving too large a problem.
- Greg discovers that a very brief small Barrier can move the bean at much lower apparent cost under this narrow condition.
- Hessa stops further attempts after nineteen casts by covering the bowl.

### REQUIRED
- Greg understands the exercise only needs the bean moved one finger, not the whole Barrier spell solved.
- Hessa notices pain/load evidence and continues only within the bounded exercise until the stop point.
- Greg's excitement makes his own fatigue/load less salient to him.
- The result remains narrow: this observed bean exercise does not establish a generalized cheap-Barrier law.

### MUST NOT DRIFT
- Hessa does not gain access to Greg's internal thought except through observable behavior and what he says.
- Greg does not receive casting clearance or broader magical permission.
- No new magic result, number of attempts, or body consequence is invented.
- Hessa's concern may shape procedure but must not be narrated as transparent emotion.

### STATE IN
- Greg is bruised, intellectually activated, and eager to turn the previous hook deflection into a larger theory.
- Hessa is concerned about both the recent violence and Greg's tendency to outrun evidence, but performs bounded procedure rather than an emotional debrief.

### STATE OUT
- Greg has a narrower working hypothesis about duration/cost under this setup and has been stopped before chasing it further.
- Hessa retains control of the exercise boundary.

### ENVIRONMENT
- Hessa's rooms, table, bowl of beans, empty bowl, cloth, repeated small Barrier attempts, accumulating headache/load, and close observation.
