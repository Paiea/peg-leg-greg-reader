## Locked

- Arlo owns the workshop process improvements and the decision about what process knowledge he shares.
- Arlo discovers that his hand-held test method introduced meaningful measurement variation.
- Arlo builds the crude wooden fixture and later the hanging-weight winding-tension control.
- Greg has the realization that mundane process control, not a grand invention, is the important development.

## Required

- Greg understands that asking for another run with changed order, temperature, or input would create different tests rather than simply repeat the current one.
- Greg understands that measurement spread dropped materially after Arlo stopped holding the lead by hand.
- Greg sees the hanging-weight tension control and understands what it does.

## Must not drift

- Arlo remains the owner of the process work. Greg does not become his boss or inventor.
- Greg may interpret future significance, but Arlo does not know the future importance of Arwick Works.
- The fixture and tension control remain crude, cheap workshop solutions.
- No new technical result is invented.

## State in

- Greg is excited by the improved regulators and wants every variable immediately.
- Arlo is tired, proud of the work, protective of authorship, and mildly defensive about how crude the fixtures look.

## State out

- Greg accepts that Arlo owns the process and recognizes the importance of measurement/process control.
- Arlo continues to the next actual workshop problem rather than becoming Greg's exposition engine.
