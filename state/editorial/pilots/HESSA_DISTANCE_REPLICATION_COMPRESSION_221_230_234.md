### Replacement prose

Then I went to the Guild.

Hessa had preserved the same room, board, glass, fins, chair marks, and far position. At this point the apparatus needed less introduction than some people I knew.

"Same question?"

"Narrower repeat."

"Far only."

"Yes."

She checked whether I had eaten, whether anything hurt, and whether I had done anything independently since the last session. I had eaten. Nothing hurt. I had done nothing magical without her.

The rest of the baseline had become routine enough that I could answer before she finished looking. Hand normal. Wrist normal. Shoulder normal. Residual limb comfortable. Phantom quiet.

Hessa still checked.

That was her part.

Mine was apparently to stop being surprised.

The supervised draw and shaping baseline brought the counts to thirty-one successful minimal draws and twenty-seven deliberate shaping attempts. Nothing about either one earned a new sentence.

Then she put the first fresh pair at far.

Attempt thirty-five.

Away only.

Nothing moved.

Target unchanged. Control unchanged.

Hessa recorded it and replaced the fins.

I looked at the empty result.

"Useful?"

"Yes."

"You say that about terrible things."

"I say it about information."

We rested.

Attempt thirty-six began the same way.

Far. Away only. Same apparatus. Same amount of effort I was supposed to use instead of the amount I wanted to use.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then the target turned.

Small.

The leading edge shifted away from me and the rest followed badly enough that I almost doubted it before Hessa told me to return.

I returned.

The control had not moved.

Hessa checked the marks from both sides of the glass.

"Observed?"

"Yes."

"Net away?"

"Yes. With rotation."

My chest tightened.

"Like thirty-three?"

She checked the earlier page.

"Similar category. Not identical movement."

I knew better than to ask whether far worked.

Mostly.

"A far response has happened twice now."

"In separate sessions under the current apparatus."

"That is what I said."

"It is not."

Of course it wasn't.

After the rest, attempt thirty-seven gave us nothing. Far again. Same request. No observed target response. Control unchanged.

One response in three far attempts today.

One earlier far response still existed.

The failures existed too.

Hessa wrote the summary while I drank the rest of the water.

FAR RESPONSE OBSERVED ACROSS MORE THAN ONE SESSION UNDER CURRENT APPARATUS.

FAR RELIABILITY NOT ESTABLISHED.

INTERMEDIATE REMAINS MORE RELIABLE ON CURRENT EVIDENCE.

NO RANGE ESTABLISHED.

NOT GENERALIZED.

I read it.

"So far works."

"No."

Worth trying.

"Far has repeated."

She considered that more carefully.

"A far response has been observed in more than one session."

"Mine was shorter."

"Yours removed the parts that matter."

I looked at the fin.

It had become a place where something had happened twice, surrounded by failures.

Annoyingly, that was not the same thing as having range.

The session ended at thirty-one successful supervised minimal draws, twenty-seven supervised deliberate shaping attempts, and thirty-seven supervised external-effect attempts.

"Restrictions?"

"Unchanged."

I waited.

Hessa waited longer.

"All of them?"

"All of them."

That saved us both the list.

I took my crutches.

"Next question?"

"Not today."

"You have one."

"Yes."

"What is it?"

"Not today."

There were times I suspected Hessa had entered medicine only because interrogation was not respectable enough.

## Chapter 230 - THE REPLICATE

### Replacement scope

Replace the Hessa protocol block beginning with:

`I put my crutches where they had gone before and sat.`

and ending with the session-closing:

`I left.`

immediately before the chapter returns to the late-day / home material.

with the following:

### Replacement prose

I put my crutches where they had gone before and sat.

The chair found its marks. So did my foot. Hessa checked the familiar body inventory, got nothing interesting, and wrote it down anyway.

Then she turned the top page around.

INTERMEDIATE.

FAR.

INTERMEDIATE.

FAR.

"That's backward."

"From last time."

"Which makes it backward."

Last time had been far, intermediate, far, intermediate. Today she had reversed the order without changing the room, board, glass, paper, positions, or requested direction.

Away only.

I looked at the page again.

"So intermediate gets fresh Greg."

"Intermediate comes first and third."

"And far gets second and fourth."

"Yes."

"The question is whether the difference survives changing order."

"Approximately."

That was enough methodology for one afternoon.

The supervised draw and shaping baselines were uneventful. They counted anyway.

Then we did the four attempts in the order Hessa had written before I arrived.

Attempt forty-two, intermediate: observed target response. Control unchanged.

Attempt forty-three, far: no observed response. Control unchanged.

We rested.

Attempt forty-four, intermediate: no observed response. Control unchanged.

That one annoyed me more than the far failure.

Hessa noticed.

"Same effort."

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Currently less."

She waited until currently became enough.

Attempt forty-five, far: no observed response. Control unchanged.

That was the whole block.

No dramatic collapse. No secret farther distance. No convenient extra attempt because the pattern looked ugly.

One intermediate response.

One intermediate failure.

Two far failures.

Four controls that spent the afternoon being exemplary pieces of paper.

Hessa put the two comparison pages beside each other.

Last session had begun far. Both intermediate attempts had responded. One far attempt had failed cleanly and the other had been contaminated when the control moved too.

This session began intermediate. One intermediate responded and one did not. Both far attempts failed cleanly.

"So the difference is still there."

Hessa did not answer.

Too large.

I tried again.

"Intermediate has produced more clean observed responses than far under this setup, across more than one session."

"Yes."

"But intermediate failed today."

"Yes."

"So intermediate is not reliable."

"Correct."

"Far still has the clean responses from earlier sessions."

"Correct."

"Today doesn't erase them."

"No."

"Today also doesn't strengthen far."

"Correct."

I leaned back.

"Did reversing the order matter?"

"We do not know."

"Excellent."

"We now have one session beginning with each condition under the same four-condition structure."

"Which tells us?"

"We have those results."

There she was.

The counts now stood at thirty-three successful supervised minimal draws, twenty-nine supervised deliberate shaping attempts, and forty-five supervised external-effect attempts.

No range.

Not generalized.

Intermediate response repeated across sessions, but not reliable.

Far reliability still unestablished.

No order effect established.

"Restrictions?"

"Unchanged."

"All of them?"

"Yes."

Efficient.

At the door I looked back at the board.

"Still several questions?"

"Yes."

"More or fewer?"

"Different."

That was somehow worse.

"Next?"

"Not decided."

The control had done absolutely nothing all afternoon and therefore had an excellent day.

I left.

## Chapter 234 - THE CONDITION

### Replacement scope

Keep the ordinary free morning, breakfast stall, cracked spoon, and Hessa note.

Replace the repetitive Guild-session block beginning with:

`Everything else was where it had been.`

and ending with the session-closing:

`I left.`

before the chapter returns to Carrow and the apartment.

with the following:

### Replacement prose

Everything else was where it had been.

Same room. Same board. Same glass. Same intermediate and far marks.

Nothing occupied far.

Hessa's page contained four identical lines.

INTERMEDIATE.

INTERMEDIATE.

INTERMEDIATE.

INTERMEDIATE.

"That looks lazy."

"It is controlled."

"No far?"

"No."

"No new distance?"

"No."

"No new direction?"

"No."

"No exciting mistake?"

"Not planned."

Fair.

The point was not another comparison. Intermediate had produced clean responses across multiple sessions and clean failures too. Hessa wanted four predeclared attempts at the same position without alternating far between them.

"So we're asking whether it keeps happening when we stop changing distance."

"Approximately."

There she was.

She checked the baseline once. I was rested, fed, uninjured, and had done no independent magical work.

The supervised draw became number thirty-four.

The shaping baseline became number thirty.

Neither deserved a scene.

Then the paper did.

Attempt forty-six, intermediate.

No observed response. Control unchanged.

Attempt forty-seven.

The target gave a small clean movement away with minor rotation.

The control stayed still.

Hessa checked the marks while I stayed where I was supposed to stay.

"Observed?"

"Yes."

I smiled.

She allowed it for almost long enough to become suspicious.

Then we rested.

Attempt forty-eight produced nothing.

Attempt forty-nine produced nothing.

Both controls remained unchanged.

I looked at the four results.

"No. Yes. No. No."

"That is one way to record it badly."

"One in four today."

"Today."

"So intermediate is unreliable."

"That was already supported."

"Does one in four become a rate?"

"No."

"Does removing far between attempts matter?"

"We did not compare that."

"Does this tell us why forty-seven worked?"

"No."

"Does it establish range?"

"No."

"Generalize?"

"No."

"Excellent session."

"It answered the question."

That was worse because she was right.

A clean intermediate response had occurred again under the same apparatus. Three other intermediate attempts in the same session had produced none. The accumulated record still supported a distance-associated difference, but intermediate was not reliable and today's block did not establish a response rate, an expectation effect, or anything about removing far from the sequence.

The counts were now thirty-four successful supervised minimal draws, thirty deliberate shaping attempts, and forty-nine supervised external-effect attempts.

"Restrictions?"

"Unchanged."

"All of them?"

"Yes."

That saved time too.

Hessa checked my hand, wrist, forearm, shoulder, and the rest of the familiar inventory. Nothing concerning had changed.

At the door I looked at the unused far mark.

"Distance still matters?"

"Under the current apparatus, the accumulated record still supports a difference associated with distance."

"Associated."

"Yes."

"Not range."

"No."

"Not reliable intermediate."

"No."

"Not generalized."

"No."

"Good."

Hessa looked at me.

I smiled.

"Still hate the word."

"Leave."

I left.

## Editorial effect

This is intentionally not "less magic."

It is less repeated setup.

The reader still sees the scientific accumulation:
- intermediate works enough to become meaningful;
- far responds, but badly and inconsistently;
- controls sometimes destroy the exciting interpretation;
- reversed order does not magically clean the data;
- intermediate itself fails often enough to resist becoming a simple capability.

The three kept full scenes carry the discoveries. The compressed sessions carry replication.

That is the intended division of labor.
