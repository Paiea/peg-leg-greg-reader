# CHAPTER 271

## THE DISTANCE

I woke rested enough to qualify.

Hessa had written **if rested**, which meant tired determination was still tired.

I ate before leaving.

Lyssa was gone already. My right shoe made no sound. I checked Savin's repaired heel once, found the seam flat and the three nail heads where she had left them, then put the shoe back on.

Hessa's note stayed in my pocket.

**Tomorrow. Midmorning. If rested. Bring nothing.**

I brought the note.

This was technically paper.

I decided to lose that argument in person.

The Guild room was open.

Same room.

Different table.

The glass apparatus was gone.

So were the paper fins.

A narrow board lay across one end of the room with three ink marks. Near. Middle. Far, if the arrangement was as obvious as it looked.

A plain square of pale paper sat on the nearest mark.

Hessa looked at my pocket.

"Bring nothing."

"You sent the note."

She held out her hand.

I gave it to her.

She put it beside the notebook.

"Now nothing. Sit."

"My dignity?"

"You did not bring that."

I stared at her.

"You prepared that."

"Sit."

I sat.

"Range," I said.

"Distance."

"That is range wearing a coat."

"No."

I pointed at the paper.

"New square?"

"Yes."

"Same stock?"

"No."

That caught me.

"You changed the apparatus and the object."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Hessa opened the notebook.

"Because I am no longer asking which fin moves. I want to know whether we can observe an external effect without the old arrangement at all."

That was much broader than a stronger fin.

"Permission?"

"Today, under supervision, you may attempt an external effect on this paper square without the glass apparatus. Nothing else. Direction away."

"Three marks."

"Near first. Three attempts planned. Middle if I continue. Far only if I have a reason."

"Distance."

"Potentially. Body first."

She checked the usual things.

Sleep. Food. Pain. Hands. Forearm. Any independent draw, shaping, or attempt to move something since the last session.

No.

My right leg felt normal after the walk. Residual limb comfortable. Shoulders fine.

She glanced at the shoe.

"Repaired?"

"Outer heel seam. Savin fixed it."

"Pain?"

"No."

"Changed gait?"

"No. She wants to see the wear again before changing anything."

Hessa nodded.

"Good."

"You would like her."

"Start position."

Apparently not.

The paper sat flat on bare wood.

No glass. No control fin. No second object.

"No control?"

"Not yet."

"Why?"

"I want to know whether anything happens before choosing what a control needs to answer."

I smiled.

"That sounded expensive."

"Expectation?"

There she was.

I looked at the square.

"I think it moves."

"Why?"

"Three different fins have responded at least once under the old setup."

"Which is gone."

"Yes. I still think it moves. Confidence unreasonably high."

She wrote that.

"You enjoy this."

"Yes."

Attempt sixty-two.

I settled my hand. Shoulder down. Breathing ordinary. Minimal permitted draw, same external intention as before.

Away.

Hessa gave the start.

Nothing happened.

I stopped on time.

"No observed response," she said.

She marked it.

Attempt sixty-three went the same way.

Same square. Same near mark. Same permission.

Nothing.

My confidence became less unreasonable.

"Glass matters," I said.

"Maybe."

"Fin shape."

"Maybe."

"Paper stock."

"Maybe."

"Squares hate me."

"No."

"Unsupported certainty."

"Begin when ready."

Attempt sixty-four.

I expected less and told her so.

For most of the attempt, the square stayed flat.

Then one corner shifted.

The whole piece slid a small distance away from me and stopped at a slight angle.

I stopped.

Hessa was already at the board.

She checked the square, the board, the room, then me.

"Small observed net displacement away. Slight rotation."

I grinned.

"No glass."

"Correct."

"Different stock. Not a fin. One in three."

"Correct."

"Broader."

"Different."

"Hessa."

"Do not make me regret the word."

I looked at the square.

Plain paper. Bare board. No glass.

It had moved.

"What can we say?" she asked.

I knew this part now.

"The old glass apparatus was not present for one observed external response. Neither was a fin shape."

"Yes."

"Different paper stock. Still paper. Still close. Still direction away. No reliability claim."

"Yes."

I looked at the middle mark.

"And no range claim."

Hessa followed my eyes.

"Stand."

I laughed.

We broke.

I walked the room while she reset. Hands warm in the ordinary way. No pain, tingling, weakness, or change in the leg.

She checked my pulse and forearm anyway.

"Urge to increase draw because the apparatus is gone?"

"Yes."

"Did you?"

"No."

"Good."

She moved the square to the middle mark.

The difference was not dramatic.

It was enough.

The old setup had taught my eyes a working space. This sat outside it.

"Three?" I asked.

"Two first."

"You said three."

"I changed the block."

"Because sixty-four moved?"

"Because I have new information and want fewer attempts before reassessing."

Fair.

"Expectation?"

"Lower."

"Why?"

"Farther feels harder."

"Evidence?"

"None here. Old life bias."

She wrote it.

Attempt sixty-five.

Nothing.

The paper stayed flat.

Attempt sixty-six began with the same permission and a worse temptation.

The square looked far enough away to invite effort.

Old magic in my head supplied habits I had not asked for. Distance usually cost something. Strength. Precision. Time. Mana. Different systems, different prices.

None of that was evidence about this.

My body still wanted to pay.

"Expectation?" Hessa asked.

"Low. And I want to push harder."

Her pencil stopped.

"Will you?"

"No. Same draw. Same time. No reaching."

She waited a moment longer, then nodded.

Start.

I worked.

Nothing happened.

Halfway through, the urge sharpened.

Not my hand. Not my shoulder. The internal reach I had begun to recognize wanted more.

I held the attempt where it was.

The end cue came.

I stopped.

The square had not moved.

Hessa marked the result.

"What changed?"

"The wanting."

"Did the attempt change?"

"No."

She closed the active sheet.

I looked at her.

"Third middle?"

"No."

"Far?"

"No."

"Because middle did nothing?"

"Partly."

"Because I wanted to push?"

"Mostly."

That annoyed me more than the paper.

"I didn't push."

"Correct."

"So I did the safe thing."

"Yes."

"And that ends the session."

"Yes."

I leaned back.

"That feels backward."

Hessa's expression did not change.

"Successful restraint is not a reason to immediately demand more restraint under a stronger temptation."

There was nothing useful to argue with in that sentence, which made it particularly offensive.

I looked at the marks.

Near: one movement in three.

Middle: none in two.

Far untouched.

Tiny numbers. Different positions. One session.

Interesting was not the same as settled.

"Near one in three," I said. "Middle zero in two. We cannot call middle a limit. We cannot call distance a reliability law."

"Correct."

"But we did get one external response without the old apparatus."

"Yes."

That was enough.

She checked my hand, forearm, pulse, and strength again. Everything remained ordinary.

"Restrictions?" I asked.

"Unchanged outside supervised sessions. Today's permission ends when you leave."

"No paper squares at home."

"No."

"Coins?"

"No."

"Thread?"

"No."

I looked down at the repaired heel.

"Shoe nails?"

Hessa looked at me.

"Absolutely not."

"Good. I like the shoe."

"Stop naming objects."

I stopped.

"Next?"

"Not selected."

"Middle again?"

"Possible."

"Control?"

"Possible."

"Different material?"

She closed the notebook.

"Leave."

Totals after the session: thirty-six supervised minimal draws, thirty-two deliberate shaping attempts, sixty-six supervised external-effect attempts.

Five new external attempts.

One response.

Outside, I passed the north fountain.

Orin stood beside his preserve cart and looked toward the cookstall.

"Lunch?" he asked.

The front bench had an open seat.

I pointed.

"Civilization solved you."

Orin looked across the street.

"Huh."

"You're welcome."

"You did nothing."

"My best work."

I kept walking.

At home, Lyssa arrived after me.

She saw the repaired shoe first, then me.

"Hessa?"

"Yes."

"Moved?"

"Once."

"Rules?"

"Same outside. Different setup."

I told her the short version.

No glass. Plain square. Three near attempts, one movement. Two middle attempts, nothing. No far attempt.

"Why stop?" she asked.

"I wanted to increase effort. I didn't. Hessa stopped anyway."

Lyssa nodded.

"Good."

"The middle paper did nothing."

"You did."

I looked at her.

She had already turned toward dinner.

That was the whole judgment.

Far remained untouched.

I still wanted to know what happened there.

For tonight, wanting could remain wanting.
