## Reading A: Imani playing Lyssa

Source: `chapters/226.html`, opening domestic/work exchange.

### Locked truth

- Lyssa is actively doing garment work at home.
- Hessa has left Greg an instruction to eat and not arrive tired.
- Lyssa observed Greg rolling his right shoulder the previous evening.
- The brown-cuff customer was charged for both requested changes, paid, and still preferred the first cuff.
- Lyssa has independent work and does not become Greg's medical authority or business exposition device.

### Named-actor take

Imani keeps Lyssa's hands and attention on the green garment while the conversation happens. She reads Hessa's note, returns it, notices Greg's attempt to relabel visible fatigue as theatrical emphasis, and continues pinning/folding rather than turning the exchange into a care speech. The customer complaint lands as work information, not a relationship lesson. Lyssa's `I charged her twice. I feel fine.` works because it closes the customer problem economically and lets her continue the garment.

### Findings

- **SOURCE WIN:** the current prose already lets Lyssa own an independent task while talking to Greg.
- **movement:** garment handling should remain active during domestic dialogue when current canon places work on the table.
- **voice:** Lyssa is distinct when she answers the actual practical issue and declines Greg's invitation to inflate it.
- **relationship behavior:** noticing Greg's shoulder is grounded in an observed repeated movement, not transparent access to his body or emotions.
- **hypothesis:** when customer rework irritates Lyssa, one plausible recurring response is to price the extra work, finish it, and move on rather than narrate grievance. This remains derived until independently supported enough for promotion.

## Reading B: Noa playing Alden

Source: `chapters/017.html`, South Canal grain-theft investigation.

### Locked truth

- Greg and Alden take the Bronze contract together.
- Alden is not merely Greg's trainee or follower.
- Alden independently notices that an outside thief would have an awkward route and asks why the sacks nearest the damaged wall were not taken.
- Greg owns the broader hypothesis management and cuts his own list of possible traps.
- The pair wait inside the warehouse after dark.

### Named-actor take

Noa plays Alden more physically and directly than Nico plays Greg. Alden leans, taps the posting, tests the damaged board, watches the sack route, challenges Verran without converting every observation into a theory, and becomes physically restless during the long wait. His strongest contribution is the concrete question about why the nearest sacks were not taken. Greg can then widen or narrow the implications.

### Findings

- **SOURCE WIN:** the investigation already contains useful independent Alden cognition.
- **movement:** Alden's body can carry impatience and risk appetite during waiting scenes rather than giving him extra clever dialogue.
- **voice:** direct operational questions distinguish him better than Greg-style systems explanation.
- **relationship shorthand:** the repeated `your face did` pattern may be earned mutual shorthand rather than generic voice collision; watch frequency before changing it.
- **hypothesis:** Alden may often notice the immediate physical problem first and state it plainly, leaving Greg to over-expand or formalize the system around it. Do not promote from this scene alone.

## Reading C: theatre ensemble

Source: `chapters/225.html`, cup/reset/rehearsal sequence from *The Second Toast*.

Cast:

- Vera / Nessa
- Malcolm / Teren
- Suki / Hara
- Jules / Marek
- Nico / Greg

### Locked truth

- red cup belongs to Hara in scene two; blue cup belongs to Pell in scenes one and three.
- Greg mistakenly privileges the less-broken blue cup over scene continuity.
- Nessa owns prop/reset continuity.
- Teren owns the acting/scene correction.
- Marek drinks from the painted blue cup, creating a real property concern, but no damage occurs.
- Hara remains a strong peer performer and later owns the audience in *The Window Tax*.

### Ensemble take

Vera does not explain prop theory. She holds up the wrong cup, asks where red is, takes the painted cup away from Marek, checks it, and resets it. Malcolm's useful intervention is narrow: Greg must read the play's required object meaning rather than improve the object's physical condition. Jules lets Marek's curiosity and improvisational energy put his hand on the wrong object, but he stops immediately when the material consequence becomes clear. Suki does not turn Hara into Greg's teacher; her `Correct.` lands as peer judgment, while her later tax-play performance demonstrates that her strongest identity does not depend on Greg at all.

### Targeted second take: Marek object collision

Reason: Marek's `Temporary identities everywhere.` is witty enough to risk sliding toward Greg's compressed meta-humor.

Physical take result: the more character-specific signal is not a replacement joke. It is Marek already having the cup in hand, freezing when Nessa identifies it as property, checking the dry paint, surrendering it, then remaining socially buoyant rather than embarrassed or stupid. The source action already carries most of this.

Verdict: **SOURCE WIN / WATCH LINE**, not an edit.

### Findings

- **Nessa:** material authority is strongest when expressed through custody, inspection, reset, and direct correction.
- **Teren:** strongest notes are local and playable; avoid promoting every useful instruction into philosophy.
- **Hara:** performance ownership and peer recovery should remain independent of Greg's arc.
- **Marek:** object engagement can cause trouble without making him a liability; physical curiosity is a stronger discriminator than giving him constant clever lines.
- **voice watch:** Marek's short meta-humor can occasionally overlap Greg. Frequency and surrounding behavior determine whether it is a real collision.
