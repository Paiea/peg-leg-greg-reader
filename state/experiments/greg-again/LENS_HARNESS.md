# GREG, AGAIN — SUBJECTIVE LENS HARNESS

Status: **ACTIVE EXPERIMENT / NON-CANON**

Purpose: make the Greg-surface experiment easier to test without turning an interesting stylistic possibility into a fixed rule.

The harness exists to answer one question:

> **Which degree of Greg mediation makes this scene most interesting without flattening the people or world around him?**

Do not optimize for novelty. Do not optimize for zero dialogue. Do not assume the same lens belongs on every scene.

## UPDATE 1 — SUBJECTIVE LENS REHEARSAL

Before a consequential scene is treated as stylistically settled, allow up to three materially different lens strengths.

### LIGHT

Conventional first-person scene architecture with a strong Greg interior.

- direct dialogue is normal
- Greg still interprets aggressively
- outside voices retain ordinary textual space

### GREG-DOMINANT

Greg owns most linguistic continuity.

- routine conversational ping-pong is compressed
- other characters remain embodied and causally active
- direct speech survives when hearing the actual voice improves the moment

This is the current default candidate, not a locked answer.

### EXTREME

Greg owns nearly all language reaching the audience.

- zero quoted secondary dialogue is allowed
- outside communication reaches the audience through Greg unless the scene fails without a voice breach
- use as a stress test, not as an ideological target

Do not generate all three by quota. Rehearse alternate lens strengths only when the scene contains meaningful uncertainty about mediation.

Selection question:

> **Which treatment is most interesting while preserving first-pass clarity and independent character life?**

If LIGHT wins, use LIGHT. If EXTREME wins, use EXTREME. The experiment has no prestige hierarchy.

## UPDATE 2 — OUTSIDE-AGENCY LOCK

Before a Greg-dominant or extreme render, capture the minimum independent state for each consequential non-Greg actor.

For each actor record only:

- **WANT** — what they want now
- **ACTION** — what they do that does not originate in Greg
- **SCENE DELTA** — how their behavior changes Greg's available state
- **GREG MISREAD** — what Greg may misunderstand, underweight, or distort
- **CONTINUATION** — what they are likely to keep doing if Greg disappears from the scene

The render may change wording. It may not erase these causal facts merely because Greg owns the linguistic surface.

A supporting character fails the lock if the scene can be summarized as `they existed so Greg could think about them`.

## UPDATE 3 — VOICE-BREACH GATE

Secondary direct speech is neither mandatory nor forbidden.

Before paraphrasing or restoring a line, ask:

> **Is hearing these exact words more interesting than hearing Greg metabolize them?**

A direct line has a strong claim to survive when one or more are true:

- the wording itself is the joke
- the wording causes the misunderstanding
- the line changes a relationship
- the speaker's verbal identity is doing real character work
- the interruption needs to puncture Greg's mental track
- the line is threatening, intimate, humiliating, surprising, or memorable in a way paraphrase weakens
- Greg would plausibly retain the wording verbatim

Do not restore dialogue merely because prose traditionally uses dialogue.

Do not remove dialogue merely because EXTREME is an available lens.

A voice breach should feel earned enough that the reader or listener notices another person has temporarily taken linguistic space away from Greg.

## UPDATE 4 — ANTI-FLATTENING + ANTI-TIC QC

After a subjective render, inspect for two different failure classes.

### Flattening

Flag passages where dialogue was removed but nothing alive replaced it.

Common warning shapes:

- `Sella explained...`
- `Jorren told me...`
- `she said that...`
- multiple exchanges compressed into factual recap with no timing, body, leverage, refusal, object, mistake, or consequence
- a supporting character becomes legible only through Greg's opinion of them

Repair by restoring embodiment first. Restore direct speech only when the voice-breach gate earns it.

### Greg tic saturation

Greg's vocal grammar is useful until it becomes generated texture.

Watch for local overuse of:

- `Right.`
- `Fine.`
- `No.`
- `Wait.`
- `Of course.`
- one-line fragments
- repeated self-correction cadence
- every observation ending as a joke
- identical setup → dry punchline rhythm across unrelated beats

Do not ban recurring tics. Ask whether each occurrence reflects an actual cognitive move.

If removing half the tics changes nothing about Greg's thinking, the renderer is imitating the surface rather than rendering the mind.

## UPDATE 5 — FAILURE BECOMES LEARNING, NOT DOCTRINE

Every useful comparison should record what won and why, including failures.

Do not promote a rule from one successful scene.

A lens preference becomes stronger only after repeated evidence across different scene types.

Useful scene classes include:

- solo exploration
- bureaucracy / negotiation
- training / action
- ensemble interaction
- intimacy / relationship turn
- mundane work or logistics
- high-emotion confrontation

For each comparison record:

- winning lens
- what became more interesting
- what became less interesting
- whether another character lost identity or agency
- whether direct speech became more valuable or merely rarer
- whether audio and prose wanted different lens strengths
- any Greg tic or flattening failure

STORY SYNC may learn repeated patterns such as `training tends Greg-dominant` or `intimacy wants more voice breach`, but these remain priors rather than hard style laws.

## STOP RULE

Do not create more style machinery because the experiment feels novel.

Add or change the harness only when a repeated failure appears, a simpler rule replaces a more complex one, or cross-scene evidence shows the current lens selection is systematically wrong.
