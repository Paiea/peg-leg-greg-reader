# Greg, Again — Chapter 1 Audio Subjective Cut 001

Status: EXPERIMENTAL

Audio score relation: alternate rendering experiment, not replacement authority
Renderer audition: AI Voice Generator, `deep` style

## Purpose

Test a more audio-native grammar where **Greg's thought-thread is the continuity track**.

The world still acts independently. Greg does not own causality. But the listener spends more time inside the associative movement of his perception, memory, judgment, embarrassment, tactical read, and bodily experience.

Direct speech from other characters is expensive. Keep it when exact wording, relationship movement, humor, misunderstanding, interruption, or vocal presence materially improves the heard scene. Otherwise let Greg experience the consequence and carry continuity forward.

## Spoken cut

I woke because my back didn't hurt.

That was wrong.

Not alarming at first. Waking normally involved a short negotiation with several pieces of me that had outlived their warranties. Lower back. Left shoulder. Right knee. The rib that clicked if I slept twisted.

Nothing dramatic. Just attendance.

This morning nobody showed up.

I moved my toes.

All of them answered.

That was when the room started bothering me.

Cheap plaster. Narrow bed. One chair with my coat hanging off it. Washbasin with the crack down the side.

I knew that crack.

Cut my thumb on it once.

I looked at my thumb.

No scar.

Wrong hand, maybe.

The other one was clean too.

Young hands.

That is a stupid phrase until you've spent fifty-nine years watching your own hands get worse at being hands.

My right index finger was straight.

That finger had not been straight in decades.

Contract outside Red Hollow. Bad landing. Broke it. Didn't reset it because we had another descent the next morning and apparently thirty-two-year-old me had considered permanent hand damage a scheduling problem.

I bent the finger.

Straight.

Again.

Still straight.

No scar. No ache. No complaint.

The sword was in the corner.

Of course it was.

Cheap steel. Bad balance. Leather grip. A nick near the guard from when I'd used it to split kindling because finding an axe had seemed like more work.

I remembered loving that sword.

That was almost more offensive than the finger.

I picked it up.

Too much weight forward.

I knew that immediately.

Then I cut downward and nearly clipped the chair.

Right.

Knowing was apparently still not doing.

Good to establish that before breakfast.

## What this cut is testing

- associative thought movement instead of clean explanatory transitions
- bodily sensation as a trigger for memory
- old-life memories arriving because the current object provokes them, not because exposition needs them
- humor emerging from Greg's judgment rather than narrator performance
- external objects interrupting and redirecting his thought line
- a male/masculine primary reader performing Greg without an artificial old-man voice
- minimal direct dialogue in the proving excerpt
