The Guild had not gotten smaller.

I had gotten used to larger rooms.

That distinction took almost the length of the entrance hall to arrive.

Wet leather. Cheap lamp oil. Metal polish. Too many people who believed coming back alive qualified them to shout indoors.

Yes.

The old Carrow Guild.

Contract boards on the far wall. Bronze left. Silver farther in. Gold behind a waist-high rail I remembered not existing later.

Gold.

There had been a time when that rail mattered to me.

Later, people moved when I walked through rooms like this.

Now a courier nearly hit me with a bundle of forms and made it very clear I was standing in his way.

I moved.

Good start.

The clerk behind the front desk had ink on two fingers and the expression of someone who had already explained the same rule six times that morning.

Young.

Not that young.

Twenty-two, maybe.

I knew her.

That was becoming an unpleasant sentence.

Not personally.

Not yet.

Future Sella had run half this building through a combination of memory, threat, filing systems, and the ability to make adventurers feel ashamed of wasting her paper.

Present Sella had one loose strand of hair stuck to her cheek and a Bronze fighter discovering, under her supervision, that forms had backs.

He had signed the front.

The declaration was on the back.

He objected to this arrangement as though Sella had invented two-sided paper specifically to hurt him.

He considered the arrow too small.

I laughed.

Damn it.

Proof that time had moved backward.

Training.

Money.

Too much.

I put the Bronze token on the counter instead and asked what it currently got me.

That word caught her.

Currently.

Probably.

Training yard included. Instructors not included.

There.

Three copper.

Enough for the yard.

Exactly.

Next.

My stomach turned over once.

Sella had already looked down at the next form.

For her it was Tuesday.

That helped.

I stepped away from the counter.

The Bronze board was full.

Rat-cellar clearances. Road escorts. Two crop-guard jobs outside the south wall. Missing pack lizard. Saltback infestation.

That one paid embarrassingly well until I remembered saltbacks reproduced in walls and excreted acid when frightened.

No.

I had died enough times near acid without making it permanent.

The yard was behind the main hall.

I heard it before I reached the doors.

Wood striking wood. Boots in sand. Someone swearing. An instructor counting repetitions without any sympathy for whether the person performing them had stopped enjoying numbers.

The sound hit harder than I expected.

Not nostalgia.

Something closer to hunger.

I pushed through the doors.

Three practice lanes. Two sparring circles. Row of posts. Weapon racks. Sand dark where people had poured water over their heads.

Bronze fighters everywhere.

Bad guards. Big swings. Too much shoulder. Feet planted too wide. One boy turned his hips before every cut, announcing the attack to anyone with eyes.

I saw all of it at once.

That was intoxicating.

For five minutes I became insufferable in complete silence.

I knew so much.

The man on the nearest lane was wasting force.

The woman with the shield stepped too deep.

Two fighters in the back watched the weapon instead of the chest.

A boy with a spear recovered his rear hand to exactly the same place every time.

Probably.

Maybe.

Less than this morning.

Still enough.

Again.

Rear foot late.

Again.

Hips get worse.

Think hips.

Grip tightens.

Loosen grip.

"You trying to fight or assemble furniture?"

Faded Silver leathers. One side of her head shaved close, the other tied back.

I did not recognize her.

Good.

Too much.

Heel down. Again.

Better.

Stop reaching. Again.

Better.

There were too many things I could work on.

That was the problem.

If she had a beginner for a month and could give him one attack and one way not to get hit, what would she choose?

That stopped her.

Because I was currently bad at twenty.

Overhead cut.

Side step.

Both.

Fine.

Overhead.

Raise. Cut. Recover.

Again.

Again.

Again.

My shoulder started burning before I learned anything profound.

Good.

Small movement. Enough to leave the line.

Mine looked like I had remembered an appointment somewhere else.

Too far.

Again.

Feet crossed.

Again.

Too slow.

Again.

Better.

Broad through the chest. Maybe twenty. Practice blade in one hand. Expression of someone who had been waiting to see whether I noticed him.

Same line.

Side step.

Hard enough to sting.

Again.

Same line.

Too early.

Jorren.

Distinct enough.

Greg.

Of course.

Again?

Excellent plan.

Again.

Heavy cut.

Correctly.

Opening.

"Dead."

Ambition.

Useful information.

Months.

No reason.

Interesting.
