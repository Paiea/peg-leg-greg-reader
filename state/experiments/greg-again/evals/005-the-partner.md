## What Chapter 5 teaches the written renderer

### 1. THOUGHT CAN BE ACTION WHEN IT CHANGES THE NEXT BET

Greg's internal modeling at the White Dog is not exposition between events.

The useful loop is:

```text
OBSERVE
→ BET / FOLD / WAIT
→ RESULT
→ MODEL UPDATE
```

Thought earns page time because it immediately changes behavior and exposure.

If the same paragraph of thought could be moved after the game without changing anything, it is probably too essay-shaped.

### 2. A STRENGTH CAN CREATE ITS OWN FAILURE MODE

Greg really is good at reading systems and people.

The gambling failure is stronger when it does **not** come from random recklessness.

He reaches enough money to solve the stated problem.

Then an unresolved model becomes more interesting to him than preserving the margin.

New pressure:

> **Greg's strengths should create temptations specific to the strength.**

Intelligence does not merely help or hurt generically. Greg can become overexposed because understanding itself is rewarding.

### 3. PRESENT LIQUIDITY SHOULD NOT BE SOLVED BY FUTURE VALUE

Arlo's first sale creates genuine upside.

It does not magically pay tomorrow's debt.

New story pressure:

> **Keep different kinds of value separate until the story earns conversion between them.**

Potential value, cash, reputation, capability, access, leverage, and ownership are related but not interchangeable.

This preserves material stakes and prevents progression from becoming mushy aggregate `success`.

### 4. PARTNERSHIP REQUIRES OUTSIDE AUTHORITY

Arlo's ownership boundary is not Greg generously giving him independence.

Arlo asserts it.

Greg can negotiate against it, but the boundary originates in Arlo's own claim over his work, tools, process, and customers.

The selected scene works because Arlo can make Greg's preferred future unavailable.

### 5. LANGUAGE AS STATE TRANSITION REPEATS

Chapter 3 established this with Vale's loan.

Chapter 5 repeats it with partnership terms.

Repeated signal:

> **When words create a durable boundary, obligation, share, price, consent, refusal, or deadline, let the commitment land in-scene.**

Greg may mediate connective negotiation, but he should not paraphrase away the instant the relationship changes shape.

### 6. CHAPTER ENDINGS SHOULD NOT BECOME REWARD INVENTORIES

The control's sword-shop material is good PLG material.

It loses power when placed after partnership resolution, gambling tension, and debt solvency because it becomes another progression receipt.

New pacing pressure:

> **After the chapter's main consequence chain resolves, do not keep spending good material merely because it is available.**

Move useful rewards/choices to the next place where they cause something.

## Current repeated written priors after Chapters 2–5

These remain priors, not locked style:

1. Greg owns continuity.
2. Other voices earn direct space for falsification, binding state change, irreplaceable character language, or motor interrupts.
3. Time is strongest as residue that changes present action.
4. Progress should reveal new constraints rather than simply close old ones.
5. Environment and other actors retain authority over capability.
6. Thought earns space when it changes behavior under pressure.
7. Greg's strengths should generate specific failure modes.
8. Different forms of value should remain materially distinct.
9. Stop chapters when the causal chain has landed; do not append reward inventory by habit.
