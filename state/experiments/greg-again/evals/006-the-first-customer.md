## What the story gained

### 1. SUCCESS GETS A REAL-WORLD ENVIRONMENT

The disk genuinely worked in Arlo's workshop and in the dyer's first small runs.

It then failed under changed conditions:

- warmer flow
- greater duration
- higher volume
- different residue load

The product is neither magically vindicated nor randomly disproven.

New repeated pressure:

> **Scale changes the problem.**

A solution must survive the environment where value is actually created.

This applies beyond technology: combat, institutions, money, relationships, magic, and logistics can all behave differently at larger scale or longer duration.

### 2. PARTNERSHIP ACQUIRES SHARED DOWNSIDE

Chapter 5 created ownership boundaries.

Chapter 6 makes those boundaries real by giving Arlo and Greg a customer loss to absorb together.

The partnership is stronger because it now contains:

- shared responsibility
- disagreement risk
- cash cost
- customer trust
- independent expertise

It is no longer a narrative label attached to mutual upside.

### 3. THE CUSTOMER BECOMES AN ACTOR

Ivena is not a test subject.

She has:

- a real process
- lost material/labor
- domain knowledge Arlo and Greg lack
- bargaining power
- willingness to distinguish `worked` from `broke`
- a reason to continue testing only if they address her actual constraints

Her line `It worked. Then it broke. Those are two facts.` is a strong outside-voice breach because it falsifies the temptation to classify the product as either success or failure too early.

### 4. OLD EXPERTISE ARRIVES AS PROCEDURAL INSTINCT

Greg does not recover S-class support power.

He recognizes the **shape** of a containment failure:

```text
ISOLATE
→ REDIRECT
→ BUY TIME
→ KEEP PEOPLE OUT OF THE FAILURE
```

He acts on that structure before he can fully explain it.

New written/story prior:

> **Old expertise can survive as prioritization, sequencing, attention, and instinct before it survives as current capability.**

This gives the second-life premise leverage without bypassing progression.

### 5. SUPPORT MAGIC GETS AN ACTION PROOF WITHOUT A POWER SPIKE

Greg's remembered response would have been a large barrier.

Current Greg produces a crooked, palm-wide shimmer for less than a heartbeat.

It does not solve the incident.

It changes one piece of the outcome.

That is enough.

The moment matters because support is closer to Greg's later identity than swordsmanship.

The failure therefore carries emotional weight that sword failure did not.

New pressure:

> **Capability can be tiny and still dramatically meaningful if used at the point of highest leverage.**

### 6. ACTION DOES NOT REQUIRE COMBAT

The chapter contains physical urgency, roles, hazards, rapid decisions, movement, failure, and consequence without another monster fight.

This supports the broader R2 action target:

> **Action is state change under pressure, not a combat quota.**

### 7. OUTSIDE VOICE AS DOMAIN AUTHORITY

The existing voice-breach grammar expands slightly.

Direct outside language is especially valuable when another actor owns knowledge Greg cannot safely translate first.

Examples in Chapter 6:

- Ivena's process instructions
- Arlo identifying trapped pressure
- fast operational commands
- customer claims that create financial responsibility

This overlaps falsification and motor interrupt but is worth noticing as repeated evidence that Greg's POV should not become epistemic ownership.
