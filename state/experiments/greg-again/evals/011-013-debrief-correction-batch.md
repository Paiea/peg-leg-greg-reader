## Result

The batch survives.

It does not establish new locked style.

It does provide evidence that the Chapter 1–10 engine can widen without abandoning the Greg-dominant identity.

## Chapter 11 — The North Gate

### What changed

Greg chooses paid work and liquidity instead of immediately converting road pay into progression.

A routine city repair becomes a structural emergency.

Greg does not solve it with the four-inch plane. He contributes through:

- clearing people from the load path
- recognizing when domain authority belongs to the masons
- managing secondary cart/horse danger after the suspended load is stabilized
- noticing a leverage change in how a support timber is being placed

### Long-shadow result

The Northern Gate future-memory anchor is now active.

Greg remembers that the gate eventually collapses.

He has no proof that:

- today's repair contributes
- the warm-colored stone matters
- the split hoist wedge matters
- this version of the gate even survives unchanged until the future event

This is the intended long-shadow state.

> **Future gravity without present certainty.**

Do not turn it into an investigation quest immediately.

## Chapter 12 — Mara

### What changed

The story finally applies the memory premise to ordinary human history rather than famous future facts.

Greg can remember:

- wars
- prices
- mature systems
- S-class support

while failing to remember:

- Tavin, who taught young Greg cards
- eight months living in Mara's mother's back room after his parents died
- two kisses with Mara
- recent conversations and promises

This creates a failure Greg cannot optimize away.

### Voice result

Direct dialogue rises substantially and earns it.

Mara's wording is often the event because she owns:

- what young Greg promised
- what he forgot
- how the forgetting affects her
- whether she still wants him in the future she is choosing

Greg-dominant does not require low dialogue when another person's exact language carries relationship state.

### Emotional result

Useful new pressure:

> **History remembers what becomes important. People remember what was important to them.**

Greg's future memory is biased toward historical consequence and his later professional life. That can make ordinary lost intimacy feel more painful, not less.

Mara leaves Carrow in four days at chapter start. By Chapter 13, two days remain.

The deadline should remain causal.

## Chapter 13 — The Good Sword

### Priority result

Greg buys the better-balanced sword.

That closes immediate options:

- no proper support instruction yet
- less business investment
- smaller reserve

This is a real choice rather than a free reward.

### Action result

The tannery hooktail response gives the sword immediate evidence without turning the chapter into equipment worship.

The better tool improves:

- line control
- wrist compensation
- recovery speed after impact

It does not improve:

- positioning judgment
- Greg's temptation to solve the obvious part of the problem
- crowd/environment management
- the fact that several hundred pounds of animal still has momentum after a successful cut

The chapter does not need a concluding essay explaining the distinction. The action already demonstrates it.

## New useful story pressure

The frontier now contains two very different clocks:

1. **Mara leaves in two days.** Immediate and human.
2. **Northern Gate eventually collapses.** Distant and uncertain.

This contrast is useful.

Greg should not respond to the distant historical shadow by neglecting the immediate person again.

That would be repetition unless the story intentionally wants the failure.

## Next move

The strongest immediate next chapter is not another upgrade.

It should force Greg to decide whether he actually shows up for Mara before she leaves, while other opportunities remain available and capable of moving without him.

Possible pressure:

- a paid contract overlaps with Mara's departure window
- Arlo/Ivena need something Greg could help with but can proceed without him
- the better-sword purchase has reduced his cash enough that turning down work has a real cost

The important question is simple:

> **Will Greg choose a person when the system gives him a profitable reason not to?**

Do not make the answer consequence-free.
