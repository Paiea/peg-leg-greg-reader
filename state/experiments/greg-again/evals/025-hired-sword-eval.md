## Route / infrastructure posture

The West Cut is now established in the present as conditionally useful infrastructure rather than merely an old road Greg remembers later.

Current evidence supports:

- the old stone crossing cannot carry loaded animals safely;
- the upstream ford is usable for the party's loaded mule;
- glasshorn movement uses the quarry corridor after cold rain, with the herd leaving the main Cut near the old quarry spur in this observed movement;
- a narrow west shelf is usable one animal at a time with careful handling;
- the west shelf becomes unreliable after hard rain because the outer edge softens;
- tight bends do not support carts in their current geometry;
- actual current travelers are already using the Cut opportunistically for light movement;
- the route reconnects to maintained western road;
- Harl's current verdict is suitable for foot couriers and single light pack animals in dry conditions, with restrictions, not for carts or general freight.

This is compatible with Greg's later memory of the West Cut as mostly foot traffic, goats, and people avoiding tolls, but the chapter does not establish that this exact survey caused that later history.
