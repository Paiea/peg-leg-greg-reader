# Chapter 32: The Longer Route

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Story search: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/032-story-search.md`

---

Mara's second letter arrived while Jorren was arguing with Noll about soap.

This seemed appropriate.

I had come back from the Guild with East Mill pay in one pocket and a folded city receipt in the other.

Jorren had apparently used the time to discover that Noll considered soap a communal philosophy rather than personal property.

"He said yours was available," Jorren said.

Noll looked offended.

"I said Greg usually has some."

"That is not the same thing."

"It was close enough to get soap."

I held up the envelope.

"I am leaving both of you to resolve this through law."

Noll saw the Halden mark.

His face changed just enough to become nosy.

"Mara?"

I looked at him.

Apparently Noll knew Mara.

Of course he did.

Young Greg's life continued refusing to be partitioned for my convenience.

"Yes."

"Tell her I said hello."

"Do you know her well enough for that?"

Noll considered.

"She knows I exist."

High standard.

I went upstairs.

Jorren followed because his bed remained my floor until he found another arrangement.

I stopped at the door.

He stopped behind me.

"What?"

"Letter."

"Yes."

"Private."

He stared.

"You want me to leave the room I sleep in?"

"You sleep on the floor."

"Important legal distinction."

Apparently everyone in Carrow had become attorneys.

I pointed downstairs.

Jorren took the hint with a level of dignity I did not deserve.

"I'll go steal Noll's soap."

"Please establish clear ownership first."

"Ruins the experience."

He left.

I closed the door.

The room was warmer than the first time Mara had written.

Stove repaired downstairs.

Weather slightly better.

Or I had stopped noticing cold until somebody mailed me permission.

The envelope used the same heavier Halden office stock.

Vey mark beneath the stamp.

Different hand at the route notation.

Not Mara's.

Office process.

I opened it.

Two pages again.

Mara remained financially irresponsible with paper.

**Greg,**

**The short letter was better. This is annoying because now I have to admit you can learn.**

Good start.

**Dena sounds more interesting than the filter. Thank you for finally understanding how people work. Please do not tell her I said that because I have never met her and would prefer not to begin a friendship by being quoted by a strange man.**

Reasonable.

**Also I told Mother you are still defending the blue cup. She says both of us need occupations. I pointed out that we have occupations. She says better ones.**

I smiled.

Mara's mother remained structurally sound.

Then the letter shifted.

**Pell still eats onions. This will not improve. I have accepted that some failures are permanent.**

Below that:

**I am not writing from the office.**

I stopped.

The page remained paper.

No emergency mark.

No rushed hand.

Mara continued.

**Vey has me riding the Halden to Bracken packet for twelve days. Maybe eighteen. They lost one route clerk to fever and another decided marriage was preferable to inventory, which is either romantic or a severe statement about the job.**

Bracken.

I knew the town.

Later.

River stop northeast of Halden.

Warehouses built into the low hill.

Famous for slate roofs and one bridge everyone hated.

Or maybe I hated it.

Memory refused to specify.

Current Bracken could be completely different.

Good.

**The work is mostly checking what leaves the barge against what arrives, then arguing about damaged seals with men who become philosophers whenever a number costs them money. I am apparently good at this. Vey's Halden manager says if I can keep three packet runs clean, he may move me into route accounts instead of desk accounts.**

There.

Not promotion exactly.

Possibility.

Mara moving.

I looked at the Halden stamp again even though I already knew where the letter had begun.

Halden had been a destination when she left Carrow.

Now it was becoming a base.

Different thing.

**I did not know route accounts existed until four days ago. This seems important because I spent a year thinking I knew what job I wanted.**

I laughed once.

Quietly.

That sentence had traveled farther than she knew.

**The packet is smaller than the one from Carrow. Two cargo decks. One covered passenger section. The captain claims the lower deck does not leak. This is technically true when it is not raining.**

Vey Shipping.

Future warehouses in six river cities.

Current captain defending a leaking deck through language.

History remained useful mostly as a source of jokes.

**Bracken smells like wet stone and fish smoke. The bridge is terrible. Everybody here agrees except the people responsible for repairing it. Pell says that makes it normal infrastructure.**

I stopped.

Bridge.

Later hated bridge.

Maybe same bridge.

Maybe not.

Lead.

Not answer.

I continued.

**I will probably be back in Halden before your next letter reaches me unless you send it through Vey. Do not do that unless you want Pell to read the address and make comments. He already believes your name appears too often.**

Interesting.

Pell knew my name.

I did not know Pell.

This asymmetry was beginning to feel personal.

**You asked me nothing in your last letter, which I liked more than I expected. So I will answer something you did not ask. I am scared of being bad at this.**

There.

Small line.

No preparation.

I read it again.

**Not the river. The work. I wanted Halden because it felt larger than Carrow and I wanted to find out if I was actually good at the thing I thought I was good at. Now someone has given me more of it before I decided whether I passed the first test.**

I set the page down.

Not because I needed a break.

Because my first response arrived too quickly.

Of course she was good at it.

Future Vey Shipping became enormous.

Mara worked there now.

I knew she mattered.

Did I?

No.

I knew Vey Shipping mattered.

I knew Mara.

Those were two facts trying to produce a third without permission.

Useful warning.

I picked the page up.

**I know I can do numbers. I know I can find bad entries. I do not know if I can do it while the floor moves and six people ask different questions and somebody is shouting about a missing crate. Apparently that is the job.**

Yes.

That sounded like most useful jobs.

**Do not write back that I will be fine. I probably will. That is not the same thing.**

Of course.

Mara continued being annoyingly precise from another city.

The last paragraph was smaller.

**You said the room was cold. Is it still?**

Then:

**And what did you spend the money on? You always spend money when you get nervous.**

I stared.

Rude.

Accurate.

One silver on Lera.

Four copper on feet.

Three copper mailing Tavin.

Sword oil.

Lunch.

Apparently my economic history was emotionally legible.

Mara signed her name.

Below it she added:

**The cup is here. Still blue. Bracken has cups too. Mother remains wrong.**

I put the letter on the table.

Then I looked at the room.

Not cold right now.

Jorren's bedroll occupied the floor beside mine.

My field pack sat by the door.

The old deck remained on the table with the burned seven visible.

Tavin's reply, if there would be one, was somewhere south of here or not written yet.

A city inspector was probably becoming unhappy under East Mill.

Meren might need another second hand someday.

Lera had one silver of my money and enough confidence to hit me with a stick.

Mara was on a packet toward Bracken because one clerk got sick and another got married.

Lives moved through stupid causes.

History cleaned those up later.

Maybe too much.

I found paper.

The first line came easily.

**Mara,**

**The room is warmer. The stove downstairs was repaired while I was gone for five days. Nobody waited for me. Very rude.**

Good.

Next.

**I spent one silver on sword instruction. I know what you are going to say. I would like credit for knowing.**

I paused.

What else?

Jorren.

Underground.

Dry wall.

Magic.

Gold party.

Too much.

Report instincts gathered at the edge of the page.

I ignored them.

**Jorren came back. He is sleeping on my floor because the Guild beds are full. Noll and he are already arguing about soap. This seems healthy.**

There.

A person Mara knew? Maybe.

I did not know whether she knew Jorren.

Fine.

She could ask.

I thought about her fear.

I could tell her she would be good at route accounts.

Maybe true.

Maybe not.

Could tell her fear meant she cared.

Terrible.

Could tell her everybody got scared before a bigger job.

Also terrible.

I wrote:

**You said not to tell you that you will be fine. I will respect that mostly because you threatened the river last time.**

Then stopped.

Small enough.

True enough.

One more line.

**I think finding out whether you are good at something while the floor moves sounds unfair. I have recently been paying people to hit me with sticks, so I may not be qualified to judge.**

That was better.

I considered mentioning Bracken's future bridge.

No.

Mara had not mailed me a historical infrastructure lead.

She had mailed me a bad bridge.

I added:

**If the bridge is as bad as you say, do not fall off it. This is the full extent of my professional advice.**

Signed.

Greg.

Short again.

Maybe letters did not need to justify postage by weight.

I folded it.

Jorren knocked on the door.

Actually knocked.

Progress.

"Done being private?"

"Yes."

He came in carrying half a loaf and a small lump of cheese.

"Noll paid for the soap."

I stared.

"With what?"

"He didn't. But he said he would."

Carrow finance.

Jorren saw the letter.

"Mara?"

"Yes."

"She coming back?"

The question hit differently than I expected.

Not painful.

Just wrong-sized.

"No. She's going farther. Temporarily."

Jorren nodded.

"Good?"

I thought about it.

Mara on a leaking Vey packet.

Route accounts.

Fear.

A bad bridge.

A blue cup traveling farther than either of us had expected.

"Probably," I said.

Jorren handed me bread.

Good enough.

The next morning I mailed my answer to Halden through the ordinary packet line.

One copper.

Not Vey.

Pell did not need additional evidence.

The clerk stamped the fold.

I watched it disappear into the Halden sack.

Mara might be back before it arrived.

Or still on the river.

For once, the uncertainty did not feel like distance.

It felt like motion...
