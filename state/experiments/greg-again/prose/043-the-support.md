# Chapter 43: The Support

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Story search: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/043-story-search.md`

---

Neris Holt charged less than Lera and hurt me in more complicated ways.

The field-support block met in the east yard two mornings after I paid for it.

Not the proper sparring lanes.

Past those.

Near the equipment shed where the ground had been packed flat by carts, boots, dropped shields, and several decades of people learning that magical theory eventually required somebody to move a box.

Neris was already there when I arrived.

Silver marks at the collar.

Brown coat with one sleeve stitched at the elbow.

Hair cut short enough that sleet had nowhere interesting to collect.

She looked perhaps thirty-five.

Maybe forty.

Jorren would have declared her deceased.

Two other students stood nearby.

A narrow man with an archer's shoulder and a Bronze token.

A broad woman wearing commercial ward gloves with no adventurer rank visible.

Neither looked impressed to see me.

Good class.

Neris held three assessment slips.

Mine had Kesra's signature at the bottom.

She read it once while I stood there.

"Irregular experience."

"Apparently."

"Dain's word."

"She has several."

Neris looked at me.

"You know her?"

Dangerous question remained popular.

"Met her once."

True in the current life.

"Then you know she writes short notes when she wants other people to suffer."

I looked at the page.

Kesra had filled most of one side.

"This is short?"

"For her."

Useful historical evidence.

Neris folded the slip and tucked it into her coat.

"All three of you passed intake. None of you are here to prove you passed intake again."

The archer lowered the hand he had been flexing.

Neris noticed.

"Good start."

He stopped flexing harder.

She pointed at the equipment shed.

"Bring out one red crate each."

Magic education.

The crates were empty.

Still heavy enough to make their handles a personal criticism.

We carried them into the yard.

Three painted lanes had been marked with chalk.

Each lane bent around two posts, crossed a low timber rail, and ended beside a waist-high platform.

No targets.

No glowing test frame.

No brass apparatus.

Neris had two yard hands waiting near the start.

One was a woman built like a doorframe.

The other was an older man with a left knee wrapped under his trousers and the relaxed expression of someone being paid to watch strangers make errors.

"They carry," Neris said. "You support."

Simple.

I distrusted it.

The commercial woman asked first.

"What is the objective?"

Neris nodded toward her.

"Good. Crate reaches platform upright. Carrier stays standing. Nothing else matters unless I add it."

Kesra's cup.

Different shape.

I felt the lesson reaching for me.

Ignored it.

Neris pointed at the archer.

"You first."

He paired with the older yard hand.

The hand lifted the red crate against his chest.

Neris stood outside the lane.

"Go."

The carrier walked.

First turn.

Second.

The archer formed a narrow pale shape against the crate's right side.

Not a plane like mine.

Curved slightly.

The carrier reached the low timber rail.

Stepped over.

His wrapped knee wobbled.

The support shape pushed the crate toward center.

Useful.

Then the carrier corrected his own balance the same direction.

Two corrections became one bad correction.

The crate struck his chin.

He swore.

Stayed standing.

Crate stayed upright.

Neris did not stop them.

They finished.

The archer looked unhappy.

"I kept it up."

"Yes."

"He moved into it."

The older man rubbed his chin.

"I did."

Neris looked at the archer.

"Did he know you were pushing right?"

Silence.

There.

Not geometry.

Information.

"Again," Neris said.

The archer reset.

This time before the rail he said, "Right side."

The carrier glanced at him.

"Meaning?"

"I will hold the crate right."

"Hold it from going right or hold it on the right?"

The archer stared.

Neris smiled without warmth.

Excellent instructor.

They tried again after language became less stupid.

"I have your right. Don't correct that side unless I drop."

Long.

Clear.

They crossed the rail.

No chin injury.

Progress.

The commercial woman went next.

Her magic was a short amber brace that linked the crate handle to the carrier's forearm.

Different again.

Not Barrier.

Not Kesra's tension sink exactly.

Related shape maybe.

I did not know.

The point was not me.

She announced it before forming.

"Handle to forearm. Left side only."

The doorframe woman carrying the crate said, "Fine."

They moved.

First turn clean.

Rail clean.

Then Neris kicked a padded block into the lane.

Not hard.

Enough.

Carrier stepped around it.

The amber brace remained attached.

Her arm moved.

Crate moved with it.

Too much.

She had to twist her shoulder to keep both.

"Off," she snapped.

The commercial woman released immediately.

The carrier recovered.

Crate stayed up.

They finished.

Neris pointed at the support student.

"Good release."

Then at the carrier.

"Good call."

No one praised the brace itself.

Interesting.

My turn.

Doorframe woman again.

She handed me the crate.

"No."

I looked at her.

"You carry it."

"You picked it up."

I gave it back.

She smiled.

This felt coordinated against me.

Neris stood at the lane edge.

"Objective?"

"Crate upright. Carrier standing."

"Good."

The woman lifted.

I took position two steps outside and behind her left shoulder.

My old place.

Not from this woman.

From hundreds of other moving people.

Support angle.

See hips.

See load.

See terrain.

Don't block retreat.

Useful habits.

Neris said, "Go."

We moved.

First turn.

No magic.

Second.

The crate shifted left as the carrier stepped around the post.

I formed a five-inch plane at the lower corner.

Small.

Fast.

It caught the crate before the shift became a tilt.

The carrier felt resistance.

Her left foot changed immediately.

She stepped wider.

My plane was where that wider step wanted the crate to move.

The crate kicked back against her ribs.

She stopped.

Not fallen.

Not happy.

I released.

Neris said nothing.

We continued.

At the rail, the woman paused.

Looked at me.

"Where?"

"What?"

"Where are you helping?"

Ah.

I had told her nothing.

Old crews knew.

Edda sometimes knew from position.

Jorren increasingly knew from habit.

Later people had known my support language before a fight began.

This woman knew Greg.

Bronze.

Tall.

Probably irritating.

That was not a support plan.

"Left lower corner," I said. "If it tips left, I push it back toward you."

She frowned.

"Toward me?"

"Small."

"How small?"

Five inches became a terrible unit socially.

"Enough to stop the crate, not move you."

Better.

She nodded.

"Call before."

There.

Not request.

Requirement.

"Before every plane?"

"Before you surprise me."

Fair distinction.

We reset from the beginning.

This time at the second turn I saw the load shift.

"Left."

Plane.

The carrier did not widen her step.

She let the pressure come through the crate.

One correction.

Not two.

Release.

"Gone."

Her body changed the moment I said it.

Tiny.

Weight returned to her own left foot.

We reached the rail.

She stepped over.

Neris kicked the padded block.

Of course.

The carrier changed direction.

I formed too early.

Wrong position.

She moved away from my plane.

No harm.

Wasted mana.

I released.

"Gone."

Then moved myself.

Two steps right.

New sightline.

The crate tilted as she cleared the block.

"Right rear."

Plane.

Contact.

The construct caught one corner.

I moved while holding it.

Bad.

My shoulder turned as if my body wanted the magic connected to my posture.

The plane warped.

Release.

The carrier grunted and corrected the last inch herself.

Still standing.

Crate upright.

We finished.

Neris looked at me.

"Why did you move while holding?"

"Needed a new angle."

"Did you need the old plane while getting there?"

No.

Kesra again.

One plane.

Not attachment.

"No."

"Then why pay for it?"

Mana as money.

Cruel and effective.

We ran again.

This time I released before moving.

Two steps.

Re-form.

Smaller delay than I expected.

Meren's cycling work had helped.

Not because Meren taught this.

Because turning magic off had become an action instead of failure.

At the block:

"Gone."

Move.

"Right."

Plane.

Catch.

Release.

Carrier cleared.

My forehead remained fine.

There.

Neris saw it.

"Again."

No praise.

Professional tradition.

We rotated carriers.

The older man with the wrapped knee took the crate for me next.

Different body.

Different pace.

His left step shortened at the rail.

I nearly formed where the doorframe woman had needed support.

Stopped.

Waited.

He crossed differently.

No plane needed.

Good.

Neris noticed that too.

"Better."

One word.

Apparently Silvers had a union agreement about praise.

Then she made the lane worse.

Two carriers.

Two crates.

Opposite directions through the same middle turn.

I looked at her.

"Both?"

"Your objective is the red crate."

The other was blue.

Clear.

"If blue falls?"

"Someone else failed."

I disliked that answer.

Which was why I needed it.

The carriers moved.

Red approached the center.

Blue approached from the other side.

Both shifted when they angled around one another.

I saw two problems.

Of course.

Red needed a left catch.

Blue was about to clip the post.

My hand twitched toward a second formation.

Kesra's cup hit the table in memory.

Not literally.

Useful enough.

"Red left."

Plane.

The red carrier trusted it.

Blue clipped the post.

The blue carrier swore.

His crate tilted.

The commercial student on the far side formed her amber brace and saved it.

Not my problem.

Not because I did not care.

Because someone else owned it.

Both crates finished upright.

Neris did not ask whether I had seen blue.

She knew.

"What was hard?"

"Not helping."

"Wrong."

I waited.

"You helped."

Right.

Language mattered.

"Not helping the other one."

"Better."

She collected the chalk blocks while we drank water.

The archer asked about larger constructs.

Neris told him to take load management later if his intake supported it.

The commercial woman asked about linked braces.

Neris told her to stop making one brace reliably expensive before learning how to make two.

Then she looked at me.

I asked nothing.

This worried her.

"You?"

"Practice calls. Release before movement. Re-form after."

"And?"

There was always an and.

I looked at the two crates.

"Objective before method."

"And?"

Of course.

"Other people do not know what I know."

Neris's expression changed slightly.

Not because the statement was profound.

Because I had finally reached the obvious thing she had been waiting for.

"They also don't know what you're about to do."

There.

Different problem.

"Field support is shared information," she said. "Magic comes after."

Not doctrine.

Procedure.

I thought about Edda crossing the West Cut.

Harl and Pate.

Jorren in East Mill.

Meren saying off.

Meral telling me hold, don't lift.

Forty years of later crews who had known my angles because we had already built the language.

I had been treating familiarity like a magical property.

It was work history.

Neris handed back my assessment slip.

She had added one line beneath Kesra's notes.

**CALLS LATE. RELEASE / REFORM GOOD. PRIORITY JUDGMENT IMPROVES WHEN OBJECTIVE STATED.**

Not flattering.

Useful.

"Next block?" I asked.

"Not yet."

I looked at her.

"I passed this one."

"Yes."

"Then why not?"

"Because this is not a staircase."

I waited.

"Go use it until you stop needing to remember the words. Then buy something else."

Lera would approve.

Different domain.

Same hostility toward my wallet.

I folded the slip.

The price hurt less now that the morning had happened.

Not cheap.

Worth it.

Outside the yard, I nearly formed a plane against a delivery crate when a porter slipped on wet stone.

He recovered before I did anything.

Good.

I kept walking.

Support did not require me to participate in every object with momentum.

That might take longer to learn than the magic...
