# Chapter 45: The Host

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Story search: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/045-story-search.md`

---

Jorren came back with grain dust in his hair and no interesting injuries.

I checked both.

"You look disappointed," he said.

"Professionally relieved."

"Same face?"

"Probably."

He dropped his pack beside the Lower Rook stair and looked up.

"You moved."

"Upstairs."

"I know how stairs work."

Good start.

He had finished the east-road grain escort that morning.

Five days.

No attacks.

One broken wheel pin.

Two nights in a barn.

One argument between a mill owner and a driver about whether wet grain counted as more grain.

"Who won?" I asked.

"Rain."

Reasonable.

Jorren had been paid.

He had also found a weekly room near East Market after coming back north weeks ago.

Not Guild beds.

Not my floor.

His own narrow room above a cooper who began hammering before sunrise.

Independent progress arrived loudly.

"How much?"

He told me.

Less than mine.

I felt superior for almost one second before remembering I paid three copper more specifically to possess empty floor.

"Come see the expensive air," I said.

Jorren followed me upstairs.

My room had not become impressive since I moved.

It had become used.

Road cloak on one peg.

Field pack in the alcove.

Boots drying on the tray near the window.

Sword rack against the inner wall.

Letters on the shelf.

Practice line worn faintly into the floorboards where my feet kept finding the same recovery step.

Two stools now.

That was new.

I had bought the second because sitting across from an empty stool felt less strange than making every visitor use the bed.

Potential hospitality.

Jorren stood in the center and turned once.

"Large."

"Three copper more a week."

"Very large."

Correct valuation.

He dropped into one stool.

I looked at him.

"I did not invite you to sit."

"You own two."

"Rent."

"Then your landlord invited me."

Dangerous legal theory.

Noll knocked before I could answer.

Not at the door.

On the wall.

Three times.

Then the door opened.

"You cooking?"

He saw Jorren.

"Floor."

Jorren pointed at himself.

"Room now."

Noll nodded as if a classification had been updated.

"Good."

I looked between them.

Apparently they had maintained their own relationship from one night of pump directions and building categories.

"I am not cooking," I said.

Noll held up a covered bowl.

"I am."

Inside was stew.

Better than Dena's.

Not difficult.

"Why?"

"Made too much."

He looked at Jorren.

"Now maybe not."

This was how my first hosted evening began.

Accidentally.

Appropriate.

I supplied bread.

Whole loaf.

The baker had eventually forgiven geometry and sliced the next one correctly.

Jorren supplied cheese from the east road.

Not expensive.

Strong enough that the room noticed when he unwrapped it.

Noll supplied stew and criticism of my bowls.

"You have two."

"I live alone."

"You have two people here."

"Temporary arithmetic."

He ignored me and went downstairs for a third bowl.

Host failure.

I added bowls to a future list.

Not urgent.

Interesting.

We ate around the table because the new room could support this without requiring anyone to sit on the bed or against the wall.

That felt larger than the square footage.

Jorren told us about the grain owner.

Noll knew him.

Not personally.

"Charges full dry weight after rain?"

"Tried."

"His father did that."

History.

Useful and petty.

I told them about Orrin and the road robbery because Jorren saw the fresh oil on the sword rack and asked.

Not dramatically.

"You use it?"

"Yes."

"How many?"

"Three."

His eyebrows rose.

Noll kept eating.

"You kill anybody?" Jorren asked.

"No."

Noll nodded once.

Different reaction.

"Good," he said.

Jorren looked at me.

"You get hit?"

"No."

"Also good."

"I missed the third man."

"Less good."

Balanced friendship.

I told him Orrin had hit one with a removable brake handle.

Jorren stopped eating.

"Removable?"

"Apparently by design."

"Useful cart."

Everyone appreciated equipment eventually.

I stopped there.

Nobody needed the complete tactical reconstruction.

Jorren's grain job involved no fighting, so he told us about the wet-grain argument instead.

This received more discussion.

Noll believed wet weight should be discounted by expected drying loss.

Jorren believed the man paying should determine what he had contracted for.

I believed I had accidentally hosted a trade arbitration.

"You own a business," Jorren said. "Decide."

"I own part of a filter business."

"Water involved."

"Not grain."

"Still wet."

Noll pointed his spoon at me.

"Third opinion."

I thought about Ivena.

"Write the condition into the contract before it rains."

Silence.

Jorren sighed.

"You made it boring."

"Professional service."

Noll looked pleased.

I was learning that this meant danger.

After food, the table remained occupied by three bowls, cheese cloth, crumbs, and no reason for anyone to leave immediately.

That was new too.

In the old room, a finished meal with another person had converted instantly into spatial negotiation.

Here there was still floor.

I saw the card deck on the shelf.

Tavin's deck.

Burned seven of cups inside.

Bad idea.

I took it down.

"Cards?"

Jorren looked at the deck.

"You play?"

"Historically."

Noll looked at me.

I corrected.

"Poorly."

"Better answer."

We cleared the table.

The deck had forty-eight cards if the burned seven counted.

Forty-seven if Tavin had committed insurance fraud through combustion.

Four suits.

Cups.

Knives.

Wheels.

Crowns.

Some cards marked by age enough that identifying them from the back should have been illegal.

Noll shuffled.

Not well.

Confidently.

Jorren cut.

I watched.

The first disagreement happened before the first hand ended.

Of course.

Jorren laid three wheels.

Noll put down a crown pair.

Then looked at me.

"Burn follows?"

I stared.

"Does it?"

Both men stopped.

Excellent host.

"Your deck," Jorren said.

"Inherited rules unclear."

Noll leaned back.

"Lower Rook, burn follows crown."

"North-road camps, burn follows wheel," Jorren said.

"That's wrong."

"We took money with it."

"Still wrong."

I pulled the burned seven from my hand.

"What happens if the seven is actually burned?"

Noll looked at the blackened corner.

Jorren looked too.

"Still seven," Jorren said.

"Depends when it burned," Noll said.

I sat very still.

Tavin's entire legal case had become jurisdictional.

"Explain."

Noll did.

If a card was damaged before the hand, it remained valid if both players accepted the deck.

If damaged during the hand deliberately, some Lower Rook tables voided it.

Jorren objected that north-road rules treated the card as valid until unreadable.

"Even if someone burns it after losing?" I asked.

"Especially then."

Tavin had apparently chosen the correct venue.

"Who burned it?" Noll asked.

"Me. Allegedly."

"After losing?"

"Evidence disputed."

Jorren laughed.

"You owe somebody money."

"Two copper."

"Pay him."

"He now claims six because postage."

Noll looked impressed.

"Good interest."

This room had turned against me.

We played under Lower Rook rules because the table lived in Lower Rook.

Noll established this without vote.

Host authority continued declining.

I lost the first hand.

Jorren lost the second.

Noll won both.

Suspicious.

The third hand produced no useful memory.

No sudden childhood game.

No image of Tavin across another table.

No Mara laughing behind him.

Just current cards in current hands.

That was enough.

I knew Jorren's tell now.

Not from old life.

Current.

When he held a strong crown run, his thumb rubbed the edge of the lowest card once before he played.

He did not know he did it.

I used this information responsibly by taking three copper from him over the next hour.

Noll saw what I was doing before Jorren did.

He said nothing because morality had entered a tournament structure.

When Jorren finally caught me watching his thumb, he stared at his hand.

Then at me.

"You bastard."

"Current evidence."

"Give it back."

"No."

"You complain about Tavin's two copper."

"Different legal matter."

Noll laughed hard enough to cough.

The room held it.

That was the strange part.

Sound.

Three people.

Bowls waiting to be washed.

Cards spread across a table.

Wet coats by the door.

No one pressed against a wall because someone needed to pass.

No one had to leave because there was nowhere else to sit.

Three copper more a week had bought more than drying space.

Not automatically.

Space did nothing until people occupied it.

I had been paying for possibility.

Tonight it became a room.

Jorren left before midnight because he had his own bed near East Market now.

Also new.

Noll carried the empty stew bowl downstairs.

He stopped at the door.

"Need bowls."

"I know."

"Three more."

"Why three?"

"People happen."

Then he left.

Annoyingly strong argument.

I washed the two bowls I owned.

Put the cards back together.

Burned seven included.

Tavin's debt remained unsettled.

Good.

Some relationships deserved durable accounting problems.

The second stool stayed pulled away from the table.

I almost pushed it back.

Didn't.

The room could survive evidence that someone had been here...
