# Chapter 47: The Alarm

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Story search: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/047-story-search.md`

---

The Guild bell woke me wrong.

Not the front bell.

Not traffic.

Not shift change.

Three fast strikes.

Pause.

Three again.

Then the city fire bell answered from farther east.

I was out of bed before memory finished arriving.

Noll's door opened across the landing.

"Where?"

I had already reached the window.

Orange above the roofs.

Wrong direction for Lower Rook.

Right direction for the Guild.

There.

Deepwinter.

Red tile.

Later dark slate.

"Guild," I said.

Noll swore.

I pulled on trousers, boots, coat.

Sword stayed.

For half a breath I looked at it.

Then left it on the rack.

Fire had enough sharp objects.

The field pack stayed too.

I took gloves.

Better equipment choice.

Downstairs, Lower Rook was awake in pieces.

Doors opening.

People leaning from windows.

Someone asking whether the north gate had gone.

No.

Different long shadow.

I reached the street.

The glow brightened between buildings as I ran.

Not sprinting.

Cold air punished lungs quickly.

Current body had learned roads.

Fire remained new.

Halfway there, smoke reached me.

Wood.

Pitch.

Something sharper underneath.

Hot ceramic glaze maybe.

Not enough to diagnose.

I stopped trying.

The Guild roof was burning when I turned the final corner.

Western half.

Upper slope above the record rooms and rear work offices.

Flame moved under the red tiles more than over them.

That was worse.

Roof fire.

Hidden spread.

The courtyard had become organized chaos.

City fire crew at the front cistern.

Bucket line from the street pump.

Guild members carrying benches, chests, boxes, loose racks, whatever someone had told them mattered.

Two city guards holding civilians back.

One man arguing that his contract purse was inside.

Sella standing in the middle of all of it with the blue intake ledger under one arm and a soot mark across her face.

Relief hit hard enough to become anger.

She was alive.

Of course she was alive.

I had no memory saying otherwise.

Memory had trained me badly.

Sella saw me.

Her eyes went to my empty hip.

Then my hands.

"Bucket line. West side."

No hello.

Good.

I went.

Neris Holt stood near the side wall with both sleeves rolled up despite the cold.

Meren Voss beside her.

Different support practitioners.

Same expression.

Busy.

A brass ward channel along the western wall glowed dull orange where it should not glow at all.

Meren had one tool clamped across it.

Neris held two fingers against a ceramic break point farther down.

Not casting at the fire.

Isolating building systems.

Correct.

"Line dead?" Neris asked.

"Front section is," Meren said. "Roof return isn't."

"Feed from where?"

"Don't know yet."

Good answer.

A firefighter in a leather hood shouted from the ladder.

"West gutter!"

Someone passed the call.

Buckets shifted.

I joined between a Bronze man I did not know and a kitchen clerk wearing one shoe.

Water came.

Pass.

Empty back.

Again.

No magic.

No old expertise.

Bucket.

Hands.

Next person.

The rhythm mattered more than individuality.

Full from right.

Turn.

Pass left.

Empty returned low.

Do not look at roof every time.

That part was difficult.

Flame moved behind tile.

One red tile cracked.

Then another.

Water struck the lower roof and steam rolled down hard enough that the line flinched together.

"Hold!" somebody shouted.

We held.

Not magic.

Position.

A ladder team climbed toward the gutter with hooked poles.

They were going to open the roof.

Had to.

Fire underneath could not be drowned through intact tile efficiently.

The first hook went under a row.

Pull.

Three tiles slid.

Flame pushed through the gap as if relieved.

Fire crew hit it with water.

Steam.

Smoke.

One man coughed hard and came down.

Replacement went up before he reached the ground.

Professional history being made without songs.

I passed buckets.

A red tile slid off the roof.

Not toward us.

Close enough to make everybody look.

It shattered in the yard.

There.

Red becoming pieces.

My stomach tightened.

This was the fire.

Not probably.

Not one of several Guild fires history had merged.

This one.

I knew the roof after.

Dark slate.

Different pitch over the west offices.

A narrow iron ridge I had walked under thousands of times without thinking.

The shape began here.

Tonight.

I had known this would happen.

I had not known it would wake me three fast bells into Deepwinter.

Useful future knowledge.

Completely useless current timing.

The kitchen clerk shoved an empty bucket into my chest.

"Move."

Right.

History could wait.

Bucket.

Pass.

The western wall ward flashed.

Every support practitioner looked at once.

Meren swore.

"Roof return!"

Neris pulled her hand away from the ceramic break.

Amber light jumped across the channel and vanished.

Not fire spreading.

Ward load moving somewhere it should not.

Meren struck the clamp lever down.

The tool snapped.

Metal piece flew.

Neris kicked it away.

"Manual break!"

A Guild worker near the corner reached for a long insulated hook.

Too slow.

The channel flared again.

This time the old wall ward answered above us.

A pale plane appeared along the inner edge of the roof.

Not mine.

Installed.

It held for less than a second.

Then dumped load into the gutter bracket.

The bracket tore free.

Iron and burning wood dropped toward the bucket line.

Now.

"LEFT!"

My voice came before the plane.

Good.

People moved because the word had meaning even if they did not know mine.

I formed at shoulder height between the falling bracket and the clerk beside me.

Five inches.

Ridiculous size.

Correct place.

The iron struck the edge.

Not stopped.

Turned.

My plane shattered immediately.

The bracket changed path enough to hit the ground between two people instead of through one.

Water exploded from dropped buckets.

Someone screamed.

Not injured.

Surprised.

I released what was already gone.

"GONE!"

Habit.

Neris looked at me once.

No praise.

"Back on line!"

Correct.

I went.

My forehead tightened.

One plane.

Enough.

Do not prove anything else.

The bucket line reformed around the iron wreckage.

The one-shoe clerk had both shoes gone now.

I did not ask.

Fire did not improve mysteries.

Sella appeared beside us carrying a wooden file box.

"Archive north room is clear. West upper is not."

A clerk behind her said, "Old closeouts still inside."

Sella stopped.

Looked at the roof.

Then the door.

Then the firefighter blocking it.

"Leave them."

The clerk stared.

"That's ten years."

"Then ten years burns."

State transition.

No hero entry.

Good clerk.

The firefighter nodded.

Door stayed blocked.

Later historians would hate her.

Current lungs benefited.

We kept moving water.

The fire spread east once.

Then stopped where Meren and Neris had finally killed the roof ward feed.

That did not mean the ward caused the fire.

It meant burning buildings made installed systems dangerous.

Important distinction.

I repeated it internally because memory wanted conclusions.

Flue.

Lamp.

Ward line.

Dry timber.

Bad luck.

Sabotage.

Some combination.

No evidence yet.

Water did not care.

By the middle of the night, fire crews had stripped half the west roof bare.

Red tiles lay broken across the yard.

Exposed rafters smoked under sleet.

Two interior rooms were black openings.

One upper wall had been cut through deliberately to stop hidden spread.

The Guild looked wounded in a way buildings rarely allowed when occupied.

No face.

Just missing structure.

People kept working around it anyway.

A healer station had formed beneath an awning across the street.

Burns.

Smoke.

Cuts.

One sprained ankle from the ladder.

No bodies covered.

I checked twice.

Bad habit.

Useful tonight.

Sella passed near me again.

I caught her sleeve.

"Everyone?"

She understood immediately.

"Accounted for."

Air returned.

"One clerk went home before alarm. Two stable hands were never in building. Lera is here. Meren. Neris. Yard staff all checked."

She looked at me.

"You?"

"Here."

"Obviously."

Good.

She continued before emotion developed administrative cost.

"Go drink water."

I obeyed.

Across the street, Lera sat on an overturned crate with a wet blanket around her shoulders.

Soot across one cheek.

I had not seen her in the crowd.

"You burn?" I asked.

"No."

"Smoke?"

"Enough."

She coughed once to prove it.

"What were you doing inside?"

"Getting practice weapons out."

I stared.

"Wooden swords."

"Yard ledger was underneath them."

Better.

"You went back for a ledger."

"Sella sent me."

Of course.

Institutional violence.

Lera looked toward the roof.

"You knew?"

The question arrived quietly.

I looked at her.

"What?"

"You had the face before the west side fell."

Faces again.

Dangerous species.

I could lie.

Not useful.

"I remembered there was a Guild fire."

Lera watched me.

We had never discussed the full shape of my memory.

Most people had not.

"When?"

"Deepwinter. I wasn't sure which year until tonight."

Her eyes moved to the roof.

"Cause?"

"No."

Clean.

"Who gets hurt?"

"I don't know."

Also clean.

She accepted both because current fire left little room for speculative interrogation.

"Useful memory."

Not cruel.

Accurate.

"Very."

Lera looked at the water cup in my hand.

"Drink."

Everyone had become instructors.

I drank.

Near dawn, the fire chief called containment.

Not out.

Contained.

Important word.

Crews remained on the roof and inside the lower rooms checking hidden heat.

No one cheered.

People sat.

That was stronger.

Bodies recognizing permission.

The sky lightened behind smoke.

Carrow roofs appeared one by one.

The Guild's west half looked worse in daylight.

Black rafters.

Missing tile.

Water pouring from the front steps.

Charred paper stuck to the courtyard mud.

I picked one piece up.

Half a contract closeout.

No name left.

Only a number and the words **RETURNED WITHOUT LOSS**.

I laughed once.

Not because it was funny.

Maybe because sleep had left.

Sella saw the paper.

"Trash pile."

I put it where she pointed.

Current authority over symbolism.

Good.

A city inspector had already started a damage list.

Roof west.

Archive upper.

Rear stair.

Ward channels.

Gutter iron.

Water damage lower offices.

Temporary closure.

There.

The Guild was closed.

Not gone.

Different thing.

Sella pinned a wet sheet to the outer fence with two nails.

**CARROW GUILD OPERATIONS TEMPORARILY MOVED TO MARKET HALL EAST ANNEX.**

Below it:

**ACTIVE CONTRACTS REMAIN ACTIVE.**

Of course they did.

The world had burned a building, not obligations.

I read the notice.

Then looked up at the roof.

Later it would be dark slate.

I knew that.

For once, future knowledge felt small enough to hold correctly.

Not cause.

Not warning.

Just aftermath.

The red roof was gone from part of the sky.

Everything underneath it was still current...
