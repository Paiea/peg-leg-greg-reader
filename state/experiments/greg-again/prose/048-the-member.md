# Chapter 48: The Member

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL SECOND-RUN PROSE / TRUE-FRONTIER STORY**

Story search: `state/experiments/greg-again/rehearsals/048-story-search.md`

---

Market Hall East Annex had been a vegetable hall yesterday.

This was still evident.

The Guild had added three tables, two contract boards, one lockbox, and Sella.

The cabbage smell remained stronger than all of them.

I arrived after sleeping four hours badly.

Not because anyone had summoned me.

That was important.

The wet notice on the Guild fence had said operations moved here.

I had an active membership token, no open contract, no staff position, and enough curiosity to make staying home unrealistic.

Also my gloves still smelled like smoke.

I had washed them.

The smoke had declined to negotiate.

East Annex occupied the smaller half of Market Hall, separated from the morning produce stalls by canvas screens somebody had hung from the rafters before sunrise.

The screens stopped sight.

They did not stop vendors.

"Two for five!"

Sella looked toward the canvas.

A woman on the other side shouted louder.

"WINTER TURNIPS!"

Sella went back to the ledger.

Institutional resilience met root vegetables.

I stepped around a queue of six adventurers and one very angry cooper.

The first board held active contracts.

The second held temporary notices.

The third was blank except for:

**DO NOT MOVE THIS BOARD.**

Someone had already moved it.

It leaned against a different pillar from the chalk outline on the floor.

Current evidence.

Lera stood on a stool tying a cord between two beams.

Not sitting on it.

Correct use according to Kesra, probably.

She saw me.

"You're alive."

"Sella confirmed that last night."

"She likes records."

"Apparently."

Lera dropped the cord end.

I caught it.

Accidentally useful.

"Hold."

I held.

She climbed down, pulled the line tight, and hung a small wooden sign from it.

**TRAINING YARD CLOSED UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE.**

Below that:

**PAID INSTRUCTION WILL BE RESCHEDULED OR CREDITED.**

I looked at her.

"Where do you hit people now?"

"Still evaluating options."

"City shortage?"

"Of people? No."

Good.

She took the cord from me.

"West yard got water and roof debris. East lane is being used for salvage. We might borrow Cooper Street drill court after midday if the watch captain stops pretending he owns sunlight."

Independent negotiations.

"My next block?"

"You don't have one booked."

Right.

I had paid for exactly one.

Growth through administration.

Lera pointed at the floor beside the temporary desk.

"If you're standing around, move those benches."

"Member labor?"

"Voluntary."

"That word is doing work."

"So are you."

Fair.

I moved benches.

Three against the east wall for waiting clients.

Two beside the payment table.

One under a drip from the hall roof, then immediately somewhere else.

Nobody promoted me.

A Bronze woman I recognized from the North Gate contract board carried a crate of blank forms past me.

"You staff now?" she asked.

"No."

"You look staff."

I was holding a bench.

"Dangerous precedent."

She kept walking.

The Guild began opening at what I assumed was the normal hour because people simply started demanding things.

Contracts did not care about architecture.

A courier brought a sealed completion slip from Rell Road.

A merchant wanted to amend a cart escort because the reopened gate had changed his departure time.

Two Bronze workers arrived to close a warehouse pest job and smelled strongly of vinegar.

A Silver woman argued with Sella about whether smoke damage to her stored winter cloak counted as Guild liability.

Sella said no.

The Silver woman said the cloak had been in an assigned locker.

Sella said the locker agreement excluded fire.

The Silver woman said nobody read locker agreements.

Sella said this was not normally considered a legal advantage.

The vegetable seller shouted about turnips again.

Current Guild.

I stopped moving furniture when there was no furniture left to move.

This created the risk of being given paperwork.

I relocated near the contract board.

Safer.

Mostly.

The board was different from yesterday's.

Not because the work had transformed overnight.

Because half the slips had been rewritten from memory or surviving desk copies.

New chalk marks identified which records were confirmed.

A small black dot beside a contract meant:

**SOURCE COPY SURVIVED.**

A blue line meant:

**CLIENT COPY CONFIRMED.**

A red slash meant:

**RECONSTRUCTION REQUIRED.**

There were more red slashes than anyone liked.

One belonged to a bridge-timber hauling job east of Carrow.

Another to a three-day well inspection.

Another to something labeled only:

**SOUTH QUAY / RATS / CLOSED?**

Question mark included.

Administrative history had texture.

A man in mud-stiff boots reached the front table while I was reading.

Mid-thirties maybe.

Bronze token.

Left sleeve patched twice.

He put a folded paper on the table.

"Closeout."

Sella looked at it.

Then at him.

"Name?"

"Bren Tal."

She checked the surviving current ledger.

Then checked again.

"Contract number?"

Bren gave it.

Sella turned three pages.

Stopped.

"Your issue copy?"

"That's the closeout."

"Issue copy."

"Burned in my room two weeks ago. Candle."

Sella stared at him.

He stared back.

"Unrelated fire," he added.

Important distinction.

Sella checked the blue ledger.

Nothing.

Then the active ledger.

His contract had been issued before the surviving active-book cutoff.

Bad timing.

"Client?"

"Hollen Brick."

Sella reached for another list.

"Their copy?"

"Foreman signed mine."

"That was not the question."

Bren exhaled through his nose.

Not angry yet.

Working on it.

"I finished four days ago. Came yesterday to close. Building was on fire."

"I remember."

"Hard to miss."

"Also my position."

Fair.

Sella looked at the closeout slip again.

It had a client mark.

I did not recognize Hollen Brick's seal.

Neither did the temporary clerk beside her.

That mattered because the seal-reference book had been stored upstairs.

West upper archive.

Gone.

There.

Not symbolic loss.

A man had done work and the burned paper between him and his pay had become a current problem.

Bren rubbed one hand over his mouth.

"It's twelve silver."

That changed the air around the desk.

Not fortune.

Not trivial.

Several days of hauling and winter exposure, probably.

"I'm not asking you to invent it," he said. "I'm asking you to pay the closeout I have."

Sella did not become defensive.

Good.

"And I'm asking for enough verification that the Guild doesn't pay a forged closeout from a seal reference I can no longer check."

Also good.

Bren looked toward the burned-building direction as if the missing book might feel shame.

"So what?"

Sella turned to the temporary clerk.

"Hollen Brick still operating?"

"East kilns."

"Send a runner."

The clerk hesitated.

"We have two."

"Then have one run."

Current prioritization.

Bren looked at the queue behind him.

"How long?"

"If their office answers, today."

"If they don't?"

Sella pulled a blank reconstruction form from the crate.

"Then we rebuild from witnesses, issue roll, and client books."

"How long?"

"Longer."

He swore.

Not at her.

Mostly at the universe.

Then he stepped aside.

No instant solution.

No lost decade becoming painless because good people cared.

A record had burned.

The institution had procedures for what happened next.

Procedures were slower than intact paper.

That seemed fair in the annoying way reality often was.

I moved to the side bench.

Not because Bren needed me.

He did not.

I just wanted to see whether the system worked when inconvenient.

The city fire investigator arrived before Hollen Brick answered.

Woman in a dark wool coat with a white ceramic badge pinned at the throat.

Two assistants behind her carrying waxed document tubes.

She spoke to Sella quietly first.

Then Neris came in from the burned building with soot on yesterday's soot.

Meren followed carrying a charred length of ceramic ward channel wrapped in cloth.

Nobody looked rested.

I recognized the investigator's question only because she asked it loudly enough when the produce hall erupted over turnip pricing.

"Origin zone?"

Neris pointed toward a rough floor plan spread across the temporary payment table.

"West roof void. Between archive flue wall and old return channel."

The investigator marked it.

"Ignition source?"

"Unknown."

Good.

Meren set the charred ceramic piece down.

"This section carried load after fire was established."

"Could it have carried before?"

"Could have."

"Evidence?"

"Not enough yet."

Better.

The investigator wrote.

"Flue condition?"

"Heat damage too severe at upper joint. Lower stack was drawing."

"Meaning?"

Neris answered.

"Meaning the flue worked after the alarm. Doesn't tell us what it did before."

The investigator nodded.

No dramatic discovery.

No suspicious missing rune.

No whispered sabotage.

A roof had burned and competent people were refusing to turn uncertainty into confidence for convenience.

I approved professionally from twenty feet away.

Then realized I was eavesdropping on a fire investigation.

I looked elsewhere.

Growth.

The blank third board now had a second notice.

**SALVAGE ACCESS BY ASSIGNMENT ONLY.**

Below it:

**NO PERSONAL LOCKER RECOVERY WITHOUT CLEARANCE.**

The Silver woman with the winter cloak read that one.

"This feels targeted."

Lera walked past carrying a bundle of chalk.

"It is."

Excellent institution.

By midday, East Annex had become less temporary without becoming permanent.

Somebody found a brazier.

Somebody else found a kettle.

The payment lockbox moved twice and ended chained to a pillar.

A chalk square on the floor became the intake queue.

The vegetable sellers negotiated a quieter interval during contract announcements in exchange for Guild people not blocking the west produce doors.

Nobody signed that agreement.

It still worked.

I bought a turnip pie because exposure had become coercive.

Two copper.

Mediocre.

The seller had won anyway.

Bren Tal remained on the bench.

He had stopped being angry enough to pace and started being hungry enough to stare at my pie.

I looked at him.

Then at the pie.

"No."

"Didn't ask."

"Good."

He smiled despite himself.

A runner came through the east door twenty minutes later.

Young enough that the Guild token at his neck looked borrowed.

He handed Sella a folded note.

She checked the seal.

Then called:

"Bren Tal."

He stood immediately.

Sella held the note beside his closeout.

"Hollen Brick confirms contract, completion, and foreman mark. Twelve silver less the standard filing deduction."

Bren's shoulders dropped.

Not dramatically.

Enough.

"You had to ask?"

"Yes."

"Fine."

He signed the reconstructed closeout line.

Sella paid him.

Then she handed the client note to the temporary clerk.

"New seal sample. Put it in current reference."

The clerk filed it into a fresh binder.

History rebuilding itself one annoying confirmation at a time.

Bren counted the silver twice.

"We're good?"

Sella looked at the form.

"We're documented."

Different standard.

He left.

No reward for administrative suffering.

Just money he had already earned.

I liked that more than I expected.

"Greg."

I looked up.

Sella had caught me observing again.

"Yes?"

"You have business?"

Dangerous question.

"Membership."

"That's a category, not business."

"I moved benches."

"Voluntarily."

"Lera's definition."

Sella rubbed the bridge of her nose.

Smoke fatigue had made her less interested in my legal theories.

Reasonable.

"Your current records survived," she said.

I blinked.

"I didn't ask."

"You were going to."

Probably.

She tapped the blue ledger.

"Bronze status. Support eligibility. Recent active closeouts. All current. Older membership issue file may have been in west upper. We haven't checked the surviving boxes yet."

Young Greg's older paper.

Maybe burned.

I waited for that to hurt.

It did.

Smaller than expected.

"Does it matter?"

Sella looked at my token.

"Operationally? No."

"Then check when you check."

She nodded once.

No hero archive recovery.

No special search because I had memory problems.

Some paper about nineteen-year-old Greg might still exist.

Some might not.

Current Greg remained standing in front of her either way.

That was irritatingly sufficient.

"Anything else?" Sella asked.

I looked around East Annex.

Contract board.

Payment table.

Lera arguing with a watch sergeant near the door about afternoon drill space.

Neris and Meren leaving with the fire investigator.

Two Bronze adventurers comparing rewritten contract numbers.

A client demanding to know why his courier had not left yet.

A runner eating half a turnip pie over a ledger and dropping crumbs where Sella would eventually see them.

The Guild.

Not the building.

Not even the board.

The repeated agreement that these marks, jobs, payments, witnesses, failures, and people still meant something tomorrow.

"No," I said.

Sella looked suspicious.

Fair.

I left before she found work for me.

Outside, I walked past the burned Guild on the way home.

The street remained blocked at one end.

City workers carried wet debris into sorted piles.

Red tile.

Charred timber.

Metal.

Paper too damaged to preserve.

The west roof was open to the winter sky.

No dark slate yet.

That would come later.

Maybe soon.

Maybe not the way I remembered.

Across town, six blocks away, somebody was probably stamping a contract beside a vegetable stall.

The thought made the ruined roof feel less like an ending.

My Bronze token sat warm against my chest under the coat.

The building had burned.

Membership had gone to work anyway...
