# Greg, Again — Chapter 3 Hostile Lens Rehearsal

Status: **EXPERIMENTAL / NON-CANON**

Control: `state/experiments/plg-r2/prose/003-the-borrower.md`

Calibration: `state/experiments/greg-again/evals/003-the-borrower.md`

Purpose: pressure-test the Greg-dominant lens where another person's language is not merely flavor but part of the causal machinery.

This rehearsal tests two boundaries only:

1. Arlo's technical challenge and Greg's accidental future-knowledge leak.
2. Vale's loan negotiation and binding terms.

The goal is not to minimize dialogue. The goal is to find the most interesting degree of mediation while keeping technical and contractual state exact.

---

## BOUNDARY A — ARLO

### LIGHT

Arlo looked at the bucket.

Then me.

"No."

"I haven't asked anything."

"You're carrying rocks into my workshop."

"Shale."

"Wonderful."

"Kestrin."

That changed his face slightly.

Not recognition. Technical interest.

Good.

"Do you work with mana-reactive ceramic mixes?"

"Sometimes."

"Have you tried powdered Kestrin in a porous body?"

"Why would I?"

There it was.

My memory wanted to become authority because I knew the finished world.

I made myself stay with what I actually had.

"Trace mana. Cheap structure. High surface area if powdered. I think heat changes how it binds residue."

Arlo looked at the bucket again.

"Think?"

"Yes."

"Based on?"

"Something I saw a long time ago."

He looked at my face.

"You're nineteen."

"It was a very formative childhood."

He did not laugh.

I liked him more.

### GREG-DOMINANT

Arlo looked at the bucket.

Then me.

"No."

I had not asked anything yet.

Apparently carrying rocks into a stranger's workshop counted as enough of a proposal to reject.

Shale, I corrected.

That did not improve the proposal.

Kestrin did.

Barely.

His eyes dropped to the bucket again. Not recognition. Technical interest.

Good.

I asked whether he worked with mana-reactive ceramic mixes.

Sometimes.

Powdered Kestrin in a porous body?

"Why would I?"

There it was.

That question mattered more than the answer I wanted to give.

My memory knew the finished object. Pale housings. Arwick stamp. Cheap filters bought by the crate decades from now.

It did not know how anybody got there.

Trace mana. Cheap structure. High surface area if powdered. Heat changed the residue binding somehow.

"Think?"

Yes.

Arlo waited.

Based on what?

Something I had seen a long time ago.

His eyes moved to my face.

"You're nineteen."

Right.

Very formative childhood.

He did not laugh.

I liked him more.

The important part was not the joke. The important part was that he did not let confidence stand in for evidence.

Then I asked his name.

Arlo.

Good.

Not proof. But good.

Arlo Arwick?

He went still.

That was a mistake.

I knew it before he asked.

"Who told you my family name?"

No sign on the door. No maker's stamp visible from where I stood.

Excellent work, Greg.

I tried the cooper next door.

Mell did not know it.

Someone else, then.

Arlo did not move on.

"Who?"

The lamp regulator suddenly became fascinating.

I asked if he could test the shale.

His expression made it clear the subject had not changed.

Mine made it clear I had no better answer.

Eventually he took one chip from the bucket and turned it over.

"Six silver."

There. A price changed the problem.

I laughed because I had two copper.

Arlo put the chip back.

"Then no."

Six silver for his time. Materials extra.

Robbery.

"Then go to a cheaper artificer."

I did not know one.

Future knowledge had given me a door.

The door charged six silver.

### EXTREME

Arlo rejected me before I opened my mouth.

Fair.

I was carrying a bucket of rocks into his workshop.

Shale.

Kestrin shale.

That last part got me a fraction more attention, enough to ask about mana-reactive ceramic mixes and porous bodies without being thrown back into the alley.

Then he wanted the reason.

The reason was the problem.

I knew the finished future. I did not know the middle.

Trace mana. Cheap structure. Powdered surface area. Heat did something to residue binding.

Something.

He heard the uncertainty immediately.

Good artificer.

Bad audience for bullshit.

He wanted my basis.

Something I had seen a long time ago.

I was nineteen.

Right.

That part remained inconvenient.

Then I made it worse by asking whether his name was Arlo Arwick.

No sign. No stamp. No reason I should know.

His attention changed completely.

He wanted the source of the family name.

I invented one.

He killed it.

I invented another.

He wanted that one too.

I changed the subject to the shale.

He did not accept the change, but he did eventually price the test.

Six silver for his time, materials extra.

I had two copper.

Future knowledge had given me a door.

The door charged six silver.

### Arlo read

**LIGHT strength:** Arlo has immediate verbal identity, but the exchange becomes ordinary dialogue ping-pong in exactly the place where Greg's epistemic problem is interesting.

**EXTREME strength:** Greg's uncertainty is very continuous.

**EXTREME failure:** Arlo loses too much of his intellectual pressure. `Think?`, `You're nineteen`, `Who?`, and `Then go to a cheaper artificer` are not interchangeable information. They are Arlo applying skeptical force.

**Provisional winner: GREG-DOMINANT.**

Preserve short Arlo breaches when they function as tests Greg cannot perform on himself. Compress the connective questioning around them.

Surviving Arlo grammar:

> **Outside speech deserves space when another mind is actively falsifying Greg.**

## BOUNDARY B — VALE

### LIGHT

"How much?"

"Forty silver."

The man beside Antonius laughed.

Antonius did not.

"Collateral?"

"No."

"Property?"

"No."

"Family?"

"Dead."

"Guarantor?"

"No."

"Rank?"

I put the Bronze token on the table.

The other man laughed again.

Antonius picked up the token.

"Forty silver."

"Yes."

"For what?"

"Training. Food. An artificer test."

...

"Ten."

Antonius looked at me.

"You changed quickly."

"I asked for what I wanted. Now I'm asking for what you might give me."

"Term?"

"Ten days. Twenty percent."

"Eight days. Thirty-five."

"Twenty-five."

"Thirty-five."

### GREG-DOMINANT

Vale wanted the number.

Forty silver.

Rusk laughed.

Vale did not.

That was the first useful thing about him.

Then came the inventory of reasons I was not worth forty silver.

Collateral?

None.

Property?

None.

Family?

Dead.

Guarantor?

No.

Rank?

I put the Bronze token on the table.

Rusk laughed again.

Vale picked up the token and looked at it long enough for forty silver to become funny without him joining in.

He wanted to know what the money was for.

Training. Food. An artificer test.

Of what?

I put a Kestrin chip on his table.

"Rock."

Shale.

"That improves things."

I liked present Antonius too.

Annoying day.

I gave him the actual hypothesis. Cheap material. Possible filtration property. Artificer interested enough to test it.

"That means he is interested in six silver."

Also fair.

Then Vale said no.

That word mattered.

Forty-year reputation did not exist. S-class did not exist. I was a nineteen-year-old Bronze asking a lender for forty silver to test rocks.

Good.

Present proof.

Ten, then.

That got the smallest shift in his mouth.

"You changed quickly."

I asked for what I wanted. Now I was asking for what he might actually give me.

"Term?"

Here the words stopped being conversation and became machinery.

Ten days. Twenty percent.

"Eight days. Thirty-five."

Twenty-five.

"Thirty-five."

Thirty and ten days.

"Thirty-five and eight."

There was the wall.

Old Greg had negotiated with people who wanted borders.

Current Greg needed ten silver.

Different leverage.

If I failed?

I worked the balance.

At what rate?

Bad. Not impossible.

Type of work?

Rusk smiled.

Legal.

"Mostly."

No.

That surprised Vale.

Good.

Legal labor. Collections, delivery, bookkeeping if he trusted me near numbers. No body disposal.

Rusk laughed again.

Vale glanced at him.

"Rusk, stop encouraging him."

Rusk.

Present first.

Vale counted ten silver onto the table and stated the deal cleanly enough that I could not later pretend I had misunderstood it.

"Eight days. Thirty-five percent. Legal labor if you fail. You miss a day, the work rate drops."

Predatory.

"Yes."

Good.

His fingers paused.

"Good?"

Clear terms.

He pushed the money across.

The deal existed now.

### EXTREME

Vale asked how much.

Forty silver.

Rusk laughed.

Vale did not.

Then he stripped the request down.

No collateral. No property. No guarantor. Dead family. Bronze rank.

Forty silver looked worse every time he touched it.

The money was for training, food, and an artificer test involving Kestrin shale.

Vale correctly observed that an artificer charging six silver proved only that the artificer liked six silver.

Then he refused forty.

I dropped to ten.

We negotiated.

I wanted ten days at twenty percent. He wanted eight at thirty-five. We moved around the interest and term until it became obvious he was not moving at all.

Eight days. Thirty-five percent.

If I failed, I worked the balance. Bad rate. Legal-ish labor. I rejected the `ish` and excluded body disposal.

Vale accepted legal labor and added a penalty if I missed a day.

I called the terms predatory.

He agreed.

That helped.

Then the money crossed the table.

The deal existed.

### Vale read

**LIGHT strength:** negotiation is naturally dramatic in direct dialogue, and the exact terms are impossible to miss.

**LIGHT failure:** the sequence becomes familiar bargaining choreography and Greg temporarily stops being the book's distinctive continuity channel.

**EXTREME strength:** the financial state remains technically recoverable.

**EXTREME failure:** negotiation loses pressure because offers and counters become retrospective summary. The listener/reader no longer experiences the leverage changing in real time.

**Provisional winner: GREG-DOMINANT with binding breaches.**

The dialogue can compress until an utterance changes the deal. Offers, hard counters, refusals, boundaries, final terms, and acceptance deserve unusually strong preservation.

Surviving Vale grammar:

> **When language changes obligations, preserve the state transition in the moment rather than summarizing it afterward.**

## HOSTILE TEST RESULT

Both boundaries select **GREG-DOMINANT**, but for different reasons.

Arlo teaches a new distinction:

> **VOICE AS FALSIFICATION**

Another character's words should break through when they apply intellectual pressure Greg cannot honestly generate for himself.

Vale teaches:

> **LANGUAGE AS STATE TRANSITION**

Offers, refusals, counters, promises, consent, deadlines, prices, and final terms may be literal story actions. Preserve their force at the moment they occur.

These pressures are stronger than the simpler Chapter 2 rule `direct speech is expensive`.

Updated working grammar:

```text
GREG MEDIATES CONNECTIVE SPEECH
OUTSIDE VOICE BREAKS THROUGH FOR:
- falsification
- binding state change
- character-specific leverage
- memorable humor / intimacy / threat
- an interruption Greg cannot metabolize without losing value
```

This still does not lock a house style.

The hostile test only establishes that GREG-DOMINANT can survive technical and contractual scenes without making other minds or commitments fuzzy.
