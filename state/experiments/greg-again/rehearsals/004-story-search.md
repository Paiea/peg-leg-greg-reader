# Greg, Again — Chapter 4 Story Search

Status: **SELECTED REHEARSAL / NON-CANON**

Input state:

- Greg has borrowed from Antonius Vale and turned money pressure into a deadline.
- Arlo has begun testing Kestrin shale and has already demonstrated that future outcome knowledge is not present technical competence.
- Greg has deliberately narrowed early sword practice to overhead cut + side step.
- Jorren has become a live current-world benchmark rather than a prop for Greg's remembered expertise.
- Sella is becoming an institutional/competence comparison point.

R1/canon and the existing R2 Chapter 4 are evidence only.

## Story question

> **What should happen next if R2 is optimizing for pacing, action, time with causal weight, and Greg's thought being forced to update by reality?**

The next chapter should not exist primarily to report that Greg trained for a month.

The chapter should make the month **pay off under pressure**.

## Candidate A — EXISTING CHASSIS / CLEANER RENDER

Keep the current `Thirty Days` structure mostly intact:

1. Guild arrival establishes one-month residue.
2. Jorren/Sella relationships show accumulated habits.
3. Arlo/Vale progress is summarized.
4. Greg takes the saltback contract.
5. Saltback cellar tests overhead cut + side step.
6. Arlo note closes the chapter.

### Strengths

- excellent time residue already exists
- supporting characters clearly moved off-camera
- saltback fight provides earned action payoff
- low continuity risk

### Weakness

The chapter spends a large amount of runway proving that time passed before the live story problem begins.

For R2's current pacing target, the **proof can arrive while the story is already moving**.

## Candidate B — RESIDUE UNDER PRESSURE

Open at the Guild with only the minimum changed-state evidence needed to orient us, then put the contract in motion quickly.

Proposed movement:

1. Sella slides Greg the register without asking for his token. One month established through habit.
2. Jorren's new boots / upcoming escort show that his life moved too.
3. Saltback contract enters almost immediately. Greg needs money because Vale still owns part of his week and Arlo's project is still consuming cash.
4. Sella forces the acid kit; Jorren negotiates a real split. Relationship history appears through compressed, earned voice breaches.
5. On the walk/cellar approach, additional month residue arrives only where it changes present interpretation: Greg's callus, worn sword, Jorren's recent experience, the two practiced movements.
6. First saltback invalidates the overhead cut because the ceiling is too low. Theory meets architecture.
7. Jorren's live voice can interrupt Greg's thought when action requires it.
8. Greg relocates the fight to the stairwell, gets the overhead cut payoff, then discovers recovery is the untrained weakness.
9. Jorren kills the second saltback. Greg does not own every victory.
10. Back at the Guild, money arithmetic reveals the contract was less profitable than fantasy promised.
11. Arlo's note proves another system advanced while Greg was elsewhere.
12. Jorren's impending departure makes time directional: the relationship will change whether Greg is ready or not.

### Strengths

- action starts sooner without deleting time
- elapsed time becomes evidence encountered during a live objective
- Greg's narrow practice receives a physical payoff and a new limitation
- Jorren's independent competence matters in the fight
- Sella's safety/process competence causes a material survival benefit
- Vale and Arlo remain causal without requiring recap blocks
- gives the written renderer a strong test of `VOICE AS MOTOR INTERRUPT`

### Risk

If compressed too aggressively, the month may feel like a montage hidden behind action rather than lived time.

Countermeasure: preserve only high-value residue with physical, economic, relational, and off-camera consequences.

## Candidate C — ABANDON SALTBACKS / FOLLOW MONEY

Instead of the monster contract, Greg's next forced action comes directly from debt or gambling:

- gambling run to clear Vale
- a failed wager creates an immediate labor/collection obligation
- Greg must use system knowledge in a social/action problem rather than a monster problem
- Arlo's technical work continues off-camera

### Strengths

- very strong causal continuation from Chapter 3
- money pressure remains immediate rather than being summarized across a month
- could accelerate Vale as a major relationship/system

### Weaknesses

- delays the fantasy/action promise again
- postpones physical proof that overhead cut + side step became real capability
- makes the opening run more economics-heavy after Chapter 3 already devoted substantial space to money/technical leverage
- loses a useful chance for the environment itself to falsify Greg's narrow training model

## Selection

> **SELECT CANDIDATE B — RESIDUE UNDER PRESSURE.**

The saltback story survives, but **not because the existing R2 chapter already contains it**.

It wins because it combines several current R2 priorities in one causal event:

- time has passed and changed bodies/relationships/systems
- Greg finally gets a fantasy/action payoff
- his practiced competence works
- the environment immediately reveals its limits
- another character knows things Greg does not
- money pressure motivates the job
- outside characters move independently
- the fight generates the next training problem instead of merely rewarding the last one

## Story-state target after Chapter 4

Required surviving deltas:

- Greg's overhead cut is now **owned current-life capability**, not remembered theory.
- Side step is also real enough to save him, but neither skill is universal.
- Greg discovers **recovery** as a live next training need because success itself exposes the gap.
- Jorren proves current local expertise can outrank Greg's future memory.
- Jorren has an independent future movement: north-road escort / impending absence.
- Sella's process/safety competence materially helps the contract.
- Greg earns money, but net profit is reduced by real costs.
- Vale remains a time/money pressure without needing a recap scene.
- Arlo's project advances without Greg and pulls him toward the next story problem.

## Written-lens target

Use the current strongest prior:

> **Greg owns continuity. Other voices own moments only they can make happen.**

Earn direct outside voice for:

- binding split / safety leverage
- Jorren's current-monster corrections
- urgent coordination where HEAR → MOVE → UNDERSTAND
- character-specific lines whose exact wording beats Greg's mediation

Do not over-compress the fight into Greg explaining combat after the fact.

## Acceptance

The selected Chapter 4 should feel like the book **started moving sooner** while still making the month feel consequential.

If the reader can delete the month without changing the fight, the time rendering failed.

If the fight could happen exactly the same with Jorren replaced by a nameless helper, supporting-character independence failed.

If Greg's thoughts delay action rather than being interrupted by it, the subjective lens failed.
