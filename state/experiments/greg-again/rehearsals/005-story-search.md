# Greg, Again — Chapter 5 Story Search

Status: **SELECTED REHEARSAL / NON-CANON**

Control/quarry: `state/experiments/plg-r2/prose/005-the-partner.md`

Input state from selected Chapter 4:

- Greg's overhead cut and side step are now real current-life capabilities with clear limits.
- Recovery is the next physical gap exposed by success.
- Jorren leaves north in nine days.
- Vale expects money in two days.
- Arlo has advanced the Kestrin project independently and urgently called Greg in.
- Greg just earned money, but the monster contract's real costs reduced the fantasy of easy profit.
- Greg-dominant written grammar is a prior, not a locked style.

## Story question

> **What should Arlo's independent success force Greg to do now, while the Vale deadline is still live?**

Chapter 5 should not merely inventory the next set of improvements.

It should convert Arlo's progress into a relationship/state change, then force Greg to solve the money consequence created by his own prior choices.

## Candidate A — KEEP THE FULL EXISTING CHASSIS

1. Arlo sells first disk.
2. Greg and Arlo negotiate ownership / partnership.
3. Greg gambles to cover Vale.
4. Greg pays Vale.
5. Greg visits a smith and buys a better sword.

### Strengths

- all material is individually useful
- partnership, gambling weakness, debt clearance, and equipment progression all move
- strong mundane/progression texture

### Weakness

The chapter resolves too many different kinds of movement.

The sword purchase is especially likely to feel like a fourth ending after the partnership, gambling, and debt payoff have already created natural closure points.

## Candidate B — PARTNERSHIP UNDER DEADLINE → GAMBLING CONSEQUENCE

1. Greg arrives at Arlo's workshop directly from Chapter 4.
2. Arlo has sold the first disk while Greg was fighting saltbacks.
3. Greg immediately starts scaling in his head; Arlo pushes back with current evidence.
4. The real conflict emerges: ownership. Greg funded the hypothesis, Arlo created the process and first customer.
5. They negotiate a bounded partnership. The language itself changes story state.
6. The first real customer creates a small production decision, showing partnership means different expertise rather than Greg control.
7. Vale's deadline remains active throughout. The partnership does not solve Greg's immediate cash problem.
8. Greg goes to the White Dog specifically to close the debt gap.
9. Gambling initially works because Greg actually is good at reading people.
10. He reaches `enough` and then nearly loses it because understanding the table becomes more interesting to him than leaving with solved money.
11. Greg leaves with the debt covered but less margin than he had already won.
12. End either on the decision to pay Vale next morning or on a clean `enough` realization, depending on page rhythm.

Move the sword purchase out of this chapter unless it becomes causally necessary later.

### Strengths

- one causal chain: Arlo progress → ownership change → cash still due → gambling solution → Greg's cognitive flaw taxes success
- social/economic action replaces a need for combat without slowing the story
- partnership gives Arlo independent authority
- gambling tests Greg-dominant thought in a domain where his interior modeling is itself the danger
- keeps Vale's deadline live instead of resolving it in a separate tail

### Risk

The chapter could still feel like two episodes if the Vale deadline is not felt during the Arlo scene.

Countermeasure: keep Greg aware that every ownership/production decision has immediate cash implications and that the first sale does not solve tomorrow.

## Candidate C — PARTNER ONLY

1. Arlo first sale.
2. Ownership confrontation.
3. Partnership agreement.
4. First production/customer choice.
5. End with Vale deadline still unresolved.

### Strengths

- very focused
- gives Arlo/partnership room
- clean relationship-state chapter

### Weakness

- risks returning to a discussion-heavy rhythm immediately after the action chapter
- defers a consequence already due
- Chapter 3 created debt pressure and Chapter 4 carried it through time; delaying the actual reckoning again weakens causality

## Selection

> **SELECT CANDIDATE B — PARTNERSHIP UNDER DEADLINE → GAMBLING CONSEQUENCE.**

The first product sale and the debt deadline belong in the same chapter because they create a useful contradiction:

> Greg has just gained a potentially valuable asset and is still short on tomorrow's cash.

That is more interesting than letting `future upside` solve `present liquidity` by narrative convenience.

The chapter then tests another central Greg problem:

> **He can be correct about a system and still stay too long because understanding it becomes rewarding in itself.**

This extends the recurring pattern:

```text
OBSERVE
→ MODEL
→ MODEL WORKS
→ SUCCESS INCREASES CONFIDENCE
→ CURIOSITY / EGO EXTENDS EXPOSURE
→ REALITY TAKES A TAX
→ UPDATE
```

## Material moved out

### Better sword purchase

The sword-shop material remains useful quarry.

It should not remain in Chapter 5 merely because it already exists.

Move it to a later scene where:

- equipment quality becomes a live constraint
- Greg must choose between tool quality and another scarce resource
- the new weapon changes training/action rather than functioning as an after-chapter reward purchase

## Written-renderer pressure

### ARLO

His direct language should earn space when:

- he falsifies Greg's scaling assumptions
- he defines ownership boundaries
- his wording creates the partnership state
- he demonstrates a different technical/economic model from Greg's

Do not let Greg narrate Arlo into becoming `the artisan who agrees with me`.

### GAMBLING

The table is a strong Greg-continuity environment because his thought is the action engine.

But avoid retrospective essay narration.

Use:

```text
OBSERVATION
→ BET / FOLD / WAIT
→ RESULT
→ MODEL UPDATE
```

The dangerous moment is when Greg has enough money and remains because the unresolved model becomes more salient than the solved objective.

That should feel like a live attentional failure, not a moral lesson delivered afterward.

## Required state-out

- Arlo owns his workshop/process boundaries.
- Greg and Arlo have a real bounded partnership rather than vague shared interest.
- first sale / customer demand proves a market signal without proving a future industry.
- Vale's immediate debt can be paid, but the solution reveals Greg's gambling/model-completion vulnerability.
- Greg has less financial margin than he briefly possessed because he stayed too long.
- better sword remains unresolved quarry, not automatic reward.
- Jorren's departure clock remains live in background state.

## Acceptance

The chapter wins if the reader experiences both halves as one consequence chain rather than `business scene + gambling scene`.

If Arlo's partnership feels like Greg granting ownership, outside agency failed.

If gambling looks like random self-sabotage rather than the dark side of Greg's actual strength, thought causality failed.

If the chapter needs the sword purchase to feel complete, the causal ending has not been found yet.
