## Live state

- Greg has enough cash to pay Vale on time, but almost no margin afterward.
- Greg and Arlo are bounded partners.
- Arlo sold one Kestrin filtration disk to a dyer.
- The product has only small-batch workshop evidence. Durability and real-use scaling remain uncertain.
- The dyer may order more if the first disk survives.
- Greg's future memory knows that later Kestrin filtration becomes useful, but not the exact development path.
- Greg's mana remains weak and expensive to improve.
- Greg's support identity has not yet received a strong current-life action payoff.
- Jorren leaves north soon.
- recovery remains Greg's next exposed sword gap.
- Greg's gambling model-completion hazard is now known.

## Search objective

The next chapter should preferably:

- follow directly from a live consequence rather than introduce unrelated opportunity
- keep pacing forward after the Chapter 5 negotiation/gambling chapter
- restore fantasy/magic pressure without requiring another sword fight immediately
- make partnership mean shared consequence, not just shared upside
- let current reality falsify future knowledge again
- give Greg's old support instincts something to do while his current mana remains inadequate

## Candidate A — VALE REPAYMENT → PAID COLLECTION / SYSTEM JOB

Greg pays Vale on time.

Vale becomes more interested rather than less. A nineteen-year-old Bronze with no collateral paid an ugly loan exactly when promised.

Vale offers Greg paid work: bookkeeping, delivery, collections, or risk assessment.

The work can become social/action pressure if another debtor refuses or a collection goes wrong.

### Strengths

- direct consequence of the debt arc
- deepens Vale as an independent system/relationship
- monetizes Greg's actual intelligence rather than future memory
- can create grounded danger and leverage

### Weaknesses

- keeps the opening heavily money-centered after Chapters 3 and 5 already spent substantial energy there
- risks turning the next story beat into another negotiation/urban-job chapter
- does little for the underused magic/support promise unless the job becomes artificially complicated

## Candidate B — FIRST CUSTOMER CONSEQUENCE → MAGIC/INDUSTRIAL INCIDENT

Greg pays Vale quickly, leaving himself cash-poor.

Before he can decide what to do next, Arlo's first customer returns or sends for them because the disk did **not** behave at workshop scale the way it behaved in Arlo's tests.

Important: the product does not fail randomly.

Workshop evidence was incomplete.

Arlo tested:

- small batches
- controlled flow
- limited duration
- known wash composition

The dyer uses:

- larger volume
- warmer wash
- continuous or repeated flow
- a different residue load

A plausible failure chain:

```text
MORE FLOW / HEAT
→ DISK LOADS FASTER
→ FLOW RESTRICTS
→ PRESSURE / BYPASS / CRACK
→ RESIDUE CONCENTRATES WHERE IT SHOULD NOT
→ MANA-REACTIVE DYE WASTE BECOMES AN ACTIVE HAZARD
```

The exact magical behavior should remain bounded and observable rather than introducing a giant new magic rule by convenience.

Possible incident:

- cracked disk releases concentrated reactive residue near hot equipment
- mana-laced wash begins heating / pulsing / arcing through wet metal or ceramic
- the danger is local but immediate: ruined batch, burns, equipment damage, worker risk

Greg's later-life support instincts recognize the *shape* of the problem before he possesses the current mana to solve it the old way.

He reaches for a barrier or containment response.

Almost nothing answers.

He must combine:

- tiny usable mana
- physical positioning
- mundane containment
- Arlo's technical knowledge
- the dyer's knowledge of the actual process

This becomes **support action**, not sword action.

### Strengths

- direct consequence of Chapter 5's first sale
- first real cost of partnership
- tests future knowledge against production reality
- brings magic/fantasy back through a mundane industrial system
- gives Greg a current-life support problem without gifting him old-life power
- lets another domain expert (the dyer) matter
- creates action without immediately repeating monster combat
- can produce a next technical/business constraint from success

### Risks

- easy to overinflate into a magical explosion merely for spectacle
- must not make the product obviously irresponsible after one tiny test unless Arlo/dyer behavior remains plausible
- must avoid turning the dyer into a customer-shaped NPC whose only job is to fail

Countermeasure: make the disk genuinely useful before it fails under a condition the workshop did not reproduce. The dyer should have her own priorities, process knowledge, losses, and bargaining power.

## Candidate C — JORREN DEPARTURE CLOCK → ONE LAST JOB / ROAD DECISION

Jorren's caravan departure accelerates or he asks Greg to take one last contract before he leaves.

This can test recovery, teamwork, and the emotional fact that time removes people from Greg's immediate life.

Alternatively, Greg could be offered a place on the north-road escort and have to choose between:

- movement/adventure
- Arlo partnership/customer obligations
- current mana/training plans
- staying in Carrow

### Strengths

- strong time pressure
- relationship movement
- traditional fantasy/action chassis
- makes Jorren's independent trajectory unavoidable

### Weaknesses

- risks making Jorren's life important only because it creates Greg's next adventure
- could pull Greg away from the newly earned Arlo consequence too quickly
- another sword/monster job immediately after Chapter 4 may flatten action variety

## Selection

> **SELECT CANDIDATE B — FIRST CUSTOMER CONSEQUENCE → MAGIC/INDUSTRIAL INCIDENT.**

Why it wins now:

1. Chapter 5 created the sale. Chapter 6 makes the sale consequential.
2. Partnership gains downside and responsibility, not only upside.
3. It reintroduces fantasy through practical magic rather than a disconnected spectacle.
4. Greg's old support instincts become relevant without granting old support capability.
5. Arlo remains technically necessary.
6. The dyer can become a real independent actor with money, damaged work, and process knowledge.
7. The incident can reveal a new product constraint and a new Greg constraint at the same time.
8. Jorren's departure remains a live clock for later instead of being consumed immediately.

## Scene architecture

### Beat 1 — PAY VALE, FAST

Do not make repayment another negotiation chapter.

Greg pays on time.

Vale notices.

One compact outside line may establish that Greg has become more interesting to him, but do not let Vale steal the chapter yet.

State change:

- debt cleared
- cash margin nearly gone
- Vale relationship remains open

### Beat 2 — ARLO / DYER INTERRUPTION

Greg reaches Arlo or is intercepted by a message/customer.

The dyer has a real problem.

She is not merely angry that the disk failed.

She has:

- a batch at risk
- labor/time already spent
- equipment/process constraints
- a reason she tried the disk in the first place
- bargaining power because she is the first real customer and source of real-use evidence

### Beat 3 — GO TO THE REAL PROCESS

The dyehouse should feel materially different from Arlo's workshop.

Scale, heat, wet surfaces, vats, pipes, stains, worker movement, smell, and mana-bearing compounds make the same disk a different problem.

TIME / SCALE / ENVIRONMENT become causal again.

### Beat 4 — FAILURE BECOMES ACTIVE

The disk worked first.

That matters.

Then load accumulated, flow restricted, and something cracked or bypassed.

Concentrated residue becomes the immediate hazard.

The incident should remain local and physically legible.

### Beat 5 — SUPPORT INSTINCT, CURRENT BODY

Greg recognizes a containment problem.

His old instinct is something like:

```text
ISOLATE
→ REDIRECT
→ BUY TIME
→ KEEP PEOPLE OUT OF THE FAILURE
```

He reaches for the kind of barrier response he remembers.

Current mana gives him almost nothing.

That failure should hurt emotionally because support magic is closer to his later identity than swordsmanship was.

He still acts.

The solution uses multiple people:

- dyer controls/knows the process
- Arlo understands the ceramic / failure point
- Greg reads the developing hazard and coordinates/positions
- Greg's tiny mana contribution matters at one narrow point without solving everything

### Beat 6 — CONSEQUENCE

After the danger:

- customer loss is real
- partnership bears responsibility
- disk is not disproven, but current formulation/use condition is limited
- Arlo and Greg have a concrete next test derived from failure
- Greg has evidence that his support instincts survived more strongly than his support capacity
- mana training becomes more emotionally/causally urgent

## Written-renderer pressure

This chapter tests a new thought/action rule:

> **OLD EXPERTISE MAY ARRIVE AS PROCEDURAL INSTINCT BEFORE EXPLANATION.**

Greg does not need to narrate a lecture about support doctrine before moving someone away from a hazard, isolating a line, redirecting flow, or spending his tiny mana at the point of highest leverage.

Allow:

```text
SEE SHAPE OF FAILURE
→ ISSUE / HEAR SHORT ACTION
→ MOVE
→ ATTEMPT OLD RESPONSE
→ CURRENT BODY FAILS
→ ADAPT
→ UNDERSTAND MORE AFTERWARD
```

Outside voices should seize space when Arlo or the dyer knows something Greg needs faster than he can infer it.
