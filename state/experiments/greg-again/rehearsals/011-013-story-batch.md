# Greg, Again — Chapters 11–13 Story Batch

Status: **FORWARD STORY REHEARSAL / NON-CANON**

Source authority: `state/experiments/greg-again/written/CURRENT.md`

New guardrails: `state/experiments/greg-again/written/DEBRIEF_GUARDRAILS.md`

## Batch objective

Keep the second-run story moving while correcting the strongest risks visible after Chapter 10:

- Greg cadence becoming manufactured
- lessons being over-explained after scenes already prove them
- support progression becoming curriculum-like
- emotional palette staying too narrow
- local chapter engine becoming stronger than the long story pull

The batch should maintain high action density, but action may be physical, institutional, or relational.

## Story state entering Chapter 11

- Greg has road pay and no Vale debt.
- He can intentionally shape and angle a tiny support plane for several breaths, but this should cool off rather than immediately becoming the next lesson track.
- Better sword remains available quarry.
- Arlo and Ivena have a safer filter design and a real four-unit order.
- Jorren has gone north.
- Meral is gone.
- Sella has become a familiar institutional relationship.
- Greg now has enough money for one or two meaningful choices, not all of them.
- Chapter 1 established several imperfect future-memory anchors, including the Northern Gate collapse, East Verrel, Red Winter, Soren, and Mara's letter.

## Long-shadow candidates

### A. Northern Gate

Greg takes a short paid city/Guild traffic-control job rather than immediately spending road pay on lessons or equipment.

A routine repair above Carrow's north gate becomes a live structural emergency when a hoist or load path fails.

This is attractive because:

- it is high-action without another fight
- it makes Greg use procedural judgment without foregrounding support spell progression
- the location already exists in his present life
- Chapter 1 established that Greg remembers a later Northern Gate collapse
- a present repair can be suspicious without being confirmed as the future cause

Do **not** make Greg solve the future collapse.

The long-shadow value is that a mundane present repair acquires future gravity while remaining uncertain.

### B. Mara

Greg has ignored a letter from Mara since Chapter 1.

Mara is useful because she pressures a different weakness: Greg remembers forty future years better than parts of his own youth.

A confrontation with someone important to nineteen-year-old Greg can create action through relationship state change even if nobody swings a weapon.

Do not define Mara only as romantic reward or exposition source.

Useful pressure:

> **She remembers a version of Greg that Greg himself can no longer fully access.**

Mara may also be preparing to leave Carrow for work or opportunity elsewhere, making the relationship subject to time rather than available indefinitely.

### C. Bigger institutional future signal

Introduce a famous later event or institution directly.

Reject for now.

The story does not need a banner saying THE PLOT HAS ARRIVED. Northern Gate and Mara can create longer shadows from material already seeded in Chapter 1.

## Selected Chapter 11 — THE NORTH GATE

Greg chooses short paid work and liquidity over immediately purchasing a better sword or formal support instruction.

The Guild needs extra bodies to keep people and carts moving while masons replace stone above the north gate after recent rain exposed a damaged seam.

During the job:

- a hoisted repair stone shifts or a line fails
- traffic and workers become the real hazard
- Greg uses current physical/procedural judgment rather than another neat support spell lesson
- somebody else owns the masonry domain and must be listened to
- Greg recognizes the gate as a future-memory anchor only after the present danger is handled
- the current repair remains ambiguous: it may matter later or may be one of dozens of irrelevant repairs

Chapter outcome:

Greg earns money and leaves with a problem that cannot be profitably solved today:

> **He knows the Northern Gate eventually collapses. He does not know whether anything he saw today is part of why.**

This is the first explicit long shadow.

## Selected Chapter 12 — MARA

Mara appears after Greg closes the gate contract or returns to his room/Guild.

She has been trying to reach him.

Do not use a giant exposition conversation.

The pressure is personal:

- Greg knows her handwriting immediately but has gaps around recent shared history
- Mara knows young Greg's habits, promises, temper, and ordinary past better than old Greg currently does
- she notices he is different
- Greg cannot optimize around the fact that forgetting details about someone can hurt them
- direct dialogue should increase here because Mara's exact language is the event more often than in technical scenes

Mara is preparing to leave Carrow for a real opportunity. Exact destination may touch East Verrel if it earns itself, but do not force a second giant plot sign.

Chapter outcome:

The relationship cannot return to its pre-reset state simply because Greg wants more time.

He must decide whether to tell her some version of the truth, lie, or admit memory failure without explaining why.

The strongest candidate is partial honesty:

> **I remember you. Not in the order you deserve.**

Do not turn the line into a speech.

## Selected Chapter 13 — THE GOOD SWORD

After two chapters where future knowledge and emotional memory create problems Greg cannot solve, give him a practical priority choice with immediate consequence.

Greg spends a meaningful portion of his road pay on the balanced sword previously preserved as quarry.

This is not a reward chapter.

The purchase closes other options:

- no proper support instruction yet
- less business investment
- smaller cash reserve

Then force the tool into real use immediately through a short urban contract or emergency where the better sword clearly improves one thing but does not repair Greg's recovery, judgment, or positioning.

Preferred action frame:

A Guild night call to clear a dangerous animal/creature from a crowded warehouse or stable district where fire, people, confined angles, and bad footing matter more than duel quality.

The point is not `better sword = victory`.

The point is:

> **A better tool removes one excuse. The remaining failures belong more clearly to Greg.**

Support magic may appear only if naturally useful. Do not make this another plane lesson.

## Batch pacing target

- Chapter 11: physical/institutional action + long shadow
- Chapter 12: relational action + emotional pressure
- Chapter 13: physical action + priority consequence

Time may move several days across the batch if repetition does not earn scenes.

## Renderer pressures

Across all three chapters:

- trim habitual `Good / Right / Of course / There / Progress` unless the word genuinely turns thought
- when the scene demonstrates the lesson, do not add a second explanation paragraph
- allow direct dialogue to rise substantially in Chapter 12
- do not convert Mara into Greg-summary
- no support-training curriculum
- no instant confirmation that the Northern Gate repair causes the future collapse
- stop each chapter after its dominant causal movement lands

## Success test

After Chapter 13, the reader should want the next chapter for at least three different reasons:

1. Greg's immediate practical life remains active.
2. Mara has made his lost youth emotionally consequential.
3. The Northern Gate has become a future shadow without becoming an assigned quest.

If all three collapse into one convenient plot, the batch failed.
