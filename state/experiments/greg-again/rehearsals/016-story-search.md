# Chapter 16 Story Search

Status: **SELECTED / DERIVED ONLY**

Frontier: after `015-the-morning-barge.md`

## Live question

What consequence arrives because Greg spent money and attention on the sword, North Gate work, and Mara while Arlo and Ivena continued moving without him?

The next chapter should raise energy through an immediate state change, but it does not need physical combat.

## Candidate A — Ivena earns ownership

Arlo and Ivena have completed the four-unit order and accumulated real operating evidence.

Ivena is no longer honestly describable as only the first customer. Her wash knowledge, safety changes, testing site, and customer referrals materially shaped the current product.

A larger follow-on opportunity requires more material / kiln capacity than the existing arrangement can fund comfortably.

Greg can preserve more of his original future-value position only by contributing cash he no longer has or borrowing again.

**Pressure:** future-value greed vs present evidence about who created the value.

**Why now:** Chapter 10 explicitly noticed that Ivena was part of the design whether the paperwork acknowledged her or not. Greg then spent several chapters elsewhere. The world should cash that observation.

## Candidate B — Vale upgrades Greg from debtor to useful operator

Vale offers Greg better-paid work after clean repayment and visible competence.

**Strength:** action / money / leverage.

**Weakness:** risks centering Vale too quickly and does less with the off-camera Arlo/Ivena motion already earned.

## Candidate C — river / Vey work

Greg takes urgent river work and encounters the small present-day Vey operation behind the future company he remembers.

**Strength:** directly revisits Chapter 1's river-shipping promise and raises physical movement.

**Weakness:** Chapter 15 already seeded Vey strongly. Immediate pursuit risks making Mara's departure and the promise ledger feel mechanically connected.

## Selection

**A — IVENA EARNS OWNERSHIP**

Use cheap local story judgment. No high-heat PERFORMANCE required because the behavioral uncertainty is low: Ivena has earned leverage, Arlo already respects evidence, and Greg's interesting uncertainty is whether he protects a remembered-future asset at the expense of present partners.

## Required state movement

- the first four-unit order has produced enough real operating evidence to attract more demand
- Ivena has independent leverage and demands formal ownership / economic participation
- Greg cannot defend his full prior share with cash without re-entering debt or abandoning other needs
- Greg's future knowledge makes dilution genuinely painful
- he accepts a smaller share of a more independently alive venture rather than borrowing merely to preserve centrality
- the resulting business has clearer three-person ownership and can continue without Greg

## Renderer pressure

Direct speech is expensive but justified for binding terms and Ivena's claim to value.

Do not turn the chapter into a business lecture. Keep hands occupied, units moving, measurements present, and decisions irreversible.

Do not append a new contract / monster / reward after the ownership state lands.
