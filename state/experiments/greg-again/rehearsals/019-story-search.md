## Candidate A — Tannery conversion reveals the service problem

Allow several ordinary days to pass in residue.

Arlo and Ivena do not wait for Greg. They complete enough of the current production run to demonstrate a unit at the tannery. The device works well enough that the customer problem moves downstream: the tannery does not want to own a clever box that its workers must learn to clean, reload, diagnose, and source replacement media for.

The tannery's binding question is not whether filtration works. It is who owns the ugly recurring labor after purchase.

This creates a materially different scale problem:

> a product that works once is not yet a system that keeps working.

Ivena has the strongest commercial response because she already owns customer access and process reality. Arlo owns the physical unit. Greg can contribute one remembered-future lead around removable media / standardized service, but current fabrication tolerances make perfect interchangeability much harder than his mature-world memory suggests.

Possible state change:

- one tannery unit becomes a bounded service trial rather than a simple sale
- the venture must now account for replacement media, cleaning, route time, labor, and maintenance responsibility
- recurring revenue may exist, but so does recurring obligation
- Greg's smaller share becomes more concrete because Ivena can create value without him and because future value is not the same thing as immediate cash

Strength:

- live causal pressure already exists
- action is commercial / operational rather than another physical hazard
- lets a few days pass without montage
- uses supporting-character agency instead of dragging Greg back to the center
- tests `scale changes the problem` and `future as lead / present as proof`

Risk:

- could become startup porn if the prose admires the business model instead of showing workers, maintenance, cost, and obligation
- must not simply repeat Chapter 16's ownership negotiation

**Keep the customer language concrete and let maintenance, not valuation, own the chapter.**
