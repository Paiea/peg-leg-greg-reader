# Greg, Again — Chapter 21 Mara Reply PERFORMANCE

Status: **DERIVED RELATIONSHIP REHEARSAL / NON-CANON**

Source state: selected written frontier after Chapter 20

Purpose: preserve Mara's agency and exact correspondence language when her first Halden reply changes the letter relationship.

## State in

- Mara left Carrow for a ledger position with Vey Shipping in Halden.
- she did not ask Greg to follow and explicitly refused to organize her future around a version of him he cannot honestly guarantee.
- she chose a smaller channel: packet letters, with `write something true` as the useful standard.
- Greg sent one letter roughly a packet interval later. It contained the lower-quay rope mistake, Arlo/Ivena production news, Tavin's cards, and ordinary current facts.
- Greg's letter was honest, but it still leaned toward reporting what happened and proving that he was trying correctly.
- several more days have now passed while Greg worked, trained lightly, and became useful at current-scale support labor.

## Character pressure

### Mara

**WANT** — build her own Halden life, keep contact with Greg only in a form that does not make her responsible for restoring him, and answer when she actually wants to answer.

**INDEPENDENT MOTION** — started the Vey ledger job, learned current Halden routines, acquired coworkers / work annoyances / ordinary daily evidence, and lived through Greg's absence without becoming narratively paused.

**LEVERAGE** — she can distinguish Greg telling the truth from Greg turning truth into a report designed to demonstrate acceptable behavior.

**LIMIT** — she still cares. That makes over-reassurance dangerous because Greg may read it as a recovered relationship state she has not chosen.

**LETTER AGENCY** — she decides what parts of Greg's letter deserve response, what parts of her own life he gets to see, and what kind of next letter she would actually want to receive.

### Greg

**WANT** — know whether the route remained real after Mara left, avoid failing the smaller promise, and understand what truthful contact looks like when he cannot repair missing history.

**PRESSURE** — a reply is emotionally valuable enough that he will be tempted to over-interpret every line.

**LIMIT** — he turns uncertain human situations into models, reports, and rules. Even honest self-explanation can become a way to control how another person interprets him.

## High-value uncertainty

> What does Mara write when Greg did what she asked, but doing it correctly does not entitle him to reassurance?

## Rejected versions

### A. Romantic reward

Mara says she misses him, praises his honesty, and implies they are rebuilding toward a relationship.

Reject. Too transactional. Greg's first true letter does not purchase intimacy.

### B. Cold procedural reply

Mara acknowledges receipt and reports Halden facts with no emotional risk.

Reject. It protects Mara so completely that choosing to continue correspondence has no cost or specificity.

### C. Ordinary reciprocal truth

Mara answers the actual letter. She jokes about Greg nearly solving a river problem incorrectly, mentions Tavin's burned card, and gives Greg several unimportant Halden facts because they are hers: the Vey office tilts, a coworker eats onions at the ledger desk, her blue cup survived, the work is harder / duller in ways she wanted anyway.

She then names the problem with Greg's letter without turning it into therapy language: he wrote as if somebody might grade him for being honest.

She does not ask for confession. She asks for less performance.

A useful line posture:

> Next time tell me something that would not make the report if you were trying to prove you were doing well.

The exact final wording may change in prose, but the action must remain Mara's.

**Selected.**

## PERFORMANCE beats

1. Elapsed time arrives first as the physical Halden envelope / stamp in Greg's room, not as a chapter-opening date summary.
2. Greg checks the handwriting before the sender mark because Mara's hand is already embodied evidence.
3. He delays opening for one practical action, not because he wants suspense but because his body finds a tiny avoidant task.
4. Mara's letter begins with an ordinary answer to Greg's closing question about Halden's smell.
5. She reports her current life through small facts rather than an emotional status report.
6. One mundane Vey detail complicates Greg's future-company image but does not settle it.
7. Mara responds to Greg's rope story and Tavin cards in her own language.
8. She acknowledges that he did write something true.
9. She falsifies the hidden performance layer: he still wrote like a man submitting evidence that he had behaved correctly.
10. She asks for one smaller kind of truth next time. No visit request. No romance promise. No forgiveness declaration.
11. Greg initially tries to model what the request means, then notices that modeling is recreating the same problem.
12. He answers with one ordinary current fact that he chooses because he wants Mara to know it, not because it proves growth.
13. He sends the reply while the packet route is available. Do not end on a stated relationship lesson.

## Halden / Vey boundary

Mara's established job is a ledger position with Vey Shipping.

Allowed present evidence:

- cramped or imperfect office
- route ledgers / current cargo problems
- ordinary coworkers
- leased / shared / patched infrastructure
- current work that is much smaller and messier than Greg's remembered later Vey scale

Do not establish future ownership causation, family destiny, later wealth source, or a clean origin myth unless present evidence truly earns it.

Mara does not know Greg's full later Vey history and must not speak as if she does.

## Renderer notes

- Mara's letter should be direct text for the lines that change relationship state.
- Greg may summarize lower-value portions only after the reader has enough Mara voice to feel her independent presence.
- avoid a long epistolary chapter made of one giant block quote. Let Greg's reading attention move through paper, handwriting, room, pauses, and selected lines.
- do not make Greg cry or recover a memory merely because the letter is emotionally important.
- no fake `Good / Right / Fair / There` cadence around every paragraph.
- keep humor dry and relational, not quippy.
