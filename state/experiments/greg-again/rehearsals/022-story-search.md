## Candidate C — Lower Rook Street fire

The first real cold stretch means stoves and flues are working again. Greg wakes to smoke and pounding in the building where he actually lives.

The event is not a Guild contract and not an S-class problem. The people who know the building own procedure. Greg's size, current body, good sword, and tiny support capability can matter at leverage points without making him field commander.

Chapter 1 already established the man next door as a concrete ordinary-life connection:

- Greg recognizes his voice.
- Greg remembers that he snores, borrows soap, and once threw a cup in the hallway.
- Greg does **not** remember his name.

A building emergency gives that relationship a live reason to move without inventing a memory scavenger hunt.

Potential scene shape:

1. Smoke / pounding wakes Greg before dawn.
2. The man next door gets Greg moving before Greg fully understands the source.
3. A building resident / keeper who actually knows the flue, pump, roof access, and shutoffs owns the response.
4. Greg obeys first and models second.
5. Bucket work, moving furniture, cutting a burning line / canvas, or holding a failing point gives the good sword and tiny support plane mundane physical use.
6. Another resident uses the neighbor's name in action, restoring one piece of ordinary current reality through present contact rather than recovered memory.
7. The building survives, but smoke / water / room damage leaves a real residue.
8. Greg's relationship to the people around his room changes from remembered habits to current mutual obligation.

**Strength:** overdue physical action, no contract-board filler, causal seasonal time, other-person domain authority, ordinary-life payment, and a chapter identity that is not about Greg's profession.
