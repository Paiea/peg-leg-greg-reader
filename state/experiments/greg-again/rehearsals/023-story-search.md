## Candidate A — The Tenant

Fire-watch inspection or landlord pressure turns the damaged building into a repair-cost, rent, or temporary-housing problem.

**Strength:** direct consequence, collective neighbor agency, mundane pressure.

**Weakness:** Chapter 22 already earned the fire's local residue. Making 23 immediately about invoices, flues, or relocation would mechanically serialize the newest problem and shrink the operating scale again.

**Preserve.** The building can keep changing offscreen and return when a repair, rent, or relationship consequence actually changes a live choice.

## Candidate B — The Buyer

North Gate congestion distorts a market or auction. Greg sees a damaged, obsolete, or badly understood item by a maker he recognizes from later history and can buy before the market understands it.

**Strength:** lets future knowledge work cleanly, opens the artifact / maker engine, and turns logistics pressure into economic opportunity.

**Weakness:** there is not yet a live need strong enough to make a specific artifact exist naturally. Selecting this now would risk inventing an object because the long-range map says artifacts are useful.

**Preserve until a real purchase, salvage right, repair need, or field problem creates the object honestly.**

## Candidate C — Field route verification west of Carrow

North Gate restrictions are pushing light couriers and small pack traffic toward alternate routes. The Guild has a short field contract to verify whether an old quarry road west of Carrow is usable before Deepwinter: one surveyor, two Bronze escorts, one pack animal, several days away from the city.

Greg has several weeks before the formal support assessment. He needs money, but more importantly he now has enough current evidence that support work, the filtration venture, letters, and ordinary city life do not require him to sit in Carrow waiting for Kesra.

He chooses the field contract.

Established Bronze fighter Edda from Chapter 13 can lead the escort side. Reusing her makes the Guild feel inhabited rather than generating a disposable adventuring party. A current route surveyor owns road condition, measurements, and whether a path can actually be certified.

The chapter should include real travel pressure, not only paperwork:

1. Chapter 22 residue remains visible but does not own the plot.
2. Greg goes to the Guild for work and deliberately passes over another city-scale maintenance option.
3. Sella shows him the west-route verification contract because North Gate congestion has made the old path economically relevant again.
4. Greg checks the calendar: several weeks remain before First Deepwinter / Kesra.
5. He signs for multiple days outside Carrow.
6. Field preparation costs money and exposes ordinary practical gaps: food, blanket, weather layer, cord, boot condition, carrying load.
7. Edda is already attached to the contract and owns escort procedure.
8. The party leaves Carrow.
9. Weather / water / a damaged crossing creates an actual route decision. Greg's later geographic memory may provide a lead, but present terrain and the surveyor prove it.
10. Greg contributes physically without reaching for support magic as the default answer.
11. The first night outside the city ends with Greg taking an ordinary watch as part of a field team.

**Strength:** changes operating scale, gives North Gate a long-distance consequence without another gate scene, reopens warrior / adventurer life without declaring a class progression, and gives Greg an active choice rather than another problem delivered to his door.
