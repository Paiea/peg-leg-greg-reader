## Candidate C — The Watchman / seasonal glasshorn migration

The cold rain and old quarry corridor naturally produce a field condition the road office did not schedule: a seasonal herd moving through the Cut during Greg's second watch.

Working creature shape:

- **glasshorns** are large herd animals with translucent mineral horn-sheaths that hold a faint pale glow in wet darkness;
- they use limestone cuts and mineral-water corridors during a cold-rain movement period;
- they are not hunting people;
- they are dangerous because a moving herd will hold its line through a constricted route, and blocking / startling the lead animals can turn ordinary movement into trampling or horn injury.

Do not explain a magical taxonomy or deep mechanism.

Greg knows the behavior from repeated first-life field experience. He hears / recognizes the herd before the others can identify it clearly.

He wakes Edda and tells her the important practical claim: get people and Pate above / out of the road, do not light the corridor, do not challenge the lead animals, let the herd pass.

Edda owns the actual escort decision. She believes him enough to move first rather than demanding a lecture. Harl helps relocate Pate and later owns what the churned road means for certification.

The herd passes. Greg is simply right.

The magical ecology is not a boss fight and not a lore reveal. It is a seasonal fact intersecting human infrastructure.

After daylight Harl records active seasonal wildlife use as a reliability problem for current pack traffic. He does not need Greg's remembered chronology to prove the route is affected now.

The contract continues rather than instantly ending. The new state is that the West Cut has a **seasonal ecological constraint**, not merely damaged stone and drainage problems.

**Strength:**

- directly pays off `second watch` rather than skipping over it;
- gives Greg's forty years of fieldcraft a clean, satisfying win;
- widens the fantasy world because the route itself earns magical ecology;
- changes Harl's route model without making Greg the surveyor;
- deepens Edda / Greg work trust through action rather than sentiment;
- demonstrates that the world contains non-hostile magical life with its own movement patterns;
- creates a durable seasonal fact without scheduling a creature arc.

**Risk:** the new world-iceberg work recently made magical ecology more available. Avoid making the scene feel like a doctrine demonstration. Keep the animal behavior simple, the prose practical, and the result bounded to the actual road job.
