## WHAT SHOULD ACTUALLY HAPPEN NEXT?

The strongest immediate pressure is not another external plot.

Greg has just completed five days of paid field work with poor sleep, wet-road wear, repeated carrying, watch duty, and ordinary equipment use. He came home with real field pay and slept without a watch to take.

That completed work has earned mundane consequences that can change a live choice now:

- his body needs recovery without being injured;
- road clothes and equipment need cleaning / drying / repair;
- one sock already ended Chapter 25 in visibly poor condition;
- boots, pack, cloak, scabbard, and sword all plausibly need post-road maintenance even though the sword was never drawn;
- field pay is meaningful but not wealth;
- the next rent, food, future support expense, and road-kit upkeep still compete for the same cash;
- Greg is now financially capable of not immediately selling the next day of labor.

The central question is therefore:

> **CAN COMPLETED ADVENTURER WORK BUY GREG ONE UNSOLD DAY?**

## Candidate A — The Day Off / recovery and maintenance

Greg wakes much later than intended because his body cashes in several nights of field sleep and watch duty.

Nothing is wrong enough to become an injury plot. He is simply sore, hungry, dirty, and stiff in the ordinary places five days of road work would earn.

He counts the field pay and starts giving it jobs:

- next-rent reserve;
- food;
- money kept aside for whatever follows the already-paid support assessment deposit;
- ordinary road-kit maintenance.

He cleans and oils the good sword because rain and a damp scabbard matter even when the blade never left the sheath. He deals with the dead sock, loosened boot stitching, dirty cloak / clothes, and small pack wear.

The repaired Lower Rook cookroom is available again. Noll remains ordinary neighbor residue rather than becoming a crisis or relationship speech.

While Greg moves through Carrow for mundane maintenance, established systems may be visible only as evidence:

- Dena can pass with Arlo-marked filtration service / delivery material, proving the venture continues without summoning Greg into the workshop;
- the Guild remains somewhere Greg could go for more work, but no contract is forced onto him;
- no Mara packet is accelerated merely to create content;
- Harl and Edda remain professional residue, not a party callback.

Greg chooses not to seek another paid shift that day.

The meaningful progression is not laziness and not wealth. It is margin.

> **FIELD WORK HAS BOUGHT HIM ENOUGH CASH TO MAINTAIN THE BODY / GEAR THAT DOES THE WORK AND ENOUGH SLACK TO LEAVE ONE DAY UNSOLD.**

He ends with the road kit maintained and ready rather than packed away as if the West Cut were an isolated adventure.

**Strength:**

- directly pays the user's requested money / body / sleep / equipment / work-life residue from the five-day job;
- changes Greg's practical relationship to adventuring without another encounter or promotion;
- allows Carrow, Lower Rook, the filtration venture, Mara correspondence, and the Guild to remain independently alive without making any of them perform on command;
- preserves the distinction between labor income, future support money, ordinary living cash, and venture ownership;
- makes a mundane chapter causally necessary rather than decorative;
- lets the adventure identity expand into maintenance and recovery, not only departure and danger;
- gives Greg a small but real new freedom: he can choose not to work today.
