## Campaign-level question

The first pass produced eight individually strong chapters with unusually efficient local selection. The second pass therefore tested whether the connected sequence had become too self-resolving because useful events kept arriving exactly when the previous chapter made them legible.

This was not treated as a defect in advance. Three incompatible campaign shapes were rehearsed.

### Campaign A — preserve the first pass

Keep all eight selected events and order. Allow unspecified time to exist between chapters where prose does not forbid it.

Strength: excellent genre variety, strong Greg continuity, accumulated work/economic/magic/relationship motion, no obvious continuity break.

Risk: Chapter 30 in particular makes three useful things coincide: Jorren returns during Greg's new recovery drill, both can immediately compare progress, and a two-Bronze underground job appears at the desk while they are standing there.

### Campaign B — same mountains, more independent clocks

Keep the selected events that still win, but let elapsed time and supporting-character work schedules create the connective tissue.

The strongest version lets several ordinary days pass after Tavin's letter. Jorren returns on his own schedule, closes his own north work, and is offered East Mill because his newly filed culvert experience is relevant. The East Mill contract needs a second Bronze. Jorren chooses Greg based on prior work trust. Their skill comparison becomes a short incidental yard exchange before the job rather than the mechanism that summons the job.

This preserves the dry-wall campaign result while giving Jorren stronger causal ownership.

### Campaign C — break the first-pass order more aggressively

Tested local swaps and removals:

- Jorren before the Tavin letter
- filtration distribution before Mara's next packet
- East Mill off-camera or assigned to another crew
- cinderwing work earlier
- no Gold party in Chapter 27
- delaying the second letter farther

These alternatives created novelty but not stronger causality. They either weakened a long-earned payoff, reduced independent-character evidence, or made downstream professional recurrence less earned.

Campaign C loses.

## Chapter 27 — The Student

2. Same lesson without Gold, making the chapter quieter and less visibly designed.
3. Greg buys a different form of instruction or delays instruction and takes paid work instead.
4. Gold appears elsewhere later rather than during Greg's lesson.

### Result

**SOURCE WIN.**

The paid lesson is the strongest use of Chapter 26's new margin. Lera is an earned returning person. `CUT. ARRIVE. RECOVER.` advances the exact physical problem already exposed in earlier sword work.

The Gold party risks looking thematically convenient, but the Guild yard is a natural place for higher-rank professionals to train, they remain independent of Greg, and the scene does not turn them into a hook. Removing them makes the world smaller without making the lesson more alive.

No rewrite.
