# DECISION

**SELECT.**

The chapter earns its place because it lets forty years of first-life field knowledge work cleanly without compensating punishment, while keeping the problem bounded, professional, ecological, and economically ordinary.

The cinderwing job is not a boss fight, a hidden artifact, or a magical-theory lecture. Greg simply knows a later-standard removal procedure that current Carrow has not yet standardized well. The procedure works.

That clean competence is important after several chapters where Greg's expertise was partial, shared, or corrected by another specialist.

---

# WHAT CHANGES

## Greg

- Greg gets a clean magical-ecology win from repeated first-life field experience.
- He is comfortable answering `Yes` when asked if he has done this before rather than hedging around the reincarnation problem.
- He earns 4 copper for roughly two hours of bounded Bronze work.
- He accepts pride directly at the end instead of immediately qualifying it away.
- Jorren makes Greg notice that specialized knowledge itself can have pricing value before the contract is accepted.

## Jorren

- Jorren trusts Greg's creature knowledge enough to follow the procedure without demanding proof first.
- He independently notices the economic consequence of expertise and raises it.
- He remains a working partner rather than an audience for Greg's brilliance.

## Carrow / magical ecology

- **Cinderwings** are established as hand-sized magical insects attracted to leaking low charge.
- Stored charge shows as orange lines near wing joints.
- Burning a charged cluster in dry storage risks a dangerous collective discharge.
- A low-charge source placed outside can draw a depleted cluster away from a structure.
- Egg cases and charged nest residue require physical removal after the adults disperse.
- Cheap leaking mana lamps can create repeat infestation pressure.
- Later-era warehouse practice is better standardized than current Carrow, but Greg does not import a full future codebook.

These facts are narrow working ecology, not a universal creature taxonomy.

## Economy / professional identity

- Greg and Jorren accept a normal posted Bronze rate and do not renegotiate after success.
- Jorren identifies a future leverage question: if Greg already knows a specialized procedure that halves time/risk, that information may justify different contract pricing **before** future jobs.
- This does not immediately create a specialist business, title, or Guild classification.

# WHY IT WORKS

## 1. The clean win actually lands

The chapter does not reveal that cinderwings behave differently now, that Greg remembered the wrong species, or that his procedure caused another hidden problem.

He knew.

He was right.

The story allows that to feel good.

## 2. Ecology changes the solution

The correct response is migration / attraction control rather than violence.

That makes magical creatures feel like living things with behavior instead of combat encounters with hit points.

## 3. Greg still works at current scale

He does not possess later ceramic draw lamps or municipal pest equipment.

He adapts with:

- shutters
- cheap hand lamp
- crates / plank
- gloves
- wooden scraper
- ceramic jar

Old knowledge helps. Current tools constrain execution.

## 4. The fantasy remains economic and ordinary

The owner worries about lamp-seal cost.

Workers move rope and canvas.

The contract pays eight copper total.

The possible future value of cinderwing residue is deliberately left alone because Greg's memory is too vague to manufacture a market responsibly.

That restraint protects the artifact/trade engine from turning every unusual material into loot.

## 5. Greg and Jorren continue accumulating work history

No party declaration is needed.

They now have:

- prior caravan trust
- weeks of independent separation
- renewed sparring
- one underground access job
- one creature-removal job

Their professional recurrence is becoming factual rather than aspirational.

# RISKS CHECKED

## Too easy?

No.

The purpose of the chapter is specifically to allow a domain of genuine expertise to pay cleanly. Difficulty is not required to validate competence every time.

## Greg makes everyone stupid?

No.

The owner has a plausible intuitive but dangerous solution. Jorren follows a specialist procedure once Greg demonstrates knowledge. Warehouse workers execute the plan. Sella's contract already encodes `WITHOUT STRUCTURAL FIRE`, showing institutional learning exists independently of Greg.

## Magical ecology becoming exposition?

The chapter explains only what the job requires. Later warehouse standards appear as Greg's practical comparison, not a full bestiary entry.

## Future knowledge becoming omniscience?

No.

Greg refuses to invent a market around vague later memory of cinderwing residue.

## Greg/Jorren becoming a declared party?

No.

They are recurring Bronze coworkers who currently fit together well.
