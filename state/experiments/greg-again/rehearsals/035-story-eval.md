# DECISION

**SELECT.**

The chapter earns its place because the Greg/Jorren recurrence becomes institutional behavior before either man turns it into a relationship declaration. The Guild now has enough completed-work evidence to pencil their names together when a job needs two Bronze field hands.

At the same time, Meral re-enters through her own Silver-grade work rather than as a mentor summoned for Greg's development. Rank is expressed through responsibility: she owns the relay contract, diagnostic decisions, repair standards, and the decision to stop for the night.

# WHAT CHANGES

## Greg / Jorren

- Sella now treats Greg and Jorren as a useful existing work pair when a contract needs two Bronze hands.
- They still have not declared a permanent party.
- Their accumulated compatibility is visible in the Guild register rather than discussed emotionally.
- Greg contributes one useful current-terrain observation about drainage when Meral considers a replacement relay-post site.
- Greg also catches himself before importing later standardized placement rules into older infrastructure.

## Meral

- Meral returns naturally through a new relay-maintenance contract.
- She remains the field lead and owns Silver authority.
- She remembers both Greg's mana-spending problem and Jorren's watch habits from prior work.
- She does not become Greg's formal teacher or ask for his reincarnation explanation.

## World / infrastructure

- Current Carrow courier relay infrastructure is old, irregular, and only partially standardized.
- Relay posts can be stone, wood/copper, or integrated into older field structures.
- Ceramic heads use line-of-sight pulse continuity between stations.
- Ground movement, contact drift, storm-felled trees, vegetation, wet conductive branches, and placement can all interrupt the old system.
- A later system Greg remembers is more standardized and tolerant, but current construction does not automatically obey those later rules.

## Professional ladder

- Chapter 27 showed Gold as exceptional integrated execution.
- Chapter 35 now shows Silver as ownership of a real work problem rather than merely stronger fighting.
- Greg functions well under competent authority without needing to become the smartest person on the job.

# WHY IT WORKS

## 1. Party formation remains behavioral

The strongest line is administrative rather than romantic:

> **Because you've finished work together.**

Sella does not call them a party. She has evidence that putting them together creates less supervision cost.

## 2. Meral is reused instead of creating a rank demonstration NPC

She already existed with current-world support authority, road experience, and a believable reason to cross Carrow again.

## 3. The magic stays inside infrastructure

No one explains a school or a Lamp. The relay system is simply work technology with material limitations.

## 4. Greg's future knowledge is useful but disciplined

He remembers later standardized relay rules and correction hardware, but recognizes that those rules belong to different equipment. His most useful intervention is actually current evidence: fresh storm debris shows the first replacement site will flood.

## 5. The chapter stops correctly

The uphill line is still dead at dusk. Meral decides to camp and inspect at first light.

No monster arrives because the chapter needs a cliffhanger.

# RISKS CHECKED

## Too much procedural work after Chapters 27–34?

The chapter has procedure, but its durable state change is social/institutional: Greg and Jorren have become a recognized work pair, and Silver authority is experienced from inside a real contract.

Chapter 36 should not merely repeat more post repair. The uphill failure needs to change the problem grammar if the relay job continues.

## Meral turning into another teacher?

No. She corrects work because she owns the contract. Her relationship to Greg remains professional.

## Gold nudge disappearing?

No problem. Chapter 27 already made the ceiling visible. Chapter 35 uses the ladder differently by showing what a competent Silver owns.
