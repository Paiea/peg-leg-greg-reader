# DECISION

**SELECT.**

The chapter gives economic and professional accumulation an ordinary-life consequence instead of converting every gain into gear, magic, or another contract.

Greg moves from the Chapter 1 room into a larger room in the same Lower Rook building for three copper more per week.

This is a recurring-cost decision, not a reward chest.

---

# WHAT CHANGES

## Housing

- Greg leaves the exact narrow room where Chapter 1 began.
- he moves to a larger third-floor front room in the same Lower Rook building.
- rent increases by three copper per week.
- existing deposit transfers; no new large capital barrier is invented.
- the new room has two windows, better warmth, a shallow gear/drying alcove, a built-in shelf, and enough clear floor for a meaningful sword-practice line.
- one window leaks cold air at the bottom and the room remains plainly working-class.

## Lower Rook continuity

- Greg does not abandon Lower Rook, Noll, the cookroom, neighborhood, or accumulated building history.
- Noll remains nearby rather than being replaced by a better-class social world.
- **Maud Pell** is established as the building's bounded rent collector / manager.
- she offers the room because Greg pays reliably enough and an internal move is easier to fill than an outside vacancy.

## Greg's economic state

- Greg can now rationally accept a higher recurring living cost using current earning evidence, even if venture distributions and good field jobs are not guaranteed.
- the economic question has shifted from `Can I pay anything?` toward `Is recurring quality-of-life improvement worth reducing future optionality?`
- three copper per week still hurts enough to remain visible.

## Physical capability / ordinary life

- road gear no longer competes directly with sleeping space.
- wet field equipment has a dedicated drying location.
- letters, cards, and marked support cord have stable shelf space.
- the sword can be practiced indoors without threatening the basin / chair on every cut.

No sword-skill increase is granted merely by obtaining space.

---

# WHY IT WORKS

## 1. Economic progression becomes physical

The reader can feel Greg's life scale change without inflating every ordinary price around him.

## 2. The Chapter 1 room is allowed to become history

Greg briefly misses it, then recognizes that waking there is not a good reason to remain there forever.

> **Memory did not need rent-free property.**

The story can preserve an origin without freezing Greg inside it.

## 3. Noll continuity survives upward movement

Greg does not need a cleaner neighborhood to prove progress. The stronger move is better capability inside an already-lived social environment.

## 4. No system is queued afterward

The new floor space could support sword practice, gear maintenance, letters, visitors, intimacy, recovery, or nothing important at all. It is infrastructure, not a chapter assignment.

---

# SURVIVING DISCOVERY

> **Progress should eventually improve where Greg actually lives, not only what he can survive.**

And:

> **An origin can remain important after Greg stops living inside it.**
