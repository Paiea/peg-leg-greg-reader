# GREG, AGAIN — WRITTEN DEBRIEF GUARDRAILS

Status: **ACTIVE EXPERIMENTAL PRESSURES / NOT STYLE LAW**

These pressures come from reading the selected Chapter 1–10 run as a book rather than grading individual experiments.

They exist to prevent the renderer from becoming too pleased with its own successful mechanisms.

## 1. DO NOT MANUFACTURE GREG

Greg has recurring surface habits such as:

- `Good.`
- `Right.`
- `Of course.`
- `Fair.`
- `There.`
- `Interesting.`
- `Progress.`
- `Apparently.`
- short corrective fragments

These are valid only when they carry a real turn in attention, judgment, embarrassment, or action.

Do not add them merely to make prose sound Greg-like.

Useful check:

> **If half the Greg-ish reset words disappeared, would Greg's actual thinking change?**

If not, trim them.

## 2. TRUST THE SCENE AFTER IT PROVES THE POINT

Do not routinely perform this sequence:

```text
EVENT PROVES SOMETHING
→ GREG NOTICES IT
→ PROSE EXPLAINS WHAT THE NOTICE MEANS
```

Prefer stopping after the first or second beat when the reader can already carry the inference.

A rope staying on a post does not always need a paragraph announcing that this was support.

A person acting independently does not always need Greg to state that the world moves without him.

Preserve explanation when the interpretation itself changes Greg's next decision.

## 3. BREAK THE CURRICULUM

Do not make every chapter a perfectly designed exercise for the previous chapter's newly exposed weakness.

Progress may:

- disappear from the foreground for several chapters
- regress under fatigue or fear
- develop a bad compensation
- become irrelevant in the next environment
- improve off-page through repeated use
- remain flat while another lane moves

Causality is desirable.

Curriculum is suspicious.

## 4. LONG SHADOW REQUIRED

The local engine is strong enough to carry chapters.

The novel also needs at least one pressure whose meaningful payoff lives much farther ahead.

A long shadow can be:

- a future event Greg remembers incompletely
- a person whose later identity he knows but whose present life does not match it
- an institution becoming something Greg remembers only in mature form
- a mundane decision that may be the seed of a later catastrophe or success
- someone Greg cares about moving toward a place or event he associates with later danger

Do not immediately solve or validate the shadow.

The useful feeling is:

> **I want Chapter 30 because something larger has begun moving.**

## 5. EMOTIONAL PRESSURE THAT RESISTS SYSTEMS

Greg's current emotional palette is strong in:

- dry amusement
- competence
- humiliation
- analysis
- frustration
- small pride

Actively search for pressures he cannot conveniently convert into optimization:

- grief
- affection
- desire
- jealousy
- guilt
- fear for another person
- being remembered differently than he remembers himself
- someone important to young Greg who is only partially available to old Greg's memory

Do not force melodrama.

The important thing is that Greg's intelligence cannot automatically make the emotional problem smaller.

## 6. ACTION STAYS BROAD AND CONSEQUENT

Keep action density high without encounter-of-the-week structure.

Useful action classes include:

- combat
- rescue
- structural/mechanical failure
- magical or industrial incident
- pursuit
- escape
- negotiation where terms change state immediately
- social confrontation where a relationship cannot return to its previous state
- travel decisions with irreversible cost
- institutional pressure

Action should change what is possible afterward.

## 7. MAKE PRIORITY CAUSAL

Greg cannot advance every lane.

When he chooses money, equipment, magic, relationship, business, investigation, or travel, allow another lane to:

- move without him
- become somebody else's opportunity
- worsen
- close
- become more expensive
- surprise him later

A choice is stronger when the unchosen thing keeps living.

## 8. KEEP FUTURE KNOWLEDGE DANGEROUS EVEN WHEN TRUE

Greg may remember a later fact correctly and still misunderstand the present because he lacks:

- chronology
- causation
- intermediate history
- present ownership
- current infrastructure
- who did the work
- what failed first

Avoid making future knowledge wrong merely to nerf him.

Prefer making correct future knowledge **insufficient**.

## 9. PAGE-NATIVE PROSE, NOT AUDIO RESIDUE

The written renderer may inherit discoveries from the Greg-subjective/audio experiments.

It should not inherit every fragment, repetition, or speech-oriented reset.

Use paragraph shape, sentence variety, visual pacing, and omission as page tools.

## 10. STOP WHEN THE CHAPTER HAS LANDED

One dominant causal event is usually enough.

Do not add a sword purchase, lesson, reward, joke, second customer, and new threat after the chapter already completed its movement.

Move good unused material forward until it causes something.
