The earlier short-horizon assumption of months to roughly one year is retired.

R2 may plausibly run roughly **500 chapters across approximately 5–10 years of Greg's life**.

That is permission, not a pacing quota. A year may take many chapters or pass quickly when residue can carry it. The story should not compress major life changes merely because the current frontier is early.

The Chapter 1 promise ledger remains useful, but its `short-horizon` language should now be interpreted as **long-delay promise memory**, not five-chapter debt.

# HARD ATTRACTOR

## Permanent left lower-leg loss

Greg eventually permanently loses his **LEFT lower leg**, with the knee preserved.

The cause, timing, location, surrounding people, responsibility, medical path, and narrative context remain open.

Do not copy R1's river-yard accident merely because it worked.

Do not rush the loss to satisfy the title.

A longer two-legged period may strengthen the event by allowing Current Greg to become a genuinely capable young warrior with movement habits, pleasures, confidence, and physical identity worth losing.

The event should arise from an earned causal system rather than authorial punishment, prophecy fulfillment, villain branding, or noble self-sacrifice by design.

The loss is not a climax that ends Greg's adventuring life. It changes the body's operating system and creates years of different constraints, work, movement, combat, intimacy, travel, equipment, money, housing, and social interaction.

Do not erase the disability through a magical prosthetic upgrade.

# STRONG GRAVITY

## 1. Greg may genuinely become a warrior / adventurer in this life

Do not assume Current Greg must rebuild Old Greg's support-specialist career.

Old Greg's decades of support and Barrier experience remain a major transferable competence reservoir, but Current Greg is allowed to pursue sword work, field work, Guild contracts, dangerous travel, underground exploration, ruins, salvage, monster work, and ordinary adventuring for years.

Let him become actually good.

Do not make every competence gain expose another failure merely to prevent power fantasy. Some repetitions, remembered techniques, field judgments, and future leads should work cleanly.

Support can remain one powerful language among several rather than his class identity.

### Barrier origin memory

Author-supplied long-range fact to preserve:

> Old Greg's path into Barrier began from a **birth-control context**.

The exact mechanics, people, timing, and anecdote are not yet established in R2. Do not invent them merely to service this note. Preserve the mundane / funny contrast so a later reveal can puncture the dignified mythology of an elite S-class support discipline.

## 2. The fantasy world should get larger

Carrow can become home without becoming a cage.

Across years, strong gravity exists toward:

- underground places
- ruins and old infrastructure
- dangerous roads and rivers
- Guild work
- strange markets
- magical ecology
- monsters and non-hostile dangerous creatures
- salvage
- travel
- new cities and regions
- people whose local knowledge beats Greg's broad old-life experience

Underground / ruin material is especially useful because it can converge warrior skill, fieldcraft, history, artifacts, institutional ownership, salvage economics, magical ecology, and present-versus-future knowledge without requiring a conventional villain.

Avoid monster-of-the-week or dungeon-grind structure.

## 3. Magical artifacts as strategic grammar, not stat progression

Artifacts should become an important long-range engine.

Prefer items that are:

- powerful but badly constrained
- damaged
- obsolete
- specialist
- cursed or socially dangerous
- legally awkward
- expensive to activate or maintain
- misidentified
- made by someone not yet famous
- designed for a purpose Greg does not care about
- useful only under strange geometry, timing, environment, or bodily conditions

A good bad item should make a reasonable buyer say **not worth it** while giving Greg a legitimate reason to value it.

Future knowledge may operate through maker marks, provenance, later-discovered uses, historical context, known defects, future manufacturing importance, or Greg remembering what a category eventually became.

Let Greg sometimes get an unfair win.

Do not make every seller stupid or every cursed object secretly optimal.

Do not create a +1 / +2 / +3 loot ladder.

### Important recurrence: body changes artifact value

The multi-year rehearsal repeatedly produced a strong post-loss interaction:

> **Greg's changed body can change which artifact drawbacks matter.**

An item whose severe drawback involves stance, movement, activation position, mobility, balance, timing, interface, or physical setup may be valued differently by a one-legged Greg than by a conventional warrior.

This is not permission to make disability an upgrade.

The value comes from a changed optimization problem. Some items become worse. Some become newly rational. Some require entirely different techniques. That gives the artifact engine and the body engine real causal interaction.

## 4. People before history knows what to call them

Greg should encounter people before the later stories about them have formed.

Possible later identities include:

- heroes
- rulers
- killers
- criminals
- inventors
- saints
- military figures
- Guild leaders
- disasters
- victims
- famous adventurers
- people remembered for something they did not actually cause

Greg's later knowledge may come from songs, tavern stories, official histories, monuments, propaganda, personal encounters, rumors, or accepted historical shorthand.

Do not assume the later story is morally or factually complete.

The useful pressure is the gap between **person now** and **historical object later**.

A future person does not owe Greg friendship, hatred, intimacy, or destiny merely because Greg recognizes the name.

Do not force a clean `future villain as young innocent` morality puzzle. Let some people really be dangerous, some stories be wrong, some later crimes be contingent, and some reputations remain impossible to resolve neatly.

## 5. Future knowledge contamination

Greg's governing method remains:

> **USE THE FUTURE AS A LEAD. MAKE THE PRESENT PROVE IT.**

Across a multi-year run, another problem emerges naturally:

> **The more Greg changes, the harder it becomes to distinguish bad memory from changed causality.**

Early in R2, the present can often falsify a remembered model cleanly.

Years later, Greg may ask:

- Was my memory wrong?
- Was the history wrong?
- Was the chronology wrong?
- Was I right until I changed an input?
- Did somebody else change it?
- Did my attention itself alter the market, institution, person, or event?

Do not formalize this as a branching-timeline lecture.

Let uncertainty arrive through changed evidence.

## 6. Ordinary life must survive expansion

A larger fantasy story does not replace the mundane engine.

Between ruins, contracts, monsters, travel, artifacts, famous future people, and major events, preserve:

- food
- rooms
- letters
- wages
- repairs
- transport
- clothing
- sex and desire
- boring work
- neighbors
- shopping
- bad sleep
- recurring merchants
- equipment maintenance
- people already having lives when Greg arrives

The larger fantasy becomes stronger when these things acquire history rather than disappearing.

# MULTI-YEAR TEMPORAL REHEARSAL RESULTS

This pass deliberately compared incompatible future shapes rather than selecting one plot.

Control dimensions included:

- early / middle / late leg loss
- theatre / no theatre
- artifacts central / artifacts secondary
- warrior identity sustained longer / diversified earlier
- Carrow-heavy / travel-heavy
- future historical people foregrounded / backgrounded

The exact events diverged substantially.

The following features recurred anyway.

## HIGH recurrence

### Warrior life remains viable for a long time

The current R2 state does not naturally demand that Greg abandon warrior identity soon.

His good sword, road work, monsters, tactical memory, field competence, Guild access, and young body all support a real warrior / adventurer period before any permanent bodily turn.

### Geography eventually expands

A 5–10 year R2 that never meaningfully leaves Carrow felt artificially constrained in most futures.

Travel need not mean abandoning Carrow. Carrow can become the place Greg returns to and measures other places against.

### Artifacts become social as well as tactical

Acquisition repeatedly creates dealers, makers, rivals, creditors, auctions, salvage rights, provenance disputes, repair specialists, rumors, and people tracking Greg's judgment.

The object should rarely be only the object.

### Historical people produce long-delay tension

Meeting people before their later legend consistently creates stronger multi-year pressure than merely remembering future events.

A name can sit in the story for years before the later historical meaning becomes relevant.

### Greg's future knowledge should work cleanly often enough to remain addictive

Repeated gotcha falsification makes the premise timid.

The more satisfying long shape alternates among:

- clean correct advantage
- correct fact with missing cause
- wrong chronology
- distorted public history
- changed present caused by Greg or others
- domains where Greg simply never knew enough

### The leg loss is stronger with substantial pre-loss life and substantial post-loss runway

The rehearsal did not support locking a chapter number or exact age.

However, the strongest versions usually allowed enough time for Greg to become physically competent and attached to his current movement before the loss, while leaving years afterward for the changed body to become ordinary life.

Treat this as gravity, not schedule.

## MEDIUM-HIGH recurrence

### Greg's attention can become market information

If Greg repeatedly buys apparently bad or low-status objects that later prove valuable, dealers and rivals may eventually learn that **Greg being interested is itself a signal**.

This naturally degrades easy artifact arbitrage without making his knowledge stop working.

### Makers before fame are as useful as heroes before fame

An obscure artificer, smith, enchanter, cartographer, performer, alchemist, or builder can be more interesting than a famous future warrior.

Greg may know the mark long before he knows the current person.

### Underground / ruin work is a strong convergence environment

Different rehearsal paths repeatedly used underground places to bring together old field experience, current warrior growth, artifacts, forgotten infrastructure, ecology, salvage economics, and historical uncertainty.

Do not turn that recurrence into `the dungeon arc`.

### Post-loss Greg can remain a warrior

The leg loss does not need to switch the book from Warrior to Theatre.

A changed combat style, different artifact choices, support geometry, team role, terrain selection, travel planning, and real physical limits can keep adventuring alive if the story earns it.

# TEMPORAL SNAPSHOT QUESTIONS

When a true long-range review is earned, sample several incompatible Greg futures rather than planning chapters.

Useful loose distances:

- current Greg / nineteen
- roughly one year later
- early twenties
- mid twenties
- later twenties if the run reaches there

For each future ask:

- what work does Greg actually do?
- what is he genuinely good at?
- what does he no longer do?
- where can he travel?
- where does he return?
- who expects him?
- who has left?
- which people became important without his permission?
- which historical names became current people?
- which artifacts changed his available strategies?
- what has happened to money, ownership, reputation, Guild standing, combat, magic, body, and relationships?
- how trustworthy is remembered history now?
- what changed because Greg intervened?
- what changed without him?
- what R1 material naturally re-emerged?
- what R1 material died?
- what R2 invention became more important than its R1 equivalent?

Then reason backward from interesting states:

> **What pressures, relationships, competencies, mistakes, losses, acquisitions, absences, travel, and ordinary accumulated life would need to become true for this Greg to plausibly exist?**

Do not ask what chapters must occur.
