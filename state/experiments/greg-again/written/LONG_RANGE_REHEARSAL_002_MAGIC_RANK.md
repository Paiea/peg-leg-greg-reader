## Working rank architecture

### Ordinary Guild ladder

Use the following as the working R2 professional ladder unless later prose falsifies it:

> **BRONZE -> SILVER -> GOLD -> PLATINUM**

Bronze, Silver, and Gold are already compatible with prose authority.

Platinum is adopted here as the top ordinary Guild tier because the story benefits from another major professional ceiling between an elite Gold party and the exceptional Ranker system. Do not over-explain the taxonomy in prose before the world needs it.

### Ranker designation

A **Ranker** is not merely the next Guild promotion.

Ranker status is a separate exceptional designation above the ordinary ladder.

Working classifications:

> **C-RANKER -> B-RANKER -> A-RANKER -> S-RANKER**

A Ranker must carry a formal **title conferred by recognized high authority**, such as a Crown, sovereign, king, or later-equivalent authority established by the world.

The exact political mechanism, jurisdiction, review process, and legal privileges remain open until prose earns them.

Important rule:

> **THE AUTHORITY NAMES THE RANKER. THE RANKER DOES NOT SELF-TITLE.**

Social nicknames may exist, but they are not the formal Ranker title unless authority adopts or grants them.

Ranker class should represent exceptional strategic / adventuring consequence, not a simple damage statistic. Different Rankers may be extraordinary for radically different reasons.

Old Greg's S-class support status is therefore compatible with swordsmen, mages, healers, scouts, artificers, or other exceptional figures who dominate different domains.

Old Greg being one of seven S-class adventurers means continental rarity. It does not mean seven is necessarily a permanent legal cap.

### Greg's titles across timelines

First-life Old Greg was **not** formally titled `Peg-Leg Greg` merely because the current project uses that name.

Legacy author quarry includes **Swaggory** as a favored first-life Ranker-title possibility. The spelling and exact first-life title remain flexible unless future prose needs the fact.

For the R2 line, **Peg-Leg Greg** is a strong future title candidate if Greg eventually earns Ranker recognition after the amputation. It is not guaranteed yet, and the authority / event / Ranker class that produces it remain open.

The joke works best if the formal institution treats the title with complete seriousness while Greg has opinions about having his missing leg preserved in official history.

## Show the ceiling before Greg reaches it

The story should expose higher competence well before Current Greg approaches it.

### Gold party - STRONG GRAVITY

A real Gold party is now a strong long-range showcase target.

Do not make them fake elites designed for Greg to embarrass.

A Gold party should be impressive enough that Current Greg, with decades of later experience, can accurately recognize:

> **These people are very, very good.**

Useful Gold-party signals include:

- quiet division of angles / work without repeated verbal assignment
- magic integrated into movement rather than paused for spectacle
- equipment modified by years of actual use
- disciplined resource timing
- specialists correcting one another without status theatre
- clean retreat / abort decisions
- practiced recovery after something breaks plan
- contract preparation that removes dangers before combat starts
- expensive or specialized tools Current Greg cannot yet afford
- Guild staff, merchants, and clients treating their arrival differently

Do not prove Old Greg's superiority by making Gold look mediocre.

A better scale reveal is:

1. show Gold solving something Current Greg cannot remotely execute
2. let Greg understand exactly why it was excellent
3. later reveal through memory or comparison that even this was far below S-class operating scale

The reader should be allowed to admire Gold.

### Platinum - RARE REGIONAL CEILING

Platinum should feel uncommon enough that institutions plan around availability.

A Platinum adventurer or party need not be famous everywhere, but their presence can change what contracts are considered possible, what support resources are allocated, and how dangerous work is structured.

Do not introduce Platinum solely to create a bigger number.

### Rankers - WORLD-SCALE PEOPLE

Rankers should feel qualitatively different because institutions and authorities have decided that an individual has become strategically significant enough to classify and title.

Meeting a Ranker should not automatically create a boss-fight aura.

A Ranker can be eating badly, arguing over transport, injured, retired, politically awkward, or doing boring work. The designation matters because of what they are capable of and what history / authority has already done to their identity.

## Magic correction: reserve is only one axis

Current Greg's low mana reserve is a real gate.

It must not become the universal explanation for why he cannot do interesting magic for dozens or hundreds of chapters.

R2 magic progression should be **multidimensional**.

Useful axes include:

- reserve / total capacity
- output / force available at once
- precision
- shape complexity
- duration
- range
- recovery
- movement while casting
- speed of formation
- simultaneous / concurrent constructs
- ability to change a construct after formation
- sensory feedback
- counter-magic / interference literacy
- integration with weapons, body mechanics, terrain, wards, artifacts, and teammates
- judgment about when not to cast

Do not turn these into visible stats or a training checklist.

They exist so progress can change kind instead of only changing magnitude.

## Barrier as geometry, not only wall

Selected R2 prose has already discovered the strongest local grammar for Greg's old Barrier expertise:

> **PLANE. DIRECTION. LOAD.**

Current Greg has used tiny planes to redirect liquid, a crossbow bolt, a loaded rope / iron eye, falling plaster, and other high-leverage failures.

Preserve the implication:

> **Barrier expertise is partly the manipulation of force geometry.**

This gives Greg enormous eventual room without requiring every improvement to become a larger shield.

Long-range progression may explore, when earned:

- faster placement
- changing angle under load
- moving / repositioned planes
- chained or layered planes
- multiple small planes instead of one large construct
- sustained load paths
- temporary anchors / guides
- shaping safe routes through dangerous force
- interaction with installed wards
- artifact-supplied effects that Greg treats as one real component inside a larger geometry

These are possibility shapes, not an immediate spell list.

## Greg may learn and use magic outside Barrier

Do not trap Current Greg inside `tiny support plane` as the only magical action available to him.

The broader fantasy world should contain lived personal magic used by fighters, warders, specialists, workers, healers, explorers, artificers, and other practitioners.

Greg is allowed to learn or recover useful non-Barrier magic when current-world practitioners, contracts, training, artifacts, or field necessity establish it.

Candidate domains that may be tested by prose include:

- physical reinforcement / enhancement
- weapon interaction
- movement / traction / balance assistance
- sensing / reading magical state
- resistance / protection
- binding / ward interaction
- concealment / perception
- elemental or material manipulation if the world establishes it naturally

Do not canonize a complete school taxonomy from this note.

Show magic through people doing jobs before writing a handbook.

## Let Greg win with magic

This pass strongly rejects a pattern where every magical attempt exists mainly to prove Current Greg is weak.

Greg should periodically receive **clean magical wins**.

Good win shapes include:

- old field knowledge identifies the right magical answer before present experts do
- a technique remembered from later practice works exactly as remembered because the underlying physics has not changed
- Greg uses less magic than another person would because he knows where force actually matters
- a future-common counter or safety practice is currently obscure and gives him a real edge
- he recognizes a magical creature / environment correctly and avoids a fight
- he combines a small personal cast with terrain, sword work, teamwork, or an artifact to produce an outsized result
- training finally converts old conceptual knowledge into embodied execution and the thing simply works

A win does not require everyone else to be stupid.

Let expertise be expertise.

## Magic should become more visible at higher rank

Higher-rank adventurers should help the reader understand the ceiling through lived action.

Bronze magic can be inconsistent, expensive, narrow, or absent.

Silver should begin to show reliable professional application.

Gold should show magic integrated into team movement, preparation, counters, terrain, equipment, and recovery.

Platinum should show rare breadth / depth / coordination that can change how a whole dangerous site is approached.

Ranker magic may become individually distinctive enough that a title, reputation, or political response forms around what that person can do.

S-class should not merely mean `larger spell`.

For an S-class support like Old Greg, the terrifying ceiling may be concurrency, judgment, range, endurance, impossible load management, battlefield-scale geometry, team enablement, and the ability to keep several otherwise incompatible systems functioning at once.

## Surprising convergence 1 - authority naming is a history machine

The Ranker-title rule does more than solve power scaling.

It creates an institutional version of R2's core history problem.

A sovereign authority takes a complicated person, a complicated deed, and complicated politics and reduces them to:

> **CLASS + TITLE**

Later songs, histories, contracts, and ordinary people may remember the title more clearly than the human being.

Greg can encounter future Rankers before that compression happens.

Eventually, if he is titled himself, he experiences the compression from the inside.

That is unusually strong R2 material.

## Surprising convergence 2 - Gold is the best way to show S-class

The strongest way to prove Old Greg's ceiling is probably **not** an S-class flashback full of giant effects.

A better route is:

1. let the reader genuinely admire a Gold party
2. make their competence concrete and expensive
3. let Greg accurately understand the craft
4. only then reveal through comparison how much farther Old Greg and the other S-class people operated

The lower ceiling gives the upper ceiling scale.

## Surprising convergence 3 - the higher Greg climbs, the more social magic becomes

Low-rank magic can look individual because the contracts are smaller.

Higher-rank rehearsal repeatedly made magic more social:

- one person's timing assumes another person's movement
- support changes what a fighter is allowed to risk
- counters assume enemy counters
- artifacts require handlers / maintenance / provenance
- field magic depends on scouts, locals, healers, engineers, warders, and logistics
- party familiarity reduces wasted explanation

This reinforces the party engine without making friendship a game mechanic.

## Backward requirement from the stronger futures

The promising futures did not require immediate power escalation.

They required accumulated evidence of:

- Greg practicing and actually improving
- present-world magic beyond Greg's tiny plane becoming visible
- occasional clean magical victories
- higher-rank professionals demonstrating real competence
- repeat people returning often enough to form trust and work shorthand
- Greg learning magic from current people instead of only remembering later answers
- money / training / access gates changing over time rather than remaining static forever
- artifacts opening capabilities without becoming substitutes for all personal growth
- Guild advancement reflecting work history, not protagonist entitlement
- authority-granted Ranker titles arriving only after the world has enough evidence to justify them
