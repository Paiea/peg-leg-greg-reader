# REHEARSAL SETUP

This pass extends the prior 5–10 year horizon with a deeper world model active:

- Seven Lamps exist as hidden backend magical architecture
- human magic is competent but partial and culturally misclassified
- magical beasts / ecology are real and not reducible to adventurer ranks
- other people's myth-shaped stories can exist independently of Greg
- trade, salvage, appraisal, transport, law, and ecology are allowed to interact with magic
- Greg is allowed clean wins and earned losses
- Greg may remain a warrior / adventurer for years
- permanent LEFT lower-leg loss with knee preserved remains a hard attractor
- theatre remains strong but defeasible
- artifacts remain important but may be central or secondary depending on the future
- party structure may grow from repeated work without being rostered in advance

- early / middle / late leg loss
- theatre / no theatre
- artifacts central / secondary
- Carrow-heavy / travel-heavy
- party-heavy / loose-network
- magical ecology foreground / background
- Seven-Lamps iceberg mostly hidden / partially exposed

# FUTURE A — FIELD PROFESSIONAL / LOW THEORY / LATE LEG LOSS / NO THEATRE

## Roughly age 19–20

Greg remains Bronze long enough for work history to matter. He becomes materially better at sword basics and gains one or two practical magical techniques beyond his tiny plane because current jobs expose them repeatedly.

His mana reserve improves somewhat but does not define his progress.

He begins to work with Edda or equivalent returning field people often enough that other workers know what Greg does well and what he does badly.

A Gold party crosses his path through a real contract or civic problem. Greg is impressed. Their magic is integrated into movement, preparation, terrain, and team assumptions rather than performed as spell demonstrations.

Greg gets several clean future-knowledge wins. One remembered creature behavior, route fact, or safety practice works exactly as remembered.

He also misreads at least one current condition because later infrastructure or ecology changed.

## Roughly age 22

Greg is a real professional adventurer. Silver is plausible or achieved. He can fight, travel, camp, evaluate contracts, and use magic under movement without needing every cast to be supervised.

He still does not understand the deep magical architecture better than mature specialists merely because he remembers the future.

A recurring beast problem teaches Greg something uncomfortable: his later-life classification was practically useful but conceptually wrong. The creature is not stronger than expected. It is operating on a mechanism human ward theory does not describe well.

Greg does not solve the theory.

He learns a better field rule.

## Roughly age 25

Greg may be Gold-adjacent or Gold depending on actual accumulated work, but the important shift is professional density rather than badge.

He has people who request him, routes he knows, equipment with repair history, and several places where innkeepers / Guild staff / merchants know what he normally needs.

The Seven-Lamps iceberg remains mostly hidden. Greg has seen enough anomalies to know human theory is not the whole structure, but he cannot diagram the truth.

Leg loss has still not happened. His two-legged combat identity is mature and worth losing.

## Later twenties

The leg loss occurs late in this future and destroys a deeply practiced movement system.

Greg spends years adapting without theatre. He remains in field work, changes contract selection, relies more heavily on team timing, and uses support geometry / artifacts differently.

The life remains rich without performance becoming a destination.

### Backward requirements

This future requires:

- repeated field work instead of one-off adventure tourism
- actual sword repetition and success
- practical non-Barrier magic exposure
- higher-rank examples worth admiring
- recurring companions without forced roster assembly
- creatures encountered as ecology, not only combat encounters
- enough two-legged mastery before amputation to make the loss alter a real identity

# FUTURE B — ARTIFACT / MARKET SPECIALIST / MID LEG LOSS / LATER THEATRE

## Roughly age 19–20

Greg gets several small acquisition wins because he recognizes maker marks, later-use categories, or future manufacturing significance.

One purchase is genuinely unfairly good.

One is genuinely bad.

The market does not conspire to balance them.

He starts meeting repairers, appraisers, pawnbrokers, salvage clerks, collectors, and artificers. Objects accumulate social history.

## Roughly age 22

Greg's attention has become weak market information. A few people notice he watches strange lots carefully.

He adapts by using intermediaries, buying bundles, walking away, or deliberately allowing other buyers to take things he does not value enough to expose his interest.

A failed or obsolete artifact exposes an impossible interaction between two human schools. Different specialists explain it differently. Nobody has the Seven Lamps answer.

Greg only knows the object later became famous for a different use.

## Roughly age 25

The leg loss happens mid-run.

His artifact preferences change because movement, balance, stance, activation time, and handling constraints now matter differently.

Some items become useless.

Some become newly rational.

This does not make disability an upgrade. It changes the optimization problem.

Theatre enters later through a real social route because Greg's post-loss body, object handling, attention, timing, and interest in controlled perception make the environment unusually compatible.

He does not stop adventuring automatically.

## Later twenties

Greg may have a strange mixed identity: field adventurer, artifact broker/user, Barrier practitioner, occasional performer or stage worker, business owner, and person with a formal or informal reputation for recognizing undervalued junk.

His remembered future is badly contaminated in markets where his own buying behavior changed demand years earlier.

### Backward requirements

This future requires:

- repeated markets rather than one legendary auction
- boring repair / storage / transport / provenance problems
- at least one clean arbitrage win that pays
- at least one bad purchase that remains bad
- other people learning from Greg's behavior
- artifacts that change strategy rather than stats
- post-loss ordinary handling constraints before theatre becomes plausible

# FUTURE C — OTHER PEOPLE'S MYTHS / TRAVEL-HEAVY / EARLY LEG LOSS / NO THEATRE

## Roughly age 19–20

Greg enters one regional story through ordinary work: escort, retrieval, creature handling, salvage, weather shelter, or a party member's obligation.

The apparent contract turns out to belong to people with older local relationships, prohibitions, rivalries, hospitality rules, or supernatural obligations.

Greg is not the protagonist.

He contributes something useful, possibly through future field knowledge, then leaves while the actual local relationship continues.

## Roughly age 22

By now Greg has been present at several places where the official Guild description was radically smaller than the local story.

He learns a useful professional rule:

> **THE CONTRACT IS NOT ALWAYS THE STORY.**

This does not make every village secretly mythic.

Most jobs remain jobs.

The early leg loss in this future occurs before Greg reaches ordinary elite rank. It interrupts warrior development while he still strongly identifies as an adventurer.

His recovery happens inside a network of people and places accumulated through travel rather than one fixed institution.

## Roughly age 25

Greg is post-loss and still traveling, though not identically.

He is more selective about terrain, transport, lodging, and contract structure.

He may be invited into difficult local situations because someone remembers that he did not try to seize control the last time he entered someone else's problem.

His reputation includes restraint as much as effectiveness.

## Later twenties

The world contains stories Greg knows only as later songs or histories, but he has now seen some of their human beginnings.

In at least one case, the later song is unfair.

In another, it is substantially true.

In another, Greg still cannot tell.

The Seven-Lamps iceberg appears mostly through creatures, place-bound phenomena, and local magical traditions rather than academic revelation.

### Backward requirements

This future requires:

- travel with actual cultural difference
- local people who own expertise and stakes
- Greg sometimes leaving without resolution
- recurring places / people years later
- myth-shaped material with tonal variety, including funny or intimate stories
- disability affecting logistics and hospitality without becoming every story's subject

# FUTURE D — MAGIC THEORY PRESSURE / PARTY-HEAVY / MIDDLE LEG LOSS / ARTIFACTS SECONDARY

## Roughly age 19–20

Greg begins recovering practical magic through work and formal instruction.

He sees several human traditions producing related effects with incompatible explanations.

At first this looks like ordinary professional disagreement.

## Roughly age 22

A stable field core has emerged through repeated work.

Party familiarity becomes capability: one person's cast assumes another person's movement, support changes which risks a fighter can take, and recovery becomes coordinated.

Greg is personally more magical now. He can cast while moving, form faster, maintain more than one simple effect, or use another earned domain of magic.

He is still far below Gold-level integrated team magic.

## Roughly age 25

The party or Greg becomes involved with a site where current magical theory repeatedly fails to predict behavior.

Different specialists each possess a piece of the answer.

The deep system is not discovered in a lecture.

It emerges as a pattern of incompatible successful techniques.

Greg may begin suspecting that human schools are projections of something deeper.

He does not recover the words `Seven Lamps` unless separately earned by world history.

The leg loss occurs around this period and forces the team's magical assumptions to change because Greg's movement and positioning change.

## Later twenties

Greg may be an unusually broad practical magician without being a grand theorist.

He knows more kinds of things work than most people do, partly because he has seen multiple traditions and decades of future practice.

He still cannot explain all of them under one model.

### Backward requirements

This future requires:

- multiple real practitioners with different traditions
- techniques that work before theory agrees
- party recurrence
- magic integrated with work rather than school exposition
- a few anomalies repeated across years before any meta-theory becomes plausible

# FUTURE E — MONEY / TRADE / ECOLOGY / WARRIOR-LONGER / LATE THEATRE OPTIONAL

## Roughly age 19–20

Greg's warrior life expands into routes where magical ecology directly changes prices and contracts.

Creature migration closes one road and enriches another town.

A material harvested from a dangerous but non-hostile beast is worth more intact than the bounty for killing it.

Greg's future knowledge lets him recognize one such distortion early and profit cleanly.

## Roughly age 22

Greg has learned that monster knowledge and market knowledge overlap.

A creature problem may be solved by changing storage, transport timing, bait, water flow, or harvest pressure rather than combat.

Other adventurers may be better fighters while Greg sees the economic/ecological leverage.

This makes him useful without making everyone else stupid.

## Roughly age 25

Greg has partial ownership in more than one small thing, or has consciously refused ownership because obligations outweighed upside.

His money is not a single net-worth number. He may hold equity, artifacts, claims, relationships, contracts, cash, debt exposure, and obligations.

A magical ecological change Greg remembers as a later historical crisis begins earlier or differently because current extraction patterns are already shifting.

Greg may have contributed to the shift by making one market more attractive years before.

## Later twenties

Future knowledge contamination becomes economic before it becomes cosmic.

Greg can no longer use remembered prices as a clean control because he has altered who noticed what, where money flowed, and which routes became viable.

Theatre may or may not appear. The future works without it.

### Backward requirements

This future requires:

- repeated ordinary pricing
- creature products / route premiums / storage / law
- ownership distinct from cash
- Greg sometimes declining apparently profitable opportunities
- consequences of extraction and market signaling
- future knowledge that changes markets by being used

# FUTURE F — HIGHER RANK / RANKER HISTORY / WORLD-ICEBERG MOSTLY HIDDEN

## Roughly age 19–20

Greg sees a Gold party and perhaps one Ranker from a distance or through institutional consequence.

The Gold party is excellent.

The Ranker is not necessarily flashy.

Current Greg is nowhere near either.

## Roughly age 22

Greg meets one person he knows by a later authority-granted title before that title exists.

The person is ordinary enough to disappoint the legend and complicated enough to make later history more interesting.

Greg does not become central to their trajectory automatically.

## Roughly age 25

Greg may be Gold or approaching it in some variants, but he is still dramatically below Old Greg's later S-class operating scale.

He has now seen enough excellent professionals to understand how extraordinary S-class really was.

The Seven-Lamps iceberg remains largely backend. Even Rankers are human experts inside partial systems.

The leg loss can occur before or after Greg reaches Gold without changing this conclusion.

## Later twenties

Greg may earn lower Ranker recognition if accumulated consequence actually justifies sovereign attention.

If the R2 title eventually becomes **Peg-Leg Greg**, the authority-granted title compresses a complicated life into a formal historical identity.

The world may remember the title more clearly than what Greg actually did.

### Backward requirements

This future requires:

- higher-rank professionals shown before Greg joins them
- authority / political systems established through ordinary world life before Ranker ceremony
- future-famous people introduced before titles
- no rush to S-class
- a long enough span for reputation to become institutional rather than merely social

# FUTURE G — CARROW HOME BASE / LARGE WORLD OFFSCREEN / THEATRE LATER / MAGIC THROUGH CIVIC LIFE

## Roughly age 19–20

Greg travels, but Carrow becomes a place he returns to.

Magical world expansion also occurs through civic systems: wards, water, building standards, fires, markets, healers, creature control, visiting adventurers, and infrastructure.

## Roughly age 22

Carrow has visibly changed.

Some changes Greg remembers from later life are underway.

Others are happening differently.

Higher-rank parties, foreign practitioners, or rare creatures can intersect the city without making Carrow a static tutorial zone.

## Roughly age 25

Greg has several layers of belonging in the same city: neighbor, Guild worker/adventurer, business owner, correspondent, customer, field contact, maybe lover or party member.

A myth-shaped story can enter through somebody else's arrival rather than Greg traveling to a mythic location.

The leg loss changes Carrow itself for Greg because stairs, routes, rooms, shops, and known distances become different practical objects.

## Later twenties

Theatre has a strong chance of entering here because a stable city can support recurring paid performance work alongside adventuring, artifact dealing, and relationships.

But theatre is still optional.

### Backward requirements

This future requires:

- Carrow changing over years
- recurring businesses / neighbors / institutions
- travel that does not erase home
- civic magic and creature ecology entering ordinary life
- post-loss accessibility emerging through actual known routes rather than inspirational framing

# STRONG GRAVITY

## 1. Greg becomes a genuinely capable warrior / adventurer

This survived every useful future, including magic-heavy and trade-heavy ones.

The longer horizon makes warrior identity more plausible, not less.

## 2. Greg becomes genuinely magical again

Also survives every useful future.

The strongest versions use multidimensional growth: precision, timing, shape, movement, concurrency, counters, integration, and judgment as well as reserve.

## 3. Human magic remains smaller than magical reality

This strengthened substantially under the Seven-Lamps model.

Even elite human practice should not exhaust what magic is.

The reader can eventually understand that different successful traditions are touching deeper architecture without characters immediately solving it.

## 4. The world needs magical ecology, not just magical combat

Beasts, plants, migrations, habitats, materials, place-bound phenomena, and extraction pressures recur because they support adventure, money, trade, travel, local knowledge, and deep magic simultaneously.

## 5. Magic and economy should interact repeatedly

Trade is not a side game.

Artifacts, creature materials, routes, salvage, law, storage, appraisal, ownership, repair, and risk all become stronger when magic affects them.

## 6. Other people need stories Greg does not own

This recurrence became stronger than expected.

A 500-chapter Greg-only gravity field feels small even when geography expands.

Let Greg enter conflicts, obligations, romances, family histories, local prohibitions, and supernatural stories already in motion.

Sometimes he contributes one important thing and leaves.

## 7. Repeat field relationships remain stronger than disposable crews

The deeper magic actually strengthens this because team timing and cross-specialty knowledge become more valuable as contracts become harder.

## 8. Higher-rank competence should be visible before Greg reaches it

Gold remains the best immediate ceiling demonstration.

Platinum / Ranker scale can follow later when the world earns it.

## 9. Future knowledge needs both clean wins and contamination

The long shape is strongest when Greg's advantage is undeniably real early and becomes less clean partly because he has used it successfully for years.

## 10. Ordinary life remains underneath everything

Rooms, meals, sex, clothing, wages, repairs, letters, neighbors, shopping, transport, bad sleep, and recurring merchants remain crucial even when the world contains deep magic and myth-shaped stories.

# SURPRISING CONVERGENCE 1 — THE DEEPER MAGIC MAKES GREG LESS CENTRAL

The Seven-Lamps iceberg could have made Greg more special.

The better recurrence is the opposite.

If humans only possess fragments, then expertise is distributed across regions, professions, families, beasts, artificers, healers, local practices, and forgotten infrastructure.

Greg's forty years matter enormously, but the world can always contain someone who knows one thing he does not.

That preserves both competence fantasy and social density.

---

# SURPRISING CONVERGENCE 2 — TRADE MAY REVEAL MAGIC BEFORE SCHOLARSHIP DOES

The strongest iceberg evidence repeatedly appeared through markets and objects rather than lectures.

An appraiser misprices an object because their school cannot explain it.

A material behaves differently after transport.

A regional technique makes an imported tool suddenly useful.

A beast product later becomes industrially important.

Merchants may discover reliable magical facts long before theorists explain them.

That is extremely compatible with R2 because money already forces claims to become practical.

---

# SURPRISING CONVERGENCE 3 — ECOLOGY IS A TEMPORAL ENGINE

Magical ecology gives the 5–10 year horizon natural clocks:

- migrations
- breeding cycles
- harvest seasons
- habitat loss
- route changes
- extraction pressure
- disease
- population collapse / recovery

Greg can remember a later `monster crisis` that was actually years of ecological pressure becoming visible.

This creates long shadows without villains.

---

# SURPRISING CONVERGENCE 4 — OTHER PEOPLE'S MYTHS SOLVE SCALE BETTER THAN MORE PLOT

A bigger world does not require Greg to acquire a master quest.

It requires Greg to encounter lives with enough independent causality that they would be stories even if he never arrived.

The moʻolelo-derived quarry is especially useful here because many of its strongest shapes are relational, geographic, familial, hospitality-based, or obligation-based rather than villain-quest structures.

The R2 translation should keep that causal richness while inventing its own cultures.

---

# SURPRISING CONVERGENCE 5 — THE LEG LOSS CAN CHANGE MAGICAL THEORY IN PRACTICE WITHOUT REVEALING DEEP THEORY

Post-loss Greg will have to rebuild stance, movement, load assumptions, timing, equipment interfaces, and team expectations.

That may cause him to discover that some rules he thought were `magic rules` were actually `two-legged practitioner conventions` or equipment conventions.

This does **not** mean disability unlocks a hidden Lamp.

It means changed embodiment can expose assumptions inside human technique.

That is a much better interaction.

---

# SURPRISING CONVERGENCE 6 — RANKERS CAN BE PEAK HUMAN PRACTICE WITHOUT BEING PEAK MAGIC

Separating human mastery from deep magical reality lets Rankers stay impressive.

They are not fake elites who merely failed to learn the secret system.

They are extraordinary people operating near the edge of what their cultures, bodies, tools, and accumulated practice make possible.

The world can still contain older, stranger, non-human, or differently organized magic beyond them.

That preserves Old Greg's achievement while keeping wonder alive.
