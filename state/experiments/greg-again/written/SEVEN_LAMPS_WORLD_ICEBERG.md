# GREG, AGAIN — SEVEN LAMPS WORLD ICEBERG

Status: **ACTIVE DEEP WORLD / MAGIC DEVELOPMENT MEMORY / NON-CANON / NOT A PUBLIC TEXTBOOK**

Purpose: preserve the deeper magical/world architecture underneath R2 while keeping the local chapter writer free to discover it slowly through people, jobs, creatures, artifacts, ruins, travel, money, and consequence.

Primary design source:

`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-08-r2-seven-lamps-world-design.md`

Current prose and current written state outrank this file.

---

# 1. THE SEVEN LAMPS ARE BACKEND REALITY

The deep magical architecture uses the seven-lamp conceptual set inherited from the prior Architecture / Exposition experiment:

- **Sacrifice**
- **Truth**
- **Power**
- **Beauty**
- **Life**
- **Memory**
- **Obedience**

These are not seven public schools, seven elements, seven colors, seven character classes, or a neat Guild curriculum.

They are different fundamental ways magical reality may be made to produce or permit change.

Different Lamps may reach superficially similar outcomes through genuinely different causal mechanisms.

Therefore:

> **SAME RESULT DOES NOT IMPLY SAME MAGIC.**

A barrier, healing effect, concealment, binding, transformation, movement effect, or artifact behavior may be possible through more than one deep principle.

Do not lock exact spell-to-Lamp mappings before story evidence earns them.

Do not write a hidden backend diagram and then leak it wholesale into character dialogue.

# 2. HUMAN MAGIC IS PARTIAL, CULTURAL, AND SOMETIMES WRONG

Human civilization can be extremely competent without possessing a complete theory of magic.

Humans may have:

- Guild practices
- ward traditions
- support disciplines
- healer traditions
- combat magic
- artificing
- regional schools
- religious or civic practices
- military techniques
- family methods
- professional rules of thumb
- dangerous folk techniques
- academic theories
- legally regulated forms

These traditions may interact with the Seven Lamps without knowing the Seven Lamps as such.

A human tradition may:

- know one deep principle unusually well but call it something else
- use a second principle only through inherited technique
- combine two or more principles and classify the result as one school
- correctly reproduce an effect while misunderstanding why it works
- preserve safety rules whose original explanation has been lost
- reject a valid foreign technique because its theory sounds wrong
- discover a useful interaction centuries before formal theory catches up
- mistake a current technical ceiling for a natural law

This means R2 can support both a hard magic system and genuine discovery.

The hardness comes from underlying causal structure.

The mystery comes from incomplete human access to that structure.

# 3. OLD GREG WAS ELITE INSIDE AN INCOMPLETE WORLD

Old Greg was one of seven S-class adventurers on the continent and an elite Barrier / support practitioner.

That gives him enormous practical authority in domains he actually lived through.

It does not make him omniscient about magic.

Old Greg may know:

- mature practical techniques
- later standard safety practice
- famous failures
- field interactions
- known counters
- artifact behavior
- high-level support geometry
- creature habits
- institutional procedure
- what became common decades later

He may still fail to know:

- the deepest reason a phenomenon works
- lost or regional traditions he never encountered
- beast magic outside human categories
- obsolete methods discarded before his prime
- ancient systems under ruins
- causal origins later history erased
- techniques another culture solved by a different deep route

Therefore the second-life premise keeps an intellectual frontier even when Greg is decisively experienced.

> **EXPERTISE IS REAL. TOTAL THEORY IS NOT.**

# 4. BARRIER REMAINS PRACTICAL GEOMETRY

Current R2 has established a useful Barrier grammar:

> **PLANE. DIRECTION. LOAD.**

Preserve this as Greg's practical human language for force geometry.

Barrier may involve:

- redirection
- bracing
- isolation
- load sharing
- force routing
- anchoring
- layering
- moving planes
- changing angle under load
- interaction with installed wards
- temporary safe channels
- multiple simultaneous constructs

Do not prematurely declare which single Lamp `owns` Barrier.

Barrier may prove to be:

- a partial human model
- a family of techniques sharing a practical grammar but not one deep cause
- a hybrid practice
- a later historical compression of several earlier methods

Preserve the author-supplied fact:

> Old Greg's route into Barrier began from a **birth-control context**.

Do not invent the exact mechanics, partner, technique, or social situation merely to explain the note.

The value is partly the mundane contrast between an undignified practical origin and later S-class mythology.

# 5. MAGIC SHOULD BE VISIBLE AS WORK

Before explaining magical theory, show people doing magic for reasons.

Useful sites include:

- construction
- transport
- medicine
- fire control
- wards
- agriculture
- mining
- salvage
- combat
- cooking
- manufacturing
- ship work
- creature handling
- surveying
- performance
- concealment
- policing
- ritual
- domestic life

Different regions or professions may normalize different magical solutions.

A Carrow practitioner may think a Halden technique is bizarre.

A Gold adventurer may use an interaction a Bronze has never seen.

A rural family may preserve one excellent technique that no academy respects.

A beast may perform something humans consider theoretically impossible.

# 6. MAGICAL ECOLOGY IS NOT A GUILD LADDER

R2 should contain magical beasts, altered ecologies, strange environments, magical plants, rare shapeshifters, territorial entities, underground life, and phenomena that do not care about human classification.

When useful, creatures should have actual ecology:

- habitat
- food
- migration
- nesting
- mating
- territorial behavior
- predators
- prey
- symbiosis
- seasonality
- mana interaction
- human avoidance
- human exploitation
- trade value
- disease
- cultural meaning
- legal status
- ecological damage caused by human extraction

Guilds may create practical danger labels.

Those labels are management tools, not cosmic power truth.

Do not translate every beast into `equivalent to a Gold adventurer`.

A creature can be easy to kill and disastrous to disturb.

A weak creature may be commercially priceless.

A powerful creature may be harmless if left alone.

A thing Greg remembers as a monster may later prove to be a regional ecological failure, displaced animal, ritual object, colony organism, or something humans had badly categorized.

Do not force every correction. Sometimes the monster really is a monster.

---

# 7. RARE SHAPESHIFTERS AND OTHER STRANGE BEINGS

Shapeshifters or similarly liminal beings may exist, but should remain rare enough that discovery matters.

Avoid making them a routine fantasy species added only for checklist variety.

Useful pressures include:

- local knowledge versus outsider assumptions
- identity hidden for practical rather than melodramatic reasons
- ecological or familial obligations
- hospitality
- marriage / courtship / kinship
- territorial rules
- political danger
- different magical mechanisms mistaken for one category by humans

Do not assume all shapeshifting comes from the same Lamp or method.

---

# 8. OTHER PEOPLE'S MYTHS

R2 may translate structural beats from prior Hawaiian / moʻolelo-inspired quarry into its own fantasy cultures, geographies, histories, and magical systems.

Preserve causal and emotional architecture.

Do **not** preserve literal Hawaiian surface markers merely to signal the source.

Useful quarry shapes include:

- retrieval journeys that become emotionally complicated
- hospitality involving powerful strangers
- rare shapeshifters
- water guardians or place-bound dangerous beings
- impossible construction or landscape-scale labor
- intimate conflict creating geographic consequence
- local prohibitions outsiders misunderstand
- rival powers
- difficult returns
- old lovers or family history complicating apparently simple work
- supernatural situations that are petty, funny, romantic, familial, practical, tragic, or dangerous rather than automatically solemn

Primary rule:

> **GREG DOES NOT HAVE TO OWN THE MYTH.**

Greg may arrive because of:

- a contract
- escort work
- shelter
- trade
- salvage
- route verification
- a party member's obligation
- creature handling
- a missing person
- political errands
- weather
- accident
- curiosity

He may:

- witness
- assist
- misunderstand
- solve one bounded problem
- make one crucial contribution
- profit
- lose money
- be injured
- leave early
- return years later

The actual protagonists of that local story continue to have lives after Greg leaves.

This supports social density and prevents protagonist gravity.

# 9. MYTH-SCALE DOES NOT MEAN GRIM-SCALE

Preserve tonal range.

A magical story may involve:

- embarrassment
- hospitality
- courtship
- family expectations
- petty supernatural behavior
- bureaucratic misunderstanding
- practical taboos
- absurdly specific local etiquette
- feuds
- grief
- violence
- generosity
- bad jokes
- ordinary meals around impossible beings

Do not make every old story a massacre, prophecy, apocalypse, or sacred solemnity.

Large magic can coexist with mundane human behavior.

# 10. TRADE, MONEY, AND MAGIC SHOULD INTERLOCK

The fantasy engine strengthens when magic changes economics.

Possible consequences include:

- creature materials
- dangerous-route premiums
- salvage rights
- artifact provenance
- appraisal
- repair
- recharge
- maintenance
- specialist transport
- legal restrictions
- poaching
- protected habitats
- breeding
- seasonal supply
- cross-school mispricing
- regional techniques
- guild monopolies
- insurance-like risk allocation
- black markets
- obsolete stock
- confiscated collections
- failed artificer work
- maker marks
- ancient infrastructure ownership

Greg's future knowledge may create value because he knows:

- a maker later matters
- a material later gets scarce
- a defect later becomes useful
- a route later opens or closes
- a creature product later changes industries
- an artifact's real use
- a current school is mispricing another school's technique
- an old ruin later becomes important

This knowledge can be cleanly profitable.

It can also fail because Greg lacks present ownership, chronology, transport, law, storage, market scale, or causal detail.

Do not punish every successful trade.

Do not protect every speculative trade.

---

# 11. ARTIFACTS CAN EXPOSE DEEP MAGIC WITHOUT EXPLAINING IT

Artifacts are especially useful iceberg evidence.

An object may:

- function through a principle its current owner cannot explain
- be misclassified by one school and obvious to another
- contain an obsolete technique
- combine effects that current theory says should conflict
- require strange user behavior because the original interface assumption was different
- interact with beasts or environments in ways human theory misses
- be valuable because Greg knows a later use while still misunderstanding the deep reason

Do not make every mysterious artifact a Seven-Lamps clue.

Do not make every artifact secretly world-changing.

The iceberg works because most of the world remains ordinary enough to live in.

---

# 12. HIGH-RANK MAGIC IS STILL HUMAN MAGIC

The ordinary Guild ladder and exceptional Ranker system remain useful human professional/social structures.

Gold, Platinum, and Rankers may demonstrate terrifying competence.

That does not imply they understand the full magical architecture.

A Gold party can be vastly better than Bronze because of:

- practiced magic
- team timing
- preparation
- specialized equipment
- counters
- recovery
- logistics
- field knowledge

A Ranker may be strategically exceptional through one bizarre mastery.

An S-class person may still encounter something genuinely outside their conceptual map.

This gives the world room above Greg without making his old achievement fake.

---

# 13. CAUSALITY PAYS BOTH DIRECTIONS

The engine recently needed a corrective toward letting Greg win.

Preserve that corrective without turning it into protection.

Use:

> **LET CAUSALITY PAY BOTH DIRECTIONS.**

Good decisions often help.

Bad decisions often hurt.

Good decisions can meet bad luck.

Bad decisions can get lucky.

Greg's decades of experience alter probabilities in domains he understands.

They do not grant narrative immunity.

He may:

- win fights
- lose fights
- make money
- lose money
- spot valuable artifacts
- buy junk
- correctly identify a creature
- badly misread one
- save someone
- fail to save someone
- avoid a disaster
- help create a different disaster

Do not staple a hidden loss onto every clean win.

Do not rescue Greg from earned loss because the story has recently emphasized competence.

---

# 14. LOCAL STORY ENGINE INHERITANCE

The chapter writer should inherit only a compact version of this file.

Useful local priors:

- magic is deeper than current human theory
- different traditions may solve similar problems differently
- humans can be extremely competent while conceptually incomplete
- magical ecology does not obey Guild rank labels
- magical beings should have lives / ecologies / purposes beyond combat
- Greg can walk into someone else's myth
- magic should touch money, law, trade, infrastructure, and ordinary work
- wins and losses both follow causality

Do not turn a local story search into a Seven Lamps reveal.

Do not invent a new school because the architecture permits one.

Do not force a magical beast, myth-shaped episode, artifact, or foreign tradition into the next chapter.

Local rule remains:

> **WHAT SHOULD ACTUALLY HAPPEN NEXT?**
