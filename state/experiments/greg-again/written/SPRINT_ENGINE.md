# GREG, AGAIN — SPRINT ENGINE

Status: **ACTIVE COMPACT SPRINT GUIDANCE / NON-CANON / NOT AN OUTLINE**

Purpose: preserve the speed and local judgment of multi-chapter Greg, Again writing sprints without letting a sequence of individually strong chapters become suspiciously well scheduled.

This file is a guardrail for **chapter-to-chapter causality and checkpoint rehearsal**. It does not replace `STORY_ENGINE_GUIDE.md`, current written authority, or local story judgment.

Core correction:

> **THE WORLD HAS NOT READ THE PREVIOUS CHAPTER.**

A new capability, relationship, mystery, payment, contact, or opportunity does not need to become the next chapter's engine merely because the reader just learned about it.

---

## 1. KEEP THE SPRINT SPEED

Do not slow to one chapter per transaction merely because a rehearsal found one improvable seam.

The 27–34 control showed that fast chapter generation can remain strong across prose, voice, character judgment, magic, money, relationships, and world expansion.

The main sprint risk is **causal over-efficiency between chapters**, not automatically weak prose inside each chapter.

Default sprint shape:

> **WRITE 5–8 CHAPTERS -> REHEARSE THE CONNECTED RUN -> CHANGE ONLY WHAT CLEARLY LOSES -> CONTINUE**

Publication/state plumbing may be batched when repository authority allows it.

## 2. INDEPENDENT CLOCKS BEAT CONVENIENT ENTRANCES

Before using a returning person, letter, contract, business result, creature problem, institutional request, or market opportunity, ask:

> **WHAT WAS THIS PERSON OR SYSTEM DOING WHILE GREG WAS NOT LOOKING?**

Useful causes include:

- somebody else's previous contract
- their own travel time
- staffing changes
- weather
- customer behavior
- family obligation
- money pressure
- injury or recovery
- institutional scheduling
- seasonal work
- another person's mistake or success
- ordinary elapsed time

Do not require a paragraph explaining the clock. The causal answer may remain mostly off-page.

## 3. DO NOT CASH EVERY COUPON IMMEDIATELY

A chapter may establish something valuable and then leave it alone.

Examples:

- sword instruction does not require an immediate combat test
- a new magical technique does not require the next chapter to showcase it
- a returning coworker does not require an immediate ideal contract
- a weird wall does not require immediate archaeology
- a business distribution does not require an immediate investment decision
- specialist creature knowledge does not require immediate specialist pricing
- a Gold-party glimpse does not require a Gold relationship

Unspent causal potential is not wasted material.

Let some things sit until another clock reaches them.

## 4. WATCH FOR THE PERFECT NEXT JOB

Guild contracts are a strong R2 engine and therefore a dangerous convenience engine.

Before accepting a perfectly shaped next contract, ask:

- why does this job exist now?
- why is Greg seeing it rather than somebody else?
- did an established worker, client, road, institution, or prior job cause the match?
- would the job still exist if Greg spent the morning somewhere else?
- could another person already own the opportunity and choose Greg?

Sometimes the answer can simply be that the Guild board has work. Do not make every job complicated.

But if every exact developmental need appears on Sella's desk immediately after the prior chapter, **Sella starts functioning like the author**.

Avoid that.

## 5. ORDINARY TIME MAY BE INVISIBLE

Days may pass without becoming prose.

Do not advertise pacing repairs with narration about skipped chapters, narrative attention, educational scenery, plot convenience, or the story not focusing on something.

Instead use changed evidence when needed:

- laundry is dry
- a letter has had time to travel
- bruises faded
- someone returned yesterday
- a job was already completed
- shelves got fuller
- weather shifted
- money was spent
- somebody learned something elsewhere

If nothing from the interval matters, the interval may remain unstated.

## 6. GREG DOES NOT KNOW HE IS IN A BOOK

Editorial language belongs in rehearsals and evaluations, never in Greg's consciousness unless the literal in-world meaning independently earns it.

Prose must not use concepts such as:

- chapter
- scene
- plot
- narrative attention
- pacing
- reader
- author
- educational scenery
- story convenience
- exposition
- foreshadowing
- callback
- arc

as meta commentary about the novel.

A rehearsal may say:

> `The Gold party would become educational scenery here.`

Greg may think:

> `I did not see them again.`

Do not let the diagnosis leak into the patient.

## 7. REHEARSAL IS A CHALLENGER, NOT A REWRITE MANDATE

At a sprint checkpoint:

> **PRESUME SOURCE WIN.**

Rehearsal must earn a change by producing a materially stronger causal or character trajectory.

Do not rewrite because:

- the tool was invoked
- a second version can be made different
- ordinary life could be messier
- an existing chapter reaches a bounded decision
- a clean Greg win lacks punishment
- the sequence contains several useful chapters in a row

Useful verdicts:

- **SOURCE WIN** — preserve prose exactly
- **RECOMPOSE** — same mountain, stronger causal ownership/order
- **REWRITE** — current chapter genuinely loses at scene/story level
- **CUT / MERGE / MOVE** — only when the connected run clearly improves

Most checkpoint chapters may legitimately be SOURCE WIN.

## 8. CHECK THE SEQUENCE BEFORE THE SENTENCES

Sprint checkpoint priority:

1. **CAUSAL OWNERSHIP** — who actually caused each opportunity/problem?
2. **INDEPENDENT CLOCKS** — did supporting people and systems move while Greg was absent?
3. **COUPON TIMING** — are we cashing every newly earned thing immediately?
4. **PERFECT JOBS** — are contracts suspiciously tailored to Greg's current lesson?
5. **UNRESOLVED RESIDUE** — did enough things remain open, delayed, or owned by others?
6. **RHYTHM** — does the run vary work, relationship, money, body, magic, travel, home, and uncertainty naturally rather than by quota?
7. **PROSE** — only then look for repeated Greg tics, over-explanation, weak openings/closings, or character flattening.

Do not turn checkpoint rehearsal into automatic line editing.

## 9. SUPPORTING CHARACTERS MAY CHOOSE GREG

A useful correction to Greg-centered causality is letting other people acquire opportunities first.

Examples:

- Jorren gets offered work because of Jorren's experience, then chooses Greg
- Meren requests Greg because previous work made him useful
- Arlo/Ivena make business decisions before Greg arrives
- Mara's employer changes her route without consulting Greg
- a local person correctly shuts down infrastructure before professionals arrive

Greg can still be protagonist without being the cause of every crossing.

## 10. CLEAN CHAINS DO NOT NEED SABOTAGE

Causal skepticism does not mean adding friction by quota.

Do not make every plan late, every contract unavailable, every letter delayed, or every successful decision complicated merely to prove the world is independent.

A clean chain can remain clean when its causes are credible.

The goal is not mess.

The goal is:

> **PEOPLE AND SYSTEMS ARRIVE FROM THEIR OWN LIVES.**

# SPRINT CHECKPOINT SHORTHAND

At the end of roughly 5–8 chapters, ask:

- **Did the world seem to know what Greg needed next?**
- **Which useful event would have happened without Greg?**
- **Who besides Greg earned or selected an opportunity?**
- **What newly established thing did we correctly leave alone?**
- **Did enough ordinary time pass without explanation?**
- **Did any rehearsal diagnosis leak into prose as meta language?**
- **Can the source beat the alternatives?**

Then use:

> **PRESUME SOURCE WIN. RECOMPOSE ONLY WHEN CAUSALITY CLEARLY IMPROVES.**

And return to:

> **WHAT SHOULD ACTUALLY HAPPEN NEXT?**
