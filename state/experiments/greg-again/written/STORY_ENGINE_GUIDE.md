# GREG, AGAIN — STORY ENGINE GUIDE

Status: **ACTIVE COMPACT STORY-SEARCH GUIDANCE / NON-CANON / NOT AN OUTLINE**

Purpose: give the local Greg, Again story engine a small set of useful positive priors without booting the full long-range rehearsal stack every chapter.

- `state/experiments/greg-again/written/LONG_RANGE_REHEARSAL_002_MAGIC_RANK.md`

Current selected prose and current written state outrank this guide.

This guide widens what may naturally win. It does not queue events.

## 1. LET GREG WIN SOMETIMES

Greg's future knowledge, old-life expertise, sword judgment, people-reading, fieldcraft, and magic are real advantages.

Do not make every successful use reveal that he was secretly wrong in a more interesting way.

Useful outcome mix across time:

- clean correct advantage
- correct fact with missing cause
- wrong chronology
- distorted later history
- present-world difference
- changed causality caused by Greg or somebody else
- domain Greg simply never knew well

A clean win may simply remain a win.

Do not append humiliation, hidden deficiency, or a compensating loss merely because the engine is trained to resist power fantasy.

The premise remains addictive only if Greg is sometimes unfairly useful.

## 2. LET GREG BECOME MAGICAL AGAIN

Current Greg has low reserve. That is one gate, not the whole magic system.

When magic is relevant, allow growth or competence to appear through different axes:

- precision
- shape
- formation speed
- duration
- range
- recovery
- movement while casting
- changing a construct after formation
- multiple simultaneous effects
- sensing / feedback
- counter-magic literacy
- integration with sword work, body mechanics, terrain, installed wards, artifacts, and teammates
- judgment about when not to cast

Do not expose these as stats or a curriculum.

Progress can change kind before it changes magnitude.

### Barrier is geometry, not only wall size

Preserve the useful R2 grammar:

> **PLANE. DIRECTION. LOAD.**

Barrier expertise can involve redirecting, bracing, guiding, isolating, distributing, anchoring, layering, moving, or changing force paths.

A five-inch construct used exactly right can matter more than a larger bad cast.

Long-term Barrier growth does not need to be `small wall -> bigger wall -> enormous wall`.

### Greg may use other magic

Do not trap Current Greg inside one tiny plane forever.

When the world naturally establishes other personal magic through practitioners, work, training, artifacts, creatures, or field necessity, Greg may learn, recover, adapt, or combine it.

Do not invent a complete magic-school taxonomy in advance.

Prefer:

> **SHOW PEOPLE DOING MAGIC FOR REAL PURPOSES BEFORE EXPLAINING THE SYSTEM.**

## 3. SHOW THE CEILING WITHOUT MAKING IT STUPID

Current rank evidence supports the working ordinary Guild ladder:

> **BRONZE -> SILVER -> GOLD -> PLATINUM**

Bronze, Silver, and Gold are already prose-supported. Platinum is working long-range architecture until prose further establishes or falsifies it.

Exceptional Ranker classification is separate:

> **C-RANKER -> B-RANKER -> A-RANKER -> S-RANKER**

A Ranker is not simply the next Guild promotion.

Formal Ranker status requires a title conferred by recognized high authority.

> **THE AUTHORITY NAMES THE RANKER. THE RANKER DOES NOT SELF-TITLE.**

Do not invent exact Crown bureaucracy, privileges, quotas, or jurisdiction until politics earn them.

### Gold should look excellent

Let Greg and the reader see genuinely impressive higher-rank adventurers before Greg approaches them.

Do not create fake elites for Greg to embarrass.

A good Gold-party showcase may reveal competence through:

- practiced spacing and angles
- magic embedded inside movement
- equipment modified by long use
- resource discipline
- quiet role handoffs
- professional retreat decisions
- preparation that removes problems before combat
- clean recovery after plan failure
- expensive specialist tools
- familiar team timing

The reader should be allowed to admire them.

Then Old Greg's later S-class scale becomes legible through comparison.

Young first-life Greg reached Silver as a warrior before Barrier changed his path. Old Greg later became one of seven S-class adventurers on the continent. There is enormous vertical room between those facts.

Do not rush Current Greg upward merely to demonstrate the old ceiling.

## 4. REPEAT PEOPLE BEFORE INVENTING PEOPLE

Current Greg is still often a flexible extra hand. That is fine.

But a multi-year adventure story should accumulate field relationships.

Before inventing a disposable fighter, warder, scout, driver, surveyor, healer, or contractor, ask whether an established person can naturally return with independent reasons to be there.

Do not force recurrence when geography, job, rank, or character desire argues against it.

Good party formation grammar:

> **WORK TOGETHER -> WORK TOGETHER AGAIN -> REQUEST ONE ANOTHER -> SHARE ROUTINES -> ACCEPT LONGER WORK -> REALIZE A PARTY EXISTS**

Do not assemble Tank / Healer / Rogue / Mage by quota.

A stable core plus rotating specialists may fit R2 better than a fixed game roster.

Jorren is a proven return possibility, not a reserved party slot.

Kesra is a future S-class peer, not a reserved party slot or friendship obligation.

Edda and other current field people may recur when their own work plausibly crosses Greg's again.

## 5. LET TEAM MAGIC BECOME SOCIAL

As contract difficulty rises, magic should increasingly interact with people rather than becoming only larger solo effects.

Useful high-level pressure:

- one person's timing assumes another person's movement
- support changes which risks a fighter can take
- counters anticipate enemy counters
- healers, scouts, locals, engineers, warders, artificers, and logistics alter magical options
- party familiarity reduces explanation and wasted motion
- artifacts create maintenance, handling, ownership, repair, and provenance needs

This is not a party buff system.

It is accumulated professional history becoming capability.

## 6. ARTIFACTS CHANGE OPTIONS, NOT JUST NUMBERS

Magical objects remain a strong engine when they alter what Greg can rationally attempt.

Prefer objects with real constraints or strange value:

- damaged
- obsolete
- specialist
- legally awkward
- cursed or socially dangerous
- expensive to operate
- misidentified
- poorly priced
- made before the maker became famous
- strong under narrow geometry / timing / environment
- powerful with an unacceptable drawback for most buyers

Future knowledge can create unfair value through maker marks, provenance, later-discovered uses, known defects, or historical context.

Let Greg sometimes get the bargain.

Over time, other people may learn that Greg's attention itself is market information.

Avoid +1 / +2 / +3 replacement grammar.

Let objects acquire years of history.

## 7. PEOPLE CAN EXIST BEFORE THEIR HISTORY

Greg may encounter people before later songs, titles, crimes, offices, victories, disasters, sainthood, or reputations have formed.

Later knowledge may come from:

- official history
- Ranker title
- song
- monument
- propaganda
- tavern story
- professional reputation
- personal memory

Do not assume later history is complete or morally correct.

A future-famous person is still a present person with independent wants and continuation.

## 8. LET THE WORLD GET BIGGER

R2 may plausibly span roughly 500 chapters across approximately 5–10 years.

Carrow may become home without becoming a cage.

Allow causally earned expansion into:

- roads
- rivers
- new towns and cities
- ruins
- underground places
- old infrastructure
- magical ecology
- monsters
- salvage
- strange markets
- dangerous travel
- different Guild cultures
- different magical practice

Do not travel merely to demonstrate map size.

Places matter when people, work, cost, route, culture, danger, memory, or relationship make them matter.

## 9. LONG TIME MEANS MULTIPLE REAL LIVES

Do not treat Greg's age nineteen state as the permanent operating system of the book.

Across years, let changes accumulate in:

- body
- skill
- magic
- work
- money
- ownership
- housing
- reputation
- Guild standing
- travel range
- equipment
- relationships
- party habits
- knowledge confidence
- what Greg considers normal

The story may contain multiple distinct eras of Greg's life without becoming a conventional act outline.

The permanent LEFT lower-leg loss with knee preserved remains the only hard long-range attractor.

Do not rush it.

Theatre remains strong post-loss gravity, not a required destination.

## 10. ORDINARY LIFE SURVIVES THE FANTASY

More adventure does not mean deleting the mundane accumulation engine.

Keep:

- food
- rooms
- letters
- wages
- repairs
- clothing
- sex and desire
- boring jobs
- neighbors
- equipment maintenance
- shopping
- transport
- recurring merchants
- bad sleep
- people doing things without Greg

Large fantasy is stronger when ordinary things acquire history.

## 11. MAGIC IS AN ICEBERG

Deep world/magic architecture lives in:

`state/experiments/greg-again/written/SEVEN_LAMPS_WORLD_ICEBERG.md`

The local writer should not explain that file.

Compact inheritance:

- human magical theory is incomplete even when practitioners are excellent
- different traditions may solve similar problems through different underlying magical principles
- current schools may be partial, hybrid, inherited, regionally specific, or theoretically wrong while practically effective
- Old Greg's expertise is real but does not exhaust magical reality
- magical beasts, artifacts, ruins, and foreign practices may exceed his conceptual map

Do not make Greg uniquely destined to discover the complete system.

Do not turn the Seven Lamps into seven visible elemental schools.

## 12. MAGICAL ECOLOGY IS NOT A POWER CHART

Magical beasts and phenomena may have habitat, diet, migration, mating, nesting, territory, symbiosis, seasonality, trade value, and relationships with human extraction or settlement.

A dangerous creature does not need a Guild-rank equivalent.

A weak creature may be economically or ecologically important.

A powerful creature may be avoidable rather than fightable.

Let creature knowledge sometimes give Greg a clean field advantage.

Let other creatures expose limits in later human theory.

## 13. OTHER PEOPLE'S MYTHS

R2 may translate causal/emotional structures from prior Hawaiian / moʻolelo-inspired quarry into its own invented cultures and geography.

Preserve shapes such as complicated retrievals, powerful hospitality, rare shapeshifters, water/place guardians, impossible construction, intimate conflicts with geographic consequence, misunderstood prohibitions, rival powers, difficult returns, and family/old-lover complications.

Do not copy Hawaiian surface markers merely to signal inspiration.

Most important:

> **GREG DOES NOT HAVE TO OWN THE MYTH.**

Greg may enter through work, travel, trade, shelter, salvage, a party obligation, a creature problem, or accident. He may help one part and leave. The people whose story it is continue without him.

Myth-scale material may be funny, petty, romantic, familial, practical, tragic, or dangerous. Do not make it automatically solemn.

## 14. MAGIC, MONEY, AND TRADE INTERLOCK

When earned, let magic affect:

- creature materials
- route premiums
- salvage rights
- artifact provenance
- appraisal
- repair and recharge
- transport restrictions
- legal status
- poaching / protection
- seasonal supply
- regional techniques
- cross-school mispricing
- storage
- ownership
- risk allocation

Greg may profit unfairly because he remembers later value.

He may also make a bad trade.

Markets do not owe him balance.

## 15. CAUSALITY PAYS BOTH DIRECTIONS

`LET GREG WIN` is a correction against compulsive nerfing, not a protection spell.

Use:

> **LET CAUSALITY PAY BOTH DIRECTIONS.**

Good decisions often help. Bad decisions often hurt. Good decisions can meet bad luck. Bad decisions can get lucky.

Greg's experience improves odds where it applies.

Do not staple a hidden punishment onto every success.

Do not rescue Greg from earned failure.
