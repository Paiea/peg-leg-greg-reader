# PLG Outward 4D — Epoch 2 Adversarial Test

**Status:** complete, derived-only, single discriminating investigation  
**Hypothesis under attack:** Greg's deeper developmental axis is access/usefulness versus ownership/maintenance.  
**Question:** What did Old Greg actually own, maintain, or remain responsible for after the danger ended?  
**Result:** **WEAKENED**  
**Canon firewall:** no manuscript, Showcase, publication, or story-authority writes  
**Exact manuscript chapters opened:** 2 (`315`, `320`)

## 1. Strongest evidence against

### Old Greg performed material repair himself

Chapter 315 gives the strongest direct falsifying evidence. When a ruined watch-post door retained a badly bounded protective ward, Greg's group did not merely summon a maintenance institution. After fire failed, **"we rebuilt enough wall around it to give the binding a stable inside and outside again."**

This is direct canon evidence that Old Greg:

- understood a practical system well enough to help produce a working remedy;
- participated in material reconstruction rather than functioning only as force;
- remained with the problem until the dangerous condition was stabilized.

This contradicts any broad version of the hypothesis where other people maintained nearly everything around him. It does **not** establish long-term stewardship: the repair ended the immediate field danger, and the group left the freestanding wall/door behind.

### Old Greg had durable institutional and social continuity

Chapter 320 remembers formal first-life portraits carrying **rank, armor, guild marks, and weapons**. Later, after S-class, people made images he never sat for. Chapter 315 says he had told the door story **for years**, usually to adventurers, and remembers named teammate Jalen inside a shared camp problem.

Together these weaken the image of Old Greg as merely a temporarily sponsored asset. He had:

- durable guild/rank identity;
- recurring professional community;
- remembered teammates;
- a reputation that persisted and reproduced without his presence;
- enough continuity for stories and images to outlast the original events.

The compact Character Bible also establishes decades of adult exposure and repeated field competence. Old Greg maintained at least his own body of practical knowledge across time.

### Financial arrogance may reflect successful self-provision

Current state says Greg understood Vale's brutal terms and assumed future earning power would rescue him. That can be explained by institutional insulation, but it can also reflect decades of actually earning and replacing resources through his own capability. Compact evidence cannot choose between them.

## 2. Strongest evidence for

### Canon distinguishes exposure from maintenance expertise

The Character Bible explicitly says Old Greg could spend decades benefiting from walls, plumbing, wards, transport, healing systems, civic infrastructure, and specialist equipment without knowing how they were built, maintained, priced, routed, regulated, or repaired. S-class experience grants systems intuition, not universal professional knowledge.

Chapter 315 fits that narrower distinction. Greg can diagnose the ward's functional problem and participate in an improvised field repair. It does not show him maintaining the watch post, owning it, administering it, or caring for it after the danger passed.

### Old-life ownership remains conspicuously unestablished

The targeted evidence produced:

- no owned house, estate, workshop, business, organization, transport system, or income-producing asset;
- no durable tool whose purchase, upkeep, replacement, or custody Greg personally managed;
- no dependents, employees, tenants, members, or community for whom he retained long-term responsibility;
- no return after a crisis to fund or maintain what remained.

Chapter 320 specifically remembers a sword placed in his image that he had **never owned**. Formal portraits show rank and patronage, but not ownership. Public representation persisted without his participation, which is evidence of reputation and access, not durable capital under his control.

Absence is not proof that Old Greg owned nothing. It means the pilot cannot promote lack of ownership into derived history.

### Current Greg's development is explicitly ownership-adjacent

By Chapter 500, current Greg has moved from debt and tiny cash work toward:

- Vale closed;
- silver-scale liquidity;
- paid acquisition access;
- a temporary Duskport base;
- purchased continuity and optionality;
- an intentionally empty paid shelf;
- bounded commitments that persist while he is elsewhere.

This remains a meaningful contrast with the old-life evidence currently visible, even though the old side is incomplete.

## 3. Ambiguities / missing evidence

1. **"We rebuilt" does not allocate labor.** Greg participated in the group solution, but canon does not say whether he lifted stone, designed the fix, directed others, paid someone, or merely shared responsibility.
2. **Emergency repair is not maintenance.** Chapter 315 ends an active hazard. It does not show post-danger stewardship.
3. **Guild marks and rank are relational, not proprietary.** They could indicate membership, employment, command, ownership, patronage, or public myth.
4. **Formal portraits imply resources but not durable capital.** Greg may have paid for some, received them, or had them commissioned by patrons/institutions.
5. **Professional community is not necessarily belonging.** Repeated adventurer storytelling and remembered teammates show continuity, but not a home, household, organization, or obligation.
6. **No evidence is not negative evidence.** Compact state was designed to preserve current writing constraints, not inventory every Old Greg asset.

## 4. Judgment

**WEAKENED.**

The strong version does not survive:

> Old Greg possessed access and status while other people maintained most durable machinery around him.

Canon now directly shows Old Greg participating in a material system repair and sustaining professional identity and community over years. The evidence does not justify casting him as a passively maintained weapon.

The deeper distinction still survives in a narrower form:

> **Current canon repeatedly separates Greg's field usefulness and institutional access from specialist expertise, durable ownership, and post-crisis stewardship. Old Greg demonstrably solved and repaired immediate field problems, but current evidence does not establish what he personally owned or maintained after those problems ended. Current Greg is now learning explicit, material forms of continuity and responsibility that the surviving old-life evidence has not yet shown.**

This is an open comparative axis, not a discovered Old Greg biography.

## 5. BEFORE implication

Keep both **Sponsored Spear** and **Road Without a Door** alive, but remove their assumption that Old Greg avoided maintenance. A better shared possibility is:

**Old Greg was an excellent field custodian who could stabilize whatever survival required, while his relationship to long-lived property, capital, institutions, and people after the crisis remained weak, delegated, or simply unobserved.**

That is a hypothesis, not canon. It cleanly distinguishes emergency responsibility from stewardship.

## 6. AFTER-500 implication

Do not treat institution-building as Greg's destined corrective arc. The discriminating future behavior is smaller:

**Will Greg maintain a paid relationship, asset, obligation, or shared system when it is not immediately useful to him and when somebody else must be trusted to operate it?**

A recurring Merrin registration, responsibly maintained equipment, shared capital, or an obligation surviving Greg's departure would test the axis more honestly than founding an organization.

## 7. Highest-information-value next question

> **Is there any accepted old-life memory where Greg returns after the danger, payment, or personal usefulness has ended and still pays for, maintains, governs, or remains answerable to a person, place, tool, or institution?**

One affirmative scene would materially weaken the refined distinction. Repeated absence across the small set of actual old-life memory scenes would still not prove he never did it, but would define the limit of what the manuscript has chosen to remember.
