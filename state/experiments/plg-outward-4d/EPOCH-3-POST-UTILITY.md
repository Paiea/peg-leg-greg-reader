# PLG Outward 4D — Epoch 3: Post-Utility Responsibility

**Status:** complete, derived-only, single falsification search  
**Question:** Did Old Greg remain responsible after danger, payment, or immediate personal usefulness ended?  
**Verdict:** **STRENGTHENED**  
**Canon firewall:** no manuscript, Showcase, publication, or story-authority writes  
**Exact manuscript chapters opened:** **2** (canonical Chapters 1 and 5)

## 1. Strongest falsifying evidence found

No qualifying post-utility counterexample was found.

The closest counterevidence remains Old Greg's sustained support practice:

- Chapter 5 says he spent decades making warriors better and learned their mechanics through amplifying them without tearing their bodies apart.
- He remembers lecturing people about recovery.
- Chapter 315, inherited from Epoch 2, shows his group rebuilding enough wall to stabilize a broken warded door.
- Chapter 320, inherited from Epoch 2, shows durable guild/rank identity, professional community, and reputation.

These establish repeated responsibility **during** work, danger, training, and team operation. They disprove a passive-serviced-weapon version of Old Greg.

They do not establish that he returned, paid, maintained, governed, supported, or remained answerable **after** his tactical usefulness or the immediate problem ended.

## 2. Why the candidate evidence does not qualify

### Decades of improving warriors

This is sustained professional practice, not necessarily post-utility stewardship. The warriors remained relevant to Greg's support role while he was making them better. Canon does not say he continued supporting them after the party, contract, campaign, or working relationship ended.

### Recovery lectures

Knowing recovery mattered and warning others is responsible behavior inside the performance system. The same Chapter 5 memory also says Greg magically forced people through failed recovery and watched the consequences. No later repair, care, compensation, or accountability is established.

### Rebuilding the warded door

The wall reconstruction ended an active hazard. It was emergency stabilization, explicitly excluded by this epoch's test unless Greg later returned or remained responsible. No such return is shown.

### Guild/rank continuity

Rank, guild marks, patrons, recurring adventurers, and persistent reputation show institutional connection. They do not prove ownership, administration, maintenance, or accountability after usefulness ended.

## 3. Strongest surviving evidence for the hypothesis

Chapter 1 contains the highest-value old-life self-audit found so far.

When Greg lists his mistakes, he remembers:

- **a party he abandoned;**
- **a contract he took for money while pretending it was strategy;**
- **a healer he pushed until she collapsed because he knew she could do more;**
- **a swordsman he kept angry because he fought better angry;**
- **people he lied to because the lie made them useful;**
- **people who died because they trusted him;**
- **people who survived because they trusted him.**

Greg's own conclusion is explicit: S-class Greg had not been a saint. He had been **useful**, and those were not the same thing. His methods worked often enough to become philosophy.

This does not prove Old Greg never later repaired harm or sustained obligations. It does directly establish that usefulness could override care, consent, honest motive, and continued responsibility in his old operating philosophy.

Chapter 1 also gives a revealing second-chance response. Faced with people who may still be alive, Greg asks whether he would tell, help, or use one of them. His immediate answer is that of course he would use him. The old utility reflex survives even while Greg recognizes it as ugly.

This materially strengthens the narrower inquiry. The missing evidence is no longer merely whether Old Greg owned property. Canon itself identifies **usefulness without moral equivalence** as a problem in how he treated people.

## 4. Important ambiguity / missing evidence

1. Greg's Chapter 1 list records harms and failures, not their complete aftermath.
2. “A party I abandoned” may hide later return, restitution, reconciliation, or continuing obligation.
3. The healer and swordsman memories do not say whether Greg later cared for, compensated, apologized to, or remained connected with them.
4. “People who survived because they trusted me” prevents flattening him into simple exploitation.
5. Decades of support work could include mentorship or long-term care not preserved in current compact state.
6. Old Greg's self-indictment may foreground guilt and omit ordinary responsibility that felt less narratively memorable.
7. No retrieved evidence about owned homes, businesses, dependents, durable tools, or governed organizations resolves the property/administration branch.

Absence remains: **canon has not shown post-utility responsibility**, not **Greg never practiced it**.

## 5. Verdict

**STRENGTHENED.**

No strong counterexample killed the refinement. The most discriminating exact memory instead shows Old Greg explicitly separating extreme usefulness from goodness and remembering cases where he abandoned, pushed, manipulated, or instrumentalized people.

However, Epoch 3 changes the center of gravity. Ownership and maintenance remain useful material tests, but the sharper distinction is:

**usefulness versus continuing accountability after usefulness ends.**

That conclusion is evidence-backed. A broader claim that Old Greg owned nothing or never maintained relationships is not.

## 6. Narrowest defensible formulation

> **Old Greg was capable of sustained support work, field repair, and repeated professional relationships. Canon also establishes that his operating philosophy sometimes treated people as useful systems and records abandonment, coercive pressure, instrumental anger, dishonest motives, and deaths tied to trust. Canon has not yet shown whether he returned after those useful relationships or crises ended to repair harm, sustain care, or remain accountable. Current Greg's accumulating obligations make post-utility responsibility a live contrast, not a settled biography.**

This is an analytical question, not a theme the manuscript must prove.

## 7. Implication for understanding Old Greg

Old Greg should not be modeled as a merely privileged adventurer who never maintained anything. He was an active maintainer of **performance**: barriers, bodies, teams, tactical capacity, and survival.

The unresolved fault line is what happened when performance was no longer required. Did his responsibility follow the person, or stop with the function?

## 8. Implication for interpreting Chapters 1–500

Current Greg's ordinary accumulation gains a more precise reading. The important movement is not simply acquiring money, tools, routes, and access. It is increasingly living inside consequences that continue when no immediate problem needs solving:

- Lyssa and Carrow remain real while he is in Duskport.
- Debt closure changes a relationship rather than ending all connection.
- Paid infrastructure persists between uses.
- Expert relationships retain their own authority and schedules.
- Belonging consists partly of expectations and obligations that cannot be optimized away.

This reading should remain observational. Chapters 1–500 do not need to be retrofitted into proof of a master theme.

## 9. Implication for possible Future Greg

The highest-value future test is not whether Greg founds an institution or owns impressive assets.

It is whether he voluntarily remains answerable when:

- the emergency is over;
- he is no longer the indispensable expert;
- there is no immediate profit, prestige, or tactical advantage;
- somebody else owns the work;
- repair requires time, money, apology, delegation, or trust rather than brilliance.

A Future Greg who can leave a system functioning without centering himself and still accept responsibility for its consequences would represent a real change. It is only a possibility.

## 10. Highest-information-value next question

Another epoch is justified only if exact evidence can be located cheaply:

> **What happened after the party Greg says he abandoned?**

That single aftermath could falsify or sharply strengthen the post-utility distinction. If canon shows return, restitution, long-term support, or continuing accountability, the hypothesis weakens. If canon shows no return or deliberate severance, it strengthens. Do not open an Epoch 4 without a compact pointer to that specific memory.
