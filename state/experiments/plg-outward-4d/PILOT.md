## BEFORE: three old-life candidates

### B1 — The Sponsored Spear

**Status:** SUPPORTED POSSIBILITY  
Old Greg became the decisive field asset inside a wealthy expeditionary, guild, crown, or patronage apparatus. He had immense access to logistics, healing, equipment, transport, and specialists, but did not own or maintain most of the infrastructure around him.

- **Explains:** broad systems literacy paired with non-expert trade knowledge; comfort with institutional procedure; expectation that future earning power can solve present financing; current fixation on what he actually owns.
- **Predicts in observed Greg:** he notices what systems can do before noticing who maintains them; he is comfortable negotiating scope but sometimes mistakes access for durable personal capacity.
- **Would contradict it:** canon establishing that Old Greg personally owned, financed, and maintained his support network or lived chiefly outside institutions.
- **Unsupported assumptions:** a patron/institution, its form, and Greg’s exact rank are invented.

### B2 — The Road Without a Door

**Status:** LIVE POSSIBILITY  
Old Greg was a high-value independent contractor who moved from crisis to crisis, accumulated reputation more than property, and treated lodging, teams, and relationships as temporary operating conditions.

- **Explains:** strong fieldcraft; adaptability across places; ease with temporary Duskport infrastructure; why ordinary Carrow routes and being expected by people carry unusual weight.
- **Predicts in observed Greg:** he can become operationally useful quickly but is slower to recognize when a place or relationship has become home; he may keep optional exits even while belonging.
- **Would contradict it:** repeated canon memory of a durable old-life household, permanent organization, or long-rooted civic role.
- **Unsupported assumptions:** near-total itinerancy and lack of durable home are not established.

### B3 — The House He Chose Against

**Status:** WILD BRANCH  
Old Greg once had a partner, family, or chosen household and lost it partly because he repeatedly chose rank, danger, or usefulness over ordinary presence. The loss need not be death; estrangement is more distinct and potentially more painful.

- **Explains:** current sensitivity to ordinary domestic belonging, Lyssa’s independence, and the danger of turning relationships into support systems.
- **Predicts in observed Greg:** under pressure he may default to solving/providing rather than remaining present, then overvalue practical usefulness as proof of love.
- **Would contradict it:** canon memories showing no durable intimate household, or showing Old Greg consistently chose presence over status.
- **Unsupported assumptions:** the people, relationship, rupture, and Greg’s responsibility are wholly invented. This risks over-explaining his current attachment and must remain wild.

## AFTER: three post-500 trajectories

### A1 — The Two-Harbor Broker

**Status:** SUPPORTED POSSIBILITY  
Greg remains rooted in Carrow while building a recurring Duskport acquisition and coordination practice. He owns access, records, options, and selected inventory rather than chasing wages or becoming a conventional merchant prince.

- **Supported by:** silver-scale liquidity; Merrin registration/digest; Anchor’s End resident infrastructure; improving contracting references; Carrow home and Lyssa’s independent life.
- **Requires:** credible acquisition latency, repeatable margins, disciplined capital use, honest separation between Greg’s competence and specialist certification.
- **Unlikely if:** he fills every empty capacity, treats every lead as plot destiny, or Duskport instantly rewards him.
- **Deliberately speculative:** scale, ownership form, and whether the practice lasts.

### A2 — The Builder of Other People’s Capacity

**Status:** LIVE POSSIBILITY  
Greg eventually owns or co-owns a small institution that makes dangerous-world competence more accessible: acquisition brokerage, adaptive equipment, route-response services, or a hybrid operation. His achievement is not regaining S-class power but building durable capability that does not disappear when he leaves the room.

- **Supported by:** systems curiosity; Hark & Venn embodied learning; capital-as-optionality; coordination skill; repeated respect for bounded expertise; concern with ownership.
- **Requires:** learning maintenance, payroll/obligation, shared authority, and the difference between creating capacity and controlling people.
- **Unlikely if:** he remains addicted to being the irreplaceable solver or the story turns specialists into his employees by protagonist gravity.
- **Deliberately speculative:** the institution, partners, mission, and endpoint.

### A3 — The Man Who Does Not Ascend

**Status:** WILD BRANCH  
Greg never returns to elite power or large authority. He becomes materially secure, physically capable on his own terms, intermittently theatrical, locally useful, and deeply embedded in a dense ordinary life. The world changes because many people can rely on him a little, not because everyone must rely on him completely.

- **Supported by:** accumulation/belonging north star; theatre’s intrinsic value; bounded magic; ordinary physical adaptation; relationships with independent lives.
- **Requires:** Greg accepting that a smaller visible life is not failure, while the narrative still supplies pressure and change.
- **Unlikely if:** unresolved external threats or old-life obligations demand large-scale authority, or Greg’s desire for reach proves irreducible.
- **Deliberately speculative:** permanent refusal of ascent and long-range world state.

## One cheap bidirectional collision

**Mode:** counterfactual branch comparison at probe fidelity. No PERFORMANCE.

B1 makes A2 more plausible but also more demanding. If Old Greg benefited from an apparatus he neither owned nor understood deeply, building an institution now is not a return to former mastery. It is a new moral and practical education in maintenance, shared authority, capital, and obligations that persist when the star performer leaves.

A2 exposes the best backward question: **What did Old Greg actually own, maintain, or remain responsible for after the danger ended?** The known middle repeatedly separates access, income, usefulness, and ownership. That distinction may be the missing axis of his old life.

A3 makes B3 worth testing but does not support it. A future chosen around presence raises the possibility that Old Greg once failed at presence. The middle supplies a recurring tension between solving and belonging, but not enough evidence for a lost spouse/family. B3 remains wild.

## Retired / contradicted branches

### R1 — Old Greg, Universal Self-Made Master

**Status:** CONTRADICTED  
Retire the attractive version where Old Greg personally understood, built, financed, maintained, and commanded every support system his S-class life used.

The known middle repeatedly establishes the opposite discipline: broad exposure is not trade expertise; observation is not mechanism; capability is not specialist authority; useful coordination is not ownership. This branch would erase the most productive distinction in Greg rather than explain him.

### R2 — Future Greg Simply Regains S-Class Centrality

**Status:** CONTRADICTED as a direct continuation, not impossible after major transformation  
A straight power-restoration future ignores bounded magic, physical adaptation, distributed authority, independent relationships, and the story’s accumulation engine. A later high-power Greg would require substantial earned transformation and must not replace ordinary life.

## Unresolved questions

1. What did Old Greg actually own rather than merely command, access, or receive?
2. Who handled maintenance, money, recovery, and logistics around him?
3. Did Old Greg have a durable home or only durable reputation?
4. Was his financial arrogance learned from reliable high earnings, institutional insulation, or repeated rescue by others?
5. When current Greg says independence, does he mean freedom from leverage, freedom from need, or the ability to choose dependence?
6. Can he build something that remains useful without making himself its center?
7. Would refusing renewed elite status feel like peace, fear, or failure to him?

## Pilot evaluation

- **Non-obvious discovery:** yes. The strongest axis is not past/future power. It is **access versus ownership and maintenance**. Current Greg may be learning forms of responsibility Old Greg’s status let him bypass.
- **Did the known middle constrain invention?** yes. It strongly retired universal-mastery backstory and made instant heroic restoration expensive. It favored futures that preserve distributed authority, material reality, and independent relationships.
- **Did backward and forward reasoning interact?** yes. The institution-building future produced the sharpest old-life question, while the sponsored-asset past turned that future from repetition into transformation.
- **Signal for a larger run?** yes, but narrowly. The next experiment should deepen only the ownership/support-system question using a few targeted old-life memories and money/authority scenes, then rerun one collision. Do not scale to all Chapters 1–500.
