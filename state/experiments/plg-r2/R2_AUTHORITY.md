# PEG-LEG GREG R2 — DISCOVERY AUTHORITY

Status: **ACTIVE EXPERIMENT / NON-CANON**

Branch: `experiment/plg-r2-opening`

Source evidence: current Peg-Leg Greg canon, current project state, Story Quarry material, Writing Lab craft evidence, and derived REHEARSAL/PERFORMANCE artifacts.

Final PLG canon remains untouched unless a later explicit promotion decision is made.

## Core premise

R2 is not a line edit and not a preservation-first rewrite.

It is a new performance of Greg's life using everything learned since the first run.

```text
CANON = EVIDENCE + PROVEN STORY MATERIAL
R2 = NEW SEARCH THROUGH THAT MATERIAL
REHEARSAL = ALLOWED TO FIND A BETTER STORY
```

The old trajectory is a strong prior, not a railroad.

R2 may preserve, compress, expand, reorder, merge, replace, or abandon old events when the new run earns a stronger causal trajectory.

A divergence is not a defect merely because canon did something else.

## Locked starting truths

Keep these unless later creator authority explicitly changes them:

- Greg wakes physically nineteen with decades of later-life memory.
- In the first life he eventually became an S-class support specialist, one of the strongest people on the continent.
- He was not secretly an elite swordsman.
- His old-life memory is incomplete, reconstructive, and sometimes wrong in useful ways.
- Greg is intelligent, analytical, observant, cocky, curious, and prone to overbuilding models.
- His intelligence creates both leverage and waste.
- He wants to use the second life rather than merely reenact the first.
- First-person Greg POV remains the primary prose mode.

Everything else should remain open to pressure where useful.

## Story-discovery rule

The run should repeatedly ask:

> Given what is true now, what story is actually trying to happen?

Do not ask only:

> How do we reproduce the next canon chapter better?

Old chapters may supply:

- characters
- outcomes
- mechanisms
- jokes
- relationships
- institutions
- objects
- scenes
- later consequences
- hidden useful setup

But each imported element must earn its place in the current R2 trajectory.

## Greg's early sword model

Greg begins as a beginner swordsman with unusual observational leverage.

His edge comes from decades spent around elite fighters, monsters, tactics, support work, positioning, fatigue, fear, equipment, and high-level failure.

That gives him better hypotheses and questions.

It does not give him automatic sword execution.

```text
OLD-LIFE KNOWLEDGE = HYPOTHESIS
CURRENT-LIFE EXPERIENCE = EVIDENCE
PRACTICE = CAPABILITY
```

Greg may see the correct answer and physically fail to perform it.

He may also remember one tiny observation from decades ago that becomes extremely useful when attached to a skill he has actually earned.

## Narrow early sword progression

For the first meaningful training block Greg deliberately focuses on only two reliable movements:

1. **overhead slash**
2. **side step**

He can attempt or understand other movements, but R2 should not treat them as dependable skills yet.

The absurdity is useful: another beginner may possess a broader curriculum while Greg becomes strangely good at two basic things.

Do not show every repetition.

Use elapsed time.

Show an early failure, a meaningful correction, then later visible evidence that hundreds of unseen repetitions occurred.

Later techniques should usually enter because lived problems demand them.

## Greg must remain Greg

Do not optimize him into a rational machine.

Useful recurring pattern:

```text
OBSERVE TOO MUCH
-> BUILD MODEL
-> GET COCKY / OVERCOMPLICATE
-> ACT
-> REALITY RETURNS RUDE DATA
-> UPDATE
-> SOMETIMES OVERCORRECT
-> SIMPLIFY / PRACTICE
-> ACCUMULATE CURRENT-LIFE EVIDENCE
```

This applies to swordsmanship, money, gambling, magic, work, relationships, accessibility, business, and future knowledge.

Being intelligent is not the same as being wise.

R2 progression should improve Greg's calibration rather than removing his appetite, curiosity, or tendency to create too many possible solutions.

## Time

R2 treats time as causal story movement.

```text
TIME = CHANGE WHILE WATCHED + CHANGE WHILE UNWATCHED
```

Do not use time skips as decoration or quota.

Elapsed time can appear through changed state:

- a practiced movement becoming automatic
- money accumulated or burned
- equipment wear
- healed or persistent injury
- relationships acquiring habits
- people no longer waiting
- a rival improving elsewhere
- institutions changing process
- prices shifting
- jobs or neighborhoods evolving
- jokes becoming old

Time may reward Greg or punish delay.

## Action

R2 should carry more fantasy/action energy than the first performance without becoming combat-only.

Action includes fighting, training, monsters, contracts, magic failure, sex/intimacy, work, pursuit, gambling, negotiation, logistics, movement, and thought when thought changes the next available state under pressure.

Fantasy peaks should recur often enough that the reader remembers the genre promise.

Quiet/mundane chapters remain central. They become stronger when unresolved external momentum already exists around them.

## Character pressure

Greg owns POV, not causality.

Recurring characters need independent wants, off-camera motion, leverage, limits, and the ability to start or redirect story.

Generated names must be checked against the active roster for spelling/sound/rhythm collisions.

Characters may become more or less important than they were in canon.

A minor canon character may become central if PERFORMANCE keeps producing signal.

A canon-important character may shrink if the new trajectory does not need them.

## Rival hypothesis

A rival is useful because rivalry externalizes progression and time.

Do not default to a mirror-image warrior rival.

Current candidate to test: **Sella**, an existing guild clerk whose future institutional competence already gives her a trajectory independent of Greg.

Possible accidental-rival engine:

- guild staff maintain some basic emergency/self-defense competency
- Greg and Sella encounter some of the same basic drills for different reasons
- Greg has theory, ambition, and ego
- Sella follows boring instruction consistently
- Greg begins measuring himself against her before she agrees a competition exists
- rivalry can expand beyond combat into execution, systems, reputation, and who actually makes institutions work

This is **not locked**.

Jorren may remain a valuable sparring partner without being the primary rival.

If REHEARSAL produces a better rival, no rival, multiple rivalries, or an entirely different pressure relationship, follow the stronger evidence.

## Story Quarry portability

Recovered ideas are parts, not automatically books.

A quarry item may become:

- a whole-book engine
- an arc
- an institution
- a relationship dynamic
- an economic mechanism
- a magic rule
- one scene problem
- one joke

Example: Crab Empire may work better as a PLG economic arc in which a low-status commodity acquires prestige, scarcity, regulation, jobs, and false historical inevitability over time.

Import only what creates native PLG consequences.

## Detail / camera policy

Expand what changes the lived state.

Compress repeated delivery of already-established information.

Do not show every practice session, workday, meal, transaction, or incremental improvement merely to prove continuity.

Let larger gains be earned by elapsed time when the underlying routine has already been established.

Protect distinctive mundane texture. Compress repeated delivery of mundane texture.

## R2 opening search target

The opening should test a stronger balance of:

- second-life wonder and grief
- Greg's cocky assumption that prior S-class experience makes restarting easier than it actually is
- early fantasy/warrior promise
- narrow earned sword progression
- money pressure and miscalibration
- future knowledge as fallible leverage
- strong supporting-character agency
- explicit or implicit elapsed time
- at least one concrete external deadline / reward / consequence

Do not plan the entire book before writing.

Write enough lived story to discover what the next story question actually is.

## Acceptance rule

A new R2 scene survives because it makes the current trajectory more alive, causally rich, character-specific, and readable.

It does **not** survive merely because:

- canon contained it
- it is clever
- it demonstrates a system feature
- it preserves a favorite old idea
- it is more traditional
- it is more unusual

The story gets to find the story.
