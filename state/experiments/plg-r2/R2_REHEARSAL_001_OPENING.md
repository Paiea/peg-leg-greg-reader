# PLG R2 — REHEARSAL 001: OPENING TRAJECTORY

Status: **derived experiment / non-canon**

## Locked state in

Greg wakes physically nineteen with later-life memories intact enough to know he eventually became S-class support.

He is not an elite swordsman. His body is young, Bronze-level, and not trained to execute the high-level movement he spent decades watching.

He has little money, weak current magic access, an old cheap sword, and a city that is recognizably Carrow but decades earlier than the one he remembers.

His parents are already dead. The reset is not a total wish-fulfillment reset.

## Opening uncertainty

The first performance spent substantial camera time verifying the premise, then moved through future-knowledge investment, borrowing, gambling, sword training, debt labor, and broader capability accumulation.

R2 asks whether the opening becomes stronger if the same ingredients overlap sooner and if fantasy/warrior promise appears earlier.

## Three trajectories tested

### A. Money-first preservation

Greg verifies the reset, immediately monetizes future knowledge, then earns the right to pursue training later.

Strengths:

- close to proven canon
- clear debt clock
- strong PLG economics identity

Risks:

- delays fantasy/action promise again
- repeats the original camera rhythm
- warrior desire remains conceptual for too long

### B. Warrior-first correction fantasy

Greg verifies the reset and immediately throws himself into sword training, convinced decades of S-class exposure let him repair his failed first-life warrior path.

Strengths:

- clear fantasy promise
- physicalizes the old-life/current-body contradiction immediately
- naturally introduces narrow deliberate practice

Risks:

- can flatten PLG into conventional training fantasy
- money pressure becomes secondary instead of integrated
- risks making swordsmanship too central to Greg's identity

### C. Collision trajectory

Greg verifies enough to act, goes to the guild because it is the fastest place to test present reality, and immediately collides with three constraints:

1. he wants to move and test the warrior path again
2. training costs money he does not have
3. his future knowledge offers possible leverage, but memory is not precise enough to function as free capital

He deliberately chooses two sword movements to practice while simultaneously trying to finance a life that can sustain training and eventually magic.

The lanes interfere with one another from the beginning rather than waiting politely for separate chapters.

Strengths:

- preserves PLG's money/systems identity
- makes fantasy promise visible earlier
- turns time into a progression tool because training can continue off-camera while economic scenes happen
- creates opportunity cost between training, money, magic, work, and relationships
- gives Greg multiple ways to overthink and miscalibrate

Risks:

- opening can become overstuffed
- too many recurring characters introduced too quickly will weaken character memory
- if every lane produces a deadline, story may feel mechanically plotted

## Selected for PERFORMANCE

**C. Collision trajectory.**

Selection is provisional. It is chosen because it gives the new TIME + ACTION + CHARACTER pressure systems something real to do without discarding the proven economic engine.

## Opening cast budget

Keep recurring-name load low.

Primary early names:

- Greg
- Sella, guild clerk, current accidental-rival hypothesis only
- Jorren, ordinary Bronze sparring/training relationship
- Antonius Vale, lender / emerging economic pressure
- Arlo, artificer if the shale hypothesis survives prose

Delay or omit other named characters until the story earns them.

## Rival test

Do not announce Sella as a rival.

If used, seed the relationship through ordinary guild behavior:

- she has required emergency/self-defense practice
- Greg sees her perform one basic movement correctly because she followed instruction instead of theorizing
- Greg privately begins using her as a benchmark
- Sella may remain mostly indifferent

If the dynamic does not become interesting on the page, drop it.

## Sword constraint

Greg's chosen early practice set:

- overhead slash
- side step

He is allowed to know other theory and to attempt other movements badly.

The story should establish repetition, then turn the camera away long enough for practice to accumulate.

Later visible competence must be earned by elapsed routine, not by old-life memory suddenly becoming muscle memory.

## Time target

Within the first opening movement, allow at least one meaningful multi-week gap.

The gap must leave residue in more than one lane, for example:

- overhead slash becomes cleaner
- Greg has less money than expected despite earning more
- Arlo's slow experiment changes shape without Greg supervising every test
- Sella acquires a routine or responsibility independent of Greg
- Jorren changes his opinion of Greg
- Antonius's leverage grows because Greg made several individually defensible decisions

Do not label all elapsed days.

## Action target

Within the opening run, include embodied fantasy pressure before the reader has to wait through a long economic-only sequence.

Possible forms:

- training that genuinely hurts/fails
- sparring
- a low-level Bronze contract
- a monster encounter where Greg's two reliable movements matter but do not solve everything

Choose from what the prose naturally earns.

## Exit condition

Stop planning once enough prose exists that a stronger next question emerges.

The next question should come from surviving behavior, not from this document.
