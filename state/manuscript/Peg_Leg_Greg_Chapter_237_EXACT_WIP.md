# CHAPTER 237

## THE CONDITION

Rinna had said no.

Vale had said maybe nothing.

Hessa had said nothing at all.

This produced a morning in which I was technically free and emotionally suspicious.

Lyssa was already gone when I woke. The brown cloth remained near the wall with the same fold and the same three pins. My damaged shirt remained on the shelf with the same three loose threads because I had finally discovered the repair method called leaving it alone.

I ate bread and one of the remaining onions.

The onions were winning the long war.

No note waited under the door.

I checked once.

Then I stopped checking because doing it twice would have become a habit and I already had enough of those.

Outside, Carrow had the clear cold light that made every wet stain on the stone look permanent. A cooper was rolling two empty barrels toward the lower lane. A woman had three chickens tied together by the legs and was losing an argument with all of them.

I went for breakfast that was not an onion.

This was my only plan.

I found a stall selling hot grain with chopped apple and bought a bowl. The woman gave me a wooden spoon with a crack down the handle.

I looked at it.

She said, "It holds."

"Strong standard."

"It holds."

I ate with it.

It held.

When I came home, a folded piece of paper was under the door.

Apparently timing remained a form of mockery.

I put the bowl down on the table and picked up the note.

Hessa's writing.

AFTER MIDDAY.

SAME ROOM.

EAT.

FOUR INTERMEDIATE.

I read the last line again.

Then I turned the paper over.

Nothing.

I looked toward the brown cloth.

"At least you are consistent."

The cloth declined comment.

Four intermediate.

No far.

No new mark.

No new direction written.

If Hessa had decided to invent a secret fifth distance, she had hidden it efficiently.

I did not try to design the session for her.

This was growth and also self-defense.

I spent the rest of the morning doing ordinary things badly enough to remain a person.

I washed the bowl from breakfast.

I swept half the room before discovering the broom was moving dust into the same corner the draft preferred.

I moved it somewhere else.

The draft won again.

I stopped.

I ate more bread before leaving because Hessa had written EAT in capital letters and I had no interest in sitting through the questions if I arrived hungry.
