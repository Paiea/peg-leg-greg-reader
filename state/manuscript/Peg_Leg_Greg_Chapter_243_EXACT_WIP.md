# CHAPTER 243

## THE SAMPLE

Hessa's note was under the door.

This improved the morning and made it more expensive.

Not in money.

In attention.

I picked it up before I put weight on the left crutch and read it standing.

AFTER MIDDAY.

SAME ROOM.

FAR ONLY.

EAT FIRST.

I read the third line again.

FAR ONLY.

The last session had been INTERMEDIATE four times in a row. One response. Three non-responses. Clean control every time.

Apparently Hessa had chosen what came next.

Lyssa was already gone.

The brown cloth remained near the wall.

Three pins.

Same fold.

My damaged shirt remained on the shelf.

Three loose threads.

The beans remained absent. The small paper-wrapped thing remained absent. Hara's shoe was somewhere in Carrow attached to Hara, which was the correct place for it and no longer my concern.

I put Hessa's note beside the salt jar.

Then moved it away from the salt jar because yesterday had taught me that salt was capable of acquiring obligations.

I ate bread and the last of the cheese.

EAT FIRST had become a recurring insult only because Hessa knew me.

Fair.

There was no theatre call.

No Vale runner.

No reason to invent work before midday because a note had already given the day a shape.

I washed the bowl, checked the crutch tip, and spent part of the morning doing almost nothing.

That was harder than it sounded.

I sharpened the kitchen knife a little better than last time. I stopped before improvement became damage. I sat by the window. A man across the street tried to lower a rolled rug from an upper window with a rope, discovered the knot was not attached to the rug, and had to come downstairs anyway.

Carrow provided education freely.

By late morning I was hungry again.

I ate another piece of bread.

Hessa would have been unbearable if I arrived hungry after being told twice in four lines not to.

The walk to her room felt familiar enough that I stopped thinking about each turn.

That was useful.

The room still did not feel familiar.

Hessa had the board set where it belonged. The glass shield was in place. The target and control fins were on their marks. The chair stood on the same floor lines. INTERMEDIATE and FAR remained marked where they had been.

Nothing new had appeared because I was curious.

Hessa was seated at the table reading her own notes.

She looked up.

"Eat?"

"Twice."

"Good."

"I dislike that word now."

"Sit."

That was worse.

I put my crutches where they usually went and sat.

My body baseline was ordinary. Shoulders fine. Right hand fine. Right leg fine. Residual limb comfortable. Left crutch tip still boring.

Hessa looked at me for several seconds.

"What is the question?"

I looked at FAR.

"You tell me."

"I wrote it down."

"You wrote FAR ONLY."

"Yes."

"That is a condition, not a question."

One corner of her mouth moved.

Possibly approval.

Possibly gas.

"Then answer."

I looked at the marks again.

Last time, four INTERMEDIATE attempts. Same direction. Same apparatus. One target response.

Before that, the alternating sessions had shown INTERMEDIATE responding more often than FAR under the current setup, though not reliably. FAR had responded cleanly before. It had also failed plenty.

I said, "You held intermediate fixed because alternating might have been hiding something about sequence."

"Close enough."

"Now you hold far fixed."

"Why?"

"To see whether clean FAR response still occurs when we stop alternating it with INTERMEDIATE."

"Better."

I waited.

"That is the question?"

"Yes."

It felt offensively small.

Which usually meant Hessa liked it.

"So not range."

"No."

"Not finding the farthest point."

"No."

"Not changing distance."

"No."

"Not changing direction."

"No."

"Not increasing effort."

"No."

"Not testing whether I can make FAR behave like INTERMEDIATE."

Hessa looked at me.

"Especially not that."

"Good. I wanted the full collection."

She slid the record page toward herself.

"Same room. Same board. Same shield. Same target and control. Same body geometry. FAR. AWAY."

"Four?"

"Four."

"Break after two?"

"Yes."

The shape mirrored the last session.

That was the point.

Hessa checked the floor marks anyway.

I knew better than to be offended by this now.

Memory was not measurement.

Neither was familiarity.

She checked the glass, then the target and control. I watched her confirm what I thought I already knew.

Nothing had shifted.

"Minimal first," she said.

I placed my right forearm where she wanted it.

No shortcuts.

No independent draw because I had done this thirty-four times under supervision and could imagine the shape of the next ten.

That did not make the thirty-fifth mine.

Hessa watched.

I drew.

Clean.

Small.

Controlled.

No strange symptom. No pain. No movement outside the expected body work.

Hessa marked it.

Thirty-five supervised minimal draws.

Then deliberate shaping.

I held the form she had allowed, narrow and familiar, with no expansion and no experiment inside the experiment.

The first few seconds felt too easy.

That made me suspicious.

I corrected nothing because nothing had actually gone wrong.

The old urge was still there, to improve a success while it was happening.

I let it be what it was.

Hessa marked the completion.

Thirty-one supervised deliberate shaping attempts.

Then she reset the page.

"External."

FAR.

AWAY.

Attempt fifty.

I settled into the same position.

The far target looked small enough that my eyes wanted to make it important.

I did not.

Hessa gave the start.

I worked.

Nothing moved.

Target still.

Control still.

No twitch I could defend.

Hessa waited the usual amount of time before checking.

"No observed response," she said.

"Control unchanged."

She marked both.

Attempt fifty-one.

Same condition.

Same direction.

Same everything we could reasonably keep the same.

I expected less this time.

That was information about me, not the apparatus.

I did not tell the target.

Hessa gave the start.

I worked.

Nothing.

Again.

Target still.

Control unchanged.

Two FAR attempts.

Two clean non-responses.

Hessa marked them and closed the record page.

"Break."

"I feel fine."

"Good."

I stared at her.

She stared back.

I stood.

My right leg had begun to stiffen from sitting, not enough to matter and enough to exist. I took the crutches and walked the short permitted circuit inside the room.

Hessa poured water.

For herself.

I looked at the cup.

She drank.

"This profession lacks hospitality."

"You ate twice."

"That was at home."

"Successful planning."

I sat again after the stiffness eased.

She was already looking at the page.

I tried not to.

Failed.

"Two nothing."

"Yes."

"Which matches a great deal of FAR."

"Yes."

"But not all of it."

"No."

I looked at the far mark.

"What would four nothing mean?"

"Four nothing."

"You enjoy this too much."

"No."

"You answer too quickly for someone suffering."

Hessa set the cup down.

"If all four are clean non-responses, that adds four clean non-responses at FAR under this fixed condition."

"And if one moves?"

"Then one moves."

"Profound."

"You keep asking before the record exists."

That was fair.

Annoying.

We waited the rest of the break.

Hessa rechecked my hand and forearm.

Normal warmth from work, no pain, no weakness, no tingling.

Residual limb comfortable.

Right leg better after walking.

"Continue?"

"Yes."

She reopened the page.

Attempt fifty-two.

FAR.

AWAY.

I knew exactly how little I was supposed to change.

That knowledge did not make sameness easy.

The body always wanted to help.

Shoulder slightly higher.

Breath held a little longer.

Hand tightening because the last two had done nothing.

I noticed the hand first.

I loosened it before the start.

Hessa saw.

"Ready?"

"Now."

She gave the start.

I worked.

For a moment, nothing.

Then the far paper fin moved.

Small.

Clean enough that I did not need to argue with my eyes.

The target shifted AWAY and settled.

No obvious rotation that I could see.

The control remained still.

I stopped because the attempt had ended, not because I wanted to stare at it until it became a theory.

Hessa was already checking.

She looked at the target mark.

Then the control.

Then the glass.

Then me.

"Small response," she said.

"Net away."

"Control unchanged."

I exhaled.

Hessa marked it.

I smiled.

She noticed.

"Don't."

"I said nothing."

"You were about to."

Apparently everyone in Carrow had learned this technique.

"FAR moved."

"Yes."

"Clean."

"Under the current apparatus, yes."

"Again."

"No."

"I meant the fact."

"I know."

I looked at the page.

Attempt fifty-two.

A clean FAR response.

Not the first.

Not reliable.

Still real.

"One out of three."

Hessa said nothing.

Right.

There was still another attempt.

Attempt fifty-three.

This was where my mind became unhelpful.

The target had moved once.

Now I expected it to move again.

Or I expected the expectation to ruin it.

Or I expected myself to think about expecting it, which was worse.

Hessa watched me.

"You're doing something."

"Thinking."

"Stop doing it with your face."

"That seems outside the experimental restrictions."

"Ready?"

I looked at my hand.

Loose.

Shoulder down.

Breathing normal enough.

"Yes."

Start.

I worked.

Nothing.

The target remained still.

The control remained still.

No second response arrived to make the first easier to explain.

Hessa checked.

"No observed response."

"Control unchanged."

She marked attempt fifty-three.

Then she put the pencil down.

I looked at the page.

Four FAR attempts.

One response.

Three non-responses.

I laughed once.

Hessa looked at me.

"What?"

"That's rude."

"What is?"

"Last session."

She waited.

"Four INTERMEDIATE. One response."

"Yes."

"Today. Four FAR. One response."

"Yes."

I pointed between the two pages.

"Same."

"No."

The answer came so fast I nearly laughed again.

"One out of four."

"Yes."

"One out of four."

"Yes."

"Those are extremely similar fractions."

"They are the same fraction."

"Thank you."

"They are not the same record."

I leaned back.

"Explain the insult."

Hessa pulled the older sheets closer.

"Because these four attempts do not erase the attempts before them."

"I know that."

"You are trying to make them."

"I am trying to admire symmetry."

"Admire it quietly."

She ran one finger down the earlier marks.

INTERMEDIATE responses across more than one session.

FAR responses too, but fewer in the accumulated current-apparatus record.

Clean non-responses in both.

A contaminated attempt where target and control had both moved, still counted as an attempt and not counted as clean target-specific evidence.

Different orders.

Now one fixed INTERMEDIATE block.

One fixed FAR block.

Hessa said, "If I take only the last eight attempts because they make a neat pair, I have selected the part of the record I like."

"One and one."

"Inside two groups of four."

"Exactly."

"Inside a longer record."

I sighed.

"Fractions do not get to be beautiful here."

"They can be beautiful. They cannot outrank the rest of the page."

That was probably the nicest thing Hessa had ever said about fractions.

I looked again at attempt fifty-two.

"So what changed?"

"FAR produced another clean observed response."

"Which establishes?"

"That FAR response can recur under the current apparatus."

I waited.

"Reliably?"

"No."

"Range?"

"No."

"Distance stops mattering?"

"No."

"INTERMEDIATE and FAR are equivalent?"

"No."

"One in four?"

"In this block."

"Not a rate."

"Correct."

I hated being trained this well.

"The apparent distance difference?"

"Still supported by the accumulated record."

"Even though the two fixed blocks match."

"Yes."

"Because the fixed blocks are part of the accumulated record, not replacements for it."

Hessa looked at me.

"Yes."

I had stolen her line before she could use it.

Small victory.

She wrote for a while.

I flexed my fingers.

Ordinary warmth in the forearm.

No pain.

No tingling.

No weakness.

No symptom worth promoting into plot.

"What next?" I asked.

Hessa did not answer immediately.

Good sign.

Or bad.

With Hessa those were often identical.

She looked at the records again.

"Nothing selected."

"You had five chapters."

She looked up.

"I had a life."

Right.

That landed without needing improvement.

I nodded.

She went back to the page.

I waited.

Eventually she said, "There are questions."

"There are always questions."

"Yes."

"But no next experiment."

"Not yet."

No reward.

No expanded permission.

No new object.

No direction change.

No closer or farther mark.

The room remained exactly as restrictive as when I entered.

Only the record had grown.

That should have been less satisfying than it was.

It wasn't.

I collected my crutches.

Hessa handed me no practice assignment because practice remained prohibited.

She handed me no target because targets remained hers.

She handed me no theory because the record did not owe me one.

At the door I looked back at FAR.

"One out of four."

Hessa did not look up.

"Leave."

I left.

Outside, Carrow had warmed by perhaps one degree and become windier out of spite.

I bought nothing.

This felt financially responsible until I remembered I was simply not hungry yet.

By the time I reached home, my shoulders felt used from the walk and my right forearm still carried mild warmth from the session. Right hand worked normally on the crutch. Right leg fine after moving. Residual limb comfortable. Left tip normal.

The room was empty.

Brown cloth.

Three pins.

Same fold.

Shirt.

Three threads.

Same shelf.

No beans.

No paper-wrapped return.

The universe resisted bookkeeping.

I put Hessa's note on the table and sat.

For several minutes I did nothing useful.

Then I made the mistake of drawing eight small marks on a scrap of ordinary paper.

Not magic.

Just ink.

Four marks on one side.

Four on the other.

One mark in each group darker.

I looked at them.

Same.

Hessa's voice existed in my head without permission.

Not the same record.

I turned the paper over.

Problem solved.

Lyssa came home after dark carrying nothing I could identify as my business.

She saw Hessa's note.

"Magic?"

"Yes."

She took off her outer layer and hung it.

"Good?"

I considered several answers.

"One moved."

She looked at me.

"Far?"

"Yes."

That was enough to make her pause.

"Good?"

"There it is again."

"What?"

"The word."

Lyssa sat down.

I told her the short version because she had not asked for the long one.

Four FAR attempts.

One response.

Three nothing.

Control unchanged every time.

Same as the last INTERMEDIATE block if all you looked at was one out of four.

Not the same as the whole record.

Lyssa listened while unwrapping bread she had apparently acquired somewhere without requiring my assistance.

When I finished, she tore a piece off.

"Moved once."

"Yes."

She handed me bread.

I took it.

"Still don't know."

"Correct."

She nodded.

That was the entire analysis she intended to provide.

Healthy.

I ate.

After a while I said, "Hessa says the fractions can be beautiful."

Lyssa looked at me.

"Did she?"

"Approximately."

"No."

"Emotionally."

"No."

I ate more bread.

The brown cloth remained folded.

The shirt remained damaged.

The note remained on the table.

Fifty-three external attempts now existed whether I liked their shape or not.

One far movement had made the page more interesting.

It had not made it simpler.
