# CHAPTER 253

## THE SUBSTITUTE

The needle case did not become more important overnight.

I checked.

Not by going back to the market.

By waking, remembering three lines inside a circle, and discovering that I still did not know what they meant.

Useful.

Lyssa was already dressed.

The reddish-brown sample had disappeared from the table. The darker spool remained, slightly smaller than before. The long-standing brown cloth still sat near the wall in the same fold with the same three pins. My damaged shirt remained on the shelf with three loose threads.

The groceries had survived.

Two onions.

Most of the greens.

Bread reduced by breakfast.

Cheese reduced by me.

Lyssa looked at the cheese.

Then at me.

"Good?"

"Improving."

"You said acceptable."

"It has had time to develop."

"Since yesterday?"

"Important period for cheese."

She took a piece.

That ended the review.

I expected her to leave first.

Instead someone knocked.

Three quick strikes.

Not Vale.

Vale's runners knocked like doors owed them money.

Not Hessa either. Hessa used paper because apparently handwriting was safer than conversation before breakfast.

I opened the door.

Marek stood there holding a hat.

Not wearing it.

Holding it.

This was immediately suspicious.

"No."

He looked offended.

"I haven't asked."

"You brought a hat."

"It's not for you."

"Then no twice."

Lyssa said from behind me, "What happened?"

Marek leaned around the doorframe.

"Nothing."

"Then why are you here?"

He looked at me.

"See? Hostile household."

"You came before breakfast ended."

"I came after my breakfast."

"That is not the same thing."

He held up the hat.

It was brown felt with a narrow brim and a dent in the crown that looked intentional until I saw the tear near one side.

"Nessa says bring Greg."

That changed the category.

"Why?"

"Pell's sick."

I looked at the hat.

"Did the hat do it?"

"No."

"Established?"

Marek frowned.

Lyssa laughed once behind me.

I asked, "What are we doing?"

"Second Toast."

That was worse.

I had already been Steward in The Second Toast. Eleven lines. Three entrances. Two cups with enough history to qualify for citizenship.

"I'm already in Second Toast."

"Yes."

"Pell is also in Second Toast."

"Very good."

"Pell has more than eleven lines."

"Also good."

"Marek."

"Nessa says bring Greg."

"Teren?"

"At theatre."

"Rinna?"

"At theatre."

"Pell?"

"Sick."

"You said that."

"Still true."

The hat drooped in his hand.

I looked back at Lyssa.

She had already gone to the table and was folding a small cloth packet I had not seen her prepare.

"Go," she said.

"I may be replacing Pell."

"Yes."

"In a play where I already have another role."

"Yes."

"This concerns you less than the needles."

"Pell needs replacing."

Apparently necessity improved things.

I took my better coat.

Marek handed me the hat.

"Why?"

"Nessa said bring that too."

"You were carrying it."

"Now you are."

He started down the stairs.

I looked at the hat.

Lyssa passed me the cloth packet.

"Food."

"I ate."

"For later."

I took it.

Now I had a coat, a hat that was not mine, food I had not requested, and two crutches.

The day had organized itself quickly.

"Need anything?" I asked.

Lyssa shook her head.

"North side."

No explanation.

None required.

I followed Marek.

He had already reached the bottom landing.

"Slow down."

"I'm waiting."

"You're moving."

"Slowly."

"That is not waiting."

He stopped.

I caught up.

Then he started again.

Compromise.

Outside, the morning was cold enough that the haircut remained a strategic error.

Marek noticed me touch one ear.

"You cut your hair."

"Several people have investigated."

"Looks better."

"Thank you."

"More head."

"There it is."

We took the wider route toward the theatre.

Marek knew it now without asking whether I wanted the stairs.

That had happened slowly enough that I had not noticed when it stopped being accommodation and became route.

He carried nothing.

I still had the hat.

"Why am I carrying this?"

"Because I gave it to you."

"Before that."

"Nessa gave it to me."

"Why?"

"She said don't lose it."

I stopped.

Marek kept walking two steps before noticing.

He turned.

"What?"

"You transferred responsibility."

"Yes."

"Immediately."

"Not immediately. I got all the way to your house."

I resumed walking.

"I respect the distinction and hate the behavior."

"Good."

Everyone knew that word.

The theatre side door was already open.

Inside, the place sounded wrong for performance and right for emergency rehearsal.

Lines from the stage.

Someone dragging a chair.

Nessa saying, "No, the other one," with the exhausted precision of a person who had already said it twice.

Marek entered first.

Nessa saw him.

Then me.

Then the hat.

Her eyes narrowed.

"Why does Greg have that?"

I pointed at Marek.

"Succession crisis."

Marek said, "I brought it."

"You gave it to him."

"Still arrived."

Nessa took the hat from me.

"Pell is sick."

"Established."

"Not dying."

"Useful ceiling."

"Teren wants to see what you remember."

That was less reassuring.

The Second Toast had not been difficult when I was Steward because the Steward's entire purpose was to enter with things other people cared about.

Pell played the Uncle.

The Uncle cared about almost everything.

He arrived early, objected to the seating, disliked the first toast, corrected the second, misread Hara's character's attempt to leave, and spent most of the play trying to control a dinner that had stopped belonging to him before the first cup reached the table.

I knew this because I had carried his cup.

Knowing a man's cup was not the same as knowing his lines.

Teren stood near the front row with pages in one hand.

Rinna sat two seats behind him, counting coins into small stacks.

Ordinary money work during theatrical emergency.

She looked up.

"Greg."

"Rinna."

"Pell is sick."

"The city has informed me."

Marek looked pleased.

Rinna looked at him.

"You told one person."

"Important person."

"Go help Davin."

Marek's pleasure died.

He went backstage.

Teren held out pages.

"Read."

I took them.

Not the whole script.

Pell's pages.

Marks beside his lines.

Some in Pell's hand.

Some probably Teren's.

One line had been crossed out and restored badly enough that both versions remained visible.

"How sick?" I asked.

"Enough."

"Today only?"

"We don't know."

"Performance tonight?"

"Yes."

There it was.

I looked toward the stage.

Hara was already seated at the dinner table. Another actor stood near the rear chair. The blue-banded cup sat where it belonged for scene one.

The cracked red-marked cup was offstage.

I knew that much immediately.

Teren saw me look.

"Don't solve props."

"I wasn't."

"You were."

"I was appreciating continuity."

"Read."

I read.

The first problem appeared before the first page ended.

I remembered more than I expected.

Not the exact words.

The shape.

Pell had repeated these lines beside me enough times that my body knew when I entered after them. Certain phrases had become doors.

When Pell said, "If we're all agreed," I entered with the first tray.

When Hara said, "You have mistaken silence for agreement," I waited.

When Pell said, "Then let us improve the silence," the scene moved.

I knew those because they controlled me.

The rest was fog with furniture inside.

I read the first section twice.

Teren waited.

"How much?" he asked.

"Not enough."

"Specific."

"I know where the Uncle is trying to take the scene. I know several cue lines because they were mine too. I know maybe a third of the exact wording without pages. Less if you make me prove it."

"Good."

I stared at him.

He ignored the accusation.

"Steward?"

"Who does it?"

Nessa answered from behind us.

"Marek."

I turned.

"Why?"

Marek was carrying a chair with Davin.

He nearly dropped his end when he heard his name.

Nessa said, "Because he has watched you do it and has six lines elsewhere that don't overlap."

"Eleven."

"Steward has eleven."

"I know."

"Then why did you say six?"

"His other role."

Right.

Marek put the chair down.

"I can carry a tray."

Nessa said, "That is the beginning of the problem, not the end."

Hara laughed from the table.

Teren pointed to the stage.

"First section. Pages in hand."

I looked at him.

"Now?"

"No. Next winter."

I went.

The Uncle's chair was on the opposite side of the table from where I usually stood as Steward.

That changed the room immediately.

From there, the blue cup was not an object I delivered.

It was mine.

Or the Uncle's.

That distinction mattered enough that I refused to think about it.

I sat.

My crutches had to go somewhere.

As Steward, I was moving almost the whole time and the routes had been built around that.

As Uncle, I stayed seated through most of the first scene and then crossed late.

I put the crutches against the chair.

One slid.

Nessa said, "No."

"I know."

Jori came from the wing, looked once, and moved a narrow side stool from beside the wall to behind my chair.

"There."

I set the crutches against it.

Stable.

"Thanks."

He was already leaving.

Teren said, "Start."

We started.

The first line was easy because it was on the page.

The second was harder because I looked down too late and lost Hara's face.

The third I knew.

I said it without looking.

Too confidently.

Hara answered.

I looked back at the page.

Missed the next entrance by half a breath.

Nobody stopped.

We continued.

Then came the first Steward entrance.

Marek entered carrying the tray.

Wrong side.

Nessa said, "Stop."

Marek froze.

"Other door."

"Greg uses this one."

"Not in scene one."

He looked at me.

I looked at him.

"Apparently I contain multitudes."

"Other door," Nessa repeated.

Marek backed out.

Teren looked at me.

"You too."

"What did I do?"

"You watched him instead of Hara."

"He was wrong."

"Not your job now."

That landed cleanly enough that I disliked it.

We restarted from two lines earlier.

Marek came through the correct door.

I kept my eyes on Hara.

The tray entered my vision anyway.

Blue-banded cup nearest me.

Correct.

Marek angled it slightly.

Toward me.

Too much.

I almost smiled.

He was making my old mistake.

Carry choices. Do not make them.

Then I remembered Teren had just told me not to do another person's job.

I took the cup.

Scene continued.

At the stop, Nessa said, "Marek."

"What?"

"Flat."

He looked at the tray.

"It is flat."

"Present it flat."

"Oh."

He looked at me.

I said nothing.

This required effort.

Hara noticed.

That made it worse.

Her mouth moved slightly.

Not enough to become a smile.

Teren said, "Again."

We did it again.

This time Marek entered correctly and held the tray neutrally.

I chose the blue cup.

He left.

The scene continued.

I got four lines in a row without pages.

Then confidently said a line from scene three.

Hara stared at me.

Not as Hara.

As a woman deciding whether to rescue me.

I knew immediately.

"Wrong toast," I said.

Teren said, "Yes."

Someone backstage laughed.

I found the line.

We continued.

By the end of the first section I had learned three things.

First, I knew more of Pell's role than I had any right to know.

Second, that amount was still nowhere near enough.

Third, watching a play from inside it did not mean I had been watching the same play as Pell.

Teren came onto the stage.

"Again after break."

"Tonight?"

"If you can do it."

"And if I can't?"

Rinna answered from the seats.

"We change the bill."

No tragedy.

No speech about the show always continuing.

Just another option.

I liked that.

"Pell's pay?" I asked.

Rinna looked at me.

"He is sick."

"That wasn't the question."

"If you perform his role, you get his performance share for that piece. Marek gets Steward share. Pell loses tonight's performance share. He doesn't lose his place in the company because he has a fever."

Specific enough.

Money attached to bodies showing up.

Also specific enough not to become a philosophy.

I looked at the pages.

More pay.

More work.

One night, possibly more.

Yesterday I had almost spent money because a mark made my memory itch.

Today money was available because Pell's body had made a different decision.

That did not make the two things related.

I was proud of myself for knowing this.

Probably.

During the break I ate half of Lyssa's packet.

Bread with something salty folded inside.

Marek sat beside me with the Steward pages.

"You make this look easier."

"I have done it."

"Yes."

"That is usually how that works."

He pointed at one mark.

"Blue here?"

I looked.

Scene one.

"Yes."

"Red in two."

"Yes."

"Blue in three."

"Yes."

He nodded.

Then I stopped myself.

"Ask Nessa."

"You just answered."

"I know."

"Was it wrong?"

"No."

"Then why ask Nessa?"

I considered this.

"Because if I answer everything, eventually one will be wrong and you'll still think I know."

Marek stared at me.

"That sounds like Hessa."

"Fuck."

He laughed so hard Nessa looked over.

"What?"

Marek said, "Greg is becoming Hessa."

Nessa looked at me.

Then at the pages.

"No."

Good.

We went back to work.

The second run was better.

Not good.

Better.

I kept the pages low enough that I could see Hara and the others over them. Teren stopped me twice for reading instead of answering. Once because I stood on the wrong line and put my right crutch where the Steward needed to pass.

Jori moved nothing that time.

"Your foot mark," he said.

I looked down.

There was already a chalk mark from Pell's usual cross.

Wrong for me.

Jori scraped it with his shoe and put another mark six inches wider.

"Yours."

We tested it.

Marek passed cleanly.

Done.

No one discussed fairness.

The third run reached the end without stopping.

I missed two words and replaced them with worse ones.

Hara covered one pause by drinking.

The other actor gave me the noun I needed inside his answer without making it obvious.

Marek entered through every correct door.

He presented the red cup neutrally in scene two.

Then almost left with the tray before Hara put her cup back.

She held it out toward empty air.

Marek saw her at the last instant and returned two steps.

The audience tonight would not know he had nearly abandoned a woman holding a cup.

If we were lucky.

At the end, Teren was quiet.

I waited.

He looked at Hara.

"Can you carry him tonight?"

Hara looked at me.

"Probably."

"Comforting," I said.

She ignored me.

"He listens. He knows the shape. If he loses words, I can keep the scene moving."

Teren looked at the other actor.

He nodded.

Then Teren looked at me.

"You don't know Pell's performance."

"I noticed."

"Don't imitate it."

That surprised me.

"I wasn't planning to."

"You started to."

I thought about the first run.

The Uncle's clipped irritation. The way Pell sat forward before certain lines. The little hand movement before the second toast.

I had copied all three without deciding to.

"Ah."

"You know what the man wants. Play that. Keep the pages available."

"Onstage?"

"On the side table."

Nessa was already moving them.

Of course she was.

Rinna said from the seats, "Then we're keeping the bill."

Decision made.

The rest of the afternoon became repetition.

Not magical repetition.

No glass shield.

No counted attempts.

No interpretation ceiling beyond whether I could get through the fucking play tonight.

We ate between runs.

Marek forgot the scene-two door once more and never again.

I stopped copying Pell's hand movement.

Hara changed one piece of timing because my slower standing cross made her old pause too long. She did not ask permission. She simply answered later.

Teren heard it.

"Keep that."

She did.

By evening, Pell's role had not become mine.

It had become performable.

That was enough.

The audience arrived.

Backstage changed temperature the way it always did when strangers filled the other side of the wall.

Nessa checked the cups.

Blue.

Red.

Marek's tray.

My pages on the side table.

The hat from this morning turned out to belong to the Uncle.

I had carried my own costume to rehearsal without knowing it.

Nessa put it on my head.

Then immediately took it off.

"Hair changed."

"Apparently."

She adjusted the inner strip with a folded bit of cloth and put it back.

Better.

"Don't lose it."

I looked at Marek.

He looked away.

The first pieces went before ours.

I helped nothing backstage because I was busy reading Pell's pages and because Nessa had enough sense not to reward panic with extra jobs.

Then The Second Toast was called.

I took the Uncle's chair.

Crutches against the side stool.

New chalk mark under my right foot.

Pages on the side table.

Blue cup coming later.

The lights shifted.

We began.

The first scene held.

I knew the opening.

Then I didn't.

I glanced down once.

Found the line.

Looked back up.

Hara was already waiting for me.

I answered.

Marek entered through the correct door.

Tray flat.

Blue cup available without being offered.

I took it.

He stayed until he had everything back.

Good Steward.

Annoying.

The first laugh belonged to Hara.

The second belonged to the other actor.

Mine came later and smaller when the Uncle tried to improve the seating arrangement for the third time and discovered someone had moved his chair while he was standing.

That part had always been funny.

It remained funny without Pell.

Halfway through scene two, I lost a line.

Completely.

No first word.

No shape.

Nothing.

I could have looked at the pages.

They were three steps away.

Hara saw it.

Her character looked at mine and said, "You had another correction. I can see it waiting."

Not the script.

Close enough to its world.

The line came back.

"I have several. I am deciding which one you deserve first."

That was the actual line.

The audience laughed.

Not because I had recovered.

Because Pell's line was good.

We continued.

Scene three was easier.

I had heard too much of it from the wing over too many performances.

When Marek brought the blue cup again, I almost looked at him to see whether he would angle the tray.

I didn't.

Not my job.

He didn't.

At the final toast, I stood on Jori's new mark.

Hara's timing shifted around the slower cross exactly as it had in rehearsal.

The scene ended.

Applause came.

Ordinary applause.

Not more because Pell was missing.

Not less enough that I could tell.

We left.

Backstage, Nessa took the hat before I could become responsible for it again.

Marek put down the tray.

"I didn't lose anything."

Nessa counted the cups.

"You almost did."

"But didn't."

"Correct."

He looked at me.

"She said it."

"Don't encourage her."

"For what?"

"Correction waiting."

"You looked empty."

"Useful diagnostic."

"Learn the line."

Also fair.

"Tomorrow morning."

"Pell still sick?"

"Probably."

"His sister sent word. Fever this afternoon."

Better.

"Same role?"

"Until he returns or we change the bill. Learn it tonight."

"All of it?"

"No, Greg. Only the words you intend to say."

I deserved that.

Marek got the Steward share.

More money than an ordinary night.

Not enough to transform anything.

Enough to notice.

No debt arithmetic.

No needle-case arithmetic either.

Convenient.

"Different role."

Three lines inside a circle.
