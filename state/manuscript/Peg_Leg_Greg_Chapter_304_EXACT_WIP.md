# CHAPTER 304

## THE QUIET TABLE

Hessa's note arrived while I was sorting beans.

This felt like criticism of both activities.

Cala had placed the basket in front of me after breakfast and left me with the same rules as yesterday.

Good in the large bowl.

Split, pale, or suspicious in the small.

Tavi had decided the small bowl was a hat.

This complicated quality control.

Then someone knocked.

Not loudly.

Two quick taps.

Cala opened the door.

A Guild runner stood outside with a folded square of paper.

Not the boy from the theatre.

Not anyone I knew.

He looked past Cala.

"Greg?"

I raised one hand.

The runner crossed the room, handed me the note, and left.

No conversation.

No mystery.

Just delivery.

I opened it.

AFTER MIDDAY.

EAT FIRST.

NO ATTEMPTS BEFORE.

HESSA.

I read it twice.

Lyssa was near the window, cutting thread.

She looked up.

"Guild?"

"Apparently my social life has become paperwork."

"Magic?"

"Probably."

Cala said, "Eat first."

I held up the note.

"Hessa has infiltrated the house."

"Good."

Tavi put the small bowl on his head.

"Guild hat."

"No."

He smiled.

I finished sorting beans.

This was important.

Not magically.

Civilizationally.

The three copper and one private bit remained upstairs.

I had no reason to bring any of them.

The shoulder bag came with me after midday because it carried the narrow scrap and charcoal, which were actually relevant to Guild work.

My body felt normal.

Clean, too.

This was still new enough to notice after North Steps.

The left leg was comfortable.

No skin issue.

Hands ordinary.

Right shoe flat.

Crutches sound.

I ate before leaving because I had developed a strong professional interest in not arguing with Hessa about food twice.

The Guild yard was busier than the last time I had come for magic.

A pair of apprentices carried a narrow copper frame across the stones.

Someone had opened three high shutters on the western building.

A clerk sat under the awning with six little glass seals laid in a row and was touching each one with a brass pick.

None of this concerned me.

I went to Hessa's room.

The table had changed.

Not the table itself.

The things on it.

The familiar pale paper square sat near the center.

Same square.

I recognized the softened corner.

Under it was a flat black slate plate I had not seen before.

Fine white lines crossed the slate into a grid, close enough that a small movement would stop being an argument about whether anyone had blinked.

A narrow brass arch rose over the plate.

At the top hung a clear bead with a blue center.

Four pale threads descended from the arch around the plate.

Not touching it.

Telltales.

The bead was dark.

I stopped at the door.

"That looks expensive."

Hessa did not look up from her notes.

"It is not yours."

"Then it looks reasonably priced."

"Sit."

I sat.

The chair remained in the same place as prior sessions.

Near mark on the board.

Middle.

Far.

The far mark existed and had become less dramatic since failing to do anything there once.

This was healthy.

Possibly.

Hessa finished writing.

Then came the questions.

Sleep.

Food.

Pain.

Independent draw.

Independent shaping.

Independent external attempt.

All ordinary.

No.

No.

No.

She checked pulse.

Hands.

Forearms.

Grip.

Shoulders.

She asked about the bathhouse only because I mentioned I had gone.

"Skin?"

"Normal."

"Residual limb?"

"Normal."

"Any redness after?"

"No."

"Any fall?"

"No."

"Hot room?"

I stared at her.

"No."

"Good."

"You sound like the older man."

"What older man?"

"Bath politics."

She ignored me.

This was probably for the best.

Then she turned to the table.

"Distance is not today's variable."

I looked at the far mark.

"Far and I have agreed to spend time apart."

"No."

"Middle?"

"No."

"Near only?"

"Yes."

I leaned back.

"That sounds dangerously like progress."

"It is measurement."

Less dangerous.

Hessa pointed to the black slate.

"Witness plate."

Then the threads.

"Draft telltales."

Then the bead.

"Still bead."

I knew the broad arrangement.

Still beads showed up around fine balances, reagent scales, ink films, hair-thin rune work, and any workshop where a person wanted to blame air less often.

They did not make a vacuum.

They calmed small local drafts.

Some were mechanical-magic hybrids.

Some used treated glass.

Some used cheap field beads that lasted three months and then became decorative disappointment.

I had no idea which this was.

I did not need to.

"You're trying to stop the room from moving the paper."

"Reduce one source of ambiguity."

"Not stop."

"Correct."

I looked at the four pale threads.

They hung almost still already.

One moved faintly toward the window.

"Why the witness plate?"

"Better visual measure."

"Why same paper?"

"Object history."

"Why near?"

"Most clean responses."

I nodded.

Near had two clean displacements in four established attempts.

Not reliable.

Just less empty than the others.

Middle had one clean in six, plus the possible edge lift from attempt sixty-eight.

Far had one attempt.

Nothing.

Hessa touched the still bead with one finger.

It remained dark.

"Three maximum today."

"All near?"

"Yes."

"Same duration?"

"Yes."

"Same permitted effort?"

"Yes."

"Same direction?"

"Away."

"Still bead on for all three?"

"No."

I looked at her.

"Comparison."

"Yes."

That was the first interesting thing.

She pointed at the plate.

"Attempt seventy-three with the still bead off. Same room condition as much as practical, improved visual reference only."

"And seventy-four?"

"Still bead on if I continue."

"Seventy-five?"

"Same as seventy-four if I continue."

"Why not alternate off again?"

"Because I am not building a complete study in one afternoon."

I sighed.

"I keep hoping ambition will become a medical requirement."

"It has symptoms."

Cruel institution.

Hessa placed the pale paper square on the black grid.

Near position.

The white lines around it made its edges obvious.

One corner sat just short of a crossing line.

I could see exactly how annoying a tiny shift would become.

The draft telltales hung around the plate.

The leftmost moved faintly.

Window air.

Hessa adjusted nothing.

Attempt seventy-three was supposed to include the room.

Not fix it.

I settled.

Hand loose.

Shoulder down.

Breathing normal.

No leaning.

No reaching with my body.

Minimal established external-effect attempt.

Direction away.

Same duration.

Hessa said, "Expectation?"

"Low to medium."

"Evidence?"

"Near has two clean responses in four attempts."

"Anything else?"

"I like the black plate."

She looked at me.

"Not evidence."

"It improves morale."

"Begin."

I worked.

The paper did nothing.

The left telltale shifted once.

The paper did not.

My mind noticed the grid lines and became convinced that maybe the near corner had moved a hair.

Then the end cue came.

I stopped.

Hessa moved before I spoke.

Paper.

Grid.

Telltales.

Window.

Table.

Floor.

Me.

She crouched to look across the slate.

Then stood.

"No observed response."

"I thought the corner might have."

"No."

"Good."

She looked at me.

"Why good?"

"Because the plate stopped me from promoting imagination into data."

Hessa wrote that down.

"I regret speaking."

"Body?"

"Fine."

"Urge to increase?"

"Low."

She checked me.

Pulse normal.

Hands normal.

Forearm normal.

Then she touched the clear bead.

A soft blue light appeared in its center.

Not bright.

Not theatrical.

The four telltale threads changed almost at once.

The left thread stopped drifting toward the window.

The right one moved once, then settled.

Within a few breaths all four hung straight.

The room still had air.

I could breathe.

Steam did not descend from the ceiling.

Nothing dramatic happened.

The little volume above the table simply became quieter.

I knew the broad sensation from good weighing benches.

My shirt sleeve stopped lifting at the cuff.

A loose corner of Hessa's note stopped trembling.

Small changes.

Useful ones.

"Still field reaches how far?" I asked.

Hessa pointed at the brass arch.

"Enough for this plate."

"That is not a distance."

"It is the distance that matters."

"Terrible teacher."

"Sit still."

I sat still.

She lifted the paper and replaced it at the same near position.

Not exactly the same grid intersection.

Close.

She marked two tiny reference points in her notes.

Attempt seventy-four.

The telltales stayed quiet.

The blue center of the bead stayed steady.

"Expectation?"

"Same ability expectation. Higher confidence in interpreting movement if movement happens."

Hessa's pencil stopped for half a second.

"Better."

"I have trained for years to receive that word."

"Begin."

I worked.

Nothing.

For the first few breaths, nothing.

Then the paper moved.

Not much.

A small clean slide away.

One white grid line appeared at the near edge where paper had covered it before.

The far edge crossed a second line by a smaller amount.

No corner popped.

No curl.

No flutter.

The square simply changed place.

I stopped on the cue.

My heart did not.

Hessa was already checking.

She did not touch the paper.

First the telltales.

All four still.

Then the bead.

Blue steady.

Then the table edge.

Then the slate.

She crouched.

Looked from left.

Right.

Across the paper.

Then she used a thin measuring strip against the grid.

I waited.

This was the hardest part of magic.

Not doing it.

Waiting for another person to tell me what I had already seen.

Hessa stood.

"Observed small net displacement away."

I breathed out.

"Clean?"

"Clean enough to log as displacement."

"Inside the still field."

"Yes."

"With telltales quiet."

"Yes."

"So not wind."

"No."

I closed my eyes.

She waited.

I opened them again.

"Ordinary room draft less likely."

"Yes."

"Not impossible."

"Correct."

"Still bead itself could matter."

"Yes."

"Table vibration."

"Checked, not eliminated."

"Body movement."

"Controlled as practical, not eliminated."

"Some cause we are not measuring."

"Always."

I looked at the paper.

"Do you ever enjoy anything?"

"Yes."

"Hidden skill."

She wrote.

I wanted the clean movement to become more than it was.

My brain tried immediately.

This proves the effect is real.

This proves air was never responsible.

This proves near works.

This proves the still bead helps.

This proves the external effect crosses calm fields.

This proves something about distance.

None of those sentences survived Hessa standing beside me.

So I chose the one that did.

"One clean near displacement under reduced-draft conditions."

"Correct."

That was enough.

Almost.

"Can I be happy?"

"You can be anything that does not change attempt seventy-five."

There it was.

The next trap.

Success created appetite.

I knew that one.

"Body?" Hessa asked.

"Fine."

"Urge to increase?"

"Medium."

"Because?"

"Because seventy-four moved and I want seventy-five to move more."

"Will you increase?"

"No."

"Certain?"

I took one breath.

Then another.

"Yes."

Hessa checked pulse.

Grip.

Hands.

Forearm.

She made me wait longer than I liked.

The paper remained where attempt seventy-four had left it until she measured and reset it.

The blue still bead stayed on.

The telltales stayed straight.

Attempt seventy-five.

Same near position.

Same paper.

Same witness plate.

Same still field.

Same direction.

Same permitted effort.

Same duration.

I settled.

The clean result from seventy-four tried to become a technique.

Whatever I had done, do that again.

That thought was dangerous because I did not know what I had done.

There had been no special breath.

No sharper focus.

No secret old-life structure.

No useful sensation I could isolate.

The paper had moved.

That was all.

I let the previous attempt go as much as I could.

"Expectation?" Hessa asked.

"Emotionally high. Evidentially low to medium."

"Why?"

"One clean response immediately prior under the same controlled condition is encouraging, but near is still inconsistent."

"Anything else?"

"I do not know what I did differently."

"Good."

"I hate that answer."

"Begin."

I worked.

Nothing happened.

The paper stayed flat.

The grid stayed visible where it had been visible.

The telltales stayed still.

The blue bead remained steady.

No slide.

No curl.

No lift.

No result.

The end cue came.

I stopped.

Hessa checked everything again.

"No observed response."

I nodded.

The disappointment was immediate.

Then something else followed.

Not relief.

Maybe shape.

Attempt seventy-four had not become a new normal.

Attempt seventy-five had killed that fantasy quickly.

Useful.

Cruel.

Useful.

"Body?"

"Fine."

"Urge?"

"Lower."

"Why?"

"Because the protocol is over."

"Also useful."

She turned off the still bead.

The blue center faded.

Within a few breaths the left telltale began drifting faintly toward the window again.

I watched it.

That mattered more now.

Not because it explained the paper.

Because it showed me the room had been doing things even when the room looked still.

Tiny things.

Enough to complicate tiny magic.

Hessa moved the paper off the slate.

"Totals."

I waited.

"External attempts now seventy-five."

"Near?"

"Three clean displacements in seven established near attempts."

"Today's near?"

"One clean in three."

"Still on?"

"One clean in two attempts with today's still-bead condition."

"Still off?"

"No response in one attempt today."

I nodded.

"Middle unchanged."

"Yes."

"Far unchanged."

"Yes."

"Can we say the still bead improves response?"

"No."

"Because one of two moved."

"Correct."

"Can we say the still bead suppresses my effect?"

"No."

"Because one moved."

"Correct."

"Can we say the still bead is irrelevant?"

"No."

I sighed.

"Wonderful device."

"It improved observation."

That was the actual point.

I looked at the black witness plate.

"Did it?"

"Yes."

"Even though it introduced a new variable."

"Yes."

"Because we know the variable."

"We know it exists."

Of course.

"Do you plan to repeat near under still conditions?"

"Maybe."

"Middle under still?"

"Maybe."

"Far?"

Hessa looked at me.

I raised both hands.

"Grief ritual only."

"Not selected."

"Next appointment?"

"Not selected."

"Next object?"

"Not selected."

"Next condition?"

"Not selected."

I nodded.

This was becoming a language.

Not selected did not mean never.

It meant not yet turned into a promise.

I liked promises too much when they involved magic.

Hessa gathered her notes.

I reached for the shoulder bag.

Then stopped.

"One question."

She looked at me.

"Why now?"

"Why the still bead?"

"Because the far test answered your question and exposed mine."

I waited.

She continued.

"You wanted to know whether far had been tested. It now has. I want cleaner evidence about what counts as response before adding more positions."

That landed harder than the clean movement.

Not because it revealed secret magic.

Because it made the next problem smaller.

We did not need to understand my power.

We needed to get better at seeing what it actually did.

I could work with that.

"That sounds almost optimistic."

"It is not."

"Of course."

"Go home."

I stood.

Right foot under.

Crutches placed.

No issue.

The Guild yard looked ordinary on the way out.

A porter was arguing with someone over a crate strap.

The clerk with the glass seals had reduced six to four.

One apprentice had acquired a broom.

Civilization advanced unevenly.

I walked home without testing anything.

This was not difficult because Hessa had made the rule explicit.

It was also not easy because attempt seventy-four existed now.

A clean near displacement.

Under quieter air.

My mind kept returning to it.

Not to the feeling.

There had been almost no feeling.

To the evidence.

The paper had crossed the grid.

The telltales had stayed still.

The still bead had remained blue.

One small piece of uncertainty had narrowed.

Not disappeared.

Narrowed.

That was enough to make the whole city look sharper for half a lane.

I passed a fruit stall.

Did not buy anything.

Private money stayed upstairs.

Household money stayed household.

No magical purchase became necessary because paper had moved.

I was becoming insufferably responsible.

At home, Cala was slicing roots.

Lyssa sat near the window with cloth in her lap.

Not the brown cuff.

Different cloth.

I did not ask.

Tavi had returned to the spoon.

"Magic?" Lyssa asked.

"Yes."

"Move?"

"Once."

Cala looked up.

"Good?"

I thought about Hessa.

"Cleaner."

Lyssa frowned.

"Cleaner magic?"

"Cleaner answer."

That was closer.

I set the shoulder bag on the table and took out the narrow scrap.

The old lines from attempt seventy-two were still there.

I unwrapped the charcoal.

This time writing made sense.

Not because something had to count.

Because numbers were part of the work.

Below the older line I wrote:

73 NEAR / STILL OFF / NONE

74 NEAR / STILL ON / CLEAN AWAY

75 NEAR / STILL ON / NONE

NEAR 3 / 7 CLEAN

Then, smaller:

STILL FIELD IMPROVES OBSERVATION.

NOT PROOF IT IMPROVES EFFECT.

I looked at that last sentence.

It was annoyingly Hessa.

I kept it anyway.

Lyssa leaned enough to read.

"Good."

"Which part?"

"Not proof."

I looked at her.

"You have joined them."

"Who?"

"People preventing me from becoming interesting."

She smiled.

Tavi hit the table with the spoon.

"Paper move."

I looked at him.

"Who told you?"

"You."

"I said magic."

"Paper."

Lyssa pointed at me.

"You talk."

A devastating accusation.

I wrapped the charcoal again.

No new sheet used.

No new appointment.

No new job.

No money.

Magic totals changed, but the house did not.

Three copper and one bit still upstairs.

Three clean gray sheets still unused.

Front door still worked.

Rain shelf still worked.

My left leg remained comfortable.

The shoulder bag still closed.

I sat back.

The narrow scrap now held a better sentence than the one I had wanted.

Not proof.

That had once felt like failure.

In my first life, power had made certainty cheap.

Cast the spell.

Break the wall.

Kill the thing.

Heal the wound.

Watch what happened.

Enough force could make a question simple.

This life kept handing me effects too small for that luxury.

A paper square.

A grid line.

Four pale threads.

One blue bead.

One movement.

Then none.

I had to learn what the old version of me had often skipped.

How to be satisfied when the only thing that improved was the quality of the doubt.

That evening, I opened the bag again once.

Not to add anything.

Just to read the three new lines.

Seventy-three.

Seventy-four.

Seventy-five.

One clean.

I closed the bag.

The paper had moved.

The question had not.

For now, that was better.
