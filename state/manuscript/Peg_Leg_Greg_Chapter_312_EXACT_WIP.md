# CHAPTER 312

## THE MARKED THIRD

Hessa's note arrived before breakfast.

This was becoming rude.

Cala had not even cut the bread yet.

Tavi was trying to fit one wooden spoon inside another wooden spoon, which was impossible but apparently worth sustained research.

Lyssa was already gone.

The knock came twice.

Cala opened the door.

A Guild runner held out a folded square.

Not the same runner as last time.

Carrow contained enough people that repetition remained optional.

"Greg?"

I raised a hand.

He crossed the room, gave me the note, and left.

I opened it.

AFTER MIDDAY.

EAT FIRST.

NO ATTEMPTS BEFORE.

SAME RULES.

HESSA.

I looked at Cala.

She looked at the note.

"Eat."

"I have not argued yet."

"Good."

"This is prejudice."

She cut the bread.

Tavi got one spoon stuck inside the other.

Progress.

I ate.

*

The folded seat cloth from yesterday's road trip was still near the door.

I noticed it when I left.

That was all.

Sevren had not appeared with another cart.

Ressa had not sent a message.

Three Wells had remained where we left it.

Good.

My body had recovered from the road well enough.

Back normal after sleep.

Right thigh normal.

Residual limb comfortable.

Hands fine.

Crutches sound.

The shoulder bag carried the narrow scrap and charcoal.

One copper and one bit stayed upstairs.

The fixed holdfast stayed wrapped on the high shelf.

Nothing expensive came with me.

The Guild yard was quieter than the last time.

A pair of apprentices were moving stacked wooden frames from one wall to another.

A woman at the far bench had three tiny blue lamps lined up in front of her and was cursing one of them under her breath.

Nobody needed me.

I went to Hessa's room.

The black witness plate was back.

So was the brass arch.

So were the four draft telltales.

The still bead hung dark at the top.

The paper was different.

Not the old pale square.

A long narrow strip lay across the black slate.

About the width of two fingers.

Maybe the length of my forearm.

Three black crossmarks divided it into thirds.

LEFT.

CENTER.

RIGHT.

The words were written on Hessa's page.

Not on the paper.

I stopped in the doorway.

"That looks like a trap."

Hessa looked up.

"Sit."

"That confirms it."

I sat.

The chair was at the near position.

Middle and far marks remained on the floor.

Unused.

Hessa asked the usual questions.

Sleep.

Food.

Pain.

Independent draw.

Independent shaping.

Independent external attempt.

No.

No.

No.

She checked pulse.

Hands.

Forearms.

Grip.

Shoulders.

Residual limb only because the road had been yesterday.

"Skin?"

"Fine."

"Pressure?"

"None."

"Pain?"

"No."

"Cart?"

"Bad furniture."

She looked at me.

"Expected."

"I would like that placed in the official record."

"It is."

I could not tell whether she was joking.

This was one of Hessa's more dangerous skills.

She turned toward the table.

"Distance is not the variable."

"Again?"

"Yes."

"Far will become jealous."

"No."

"Middle?"

"No."

"Near only."

"Yes."

I looked at the strip.

"Same paper, different places on it."

"Yes."

There it was.

I leaned forward.

Not enough to cross the line.

Just enough to be interested.

Hessa pointed at the strip.

"One object."

"Three targets."

"Three marked regions."

"That is three targets."

"Maybe."

I stared at her.

She stared back.

I smiled.

"No."

"What?"

"You used maybe."

"I use words."

"Not that one."

"Greg."

"Fine."

I looked at the paper again.

The strip ran left to right across the witness plate.

All three marked regions sat at approximately the same forward distance from me.

Lateral change only.

Not perfect.

Nothing in a room was perfect.

Good enough to ask something.

"You're testing whether I affect the object or the part I mean."

"I am testing whether target specification changes the observed response."

"That is the same sentence after a clerk attacked it."

"It is a narrower sentence."

Of course.

I looked at the left third.

Then the center.

Then the right.

"If I say right third and the whole strip moves?"

"We record what moves."

"If only the right end moves?"

"We record what moves."

"If nothing moves?"

"We record nothing."

"Your profession has a theme."

"Yes."

The still bead remained off.

One telltale drifted faintly toward the window.

Hessa touched the arch.

"Still field on for all three."

I looked at her.

"No off comparison?"

"Not today."

"Why?"

"Because air is not today's comparison."

She turned the bead on.

Soft blue filled the clear center.

The telltales settled one by one.

The room around the table became quieter.

Not silent.

Not dead.

Just less interested in the paper.

Hessa waited until all four threads hung straight.

Then she used a thin brass rule to set the strip along two grid lines.

Left edge.

Right edge.

One long side almost exactly parallel with the nearest white grid line.

She wrote the positions down.

"What is the instruction?" I asked.

"Same external effect. Away."

"Target?"

"Left third."

"Meaning I am supposed to push the left third away."

"Do not invent push."

I closed my mouth.

She continued.

"Direct the established external attempt away. Specify the left marked region as target."

"Without increasing effort."

"Correct."

"Same duration."

"Correct."

"Same body position."

"As practical."

"Three maximum."

"Yes."

There was one question I disliked.

I asked it anyway.

"If the whole strip moves, does that mean I cannot target part of it?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because one result would not establish that."

I sighed.

"What if only the left third moves?"

"Paper is continuous."

"Rotates."

"Then we measure rotation."

"So if the left end goes away and the right end stays, that is interesting."

"Yes."

I waited.

Hessa added, "Interesting is not proof of your preferred explanation."

There she was.

I settled.

Hands loose.

Shoulder down.

Right foot flat.

Left knee clear.

No leaning.

No body reach.

The strip stayed flat.

Attempt seventy-six.

Hessa said, "Expectation?"

"Low to medium."

"Evidence?"

"Near has three clean responses in seven attempts."

"Relevant to sub-object targeting?"

"No direct evidence."

"Anything else?"

"I have opinions."

"Not evidence."

"They are extremely well organized."

"Begin."

I worked.

Left third.

Away.

Not harder.

Not more.

Just the same strange narrow attempt that had occasionally moved paper and mostly insulted me.

Nothing happened.

The left third remained exactly where it was.

Center remained.

Right remained.

The telltales stayed straight.

Blue bead steady.

The paper did not curl.

Did not slide.

Did not rotate.

The end cue came.

I stopped.

Hessa checked the strip without touching it.

Grid.

Edges.

Crossmarks.

Telltales.

Table.

Me.

Then the brass rule.

"No observed response."

I nodded.

"Body?"

"Fine."

"Urge to increase?"

"Low."

"Interpretation?"

"I remain bad at moving one third of paper."

Hessa wrote something.

"That is not useful."

"It is emotionally accurate."

"Try again."

"Right third?"

"Yes."

She did not move the strip.

That mattered.

Same object.

Same placement.

Same still field.

Different specified region.

At least as close as practical.

Hessa pointed at the right third.

"Attempt seventy-seven. Target right marked region. Direction away. Same duration. Same permitted effort."

I looked at the strip.

The first attempt had left me calmer than a success would have.

Failure was useful that way.

It took some appetite out of the room.

"Expectation?" Hessa asked.

"Low."

"Why lower?"

"Seventy-six did nothing."

"Is that enough?"

"No."

"Then?"

"Emotion."

"Good."

I hated when she approved the annoying answer.

"Begin."

I worked.

Right third.

Away.

For several breaths, nothing.

Then the strip turned.

Not much.

The right end shifted away from me.

The left end shifted slightly toward me.

The center barely changed position.

A rotation.

Small.

Clean enough that the long edge no longer followed the white grid line.

I stopped on the cue.

My heart sped before my thoughts did.

Hessa moved immediately.

Not touching the paper.

Telltales first.

All straight.

Still bead steady.

Table.

Floor.

My chair.

My right foot.

Crutches.

Her own sleeve.

Then the strip.

She crouched.

Looked along the grid.

Moved to the other side.

Measured both ends.

The right end had moved farther away.

The left had moved a smaller amount toward.

The center crossmark had barely translated.

I knew what that looked like.

I also knew what I wanted it to mean.

Those were not the same thing.

"Rotation," I said.

"Yes."

"Right side away."

"Yes."

"Left side toward."

"Yes."

"Target was right."

"Yes."

I looked at her.

She kept measuring.

"You can say it."

"I am measuring."

"Hessa."

She wrote two numbers.

Then a third.

"Observed small rotation. Right end displaced away relative to left."

My skin prickled.

"Clean?"

"Clean enough to log as rotation."

"Under still field."

"Yes."

"With right third specified."

"Yes."

"So target matters."

"No."

I closed my eyes.

Of course.

"Target might matter."

"Yes."

"Because the response geometry matches the target."

"It is consistent with that possibility."

"You ruin language."

"I narrow it."

"Same violence."

She ignored me.

I looked at the strip.

It had rotated only a little.

Nothing dramatic.

A person walking past the door would not have noticed.

But it was not the same kind of movement as attempt seventy-four.

That had been a small clean slide.

This had turned.

The right third had gone where I had asked more than the left.

Maybe.

There was the dangerous word.

Maybe.

Hessa checked my pulse.

"Body?"

"Fine."

"Urge to increase?"

"High."

"Why?"

"Because I want to do that again on purpose."

"Can you?"

"I have no idea."

"Good."

"Stop praising ignorance."

"No."

She checked my hands.

Grip.

Forearm.

Shoulder.

Everything remained ordinary.

Inside my head, the rotation was becoming a mechanism.

I had targeted the right.

The right moved.

Therefore I could apply force to a region.

Therefore the effect had spatial resolution.

Therefore I could learn to twist things.

Therefore I could shape pressure.

Therefore I could eventually move larger objects by choosing leverage.

Therefore I was halfway back to being dangerous.

Old Greg could build a staircase out of one paper strip faster than any carpenter.

I recognized the habit.

Did not stop it.

Just watched it happen.

Hessa reset the strip.

Same grid position.

Same long edge.

Same crossmarks.

Same orientation as before seventy-six.

The blue still bead remained steady.

"Attempt seventy-eight," she said.

"Right third again?"

"No."

I looked up.

"Whole strip."

That interrupted the staircase.

"Why?"

"Comparison."

"If whole target slides, and right target rotates..."

"We will have two observations."

Cruel.

"If whole target does nothing?"

"We will have two observations."

"One of which is nothing."

"Nothing is an observation."

"You should put that over the door."

"No."

I settled again.

The clean rotation from seventy-seven kept replaying.

I could feel the imagined motion in my mind.

Not a sensation.

A memory.

Right end away.

Left end toward.

Turn.

The temptation was to recreate it.

But seventy-eight was not right third.

Whole strip.

Away.

Same external attempt.

No extra effort.

I forced myself to stop aiming at the remembered right side.

Whole strip.

Not left.

Not center.

Not right.

The object.

That word was dangerous too.

Hessa said, "Expectation?"

"Emotionally high."

"Evidence?"

"One clean rotation immediately prior under the same still field, with a different target specification."

"Prediction?"

I thought.

"If specification matters, whole-strip target may produce more translation and less rotation."

"May."

"Yes."

"Anything else?"

"If specification does not matter, I have no useful prediction."

"Good."

"That sounded almost kind."

"Begin."

I worked.

Whole strip.

Away.

Nothing.

I held the permitted effort.

Did not chase.

Did not sharpen.

Did not lean.

The strip remained where Hessa had placed it.

The telltales stayed straight.

The blue bead stayed steady.

No slide.

No turn.

No lift.

The end cue came.

I stopped.

Hessa checked.

Measured.

"No observed response."

I laughed once.

Not because it was funny.

Because it was exactly the kind of answer the universe preferred.

One failure.

One clean rotation.

One failure.

Perfectly shaped to prevent confidence.

"Body?" Hessa asked.

"Fine."

"Urge?"

"Lower."

"Interpretation?"

I looked at the strip.

"Attempt seventy-seven is real."

"Observed rotation is real."

"With right region targeted."

"Yes."

"Seventy-six and seventy-eight do not repeat it."

"Correct."

"That makes ordinary random room movement less likely because still field and telltales."

"Less likely."

"Not impossible."

"Correct."

"Target specification may affect response geometry."

"Possible."

"Not established."

"Correct."

I hated how satisfying that still was.

Not because we knew the answer.

Because the question had become sharper.

Before today, the external effect had mostly been discussed as distance and whether paper moved at all.

Near.

Middle.

Far.

Move.

No move.

Now there was another axis.

What exactly counted as the thing I was trying to affect?

Whole paper?

Part of paper?

A region I named in my own head?

Did the effect care?

Maybe not.

One rotation was not a law.

But the experiment had made the question legitimate.

Hessa turned off the still bead.

The blue center faded.

The left telltale began drifting faintly after several breaths.

She picked up the strip only after recording the final positions.

"Next time?" I asked.

"No."

I stared at her.

"You have developed that answer into art."

"No next protocol selected."

"That is longer."

"More accurate."

"Do you need different material?"

"Maybe."

I smiled.

Hessa looked tired immediately.

"You did it again."

"I used a word."

"That word."

"Leave."

I did not.

Not yet.

I took out the narrow scrap from my bag.

There was not much room left.

Ch304 still-field note.

Ch306 holdfast note.

Now this.

I wrote small.

76 LEFT THIRD: NONE

77 RIGHT THIRD: CLEAN SMALL ROTATION. RIGHT AWAY / LEFT SLIGHT TOWARD.

78 WHOLE STRIP: NONE.

TARGET SPECIFICATION MAY AFFECT RESPONSE SHAPE. ONE RESULT ONLY.

I showed Hessa.

She read it.

"Add `under still field`."

I did.

"Anything else?"

"Do not write `control`."

I had not.

I had been thinking it.

Annoying woman.

I added:

NOT CONTROL YET.

Hessa nodded once.

That felt more valuable than it should have.

I packed the charcoal.

The strip remained on her table.

Not mine.

The witness plate remained.

Still bead.

Telltales.

No souvenir.

No new apparatus.

No assignment.

At the door I stopped.

"Why this question?"

Hessa looked at the strip.

"Because distance has not explained enough."

That was the most generous sentence she had given me all afternoon.

I waited for more.

None came.

Of course.

I left.

Outside the Guild, Carrow had continued while I was trying to move one third of paper.

A man was selling hot chestnuts.

Two girls were drawing squares on the paving with red chalk.

A delivery cart had parked badly enough to block half the lane.

Nobody knew attempt seventy-seven existed.

Good.

I walked home slowly because I had no reason to hurry.

The new result tried to become useful every thirty steps.

Could I rotate a latch?

No.

Could I twist a coin?

No.

Could I affect one side of the holdfast?

Absolutely not.

Could I move part of a larger object?

Unknown.

Could I target inside an object?

Unknown.

Could I use leverage?

Unknown.

Could I turn a cup?

Unknown.

Could I make the blue line move without the cloth?

That thought arrived uninvited.

I stopped walking.

Not because it was a good idea.

Because it was a perfect example of a bad one.

Lyssa's customer.

Lyssa's work.

A charm line Greg had spent an entire chapter learning not to adopt.

And now my brain had taken one rotating paper strip and tried to volunteer for it.

I laughed.

A woman passing with a bundle of reeds looked at me.

"Fine," I said.

She had not asked.

I kept walking.

No blue line.

No holdfast.

No latch.

No cup.

Paper first.

Maybe paper for a long time.

That was not cowardice.

It was evidence.

Hessa would approve that sentence.

I chose not to tell her.

At home, Cala was slicing something green.

Tavi was asleep under a thin blanket on the floor.

The wooden spoons had separated.

Civilization restored.

Lyssa had returned and was near the window with a dark roll of cloth across her knees.

Not the green vest.

I did not ask.

She looked at my face.

"Moved?"

"One."

"Good?"

I considered.

"Interesting."

She nodded.

No technical questions.

No request to explain.

I put the bag down.

Cala pointed at the water pot.

"Drink."

Apparently Hessa had infiltrated the house permanently.

I drank.

The one copper and one bit remained upstairs.

The fixed holdfast remained wrapped.

Three clean sheets remained clean.

Nothing had become profitable.

Nothing had become solved.

But the paper had turned.

Not much.

Enough to change the question.

I sat by the window.

Lyssa kept working.

After a while she nudged my right shoe with hers.

Not an apology.

Not a lesson.

Just contact.

I nudged back.

Outside, somebody shouted about onions.

Carrow remained committed to its themes.

I looked at my hands.

Then at nothing.

For once, I did not try to move it.
