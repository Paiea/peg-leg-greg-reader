# CHAPTER 332

## THE HOUSING

Five copper was enough money to become irresponsible in more interesting ways.

I discovered this the next morning.

Not by spending it.

By realizing I could.

Two copper had made Mara's prices theoretical.

I could pay two for a mark read and have nothing left.

I could not pay three for a housing read at all.

Four plus materials had been a joke told by arithmetic.

Five changed that.

Not into affordability.

Into choice.

Which was worse.

I ate breakfast while thinking about the holdfast upstairs.

Cala noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You're chewing like money."

I stopped chewing.

"That sentence means nothing."

"It means you're counting."

"I'm eating bread."

"Expensively."

Lyssa laughed into her cup.

Tavi looked at my bread.

"Can I have expensive bread?"

"No."

Cala gave him another piece.

Economic policy collapsed immediately.

The holdfast had cost two copper.

Fixed base.

Ring.

Seller demonstration.

Unknown safe load.

Unknown surface requirement.

Unknown release behavior.

Unknown service history.

Unknown almost everything useful.

Mara's housing read cost three copper.

Five minus three left two.

That was not a comfortable reserve.

It was a reserve.

The difference mattered.

I finished breakfast.

Then did not go to Mara.

I washed the cup.

Helped Tavi find a missing strip of cloth he had put inside a boot because apparently boots were storage now.

Checked the temporary front-hall conduit with my palm near the wall for three breaths like the linewright had said.

Normal.

Went upstairs.

Looked at the holdfast.

Still there.

Very patient object.

I took out Mara's checklist.

MARK.

MATERIAL.

MOVING PARTS.

STONE OR CORE.

VISIBLE DAMAGE.

KNOWN USE.

The holdfast had a mark.

Maybe.

I had not copied it carefully enough.

Material: metal housing, dark stone or core inside ring assembly.

Moving parts: ring definitely.

Maybe internal catch.

Visible damage: wear at base edge, old scratch near rear screw.

Known use: seller fixed it to a bench and made the ring hold under a pull I had watched.

That was enough to ask a better question.

Not enough to carry it across town carelessly.

I unwrapped it without turning the ring.

The tag still read:

DO NOT TURN RING.

Good tag.

I inspected only what was already visible.

No cleaning.

No oil.

No polishing.

The mark was on the underside of the base.

I had forgotten that.

Which meant seeing it required lifting the object.

Not activating it.

Still a load problem.

I used both hands while seated, raised it only enough to slide a folded cloth under one edge, then leaned the base against the cloth without touching the ring.

Slow.

Boring.

Alive.

The mark was shallow.

Two nested hooks facing opposite directions with a short vertical cut between them.

I copied it.

Then noticed something else.

The rear screw scratch was not a scratch.

Not exactly.

A narrow line circled half the screw head.

Tool wear.

Someone had opened the housing before.

Or tried.

That mattered.

I wrote:

REAR SCREW TOOL WEAR / UNKNOWN SERVICE

Then rewrapped it.

Now I went to Mara.

The holdfast did not fit comfortably in my shoulder bag.

Of course it didn't.

I wrapped it in the ordinary sailcloth and tied the bundle to the outside of the bag with two loops, base inward, ring protected against my side.

Then hated that.

If I fell, I could land on it.

If someone hit the bag, the ring could move.

If the tie slipped, two copper could become street noise.

I untied it.

Cala watched from the doorway.

"Going somewhere?"

"Eventually."

"With that?"

"Maybe."

"You have a basket."

I looked at the repair-sweep basket.

Of course.

The remaining mundane junk came out onto the floor cloth.

Eighteen pieces.

Still eighteen.

No spontaneous treasure multiplication.

I put the wrapped holdfast in the basket.

It sat low enough that the ring did not touch the sides.

I threaded a cloth loop through the basket handle and around my right wrist.

Then picked up both crutches.

Awkward.

Not impossible.

The basket hung from my forearm, not hand.

Weight pulled wrong.

I took three steps.

Stopped.

"No."

Cala said, "Good."

"You could have said that earlier."

"You were learning."

Everyone had become Hessa.

I needed transport.

Not a cart.

Not for one object.

A strap.

Something that let the basket sit against my back or side without occupying a hand or swinging into the crutch.

I had cloth.

Lyssa had opinions.

Dangerous combination.

She looked at the basket, then at me.

"No."

"I haven't asked."

"You want me to make that carryable."

"I want to borrow a strap."

"For the thing with the ring you're not supposed to turn."

"Correct."

"No loose strap."

"Why?"

"Because it'll swing."

I knew that.

I disliked hearing it.

She took one of the older household carrying slings from a peg downstairs.

Not one of the clean gray sheets.

A wide woven band used for bundles.

She folded it through the basket handles, crossed it underneath, then tied the ends high enough that the basket would sit flat against my lower back rather than my hip.

"Try."

I did.

Better.

Not good.

Better.

The basket touched my back but did not swing.

The holdfast stayed low.

I could use both crutches.

"If it shifts?" Lyssa asked.

"Stop."

"If the ring catches?"

"Stop."

"If you fall?"

"Complain."

"Greg."

"Protect head, don't twist toward basket, then complain."

"Better."

Tavi said, "Can I carry treasure?"

Three adults said, "No."

Excellent household coordination.

Mara's shop was open.

That felt suspiciously convenient until I remembered she had told me three mornings a week if she was there.

This was apparently one of them.

A man stood at the counter holding a brass plate.

Mara was not touching it.

"You want age," she said.

"Yes."

"I can tell you what the mark supports."

"Same thing."

"No."

"Close."

"No."

I waited outside.

Good specialist.

The man eventually paid something I did not hear and left with his brass plate looking disappointed by reality.

Mara saw me.

Then the basket.

"Questions first?"

"Questions first."

"Good."

I set the basket on the floor without crossing the threshold.

The oval clicked in my shoulder bag.

Normal.

Ignored.

"I have a fixed holdfast," I said. "Bought used. Seller demonstrated one pull on a bench. I have not turned the ring since. I copied the underside mark and noticed tool wear around a rear screw. I want to know what a housing read can actually tell me before I pay for one."

Mara looked at me.

"Better."

"Than what?"

"Most people."

"Dangerous praise."

"Don't enjoy it."

She pointed at the sign.

HOUSING 3 C.

"Housing read means I inspect accessible exterior housing, visible seams, fasteners, core seat if visible without opening, wear, likely service points, and whether the housing family tells me anything supportable."

"No opening."

"No opening."

"No function claim."

"Not unless housing itself supports one."

"No safe load."

"No."

"No promise it won't hurt me."

"No."

"No activation."

"Not for three."

"Would you turn the ring?"

"Not without a reason."

Good.

"If the mark is enough for a two-copper mark read, why pay three?"

"Because your screw wear may matter more than your mark."

There it was.

I looked at the basket.

Five copper.

Three spent.

Two left.

Still able to walk away with money.

Still unable to buy anything substantial afterward.

Still enough to make another decision.

I said, "Housing read."

Mara held out her hand.

I paid three copper.

It hurt.

Not survival hurt.

Good hurt.

Money becoming information.

I had two copper left.

Mara said, "Bring it in."

I crossed the threshold.

Click.

The oval pointed back toward the door.

I ignored it.

Mara cleared a section of counter.

"Set basket there. Don't lift the object until I tell you."

I set the basket down.

She untied the carrying sling herself.

Not because she distrusted my knots.

Probably because she distrusted everyone.

Correct profession.

"Show me how it's wrapped."

I did.

She watched my hands.

"Ring hasn't moved?"

"Not since purchase."

"You sure?"

"As sure as I can be."

"Seller moved it?"

"Yes."

"How?"

I described the demonstration exactly.

Bench.

Base fixed.

Ring turned.

Held under pull.

Released.

No invented force.

No invented duration.

Mara asked, "What was pulling?"

"Seller's hand through a strap."

"Body weight?"

"Some. Not full."

"Jerking?"

"No. Steady."

"How long?"

"A few breaths."

She nodded.

Then unwrapped the holdfast.

Did not touch the ring.

First she looked.

Longer than I had.

Then she used a narrow wooden wedge to lift one side of the base.

"Mark."

I showed her my copy first.

She compared.

"Copy's good enough."

"Meaning?"

"Meaning your hand works."

"Cruel."

She leaned closer to the actual mark.

Two opposing hooks.

Short vertical cut.

Her face changed slightly.

Not recognition.

Attention.

"Trade family?" I asked.

"Maybe."

Good.

She took out a small lens mounted in a square wooden frame.

Not magical-looking.

Probably magical anyway.

She held it over the mark.

No glow.

No dramatic revelation.

"Cut's older than the rear tool wear," she said.

"How much older?"

"Enough that I won't call them same service event."

Useful.

She moved to the rear screw.

The line around the head.

"Opened."

"Definitely?"

"Turned with a bad tool, at least."

"Could be tightened."

"Could."

"Could be loosened."

"Could."

"Could have failed."

"Everything could have failed."

Fair.

She inspected the seam around the housing.

There were four screws total.

Only one showed obvious tool wear.

That was strange.

"Why one?" I asked.

"Because somebody only needed one."

"For what?"

"That's your question, not my answer."

She took a thin strip of pale paper from a drawer and slid it along the housing seam.

It stopped near the worn screw.

She tried another point.

Paper went farther.

Back to worn screw.

Stopped.

"Housing distorted?"

"Slightly."

"From opening?"

"Maybe."

"Impact?"

"Maybe."

"Heat?"

"No obvious heat color."

Good.

She turned the base without turning the ring.

One corner had old wear.

Another had a small polished patch.

"Mounted often," she said.

"Seller called it fixed."

"Fixed doesn't mean permanent."

"Portable fixed point."

"Maybe."

That was more interesting.

A holdfast that had been repeatedly mounted, not built into one bench.

Still not a function beyond what I knew.

But it changed the object category slightly.

Mara inspected the underside again.

Then stopped.

"There."

A second mark.

Tiny.

Near the edge of the base.

Not cut like the trade mark.

Scratched by hand.

Three vertical strokes.

Then one diagonal through them.

A tally.

Maybe.

"Service mark?" I asked.

"Could be owner mark. Could be count. Could be somebody bored."

"Excellent."

"You paid for supportable."

"I know."

She looked at the ring.

Did not touch it.

The ring sat inside a dark collar.

The collar had a narrow gap around one side.

Mara used the lens.

"Core seat visible?"

"Barely."

"Material?"

"Dark stone. Maybe glass composite. Not enough exposed."

"Cracked?"

She shifted angle.

"I don't see a crack."

That was not `not cracked.`

Good.

"Anything else?"

She looked for another minute.

Then said, "This housing family was made to be serviced."

I waited.

"Supportable?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

She pointed at the screw pattern.

"Four exterior fasteners, separate collar seat, replaceable base plate. Not sealed manufacture. Somebody expected access."

"Common?"

"Not rare. Old working equipment does that."

"Does the mark tell you maker?"

"No."

"Period?"

"Broadly older than current shop standard. That's not useful enough to sell as a date."

"Trade?"

"Anchoring, rigging, restraint, load control. Something in that family."

That matched seller demonstration.

Visible value priced.

No miracle.

"Would you open it?"

Mara looked at me.

"Four copper plus materials."

"I know the sign."

"Then why ask?"

"Would you?"

She looked again at the worn screw.

"Not yet."

That mattered.

"Why?"

"Because one fastener has been worked badly and the housing is slightly distorted there. If I open it, I may turn a readable problem into a broken one."

"So don't."

"Correct."

"What would you do first?"

"Find out whether the ring behaves normally under a controlled load without opening the housing."

I stared at her.

"You said housing read doesn't activate."

"It doesn't."

"So that's separate."

"Yes."

"Price?"

"Depends on setup."

"Of course."

"Bench test with my fixtures, no guarantee, owner operates ring, I observe housing? Two copper if nothing special. More if I need another pair of hands or a load frame."

I had two copper.

Exactly.

There it was.

The old trap.

Exactly enough.

I looked at the holdfast.

Then at Mara.

"Today?"

"Could."

"For two."

"If simple fixture."

"Leaving me zero."

"Your purse is not part of my pricing."

"Rude."

"Useful."

I wanted to know.

Very badly.

Five copper had turned into the ability to ask one expensive question and still have another expensive question waiting behind it.

That was the real ladder.

Not buying everything.

Discovering that information itself had depth.

I could spend the remaining two.

Get a controlled load observation.

Maybe learn whether the worn screw mattered.

Maybe learn nothing.

Then leave with zero.

Or I could stop.

Take the housing read.

Keep two copper.

Earn again.

Return when two copper was not my entire remaining ability to move.

Mara waited.

No sales pressure.

Also good specialist.

"Not today," I said.

She nodded once.

No praise.

Even better.

"What did I buy for three?" I asked.

She looked annoyed.

"You were here."

"I want the summary."

That changed her expression.

Slightly.

"Fine. Housing family consistent with older serviceable working equipment in anchoring, rigging, restraint, or load-control use. Repeated mounting wear. One rear fastener worked with poor or mismatched tool after original housing mark was cut. Slight seam distortion at that fastener. No obvious exterior heat damage. No visible core-seat crack from accessible angle, which is not certification. Housing designed for service, but I would not open it before a controlled behavior check because the worked fastener may be part of a preexisting problem."

Perfect.

I wrote it down.

Not all of it.

Enough.

Then added:

DO NOT OPEN FIRST

CONTROLLED LOAD BEFORE SERVICE

BENCH OBSERVATION ~2 C IF SIMPLE / NOT TODAY

Mara watched.

"You missed one."

"What?"

"Repeated mounting wear means somebody considered it worth moving and setting again."

I looked at the base.

That was interpretation.

Supportable enough.

"Useful."

"Sometimes."

I added:

REPEATEDLY MOUNTED / WORKING TOOL, NOT ONE-BENCH FIXTURE ?

Question mark.

Mara approved by not objecting.

I rewrapped the holdfast exactly as before.

She stopped me before I tied the sling.

"Your basket is wrong."

"It got here."

"That is not the same thing."

"What's wrong?"

"Ring side needs a rigid guard if you're carrying it against your body. Cloth keeps dirt off. It does not stop pressure."

I looked at the basket.

"I didn't turn it."

"Good. Keep being lucky."

That was unpleasant.

"What would you use?"

She pointed at a scrap bin under the counter.

"Two thin slats across basket mouth. Tie them so the bundle can't rise into them. Ring below slats."

"Price?"

"For scrap wood?"

"Yes."

She stared.

"Go away."

Free advice.

Excellent return on three copper.

I found two discarded packing slats outside a grocer three doors down after asking.

Free.

Tied them across the basket mouth.

The holdfast sat below them.

Now if the basket pressed against my back, the slats took the pressure before the wrapped ring did.

Better.

Still awkward.

But less stupid.

I walked home with two copper.

The oval clicked at Mara's door when I left.

Normal.

Then at the building front door.

Normal.

Then upstairs.

Normal.

It did not tell me whether spending three copper had been smart.

Good.

That would have been intolerable.

Lyssa was at the table when I returned.

She looked at the basket.

Then the slats.

"Better."

"Mara insulted our transport."

"Mara was right."

"This household is hostile."

"What did she say?"

I gave her the short version.

Older serviceable working housing.

Repeated mounting.

One badly worked fastener.

Slight distortion.

Do not open first.

Controlled load next if I wanted more.

"How much?" she asked.

"Three."

She looked at me.

Not shocked.

Just recalculating.

"You have two left."

"Yes."

"And the next thing costs two."

"If simple."

"You're not doing it today."

"Correct."

"Good."

"Stop approving me."

"No."

I put the holdfast back on the high shelf.

Same orientation.

Ring sideways.

Tag visible.

DO NOT TURN RING.

Then I took out my narrow scrap.

Five copper had become two.

But I knew more.

Not what the holdfast truly was.

Not what it could safely hold.

Not whether the worn screw was harmless service damage or the beginning of failure.

I knew the next question.

That was worth something.

Maybe three copper.

Hopefully.

I looked at the remaining two coins.

The old version of me would have hated the smaller pile.

The newer version hated it too.

Growth was beautiful.

But the pile did not feel like failure.

It felt like runway I had chosen not to burn.

That was different.

I put the two copper away.

Upstairs, the oval hung quietly.

The holdfast sat wrapped beside its warning tag.

One artifact had taught me that useful did not mean trustworthy.

The other had just taught me that owning a question could be more expensive than owning the object.

I suspected this hobby was going to be terrible for my finances.
