# CHAPTER 343

## THE DUE DATE

Vale sent me a bill the morning after I earned money.

This felt supernatural.

The paper arrived folded once, clean, and offensively ordinary.

Cala took it from the boy at the door, read the outside, and handed it to me without comment.

That was worse than commentary.

The front said:

GREG

VALE OFFICE

I opened it.

The inside said:

ACCOUNT REVIEW

THIRD DAY / AFTER MIDDAY

BRING RECEIPTS

NO PAYMENT REQUIRED AT REVIEW

I read the last line twice.

Lyssa looked over from the table.

"Bad?"

"Not immediately."

"That's not an answer."

"It's Vale. That's as close as they get."

Cala put bread down.

"What does it say?"

I handed her the paper.

She read it.

"Bring receipts."

"I noticed."

"Do you have them?"

"Some."

That got both women looking at me.

"I have the Vale one."

"Some," Lyssa repeated.

"The important some."

"Greg."

"Yes."

"Where?"

"Stored."

"Where?"

"Safely."

"Where?"

I went upstairs.

This was not retreat.

It was document retrieval.

The Vale receipt from the seated office hour was where I had left it.

Good.

The expired Antonius cash-work note was there too.

Not relevant to Vale debt unless they wanted evidence I had once missed money, which seemed unnecessarily intimate.

I brought down the Vale receipt.

Lyssa checked it.

"One copper credit."

"Yes."

"Any others?"

"Not from Vale."

Cala asked, "Do you know what you owe?"

I paused.

"Approximately."

Silence.

"That is a bad word around debt," she said.

"I know what the original arrangement was."

"Do you know the current number?"

"No."

There.

Clean.

Ugly.

True.

The exact remaining Vale balance had become one of those facts I had carefully not needed while surviving other things.

This was acceptable when poor enough that exact arithmetic changed nothing.

I was less poor now.

Not rich.

Three copper was not rich.

Three copper was enough to make ignorance embarrassing.

I folded the review notice around the receipt.

"Third day."

Lyssa said, "Same day as theatre?"

I counted.

Yes.

Third night was second theatre shift.

Vale after midday.

Theatre second bell.

Not necessarily conflict.

"Probably both."

"Probably?"

"Vale first. Theatre later."

"And if Vale takes longer?"

"No payment required at review. It should be review."

She gave me a look.

"You said approximately about your debt."

"I am being punished enough."

Cala said, "Eat."

I ate.

The review was two days away.

Which meant I had two days to become a person who knew what he owed.

Unfortunately, the person who could tell me was Vale.

I considered going early.

Then didn't.

The notice gave a time.

I had other things to do.

This was another new behavior I disliked noticing.

I did not sprint toward uncertainty simply because it had sent stationery.

I spent the morning doing almost nothing important.

Laundry.

A market errand.

Rewrapping the charcoal because it had escaped its paper.

Tavi asked whether debt could be tied to a chair like his spoon carrier.

I asked why he knew we were talking about debt.

"You said debt."

Fair.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because debt is imaginary until somebody with paper finds you."

Cala said from the other room, "Don't teach him that."

"Debt is a serious financial obligation recorded between parties."

Tavi looked disappointed.

Good.

Education should occasionally disappoint.

By afternoon my shoulders had stopped complaining about theatre.

I had three copper.

Mara's simple controlled holdfast observation cost two if nothing special was required.

The thought returned.

Not urgently.

Just available.

I could go.

Pay two.

Keep one.

Learn something.

Then Vale's folded notice sat on the table.

I looked at it.

The two expenses were not actually competing yet.

Vale explicitly said no payment required at review.

But information could change decisions.

That was the whole point of information.

Spending two copper before learning my debt schedule would be technically affordable and strategically stupid.

I hated maturity.

The holdfast stayed on the shelf.

The rest stone stayed boxed.

I went for a walk instead.

Free activity.

Mostly.

Carrow had posted a North Gate board by then.

NORTH SECONDARY CLOSED

DAY TRAVEL VIA EAST CUT / LOWER ROAD

REVIEW PENDING

No explanation.

Good public notice.

You did not need magical theory to choose another road.

Two men nearby were providing magical theory anyway.

"Old road spirit," one said.

"There isn't a road spirit."

"Then why's the road doing it?"

"Roads do things."

"That's a road spirit."

I kept walking.

Democracy had reached infrastructure.

At Tool Lane I passed the old no-sign stall where I had bought the repair basket.

The man was there.

Different baskets.

Different junk.

He recognized me.

Not warmly.

Not coldly.

Recognition.

"Anything in that lot work?" he asked.

I stopped.

Dangerous question.

"Some of it held a spoon."

He stared.

"What?"

"Household engineering."

"You bought repair sweep."

"And received repair sweep. Excellent transaction."

He grunted.

I did not tell him about the oval.

That was not dishonesty.

That was ownership.

His current nearest basket had bent brass clips, two cracked wooden handles, a little iron wheel, and something that looked like half a lamp throat.

Price card: 1 C.

I had three.

I walked away.

This was getting ridiculous.

I could afford things and not buy them.

Somebody should have warned me money contained this much restraint.

The second day passed.

No message from theatre.

No message from Hessa.

No message from Beren.

No road emergency.

No artifact screamed.

Tavi's spoon carrier lost another loop.

Normal life survived.

On the third day, I put the Vale receipt in my shoulder bag.

I also put the review notice in.

Then removed the oval.

No reason to bring it.

That felt good.

Vale's office looked exactly like a place designed to make numbers feel physical.

Same narrow front room.

Same desk.

Same shelves.

Different clerk.

The woman behind the desk had short gray hair and a small ink stain on the side of her thumb.

"Greg?"

"Unfortunately."

She did not care.

Excellent.

"Receipt."

I handed it over.

She checked the paper against a ledger.

Not one ledger.

Two.

One thick bound book.

One narrower account book.

She found my line in the second.

"One-copper service credit posted."

"Good."

"You were told review today?"

I showed notice.

She checked that too.

"Sit."

I sat.

Crutches against chair where I could reach them.

She turned the account book toward herself, not me.

Healthy boundary.

Then she read.

"Original balance recorded at thirty-two copper equivalent."

There it was.

A number.

Not new history.

Just finally exact in current accounting.

I kept my face still.

Thirty-two.

"Credits posted since opening: six copper equivalent total."

I looked at her.

"Including the one?"

"Including the one."

"So twenty-six remaining?"

"Principal equivalent, yes."

Principal.

Ugly word.

"And not principal?"

She looked at the book.

"No penalty balance currently posted."

Currently.

"Interest?"

"Not under this account structure while review terms remain current."

That was much better than expected.

"What makes review terms not current?"

She tapped the notice.

"Missing required review without arrangement. Refusing account verification. Default event under original agreement."

"What counts as default event?"

"I can read the listed categories."

"Please."

She did.

Death.

Flight from district without notice.

Fraudulent identity.

Refusal of contracted service credit when properly scheduled under agreement terms.

Failure to attend two consecutive required reviews without arrangement.

A few others that did not apply to me.

I listened carefully.

"Am I currently in default?"

"No."

"Any penalty pending?"

"No."

"Any payment due today?"

"No."

"Any payment due before next review?"

"No fixed cash payment."

There.

I breathed normally.

"Then what is this review for?"

She finally looked directly at me.

"To make sure both sides agree the account exists."

That was almost funny.

"I agree."

"Good."

"Do you?"

"The ledger does."

More romantic than Vale deserved.

She continued.

"Current account remains service-credit eligible. Cash payment accepted but not required under current terms. You may request service opportunities when available. Vale may also offer qualifying service. You may refuse individual opportunities unless the original agreement marks them mandatory."

Important.

"How do I know which are mandatory?"

"Written offer states it."

"Have I refused any mandatory service?"

"No."

Good.

"Can I get the remaining balance in writing?"

She paused.

"Account copy is one bit per page."

Of course.

"How many pages?"

"Your current summary is one. Original agreement is four."

I had zero bits.

No conversion.

I could not simply decide a bit was some fraction of copper and make arithmetic happen.

"Can you write the number on the review receipt?"

She considered.

"I can stamp current reviewed principal equivalent."

"Free?"

"Included in review."

Civilization survived again.

She took a small square slip.

Stamped date.

Stamped REVIEWED.

Wrote:

PRINCIPAL EQUIVALENT REMAINING: 26 C

NO PENALTY BALANCE POSTED

NO FIXED CASH PAYMENT DUE BEFORE NEXT REVIEW

Then added a mark and initial.

I read it twice.

"Next review?"

"Sixth week from today unless notice changes it."

"Can notice change it earlier?"

"For qualifying account event."

"Meaning?"

"Service scheduling. Dispute. Payment posting error. Default concern. Account transfer."

"Account transfer?"

"Vale can transfer servicing rights under original agreement."

That was less pleasant.

"To anyone?"

"No."

"Who?"

"Eligible registered account holder. Terms remain attached unless agreement permits amendment."

"Does mine?"

She looked at the ledger.

"Not without your signature for payment structure. Servicing holder can change."

Interesting.

Not immediately threatening.

Worth knowing.

"Any transfer pending?"

"No."

Good.

I asked one more question.

"If I pay cash, does one copper reduce one copper equivalent?"

She looked at me like this was finally a sensible question.

"Under current account, yes, for copper payment."

"Bits?"

"Converted at posted office rate on payment day."

There.

A conversion existed institutionally.

Not established as universal fixed currency conversion.

Good.

"What's today's rate?"

"Are you paying bits?"

"No."

"Then it doesn't matter."

I smiled.

I liked her too.

"Fair."

She stamped my old one-copper receipt as VERIFIED and handed both papers back.

"Sign review line."

I signed.

"Done?"

"Done."

"That's all?"

"Were you hoping for debt theater?"

I stared at her.

"Do you know where I work tonight?"

She looked confused.

Good.

Coincidence remained alive.

"Never mind."

I stood.

Three copper still in my purse.

Twenty-six copper equivalent still owed.

No payment required today.

No penalty.

No hidden crisis.

The debt was simultaneously better and worse now that it had a number.

Twenty-six was not impossible.

That was the problem.

Thirty-two had once belonged to the category of enormous bad thing.

Twenty-six now belonged suspiciously close to the category of expensive artifact opening bid.

I had watched a harness open at twenty-six.

I had watched it sell for thirty-six.

I had three.

Numbers were beginning to occupy the same world.

That changed them.

Outside Vale, I sat on a low public wall for a minute.

Not because I was devastated.

Because my shoulders preferred sitting when thinking.

Twenty-six debt.

Three cash.

Two more possible tonight if theatre happened as scheduled.

Not earned yet.

Mara holdfast observation two.

Rest stone one already spent.

Future artifacts twenty, thirty, forty, more.

I could pay Vale one copper now.

I had not.

Should I?

Maybe.

But the account charged no current penalty and required no fixed cash payment.

Cash had option value.

Debt had psychological ugliness.

Those were different things.

I wrote on the narrow scrap:

VALE REVIEW

26 C PRINCIPAL EQUIV REMAINING

NO PENALTY CURRENT

NO FIXED CASH DUE BEFORE NEXT REVIEW

SERVICE CREDIT ELIGIBLE

NEXT REVIEW 6 WEEKS UNLESS NOTICE

Then:

CASH PAYMENT 1 C -> 1 C EQUIV TODAY

I almost wrote PAY IT FAST.

Stopped.

That was emotion pretending to be strategy.

Then I almost wrote DON'T PAY CASH.

Stopped again.

That was strategy pretending certainty.

I wrote:

DECIDE WITH OPPORTUNITY COST.

Ugly.

Useful.

I went home before theatre.

Lyssa was there.

"Well?"

I handed her the stamped summary.

She read it.

"Twenty-six."

"Yes."

"You knew approximately?"

"My approximation was emotionally correct."

"What does that mean?"

"Large."

She read the rest.

"No payment due."

"Correct."

"No penalty."

"Correct."

"So you're okay."

"Currently."

She handed it back.

"Are you paying some?"

"Not today."

No argument.

No lecture.

She just nodded.

Good household.

Cala asked the more dangerous question.

"Are you still going to theatre?"

I checked the time.

"Yes."

"Then eat first."

Of course.

Debt became dinner.

Dinner became work.

Work might become two copper.

Two copper might become reserve.

Reserve might become an artifact.

An artifact might become a problem.

Life had developed a suspiciously functional loop.

I did not write that down either.
