# CHAPTER 344

## THE QUIET GUEST

The second theatre shift began with a woman whose face I could not remember.

This was not immediately concerning.

I forgot faces all the time.

Most people did not deserve permanent storage.

She arrived before first bell wearing a dark green coat, pale gloves, and a silver clasp at her throat shaped like a closed eye.

I remembered all of that.

I remembered her ticket.

House right.

Row five.

Near aisle.

I remembered the young ticket woman checking the paper twice.

I remembered the woman saying, "I was told active privacy pieces were permitted if declared."

I remembered Rinna being called.

I remembered the silver clasp.

I did not remember the woman's face.

Not badly.

Not vaguely.

I could look directly at her, turn away, and lose the arrangement.

Hair color stayed.

Height stayed.

Age category approximately stayed.

Face did not.

I hated it.

Rinna did not.

Or hid it better.

She stopped three steps away from the woman.

"Declared where?"

The woman produced a small folded registration slip.

Rinna read it.

"This says personal privacy field, low reach, no identity substitution."

"Correct."

"Active now?"

"Yes."

"You need to deactivate during admission check."

The woman stiffened.

"Why?"

"Because my staff must be able to match you to your ticket if you leave and return."

"The registration is valid."

"I'm not disputing the registration. I'm telling you the house condition."

Good.

Not artifact law.

Theatre rule.

The woman looked toward the front table.

"I was told active privacy pieces were permitted."

Rinna said, "Some are. This one prevents reliable visual recognition at the door. Deactivate for admission. You may reactivate once seated if its field stays inside your row and does not interfere with performance equipment or other patrons."

That was more specific than I expected.

The woman touched the silver clasp.

A tiny blue line appeared beneath her thumb.

Then vanished.

Her face arrived.

Not magically dramatic.

Just available.

Long nose.

Dark brows.

Small scar near left side of mouth.

Maybe thirty-five.

Maybe forty.

The ticket woman checked her against the ticket mark and registration slip.

Then nodded.

The woman reactivated the clasp after crossing into the house.

Her face became disposable again.

I leaned toward Nessa.

"I hate that."

"Good. Don't buy one."

"How expensive?"

"No idea."

Healthy.

"Does theatre get many?"

"Privacy pieces? Sometimes. That kind? Not often."

"Why allow it active inside?"

"People pay to watch a play, not be watched by strangers."

Fair.

"And if it affects the stage?"

"Then we tell them to turn it off."

Also fair.

The woman disappeared into row five without disappearing at all.

My job tonight was almost the same on paper.

House route.

Late reset.

Two copper after clear.

But the North Secondary closure had reduced the late-arrival problem instead of worsening it.

People had adapted.

Carrow was irritatingly competent when given a full day.

Traffic came through East Cut earlier.

Patrons arrived in smaller groups.

The front table did not become a human dam.

Good.

I had prepared for the wrong problem.

Normal work.

Rinna changed nothing because there was nothing to change.

I checked house-left.

Blue lamp.

Back wall.

Flower-frame pocket.

Rope post.

All fine.

Nessa checked right.

The young ticket woman counted.

Mustached worker said polite no to a man trying to bring a bottle inside.

Civilization repeated itself.

First bell.

Doors narrowed.

Second bell.

House settled.

The show started.

I stood rear-left.

Tonight my shoulders were less stupid.

Not stronger.

I knew when to sit.

Different thing.

The first act ran clean.

Marek entered from the correct door.

Hara carried a quiet line through the second voice.

No giant boots.

No late crowd wave.

I began to suspect I might earn two copper through simple competence.

Then row five started whispering.

Not loud.

Wrong.

A cluster of people around the privacy-clasp woman kept turning toward one another.

I could not hear what they said.

At first that seemed polite.

Then I realized I could not hear anything from that section.

Not whispers.

Not clothing.

Not chairs.

Not the stage.

The audience in a rough circle around row five had become acoustically absent.

Hara spoke.

The second voice carried her line cleanly across the room.

Except near row five, where the line seemed to fall into a hole.

People inside the hole leaned forward.

One man cupped a hand to his ear.

There.

Not privacy of face.

Something else.

I caught Nessa's eye from across rear house.

She had noticed too.

Good.

No lone genius.

She pointed two fingers toward row five, then toward Rinna at back center.

I moved along wall.

Quietly.

Rinna met me halfway.

"Row five."

"I know."

"Sound gone in a radius."

"How far?"

"Maybe two seats either side. Hard to tell."

"Clasp?"

"Probably."

She looked toward the stage.

Scene still running.

No immediate danger.

Performance disruption localized.

Rinna waited for the next scene-change cue.

Then went down the right aisle herself.

I stayed rear-left.

Again, good.

Authority handled patron artifact.

I handled my route.

The blue lamp went dark.

Canvas wall turned.

Rinna reached row five and crouched near the woman.

I could not hear them.

Of course.

The woman touched the clasp.

Blue line.

The acoustic hole vanished.

Audience noise returned all at once.

Chair creak.

Breathing.

A cough.

The stage.

Then the woman's face became available again too.

Interesting.

Same activation controlled both effects?

Maybe.

Or one failure had dragged another function with it.

No conclusion.

Rinna spoke to her.

The woman looked angry.

Not embarrassed.

Important distinction socially.

She produced the registration slip again.

Rinna looked at it.

Then shook her head once.

The woman stood.

Row five had to let her out.

I moved down left? No.

House right.

Not mine.

Nessa handled aisle.

I stayed where I was.

This restraint deserved money.

The woman was escorted to rear house, not thrown out.

Rinna brought her to the side vestibule near front table.

The show continued.

Most audience watched the stage.

A few watched the vestibule.

Humans preferred nearby administrative conflict to fictional tragedy.

At the next safe interval, Nessa came to me.

"Keep left."

"Already doing that."

"Good."

"What happened?"

"Piece widened."

"Privacy field?"

"Apparently."

"Sound suppression listed?"

"No idea yet."

She left.

Healthy information boundary.

I loved competence when it denied me gossip.

The act continued.

Without the clasp active, row five heard normally.

The woman's seat remained empty.

At interval she was still in vestibule.

Now her face stayed visible.

The silver clasp was inside a little folded cloth pouch on the table.

Not confiscated exactly.

Contained.

Rinna stood with the woman and the mustached front worker.

A city house-ward runner had arrived.

I knew the type by sash.

Not police.

Not artifact examiner.

Public building safety runner.

At least broadly.

He had a narrow wooden case and a copper-tag board.

I did not approach.

Then Rinna called me over.

So I approached.

"Greg."

"Yes."

"You were rear-left when it changed?"

"Yes."

The runner looked at me.

"Describe only what you observed."

I liked him immediately.

"Before act one, active clasp made her face difficult to retain. Registration was shown. Rinna required deactivation during admission. Face became normally memorable. She reactivated after seating."

"Then?"

"During act one, roughly row-five area became quiet in a way that removed stage sound too. I couldn't hear whispers, chair noise, or performance sound from a small area around her. I did not measure radius."

"Did you see activation at that moment?"

"No."

"Did you see her touch piece before effect began?"

"No."

"Did you see effect end?"

"Yes. Rinna reached her during scene change. Woman touched clasp. Blue line showed briefly. Sound returned. Her face also became easy to retain again."

"Any flash, heat, smoke, smell, physical injury?"

"None I observed."

"Anyone collapse?"

"No."

"Anyone unable to leave?"

"No."

"Good."

He wrote.

The woman said, "It has never done that."

Runner said, "I'm not deciding why it did."

Excellent.

She pointed at registration slip.

"It's licensed for privacy use."

"Registration describes declared function at registration. It does not certify current condition tonight."

There.

Artifact life again.

Documents described history.

Objects continued aging afterward.

The woman looked toward the pouch.

"Can I take it home?"

"Probably."

She did not like probably.

Neither did I.

Runner continued.

"House can refuse reactivation on premises. I can tag observed incident and advise inspection. If I find active public danger I can hold it under building-safety authority. I haven't established that."

Rinna said, "It stays inactive here or it leaves with her."

The woman asked, "If I leave, do I get readmission?"

Ticket woman answered from the table.

"At interval, yes, if ticket remains matched and house has not closed reentry."

Institutional chorus.

The woman exhaled.

"Fine. It stays off."

Runner opened his narrow case.

Inside were paper tags, thin lead seals, two chalk sticks, a small brass fork, and nothing dramatic.

He did not touch the clasp.

He wrote an incident tag.

Asked the woman to read it.

She read.

"Observed widened effect?"

"That's what witnesses describe."

"Not failure?"

"I didn't inspect it."

Good.

"Not illegal use?"

"Not established."

Very good.

"Not sound suppression?"

"Observed local sound loss. Cause unestablished."

The runner might have been the most attractive bureaucracy I had ever seen.

He attached the paper tag to the cloth pouch string, not the artifact.

"Take it for inspection before next use in public building. House may set stricter conditions."

Rinna said, "We do."

The woman looked at her.

"No active privacy pieces?"

"No. This piece. Until you bring inspection."

Specific.

Again.

The woman nodded once.

Angry but reasonable enough.

The runner left before interval ended.

No arrest.

No confiscation.

No secret assassin.

No cursed artifact revelation.

Just an expensive-looking personal magical object behaving outside expected bounds in a crowded building.

Which was honestly more unsettling.

The woman returned to row five with the clasp inactive in its pouch.

Her face remained visible.

I found myself remembering it aggressively.

Long nose.

Dark brows.

Scar near mouth.

This was petty.

I allowed it.

Second act started.

Then something else happened.

The woman laughed.

Not at the play.

At Marek missing a cup.

He reached for a cup that was not there, looked at his hand, then at Hara.

Hara handed him her cup without breaking line.

Audience laughed.

The woman laughed too.

Normal sound.

Normal face.

Normal person with a malfunctioning object in a pouch.

That mattered.

Artifacts wanted to turn owners into extensions of themselves in my head.

Carrier harness man.

Privacy-clasp woman.

Rest-stone buyer.

As if owning strange magic made a person primarily a magical event.

It didn't.

Usually.

Marek drank from Hara's cup and made a face because it was apparently empty.

She said, perfectly in character, "Even theft requires preparation."

The audience liked that too.

I did not know if it was written.

Better not knowing.

The rest of the shift became ordinary.

Which was almost disappointing.

One late couple.

One child asleep across two seats.

One man standing in the flower-frame pocket again, different man, same species.

At bows the audience exited.

The privacy woman stopped at front table.

She asked Rinna for a written house incident copy.

Rinna gave her one.

Good.

Then she looked at me.

I remembered her face.

Victory.

"You were the witness?"

"One of them."

"Did it bother you before it widened?"

Dangerous question.

"Personally? Yes. Operationally? Not after admission check."

She almost smiled.

"Most people like not being remembered."

"I prefer choosing what I forget."

"Fair."

She touched the pouch at her throat.

"It was my mother's."

There.

A whole history trying to happen.

I did not ask.

She waited half a breath.

Then said, "Good night."

"Good night."

She left.

Nessa appeared beside me.

"You didn't ask."

"Ask what?"

"Her mother."

"I am growing."

"Terrible."

"Painful."

Reset took longer tonight because incident paperwork had stolen Rinna and front-table time.

So I did more chairs.

Programs.

Rope post.

Checked left rear.

Helped Nessa carry the empty program box to storage.

Not backstage.

Storage off front corridor.

Scope survived.

At final clear, Rinna checked the house.

Front doors closed.

Mustached worker counted slips.

Young ticket woman balanced something in her book.

Nessa gave me the gray sash back.

Rinna handed me two copper.

"Second evening."

I counted once.

Two.

"Do I sign?"

"Yes."

I signed.

Five copper total now.

That number felt much larger than three and much smaller than twenty-six.

Economics was rude.

Rinna said, "No more house nights scheduled."

"Good."

She raised an eyebrow.

"I mean, healthy boundaries."

"If we need you, we'll ask."

"Also good."

No standing employment.

No emotional misunderstanding.

A completed bounded job.

I liked that more than I expected.

Nessa asked, "You going to spend it?"

"Why does everyone ask me that?"

"Because you look like you already spent it in your head."

I hated observable behavior.

"I have options."

"That sounds expensive."

"It is."

Outside, the street was cool and mostly dry.

I had five copper.

Twenty-six debt.

A two-copper holdfast observation available.

A one-copper retired rest stone already sitting uselessly at home.

A one-copper oval that had become useful enough to carry and weird enough to distrust.

And now a stranger's inherited privacy clasp had widened into an undocumented sound hole in the middle of a play.

I did not know its price.

I did not know its maker.

I did not know whether the sound loss was a defect, hidden function, aging interaction, bad repair, environmental interference, or something else.

I knew one thing.

Registration had not made it simple.

That mattered more than price.

At home, Lyssa was awake.

"Paid?"

I put two copper on the table beside the other three.

"Five."

She looked at the coins.

Then at me.

"Now what?"

I looked upstairs.

Not literally at the holdfast.

Directionally.

She noticed.

Of course.

"Mara?"

"Maybe."

"Debt?"

"Maybe."

"Keep it?"

"Maybe."

"You're unbearable with money now."

"This is financial sophistication."

"It's indecision."

"Sophisticated indecision."

She laughed.

Then I told her about the privacy clasp.

Her first question was not what it did.

"Did anyone get hurt?"

"No."

"Then what happened?"

I explained.

Face hard to remember.

Declared privacy field.

Later local sound vanished too.

Artifact turned off.

Runner documented observation without pretending to know cause.

Owner kept it, inactive, with advice to inspect before public use.

Lyssa frowned.

"Would you buy one?"

"Absolutely not."

"Because it broke?"

"Because people forgetting my face sounds useful until I remember I enjoy being recognized when somebody owes me money."

"You don't have anyone owing you money."

"Exactly. I need to preserve future capability."

She shook her head.

I carried the five copper upstairs.

Not loose.

Wrapped.

Separate from papers.

The holdfast sat on the high shelf.

The rest stone below it.

Oval wrapped near my bag.

I had enough money now to pay Mara two copper and still have three.

That was different.

At three, the observation had been possible.

At five, it might be comfortable.

Not cheap.

Not automatic.

Comfortable enough that I could ask whether the information was worth two rather than whether losing two would make me stupidly exposed.

That was the economic change.

I did not take the holdfast down.

Not tonight.

I wrote one line on the scrap instead:

5 C CASH / 26 C VALE

Then below it:

PRIVACY CLASP: DECLARED FUNCTION != CURRENT BEHAVIOR

I stared at that.

Too broad.

Of course declared function did not equal every current behavior forever.

I crossed it out.

Wrote:

PUBLIC REGISTRATION DID NOT CERTIFY CURRENT CONDITION.

Better.

Then I added:

OWNER STILL PERSON.

I stared at that one longer.

It was embarrassingly sentimental.

I crossed it out too.

Some lessons did not need durable storage.

I put the charcoal away.

Five copper stayed wrapped.

All three of my strange objects stayed where they were.

For one night, money was the most active artifact I owned.
