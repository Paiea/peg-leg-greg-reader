# CHAPTER 361

## THE MODEL

Senna found me because apparently sitting still had become a marketable skill.

This was insulting for several reasons.

I was halfway through breakfast when somebody knocked three times, waited, then knocked twice more like the door had failed an examination.

Cala looked at me.

"Your person."

"How do you know?"

"Mine knock once."

Reasonable system.

I opened door.

Senna stood outside with charcoal on one cheek, a rolled cloth under one arm, and the expression of somebody whose morning had already become other people's fault.

"Good," she said.

"Hello."

"You're here."

"Disturbingly."

"I need your leg."

I looked down.

"This one?"

"The missing one."

"Harder to lend."

"Not like that."

"You opened poorly."

She pushed past me.

Cala said, "Tea?"

"Please."

Traitor.

Lyssa was at work table wearing the charcoal coat despite being indoors.

Not fully.

Open front, sleeves still settling into new shape.

She had worn it twice since buying it and each time behaved as if nobody should notice.

Everyone noticed.

Tavi noticed most.

He had already asked whether the pockets could carry bread, a hammer, a small bird, and him.

The answer had remained no in increasingly specific ways.

Senna looked at coat.

"That's good."

Lyssa said, "I know."

No shame now.

Good.

Senna dropped rolled cloth onto chair.

"I need Greg for two hours."

I said, "You do not own two hours."

"I can pay."

That changed sentence structure.

"Continue."

"One copper."

"For what?"

"Sitting."

"I am excellent at that."

Lyssa said, "You complain while sitting."

"Premium feature."

Senna unrolled cloth.

Inside were six large sheets of heavy gray drawing paper and three thinner sheets covered in quick body studies.

Not portraits.

Positions.

A shoulder carrying a basket.

A man crouched with one heel lifted.

An old woman leaning into a cane while turning.

A child asleep against somebody's chest.

Then one unfinished figure with two crutches.

No face.

No clothes beyond rough lines.

Left leg ended below knee.

Not me exactly.

Close enough to become suspicious.

"You drew me from memory."

"Badly."

"Comforting."

She tapped unfinished figure.

"I need weight transfer."

I looked again.

The drawing had both crutches planted wide and right foot too centered.

The body floated between them.

No load.

No reason.

"That's wrong."

"I know."

"Then why show me?"

"Because I know it's wrong and don't know which wrong."

Better.

I sat at table.

Senna drank Cala's tea in three quick swallows that suggested temperature was somebody else's problem.

"What is this for?" I asked.

"Guild wall."

"Which guild?"

"South Cloth Mutual."

Lyssa looked up.

"Why?"

Senna pointed at studies.

"They want working bodies. Not trade portraits. Carrying, standing, reaching, resting. Twelve panels around new stair hall."

"Why?" I asked again.

"Because somebody with money decided workers like pictures of workers."

Lyssa said, "Do they?"

"They like walls less when empty, apparently."

Good artist.

Senna continued, "They asked for age range, body range, different work. One panel has crutches."

I looked at her.

"Because diversity?"

She made a face.

"Because the stair hall also has ramp entrance and somebody on committee noticed their first sketch had twelve people carrying things up stairs."

I laughed.

Could not help it.

"Excellent."

"I said if they want a crutch user, I need one who actually stands on crutches."

"There are others."

"Yes."

"Why me?"

"I know you."

There it was again.

Not destiny.

Proximity.

"And you sit long enough."

"Less flattering."

"One copper. Two hours. Breaks when you need. No face if you don't want. Clothes stay on."

"Generous."

"I learned to specify."

I did not ask from what.

Cala did.

"From what?"

Senna stared into tea.

"Artists."

Enough.

I considered money.

Seven copper.

One more would be eight.

No bits involved.

Good.

I considered time.

Two hours.

No scheduled work.

No Hessa note.

No reason to protect entire day simply because I had recently discovered optionality.

That would become its own stupidity.

"Where?"

"Old dye court. South Cloth has room with north windows."

Lyssa said, "Flat floor?"

Senna nodded.

"Wide door. Bench."

She looked at me.

"I checked."

That landed quietly.

Not heroic.

Not praise.

She had checked whether place worked before asking me to work there.

Good.

"One copper," I said. "Two hours."

"Yes."

"If you need longer, ask before longer."

"Yes."

"Breaks don't extend clock unless I agree."

Senna frowned.

"I was going to say breaks don't count."

"Then one copper for two working hours?"

"Yes."

"Different offer."

She thought.

"Two working hours. Breaks outside."

"Fine."

Lyssa said, "Look at him negotiate sitting."

"Every profession matures."

Tavi asked, "Can I get paid to sit?"

Cala said, "You can start free."

He sat immediately.

"Now what?"

"Stay."

He lasted perhaps four seconds.

Competitive field.

I did not bring Three Reeds.

Did not bring holdfast.

Did not bring rest stone.

Oval stayed in shoulder bag because that was where oval lived now, which was not same as bringing it for work.

Seven copper stayed in purse.

The city was bright after two days of thin cloud.

Hanging Road remained closed at west approach.

I could see closure board from two streets away.

Did not detour.

North Secondary was not on route.

Good.

Old dye court sat behind South Cloth hall in district I knew mostly by smell.

Soap.

Wet wool.

Dye bark.

Hot water.

Metallic bite from mordant vats somewhere behind walls.

The guild building was not impressive.

Three stories of pale brick with dark timber braces and wide loading doors.

The new stair hall attached to east side in cleaner stone.

Ramp curved around outer wall instead of cutting straight up.

Not elegant.

Usable.

I appreciated buildings that admitted bodies had geometry.

Senna led me through side door.

A clerk at desk looked at my crutches, then at Senna.

Not suspicious.

Just checking.

"Model?"

Senna said, "Yes."

There.

Role acquired.

The clerk wrote something.

"Second room. North windows. Keep charcoal off new floor."

Senna looked offended.

"I know."

Clerk looked at her cheek.

Senna wiped wrong side.

Charcoal remained.

I said nothing.

Professional solidarity.

Second room was better than promised.

Wide.

High windows.

Pale daylight.

A long bench against wall.

Two straight chairs.

One low platform maybe six inches high.

I looked at platform.

Senna said, "You don't need to use it."

"Good."

"I thought maybe seated pose later."

"Fine."

"First standing."

Less fine.

She set paper on tall board.

Charcoal sticks in shallow tray.

No magical art tool.

No hidden artifact.

Just work.

"What do you need?" I asked.

"Natural standing rest first."

"With both crutches?"

"Whatever natural means."

Dangerous instruction.

I stood how I stood when waiting.

Right foot under me.

Left knee slightly forward.

Both crutches not symmetrical.

Right crutch farther out.

Left closer.

Shoulders not level because bodies were rude.

Senna stared.

Then moved my unfinished sheet aside and started new one.

"Don't pose."

"I'm not."

"You are."

"I became aware of being observed."

"Stop."

"Excellent advice."

She drew.

Silence lasted almost a minute.

Then:

"You lean more than I remembered."

"Which way?"

"Right."

"Usually."

"Why?"

"Because that's where foot is."

She looked annoyed.

"More than that."

I thought.

"Depends on rest. If I'm actually waiting, I let right leg take more and use crutches to widen base. If I'm about to move, weight changes before first crutch does."

"Show me."

I did.

Not step.

Preparation.

Small forward shift.

Shoulders changed.

Hands loaded differently.

Right knee unlocked.

Senna's charcoal moved fast.

"Again."

I reset.

Shifted.

"Again."

Reset.

Shifted.

By fourth, it stopped being natural because repetition makes every movement stupid.

"Enough," she said.

Good.

She turned paper.

"Now start a step and stop before foot moves."

"Crutch moves first sometimes."

"Then that."

I set left crutch slightly forward.

Right followed enough to open path.

Body leaned.

"Hold."

I held.

This was not a comfortable static position.

"How long?"

"Thirty breaths."

"Whose?"

"Mine."

"You breathe badly."

"Twenty."

Negotiation successful.

She drew.

At breath twelve my right thigh started complaining.

At fifteen hand pressure increased.

At eighteen I said, "Done."

She said, "Two more."

"No."

I reset.

Senna blinked.

Then nodded.

Good.

No artistic suffering doctrine.

She looked at sketch.

"Your hand changed."

"Yes."

"Before leg?"

"Probably."

"Can we do shorter holds and I watch hand?"

"Yes."

There.

Better question.

We did six short starts.

No counts in manuscript state because this was not Hessa and civilization had limits.

Senna watched fingers.

Crutch grip.

Shoulder.

Right foot.

The strange empty line below left knee where other bodies would have another contact point.

She stopped trying to make me symmetrical.

Drawing improved immediately.

I could see it even unfinished.

Not because I was art expert.

Because figure finally looked like it would fall if you removed crutches.

Which meant crutches mattered.

"There," I said.

"What?"

"Now they're doing something."

She looked at page.

"Yes."

"Before they were accessories."

Her mouth tightened.

Not defensive.

Thinking.

"That's what was wrong."

"Part of it."

"What else?"

I pointed at torso without touching paper.

"You drew missing foot, not missing load path."

She stared at me.

I regretted sentence because it sounded intelligent.

"Say that again."

"No."

"Greg."

"You removed lower leg from ordinary standing person. Body doesn't work like ordinary standing person minus one piece. Whole balance changed."

"That's better."

"Less quotable."

"More useful."

She wrote something tiny at page edge.

I did not ask.

Maybe insult.

Then door opened.

A woman in blue work apron entered carrying three folded cloth samples.

She stopped.

"Oh. Sorry."

Senna said, "We're working."

"I can see."

Woman looked at me.

Not long.

Enough to register crutches.

Then at drawing.

"That's the stair panel?"

Senna said, "Maybe."

"Committee wanted carrying."

I waited.

Senna's face changed.

Not Greg dry.

Hers got longer when irritated.

"Committee wanted working bodies," she said. "He can carry if you want to hire him carrying. Today I hired him to stand and move because I need the mechanics right before I invent a task around them."

Woman lifted hands.

"I wasn't criticizing."

"You were about to ask why he isn't holding cloth."

"I was going to ask whether he could."

Senna looked at me.

Good.

She did not answer for me.

I said, "Yes. Depending what and how much."

Woman nodded.

"Could you carry folded length under one arm?"

"With crutches?"

She looked at my hands.

Realized problem.

"Right."

Not mockery.

Actual correction.

"Shoulder bag," I said. "Back load. Sling. Something attached. Not under arm while using two crutches unless I'm stationary."

"What about over shoulder?"

"Maybe if balanced and not interfering with crutch swing. Depends weight."

Senna said, "That is why I'm not inventing carrying yet."

Woman sighed.

"Fine. You're right."

Senna blinked.

Apparently this was unexpected.

Woman put cloth samples on far table.

"Committee meets in six days. They want first three panels."

"They'll have them."

"Including this one?"

Senna looked at sketch.

Then me.

"Maybe."

Woman left.

Door closed.

I said, "You enjoyed that."

"No."

"You did."

"A little."

There.

Human.

Messily.

"They keep wanting symbols before bodies. Potter holding pot. Weaver holding cloth. Carrier holding box. Old person holding cane. Then they complain everybody looks like signs over shop doors."

"Because they are."

"Yes."

"You could draw Greg holding Greg."

"What does that mean?"

"No idea."

"I hate you."

"One copper."

"I hate you professionally."

Better.

"That."

"That is worst place."

"I know. Five breaths."

Again.

"Yes."

"Correct."

"Usually."
