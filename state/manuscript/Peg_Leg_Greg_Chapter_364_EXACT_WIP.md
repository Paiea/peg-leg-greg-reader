# CHAPTER 364

## THE DEBTOR

Vale found me through bread.

Not metaphorically.

A folded gray slip sat under loaf Cala brought home from corner baker, which meant somebody had handed it to baker, who had handed it to Cala, who had put bread directly on top of my financial obligations.

Efficient.

"Your debt has become breakfast," Lyssa said.

"Finally useful."

Cala cut loaf.

"Read it before butter gets on it."

I pulled slip free.

Vale office mark.

Not review notice.

Different corner stamp.

I opened it.

AVAILABLE SERVICE CREDIT

EAST LEDGER ANNEX

TOMORROW / THIRD BELL

DOCUMENT TRANSFER ASSISTANCE

SEATED / TWO HOURS EXPECTED

CREDIT: 3 C PRINCIPAL-EQUIVALENT

REFUSABLE

CONFIRM BY OFFICE CLOSE TODAY

I read it twice.

Lyssa said, "Three?"

"Three."

"For sitting?"

"Apparently my strongest profession."

Cala put butter on bread.

"What kind of documents?"

"Transfer assistance."

"That means nothing."

"It means documents will be transferred."

"Greg."

"I don't know."

That part mattered.

Vale debt sat at twenty-six copper principal-equivalent.

Three credit would make twenty-three.

No cash in hand.

No bits.

No purchase.

Just less future obligation.

I had eight copper private cash.

Enough that three copper of debt reduction no longer felt same as three copper of survival.

Still real.

Very real.

Lyssa leaned across table.

"You going?"

"Probably."

"Why probably?"

"Because I want to know what transfer assistance means before I agree."

"Slip says confirm."

"Office exists."

She smiled slightly.

"Look at you."

"What?"

"Reading terms before accepting money."

"Credit."

"Worse."

Cala said, "Eat first."

This household had priorities.

After breakfast I walked to Vale office.

Not because three copper needed emotional ceremony.

Because service credit came from debt agreement, and debt agreements became dangerous when words blurred.

Vale's front room looked exactly as it had during review.

Two benches.

Three writing desks behind waist-high rail.

Posted rate board.

Posted service notices.

One woman arguing quietly about whether a delivery counted as late if receiving office closed early.

A clerk I had not met took slip.

"Name?"

"Greg."

"Account mark?"

I gave it.

She checked ledger.

"Yes. East Annex tomorrow. Third bell."

"What is document transfer assistance?"

She looked at me.

Not annoyed.

Assessing how much explanation I needed.

"Old secured accounts are being moved from annex paper shelves into consolidated registry ledgers. Staff reads account marks and page references. Assistant confirms physical bundle labels against transfer sheet, keeps page stacks in order, and marks missing or damaged leaves for clerk review."

"Do I interpret account terms?"

"No."

"Copy balances?"

"No."

"Handle money?"

"No."

"Read private account contents?"

"Only cover marks and page numbers unless clerk directs otherwise. You are not authorized for terms."

Good.

"Why me?"

She checked ledger again.

"Your account allows service credit. Annex requested people who can read clearly and remain seated."

I waited.

She added, "You have completed office service before without dispute."

There.

Not special.

Known enough.

"Three principal-equivalent for expected two hours?"

"Three if completed."

"If it runs longer?"

"You may stop at two hours unless you agree extension. Extension rate would be stated before extension."

"If they end early?"

"Full three if supervisor releases you as complete."

"If I leave early?"

"No credit unless supervisor records completed scope."

Reasonable.

"Does accepting change next review?"

"No."

"Any obligation to accept future service?"

"No."

"Does refusal affect account standing?"

"No."

"Does completion reduce principal-equivalent from twenty-six to twenty-three?"

She checked.

"If no other account event posts before then, yes."

Correct answer.

Not promise against universe.

"I'll take it."

She marked slip.

"Bring this. East Annex, not main office. Third bell."

"Where?"

She gave directions.

I left.

No drama.

No hidden trap.

Debt could simply be work.

That was almost more unsettling.

I spent rest of day not preparing for paper.

This required discipline.

Tavi asked what job was.

"Looking at numbers."

"You do that at home free."

"I have been exploited."

Cala said, "By yourself."

"Worst employer."

Lyssa was trimming thread ends from a finished cuff.

"Three off Vale?"

"If completed."

"Twenty-three after?"

"If nothing else posts."

She looked at me.

"You went to ask that exact sentence, didn't you?"

"Yes."

"Good."

No joke.

Just good.

That landed.

Next morning I went East Annex.

No artifacts beyond oval in normal shoulder bag.

No Three Reeds.

No holdfast.

No rest stone.

Eight copper stayed home except ordinary carry coin I did not count because bit continuity remained a problem nobody in world knew existed.

I disliked that sentence immediately.

Not because of money.

Because I had started thinking like ledger.

East Annex sat behind old court buildings where streets narrowed and walls became older than signs attached to them.

Three-story stone building.

Small windows.

Iron shutters open for morning.

Vale mark over door, older style than main office.

Inside smelled like paper, dust, wax, and old damp that had lost argument with limewash but not memory.

A man at front table took my marked slip.

Late forties maybe.

Thin hair.

Sleeves rolled exactly same height.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

"I'm Orsen. Transfer clerk."

Role supplied.

"Sit."

I sat.

Good beginning.

Table had three shallow wooden trays.

LEFT SHELF

TRANSFER

HOLD

A stack of narrow gray sheets.

A pencil.

A small square stamp with no ink pad because stamp itself held blue marking stone in base.

Magical office equipment.

Naturally.

Orsen pointed.

"You don't use stamp."

"Good."

"You read bundle cover. Read transfer line. If account mark and page range match, move bundle from left tray to transfer tray. Keep sequence. I stamp after."

"If mismatch?"

"Hold tray. Say mismatch."

"Missing page?"

"Hold."

"Damaged?"

"Hold."

"Unreadable?"

"Hold."

"If I think I know why?"

He looked at me.

"Do you?"

"No."

"Then hold."

I liked Orsen.

Not personally yet.

Professionally.

He handed first transfer sheet.

Account mark.

Shelf code.

Page range.

Nothing about debt terms.

Nothing about names except abbreviated holder initials on some bundle covers.

Private enough to matter.

Boring enough to protect.

First bundle:

E-14-77

pages 1 through 6.

Transfer line:

E-14-77

1-6.

I moved it.

Orsen stamped transfer line.

Blue mark flashed once, then went dull.

Next.

E-14-81.

Pages 1-4.

Match.

Transfer.

Stamp.

Third.

E-14-83.

Pages 1-9.

Cover said 1-8.

"Mismatch."

Orsen stopped stamp.

"What?"

"Transfer says one through nine. Bundle cover says one through eight."

He took bundle.

Did not open.

Placed HOLD.

"Good."

No investigation.

Next.

Paper work had rhythm.

Not exciting rhythm.

But satisfying.

Read.

Compare.

Move.

Pause.

Stamp.

Blue flash.

The magical stamp was probably some registry-authentication device.

I did not ask.

Growth again.

At maybe twentieth bundle, Orsen said, "You can ask."

I looked at him.

"What?"

"Stamp. You've looked at it twelve times."

Damn.

"What does it do?"

"Marks transfer clerk, office, date block. Harder to alter than ink."

"How?"

"Don't know."

Perfect.

"Who services it?"

"Registry instruments."

"How often?"

He stared.

I stopped.

"Sorry."

"Quarterly unless it starts ghosting."

"Ghosting?"

"Double mark."

"Cause?"

"Greg."

"Stopping."

He almost smiled.

Dialogue victory.

An hour passed.

My back wanted different chair.

I shifted.

Orsen noticed.

"Break?"

"Two minutes."

He nodded.

I stood.

Hands stretched.

Right leg loosened.

No issue.

Across room two other clerks worked with larger ledgers.

One woman had tiny brass page-weight that crawled itself half inch whenever she turned page.

I looked once.

Then away.

Not everything magical needed me.

This city was becoming increasingly inconsiderate about that.

I sat again.

Second hour started with damaged bundle.

Not dramatic.

Water stain along lower edge.

Cover page readable.

Page range maybe complete.

"Damage," I said.

Hold.

Then three matches.

Then one shelf code mismatch.

Hold.

Then a bundle with loose thread binding.

"Damage?"

Orsen looked.

"Hold."

"Even pages intact?"

"Binding is part of transfer condition."

Good.

I stopped trying to infer categories and just used rule.

Halfway through second hour door opened.

A woman entered carrying a red corded packet.

Not Vale clerk.

Clothes too expensive for archive dust.

Dark blue outer coat.

Silver buttons.

No obvious artifact.

She spoke to front clerk quietly.

Front clerk pointed toward Orsen.

She came over.

Orsen's posture changed.

Not fear.

Formality.

"Mistress Ceral."

"Orsen. I need transfer copy for Morrow account block."

"Which mark?"

She gave one.

I kept eyes on my sheet.

Private account.

Not mine.

Orsen checked shelf list.

"That block is in today's transfer."

"Then I need it before transfer closes."

"If it has moved, request through consolidated desk after stamp."

"I need it now."

"If it has not moved, I can route request. If it has moved, consolidated desk."

Her voice tightened.

"Orsen."

His did not.

"Mistress Ceral."

Same words.

Different war.

I looked at next bundle.

M-22-11.

Not Morrow maybe.

Could be.

Didn't matter.

Transfer sheet matched.

I moved it.

Orsen stamped.

Ceral noticed me.

"Who is this?"

Orsen said, "Service assistant."

There.

Role within role.

She looked at my tray.

"Does he have access to account papers?"

"Cover marks and page ranges only."

"He can read names."

"Initials where present."

"That is still access."

Orsen said, "Authorized under transfer protocol."

She looked at me.

I said nothing.

This was not my argument.

Her gaze dropped to crutches.

Then back to face.

No comment.

Good.

"Which bundles have moved?" she asked.

Orsen said, "I cannot disclose other account transfer status to you. Give front clerk your mark and request."

"I gave you mark."

"Give front clerk."

She stood still.

Then turned and left table.

No explosion.

No villain reveal.

Just person accustomed to access meeting procedure.

I waited until she was out of earshot.

"Do I need to know who that was?"

"No."

"Excellent."

Orsen stamped next line.

We continued.

Ten minutes later front clerk brought a request slip.

Orsen checked it.

Looked at HOLD tray.

One bundle there matched requested account mark.

Interesting.

He took it.

Not to Ceral.

To another clerk.

"Condition hold before request."

Other clerk nodded.

That was all.

I did not ask why.

I did not read name.

I did not connect imaginary dots.

Debt office contained other people's lives.

Being allowed near paper did not make them mine.

We finished expected stack with maybe six minutes left.

Orsen counted transfer tray.

Checked hold slips.

Four holds.

One page-range mismatch.

One water damage.

One shelf-code mismatch.

One loose binding.

No numbers beyond that mattered.

He looked at clock.

"Done."

"Full scope?"

"Yes."

He signed my service slip.

Then stamped it.

Blue flash.

"Take main office or front desk here?"

"Front can post."

"Today?"

"Usually before close."

Usually.

Good word.

I brought slip to front.

Different clerk entered account mark.

Checked service record.

"Three principal-equivalent posted."

"Current balance?"

She looked.

"Twenty-three copper principal-equivalent."

There.

Real.

Not future.

Not maybe.

Twenty-three.

"Any other account change?"

"No."

"Next routine review still same date?"

"Yes."

She gave small stamped receipt.

No fee.

I folded it into shoulder bag with prior Vale paper.

Then walked outside.

I still had eight copper.

That was strange part.

When debt had been larger relative to everything, reducing it felt like money disappearing into hole.

Now I could see two separate things.

Cash bought options now.

Debt reduction bought fewer claims on later.

Neither was fake.

Neither was same.

I stood outside East Annex while carts passed.

For first time, twenty-three looked meaningfully smaller than twenty-six.

Not small.

Smaller.

Stair step.

Good.

I did not celebrate by spending money.

This was heroic restraint and should be recorded by historians.

Instead I went home.

Lyssa was at table.

Cala sorting dried beans.

Tavi drawing what appeared to be fish stabbing building.

Continuity.

Lyssa looked up.

"Three?"

I held up receipt.

"Posted."

"Twenty-three?"

"Twenty-three."

She smiled.

Not huge.

Enough.

"Nice."

Cala said, "Good."

Tavi said, "Can debt buy things?"

"No."

"Then why have it?"

"Excellent question."

Lyssa laughed.

Cala did not.

"Don't teach him that."

"I didn't create debt."

"You created yours."

Technically previous Greg had.

Same body.

Same legal person.

Mine enough.

"Fair."

Tavi looked between us.

"What happens when it gets zero?"

I answered before joke.

"Then Vale doesn't have that claim on me anymore."

He thought.

"So you're done?"

"With that debt."

"Then get zero."

Children remained strong strategists.

"Working on it."

Later I opened notebook.

Wrote:

VALE

SERVICE CREDIT POSTED: 3 C PRINCIPAL-EQUIVALENT

CURRENT: 23 C

CASH: 8 C

NEXT REVIEW UNCHANGED

NO FUTURE SERVICE OBLIGATION CREATED

Then stopped.

I almost wrote what job taught me.

Something about debt being time.

Something about cash and future claims.

Something very wise.

It all sounded like a man trying to turn two hours of moving paper into religion.

So I crossed blank space instead.

The role was simpler.

Debtor.

Still.

Less than yesterday.

But still.

That was fine.

Some roles did not end because chapter did.
