# CHAPTER 367

## THE INSTRUCTOR

Jori wanted me to teach three stagehands how not to die.

This was flattering until I met stagehands.

"They're not idiots," she said.

We stood inside theatre side door while rain tapped roof hard enough to make conversation private.

"You said that before I said anything."

"Your face did."

Apparently face remained traitor across districts.

"What am I teaching?"

"Moving under bad light."

That was more specific.

Jori pointed toward rehearsal floor.

Theatre had changed since last time.

Not structurally.

People.

A half-painted wall flat leaned near rear rail. Two rope coils sat on marked boards. Someone had chalked three pale lanes across floor from wings to center and then erased half of one badly.

Nessa stood on ladder adjusting hood over practice lamp.

Not Three Reeds.

Normal theatre equipment.

Good.

"We had two near collisions last week during blackout shift," Jori said. "No injuries. One dropped prop. One person walked into frame because they were watching glow mark instead of route."

"Why me?"

"Because you move with occupied hands and limited step options every day."

I looked at crutches.

"My hands are not occupied. They are infrastructure."

"Fine. Because your infrastructure makes you think about route before moving."

Better.

"And because you noticed rear spill during Three Reeds test before Nessa did."

From ladder Nessa said, "I heard that."

Jori raised voice.

"Good."

Nessa said something rude under breath.

Dialogue alive.

"I am not theatre safety instructor," I said.

"I know."

"I am not rigging specialist."

"I know."

"I am not blackout movement specialist."

Jori looked at me.

"Do you know how to walk through dark room without hitting things?"

"Usually."

"Can you explain why?"

Maybe.

"How much?"

Jori smiled slightly.

There he is.

"Two copper. Two hours. Today. You teach route discipline under low light. I own theatre rules. Nessa owns lamp. You do not certify anyone."

Clear.

"Three people?"

"Three."

"Who?"

"Mett, Olo, Brenna."

Brenna I knew by sight maybe.

Mett maybe not.

Olo definitely not.

No extortion Bren.

Different name.

Thank gods.

"What does success mean?"

Jori's expression changed.

Not annoyed.

Interested.

"They can cross marked route under reduced light, stop on lost orientation instead of guessing, communicate obstruction, and restart from known point."

"Carrying?"

"Empty first. Then one light prop each."

"No speed test."

"Agreed."

"No surprise obstacles."

"Agreed."

"No pretending actual blackout if enough light remains to cheat."

Nessa climbed down.

"That one is mine."

"Can you make it dark enough?"

She looked offended.

"Yes."

"Safely?"

More offended.

"Jori."

Jori said, "He asks everyone like that."

"I dislike him."

"You liked him last time."

"Conditions changed."

I said, "Healthy relationship."

Nessa pointed at me.

"Two hours. Then leave."

Good.

I accepted.

Two copper mattered.

Not desperate money.

Still money.

Cash would go eight to ten if paid.

Vale stayed twenty-three.

Different stair.

The three stagehands arrived together.

Mett was broad, maybe twenty-five, hair shaved at sides, talked before fully entering room.

"Jori said this isn't a test, which means it is probably a test in the way people say something isn't a test right before they remember it later when deciding whether you get shifts."

Jori said, "It isn't a test."

Mett nodded.

"Exactly."

Brenna was older than me by maybe ten years, narrow face, sleeves rolled unevenly, expression skeptical.

Olo was younger, maybe seventeen, tall and quiet enough that silence itself seemed intentional.

Three different problems already.

I stood at chalk line.

"I'm Greg."

Mett said, "We know."

Brenna said, "He knows. I know you have crutches. That's different."

Olo said nothing.

Excellent.

"Jori hired me for two hours to teach route discipline under low light. I am not grading you. I am not certifying you. If Jori secretly grades you afterward, haunt her."

Jori, behind me, said, "Useful opening."

"I establish trust."

"Poorly."

Mett raised hand.

"Can we haunt while employed?"

"Advanced course."

Brenna almost smiled.

Good.

I pointed to chalk route.

"Show me how you cross now. Full light."

Mett went first.

Fast.

Too fast.

He looked at destination, not floor.

Fine in full light.

Brenna slower, checked edges, stepped around rope coil even though outside route.

Olo followed chalk exactly.

Exactly enough to be problem.

At erased section he hesitated, then guessed where line continued.

There.

Three habits.

"Again," I said.

Mett groaned.

"Already?"

"We have achieved repetition."

"I hate training."

"Useful information."

Second crossing I stopped each at midpoint.

"Where is nearest known boundary?"

Mett looked around.

"Wing post."

Brenna said, "Rear rail."

Olo pointed at chalk under foot.

"That isn't boundary," I said.

He looked at me.

"It's route."

"What if mark disappears?"

He shrugged.

First words came quiet.

"Then I know roughly."

"Roughly is how frame gets forehead."

Brenna said, "That happened to Mett."

Mett touched forehead.

"Once."

"Last week."

"Still once."

Good.

I walked route myself.

Not to demonstrate speed.

Opposite.

"I cannot make lateral correction quickly to left."

I tapped left crutch.

"So before moving I want three things. Start point. Next boundary. Stop condition."

Mett said, "That's very dramatic for twelve steps."

"Twelve steps can still end in wall."

"Fair."

"Start point is where you know you are. Next boundary is something physical or fixed enough to orient from. Stop condition is what makes you stop rather than inventing route."

Brenna crossed arms.

"We have glow marks."

"Good. What happens when prop blocks one?"

"Use next."

"What happens when next is behind you?"

She frowned.

"Then stop."

"Yes."

"That's it?"

"Mostly."

She looked disappointed.

People wanted expertise to be secret.

Usually it was boring things done before panic.

Nessa reduced lamps.

Room dimmed.

Not dark.

Then dimmer.

Amber route marks near floor became visible.

Different from chalk.

Small painted tabs with weak stored glow.

I knew general family.

No need lecture.

"First pass," I said. "No props. No speed. Say boundary before moving."

Mett stared.

"Out loud?"

"Yes."

"This is humiliating."

"Good. Humiliation improves memory."

Jori said, "Not official theatre doctrine."

"Coward."

Mett went.

"Wing post."

Step.

"Low mark."

Step.

"Center seam."

He reached erased-chalk section, but low-light route used glow tab at wall instead.

"Rear lamp bracket."

Better.

Brenna did same with fewer words.

Olo moved too soon after naming mark, before eyes adjusted from brighter wing.

I stopped him.

"Why?"

He looked annoyed.

Good.

"I saw it."

"You saw one thing."

"That's enough."

"No."

Silence.

I knew this kind.

Not stupid.

Hated being corrected in public.

Maybe especially by nineteen-year-old with crutches.

Fair enough.

"Come here," I said.

He did.

"Look at center mark."

He did.

"Now tell me what's left of it."

"Frame."

"Right?"

"Rope."

"Behind?"

He glanced.

"Wing post."

"Now move."

He crossed cleanly.

"You weren't waiting for mark," I said. "You were waiting for room."

He nodded once.

That was enough.

Second pass Nessa reduced light again.

Mett said, "Can she stop doing that?"

Nessa said, "No."

"I respect consistency."

Now room was dark enough that faces became suggestions.

Glow marks stronger by contrast.

Jori stayed near wall.

I heard her more than saw.

"Carry props," she said.

Three objects waited.

Wooden bowl.

Rolled cloth.

Light frame corner.

Mett took bowl.

Brenna cloth.

"Why?"

"Frame changes width. You haven't practiced width."

"Brenna, frame. Olo, cloth."

"Why me?"

"You already route around things you don't need to. I want see if you overprotect width."

"That's rude."

"Educational."

Fine until bowl blocked low glow mark when held naturally.

"Stop," I said.

He froze.

Good.

"What do you know?"

"Wing post behind."

"Next boundary?"

"Center seam."

"Can you find without mark?"

"Floor seam."

"Use it."

No drama.

She kept staring at frame corner.

Not route.

"You're escorting object," I said.

"I don't want hit anything."

"Then know where you are. Object comes with you."

"Easy for you to say."

"I bring two long objects everywhere."

Pause.

Mett laughed first.

Brenna looked at crutches.

"Fine. Annoyingly fair."

She reset.

Second try better.

Olo with rolled cloth moved cleanly until Nessa's hood shifted slightly.

Light angle changed.

One glow tab became weaker.

He stopped.

Immediately.

No guess.

Good.

"What happened?" I asked.

"Mark changed."

"What do you do?"

"Stop. Find room."

"Do it."

He did.

There.

Teaching was mostly waiting for someone to make correct mistake.

Third round I added communication.

Not surprise obstacles.

Known obstruction.

A low empty crate placed partly across route.

Everyone saw it under full light first.

Then Nessa dimmed room.

"You're not testing memory of crate," I said. "You're practicing what happens when route isn't clear. First person finds it. What do you say?"

Mett said, "Crate."

"Where?"

"In way."

"Where?"

"Middle."

"Middle of what?"

He sighed deeply.

"Route between wing post and center seam, low obstruction, right half blocked."

"Good."

"That was many words."

"Fewer than ambulance."

Jori said, "We don't call it that."

"Whatever Carrow calls carrying idiot to healer."

Brenna said, "Stretcher team."

"Fewer than stretcher team."

Mett nodded.

"Less punchy."

They practiced.

Brenna's report was best.

Olo's too short.

"Low block, right."

"Which segment?"

"Wing to center."

"Good."

Then Mett tried to be helpful and moved crate in dark.

I heard scrape.

"Stop."

He froze.

"What?"

"Who told you move it?"

"Nobody."

"Then don't change map everyone else is using."

That landed.

Jori said, "Again."

Mett said, "I was fixing it."

Jori answered, "You were creating new condition without telling anyone."

Different voice from mine.

Authority.

Good.

We reset.

No punishment.

Again.

Unexpected occupational hazard.

Nessa raised lamps halfway.

Mett sat on floor.

Brenna leaned against frame.

Curious.

"Carefully."

"No, I mean with both crutches."

"Depends stairs. Rail changes everything."

"Up or down?"

"Both."

"You want second course."

"I thought he liked talking."

"He likes asking questions. Different disease."

"You learned quickly."

Direct.

"She said because I think about route before moving."

"Partly."

No discomfort.

"Makes sense."

Then drank water.

I liked him more.

Second hour we made routes less verbal.

Full spoken boundary every move was training scaffold, not actual backstage behavior.

Jori wanted short calls.

We settled on three useful ones already close to theatre language:

HOLD.

BLOCK.

CLEAR.

Jori owned exact meanings.

HOLD meant stop movement, keep position.

BLOCK meant route segment obstructed, followed by location.

CLEAR meant stated segment checked and open, not whole room safe forever.

I did not invent theatre vocabulary.

Important.

We practiced pairs.

One moved.

One watched route from fixed point.

Then swapped.

I became instructor instead of participant.

Strange.

Sword grip.

Mana circulation.

How to sleep in wet armor.

How to tell young adventurer that bravery was not substitute for map.

Some died anyway.

It just removed stupid deaths from pile.

That was enough.
