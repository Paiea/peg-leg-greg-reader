# CHAPTER 400

## THE SOJOURNER

I paid one copper for hot water.

Best purchase of expedition.

Possibly best artifact too.

Nobody liked that comparison.

North Reach bathhouse had four stone basins separated by waist-high walls, one cold channel, one hot channel, and a woman at desk who looked capable of denying civilization to anyone who annoyed her.

I gave her copper.

She bit it.

Normal.

"Half bell."

"Can I buy another half?"

"After first half."

"Excellent system."

She stared.

I stopped talking.

Also excellent system.

Hot water reached shoulder and every muscle in body immediately filed complaint.

There.

That was the problem with being busy.

Body waited until safe before becoming dramatic.

Right shoulder loosened slowly.

Palm stopped feeling like somebody had packed it with gravel.

Forearm still sore.

Nothing healed.

Everything became less stupid.

I sank lower.

First life had baths too.

Better ones sometimes.

Worse ones often.

Hot spring under ruined keep.

Copper tub in mountain inn.

One terrible adventurer bath where three men argued about whose blood clogged drain.

This was better because nobody was dying.

Also I was nineteen.

Again.

Still weird.

Less weird than before.

I thought of Carrow.

Cala's kitchen.

Tavi's noise.

Lyssa.

Theatre.

Vale.

My chest did small thing.

Not pain.

Pull.

Home.

Then I thought of map upstairs.

Kettle Vale.

White Span.

Lume River.

Wrong sunrise.

Pull opposite direction.

Interesting.

Maybe belonging was not rope.

Maybe it was weight.

Home could pull without preventing movement.

I liked that better.

Bath woman knocked wood against stone partition.

"Five minutes."

Cruel.

I stood carefully.

Shoulder better.

Still there.

Good.

I did not buy second half.

One copper had done job.

There.

Restraint.

Cash fourteen.

Vale twenty-three.

Still not dying.

I dressed and returned to Survey office smelling less like hound, marsh, wind, and bad decisions.

Sori noticed first.

"You look human."

"Expensive."

"One copper."

"Exactly."

Dask looked at shoulder.

"Range improved?"

"Do not turn bath into test protocol."

"I wasn't."

"You were halfway there."

He considered.

"Maybe."

Growth.

Ira Fen had extension papers ready.

Three sheets.

One station stamp.

One regional seal.

One line I actually cared about.

**OBSERVATIONAL CONTINUITY AUTHORIZED THROUGH WHITE SPAN / LUME RIVER.**

Vessa read everything.

Sori read everything again because that was Sori.

Dask tried to read over shoulder.

Sori moved page three inches away.

He moved with it.

She moved again.

He stopped.

"I'm not touching."

"You're breathing on custody."

"Air is not custody interference."

"Keep arguing and it becomes one."

Ira stamped page.

"Food through Kettle Vale covered. Public lodging covered if station bunks unavailable. White Span local boats are not automatically covered unless survey use is authorized there. Personal purchases remain personal."

Greg economy loved sentences like that.

Vessa asked, "Route conditions?"

Ira tapped map.

"North basin road dry. Wind moderate. Two long grades. One white-stone causeway across lower grass floodplain. No weather hold. Kettle Vale has well, station desk, inn, repair stable, market on alternating days. Today is not market day."

Damn.

Ira continued.

"White Span road beyond Vale is open. River crews want outside witnesses at dawn if reflection repeats. That means you need Kettle Vale tonight. Leave within half bell."

There.

Bath then north.

Promise kept.

We packed.

Nothing dramatic.

That was becoming its own pleasure.

Kel checked straps.

Sori sealed papers.

Dask tied pencils again even though wind inside basin looked less violent.

Maro stood outside looking north like person who had done this before and forgotten enough to enjoy it again.

Vessa counted people.

Six.

Always six.

I checked bag.

Quietener.

Tavi rock.

Knife.

Food.

Water.

Paper.

Fourteen copper.

No oval.

Artifacts had geography now.

Some home.

Some Merecross.

Some with me.

Collection becoming map too.

We left North Reach Station before second bell.

High basin swallowed us differently than ridge.

Wind still existed, but no longer had teeth.

Dark grass bent in huge sheets from west to east.

White stone ribs rose from ground every few hundred paces like backs of buried animals.

Road was pale hardpack with black drainage cuts.

Long sightlines.

Nothing could hide unless it tried very hard.

I liked it immediately.

Then disliked it after forty minutes because long sightlines made distance rude.

You could see hill forever before reaching it.

Maro said, "That one is first grade."

I looked at hill.

"That one?"

"Yes."

"We've been walking toward it since birth."

"Maybe longer."

Dask said, "Approximately forty-three minutes."

Sori groaned.

"Nobody asked."

"Greg implied duration."

"Greg implied suffering."

"Those can overlap."

Hard to argue.

First grade climbed steadily.

Not steep.

Worse.

Steady meant no excuse to stop.

Shoulder warmed, then complained.

Palm okay.

Right crutch repair held.

At top Vessa gave five breaths without me asking.

I took eight.

Nobody counted except Dask.

He did not say number.

Actual growth.

From ridge crest we saw Lume River clearly for first time.

Silver line far north.

Too far for water detail.

Wide enough to matter.

White Span itself invisible.

Kettle Vale somewhere lower, hidden behind rolling grass and stone.

I stood there longer than needed.

Not because magic.

Scale.

Carrow had become small in imagination behind me.

Not less important.

Just geographically small.

Different.

In first life I had crossed kingdoms.

This should not impress me.

It did.

Second life made distance personal again.

Maybe because there were people waiting behind it now.

First life at nineteen had mostly wanted away.

Current nineteen had something to return to.

That made north feel farther.

And better.

Maro said, "You okay?"

"Yes."

"You're staring."

"River."

"Still there."

"Good."

We descended.

At lower grass floodplain road became causeway.

White stone blocks, three feet above surrounding dark grass.

Grass looked solid until wind moved it and revealed water shining beneath.

Floodplain.

Not marsh exactly.

Seasonal sheet water hidden under grass roots.

Causeway had gaps every fifty paces with narrow bridges over flow cuts.

No rails.

Because apparently this region hated decorative safety.

Kel said, "Watch right edge. Some stones undercut."

Local sign agreed.

**KEEP CENTER / WHITE MARKS SAFE / BLUE MARKS UNDER REVIEW**

Blue paint appeared every so often.

Dask loved system.

"Clear visual coding."

Sori said, "Please marry road."

"Road has no legal personhood."

"Coward."

Halfway across, grass to east moved wrong.

Not wind.

Something long traveled beneath.

We all saw.

Maro stopped.

Shape maybe ten feet.

Could be water channel.

Then head surfaced.

Pale.

Flat.

Two dark side fins.

Mud sailer maybe.

There.

The bridge animal again? Not same animal. Too far.

Species perhaps.

Creature surfaced once, breathed with ugly wet cough, then vanished.

Dask whispered, "That was likely a mud sailer."

Maro: "Likely."

"Could we stop?"

Vessa: "No."

"For thirty seconds?"

"No."

"Ten?"

"Walk."

He walked.

Greg understood pain.

I looked anyway while moving.

Nothing returned.

Good.

No monster fight.

Just animal.

Road after causeway climbed second grade.

Shorter.

Steeper.

My shoulder disliked more.

I stopped once.

Ten breaths.

Asked before world asked for me.

There.

Smart.

At top Kel offered to take bag.

I almost said no.

Then checked shoulder.

"Twenty minutes."

He took it.

No ceremony.

Sori saw.

Said nothing.

Good.

Locked Greg had turned people into variables.

Normal Greg could let variable carry bag.

Maybe that was healthy.

Too high.

I endured for three minutes.

"Lower strap."

He adjusted.

"More."

Adjusted.

"There."

"You're difficult."

"I'm injured, not dead."

"Debatable."

Normal crew.

Road crossed little stone marker field near afternoon.

Dozens of waist-high white pillars on both sides.

Each had shallow bowl carved top.

Most empty.

Some held water despite no rain.

Dask slowed.

Maro said, "Old wind wells."

"Collect condensation when nights cool. Travelers used to drink."

"Not now. Birds shit in them."

Magic defeated by bird.

Excellent.

One bowl had tiny silver shimmer around edge.

I looked.

Kept walking.

Not everything needed investigation.

This was becoming real skill.

Late afternoon road finally dropped.

Kettle Vale appeared all at once.

Not valley exactly.

Bowl between white stone ridges.

Maybe forty buildings.

More than Lark Spur.

Less than Carrow.

Roofs dark slate.

Several round steam vents.

One tall black chimney.

Fields in terraces.

Roads converging from east and north.

And kettles.

Actually kettles.

Huge iron ones outside buildings, some big enough to bathe horse.

I stopped.

"Why?"

Maro smiled.

"You'll like this."

"That worries me."

We entered under stone arch painted:

**KETTLE VALE / HOT SPRINGS, BREWERS, ROAD REST**

There.

Of course.

Natural hot water.

Brewer town.

Road stop.

Name literal enough.

Smell hit next.

Mineral water.

Malt.

Smoke.

Bread.

I forgave region immediately.

Station desk occupied front room of inn called **The Copper Kettle**.

Too obvious.

Perfect.

Desk clerk was broad man named **Orren Pike**, beard braided in two little ropes, voice too formal for room.

"Good afternoon. Survey travelers under regional extension?"

Vessa showed papers.

"Yes. Six."

"We have been informed of your likely arrival. Accommodations are available in rear annex. Evening meal covered under road assignment. Bathing is not included because our communal spring is separately operated."

I looked at Vessa.

She said, "You already bought one."

Cruel again.

Orren continued like tax record.

"White Span departure recommended before first bell. Dawn reflection, if it repeats, begins shortly after first direct light upon western bank. Ferrymaster requests observers positioned before sunrise. Road distance approximately six miles."

Dask asked, "Duration of prior reflections?"

Orren consulted note.

"Morning one: about six minutes. Morning two: eight or nine. Morning three: seven. Morning four: five. Times are local estimates except morning four, which was clocked at five minutes and twenty breaths by bridge assistant."

Good.

Better evidence already.

"Any effect on people?" Vessa asked.

"None reported."

"Boats?"

"None reported."

"Fish?"

Orren paused.

"One ferryman claims fish rise toward false light. Second ferryman says first ferryman lies when sober. I cannot classify."

I liked Orren too.

Sori said, "Market?"

Orren looked at her.

"Tomorrow afternoon."

I suffered quietly.

Vessa said, "Tomorrow is not today."

"I know."

"Good."

Six bunks.

Again.

No argument this time.

Luxury.

Of course.

Human.

Far from home.

And ordinary.

A road with strange grass.

Probably something loud.

Lyssa probably had own day entirely independent of my dramatic wind ridge.
