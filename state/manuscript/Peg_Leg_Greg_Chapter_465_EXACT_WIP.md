# CHAPTER 465

## THE WITNESS

Mabel woke me before sunrise by putting a bowl near my head.

Not on my head.

Near it.

Hospitality had limits.

"Eat."

I opened one eye.

"Good morning."

"It'll improve after the bowl."

The bowl held oats, chopped dried apple, and enough salt to make the whole thing taste intentional.

My shoulder had stiffened overnight.

Not worse exactly.

Stiffer.

I sat up against the White Reed wall and tested it before breakfast because apparently I had become a man with morning procedures.

Forward lift.

Fine.

Side lift.

Ache.

Overhead.

Still no.

Crutch load.

Stable.

Good enough for maintained road.

Mabel watched me press into the right crutch.

"You always do that?"

"Recently."

"Comforting answer."

"I have others."

"Keep them."

I ate.

The district sample sat wrapped in spare cloth inside the pack, seal checked again before I closed it.

Filled.

Sealed.

Intact.

Still capable of disappointing everyone.

Jonah had already left for Morrow before dawn.

Mabel said Helena sent him back because apparently sleeping indoors was now an inefficient use of young men with legs.

I returned Jonah's flask to him last night.

One less thing.

Mabel handed me a folded heel of bread when I stood.

I looked at it.

"Route-note copy," she said.

"You already fed me for that."

"That was floor and stew. This is breakfast theft prevention."

"Different contract."

"Very."

I put it in my pocket.

"You're ruthless."

"I've lived at a ford for twenty-six years."

Fair.

I left White Reed in gray light.

The maintained road felt obscene.

Flat enough that I could see wheel ruts.

Wide enough for two carts in places.

No slide scar.

No hidden lower shelf.

No need to ask basalt formations what time it was.

I made better speed and hated my shoulder less for it.

Morrow appeared after second bell as rows first, then roofs.

The lower channels were visible before people.

Water still moved.

That mattered.

How much?

Not from road.

I did not turn moving water into answer because I'd apparently developed a personality defect.

At the first cooperative gate, two orchard workers looked up from a channel board.

One recognized me.

Not personally.

By crutches.

And probably because Morrow currently had one traveling amputee associated with its water problem.

"That's him."

I considered asking which part.

Didn't.

Helena met me outside the packing shed.

She had flour on one sleeve and no interest in greeting ceremony.

Her eyes went to my shoulder, then pack.

"Bottle?"

"Intact."

She exhaled.

"Good."

Then, after half a beat:

"You?"

"Also mostly intact."

"Mostly wasn't in the contract."

"It was implied by the payment structure."

She closed her eyes briefly.

"Come inside before my father finds you."

Garrick found me first.

Of course.

He came from the channel side with his wrapped knee stiff and one hand on a walking stick he had not used when I left.

"You reached it."

Not a question.

"Yes."

"Water?"

"Visible flow present."

His face changed.

Hope first.

Then irritation because apparently Jonah had already warned everyone about my answer.

"Greg."

"I know."

"Was there water or not?"

"Yes."

"Thank you."

"That does not tell us whether flow is normal."

"There he is," Helena said.

Garrick looked at her.

"Don't encourage him."

"I'm not sure anyone could."

That almost became a family joke.

Then a bell rang at the lower shed.

Not alarm.

Meeting bell.

Helena's mouth tightened.

"They've been waiting."

"Who?"

"Everyone who thinks your bottle belongs to their argument."

Excellent.

The cooperative room had been designed for fruit tallies.

Long table.

Wall boards.

Hooks for scales.

Not enough chairs for politics.

Morrow had solved this by making angry people stand.

Jonah was near the back with Ada and June absent, thankfully.

Two lower-row growers stood beside Helena's brother-in-law? No. I did not know that. A man with stained cuffs stood beside them. Unnamed.

Across the room were three north-channel workers.

One was the saw carrier I had met on the return road.

Good.

No new name needed.

At table end sat a woman in Lakeward district gray with a brass seal case.

She was new.

She mattered.

Naming guardrail internally: check before naming. I had not checked registry here in prose, so avoid name. She could remain district examiner.

The district examiner looked at me.

"Greg?"

"Yes."

"Contract field walker?"

"Yes."

"Bottle first. Conversation second."

I liked her immediately.

I put pack on table carefully.

Shoulder thanked me.

Unwrapped cloth.

Showed seal without handing it across until she cleared space.

She checked number against form.

Checked wax edge.

Checked cap.

Asked where filled.

"Main center outflow immediately below Mirror Spring lip. Bottle did not contact basin or stone."

"Witness?"

"None at sampling. An upper-ridge grower arrived afterward."

"Did he handle bottle?"

"No."

"Did anyone?"

"No."

She took it.

That was the first moment since Mirror Spring when the bottle stopped being my problem.

Not entirely.

But physically.

Wonderful sensation.

She placed it in a padded district box and closed the latch.

"Chain accepted for transport. Sample goes Lakeward."

Helena leaned forward.

"Accepted means his contract is complete?"

The examiner looked at Garrick.

"Contract says valid district supplemental source sample returned with completed seal form. This is returned. Form is complete. Seal is valid on inspection. Analysis isn't a condition of his field pay unless you wrote something different on your copy."

Garrick said, "We didn't."

"Then pay him."

Twelve copper.

Simple sentence.

Beautiful sentence.

Garrick reached for purse.

One north worker said, "Before we start paying people, can we hear what he found?"

Helena turned.

"The pay isn't a vote."

"Didn't say it was."

"Sounded like it."

The examiner tapped one finger on table.

"Pay the contract. Then field statement. Then arguments. In that order if you'd all like me to remain here."

Nobody tested her.

Garrick counted twelve copper onto table.

I counted it again because I respected money more than theater.

Twelve.

Cash twenty-four.

Vale twenty-three.

Ten-copper floor suddenly looked less like floor and more like a thing beneath me.

Not rich.

Not even close.

But the quest had paid.

Real money.

For real value.

That mattered.

I put coins away.

The examiner opened my district notes.

"Now. Start with route."

I did.

White Reed.

Sleeping Stone.

Slide.

Rooted bypass.

Three Sisters lower bench cut.

North-face upper track unused under explicit safety warning.

Southern stone descent.

Lower maintenance shelf.

Cracked plank rejected.

Shallow ford.

Observed pulse one day, lower/stabler next morning.

Recent branch cutting.

Rejoin with old lower bench.

Mirror Spring.

No embellishment.

The room got quieter as route became less abstract.

The saw carrier interrupted once.

"You used the lower shelf?"

"Yes."

"Both ways?"

"Yes."

"Morning return?"

"Yes."

He nodded to another north worker.

"Told you."

The examiner looked up.

"Told him what?"

"That lower shelf still passes if the seep is down."

"Recorded anywhere?"

He grimaced.

"Not district. Water crew knows it."

The examiner wrote something.

He watched her write and looked like a man seeing his own irritation become permanent.

I understood.

Then spring observations.

"Main center outflow visibly flowing. Left side seep visibly stronger than right by eye only. No calibrated flow measurement. No known normal available to me."

Garrick shifted.

"But water was there."

"Yes."

"The old path was right."

Helena said, "Father."

He ignored her.

"White Reed, Sleeping Stone, Three Sisters, Mirror Spring. All there."

"The route memory was materially useful," I said.

His face brightened.

"There."

"That does not establish every explanation attached to it."

His face unbrightened.

Helena rubbed forehead.

"He did warn you."

One north worker said, "What explanation?"

Garrick turned.

"That water above Morrow existed before your damned share boxes."

"Nobody said it didn't."

"You act like paper invented the hill."

"And you act like your grandfather personally dug the spring."

There.

Human beings.

Finally escaping evidence into identity.

The examiner raised voice slightly.

"Field statement."

It worked.

Not because they agreed.

Because she could leave with bottle.

I continued.

"A newer secondary diversion flap sits below catch basin, half closed during observation, directing some water east."

Morrow side of room changed.

North side changed differently.

Helena's eyes sharpened.

Garrick said, "How much?"

"I did not measure volume."

"Quarter?"

"Upper-ridge grower later estimated less than roughly a quarter by old share marks. I did not verify that."

Saw carrier spoke.

"That's about right."

A lower-row grower said, "Of course you'd say that."

"Because I can count marks?"

"Because your trees drink them."

"Our nursery keeps half your replacement stock alive."

"Not if our fruit drops first."

The room went again.

Examiner let it this time.

Interesting.

Maybe because argument now concerned choice, not field fact.

Helena looked at north workers.

"Is the fire cistern actually on that branch?"

"Yes."

"Directly?"

"Nursery line feeds the cistern reserve after second split."

"How full?"

Pause.

"Yesterday morning, about two-thirds."

"Recorded?"

The worker's jaw tightened.

"Board at nursery."

Examiner wrote.

Helena nodded once.

"Good. That's useful."

Garrick looked at her.

"Useful for what?"

"For not accidentally solving fruit by emptying fire water."

"One notch isn't emptying anything."

"You don't know that."

"Neither do they."

"Exactly."

Jonah, from back:

"That's what I've been saying."

Everyone looked at him.

He regretted participation instantly.

I almost smiled.

The examiner turned to me.

"Upper-ridge grower statement."

I read it.

East diversion existed for years according to him.

Current flap replaced after spring thaw.

Less than quarter estimate by share marks.

Lakeward office supposedly holds written schedule.

Spring looked one share-mark lower than same season last year according to his memory.

Basis: moss-color band and seasonal experience.

Not personally recorded.

Not calibrated.

Garrick seized one piece.

"Lower."

North worker seized another.

"Diversion existed for years."

Helena said, "You're both doing the thing."

"What thing?" Garrick asked.

"Taking the sentence you like."

Good.

I liked Helena.

The examiner apparently did too.

"Correct."

That annoyed Garrick more than when Helena said it.

The district woman pulled a second sheet from her case.

"Lakeward schedule copy. I brought it because your runner reached us before dawn."

Room changed again.

Good runner.

The examiner flattened paper.

"Mirror east branch is authorized as seasonal nursery and fire reserve draw. Maximum share is written as one quarter of marked catch division under ordinary condition."

Morrow grower said, "So they can take a quarter while we're losing fruit?"

"Under ordinary condition," examiner repeated. "Drought restriction can alter share. I do not have authority in this room to declare restriction."

"Who does?"

"Lakeward water panel after current readings, or emergency local action under immediate safety provisions. Fruit loss alone is not written as emergency safety. Fire reserve can be."

That was not going to make anyone happy.

Excellent law.

The north saw carrier folded arms.

"Then Morrow's gate change was unauthorized."

Helena said, "Probably."

Garrick looked at her.

"Helena."

"What? We changed it without district order."

"We changed our lower exchange gate."

"Which affects shared channel."

"Because our trees are dropping fruit."

"Yes. And it can still be unauthorized."

Garrick's face went red.

Not rage exactly.

Humiliation.

Different.

Important.

He had hired me because he wanted evidence.

Now evidence was arriving and refusing to behave like ally.

I knew that feeling.

The examiner asked, "Who ordered the one-notch reduction?"

Silence.

Then Garrick said, "I did."

Helena turned fully toward him.

Jonah stared.

I had suspected cooperative action.

Apparently cooperative meant somebody made decision while everyone else was shouting.

Examiner: "Under what authority?"

"We were losing fruit."

"That's reason. I asked authority."

Garrick leaned on stick.

"The lower exchange gate sits on Morrow stone."

"Shared water agreement?"

"Old agreement."

"Still active."

"According to Lakeward."

"According to signed schedule."

There it was.

Garrick looked older suddenly.

Not defeated.

Cornered by a system he thought had forgotten the land.

He said, quieter, "When I was Jonah's age, if lower rows started dropping fruit, you changed water before you asked somebody a day's ride away whether the trees were thirsty."

Examiner didn't mock him.

"And when that change reduced another family's cistern?"

"We knew who lived uphill."

"Do you now?"

Garrick looked at north workers.

One of them looked away first.

That was answer enough.

Social density lost over time.

Paper replaced some of it.

Not all.

Maybe badly.

But something had changed beyond bureaucracy.

People no longer knew each other's channels intimately enough to improvise without collateral damage.

Helena said, "Can we put it back one notch until panel?"

Garrick snapped, "And lose another day?"

North worker said, "Yes."

Lower grower said, "Easy for you."

"Our nursery supplies your graft house."

"Next year doesn't help this year's fruit."

"Fire doesn't wait for fruit either."

Everyone had a sentence.

All of them true enough to hurt.

The examiner looked at her schedule.

"I can order no further changes pending panel review. I can also recommend restoring prior exchange setting because current change appears outside standing agreement."

"Recommend," Garrick said.

"Yes."

"Not order."

"Not under my field authority absent immediate safety finding."

He looked at Helena.

Then north workers.

Then me.

Why me?

Because he'd hired me.

Bad reason.

"You walked it," he said. "What would you do?"

No.

There it was.

The trap disguised as respect.

I could say not my authority.

True.

Cowardly if used alone.

I had information.

I had judgment.

I just didn't have mandate.

Different things.

"I wouldn't close anything farther," I said.

North side nodded.

Morrow side frowned.

"I would restore the one notch until you know what that change does to nursery and fire reserve."

Now Morrow side frowned harder.

Garrick's face shut.

I continued before anyone mistook this for verdict.

"That's not because spring proves north is entitled to more water. It doesn't. It's because you changed a shared control before you knew downstream and upper consequences, and now you know one claimed consequence includes fire reserve. Restore known setting while district checks current flows and share condition."

Lower grower said, "And Row Four?"

"Gets worse maybe."

He stared at me.

"That's your answer?"

"It's part of it."

"Easy when they aren't your trees."

"Yes."

That stopped him.

Not because good answer.

Because I didn't pretend otherwise.

"If there were a painless setting," I said, "you wouldn't need this room."

Garrick looked at sample box.

"I paid twelve copper for that?"

"You paid twelve copper to know more than you knew four days ago."

"And we still don't know what to do."

"You know the spring isn't visibly blocked. You know a legal seasonal east diversion exists up to written limit under ordinary condition. You know the lower route still passes under some conditions. You know your one-notch change may affect a nursery and fire reserve. You know spring flow may be lower than one grower's seasonal memory, though that isn't calibrated. You know you need current channel readings between source and Morrow before blaming one gate. That's more."

"Not enough."

"No."

He looked furious.

Then tired.

"No," he repeated.

Helena put hand on table.

"Father. Put the notch back."

He didn't answer.

"For one day," she said. "District checks. We count Row Four drop properly instead of shouting about it. North sends cistern board reading. If panel says restriction, then we fight about restriction with numbers."

North saw carrier said, "We'll send the board reading."

Lower grower said, "And if we lose another cart of fruit?"

Helena looked at him.

"Then we record that too."

He laughed without humor.

"Paper fruit."

"No. Dead fruit with a count. So nobody gets to tell us later it wasn't happening."

That landed.

Garrick stared at her.

Something changed there.

Not agreement.

Recognition maybe.

His daughter wasn't choosing paper over orchard.

She was trying to make orchard legible enough that distant authority couldn't flatten it.

Same problem as ridge grower's moss band.

Different direction.

Garrick finally said, "One notch back. Until tomorrow's reading."

North worker exhaled.

Lower grower swore softly.

Examiner wrote.

No one won.

Good.

The meeting dissolved into tasks.

That was more useful than agreement.

North crew would send nursery/cistern board copy.

Morrow would restore exchange gate one notch and record time.

Lower rows would count fresh fruit drop by row instead of estimating by mood.

District examiner would take sample to Lakeward and request same-day channel readings at two intermediate boxes plus current Mirror branch setting verification.

Garrick would complain.

Not formally assigned.

Likely.

"You can stop being useful now."

"Dangerous precedent."

"Sit."

"So that's it?"

"No."

"I knew you'd say that."

"You're learning."

"I hate it."

"Also learning."

"Did you like it?"

"The meeting?"

"The trip."

Different question.

Sleeping Stone.

A road that stopped being road.

Three shadows touching.

The moment Jonah turned back and I kept going alone.

"Parts."

"That's not an answer."

"It's a large answer made smaller."

He groaned.

Good.

Then I said, "Yes."

He stopped.

"Yeah?"

"Yeah."

I had.

Even hurt.

Even hungry.

Even while being reminded that adventures were mostly inconvenient terrain between arguments.

Something in me had liked waking up and not knowing exactly what the day would require.

Not because danger.

Danger was expensive.

Because distance changed the questions.

This route kept handing me systems I didn't own yet.

Old stories that were maps but not instructions.

Water that could be both shared and fought over.

People who were right at different scales.

I liked that.

That was potentially a problem.
