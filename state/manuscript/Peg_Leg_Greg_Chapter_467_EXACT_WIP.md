# CHAPTER 467

## THE PROSPECTOR

The Bracken Vale cart left before the lake decided what color it wanted to be.

Gray first.

Then silver.

Then one long strip of pale yellow where the morning sun found a gap between ridges.

I watched it from the back bench while Lakeward became roofs, then smoke, then nothing behind wet fields.

Sixteen copper.

Twenty-three with Vale.

One paid seat already spent.

No contract waiting at the other end.

That was new.

I had paid four copper because a broken road sounded interesting.

Financial discipline remained alive but had begun making questionable friends.

The cart carried sacks, two empty milk cans, a woman with a basket of green onions, and a man who slept through every rut with professional commitment.

Nobody needed a name.

Good.

My shoulder was better again.

Not healed.

Better.

Right crutch load no longer produced immediate complaint. Overhead still pulled the bruise sharply enough that I avoided it.

The trial limb stayed wrapped behind my pack.

Road was dry stone and packed earth, but a moving cart was not where I wanted to experiment with a shop's unfinished property.

We climbed east.

Lakeward country changed slowly.

Reed flats gave way to low orchards, then scrubby slopes where black rock showed through red soil.

Fog sat in the folds between hills.

Not thick enough to hide road.

Enough to make distance dishonest.

The driver stopped once at a ridge post to turn a small square plate from white side to black.

I watched.

"Fog mark?"

"East face."

"Meaning?"

He pointed without looking at me.

"Black toward Bracken means carts coming west know this side was fogged before first ring. White means clear. Gets turned back at midday if it burns off."

Mechanical information.

Human-maintained.

No magic required.

"Useful."

"That's why we do it."

Excellent conversation.

We continued.

Bracken Vale appeared as a dark green cut between two ridges.

Not a town exactly.

A road village stretched along one side of a narrow valley, houses and workshops tucked behind hedges of shoulder-high fern. Water wheels turned in a shallow stream. Roofs were steep. Stone chimneys taller than Lakeward's.

The place smelled like damp leaves, hot iron, and something sweet being boiled.

No sea.

Still.

The cart stopped beside a long shelter with three route boards nailed under its eave.

One board pointed south:

**LAKEWARD / DAILY**

One east:

**LOW RIDGE ROAD / CINDER CROSS / 5C**

One older board had been painted over twice.

Under the newer paint I could still make out:

**SUNROAD**

Arrow northeast.

No fare.

No service.

There we go.

I got down carefully.

Shoulder fine.

Pack heavier than memory because memory was a liar.

The driver unloaded sacks.

"Sunroad Cut?" I asked.

He looked at the old board.

"You walking?"

"Considering it."

"Don't use bridge."

"I heard it washed out."

"Then you heard correctly."

"Upper road?"

He shrugged.

"People go up."

"People come back?"

He looked at me for first time.

"Usually."

Useful distinction.

"Three Rays pieces?"

Another shrug.

"People come looking."

"Find them?"

"Sometimes they come back dirty."

I smiled.

He didn't.

Bracken Vale had a small public board beside the shelter.

Work notices.

Lost goat.

Wheelwright apprentice wanted.

Road closure south after heavy rain, crossed out.

One hand-lettered card:

**OLD SUNROAD: WEST WALK ACCESS OPEN TO MARKER 7. BEYOND 7 NOT MAINTAINED. BRIDGE ACCESS CLOSED. RIDGE OFFICE ACCEPTS HAZARD NOTES. NO SALVAGE REMOVAL FROM ROAD STRUCTURES.**

There.

Much better than collector hearsay.

Marker Seven.

Walk access open.

No salvage removal from road structures.

That last line mattered.

If I found Three Rays hardware bolted to a wall, it belonged to road structure until somebody said otherwise.

Loose object?

Unknown.

Do not build legal theory from sign punctuation.

I copied notice.

Then found ridge office.

It occupied half of a stone building beside stream, other half apparently a cooper's shop because barrels were everywhere and the whole place smelled of wet oak.

Inside, one clerk sat behind a desk covered in route slips.

No reason to name him yet.

"Sunroad walk access?"

He looked at crutches.

Then at me.

Not rude.

Assessment.

"To Seven is public at your own risk. Surface gets rough after Four."

"Bridge?"

"Closed means closed."

"Upper road past Seven?"

"Not maintained. Not prohibited. Ridge office doesn't certify it."

"Any map?"

He pulled a sheet from drawer.

Not free.

"Two bits for route copy."

I paid.

Worth it.

The map was simple.

Bracken Vale.

Old Sunroad climbing northeast.

Markers One through Seven.

At Seven, old road forked.

Lower branch descended toward washed bridge.

Upper branch continued along ridge, then disappeared from current maintained map after a symbol shaped like three short lines over a circle.

I stared.

"That's Three Rays."

The clerk looked down.

"Old roadworks mark."

"What did it mean here?"

"Depends which old book you believe."

Good.

"What books?"

"One says service station. One says route-check station. One says marker house. There's no house now."

"Anything left?"

"Stone pad. Maybe some iron. Collectors dug around it enough that office posted the salvage rule."

"So people did find pieces."

"People found things they sold as pieces."

Better answer.

"Any verified?"

He leaned back.

"You buying?"

"Not yet."

"Then you're already smarter than half of them."

I liked Bracken Vale.

"Verified Three Rays brass came out of Sunroad twenty years ago when bridge repairs exposed an old storage box. That was before my time. Ridge record has it. Since then? Mostly fragments. Teeth. Glass chips. Stamped fittings. Some real, some probably dropped by people who came looking for real ones."

"Dropped intentionally?"

"If you can sell a three-copper brass scrap for twelve because it came from a famous road, eventually somebody notices brass is portable."

Human beings remained the strongest magical ecology.

"What about the saying? Three shadows."

He frowned.

"Which version?"

Excellent.

"Sunroad never lost a traveler who kept three shadows."

"Collector version."

"There's another?"

"My grandmother said, `Don't trust Sunroad until your third shadow points home.`"

Different.

"What does that mean?"

"She said it meant leave late enough that morning fog has burned but early enough to be off ridge before evening fog comes back."

That was plausible.

"Third shadow?"

"She also said I asked too many questions."

"Strong family tradition."

He almost smiled.

"There are three standing stones before Marker Five. People say your shadow touches each one as sun moves. Or used to. Trees changed. Road changed. Could be that."

So:

collector version made Three Rays sound magical;

grandmother version made it weather timing;

standing stones offered physical route image;

maker symbol happened to be three rays.

No clean answer.

Good.

"Any reason not to walk to Seven today?"

He looked outside.

"Fog lifting. Dry yesterday. No rain watch. Shoulder?"

"Bruised. Functional."

"You alone?"

"Yes."

"Then I'd turn at Seven."

"Because?"

"Because Seven is where office stops maintaining road and you don't know road."

Fair.

"Anything dangerous before?"

"Loose outer edge after Four. Don't lean on old rail. Marker Six has a spring crossing that makes stone slick. No bridge issue until lower branch after Seven."

"Animals?"

"Mostly goats."

"Hostile?"

"Only morally."

There it was.

One dry line.

He was allowed one.

I copied hazards.

Then asked salvage.

"Sign says no removal from road structures. What about loose surface finds?"

He sighed.

This question had history.

"If it's obviously road hardware, leave it and report location. If it's loose common scrap, ridge doesn't care unless marked. If it carries Three Rays stamp, leave it and report. If you're not sure, leave it and report."

"Finder's fee?"

"Sometimes, if office recovers something useful or catalogable. Don't plan your retirement."

"I already have a retirement plan."

"Good."

It was mostly not getting killed.

The ridge office sold no adventure permit.

Charged no access fee.

Offered no guide.

Just information.

I left with map and less ignorance.

Before walking Sunroad, I needed somewhere to leave unnecessary weight.

Bracken Vale had a bunkhouse behind the cart shelter.

One copper for floor space and locked pack cage until next morning.

I paid.

Sixteen to fifteen.

Ten-copper floor left five discretionary.

I kept notebook, water, food, repair wrap, one spare liner, road map, line quietener, current reader, and HT-LP-4 tile.

Lot Sixty-Seven stayed locked with main pack.

Trial limb stayed locked too.

That took weight off shoulder.

Good.

No need to carry every interesting object I owned into every interesting place.

I ate bread and cheese from a street stall for bits.

Then started Sunroad.

Marker One was boring.

A waist-high stone with a carved numeral and newer white paint.

Marker Two was also boring.

Wonderful.

Road climbed through fern and low trees.

Old paving appeared in patches under dirt, broad flat stones fitted tightly enough that cart wheels once had an easier life than I currently did.

At Marker Three, I found first old rail.

Iron posts along outer edge with nothing between them.

Maybe once cable.

Maybe timber.

Rust deep.

I did not lean.

The valley dropped away to my left.

Bracken roofs looked smaller than expected.

Shoulder remained manageable.

I rested.

Then Marker Four.

Road narrowed.

Outer edge had slumped in two places.

Not dramatic.

Enough that I stayed inside.

No action scene required.

Sometimes hazard's job was to make you walk correctly.

Beyond Four stood the first of three stones.

Not marker stones.

Tall narrow slabs on inside edge, each with a shallow polished face angled differently.

First was nearly black.

Second visible farther up.

Third maybe another hundred paces beyond that.

Three shadows.

I checked sun.

Late morning.

My shadow fell behind me and slightly downhill.

It did not touch first slab.

I moved along road.

At one point, shadow of my crutch crossed slab face.

Probably not what grandmother meant.

I wrote nothing yet.

Second standing stone had a groove near base.

Not natural-looking.

Horizontal slot.

Empty.

Third had same.

Interesting.

Could have held signboards.

Could have held brass frames.

Could have held drainage guides.

No stamp visible.

At Marker Five, road bent sharply east.

Fog had mostly burned away.

From there I could see farther ridge and, below it, a pale scar where something large had cut through forest.

Old bridge valley maybe.

The three standing stones behind me were no longer visible together.

I walked back twenty paces.

Two visible.

Another twenty.

All three.

Sun angle made each cast a separate narrow shadow across road.

Three shadows.

Not one.

Could simply be why phrase existed.

Then I noticed the polished faces.

Each faced a slightly different direction.

If you stood at a particular stretch between Four and Five, reflected sunlight from the first was visible.

Move farther.

Second flashed.

Then third.

Not simultaneously.

Sequential.

Three bright flashes as position changed.

I stopped.

That was interesting.

Not magic.

Could be deliberate sighting system.

Polished stone faces as daytime route confirmation through patchy fog?

Maybe.

But trees now different. Original sight lines unknown.

I recorded:

**Three standing slabs between M4-M5. Polished angled faces. From westbound/eastbound approach? Current late-morning position gives sequential reflected flashes while moving. Base slots on at least 2 and 3. Could be route/timing markers. Could be unrelated later modification.**

I turned around and walked approach again.

One flash.

Then second.

Then third.

Repeatable at current sun angle.

No need for twenty trials.

This was road, not laboratory.

Marker Six had spring crossing exactly where clerk said.

Water ran over paving in a thin sheet.

Stone green at edges.

I used inside rough strip.

Slow.

No drama.

Then Seven.

Marker Seven was taller than others and older.

New white paint covered one face.

Behind it, road forked.

Lower branch descended steeply into trees.

A newer board blocked it:

**BRIDGE CLOSED. NO THROUGH ACCESS.**

Upper branch climbed.

No barrier.

No maintenance paint.

And twenty paces up, half hidden by fern, sat a rectangular stone pad.

Three short lines over a circle were carved into its front edge.

There.

Actual Three Rays site.

I stayed at Seven first.

Office advice: turn here.

I had daylight.

Shoulder good enough.

Weather clear.

Upper road not prohibited.

But unknown.

I could inspect the stone pad without committing beyond.

Twenty paces.

Reasonable.

I went.

Pad was roughly room-sized.

No house.

Four post holes.

Rust stains.

Broken ceramic bits.

Old dig scars around two corners.

Collectors had absolutely been here.

One shallow channel crossed pad center.

At its end was a brass-colored fleck.

I crouched carefully.

Not brass hardware.

Just mineral stain.

Good lesson.

The carved Three Rays mark was clean enough to trace with finger without damaging anything.

Three lines.

Circle.

The three lines were not parallel.

They spread slightly.

Rays.

Maybe sun.

Maybe road branches.

Maybe branding.

I looked back toward standing stones.

Could not see them from pad.

So no immediate magical alignment revelation.

Excellent.

Then I saw something under fern on uphill side.

A piece of black stone.

Round edge.

My heart did one stupid thing.

I moved fern aside with crutch tip.

Half a disk.

Black.

Palm-sized.

One drilled hole near edge.

No visible stamp.

Could be Echo Step fragment.

Could be roof tile.

Could be someone's dinner plate if people here hated ceramics.

It was loose.

Marked?

I turned it only enough to see underside without lifting away.

Three tiny incised lines near broken edge.

Not full maker mark.

Maybe.

Salvage rule:

If not sure, leave and report.

Damn responsible governance.

I sketched position.

Pad uphill side, two handspans from northeast post hole, under fern.

Did not remove.

Then noticed the drilled hole had a polished inner rim.

Use wear.

Maybe mounted.

Interesting.

Worth reporting.

Could earn nothing.

Could be catalogable.

Could confirm black stone disks actually belonged at site.

That was enough.

I stood.

And looked up upper road.

Past the pad, paving narrowed into two wheel tracks swallowed by grass.

Maybe fifty paces ahead, an old iron frame leaned beside road.

Beyond that, ridge curved out of sight.

No marker eight visible.

No current sign.

No obligation.

I had come to inspect road.

I had reached maintained limit.

Found real site.

Found possible fragment.

Learned three-shadows story had at least one plausible nonmagical road-marker interpretation.

Enough for one day.

Turn around.

I did.

That was the interesting part.

Not because I lacked curiosity.

Because I had more now.

Curiosity was not an instruction to spend every remaining margin immediately.

On return, the three standing stones flashed in reverse order.

Third.

Second.

First.

I stopped after first.

Reverse sequence.

Could matter.

Could simply be geometry.

Notebook:

**Return westward near midday: reflected flashes occur reverse order. Potential directional confirmation? Need original sight lines / old roadworks description before stronger claim.**

At Bracken Vale ridge office, I reported black half-disk location.

The clerk pulled a small form.

"You didn't take it?"

"Sign said not to."

He looked genuinely surprised.

"That does simplify things."

"Disturbing."

He copied my sketch.

"We'll send somebody tomorrow or next ridge round."

"If it's real?"

"If it's marked road hardware and catalogable, there may be a finder payment. Usually one to three copper depending usefulness."

"If it's roof tile?"

"You get spiritual growth."

Two dry lines.

Bracken Vale was corrupting him.

"Can I see the old record you mentioned? The storage box from bridge repairs."

He considered.

"Public copy costs one bit to pull."

I paid.

He disappeared into back room and returned with a copied inventory sheet.

Twenty years old.

**SUNROAD LOWER BRIDGE WEST ABUTMENT / REPAIR EXCAVATION**

Items recovered from sealed stone box:

three brass toothed frames;

six black route disks, paired by stamped numbers;

three cloudy glass strips;

one folded copper instruction plate, corroded;

miscellaneous fasteners.

Disposition: transferred Lakeward district archive, then two frames and four disks later deaccessioned to roadworks collection after duplication of markings.

There.

Real system.

Not collector fantasy.

At least those component families belonged together historically.

I read line again.

Paired by stamped numbers.

Echo Step orphan disk at Lakeward had no pair.

Half-wheel frame had three slots and cloudy glass.

Maybe same family.

Not proof exact match.

But stronger.

"Instruction plate copied?"

"Partly. Corrosion ate most. Archive transcript says only fragments legible."

"Can I see transcript?"

"Lakeward archive."

Of course.

I had just left Lakeward.

Wonderful.

Then clerk added:

"Unless you want the ridge rubbing."

I looked at him.

"The what?"

"We have a charcoal rubbing from one legible line because old road supervisor thought it related to Marker Five."

"One bit?"

"No. That's on wall."

He pointed behind me.

I had walked past it twice.

Small framed paper.

Faded letters:

**... THIRD RETURN ... FACE WEST ... LIGHT ... HOME MARK ...**

Missing words.

Maybe instruction.

Maybe maintenance note.

Third return.

Face west.

Light.

Home mark.

Grandmother's version:

Don't trust Sunroad until your third shadow points home.

I stared longer.

Not proof.

But now story and damaged technical text touched each other.

That was different.

I copied exact visible fragment and gaps.

The clerk said, "Collectors love that one."

"Because it proves the saying?"

"Because they can make it prove whatever they're selling."

Excellent.

I closed notebook.

Tomorrow I had choices.

Return Lakeward and inspect archive transcript.

Stay Bracken and wait for ridge recovery of half-disk.

Walk Sunroad again and go beyond Seven.

Take Low Ridge Road toward Cinder Cross and keep moving east.

Four roads, effectively.

No contract.

No one waiting.

Sixteen copper became fifteen after bunk.

Five above floor.

Enough to choose.

Not enough to be stupid indefinitely.

I went back to bunkhouse.

Shoulder tired but not angry.

That was improvement.

I unwrapped trial limb only to inspect it.

Dry.

Healthy.

No wear today.

Magic stayed one hundred thirty-nine.

Lot Sixty-Seven stayed locked.

No purchases.

No artifact in my pocket.

Still, I had more than yesterday.

A real Three Rays road station.

A possible black route-disk fragment left where found.

A twenty-year inventory connecting frames, paired disks, and cloudy glass.

Three standing stones that flashed in sequence under current sun.

A damaged instruction fragment:

**third return**

**face west**

**light**

**home mark**

And an old saying that had changed shape between market and grandmother.

That was enough to make nine copper feel different.

Not cheap.

Not yet justified.

Different.

I lay back carefully so shoulder didn't complain.

The road had not given me treasure.

It had given me a better question.

Apparently that was becoming my preferred kind.
