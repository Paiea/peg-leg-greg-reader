## THE ARCHITECT

My shoulder hurt less in the morning.

My knee looked worse.

That was mostly because scrapes enjoyed color.

Red yesterday.

Dark red today.

A little purple around one edge because apparently my body had discovered decoration.

I sat on the Bracken bunkhouse floor with my back against the wall and did the same increasingly familiar inventory.

Head fine.

Neck fine.

Right shoulder sore, better than last night, still poor overhead.

Right knee scraped, flexion fine.

Cash fifteen copper.

Vale twenty-three.

Lot Sixty-Seven still mine.

Sunroad closed because part of it tried to become hillside.

And for once, instead of asking what happened next, I asked a different question.

What the hell was I doing?

Not existentially.

Too early for that.

Operationally.

I had been letting the road answer for me.

A job appeared.

I took it.

A market had something strange.

I looked.

I followed it.

Because losing count meant I had stopped measuring the journey as a project.

It had become weather.

Weeks.

Definitely weeks.

Long enough that I had learned the smell of cities I had not known existed.

Long enough that home no longer shared the same air.

Long enough that I had almost died more than once.

Long enough that twenty-four copper had briefly felt rich.

That part annoyed me.

I stared at purse.

Fifteen.

Five above the floor I had invented because being reduced to nine copper while traveling sounded stupid.

It was a good floor.

It was also poverty thinking dressed as policy.

I knew the difference.

Old me had managed supply lines that could keep people alive through disasters.

Current me could buy a broken brass frame or travel four copper without being able to comfortably do both.

That was not tragic.

It was simply unacceptable as a permanent state.

I wrote a heading.

**AUDIT.**

Then six lines beneath it.

**Body.**

**Magic.**

**Money.**

**Tools.**

**Information.**

**People.**

I looked at them.

"There you are."

The man on the bunk across from me rolled over.

"What?"

"Not you."

"Good."

He went back to sleep.

I started with body because body collected interest whether I planned for it or not.

### BODY

Left below-knee amputation.

Knee good.

Right leg doing too much.

Shoulders doing too much because crutches rented both hands and charged by the hour.

Hands better than first months.

Endurance better.

Terrain judgment much better.

Climbing still expensive.

Carrying still stupid without support.

Trial work limb had already changed the shape of some tasks even before final fitting.

Both hands free.

Short carries.

Standing without making every object somebody else's problem.

The thing wasn't a leg back.

It was access to hands.

More hands.

Still true.

The finished limb cost one silver and eighteen copper total.

Twenty copper deposit already paid.

Remaining balance still ugly because silver and copper weren't one neat arithmetic line I could flatten for emotional convenience.

Fine.

Priority remained.

But there was another body problem now.

I had treated conditioning as consequence.

Walking because road required it.

Climbing because route required it.

Resting because body forced me.

That wasn't training.

Training had intent.

I knew that.

Old me had known exactly which movement failed first under fatigue.

He had known where somebody else needed to stand because he had spent years learning what his own body could cover.

Current me was still discovering limits by accidentally arriving at them.

Not enough.

I wrote:

**Recover shoulder. Resume trial wear when stable. Finish work-limb funding. Build conditioning deliberately, not only through travel. Track carry / standing / climb / recovery.**

No heroic montage.

No pretending one limb would turn me into old body.

Just less accidental weakness.

### MAGIC

Fifty-nine minimal.

Fifty-three shaping.

One hundred thirty-nine external.

That number looked impressive until you remembered what one hundred thirty-nine contained.

Trembles.

Maybe-movements.

Tiny shifts.

Ambiguous results.

A few clean nudges.

One rock that moved enough to hit shoulder instead of skull.

Useful.

Not reliable.

Old me had been support.

Not `standing in back making people glow` support.

Real support.

I had made strong people harder to kill.

Faster to understand.

Better positioned.

Less likely to waste their best attack on wrong problem.

I had controlled lines.

Created time.

Interrupted bad timing.

Protected exits.

Moved information before panic could outrun it.

Made specialists better because I was there.

At the highest level, support wasn't helping.

It was changing what everyone else in the room was capable of getting away with.

That was what I missed.

Not rank letter.

Not applause.

That feeling.

Seeing six things going wrong and making five of them stop mattering.

Current magic couldn't do that.

Current body couldn't do that.

Current purse definitely couldn't do that.

But the brain that had learned how still existed.

Mostly.

I wrote:

**Next magic goal is not bigger. Reliable support use. Ask Hessa how to turn bounded external nudge/deflection into controlled repeatability without expanding claim. Prioritize interruption / stabilization / signaling / positioning before raw force.**

That felt right.

I didn't need to move a wagon.

If I could reliably move the right small thing at the right moment, that was already support.

A latch.

A line.

A falling fragment.

A tool just out of reach.

A projectile a handspan wrong.

A signal.

A balance point.

Not claims.

Targets.

Important difference.

### MONEY

Fifteen copper.

Twenty-three with Vale.

Twenty copper already sunk as work-limb deposit.

Several artifacts owned.

Some with resale value probably.

Not liquid.

Not counting them as grocery money.

I had earned more on one real field contract than on several days of tiny road jobs combined.

There.

That was data.

The little jobs were useful when stranded.

They were not progression.

Not anymore.

Two copper for washing something was fine if I needed two copper.

It was not a career plan.

The Mirror Spring contract paid twelve because uncertainty, route risk, evidence discipline, and trust had value.

What else could I sell at that level?

Field observation.

Artifact screening maybe, once reputation justified it.

Route assessment.

Hazard documentation.

Theatre work at higher responsibility if I returned long enough to build it.

Specialized carrying/inspection less likely until leg finished.

Support work eventually.

Not yet.

Information brokerage?

Dangerous word.

But knowing where things were and who cared absolutely had value.

I wrote:

**Ten copper remains survival reserve, not goal.**

Then:

**First liquid target: 30c cash above ordinary immediate needs, not counting Vale, limb deposit, or owned gear.**

Thirty wasn't wealth.

It was enough that a nine-copper artifact could appear and I wouldn't have to choose between opportunity and being one bad bunk away from stupidity.

I underlined it.

**Stop celebrating survival-floor liquidity as success. Build opportunity money.**

There.

That one felt mean.

Good.

**Three Rays: investigate as support system, not treasure. What problem did distributed frames/disks/shade glass solve for ordinary travelers?**

**Do not buy component until function / pairing / compatibility evidence crosses threshold.**

Still disciplined.

Unfortunately.
