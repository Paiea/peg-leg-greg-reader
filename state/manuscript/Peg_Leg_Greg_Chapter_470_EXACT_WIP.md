The Three Rays reconstruction occupied a long room with shutters on both east and west walls.

Not impressive at first glance.

Three waist-high iron frames sat on a wooden track about twelve paces long.

Each frame held a cloudy glass strip.

At the center of each was a black disk mounted behind the glass.

Brass toothed arcs allowed the disk assembly to rotate through limited positions.

On the wall above them:

**RECONSTRUCTION FROM SUNROAD RECOVERY RECORDS**

**REPLACEMENT FRAMES / REPLACEMENT GLASS IN TWO STATIONS**

**ONE ORIGINAL DISK PAIR USED FOR DEMONSTRATION**

**BEHAVIOR ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT CERTIFIED TO HISTORIC FIELD PERFORMANCE**

Good museum.

A staff demonstrator waited until group settled.

Also unnamed.

"This reconstruction is intended to show one plausible operating principle supported by surviving instructions and component wear. It is not a claim that every Sunroad station functioned identically."

I liked her too.

"But does it?"

At the end she held up a small white board with three drawn crescent shapes.

"The paired disks carry numbered notch families. Under this reconstruction, a traveler or road keeper could compare the observed light shape at one station against the expected paired shape at another. If the pair and frame orientation agreed, the station could provide a directional or timing confirmation even when distant landmarks were obscured."

One collector raised hand.

"So navigation."

"Possibly route confirmation. Not general navigation."

"Fog?"

"Direct heavy fog would reduce useful light. Surviving notes suggest shade or broken fog conditions may have been relevant. We do not know operating thresholds."

"This is why the surviving instruction fragment `do not read single disc` matters. A disk alone does not tell us enough. The relationship between paired markings, frame orientation, and expected light condition appears to have carried the useful information."
