# CHAPTER 492

## THE RESPONDENT

The morning after drinking with Antonius, somebody knocked on the door hard enough to make my head object on principle.

Not pain.

Just criticism.

Two small beers had apparently left behind one dry mouth, one slower first thought, and a powerful belief that doors should be quieter before breakfast.

Lyssa was already awake.

Of course.

She sat at the table with dark cloth spread across her lap and three pins held between her lips.

I had one blanket over my legs and no moral authority.

The knock came again.

Lyssa looked at me.

I looked at the door.

She raised one eyebrow.

"I am recovering."

She removed the pins.

"From walking home?"

"Social activity."

"Open the door."

Cruel household government.

I put both crutches under me and stood.

Body fine.

Right shoulder ordinary.

Residual limb ordinary.

No strange skin complaint.

Only mouth dry enough that swallowing felt administrative.

I crossed the room.

The person outside was not Antonius.

Good.

One chapter of voluntary friendship-adjacent behavior was enough pressure for civilization.

It was a district runner.

Not Carrow district colors exactly.

Blue strip on gray shoulder.

Travel pouch.

Flat document case.

He looked at me.

Then at the folded paper in his hand.

"Gregory?"

"Depends."

"Gregory of Carrow, temporary route witness and commercial screener?"

That was worse.

"Yes."

He held out the paper.

"Lakeward administrative relay. Receipt requested."

There it was.

Lakeward.

My brain became sober immediately.

Not fully.

Enough.

I took the paper.

Did not open it yet.

"What kind of relay?"

"I carry it. I don't interpret it."

Excellent profession.

He produced a little receipt slip.

My name.

Origin mark.

Carrow delivery line.

Nothing saying arrest, fine, charge, complaint, summons, execution, or congratulations on successful regional smuggling.

Promising.

"Sign here?"

"If you're Gregory."

"I am increasingly sure."

I signed.

He checked it.

"Response instructions are inside if response is required."

"That sounds ominous."

"People say that about every letter."

"Are they usually right?"

"I carry them."

Right.

He left.

I closed the door.

Lyssa had stopped sewing.

That was evidence.

Not proof of concern.

She looked at the folded paper.

"Lakeward?"

"Yes."

"Work?"

"Maybe."

Bad answer.

I looked at the seal again.

Not merchant-house.

Not field office I recognized.

Lakeward district mark with smaller stamp beneath.

**HERITAGE RELEASE / RECORD RECONCILIATION**

Oh.

There it was.

I knew exactly one reason that office might know my name.

The gray freight.

My mouth became drier without help from beer.

Lyssa watched my face.

"Not work."

"Probably not."

"Greg."

"I am opening it."

I sat before doing that.

No reason to perform standing anxiety when chair existed.

The document was three sheets.

First page formal.

Second page questions.

Third page return instructions.

I read the first page once too fast.

Then again like Hessa had been hiding behind my shoulder.

**LAKEWARD DISTRICT HERITAGE RELEASE OFFICE**

**REQUEST FOR FACTUAL STATEMENT**

**POST-SALE RELEASE RECONCILIATION / PRE-MERGER ROUTE HARDWARE**

Not summons.

Not charge.

Request.

Keep reading.

The office was reconciling a municipal surplus sale involving pre-merger route hardware later presented outside district without prior export release.

One marked lot had apparently been returned to Lakeward jurisdiction for release review.

Returned by whom?

Not stated.

Current owner?

Not stated.

The office had municipal sale records, Cinder Cross intake records, and a buyer notation identifying me as an external screener present when the marked lot was examined.

There.

The buyer.

Of course he wrote things down.

Functional people were dangerous like that.

The letter said my statement was requested to establish **observed custody, markings, disclosed knowledge, and condition at examination**.

It explicitly said:

**Do not provide legal conclusion unless personally qualified.**

Beautiful.

I almost liked them.

Then the next line:

**A factual response does not waive any district authority concerning persons, property, release status, transport, penalty, or later inquiry.**

There we go.

Less beautiful.

Lyssa said, "What is it?"

I kept reading.

Questions.

One: Were you present when municipal surplus route hardware was transported from Lakeward toward Cinder Cross?

Yes.

Two: Did you review sale records before transport?

Yes.

Three: Did those records appear to identify municipal sale of the transported lots?

Yes, with limits.

Four: Before departure, were you aware that any pre-merger route-hardware lot lacked district export release?

Yes.

There.

No clever wording available.

Five: Did you personally conceal, alter, remove, forge, or destroy a release mark, ownership mark, sale record, crate seal, inventory entry, or inspection record?

No.

Six: Did you instruct another person to do so?

No.

Seven: At Cinder Cross, did you identify any piece as Three Rays marked or compatible?

One piece as Three Rays marked.

Compatibility for others uncertain.

Eight: Was the marked lot purchased by the Cinder Cross buyer?

No.

Nine: What was the last custody state you personally observed?

Marked lot remained with seller after buyer refused current purchase without release or deeper discount.

Ten: Do you know current location?

No.

Eleven: Do you know original removal site or whether hardware was ever removed unlawfully from an active route?

No.

Twelve: Were you compensated in connection with transport or sale?

Yes.

Ah.

There was the eight silver.

No reason to hide it.

Reason to dislike writing it.

Lyssa said, "Greg."

I put the paper down.

"I did something illegal in Lakeward."

Silence.

Good opening.

Maybe not.

Lyssa set the cloth aside.

Not dramatically.

Carefully, because pins.

"How illegal?"

Useful question.

"Export paperwork illegal. Not stolen-goods illegal by what I knew."

"By what you knew."

"Yes."

She waited.

The converged version of me lasted longer than five seconds.

Not much longer.

"Municipal surplus," I said. "Road hardware. Two lots had pre-merger export controls. Seller had bought them legally from Lakeward district, at least according to sale receipts that matched the lots. She had not gotten the separate release stamp before moving them east. Waiting for release would have missed her buyer window."

Lyssa looked at the letter.

"You knew."

"Yes."

No defense.

That felt worse and better.

"Before you went?"

"Before we left Lakeward."

"And you went anyway."

"Yes."

She leaned back.

Not angry enough to perform anger.

More like fitting a new piece into an old pattern she had not requested.

"Why?"

That one had several answers.

Ride east.

Money.

The stock appeared legitimately owned.

The release rule looked badly applied to old warehouse junk.

The buyer window.

Curiosity.

Confidence.

A little criminal nostalgia probably deserved no daylight.

"Because the property looked legitimately purchased, the release rule looked administrative rather than protecting somebody from immediate harm, and the pay made the risk real."

"How much?"

"Eight silver plus the ride after the marked lot failed. Twelve if whole load had sold."

Her eyebrows moved.

Not because eight silver was impossible money anymore.

Because I had omitted an eight-silver illegal job from the travel report.

"You told me about glass."

"Yes."

"You told me about the shoulder."

"Yes."

"You told me about the road."

"Yes."

"You did not tell me this."

"No."

There.

No clever version.

She looked at the letter again.

"Was anyone else taking the risk without knowing?"

That surprised me.

Then it didn't.

Practical.

"Driver knew the release was missing. Seller was paying ordinary rate. I made her negotiate a risk premium with him before I agreed."

"Made?"

"Refused my part until she did. Driver asked four silver. She paid four."

"Good."

Not absolution.

Just one fact landing where it belonged.

"Were the things stolen?"

"I saw municipal sale receipts. Lot numbers matched. No active-road removal in the sale record. I do not know where the district got them before the warehouse sale."

"So you don't know."

"Correct."

"Did you lie at the gate?"

That one was uglier.

"Not directly."

Her face changed.

"Greg."

"The load was listed as retired repair stock. That was true and incomplete. Clerk asked about charged glass and active signal parts. Seller answered no. Nobody asked whether pre-merger release was missing. I had already told seller that if anyone asked me directly, I would answer. Nobody did."

Lyssa stared at me.

"That is lawyer-shaped."

"Yes."

"Were you a lawyer?"

"No."

"Then don't."

Fair.

"I knowingly let incomplete paperwork carry the load through."

"Better."

"Worse legally."

"Better sentence."

Also fair.

I picked the letter up again.

"They aren't asking me to decide law. They want factual statement."

"Will you answer?"

Immediate instinct: yes.

Second instinct: ask what answering exposed.

Third: maybe I should find somebody who understood district procedure.

That did not mean Antonius.

Absolutely not Antonius the morning after beer.

Also he was not automatically my lawyer because he owned ledgers aggressively.

I read the return instructions.

Statement could be returned through Carrow district relay within twelve days.

Signature had to be witnessed by a district clerk, registered notary, licensed advocate, or magistrate-office clerk.

No appearance in Lakeward required unless later ordered.

Voluntary factual response.

Failure to respond could leave the record unresolved but was not itself listed as offense.

Interesting.

"I think yes," I said.

Lyssa nodded once.

"Think?"

"I want somebody local to tell me what `voluntary factual response` actually does before I sign it. Not what happened. I know what happened. What signing means procedurally."

"That sounds less stupid."

"Growth."

"Don't celebrate yet."

Hostile household again.

I ate bread.

Drank water.

Then read the questions a third time.

No need to rush to district office with yesterday's beer still arguing quietly with my body.

By second bell I felt normal enough to stop thinking about it.

We went separately.

Lyssa had work.

That mattered.

She did not come supervise my administrative consequences.

Before leaving she said, "Tell me what they say."

"Yes."

"Actual words."

"Cruel."

"Greg."

"Actual words."

Carrow district relay office sat near the records quarter where every building looked like it had been designed by someone who believed windows encouraged fraud.

I had used the place for route receipts before.

Never as a respondent.

Title acquired.

Great.

The front clerk was an older man with a narrow beard and no interest in my internal development.

Perfect.

I showed the Lakeward letter.

"I want to know what this response does before I submit it."

He read the header.

"Heritage release reconciliation."

"Yes."

"You need legal advice?"

"I need to know whether your office can tell me what the process means without telling me what I should say."

He looked at me over the paper.

"Better question."

I was collecting those.

He pointed to a side desk.

"Records clerk can explain relay and witness function. Legal advice is advocate."

Good.

Different authority.

The records clerk was a woman with square spectacles and six piles of paper arranged by a system I wanted to ask about but valued survival.

She read the return page.

"Voluntary means Lakeward requested it without current order compelling you to answer."

"If I answer, does Carrow decide whether it's true?"

"No. We witness signature and identity only."

"Does witness mean you certify content?"

"No."

"Does sending it waive anything?"

She tapped the sentence that already said it did not waive district authority.

"Not according to this form. I am not giving you broader legal advice."

"Good."

"If you want advice about exposure, hire advocate."

There.

Money question.

I had twelve silver-ish reserve.

Not poverty.

Also no reason to spend silver reflexively if the form only asked facts I already intended to state honestly.

Still, knowingly participating in controlled-export movement was not nothing.

"What does a short review usually cost?"

"Ask an advocate."

Right.

I left district office without signing.

That felt responsible enough to be suspicious.

There were three licensed advocates within two streets according to posted board.

One handled family/property disputes.

One commercial claims.

One transport and municipal licensing.

Useful specificity.

I chose transport.

Not because destiny.

Because sign.

The advocate's front room had two benches, one clerk, and a wall map with colored route strips pinned over it.

The clerk read my letter.

"Document review only?"

"I want to know what answering factually exposes or changes. I am not asking anyone to invent a defense before I know whether I need one."

"Consult fee three copper."

That was almost comforting.

Not silver.

Not everything had to scale because my purse did.

I paid.

Three copper could be three copper.

The advocate was a woman named Irena Voss according to the plate on her desk.

Name established by furniture.

Excellent provenance.

She read the Lakeward request without speaking.

Then the questions.

Then looked at me.

"Did you know release was missing?"

"Yes."

"Before transport?"

"Yes."

"Did you own the goods?"

"No."

"Drive?"

"No."

"Arrange original sale?"

"No."

"Remove marks?"

"No."

"Alter paperwork?"

"No."

"Get paid?"

"Yes."

"For transport?"

"Screening and helping preserve sale. Ride included."

"Amount?"

"Eight silver after partial acceptance. Twelve would have been full-load acceptance."

She paused at that.

Not judgment.

Scale.

"What did you actually do during transport?"

I described it.

Reviewed receipts.

Compared lots.

Confirmed release missing.

Refused involvement until driver negotiated risk premium.

Rode with load.

Did not open sealed crates.

Did not lie when asked nothing useful.

That sentence got corrected immediately.

"Do not say it that way."

"Lyssa already objected."

"Lyssa is correct."

Disease spreading.

"Say what happened. Do not turn omission into a legal theory."

"Fine."

At Cinder Cross, seller and buyer opened lots.

I identified one Three Rays mark.

Buyer rejected marked lot because missing release created resale/capital risk.

Seller retained it.

I was paid partial fee.

Last saw marked lot with seller.

No current location.

No original removal provenance.

Irena read question four again.

"Your truthful answer is yes."

"Yes."

"That answer may be against your interest if Lakeward later examines transport liability."

"I assumed."

"But refusing now does not erase Cinder records identifying you."

"Also assumed."

"Do not assume legal consequences."

Right.

"What would you do?"

She looked at me.

"You are paying for advice, so I will answer. Based only on this letter and your account, I would respond narrowly, truthfully, and without volunteering legal conclusions or motives. You have documentary facts that may help establish lawful municipal ownership and later custody. You also have a fact that may expose knowing participation in export without release. Both can be true."

There.

Observation could be real while attribution remained unsettled.

Annoying when my own system turned around and bit me.

"Should I explain why I thought the release rule was stupid here?"

"No."

Fast.

"Why?"

"Because they did not ask whether you approved of the rule."

Good.

"Should I explain driver premium?"

"Question twelve asks compensation in connection with transport or sale. If you mention your own compensation, and driver premium is relevant to what you were paid to do, maybe. But do not write a moral essay proving everyone consented."

"I hate how accurately you predicted the essay."

"Common problem."

"Greg problem."

"I have known you eleven minutes."

Fair.

We drafted nothing together.

Important.

She told me process and risk.

My statement remained mine.

I went back to district office with four pages in notebook first.

Too many.

Cut them.

Three pages.

Still too many.

The records clerk watched me cross out half a paragraph.

"Need desk?"

"Apparently."

She pointed to one.

I wrote the statement cleanly.

No story.

No defense.

No apology.

No cleverness.

Just sequence.

**I was present before and during transport from Lakeward toward Cinder Cross.**

**I reviewed municipal sale records presented by the seller. The lot numbers I checked matched the transported inventory. I did not independently verify the district's chain before municipal sale.**

**Before departure I knew two pre-merger route-hardware lots lacked export release.**

That line looked ugly.

Stayed.

**I did not alter, remove, forge, conceal, or destroy any release mark, ownership mark, sale record, crate seal, inventory entry, or inspection record, and I did not instruct another person to do so.**

**At Cinder Cross the owner and buyer opened the relevant lot. I identified one toothed arc bearing a Three Rays mark. I described other pieces only as possibly compatible where I was not certain.**

**The buyer declined current purchase of the marked route lot without release or a larger discount. The seller retained that lot. That is the last custody state I personally observed. I do not know its current location.**

**I do not know the original removal site or whether any piece was unlawfully removed from an active route before municipal sale.**

**I was compensated eight silver plus transport after the buyer accepted the clean remainder and rejected the marked lot. My agreed compensation would have been twelve silver plus transport if the buyer accepted the full load.**

Then one extra sentence.

I stared at it before deciding.

**Before I agreed to participate, I confirmed the driver knew the release was absent; the driver separately negotiated a four-silver risk premium with the seller.**

Relevant?

Maybe.

It established disclosed knowledge among participants, not innocence.

Question: disclosed knowledge.

Keep.

No statement that this made transport acceptable.

No statement that law was stupid.

No statement that nobody was harmed.

I did not know that.

The records clerk read only enough to check blank spaces and identity line.

"Content is yours."

"Good."

"Lakeward copy leaves afternoon packet."

"Can I keep exact copy?"

"You should."

Of course.

Ordinary money.

No ceremony.

Outside, I stood on the records-office step with two crutches and one document proving I had voluntarily told a district office that yes, I knowingly participated in moving export-controlled hardware without the required release.

This was not how I imagined financial independence feeling.

Good.

Probably healthier.

Lyssa was not there.

Also good.

One cup upside down by basin.

Someone downstairs arguing about onions.

Just with papers.

So I ate.

Repaired one loose wrap on crutch handle.

Checked my notebook.

Did not invent a Lakeward outcome.

Did not calculate fines nobody had named.

Did not decide the marked lot had become historically important because an office cared about release.

Administrative attention was not value proof.

It was administrative attention.

"That sounds true."
