# CHAPTER 495

## THE SUBSCRIBER

Losing auction taught me important lesson.

I needed more money.

This was not the lesson.

Unfortunately.

I had enough money to bid five silver and hate myself.

What I did not have was information before public board appeared beside breakfast.

The blue-coat buyer probably did.

Maybe not about that exact lot.

About sales.

Routes.

Retirements.

Shops.

Inventory.

People who knew which broken things were worth opening wallet for.

I knew one thing very clearly.

Following him would be pathetic.

So I did not.

Growth continued to be mostly denying myself efficient bad ideas.

Anchor's End breakfast.

Pell put eggs down.

"Buying another bad compass?"

"No."

"Good."

"Buying information."

He sighed.

"Worse."

Maybe.

I asked where instrument shops clustered.

Not where blue coat worked.

General question.

Pell pointed north.

"Survey Row. Three real shops. Two repair benches pretending to be shops. One broker upstairs over lamp seller."

"Broker?"

"Finds things. Charges for finding things."

There.

"Name?"

"Merrin List."

I stared.

"That's a person?"

"Merrin is person. List is what she keeps."

"Disappointing."

"You asked."

Survey Row was narrower than name deserved.

Three blocks.

Brass signs.

Glass windows.

Instrument cases displayed like jewelry for people who hated jewelry.

I liked immediately.

First shop sold current navigation tools.

Prices hurt.

Good.

Second repaired survey equipment.

Third had old instruments and parts, but everything useful was labeled by function, maker, condition, and whether current certification existed.

No mystery pile.

Civilization.

I asked owner about retirement-sale notices.

He said, "Which office?"

"Harbor survey."

"Public sheet."

"I found that part. Before breakfast would be better."

He looked at me.

"Then subscribe."

There.

Apparently economy had invented newsletters.

"Where?"

He pointed upstairs across lane.

Lamp seller below.

Broker above.

Merrin List was not called Merrin List.

Pell had lied through compression.

Sign said:

**MERRIN VALETT**

**INSTRUMENT LOTS / ESTATE GOODS / SURVEY RETIREMENTS / WANTED NOTICES / COMMISSION BUYING**

Better.

No relation to Vale debt.

Probably.

I refused to ask surname history.

Inside was one room with shelves of paper.

Not objects.

Paper.

Merrin sat behind desk with three ledgers open and spectacles low on nose.

Maybe forty.

Maybe fifty.

Ink had won fight with two fingers.

She did not look up.

"If you're selling, card left. If you're buying, sit. If you're lost, stairs."

I sat.

She looked up.

"Buying."

"Information."

"More expensive."

Pell's people coordinated jokes.

I put yesterday's public lot notes on desk.

Not bid sheet with buyer code.

Just copied facts.

"I learned about Harbor Survey retirement sale after it opened. I want earlier notice of similar public sales and maybe specific categories."

"Then you want list service."

"How early?"

"Depends source. Municipal notices usually one to three days before public posting. Estate notices sometimes same day I get them. Private lots only if seller authorizes circulation."

"How?"

"Runner packet if local. Post packet if not. Weekly digest if you're patient."

"I'm not."

"Then expensive."

She turned ledger.

Terms written cleanly.

Weekly digest: one copper per month.

Local same-day runner notices: one bit each, only when matched.

Post forwarding outside Duskport: postage plus one bit handling per packet.

Wanted-item registration: one copper for three months, up to three categories.

Commission buying: percentage negotiated by lot/value/risk.

No guarantee of availability.

No guarantee of authenticity beyond stated source.

No guarantee of winning.

Excellent.

"You charge one copper to remember three things?"

"I charge one copper to have six clerks, four runners, seven shop relationships, three municipal boards, two estate houses, and one unpleasant cousin remember three things for you."

Fair.

"What's cousin do?"

"You don't get him for one copper."

Also fair.

I read again.

This was infrastructure.

Not exciting.

Exactly why useful.

"What categories?"

"Specific enough to avoid wasting my runners. Broad enough to exist."

"Early Veyr response instruments."

She looked at my copied lot note.

"You lost thirty-one."

I looked at her.

"Public result?"

"I track sales."

Of course.

"Yes."

"Five seven."

"Yes."

"Reasonable."

That hurt more than losing.

"You think?"

"For ring and housing if buyer has use. High for broken compass if not."

Same market logic.

"Do you know buyer?"

She looked at me over spectacles.

There was moment.

I could ask.

Easy.

Maybe she would tell.

Maybe fee.

Not needed.

"Doesn't matter."

Her expression changed slightly.

Not approval.

Recalculation maybe.

"Good."

She tapped category line.

"Early Veyr response instruments is one."

I thought.

What else did I actually want?

Not every artifact.

That was useless.

Not magical instruments.

Too broad.

Not broken things Greg might exploit.

Embarrassing category.

I had Tere gauge.

Stillhook not mine.

Road Response Kit.

Sway shoes.

What changed operating rules?

Old navigation/survey response hardware with unusual failure modes.

Still broad.

Merrin waited.

Did not help me invent desire.

Good.

"Retired survey or route-response instruments with intact magical components but failed certification."

She wrote slower.

"That's broad."

"Too broad?"

"Maybe useful broad. You want cheap failures."

"I want interesting failures."

"Everyone says that until interesting failure burns shop."

"No burn."

She added:

**NO FIRE / HEAT DAMAGE LOTS.**

Good.

Third category.

I almost said Tere.

No.

I already owned one.

Maybe early makers whose later work mattered.

That was not category I could explain without sounding insane.

"Leave third blank."

"You paid for three."

"I know."

"You can add later within term."

Excellent.

One copper.

I paid.

Small money.

Not trivial because service.

Merrin wrote receipt.

Three months.

Two active categories.

One blank.

Then:

"Local or forwarding?"

"Carrow."

"Weekly digest?"

I considered.

One copper per month.

Cheap.

Maybe too much paper.

Same-day notices forwarded from Duskport would arrive late anyway.

"Weekly digest to Carrow. One month first."

"Postage separate."

"Yes."

Another copper.

She wrote.

"Wanted matches can go in digest unless you pay packet forwarding."

"Digest. If something has closing date under four days, send separate packet."

"Post may still lose race."

"I know."

"Then write that."

She pushed instruction card.

I wrote:

**For matched wanted notice closing within four days of Merrin receipt, forward immediately to Carrow at my postage + handling. No guarantee notice arrives before close.**

Signed.

There.

System.

Not object.

I felt weirdly better.

Which meant danger.

"Commission buying," I said.

Merrin nodded.

"What about it?"

"If I know maximum and cannot reach Duskport in time?"

"You authorize ceiling. I bid or buy within instructions. Deposit must already be held or guaranteed. Commission depends amount and work."

"Can you inspect?"

"I can look. I am not appraiser. If you want appraiser, you pay appraiser."

Excellent boundary.

"Can you authenticate?"

"No."

"Can you recommend someone?"

"Yes. Recommendation is not guarantee."

I smiled.

"I like this business."

"You like disclaimers."

"Recently."

Lakeward disease.

She closed ledger.

Transaction over.

I remained seated.

Bad sign.

"Anything else?" she asked.

I wanted to ask how much capital serious buyers kept available.

How they financed inventory.

Whether shops borrowed.

Whether deposits could be held.

How many sales per month.

How often good artifacts appeared.

Entire economy.

No.

One question.

"If somebody wants to be ready for lots like thirty-one, what's usual mistake?"

Merrin answered immediately.

"Thinking winning is goal."

I blinked.

"That's annoyingly relevant."

"Winning is easy. Bid too much."

There.

"Second mistake?"

"Buying things you cannot store, test, repair, move, or resell because price looked low."

"Third?"

"You paid for one question."

"Did I?"

"No. But leave."

Fair.

I left.

Outside, Survey Row continued without me.

Repairman carried lens housing between shops.

A woman argued over certification date.

Runner pinned new municipal notice to board.

I checked it.

Not because addiction.

Professional development.

It was dredge-chain surplus.

I did not want dredge chain.

Good test.

I walked away.

At harbor I bought paper envelope and copied Merrin receipt into notebook.

Not every term.

Enough.

Three months.

Two categories.

Weekly digest one month.

Urgent under-four-day matches forwarded immediately at postage plus handling.

No guarantees.

No buyer stalking.

No artifact.

Still useful.

I had spent two copper plus future postage to become slightly earlier.

That felt more like capital than the five silver I had almost spent yesterday.

Not because amount.

Because option.

I hated when Antonius-shaped concepts appeared without Antonius.

He would enjoy this.

I did not send him letter.

Not everything needed witness.

Afternoon I did something even stranger.

I went to Hark & Venn.

Not inside.

Across street.

Shop open.

Masta visible through front window once, carrying leather form from bench to back.

My fitting tomorrow.

Not today.

I kept walking.

This was becoming absurd.

I had traveled entire road and now practiced not entering building.

Useful skill apparently.

I checked skin at Anchor's End.

Clean.

Liner folded.

Money lower than Carrow, obviously, but not wounded.

No Veyr compass.

One subscription.

One appointment tomorrow.

At dinner Pell asked, "What did information cost?"

"Two copper."

"Cheap."

"Plus postage later."

"Still cheap."

"She says winning auctions is easy if you bid too much."

Pell nodded.

"That's why I don't bid."

"You have wisdom by abstinence."

"I own inn. Same disease bigger."

Fair.

I went upstairs.

Set liner on chair.

Then tray idea in notebook.

**LOADED TRAY. TWO CUPS. MAYBE BOWL. MASTA DECIDES WEIGHT / SAFETY.**

Tavi would demand soup.

No soup.

Probably water.

Tomorrow PERFORMANCE would finally earn its dramatic capital.

I did not know that phrase.

Good.

I knew only that tomorrow I had to put on a thing built for my body and stop being clever enough to hide what it did wrong.

Much worse.

I slept better than after auction.
