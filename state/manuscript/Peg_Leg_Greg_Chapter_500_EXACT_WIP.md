# CHAPTER 500

## THE RESIDENT

Pell charged me by night.

This had been fine when I was passing through.

I was no longer passing through.

I was orbiting a leather shell.

Three more days until next fitting.

Then maybe more.

Masta's favorite word remained depends.

At breakfast I counted what repeated nightly lodging plus meals was doing.

Not dangerous.

Annoying.

More importantly, stupid if Anchor's End had longer-stay terms.

I asked Pell.

"Do you have weekly rate?"

He looked up.

"You staying week?"

"Maybe."

"Then no."

"Why?"

"Weekly rate is for week."

Fair.

"If I stay seven nights total?"

"You've already stayed five."

I counted.

Had I?

Travel arrival.

Auction.

Merrin.

Fitting.

Letter.

Wind.

Fitting.

Time had become leather-shaped.

"Apparently."

Pell pulled ledger.

"You want room held if you're out?"

"Yes."

"Meals included?"

"Breakfast. Maybe dinner."

"Storage?"

"One small box eventually maybe. Not now."

"Laundry?"

"No."

He looked at shirt.

"Maybe."

Rude.

Pell wrote numbers.

Not mine.

His.

Good.

"Seven-night resident rate starts when paid ahead. Room, breakfast, one wash basin daily, one locked shelf behind desk for small goods. Dinner separate. If you leave early, no refund. If you stay after seven, renew."

"How much?"

He named amount.

I compared nightly rate.

Savings real but not magical.

About one night cheaper across week.

"Why locked shelf?"

"Residents leave things. Travelers pretend they don't."

Good distinction.

"Can post come here?"

"Already does."

"Officially."

"What do you think happened with Lyssa letter?"

"Pell."

"Yes. Mail can come here. Name plus Anchor's End. If you're gone, I hold it. Not responsible for government stupidity, fire, flood, theft by army, or your enemies."

"Do I have army enemies?"

"Weekly rate doesn't cover research."

Fair.

I looked at terms.

This was not home.

Important.

Not Carrow replacement.

Not move.

A room I could reliably return to while Duskport had reasons to keep me here.

Fitting.

Merrin notices.

Instrument market.

Maybe future work that deserved city.

Maybe nothing.

Option.

Again.

"Can I renew without being here?"

"Pay ahead."

"Can somebody else pay for me?"

"If money real."

"Can you forward mail to Carrow when I'm gone?"

"For postage and one bit each packet if you leave written address."

Merrin charged same handling.

Apparently bits were natural unit of making other people deal with your paper.

"Can Merrin Valett send notices here?"

"Anyone can send notices here if your name on them."

"Do you know Merrin?"

"Everybody who receives too much mail knows Merrin."

Excellent.

Infrastructure connecting itself without me inventing company.

I considered weekly rate.

Three days until fitting.

Could stay at least that.

Likely more if Masta continued progression.

Paying seven gave four-day buffer beyond appointment.

If limb somehow finished sooner, lose unused nights.

Not disaster.

If not, avoid daily decision.

I paid week.

Pell counted.

Receipt.

"Resident," he said.

"Temporary."

"Receipt doesn't care."

I looked at word.

**RESIDENT RATE / 7 NIGHTS.**

There.

New title apparently purchased.

"Do I get key?"

"You already have key."

"Different key?"

"Why would I give you more doors?"

Fair.

"Shelf?"

He showed locked cabinet behind desk.

Small cubbies.

Mine for week.

I had nothing to put there.

This bothered me.

Paid resource unused.

Dangerous brain.

I almost went upstairs to find object.

No.

Blank category lesson.

Empty shelf could remain empty.

I hated growth.

Pell saw me staring.

"Don't buy something for shelf."

I looked at him.

"Get out of my head."

"You're easy."

I went upstairs.

Room looked same.

Bed.

Chair.

Liner.

Crutches.

Notebook.

Except now seven nights had been bought in advance.

Small thing.

Large difference.

I no longer needed each Duskport day to justify lodging separately.

That was probably dangerous too.

Could waste time professionally.

I wrote Merrin address instruction:

**WHILE IN DUSKPORT: GREG, ANCHOR'S END.**

Then stopped.

No need to run there today.

Could tell her next time actual reason.

Subscription digest still going Carrow.

Urgent notices could perhaps route Duskport later if I stayed.

Not now.

No optimization avalanche.

I went downstairs.

Pell had new guest at counter.

Woman with two bags.

He was explaining bath water.

I waited.

Not my business.

Then another guest asked where ferry office was.

I knew.

I almost answered.

Pell answered first.

Correct.

His inn.

I sat near window.

Resident did not mean assistant manager.

Important.

Breakfast crowd thinned.

Pell brought tea I had not ordered.

"Included?"

"No."

"Then why?"

"You look like you'll ask seventeen questions. Tea buys silence."

"Who pays?"

"Me. Investment."

I drank.

Worked.

Afternoon I took notebook to harbor and reviewed money.

Not because crisis.

Because capital had started buying things that were not objects.

Passenger fare bought clean body-state evidence.

Merrin bought earlier information.

Weekly room bought continuity.

None returned profit yet.

All changed options.

I started writing sentence about capital.

Stopped.

Manifesto.

Instead numbers.

Better.

Lakeward still silent.

No update.

Merrin still no match.

No update.

Leg still at shop.

Actual update: next session three days.

Carrow letter sent.

Cord sent.

Done.

I closed notebook.

A ferry arrived.

People got off carrying bags.

One man had prosthetic arm.

Wood and leather, hook attachment.

I noticed.

He scratched neck with other hand and argued with porter over trunk.

Normal.

I did not interview him.

This required effort.

Later I bought dinner.

Pell charged separately exactly as promised.

"Resident discount?"

"No."

"Loyalty?"

"Leave."

"I live here now."

He stared.

I smiled.

"Temporary."

"Seven nights is long enough to throw you out."

Good.

At room I put receipt in notebook.

Then thought about chapter numbers.

No.

I did not know chapter numbers.

Excellent.

Nothing special happened because arbitrary count reached round number.

I had a room for week.

A fitting in three days.

A broker who might eventually send paper.

A woman in Carrow who would receive blue cord.

A child waiting for soup certification.

A government office that might or might not write back.

Two crutches.

One unfinished leg.

Money enough to wait without pretending waiting was failure.

That last part felt new.

I let it.
