# ARLO - VOICE & DIALOGUE GUIDE

Status: **SPECIALIST EDITORIAL AID**

Canonical Book I name: **Arlo**. Older reader/export surfaces may still contain `Pell` or `Pellian`; the Book I final name map outranks those stale names.

Manuscript authority outranks this file.

## Core conversational lens

Arlo thinks from the bench outward.

He cares about:
- what was actually tested;
- mechanism;
- material behavior;
- tolerances;
- completeness;
- whether a result replicates;
- whether touching, removing, heating, or powering something could damage it;
- what his time costs;
- who owns the work.

His precision comes from craft practice, not from generalized epistemology.

## Sentence shape

Default:
- short to medium;
- concrete;
- questions often narrow the mechanism;
- technical explanations become longer only when there is an actual object, process, or result to explain;
- he can answer Greg's abstraction with a smaller bench-level correction.

He does not usually narrate the whole theory of why uncertainty matters. He says what the sample did, what he cannot identify yet, what would need testing, or why Greg should stop touching something.

## Vocabulary and register

Arlo has working technical literacy.

Natural domains:
- materials;
- ratios;
- clay/ceramic mixtures;
- heat;
- channels;
- regulators;
- tools;
- workshop registries;
- maker marks;
- time and labor;
- failure modes.

He may know a technical concept Greg only remembers historically, while Greg may recognize the future significance Arlo cannot yet see.

Do not turn his speech into professor voice. He is an artificer doing paid work.

## Humor

Dry and precise.

Arlo's humor often comes from:
- charging Greg for the thing Greg wants to pretend is free;
- correcting Greg's inflated description;
- treating Greg's confidence as a hazardous variable;
- refusing to reward an incomplete technical answer;
- workshop practicality.

He can swear. It should feel like a craftsman reacting to Greg, bad equipment, or an expensive implication, not like a rehearsed comic persona.

## Information behavior

Arlo is willing to say:
- `I don't know.`
- `Unknown.`
- `Maybe if...`
- `That is not what I tested.`

But do not let these phrases become Hessa-style evidence doctrine.

Arlo's uncertainty is usually attached to a **specific physical thing**.

Example distinction:
- Hessa: stop because the body/mana evidence does not justify the next step.
- Arlo: stop because removing the stone may destroy the matched set, the sample is inconsistent, the mechanism is unknown, or he has not tested that variable.

## Conflict / disagreement

Arlo protects:
- his time;
- his shop;
- his authorship/ownership;
- the integrity of the work;
- physical safety around uncertain devices.

When Greg pushes, Arlo is likely to answer with:
- cost;
- what was actually agreed;
- what the test did not prove;
- what Greg is asking him to risk.

He does not need to out-argue Greg philosophically.

## Relationship language with Greg

Early relationship engine:
- Greg arrives with a lead or remembered possibility.
- Arlo makes it survive contact with materials and procedure.
- Greg sees future leverage.
- Arlo sees today's work, today's time, and today's ownership problem.

Mutual respect grows because each catches something the other cannot.

Greg should not become Arlo's boss by conversational default. Arlo can be curious enough to keep working with Greg while still insisting that his name stays attached to the work and that terms exist before the next round.

## What he should not become

Do not flatten Arlo into:
- Hessa with tools;
- exposition artificer;
- generic skeptic;
- Greg's permanent technical subordinate;
- future-industrial-dynasty foreshadowing machine;
- character who exists only to confirm Greg's old-life memory.

The present craft matters even if the future name turns out important.

## Useful canon fingerprints from early Book I

Patterns worth protecting:
- `For my time.`
- `I'm being paid to test a hypothesis.`
- concern with the exact mechanism instead of Greg's remembered outcome;
- `I don't guess` / professional resistance to convenient estimates;
- insisting his name stays attached to the work;
- identifying that the Tere set matters because it can make work **consistent**, not merely stronger;
- repeatedly stopping Greg from touching uncertain magical hardware.

These are patterns, not catchphrases.

## Attribution note

Arlo, Antonius, and Greg can all deliver dry one-line corrections. When all three share a scene, tag aggressively at pivots. Domain voice should enrich attribution, not replace it.
