# DAVIN — VOICE AUTHORITY

Exact manuscript prose outranks this file.

## Core lens

Davin notices how things are made, hauled, pinned, repaired, braced, fitted, loaded, tied, balanced, and kept moving when the glamorous part of theatre is somewhere else.

He is materially competent and does not need to sound polished to prove it.

Chapters 142-145 extend that competence onto the road. Wagon rails, axle load, seat/cargo changes, canvas ties, sleeping geometry, and whether a knot will still release after vibration all belong naturally to his attention.

## Spoken rhythm

Practical, sometimes blunt, sometimes dry. He can explain by handling the object instead of narrating the entire mechanism.

He may speak in short diagnosis/action pairs:
- what failed;
- what he needs;
- what to hold;
- what to test;
- where the load goes;
- whether the current arrangement can keep moving.

Do not turn every sentence into tidy technical doctrine.

On the road, very short answers can be complete answers for Davin: `Keep.`, `Need room.`, `Weight.`, `Not flat.` The missing explanation is often visible in the object he is already moving.

## Humor

Workplace credit, mild defensiveness, irritation, and jokes that make sense because he and the other crew have already done this bullshit together.

He can also end Greg's attempt to turn a practical lesson into methodology with a plain statement of job identity: `I fix wagons.`

## Disagreement

Davin can be wrong. When wrong, he should remain a competent person correcting a mistake rather than collapse into comic liability.

He may argue about method, ownership of a fix, whether somebody's extra check is necessary, or whether the current load can occupy the space another worker wants.

His `No` often means the physical system has already answered the question.

## Information behavior

He often knows fabrication/repair details because he physically did the work. He may assume others can see what he sees and under-explain.

That under-explanation is different from Hessa's deliberate evidentiary control.

Chapters 142-145 make this especially clear around wagons. Greg may ask why cups moved, why a trunk cannot lie flat, why a second knot exists, or what changed at the axle. Davin may give only the next material fact because he is currently moving the thing.

He does not pretend proximity gives him authority over unrelated theatre business. In Chapter 144 he hears Teren's hall negotiation and still answers Greg's questions with `Don't know.`

## Teaching Greg

Davin teaches by making the useful movement available, not by building a curriculum.

Chapter 143's slipped-hitch sequence is the clean model:
- demonstrate once;
- demonstrate again more slowly;
- let Greg try;
- allow failed attempts without commentary theater;
- accept one correct load-and-release cycle;
- say `Done.`

When Greg objects that one successful reproduction is poor instruction, Davin answers `I fix wagons.` The skill can be reproduced later. The work does not need a theory before the wagon moves.

Later, when Greg ties the hitch correctly to his crutches, Davin checks it with one tug and adds no praise. That quiet verification is enough.

## With Greg

Greg may ask more questions than Davin thinks the job deserves. Davin can answer enough to keep Greg useful and then return to the work.

The relationship can become lightly reciprocal without turning Davin into Greg's mentor. Greg can hold a plate, carry a small bag, learn a knot, stay out of a loading path, or deliberately decide not to ask about every rope. Davin notices usefulness more readily than he narrates it.

## Canon lines / patterns

- **CANON LINE:** `I fix wagons.`
- **CANON LINE:** `Done.` after Greg completes one correct slipped-hitch cycle.
- **CANON LINE:** `Keep.` when Greg's personal bag must remain with him because wagon space changed.
- **CANON LINE:** `Weight.` when Greg keeps asking why the seat/cargo arrangement changed.
- **CANON PATTERN:** Diagnoses the missing cart pin as pulled rather than broken.
- **CANON PATTERN:** Fabricates replacement hardware and directs a rolling test.
- **CANON PATTERN:** Makes a real mistake with the reversible name board, stays on the repair, and changes later handling.
- **CANON PATTERN:** Dry credit/correction humor works because he is genuinely useful.
- **CANON PATTERN:** adds a secure crutch knot for travel, later teaches Greg the releasable hitch, and verifies Greg's independent tie with one physical tug rather than a speech.
- **CANON PATTERN:** solves temporary sleeping geometry by looking at the actual stable, loft, stalls, and wagon rather than debating fairness abstractly.

## Voice tests — NON-CANON

- `Hold that end. No, the other useful end.`
- `It didn't break. It came out.`
- `If you want pretty, wait. If you want it moving, lift.`

Calibration only.

## Overlap allowed

Davin and Jori share practical language and history. Their distinction should not be exaggerated into incompatible personalities.

Jori often reads usable geometry/verification. Davin often reads fabrication/material handling. The exact problem can reverse who has authority.

Davin and Nessa can both stop a bad physical choice quickly. Davin's center is load, hardware, rope, wagon, scenery, and repair. Nessa's is costume/prop continuity and what the performed object must do in the scene.

## Identity caution

Exact Chapters 138-145 contain a male soot-shirt / wagon / backstage worker who is explicitly named Davin in Chapter 143. Earlier theatre chapters also contain an unnamed soot-shirt worker described with she/her pronouns.

Do not silently merge those strands or normalize one into the other from the role label alone. This page governs named Davin. Stronger manuscript continuity authority must settle the earlier unnamed worker if needed.
