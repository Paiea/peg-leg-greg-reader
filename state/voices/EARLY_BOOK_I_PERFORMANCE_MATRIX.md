# EARLY BOOK I DIALOGUE PERFORMANCE MATRIX

Status: ACTIVE CALIBRATION AID

This file supplements the individual recurring-character voice pages and `state/DIALOGUE_VARIANCE_PERFORMANCE_LAYER.md`.

It does not replace `GREG.md`, `ANTONIUS.md`, `ARLO.md`, `HESSA.md`, `JORREN.md`, or `ALDEN.md`.

Its job is comparative: several early Book I characters are legitimately terse, competent, and dry. The important question is how they behave differently inside the same exchange.

## Greg

**Compression:** very high

**Extends the bit:** very high. Greg often takes another person's wording one step farther than usefulness.

**Silence:** low by default when interested. He tends to keep turning the object, rule, sentence, or contradiction over.

**Explanation trigger:** high when defending a distinction, excited by a mechanism, embarrassed, negotiating scope, or trying to make an old-life category legible.

**Typical exit:** often does not exit quickly. He extends, reframes, jokes, rationalizes, or asks the next question.

**Humor ownership:** primary owner of compressed comic reduction.

**Useful imperfection:** overexplains, rationalizes desire as investment or necessity, gets performatively clever when embarrassed, sometimes solves a larger problem than the scene asked.

**Contamination watch:** if another character repeatedly uses Greg's exact setup, reduction, and final-button architecture, protect Greg's gravity by changing the other character's response mode first.

## Antonius

**Compression:** high

**Extends the bit:** low. He may make one dry correction, but he usually has no need to keep fencing.

**Silence:** very high. Waiting is part of his control.

**Explanation trigger:** when Greg finally asks the decision-sized question, or when repayment logic genuinely requires the mechanism.

**Typical exit:** return to ledger, borrower, collateral, inventory, ownership, route, work, or the immediate decision.

**Humor ownership:** dry reduction through scope discipline, not through chasing Greg's wording.

**Useful imperfection:** younger Antonius can laugh, be irritated, be curious about Greg, or give a partial answer without converting every interaction into finance doctrine.

**Contamination watch:** repeated predictive `No`, repeated reading of Greg's hidden sentence, or winning several polished joke volleys in a row.

## Arlo

**Compression:** medium to high

**Extends the bit:** low

**Silence:** medium. He is comfortable returning attention to the object rather than answering every provocation.

**Explanation trigger:** a material, mechanism, test result, failure mode, ownership boundary, or cost actually needs explanation.

**Typical exit:** back to the bench, tool, fixture, sample, test, time cost, or authorship of the work.

**Humor ownership:** craft practicality, expensive implications, Greg touching things, and inflated descriptions colliding with bench reality.

**Useful imperfection:** can swear, be possessive of work, refuse abstraction, or say he does not know without turning uncertainty into a lecture.

**Contamination watch:** `No` ladders, face-reading jokes, transferable `you problem` lines, or abstract epistemic duels that could belong to Hessa or Greg.

## Hessa

**Compression:** high when the procedure needs it

**Extends the bit:** very low

**Silence:** high and meaningful

**Explanation trigger:** evidence boundary, body state, stopping condition, permission distinction, or an actually changed variable.

**Typical exit:** restore the procedure, record the result, stop the attempt, ask the next bounded question, or say nothing.

**Humor ownership:** rare, dry, often secondary to the work.

**Useful imperfection:** fatigue can make her more cautious or more clipped; concern can sharpen procedure; she can allow Greg an emotional reaction without converting it into permission.

**Contamination watch:** Hessa repeatedly landing superior punchlines after Greg jokes, or her methodological precision spreading into every specialist's speech.

## Jorren

**Compression:** medium to high

**Extends the bit:** low

**Silence:** medium

**Explanation trigger:** the physical exchange actually needs naming, especially stance, distance, rhythm, fatigue, overcommitment, or readiness.

**Typical exit:** let the body prove it, change the drill, stop the session, move, hit, eat, wait, or simply continue the ordinary task.

**Humor ownership:** peer teasing grounded in visible physical or social behavior.

**Useful imperfection:** he can be casually unambitious about empty time, miss Greg's larger theory, or decide an event deserves only a small reaction.

**Contamination watch:** becoming Greg's universal reality-check machine, predicting hidden sentences, or repeatedly joining face-reading and polished comeback ladders.

## Alden

**Compression:** high

**Extends the bit:** medium to high. Of the early supporting cast, Alden is the most naturally allowed to keep up with Greg for several turns.

**Silence:** low when an opening or useful question exists

**Explanation trigger:** low to medium. He wants the usable boundary, correction, opportunity, or next move rather than the full model.

**Typical exit:** test the advice, take initiative, claim the next opening, reserve the contract, ask the practical follow-up, or move on once a direct boundary is clear.

**Humor ownership:** active teasing, pressure-point wording, confident social push.

**Useful imperfection:** overuses a new insight, assumes initiative too quickly, gets restless, treats an early success as proof he can spend the advantage again.

**Contamination watch:** do not reduce his ability to match Greg merely to force contrast. The contrast is that Alden uses speed to act, while Greg often uses speed to interpret, extend, or complicate.

## Pairwise collision tests

### Greg and Antonius

Both may be terse and dry.

Greg expands the frame. Antonius should usually reduce it to the actual decision and then stop.

If Antonius repeatedly keeps playing after the operational answer has already landed, review for convergence.

### Greg and Arlo

Greg sees future system leverage. Arlo sees today's material and today's work.

If both men keep debating abstraction after Arlo has a physical test available, let the bench reclaim Arlo.

### Greg and Hessa

Both can care about distinctions.

Greg likes to keep turning them over. Hessa uses distinctions to govern evidence, body safety, and permission, then stops.

If Hessa starts sounding entertained by every distinction, review.

### Greg and Jorren

Greg interprets. Jorren often trusts what the body just did.

If Jorren repeatedly explains Greg to Greg instead of pointing at the physical fact or moving on, review.

### Greg and Alden

Overlap is intentional and valuable.

Do not erase the chemistry. Instead preserve the difference in direction: Greg tends toward interpretation and compressed framing, while Alden tends toward initiative and immediate use.

### Antonius and Arlo

Both can be practical and terse.

Antonius owns value, repayment, access, and transaction consequence. Arlo owns material, mechanism, test integrity, time, and authorship.

If either begins speaking as though they own the other's responsibility without evidence, review.

### Hessa and Arlo

Both can say `I don't know` and resist overclaiming.

Hessa's uncertainty governs body, evidence, and permission. Arlo's uncertainty stays attached to the physical object or test.

Do not let shared caution become shared doctrine.

### Jorren and Alden

Both are physically capable, direct, and willing to act.

Jorren is more willing to let the current physical answer end the question. Alden is more likely to ask what the answer buys next.

## Calibration rule

These fingerprints are tendencies, not buttons.

A scene passes when the characters remain recognizable across several turns and their modes diverge naturally enough that the exchange has social texture.

A scene does not fail because two characters both use a short sentence, both swear, both say `No`, or both make a joke.

The failure is repeated shared **architecture** without enough relationship, state, domain, or action difference to justify it.
