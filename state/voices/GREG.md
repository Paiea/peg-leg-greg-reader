# GREG — VOICE AUTHORITY

Exact manuscript prose outranks this file.

## Core lens

Greg notices systems, constraints, routes, money, object behavior, work boundaries, and the gap between what somebody says and what the situation actually permits.

His old-life experience gives him broad familiarity and comparison points. His second life makes ordinary mechanisms newly visible because he now walks, waits, pays, carries, depends on tools, and cannot brute-force inconvenience.

He is analytical, but he should not sound as though he has already written the essay explaining his own scene.

## Spoken rhythm

Greg often answers quickly. He likes short dry counters, literal interpretations, and extending another person's wording one step past usefulness.

He can also speak in longer runs when:
- negotiating scope;
- explaining an old-life category;
- defending a distinction he actually cares about;
- getting excited by a system;
- trying to talk his way around embarrassment.

Do not make every line a one-sentence punchline.

## Humor

Humor is often:
- defensive;
- boredom management;
- mild rebellion against authority;
- literal-minded escalation;
- self-directed embarrassment;
- turning ordinary inconvenience into mock bureaucracy.

He is funniest when somebody else refuses to play along.

## Disagreement

Greg likes distinctions. He may keep asking until the boundary becomes legible.

Danger: this can become Hessa-lite or authorial thesis delivery.

Let him sometimes:
- lose interest;
- accept a bad local explanation because it is enough for the job;
- be wrong longer;
- react emotionally before analytically;
- shut up because another person clearly owns the domain.

## Information behavior

Greg wants to know mechanism, category, cost, scope, and consequence.

He is learning to separate:
- recognition from expertise;
- observation from inference;
- wanting from evidence;
- availability from obligation;
- usefulness from belonging.

The later manuscript should increasingly show these distinctions through behavior rather than having him announce them.

## Relationship-specific Greg

### With Lyssa
More domestic, teasing, needy, sexual, irritated, soft, and willing to be ridiculous. He should not speak to her like a client debrief or Hessa session unless the scene specifically earns that collision.

### With Hessa
More argumentative precision. He performs intelligence partly because she will punish sloppy claims. Their humor is dry and adversarial, but Hessa does not owe him a joke.

### With theatre people
More performer/workplace energy. He can be embarrassed, competitive, eager to impress, and socially looser than with Guild authority.

### With clients / strangers
He increasingly scopes work, but do not turn every encounter into contractual boilerplate.

### With children
Literal play and fake seriousness often work better than polished adult wit.

## Canon lines / fingerprints

- **CANON LINE:** `I am nineteen. It is one of my remaining freedoms.`
- **CANON LINE:** `Personal growth is expensive.`
- **CANON LINE:** `I have seized the theatre.`
- **CANON LINE:** `I am specifically being paid not to interfere with the jars.`
- **CANON LINE:** `Grief requires ritual.`
- **CANON LINE:** `People carrying things through cities became temporary architecture.`

These are evidence of range, not catchphrases.

## Voice tests — NON-CANON

These lines are calibration only:

- `That sounds like a problem for somebody with a ladder.`
- `I understand the rule. I object to its personality.`
- `You have answered the question I asked and created two worse ones.`

Do not copy them into manuscript merely because they sound Greg-ish.

## Watch list

Cut or vary when repeated:
- `This was growth.`
- `Possibly.`
- `Good.` / `Excellent.` as isolated reaction paragraphs;
- `That mattered.`
- `Not X. Y.`
- `X was one thing. Y was another.`
- repeated declarations that a thing is not his job/project/profession;
- repeated explicit statements that he is not overclaiming after the scene already demonstrates restraint.

Greg remains analytical. The edit should remove redundant self-explanation, not his mind.
