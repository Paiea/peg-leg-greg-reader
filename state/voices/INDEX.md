# PEG-LEG GREG — CHARACTER VOICE LIBRARY

Durable dialogue/voice authority for recurring characters.

This folder exists to answer a practical writing question quickly:

**When this person opens their mouth, what makes the line belong to them rather than to Greg, Hessa, or the project-wide dry house voice?**

Exact manuscript prose outranks every page here. `CHARACTER_BIBLE.md` owns broader durable character facts. `PROSE_PLAYBOOK.md` owns project-wide craft. These files are focused speech/interaction references.

## Use

For a substantial scene involving a recurring character, read that character's page plus the exact recent manuscript context that matters.

Do **not** read the whole folder every chapter.

Do **not** force a voice page onto a scene when newer prose has clearly evolved the character. Update the page instead.

## Evidence labels

Voice pages may contain:

- **CANON LINE** — exact or tightly preserved manuscript dialogue used as evidence.
- **CANON PATTERN** — repeated manuscript-supported behavior/rhythm summarized without claiming exact wording.
- **VOICE TEST — NON-CANON** — a constructed line used only to hear the voice. It never establishes plot, fact, relationship, terminology, or world rules.

Never treat a VOICE TEST line as something the character actually said.

## Core principle

**Distinct cognition before decorative quirks.**

Differentiate characters through:
- what they notice first;
- what they think deserves explanation;
- what they consider a finished answer;
- whether they pursue, redirect, refuse, or abandon an argument;
- sentence length and interruption habits;
- certainty tolerance;
- relationship-specific permission;
- what kind of joke they make and why;
- what they regard as work, waste, danger, affection, disrespect, or bullshit.

Avoid fake differentiation through:
- accents invented after the fact;
- catchphrases assigned by author fiat;
- one-word gimmicks;
- everyone becoming more colorful merely because a voice file exists.

Shared Carrow colloquial speech is allowed. Shared workplace shorthand is allowed. People who spend time together may borrow phrases from one another. The goal is not maximum contrast. The goal is that recurring people possess minds and histories independent of Greg.

## Dialogue tagging policy

Voice distinction does **not** replace attribution.

Use `said` and `asked` freely.

Two-person exchanges may run untagged when sequence and voice are effortless.

For three or more speakers, err toward clarity. If the reader has to count alternating lines, add a tag or an action beat that earns itself.

A clean tag is better than a decorative shrug, nod, look, or hand motion included only to avoid `said`.

## Greg/Hessa contamination check

A recurring project failure is that everybody begins speaking the same compact analytical language:

- `Maybe.`
- `Probably.`
- `Useful.`
- `Good.`
- `What exactly do we know?`
- `That is a different question.`
- `Do not make it more than that.`

Some of these are naturally Greg-shaped. Some are strongly Hessa-shaped. Other people may use the same ordinary words, but repeated *reasoning architecture* should differ.

A cobbler may say `Don't know. Come back when it wears again.` without giving Hessa's methodological speech.

A worker may follow an inherited rule they cannot explain.

A neighbor may be confidently wrong.

A specialist may care about whether a thing works and not care what Greg calls the evidence.

## Page maintenance

When updating a voice page:
1. read actual relevant prose;
2. preserve contradictions rather than sanding them into one clean personality;
3. record relationship-specific differences;
4. remove stale guidance when later prose disproves it;
5. add only a few high-value canonical lines, not transcript dumps;
6. keep constructed voice tests clearly non-canon.

## Quick editorial test

Hide the tags in a multi-turn Greg/recurring-character exchange.

After several turns, can the speakers usually be inferred from priorities, rhythm, assumptions, and relationship rather than only from punchlines?

Then restore the tags anyway wherever clarity benefits.

If everybody could trade lines without changing the scene, the voices are too flat.
