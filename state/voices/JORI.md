# JORI — VOICE AUTHORITY

Exact manuscript prose outranks this file.

## Core lens

Jori notices whether a thing can be used safely and repeatedly in the space where it actually has to work: clearance, route, geometry, movement, witness marks, setup, and whether a repair behaves after somebody stops talking about it.

His practical authority overlaps other workers. It is not universal.

## Spoken rhythm

Concrete and work-shaped. He can be dry, but his sentences should usually stay attached to the object or movement in front of him.

He may communicate through:
- pointing at the exact interference;
- changing the geometry;
- asking for a roll/test;
- marking a position;
- giving one practical restriction.

He does not need to explain the philosophy of verification.

## Humor

Often emerges from shared work history, technical disagreement, credit, and somebody making the same irritating choice again.

## Disagreement

Jori can disagree with Davin or somebody else without that establishing hierarchy. Let the problem decide who knows more.

He may prefer testing over argument when the object can answer cheaply.

## Information behavior

He tends to care about whether the repair/setup is legible and repeatable in use.

He may know less about fabrication than Davin in one scene and more about stage clearance in the next.

## With Greg

As Greg learns the theatre, Jori can stop explaining obvious routes and simply adjust space or give shorthand. Practical accommodation can become automatic.

## Canon patterns

- **CANON PATTERN:** Uses chalk/witness marks for verification.
- **CANON PATTERN:** Adjusts stage geometry so people and objects have usable routes.
- **CANON PATTERN:** Shares repair/testing authority with Davin rather than supervising him universally.

## Voice tests — NON-CANON

- `Roll it again.`
- `Not the wheel. The corner.`
- `Mark it there so we know if it moves.`

Calibration only.

## Overlap allowed

Jori can be precise like Hessa. Difference: Jori usually wants the thing to work in physical space. He does not need to formalize the evidentiary claim after the test.

## Watch list

Avoid:
- universal engineer;
- safety oracle;
- formal technical boss;
- Hessa-with-tools;
- explaining every successful test after the object already proved the point.
