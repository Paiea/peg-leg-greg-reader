# JORREN - VOICE & DIALOGUE GUIDE

Status: **SPECIALIST EDITORIAL AID**

Manuscript authority outranks this file.

## Core conversational lens

Jorren thinks from the physical exchange outward.

He notices:
- stance;
- feet;
- rhythm;
- breathing;
- conditioning;
- whether somebody is overcommitting;
- whether a fighter is repeating an answer after the fight has changed;
- whether Greg is looking instead of moving;
- whether a body is tired regardless of what its owner says.

He does not need Greg's theory to recognize a fighting problem.

## Sentence shape

Default:
- short to medium;
- plain;
- corrective without lecture;
- comfortable letting the next spar prove the point.

Jorren can explain when Greg asks the right question, but his explanation tends to stay attached to what somebody physically did.

He is more likely to say a fighter `chased the next movement too hard` than to describe a generalized theory of initiative.

## Vocabulary and register

Working adventurer / training-yard language.

Natural domains:
- guard;
- distance;
- feet;
- rhythm;
- fatigue;
- getting hit;
- jobs;
- rank;
- whether the contract is actually finished.

He can be perceptive without sounding academically analytical.

## Humor

Dry peer humor.

Jorren is funny through:
- refusing Greg's grand self-description;
- pointing out the part immediately before Greg fell;
- hitting someone instead of extending the argument;
- recognizing `the face`;
- practical insults earned by shared training.

His humor is more physically grounded than Rusk's job-boundary humor.

## Information behavior

Jorren knows what repeated guild work and sparring have taught him.

He may see something Greg misses because Greg is busy building a larger model.

He does not need to posture as an instructor. He can simply have watched more fights from inside them.

## Conflict / disagreement

Jorren's strongest correction is often to make the physical situation contradict the story somebody is telling about it.

Examples of the pattern:
- Greg says he knows what to do; his body gets there late.
- Alden wins the first beat; Jorren punishes the chase.
- somebody says they are not tired; Jorren stops the session anyway.
- Greg spends the morning making other people better; Jorren reminds him he is one of the people.

## Relationship language with Greg

Jorren is a peer before he is a mentor.

Their relationship works because:
- Greg can see things in Jorren's fighting;
- Jorren can see the gap between Greg's mind and body;
- both are willing to hit the other person's weak assumption;
- neither needs the other to be impressed.

Do not promote Jorren into a universal wisdom figure. His authority is strongest around fighting, guild work, physical readiness, and the behavior he has actually observed.

## Relationship language with Alden

Jorren is less seduced by Alden's speed and confidence than Greg is.

He sees:
- overcommitment after early success;
- when Alden is actually tired;
- when a new correction has become a trick rather than a learned habit.

He is willing to let Alden improve and then immediately hit him for misusing the improvement.

## What he should not become

Do not flatten Jorren into:
- generic sparring partner;
- wise warrior mentor;
- Rusk with a sword;
- Greg's permanent reality-check machine;
- a man who has a philosophical explanation for every physical correction.

He can be wrong. He can miss things. His strongest voice comes from embodied familiarity, not omniscience.

## Useful canon fingerprints from early Book I

Patterns worth protecting:
- `You fight like an old man.`
- `Maybe tomorrow.` when Greg's body is visibly done;
- `You keep checking things you've already checked.`
- identifying Alden's mistake as spending a lead before knowing what it bought him;
- `You keep forgetting you're one of the people.`

These are patterns, not catchphrases.

## Attribution note

Jorren, Greg, and Alden all use compact dry lines. In three-man training scenes, tag pivots and interruptions rather than relying on cadence alone.
