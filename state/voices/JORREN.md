# JORREN - VOICE & DIALOGUE GUIDE

Status: **SPECIALIST EDITORIAL AID**

Manuscript authority outranks this file.

## Core conversational lens

Jorren thinks from the physical exchange outward.

He notices:
- stance;
- feet;
- rhythm;
- breathing;
- conditioning;
- whether somebody is overcommitting;
- whether a fighter is repeating an answer after the fight has changed;
- whether Greg is looking instead of moving;
- whether a body is tired regardless of what its owner says.

He does not need Greg's theory to recognize a fighting problem.

Outside work, Jorren is also unusually willing to let an hour simply be an hour. If there is no job for sixty minutes, he does not automatically convert the gap into training, planning, or improvement. He can sit, eat, wait, and call that complete.

## Sentence shape

Default:
- short to medium;
- plain;
- corrective without lecture;
- comfortable letting the next spar prove the point.

Jorren can explain when Greg asks the right question, but his explanation tends to stay attached to what somebody physically did.

He is more likely to say a fighter `chased the next movement too hard` than to describe a generalized theory of initiative.

Off duty, the same plainness becomes social ease rather than correction. He can answer a large Greg question with the amount of reaction the event actually deserves and stop there.

## Vocabulary and register

Working adventurer / training-yard language.

Natural domains:
- guard;
- distance;
- feet;
- rhythm;
- fatigue;
- getting hit;
- jobs;
- rank;
- whether the contract is actually finished.

He can be perceptive without sounding academically analytical.

In ordinary friendship scenes, his vocabulary can become even simpler: food, work later, sitting now, whether something hurts, whether Greg wants him to shout. Do not force training language into every conversation merely because it is his strongest professional domain.

## Humor

Dry peer humor.

Jorren is funny through:
- refusing Greg's grand self-description;
- pointing out the part immediately before Greg fell;
- hitting someone instead of extending the argument;
- recognizing `the face`;
- practical insults earned by shared training;
- accepting contradictory ordinary truths without needing to resolve them;
- offering exaggerated ceremony only after Greg complains that the real reaction was too small.

His humor is more physically and socially grounded than Rusk's job-boundary humor.

## Information behavior

Jorren knows what repeated guild work and sparring have taught him.

He may see something Greg misses because Greg is busy building a larger model.

He does not need to posture as an instructor. He can simply have watched more fights from inside them.

He also does not treat every piece of personal news as a problem requiring analysis. In Chapter 95, Greg's first post-amputation mana draw is simply good news. Jorren does not need the copper strip, phantom-sensation taxonomy, or testing protocol in order to be genuinely glad.

## Conflict / disagreement

Jorren's strongest correction is often to make the physical situation contradict the story somebody is telling about it.

Examples of the pattern:
- Greg says he knows what to do; his body gets there late.
- Alden wins the first beat; Jorren punishes the chase.
- somebody says they are not tired; Jorren stops the session anyway.
- Greg spends the morning making other people better; Jorren reminds him he is one of the people.

Off duty, disagreement can be even less elaborate. Greg asks why an empty hour must become something; Jorren's answer is that he is already sitting. The refusal to optimize is itself the difference.

## Relationship language with Greg

Jorren is a peer before he is a mentor.

Their relationship works because:
- Greg can see things in Jorren's fighting;
- Jorren can see the gap between Greg's mind and body;
- both are willing to hit the other person's weak assumption;
- neither needs the other to be impressed.

Later ordinary-life scenes add another important layer: they can spend time together without producing an outcome. Food, waiting for a bell, shared mockery, or sitting beside a dry fountain can be the whole scene.

Jorren's support is often unceremonious. He can say a small achievement is good, mean it completely, and refuse to inflate the moment unless Greg explicitly asks for a performance. That keeps friendship from becoming another coaching system.

Do not promote Jorren into a universal wisdom figure. His authority is strongest around fighting, guild work, physical readiness, and the behavior he has actually observed. His ordinary social ease is not secret philosophy. Sometimes he is just sitting.

## Relationship language with Alden

Jorren is less seduced by Alden's speed and confidence than Greg is.

He sees:
- overcommitment after early success;
- when Alden is actually tired;
- when a new correction has become a trick rather than a learned habit.

He is willing to let Alden improve and then immediately hit him for misusing the improvement.

## What he should not become

Do not flatten Jorren into:
- generic sparring partner;
- wise warrior mentor;
- Rusk with a sword;
- Greg's permanent reality-check machine;
- a man who has a philosophical explanation for every physical correction;
- a friend who exists only to validate Greg's milestones;
- a productivity foil who makes a doctrine out of relaxing.

He can be wrong. He can miss things. His strongest voice comes from embodied familiarity and ordinary peer permission, not omniscience.

## Useful canon fingerprints from early Book I / early Book II

Patterns worth protecting:
- `You fight like an old man.`
- `Maybe tomorrow.` when Greg's body is visibly done;
- `You keep checking things you've already checked.`
- identifying Alden's mistake as spending a lead before knowing what it bought him;
- `You keep forgetting you're one of the people.`
- **CANON LINE:** `Both can be true.`
- **CANON LINE:** `Then nothing.` when Greg cannot understand what Jorren plans to do with an hour before work.
- **CANON LINE:** `You want me to be impressed?`
- **CANON PATTERN:** Greg reports one tiny supervised mana draw; Jorren calls it good, means it, and offers to shout only when Greg complains the reaction is insufficient.

These are patterns and evidence, not catchphrases.

## Attribution note

Jorren, Greg, and Alden all use compact dry lines. In three-man training scenes, tag pivots and interruptions rather than relying on cadence alone.

In Greg/Jorren two-person downtime, lighter attribution is usually safe because physical companionship and the long-established peer rhythm carry ownership. Re-tag when a third person, vendor, trainer, or work authority joins the exchange.
