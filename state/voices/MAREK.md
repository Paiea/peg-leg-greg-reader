# MAREK — VOICE AUTHORITY

Exact manuscript prose outranks this file.

## Core lens

Marek is a performer with social momentum. He notices what is funny, what can be played, what embarrassment can be survived through confidence, and what the current scene or room will let him turn into forward motion.

He is not Greg with louder jokes.

Chapters 146-151 add an important professional layer: Marek's looseness is not the opposite of competence. He has enough touring history to understand scene shape, emergency substitution, audience energy, and the ugly practical fact that sometimes the work is simply doing the show with what exists.

## Spoken rhythm

More willing than Greg to commit before fully analyzing.

He can:
- answer with confidence;
- improvise;
- exaggerate;
- defend a bad idea because it is already happening;
- pivot quickly when concrete correction lands;
- cover a live mistake without stopping to prove he understood it;
- give one practical sentence about theatre and then stop before it becomes wisdom.

His rhythm is socially forward and less internally qualified than Greg's.

## Humor

Performance energy. Teasing, shamelessness, self-preservation, turning embarrassment into material.

The joke should sometimes cost him something or expose him, not always prove he is clever.

He can also borrow other people's vocabulary as a joke. Chapter 149's `Evidence unclear` about Greg's bitten apple is relationship contamination, not evidence that Marek has become Hessa-shaped.

## Work cognition

Marek knows enough touring reality to think in usable scene shape rather than ideal preparation.

Chapter 150 gives the cleanest version. Show frequency depends on town, money, weather, sickness, hall conditions, and whether people come. Rehearsal can be a week, three days, or somebody being told they are the priest now.

When Greg keeps waiting for the deeper lesson, Marek says only:

`That's the work.`

Do not turn that into a philosophy speech. The line matters because it is finished.

Iven names `the shape` of a scene, and Marek supplies practical components: who wants what, who enters, who leaves, which line somebody actually needs. This is working actor knowledge, not a formal dramaturgy system.

## Live recovery

Marek's improvisation is strongest when it preserves social momentum and scene function.

Examples:
- when another actor or prop changes, he can answer the current state rather than insist on the planned version;
- if he forgets a character name, `my loyal friend` can hold the scene until the name returns;
- when a chair collapses under him during the paid show, `I have been betrayed` converts an accident into scene material and lets the play continue.

A recovery being funny does not mean every accident should become Marek's comedy opportunity.

## Disagreement / refusal

Marek can argue reflexively, but he responds well to concrete domain-shaped correction when the person has earned authority there.

He does not need a long debate every time Nessa says stop, Serra corrects a partner beat, or Teren says the scene must move.

He can also refuse Greg access to information that is not Greg's. Chapter 147's `Not yours.` about Serra's private interaction is a useful peer boundary. Marek may know something socially without turning it into gossip because Greg asked.

## Information behavior

He may know current theatre changes better than paper because he was in the rehearsal where they changed.

He can create information by touching the object, trying the route, or making the scene move. That result may be useful or annoying.

Chapters 146-151 also establish that Marek has old history with the eastern hall and Coln. Exact prose does not yet explain enough to reduce that history to one clean summary. Preserve the partial visibility rather than making Marek deliver exposition.

## With Greg

More peer/rival/comic energy than methodological debate.

They can:
- needle each other;
- compete lightly;
- steal food;
- compare audience results;
- share performance context;
- recognize when the other has stepped on a private subject and move on.

Marek sometimes cares more about whether something plays than whether Greg has categorized it correctly.

He can also provide ordinary care without changing register. Chapter 150 notices Greg has eaten only bread and sends him downstairs for food. No caretaker framing follows.

## Voice tests — NON-CANON

- `It worked until you looked at it.`
- `I knew that. I was testing whether you did.`
- `The chair survived. This feels judgmental.`

Calibration only.

## Overlap allowed

Marek and Greg can both joke constantly. Difference: Greg often uses humor to analyze or control discomfort. Marek more often uses humor to keep social momentum and turn the current embarrassment into something playable.

Marek and Iven can both understand scene shape. Marek is more socially expansive and performative; Iven is more likely to give one dry timing/partner correction and stop.

Marek and Serra can both repair a live scene. Serra often repairs from partner information and scene relationship. Marek often repairs through immediate social commitment and momentum.

## Watch list

Avoid:
- permanent idiot;
- chaos gremlin with no competence;
- Greg syntax plus extra exclamation;
- every object touch causing trouble;
- every Nessa exchange becoming scold/comic punishment;
- making him secretly profound whenever the manuscript needs a lesson;
- turning `That's the work` into a recurring sermon;
- making old-hall history into automatic exposition because Marek visibly knows more;
- treating every live accident as an invitation for a joke when sometimes the professional move is simply to continue.
