# MERRIN — VOICE AUTHORITY

Exact manuscript prose outranks this file.

## Core lens

Merrin thinks from **institutional ownership, records, and routing** outward.

He cares about:
- which desk or registry actually answers the question;
- who has authority to mark, release, accept, or redirect something;
- whether a request belongs to front desk, yard, stores, kitchen, or another named person;
- what a substitute is explicitly allowed to do;
- what information is not the other person's business.

His precision is administrative rather than abstract. He knows the Guild because he works inside its permissions and handoffs, not because he wants to theorize systems.

## Spoken rhythm

Short to medium, clipped, and often impatient because the queue is real.

Merrin can give Greg a list of constraints when the role requires it, then stop. He does not pad an instruction with explanation unless the explanation is needed for the work.

He is comfortable with a direct `No`, `Wait`, or `Not your business.`

## Vocabulary and register

Natural domains include:
- ledgers;
- registries;
- tokens;
- bay boards;
- marks and releases;
- queues;
- which person or department owns a request;
- document review and clerical procedure.

Do not make him sound like a policy manual. His authority is practical and local.

## Humor

Dry institutional humor, usually at Greg's expense when Greg mistakes proximity to the desk for authority.

He can pay Greg one bit for being a `substitute clerk` while immediately denying that Greg was actually clerking well.

Humor should remain secondary to the fact that Merrin keeps the front hall moving.

## Information behavior

Merrin knows which source is valid for which question.

Chapter 89 makes several boundaries concrete:
- a bay can be released but still need the correct mark;
- Dena owns the bay board when Merrin is off desk;
- a Tavin check-in question belongs to a different registry from the ledger Greg almost opened;
- damaged spearheads route to stores;
- Guild wash can have a local receiving rule even when other staff disagree about where the basket belongs.

He does not disclose unrelated upstairs business just because Greg is curious.

## With Greg

Merrin is willing to use Greg for a narrow temporary function while denying him the authority Greg would naturally grow around it.

Their useful friction is:
- Greg sees the shape of the problem and wants to route or infer;
- Merrin knows which inferences are actually authorized and which named person owns the next step.

Merrin can acknowledge when Greg's redirect was useful without promoting him into the role.

## Canon lines / fingerprints

- **CANON LINE:** `Not your business.`
- **CANON LINE:** `Sit there. If anyone asks where I am, say upstairs.`
- **CANON LINE:** `You'd be terrible.`
- **CANON LINE:** `Substitute clerk.`
- **CANON PATTERN:** knows that Dena owns the bay board when he is off desk and that a Tavin check-in belongs to a different registry from the visible front-desk ledger.
- **CANON PATTERN:** routes a question to the correct registry/person instead of assuming the front desk or current ledger is universally authoritative.

These are evidence, not catchphrases.

## Overlap allowed

Merrin can be concise like Edrin, Merra, Octavia, or Antonius.

The distinction is what closes the question:
- Edrin closes on warding/custody and investigative access;
- Merra closes on what archival records support;
- Octavia closes on sequence, timing, route, and the next handoff;
- Antonius closes on what the operation is willing to buy or risk;
- Merrin closes on who owns the request and which record or permission can actually authorize the next action.

## Watch list

Avoid turning Merrin into:
- generic bureaucrat satire;
- omniscient Guild clerk;
- Hessa with paperwork;
- a universal gatekeeper for every Guild function;
- somebody who explains the institution to Greg for reader convenience;
- a man whose only joke is telling Greg no.
