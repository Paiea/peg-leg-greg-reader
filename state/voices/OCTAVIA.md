# OCTAVIA — VOICE AUTHORITY

Exact manuscript prose outranks this file.

## Core lens

Octavia notices **sequence, timing, route, and what people actually did before they started explaining it**.

Her freight work trains her to care about what moved where, in what order, under which constraint, and what the next handoff requires. Chapter 87 shows the same practical memory operating socially: she remembers Greg's first fast bird several exchanges later and uses that fact without turning it into a theory lecture.

Chapter 108 sharpens the social version further. Noticing Greg's signal is not the same as believing the message Greg hoped the signal would carry. Octavia can update on the behavior, keep a coarse useful model, and still make a different decision at the table.

She is not Hessa with cargo and not Greg with better social instincts. She usually keeps only the information that remains useful to the next decision.

## Spoken rhythm

Short to medium, confident, and comfortable refusing unnecessary explanation.

She can give a complete practical answer when the route or rule requires it, but she does not automatically explain strategy, motive, or the whole chain of reasoning merely because Greg asks.

Her refusals can be playful rather than defensive.

In a crowded table scene, she can also reclaim the exchange with one name or one rule. Her authority does not require ornate dialogue.

## Vocabulary and register

Natural domains include:
- freight;
- routes;
- timing;
- offices and handoffs;
- what arrived or still has to move;
- game rules when playing socially;
- observable behavior that changed the immediate decision.

Do not turn her into a generalized behavioral analyst because she is good at reading a card table.

## Humor

Confident, social, and willing to let another person walk into the joke.

Octavia often enjoys Greg's attempt to control how he is being read more than she enjoys explaining that she sees through it.

She can tease without making every line a punchline.

Chapter 108 shows that her humor can come from using a deliberately coarse model instead of dignifying Greg's recursive theory. When he asks whether she updated her model of him, she can truthfully say she did: `Greg is going to do something irritating.` That is funny because it is locally sufficient, not because she has solved him.

## Information behavior

Octavia distinguishes between **what another person needs to participate** and **what would help them compete against her**.

Chapter 87 makes this explicit:
- she answers rule questions;
- refuses strategy questions;
- remembers a small earlier behavior when it later matters;
- does not overclaim that one tell explains the whole player;
- treats a clue as enough `for that hand`, not as a universal law.

Chapter 108 adds another important distinction: **observed signal, inferred message, and final decision are separate events**. Her line to Greg is explicit: `You think because I noticed the thing, I believed the message attached to the thing.` She may notice his performed tell, infer something other than what he intended, and still choose based on cards, history, or the way the current hand developed.

She also adapts across sessions. Greg's previous success becomes part of today's sequence. In one hand she calls with only middling cards because he appears too pleased with an early bad decision and because she remembers his prior appetite for turning one trick into a system. This remains local judgment, not omniscience.

After a hand, she can refuse Greg's requested postmortem entirely. `No postmortem` and `House` preserve the game as a social game rather than converting every loss into a lesson she owes him.

That boundary matters. Her practical social reading should stay local and usable rather than becoming Hessa-style evidence doctrine.

## With Greg

Octavia is comfortable letting Greg be clever without granting him control of the exchange.

She notices:
- his informative face;
- when he is deliberately performing uncertainty;
- when his attention has left the actual cards for a theory about the room;
- when not explaining hurts him more than losing;
- when he has become committed to a bad earlier choice because he wants the choice to mean something.

She can encourage his playfulness by refusing to over-service his need for explanation.

Importantly, she does not need to beat him every time. Chapter 108 ends with Greg winning an honest strong hand because the cards are simply good, then leaving while ahead on that hand and still down overall. Octavia's authority survives ordinary losses.

## Overlap allowed

Octavia can be concise and practical like Sevren, Nessa, Antonius, or Merrin.

The distinction is what she keeps:
- Sevren keeps route/custody facts needed to finish the delivery;
- Nessa keeps material continuity needed to move objects correctly;
- Antonius keeps operation facts that change what should be bought or risked;
- Merrin keeps institutional ownership and record-routing facts;
- Octavia keeps sequence, timing, handoff, and behavior that changes the next move.

## Watch list

Avoid turning Octavia into:
- a poker savant whose main trait is reading tells;
- a generic freight clerk;
- Hessa with social evidence;
- an omniscient reader of people;
- somebody who explains every trick after using it;
- a character engineered to beat Greg at every conversational game;
- somebody who assumes noticing a performance means understanding the performer's intended message;
- Greg's private strategy tutor after every hand.
