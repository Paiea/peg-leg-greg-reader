# PESSA — VOICE AUTHORITY

Exact manuscript prose outranks this file.

## Core lens

Pessa notices **what the body, road, route, or drill is actually doing** and what decision follows from that physical reality.

Her precision is embodied and field-practical. She is not Greg with fewer words and not Hessa transplanted outdoors.

When Greg wants a category, she often wants the consequence.

When Greg wants a model, she often wants the next usable decision.

When a trainee wants to count an outcome as success, she asks what the drill was supposed to train.

Later adaptive training sharpens this further: Pessa does not begin with a theory of disabled combat or by carving Greg's old fighting into a list of supposedly transferable parts. She first asks what he actually wants to be able to do, then what changes when the body meets one concrete object or demand.

## Spoken rhythm

Compact and declarative. She can explain fully when a correction needs a reason, but usually starts with the smallest physical truth that changes the attempt.

Common shapes:
- one-word correction;
- concrete observation;
- short explanation tied to body/ground/objective;
- reset and try again.

She does not usually turn a correction into a general theory unless the scene genuinely requires it.

## Adaptive training with Greg

Chapters 92-93 establish a useful method that future scenes should preserve without turning it into a formal named system.

Pessa refuses Greg's first instinct to decompose `combat` into a checklist and build an adaptation architecture from twenty minutes of observation. She narrows the goal until it becomes physically usable: what does Greg want to be able to do today?

From there she starts smaller than Greg wants:
- hold the shield;
- notice what changes;
- change crutch position;
- turn the head;
- track an object;
- push;
- stop when the body changes enough to matter.

The first phase is **inventory before architecture**. A useful observation does not automatically become a universal solution.

She is particularly alert to Greg solving the exercise instead of learning the body. If he moves the chair against a wall to prevent sliding, her answer is that he will then learn the wall. If he starts seated because seated is easier, he may prematurely decide seated is the answer.

Pessa can admit `I don't know` when Greg's changed body creates a training problem she has not yet solved. That uncertainty is local and practical, not a performance of methodological humility.

## With Greg

Pessa does not treat Greg's analysis as final merely because it is intelligent.

She is comfortable saying `No` when he is close but wrong, then giving the narrower physical correction.

She can use Greg as an observer or extra set of eyes while retaining final authority inside her lane.

She does not need to reassure him that his interpretation was almost right.

When Greg jumps from an event to a category, Pessa often pulls him one level back. `The problem is sequencing` may become `Maybe the problem is sequencing.` `Less torque` may become the more useful human answer `Feels safer.`

## Jurisdiction with Hessa

Pessa does not compete with Hessa for total authority over Greg.

Hessa owns the magic lane. Pessa owns the movement/training lane. Pessa cares about magical work when it changes the body she is about to train, but she does not need the magical phenomenology itself if Hessa already owns that question.

Likewise, Pessa does not surrender her own stopping conditions merely because Hessa is involved. Her position is practical: she cares what she does, and tomorrow's training depends on swelling, hands, fatigue, and the actual physical response to today's work.

## With Alden

More coach than peer.

She likes repeatable attempts, shorter commitments, and letting fatigue expose what speed hides.

She does not confuse winning the exchange with succeeding at the drill.

Alden's eagerness is material to work with, not a personality defect to lecture away.

## Humor

Dry and situational. She can undercut Greg or Alden without changing register into a comedy voice.

Her jokes usually come from the immediate work or from somebody pretending a physical fact does not exist.

## Voice tests — NON-CANON

- `Road still carries. Fix the wash first.`
- `You won the exchange. That wasn't the drill.`

Calibration only.

## Overlap allowed

Pessa can be concise like Hessa, Rinna, Tam, or Teren.

The distinction is source of precision:
- Hessa: safety, repeatability, body/magic evidence, expectation control, and permission;
- Teren: playable stage result;
- Tam: customer use and shoe material;
- Pessa: physical route/body consequence, drill objective, load, fatigue, and the next usable movement decision.
