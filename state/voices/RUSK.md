# RUSK - VOICE & DIALOGUE GUIDE

Status: **SPECIALIST EDITORIAL AID**

Manuscript authority outranks this file.

## Core conversational lens

Rusk thinks from the assigned job inward.

He cares about:
- what Antonius told them to do;
- what needs moving, collecting, carrying, or securing;
- whether Greg is turning one task into six;
- whether the immediate work is finished;
- whether a proposed clever solution exceeds their authority.

He is not incurious or unintelligent. His narrowness is often deliberate operational discipline.

## Sentence shape

Default:
- short;
- direct;
- low explanation density;
- comfortable with one-word answers;
- often lets an action, point, shrug, or look complete the thought.

Rusk should rarely give a Greg-length explanation unless the situation truly requires one.

## Vocabulary and register

Plain working register.

He speaks naturally about:
- carts;
- routes;
- money to collect;
- orders;
- security/collateral when the job requires it;
- what Antonius said;
- whether something is actually their problem.

He may know contract or collection language from doing the work without sounding formally educated about finance.

Education is not intelligence. Rusk often sees Greg's behavioral pattern before Greg does because he is watching the person rather than building the theory.

## Humor

Understatement and refusal.

Rusk is funny when:
- Greg announces a complicated plan and Rusk says `No.`
- Greg claims not to be interested after buying chalk.
- Greg says `not enough information yet` and Rusk translates it as `you don't know`.
- a practical fact punctures Greg's self-image.

Do not give him joke setups. His humor works because he is not trying very hard to be funny.

## Information behavior

Rusk knows what his work has taught him.

He may know:
- which debtor usually pays;
- who Jast is;
- how an agreement is actually written;
- which routes are used;
- what Antonius instructed;
- when Greg is improvising beyond permission.

He does not need to volunteer background simply because Greg wants the whole system.

A shrug or `No idea` is legitimate Rusk dialogue.

## Conflict / disagreement

Rusk usually attacks scope, not Greg's intelligence.

Typical disagreement shape:
- Greg: make the system larger.
- Rusk: collect the money / move the box / do the work.

He can participate once Greg finds a practical structure, as in the Selka negotiation. That matters. He is not a wall who says no to everything. He changes position when the job-level solution becomes clear.

## Relationship language with Greg

Rusk quickly learns Greg's patterns:
- `the face`;
- disappearing into his head;
- expanding assignments;
- wanting credit for almost knowing;
- finding open-ended uncertainty irritating.

He can needle Greg because he spends enough time beside him to see the repetition.

Greg may initially treat Rusk as muscle or obstacle. The prose should keep proving Rusk has his own operational competence and judgment.

## What he should not become

Do not flatten Rusk into:
- dumb muscle;
- Antonius's generic henchman;
- comic `No` machine;
- secret philosopher;
- Hessa-style disciplinarian;
- someone who exists only to tell Greg he is wrong.

His value is that he knows the work, the boundary, and the person next to him.

## Useful canon fingerprints from early Book I

Patterns worth protecting:
- `Cart.`
- `Floor.`
- `Money.`
- calling creditor labor work when Greg tries to redesign it instead;
- identifying `not enough information yet` as wanting credit for almost knowing;
- knowing the language of an agreement when Greg understands the structure but not the local paperwork;
- participating in the two-day Selka extension once the practical terms become usable;
- noticing Greg disappears into his head and stops avoiding carts.

These are patterns, not required repeated phrases.

## Attribution note

Rusk's short lines can disappear inside a Greg/Antonius/Selka scene if the prose relies only on alternation. Re-establish him with `Rusk said/asked` at pivots rather than padding his lines to make them self-identifying.
