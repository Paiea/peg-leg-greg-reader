# SERRA — VOICE AUTHORITY

Exact manuscript prose outranks this file.

## Identity

Serra is the recurring rehearsal/acting woman who is explicitly named in Chapter 143.

Keep her distinct from `SERA.md` and `SERA_DAIN.md`. Similar names are not identity evidence.

## Core lens

Serra notices **what a scene partner can actually play from**: path, distance, eye line, timing, whether a reaction has forced another reaction, what the audience is looking at, and whether the current behavior belongs to this scene.

Her theatre precision is actor-facing rather than page-facing or object-facing.

She can care whether a recovery works even when Teren separately cares whether the intended entrance or cue was correct. Those are different questions.

## Spoken rhythm

Direct, playable, and comfortable with short corrections.

Useful Serra shapes include:
- `Again.`
- `Choose.`
- `Once.`
- `Don't.`
- `Too grand.`
- naming what she physically had to answer from Greg rather than theorizing acting in general.

She can explain more when Greg asks a question that belongs to her experience as a scene partner, but she does not need to turn that answer into doctrine.

## Acting / staging behavior

Serra's strongest corrections come from relationship inside the scene.

Examples from the rehearsal run:
- Greg moves aside twice when only one committed move is useful: `Choose.` / `Once.`
- Greg nods during a silent role and she stops him because the nod creates something she now has to answer.
- Greg's sword voice can make her look at Greg instead of the prop; that is immediately useful actor evidence.
- a voice can become `too grand` because the threat becomes so consequential that the playable comedy changes.
- a line can work because the partner receives something usable, even when Greg cannot yet describe what he did.

Do not turn these into universal acting rules. They are observations from somebody sharing the scene.

## Humor

Social, quick, and comfortable inside rehearsal disorder.

Serra can tease Greg's accumulating theatre identities without needing to dominate the scene. `Messenger`, `Landlord`, `Person`, and later role jokes work because she has watched the room keep using him.

She can also let a ridiculous prop, cabbage, missing belt, or bad performance choice remain funny without converting it into commentary.

## Disagreement / refusal

Serra can say `No` because something gives her the wrong partner behavior, forces an unnecessary response, or breaks the scene she is currently playing.

Her refusal should not sound like Hessa's evidence boundary or Nessa's material continuity. It is often: **I cannot play the next beat cleanly from what you just gave me.**

She does not need to explain every `No` before another run answers the question.

## Information behavior

Serra knows troupe history, roles, actor habits, and enough current work to move inside the company without explaining herself to Greg.

Chapter 143 shows that she knows the names behind the role labels Greg has been using: Iven, Marek, Orin, Davin, and her own.

She is not the company's contract, pay, or route authority. She can know a lot socially without owning Teren's hall negotiation or Rinna's business records.

She may also have relationships and errands outside Greg's view. Chapter 145 lets her leave with a woman in a yellow scarf without stopping to supply Greg context. That is ordinary independent social life, not a mystery-box signal.

## With Greg

The relationship is increasingly peer-like and low ceremony.

Serra can:
- correct his stage movement;
- laugh at him;
- give actor feedback;
- hand him a bag without negotiating whether he has joined the troupe;
- give him a blanket when the wagon floor is impractical;
- notice when his body answer comes too quickly after travel;
- move on once the useful answer arrives.

Chapter 142 gives a clean disability/travel pattern. Serra suggests the wagon floor. Greg says getting up is the problem. She understands, moves a shoe bag, and pushes him a folded blanket for the crate. No apology, pity, or speech.

Chapter 143 repeats the pattern after a long ride. Greg's right leg nearly folds as he dismounts. Serra is beside him immediately but does not grab him. When his first `yes` comes too quickly, she asks again. `Stiff. Ordinary.` is enough. She leaves.

That is care at peer scale, not medical ownership.

## Voice tests — NON-CANON

- `You gave me nowhere to look but you.`
- `That works. It isn't the same as right.`
- `If you move twice, I have to decide which move you meant.`

Calibration only.

## Overlap allowed

Serra and Teren can both say `Again`, `No`, or give small performance corrections.

The distinction is what they are protecting:
- Teren: page, cue, prompt, cut, local continuity, and current company consequence;
- Serra: partner relationship, staging behavior, eye line, playable reaction, and what the actor can do with the beat.

Serra and Nessa can both care where bodies and objects go. Serra's question is what the actor can play from. Nessa's is what the costume/prop/material path must physically do.

Serra can share Lyssa's low-ceremony practical care around Greg's body, but the relationship species is different. Serra is a theatre peer, not Greg's intimate partner.
