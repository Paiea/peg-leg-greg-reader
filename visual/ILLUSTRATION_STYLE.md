# Illustrated Reader Generation Style

## North star
Create a higher-quality version of the visual language already present in Peg-Leg Greg. New art should feel as though the same illustrated edition continued while the artist became more technically accomplished.

## Keep
- sketch-forward ink/pencil linework
- visible hand-drawn texture
- storybook / graphic-novel readability
- restrained painterly or watercolor-like fills
- warm, tactile materials and paper-like atmosphere
- expressive but believable faces
- practical, lived-in fantasy spaces
- compositions that support the prose rather than advertise themselves

## Improve
- facial consistency and age accuracy
- hands and anatomy
- perspective and spatial clarity
- lighting hierarchy
- fabric, wood, stone and metal rendering
- background specificity
- silhouette separation
- scene readability at reader-page size

## Avoid
- photorealism
- glossy 3D rendering
- generic high-fantasy concept-art splash screens
- anime/manga visual reset
- excessive cinematic bloom
- hyper-clean vector/comic rendering
- decorative text baked into production illustrations
- montage/contact-sheet output as final chapter art

## Composition policy
A chapter illustration is a single scene image. Contact sheets may be used for internal review only. Production images should not contain chapter numbers, captions, fake quotes, checklists, invented signage, or placement instructions unless that text physically exists in the scene.

For Greg specifically, favor upper-body and occluded compositions to protect mobility continuity. Do not let this become repetitive: vary camera distance, foreground objects, other-character emphasis, over-the-shoulder views, seated framing, prop framing and environmental shots.

## Quality test
At a glance, a reader who has seen Chapters 1-155 should recognize the same book. On closer inspection, the new image should have cleaner anatomy, more confident draftsmanship, stronger lighting and more specific environments.
